I lower that anchor to **1.5%** because the apparent conflict is less clean than the petition's characterization and the record has no affirmative request for a response, relist, or other additional Court attention. The waiver is modest negative evidence, not dispositive. A published, unanimous appellate opinion and an issue potentially affecting accommodation practice leave a genuine, small grant possibility.

The petition's QPs I-II and reasons section, printed pages 12-16, assert that some circuits recognize independently actionable process failures. But its own quoted Taylor and Cutrera formulations connect the process failure to a lost reasonable accommodation. That is not plainly a conflict about liability despite an effective accommodation. The provisioned appellate opinion, Appendix A, pages 8a-12a, identifies two grounds: Redding's pleaded inability to perform the desired Regional Coordinator position's essential functions and TSA's provision of reasonable reassignment. It also expressly finds good-faith collaboration, rather than simply acknowledging bad faith and declaring it irrelevant. Those differences create both factual and vehicle obstacles. I treat the alleged concealment and training deficiencies as petitioner's allegations, not independently established facts.

I checked Strife v. AISD, 138 F.4th 237 (5th Cir. 2025), through CourtListener. The opinion's failure-to-accommodate discussion treats qualification as undisputed and addresses an alleged six-month delay in providing an accommodation. My inference is that a period of unjustified nonaccommodation can be distinguished from this panel's findings of disqualification for the desired job and a reasonable alternative reassignment. Strife gives the petition a substantive argument, but does not establish that identical facts would receive opposite judgments.

I also checked A.J.T. v. Osseo Area Schools, 605 U.S. 335 (2025), especially the majority's discussion at 345. It declined to decide a proposed across-the-board liability standard that was neither resolved below nor within the question presented. Read alongside the petition's own account at pages 15-16, that makes the asserted direct conflict weaker than a claim that A.J.T. already settled this disclosure duty. The decision predates the March 3, 2026 appellate judgment, so it is not an intervening postjudgment authority. QP III's Bumper analogy would require a substantial extension from search consent to employment accommodation; I give that analogy little independent grant weight without treating it as an established employment rule.
