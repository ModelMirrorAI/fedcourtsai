# Forecast: Redding v. Mullin, No. 25-1336 (evt-petition-disposition)

**Disposition.** The Court will deny the petition. The likeliest path is a
denial on the order list following the September 28, 2026 long conference,
with no separate writing.

**Relists.** The docket shows **one distribution** (for the conference of
9/28/2026) and no relist. I expect no further distribution: the federal
respondent has waived its response, the petition arrives at the long
conference where the great majority of waived-response paid petitions are
denied in a single pass, and I find no pending Supreme Court case on the
interactive-process question that could make this a hold. The main way the
count rises is a call for a response from the Solicitor General, which would
push the petition to a later conference after the response is filed; I put
that, together with an ordinary reschedule, at roughly one chance in seven.

**CVSG.** None. The Solicitor General already represents the respondent, so
there is no non-party federal interest for the Court to invite views on; a
CVSG is effectively foreclosed by the caption.

**Which question, if any.** If the Court took anything it would be a
reformulated version of QP I/II: whether an employer's failure to conduct
the interactive process in good faith is independently actionable under the
ADA/Rehabilitation Act when a reasonable accommodation was ultimately
provided. It would not take QP I as drafted (fact-bound to this record), nor
QP III, which imports Fourth Amendment consent doctrine (Bumper) into an
employment accommodation dispute and has no lower-court footing.

**Summary disposition.** Not the likelier route, conditional on a grant. No
intervening decision of this Court bears on the interactive-process split
(A.J.T. v. Osseo predates the Fourth Circuit's decision and the panel did
not decide the standards question A.J.T. left open), so a GVR has nothing to
be "in light of." A grant, if it happened, would most likely come after a
call for a response and set the case for argument. I hold some weight on a
summary route only because weak-vehicle grants against the government
occasionally resolve in the cert order.

**Dissent from denial.** None expected. The A.J.T. concurrences show two
Justices interested in intent standards under the disability statutes, but
this record (a conceded inability to perform the essential functions of the
desired position, an alternative holding that a reasonable accommodation was
in fact provided, and a unanimous ideologically mixed panel) is not the
vehicle a Justice would write on.
