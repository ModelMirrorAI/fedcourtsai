## What happened

Stephanie Redding worked as a Federal Air Marshal for TSA until medical conditions made her unable to meet flight duty requirements. While her application for federal disability retirement was pending, TSA offered her a reassignment to a different agency, which she accepted. Redding's petition says TSA never told her that accepting the reassignment would cancel her retirement benefit, and closed her accommodation case within days, before she finished required training. A federal district court dismissed her disability-discrimination lawsuit, finding she had not shown she could do her prior job's essential functions. The Fourth Circuit affirmed, holding that the completed reassignment was "manifestly reasonable" and that this ended the inquiry.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked whether an employer meets its duty to work in good faith with a disabled employee toward an accommodation when it offers a job transfer while withholding that accepting it will cost the employee a separate retirement benefit, and then declares the process finished before the new job's training is done. Relatedly, the petition asks whether it was wrong for the appeals court to treat a completed reassignment as closing off any look at whether the process behind it was conducted honestly, and whether an employee's agreement to an accommodation can count as voluntary when the government withheld facts material to that choice.
