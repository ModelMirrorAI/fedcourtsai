## In brief

The Court declined to hear a former TSA employee's bid to revive her disability-accommodation lawsuit against the Department of Homeland Security.

## What happened

Stephanie Redding, a former Federal Air Marshal, left her job after medical conditions made her unable to perform required duties. TSA reassigned her to a lower-paying position at another agency as an accommodation, then closed her case. Redding says TSA never told her the reassignment would cancel a disability-retirement application she had pending, and that TSA ended the accommodation process before she finished required training. A district court dismissed her Rehabilitation Act lawsuit, finding she had not adequately claimed she could do the job she wanted even with help. The Fourth Circuit affirmed, holding the reassignment was a reasonable accommodation and that TSA had acted in good faith.

## What the Court is being asked

Redding asked the Court to decide whether an employer satisfies its duty to work in good faith with a disabled employee seeking accommodation when it offers a reassignment while hiding that accepting it will cancel a separate retirement benefit, and ends the process before the new assignment's training is finished. She also asked whether a completed reassignment should block courts from examining whether the process behind it was conducted honestly, and whether an employee's participation can count as voluntary consent when the employer withheld facts about the consequences.

## Where it stands

The federal government waived its right to respond to the petition. The case was distributed for the Court's September 28, 2026 conference, and the Court denied the petition on October 5, 2026.

## What each outcome would mean

Redding, as the petitioner, asked the Court to take up her case. With the petition denied, the Fourth Circuit's ruling against Redding stands, meaning her lawsuit remains dismissed and DHS's position prevails. Had the Court instead granted the petition, it would have agreed to hear full arguments on the question without yet deciding who was right.
