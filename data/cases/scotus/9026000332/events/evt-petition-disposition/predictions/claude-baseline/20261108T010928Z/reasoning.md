# Reasoning: P(grant) = 0.20 for No. 26-332, Gulf Coast Racing v. HISA (cross-petition)

**Posture (snapshot 2026-11-08.json).** This is a paid cross-petition from the Fifth Circuit's June 11, 2026 post-GVR decision in *National Horsemen's v. Black* (No. 23-10520). The decision held HISA's enforcement provisions facially unconstitutional under the private-nondelegation doctrine. It upheld the rulemaking provisions and rejected Gulf Coast's Appointments Clause claim. The petition presents two questions: QP1 asks whether the Authority's directors must be appointed under the Appointments Clause, and QP2 is a fallback private-nondelegation question. The case was docketed 9/11/2026. Two oppositions were filed on 10/13: a short memorandum from the federal respondents (SG/FTC) and a full BIO from HISA, both covering 26-332, 26-335 and 26-430. The reply was filed 10/27. The petition was distributed once, on 11/4, for the 12/4/2026 conference. There is one amicus brief (Repole Stable), shared with 26-199, 26-201 and 26-335. There is no CVSG, but the SG is already a party.

**Companion structure (from my own docket retrieval, forward mode).**
- On supremecourt.gov, No. 26-199 (HISA v. NHBPA) is the lead petition on the enforcement holding. Every challenger, Gulf Coast included, filed a response agreeing it should be granted. It is distributed for the same 12/4 conference. No. 26-201 is the FTC's parallel petition.
- No. 25-1325 (Oklahoma v. United States, CA6) raises the rulemaking question. It was rescheduled from 10/9 and is also set for 12/4.
- A grant in 26-199/26-201 looks very likely. There is an acknowledged CA5/CA6 split, and a federal statute was held facially invalid. The real question is what the Court does with the cross-petitions.
- `related/26-335.json` (Texas's cross-petition, relation `vided-amicus`, one shared brief) has a trajectory identical to this one.

**Why not higher.**
- The SG and HISA both oppose this cross-petition specifically.
- No court has accepted the Appointments Clause claim, and the CA6 case did not raise it, so there is no split.
- HISA raises a standing objection: it says the cross-petitioners are not subject to HISA rules. The reply answers that the objection was not raised below and that the FTC does not join it. Still, it is a vehicle cloud.
- Respondents in 26-199 can defend the judgment on alternative grounds without a grant of their own petition.
- Ordinary practice is to grant the government's petition and deny, or hold and later deny, the cross-petitions.

**Why not lower.**
- The Fifth Circuit said only the Supreme Court could adopt the cross-petitioners' Appointments Clause theory.
- Justice Gorsuch flagged the officer question in his *Consumers' Research* dissent (606 U.S. at 720 n.6).
- The Court may want the whole HISA structure before it in one case.
- A hold followed by a GVR in light of the merits decision in 26-199 also counts as a grant.
- My rough split: about 0.12 for an outright grant (alone or consolidated) and about 0.08 for a hold then GVR. The rest is denial, either at the 12/4 conference or after a hold.
