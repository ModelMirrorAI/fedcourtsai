No. 26-332 is a conditional cross-petition for a writ of certiorari arising out of the Fifth Circuit's decision on remand in *National Horsemen's Benevolent & Protective Ass'n v. Black*, 163 F.4th 294 (5th Cir. 2026) (*Horsemen's III*), which reinstated its prior decision in *Horsemen's II*, 107 F.4th 415 (5th Cir. 2024). In the decision below, the Fifth Circuit held that the enforcement provisions of the Horseracing Integrity and Safety Act of 2020 (HISA), 15 U.S.C. §§ 3051–3060, violate the private nondelegation doctrine, Creating a direct circuit split with the Sixth and Eighth Circuits. Both the Horseracing Integrity and Safety Authority (No. 26-199) and the Federal Trade Commission / Solicitor General (No. 26-201) have petitioned for certiorari to review the Fifth Circuit's invalidation of HISA's enforcement provisions, and all parties agree that certiorari should be granted in those lead petitions.

Alongside the lead petitions in Nos. 26-199 and 26-201, the three plaintiff groups in the consolidated Fifth Circuit litigation filed separate conditional cross-petitions challenging aspects of the Fifth Circuit's decision that rejected their alternative constitutional attacks on HISA:
1. **No. 26-332 (*Gulf Coast Racing LLC, et al. v. HISA, et al.* — this cell):** Raises (1) an Appointments Clause challenge arguing that HISA's Directors are Officers of the United States notwithstanding the Authority's incorporation as a private Delaware nonprofit corporation and the three-prong test of *Lebron v. National Railroad Passenger Corp.*, 513 U.S. 374 (1995); and (2) in the alternative, a private-nondelegation challenge to HISA's rulemaking and enforcement provisions.
2. **No. 26-335 (*Texas, et al. v. Black, et al.*):** Raises a private-nondelegation challenge to HISA's rulemaking provisions (which the Fifth Circuit upheld following Congress's December 2022 amendment to 15 U.S.C. § 3053(e)).
3. **No. 26-430 (*National Horsemen's Benevolent & Protective Ass'n, et al. v. HISA, et al.*):** Also challenges the Fifth Circuit's upholding of HISA's rulemaking provisions.

While the Supreme Court is virtually certain to grant review in the lead petitions (Nos. 26-199 and 26-201) to resolve the entrenched circuit split over HISA's enforcement provisions, the likelihood that the Court grants plenary review on Gulf Coast Racing's conditional cross-petition (No. 26-332) or enters a GVR is low (`0.08` total; `0.03` plenary grant, `0.05` GVR, `0.92` denial). Rather than granting No. 26-332 now, the Court is likely to hold No. 26-332 (along with Nos. 26-335, 26-430, and 25-1325) pending merits disposition of Nos. 26-199 and 26-201, and ultimately deny the cross-petition once the merits of the lead cases are resolved.

## 2. Procedural Posture and Litigation History

1. **Enactment and *Horsemen's I*:** Congress enacted HISA in December 2020, recognizing the Horseracing Integrity and Safety Authority—a private, nonprofit Delaware corporation—to develop and implement a national horseracing safety and anti-doping program subject to oversight by the Federal Trade Commission (FTC). In *National Horsemen's Benevolent & Protective Ass'n v. Black*, 53 F.4th 869 (5th Cir. 2022) (*Horsemen's I*), the Fifth Circuit held that HISA's original rulemaking structure violated the private nondelegation doctrine because the FTC lacked authority to modify the Authority's rules. Shortly thereafter, Congress amended 15 U.S.C. § 3053(e) to empower the FTC to "abrogate, add to, and modify" the Authority's rules.
2. **Consolidation and *Horsemen's II*:** On remand, the Northern District of Texas consolidated the original suit (brought by the Horsemen and intervened in by Texas) with a new suit brought by Texas racetracks (Gulf Coast Racing LLC, et al.) that added an Appointments Clause challenge. After a bench trial, the district court upheld HISA as amended in its entirety (672 F. Supp. 3d 220). In July 2024 (*Horsemen's II*, 107 F.4th 415), the Fifth Circuit affirmed in part and reversed in part:
   - It **affirmed** that the 2022 statutory amendment cured the private nondelegation problem with the Authority's **rulemaking** power (agreeing with the Sixth Circuit in *Oklahoma v. United States*, 62 F.4th 221 (6th Cir. 2023), and later the Eighth Circuit in *Walmsley v. FTC*, 117 F.4th 1032 (8th Cir. 2024)).
   - It **reversed** as to HISA's **enforcement** provisions, holding that empowering the Authority to investigate, issue subpoenas, search, levy fines, and bring civil actions for injunctions without front-end FTC control violated the private nondelegation doctrine (splitting with the Sixth and Eighth Circuits).
   - It **affirmed** the rejection of the Horsemen's Due Process challenge.
   - It **affirmed** the rejection of Gulf Coast Racing's **Appointments Clause** challenge, holding that under *Lebron v. National Railroad Passenger Corp.*, 513 U.S. 374 (1995), the Authority is a private entity—not created by special federal law, not created by the government, and not controlled by directors appointed by the federal government—and therefore its directors are not subject to Article II's Appointments Clause.
3. **OT2024 Petitions and GVR:** In October 2024, the FTC (No. 24-429) and the Authority (No. 24-433) petitioned for certiorari on the enforcement issue, and Texas (No. 24-465), the Horsemen (No. 24-472), and Gulf Coast Racing (No. 24-489) filed cross-petitions. On June 30, 2025, following its decision in *FCC v. Consumers' Research*, 606 U.S. 656 (2025), the Supreme Court granted, vacated, and remanded all five petitions (`24-429`, `24-433`, `24-465`, `24-472`, and `24-489`) in a single order for further consideration in light of *Consumers' Research*.
4. **Remand Decision (*Horsemen's III*):** On June 11, 2026, a divided Fifth Circuit panel (163 F.4th 294) reinstated *Horsemen's II* in full, holding that *Consumers' Research* addressed only public nondelegation, advisory private calculations subject to prior agency approval, and the Fifth Circuit's "combination" theory, and therefore did not alter *Horsemen's II*'s conclusions on enforcement, rulemaking, or the Appointments Clause. Chief Judge Elrod dissented as to the enforcement holding, concluding that under the principles of *Consumers' Research*, HISA's enforcement provisions are constitutional because the Authority is subordinate to the FTC's pervasive rulemaking and plenary de novo review.
5. **Current OT2026 Posture:**
   - The Authority (No. 26-199) and the Solicitor General on behalf of the FTC (No. 26-201) filed petitions for certiorari on August 17, 2026, challenging the Fifth Circuit's invalidation of HISA's enforcement provisions.
   - Gulf Coast Racing (No. 26-332, docketed Sept. 11, 2026), Texas (No. 26-335, docketed Sept. 14, 2026), and the Horsemen (No. 26-430, docketed Sept. 30, 2026) filed conditional cross-petitions.
   - Both the Federal Respondents (via a letter brief waiving a full response) and the Authority Respondents (via a consolidated Brief in Opposition filed in Nos. 26-332 and 26-335) oppose the cross-petitions.
   - Nos. 26-332, 26-335, and 26-430 are distributed for the Conference of December 4, 2026.

## 3. Analysis of Certworthiness and Disposition Pathways

### A. Base Rates and Anchor
The 10-term SCOTUS cert grant base rate in `context.json` (`cert_anchor.rate`) is **5.15%** (`0.0515`), with paid petitions distributed once having an empirical grant rate of roughly 9.1% (and conditional cross-petitions facing a substantially lower plenary grant rate when opposed by the Solicitor General and when the questions presented lack a circuit split).

### B. Why Plenary Grant (`granted`) Is Unlikely (~3%)
1. **Absence of Any Circuit Split on Either Question Presented:**
   - **Question 1 (Appointments Clause / *Lebron*):** Every court to consider whether HISA's directors are subject to the Appointments Clause has rejected the claim under *Lebron v. National Railroad Passenger Corp.*, 513 U.S. 374 (1995), and *Department of Transportation v. Association of American Railroads*, 575 U.S. 43 (2015) (*Amtrak II*). Moreover, as the Fifth Circuit noted, every circuit to consider analogous self-regulatory organizations (such as FINRA) or private corporations not created and controlled by the federal government has applied *Lebron*'s three-part test (governmental creation by special law, governmental objectives, and permanent federal control over a majority of directors). There is zero conflict in the circuits.
   - **Question 2 (Private Nondelegation as to Rulemaking / Entire Act):** All three circuits to consider HISA's amended rulemaking provisions—the Fifth Circuit (*Horsemen's II* and *III*), the Sixth Circuit (*Oklahoma v. United States*, 62 F.4th 221), and the Eighth Circuit (*Walmsley v. FTC*, 117 F.4th 1032)—have uniformly held that the 2022 amendment to 15 U.S.C. § 3053(e) giving the FTC power to "abrogate, add to, and modify" any Authority rule cured any private nondelegation problem with rulemaking. And as to enforcement, that question is already squarely presented by the lead petitions in Nos. 26-199 and 26-201, where Gulf Coast Racing is already a respondent in full standing and can defend the Fifth Circuit's judgment on enforcement without needing its own cross-petition granted.
2. **Mutual Exclusivity / Tension Among Challengers and Solicitor General Opposition:**
   - As the Fifth Circuit explicitly observed in *Horsemen's II* (107 F.4th at 437), challenges based on private nondelegation and the Appointments Clause are mutually exclusive: an entity is either governmental (triggering the Appointments Clause) or private (triggering private nondelegation analysis). The Horsemen themselves called Gulf Coast Racing's Appointments Clause theory "fundamentally incompatible" with their private nondelegation challenge.
   - Both the Solicitor General (on behalf of the FTC) and the Authority oppose certiorari in No. 26-332, explaining that expanding the scope of review beyond the enforcement question in Nos. 26-199 and 26-201 would inject splitless, threshold questions that were uniformly rejected below.
3. **Alternative Grounds for Affirmance Do Not Require a Cross-Petition unless Enlarging the Judgment:**
   - Under *Jennings v. Stephens*, 574 U.S. 271, 276 (2015), a respondent may argue any ground in the record that supports the judgment below without filing a cross-petition, whereas seeking to invalidate the *rulemaking* provisions (which the Fifth Circuit upheld) would enlarge the judgment and require a cross-petition. Because all three circuits agree that the amended rulemaking provisions are constitutional—and *FCC v. Consumers' Research*, 606 U.S. 656 (2025), further reinforced the validity of agency oversight under *Sunshine Anthracite Coal Co. v. Adkins*, 310 U.S. 381 (1940)—the Court has virtually no appetite to grant a cross-petition to strike down HISA's rulemaking provisions or to overrule/narrow *Lebron*.
   - Still, there is a small (~3%) chance that four Justices (for instance, Justices Gorsuch, Thomas, and Alito—who dissented in *Consumers' Research* and where Justice Gorsuch noted in a footnote that Appointments Clause questions had been raised by amici regarding USAC—plus one more Justice) vote to grant the cross-petition alongside Nos. 26-199 and 26-201 so that all constitutional questions surrounding HISA's public/private status and rulemaking are on the table at oral argument.

### C. Why a Second GVR (`gvr`) Is Unlikely (~5%)
- In June 2025, the Court GVR'd all five OT2024 HISA petitions (including Gulf Coast Racing's prior cross-petition, No. 24-489) in light of *FCC v. Consumers' Research*. At that time, the Court had not yet granted plenary review of the Fifth Circuit's HISA decision, so vacating the single consolidated Fifth Circuit judgment across all pending petitions and cross-petitions was standard housekeeping.
- Now, in OT2026, the Court will decide the HISA enforcement issue on the merits in Nos. 26-199 and 26-201.
- Once the Court decides Nos. 26-199 and 26-201 on the merits:
  - **If the Court reverses the Fifth Circuit** (holding that HISA's enforcement provisions are constitutional under *Sunshine Anthracite* and *Consumers' Research*, in agreement with the Sixth and Eighth Circuits and Chief Judge Elrod's dissent), the Fifth Circuit's affirmance of the district court's rejection of Gulf Coast Racing's Appointments Clause and rulemaking challenges will only be reinforced. The Court reverses in Nos. 26-199/26-201 and simply **denies** the held cross-petitions (Nos. 26-332, 26-335, 26-430).
  - **If the Court affirms the Fifth Circuit** (holding that HISA's enforcement provisions violate the private nondelegation doctrine), the judgment invalidating HISA's enforcement provisions is affirmed in Nos. 26-199/26-201, and there is still no reason to disturb the Fifth Circuit's rejection of the Appointments Clause or rulemaking challenges. Again, the held cross-petitions are **denied**.
- A GVR of No. 26-332 (~5% probability) would occur only if the Court's merits opinion in Nos. 26-199/26-201 articulates a novel doctrinal test for distinguishing public versus private entities or agency subordination that casts doubt on the Fifth Circuit's *Lebron* or rulemaking analysis, or if the Court chooses as a formal administrative matter to vacate the entire Fifth Circuit judgment across all dockets consolidated below. Standard Supreme Court practice when reversing on a lead petition while rejecting cross-petitions on affirmance grounds is to reverse on the lead petition and deny the cross-petitions.

### D. Conclusion
Weighing the strong likelihood that the Court grants only the lead enforcement petitions (Nos. 26-199 and 26-201), holds the conditional cross-petitions pending the merits decision, and subsequently denies the cross-petitions without disturbing the Fifth Circuit's splitless holdings on the Appointments Clause and rulemaking, I assign a `0.92` probability to `denied`, `0.05` to `gvr`, and `0.03` to `granted`, yielding an overall favorable-outcome `probability` of `0.08` and `predicted_disposition = "denied"`.
