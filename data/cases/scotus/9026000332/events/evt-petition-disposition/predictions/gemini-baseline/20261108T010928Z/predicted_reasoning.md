# Predicted Court Action and Procedural Trajectory: *Gulf Coast Racing LLC, et al. v. Horseracing Integrity and Safety Authority, Inc., et al.* (No. 26-332)

## 1. Distribution and Relist Trajectory (`relist-increment`: `0.45`)
The docket currently shows a single distribution (`distribution_count = 1`), scheduled for the Conference of December 4, 2026, alongside companion cross-petitions *Texas, et al. v. Black, et al.* (No. 26-335) and *National Horsemen's Benevolent & Protective Ass'n, et al. v. HISA, et al.* (No. 26-430). Expect the Court to grant the lead petitions filed by the Authority (No. 26-199) and the Federal Trade Commission (No. 26-201) to resolve the direct circuit split between the Fifth Circuit and the Sixth and Eighth Circuits over whether HISA's enforcement provisions violate the private nondelegation doctrine.

For this conditional cross-petition (No. 26-332), there is a substantial probability (`0.45`) that the petition is distributed at least once more (`relist-increment`). Specifically, the Court may relist the entire cluster of HISA petitions once ahead of a grant order on the lead petitions, or—more likely—hold No. 26-332 (along with Nos. 26-335 and 26-430) pending the merits disposition of Nos. 26-199 and 26-201, which will result in one additional formal distribution for conference when the merits opinion in the lead cases is ready to issue in spring/summer 2027. Conversely, if the Court immediately holds No. 26-332 at the December 4, 2026 Conference without an intermediate relist and disposes of it without recording a separate distribution entry, or denies the cross-petition outright at the time it grants the lead petitions, the distribution count will remain at 1.

## 2. Call for the Views of the Solicitor General (`cvsg-increment`: `0.01`)
No CVSG will issue (`0.01`). The Solicitor General represents the Federal Trade Commission and its Commissioners as respondents in this docket and as petitioners in lead petition No. 26-201, and has already filed a letter response on October 13, 2026, opposing the conditional cross-petitions. Because the United States is already a party on the record, a CVSG is functionally inapplicable.

## 3. Disposition and Summary-Disposition Route (`disposition`: `0.08`; `summary-disposition-route`: `0.625`)
The Court will most likely **deny** this conditional cross-petition (`0.92` probability of denial; `0.08` probability of any grant), either at the time it grants the lead enforcement petitions in Nos. 26-199 and 26-201 or after holding No. 26-332 pending the merits decision in those lead cases.

Conditional on the Court granting No. 26-332 in any form (`0.08`), a summary disposition via **grant, vacate, and remand (GVR)** is the likelier route (`summary-disposition-route = 0.625`, corresponding to an unconditional `0.05` GVR probability versus `0.03` plenary grant). A GVR would occur if the Court holds No. 26-332 for Nos. 26-199 and 26-201 and, after issuing its merits decision on HISA's enforcement provisions, vacates and remands the consolidated Fifth Circuit judgment across all pending dockets for further proceedings consistent with its merits opinion (just as the Court GVR'd all five OT2024 HISA petitions—Nos. 24-429, 24-433, 24-465, 24-472, and 24-489—following *FCC v. Consumers' Research*, 606 U.S. 656 (2025)). However, because the Court's merits resolution of the enforcement question in Nos. 26-199 and 26-201 is unlikely to cast doubt on the Fifth Circuit's splitless holdings rejecting Gulf Coast Racing's Appointments Clause and rulemaking challenges, the standard disposition after holding a protective cross-petition is a routine denial once the lead merits case is decided.

## 4. Questions Presented and Conditional Merits Scope
If the Court were to grant plenary review in No. 26-332 alongside Nos. 26-199 and 26-201, it would likely limit review to Question Presented 1—whether the Horseracing Integrity and Safety Authority is part of the federal government for purposes of the Appointments Clause notwithstanding *Lebron v. National Railroad Passenger Corp.*, 513 U.S. 374 (1995)—so that the Court could consider the threshold public-versus-private status of the Authority alongside the private-nondelegation challenge to its enforcement powers, while leaving behind Question Presented 2's cumulative challenge to HISA's amended rulemaking provisions (which the Fifth, Sixth, and Eighth Circuits have all upheld). If reached on the merits, the Court would likely affirm the Fifth Circuit's holding on Question 1 that under *Lebron*, a privately incorporated entity neither created by special federal statute nor controlled by government-appointed directors is not part of the federal government for Appointments Clause purposes, and that accountability is policed instead through the private nondelegation doctrine and agency oversight under *Sunshine Anthracite Coal Co. v. Adkins*, 310 U.S. 381 (1940), and *FCC v. Consumers' Research*, 606 U.S. 656 (2025).

## 5. Separate Writings on Denial (`dissent-from-denial`: `0.04`)
Should the Court deny the cross-petition, expect no separate writing (`0.96`). Because the core constitutional dispute over HISA's enforcement structure will be heard on the merits in Nos. 26-199 and 26-201, Justices who are skeptical of private delegations or interested in the intersection of the Appointments Clause and private corporations (most plausibly Justice Gorsuch or Justice Thomas, as reflected in Justice Gorsuch's dissenting footnote in *FCC v. Consumers' Research*, 606 U.S. at 721 n.15) will have an opportunity to write in the merits case itself rather than dissenting from the denial of a conditional cross-petition. There remains a small chance (`0.04`) that Justice Gorsuch or Justice Thomas issues a brief statement respecting the denial or dissent from denial highlighting the Appointments Clause question left unreviewed.
