## In brief

Gulf Coast Racing and others ask the Court to decide whether a private horseracing authority's leaders must be appointed under the Constitution's rules for federal officers.

## What happened

Congress created the Horseracing Integrity and Safety Authority, a private nonprofit, to write and enforce nationwide safety and anti-doping rules for thoroughbred racing, subject to Federal Trade Commission oversight. Gulf Coast Racing and other horsemen sued, arguing the arrangement is unconstitutional. After earlier proceedings and a prior trip to the Supreme Court, the Fifth Circuit held that the Authority's rulemaking powers and its appointment structure were constitutional, but that its enforcement powers were not. Gulf Coast Racing, joined by the Authority, the federal government, and other challengers, separately asked the Supreme Court to review the decision.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked whether the people who lead the Authority must be chosen the way the Constitution requires for federal officers, given the significant regulatory power they hold. A related question, which would arise only if the answer is no, is whether letting a private organization hold this much nationwide rulemaking and enforcement power is constitutional at all.

## Where it stands

The petition and other related petitions have been distributed for the Court's conference. Briefing is complete, including opposition briefs from the Authority and the federal government and a reply from Gulf Coast Racing.

## What each outcome would mean

Gulf Coast Racing asks the Court to take up this question. The Authority and the federal government, which lost on the enforcement-powers issue below, instead want the Court to grant their own petitions on that separate question while denying Gulf Coast Racing's. If the Court grants Gulf Coast Racing's petition, it would hear full arguments on whether the Authority's leaders need constitutional appointment; a decision could then uphold or reject the current structure. If the Court denies the petition, the Fifth Circuit's ruling that the Authority's directors do not need such appointment stays in place.
