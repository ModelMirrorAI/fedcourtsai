## In brief

Michael Jackson, sentenced to death after a non-unanimous jury vote, asks the Court to say the Constitution requires unanimity for a death recommendation.

## What happened

Jackson was convicted in 2007 of murdering an elderly couple and was sentenced to death after a jury recommended death by a vote of eight to four. His death sentence was later vacated, and at a 2023 resentencing, conducted under a new Florida law allowing non-unanimous death recommendations, a jury again voted eight to four for death, and the judge again imposed death. The Florida Supreme Court affirmed, rejecting Jackson's arguments that the law violates the federal constitution and various other challenges. Florida's top court denied rehearing.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked whether the Constitution's jury-trial and cruel-and-unusual-punishment protections require that a jury's recommendation of a death sentence be unanimous, or whether a state may let a smaller supermajority of jurors, here eight of twelve, recommend death.

## Where it stands

The petition was distributed for an October 2026 conference, then rescheduled; it was later distributed again for a December 2026 conference and rescheduled once more. The docket shows no action yet on whether the Court will hear the case.

## What each outcome would mean

Jackson, who lost in the Florida Supreme Court, is asking the Court to overturn that ruling. If the Court grants review, it would hear full arguments on whether non-unanimous death recommendations are constitutional, deciding nothing by the grant itself. If it denies review, the Florida Supreme Court's decision upholding Jackson's death sentence stands, leaving Florida's law, which allows death sentences based on an eight-to-four jury vote, in effect for now.
