## In brief

Michael Jackson, sentenced to death in Florida by a non-unanimous 8-4 jury vote, asks the Court to say that vote violated his constitutional rights.

## What happened

Jackson was convicted of murdering an elderly couple in 2005 and originally sentenced to death. His sentence was vacated after a ruling required unanimous jury recommendations for death, but by the time he was resentenced in 2023, Florida had changed its law to allow a sentence of death based on only eight of twelve jurors agreeing. His new jury again voted 8-4 for death, and the judge imposed that sentence. The Florida Supreme Court affirmed, rejecting Jackson's constitutional challenges to the state's non-unanimous sentencing law, and later denied rehearing.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked whether the right to a jury trial, or the ban on cruel and unusual punishment, requires that a jury's recommendation of a death sentence be unanimous, or whether a state may allow a death sentence based on a supermajority of jurors rather than all twelve agreeing.

## Where it stands

The petition has been fully briefed, including a brief opposing review, amicus briefs, and a reply. It was distributed for conference in September and again in December after being rescheduled twice; the docket does not yet show a ruling on whether the Court will hear the case.

## What each outcome would mean

Jackson, who lost in the Florida Supreme Court, is asking the Court to take up his case and reverse that ruling. If the Court grants review, it would hear full arguments on whether non-unanimous jury death recommendations are constitutional, deciding nothing about who wins until it rules. If the Court denies review, the Florida Supreme Court's decision upholding Jackson's death sentence stands, though denial would not mean the Court agrees that decision was correct.
