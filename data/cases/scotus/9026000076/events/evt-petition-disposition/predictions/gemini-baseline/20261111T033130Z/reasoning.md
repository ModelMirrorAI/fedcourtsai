## III. Adjustments from the 5.16% Anchor to P(Grant) = 0.14

### Upward Adjustments (+14% to +18% over the 5.16% anchor before doctrinal discount)
- **Advocate quality and amicus coalition:** Petitioner is represented by Jeffrey L. Fisher (Stanford Supreme Court Litigation Clinic) and the ACLU Capital Punishment Project, supported by three high-profile amicus briefs (including WilmerHale, Debevoise, and Gibson Dunn). A paid-segment petition with 3 amicus briefs (`2+` bucket = 22.7% marginal grant rate) and elite repeat-player counsel is in the top decile of baseline-band petitions.
- **Clean first-impression statutory vehicle:** *Jackson* (along with *Hunt*) is the first direct-appeal test of Florida's 2023 statute (Fla. Stat. § 921.141, amended by SB 450), which reduced the threshold for a capital sentencing recommendation from unanimity to 8–4. The jury voted 8–4 on both counts, making the nonunanimity rule strictly outcome-determinative under the statute, and the federal Sixth and Eighth Amendment questions were preserved and squarely decided below.

### Downward Adjustments (bringing final probability to 0.14)
- **Doctrinal headwinds under *McKinney v. Arizona* and *Hurst v. Florida*:** In *McKinney*, 589 U.S. 139, 144–45 (2020), a 5–4 majority written by Justice Kavanaugh held that while a jury must find the aggravating circumstance that makes a defendant death-eligible, "a jury (as opposed to a judge) is not constitutionally required to weigh the aggravating and mitigating circumstances or to make the ultimate sentencing decision within the relevant sentencing range." In Jackson's 2023 resentencing, the jury **unanimously** found all eight statutory aggravating factors beyond a reasonable doubt before making its 8–4 sentencing recommendation. Because five members of the *McKinney* majority (Roberts, Thomas, Alito, Gorsuch, Kavanaugh) plus Justice Barrett remain on the Court, there is little prospect that four Justices from the majority want to revisit *McKinney* or *Spaziano v. Florida*, 468 U.S. 447 (1984), to extend *Ramos v. Louisiana*, 590 U.S. 83 (2020), from elements/eligibility to capital sentencing selection.
- **Absence of a live split among active death-penalty states:** Although Petitioner cites *Rauf v. State*, 145 A.3d 430 (Del. 2016), and *State v. Daniels*, 542 A.2d 306 (Conn. 1988), as conflicting with Florida (*Jackson*, *Poole*) and Alabama (*Ex parte Bohannon*, 222 So. 3d 525 (Ala. 2016)), both *Rauf* and *Daniels* predate *McKinney* (2020), and neither Delaware nor Connecticut has an active death penalty. The Court has repeatedly denied certiorari in post-*Poole* and post-*McKinney* petitions raising closely related Sixth and Eighth Amendment challenges to Florida and Alabama capital sentencing (*Poole v. Florida*, 141 S. Ct. 1051 (2021); *Capote v. Alabama*, 141 S. Ct. 2518 (2021); *McKenzie v. Florida*, 143 S. Ct. 230 (2022)).
- **Aggravated facts:** The underlying crime—kidnapping a frail elderly couple and burying them alive to extort their bank PINs, with eight unanimous statutory aggravators—makes this a difficult vehicle for persuading moderate-conservative Justices to reach out and invalidate a state statute.

Netting these factors, I place `probability` (`disposition`) at **0.14**, `granted: 0`, and `predicted_disposition: "denied"`.
