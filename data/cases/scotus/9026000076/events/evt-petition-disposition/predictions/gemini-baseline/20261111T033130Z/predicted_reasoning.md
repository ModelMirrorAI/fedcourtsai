## II. Disposition and Summary-Disposition Route

1. **Disposition (`disposition`: 0.14; `predicted_disposition`: `denied`):**
   The Court will likely deny the petition for a writ of certiorari after conferencing it together with *Hunt v. Florida* (No. 26-78). A majority of the Court adheres to *McKinney v. Arizona*, 589 U.S. 139, 144–45 (2020), and *Hurst v. Florida*, 577 U.S. 92 (2016), under which the Sixth Amendment requires a jury to unanimously find the existence of an aggravating factor that renders a defendant eligible for the death penalty, but does not require a jury (or jury unanimity) for the weighing of aggravating and mitigating factors or the ultimate selection of a death sentence. Here, the resentencing jury unanimously found eight statutory aggravating factors beyond a reasonable doubt. Furthermore, the state-court split cited by Petitioner involves two jurisdictions (Connecticut and Delaware) that have abolished the death penalty and whose decisions predate *McKinney*.

2. **Question Presented and Conditional Merits Ground (if granted):**
   If the Court were to grant certiorari, it would take the single question presented as written—whether the Sixth or Eighth Amendment requires a jury determination that a defendant should be sentenced to death under Fla. Stat. § 921.141(2)(c) to be unanimous rather than made by an 8–4 vote—potentially consolidating or holding *Hunt v. Florida* (No. 26-78). If granted and decided on the merits, the likely ground for affirmance would be that under *McKinney*, *Ring*, and *Hurst*, the Sixth Amendment jury-trial right attaches only to the factual finding of a statutory aggravating circumstance that increases the maximum authorized punishment from life imprisonment to death, whereas the ultimate normative recommendation between life and death is a sentencing-selection judgment not governed by *Apprendi* or *Ramos v. Louisiana*, 590 U.S. 83 (2020). Conversely, if the Court granted to reverse, it would likely rest on the Sixth Amendment rule of *Andres v. United States*, 333 U.S. 740 (1948), and *Ramos*, holding that when a statute conditions a capital sentence on an affirmative jury recommendation, all statutory prerequisites entrusted to the jury must be found unanimously.

3. **Summary Disposition Route (`summary-disposition-route`: 0.05 conditional on grant):**
   Conditional on a grant, summary disposition (a GVR or summary reversal) is very unlikely (5%). The Florida Supreme Court squarely addressed *Hurst*, *McKinney*, *Ramos*, and *Spaziano*, no intervening decision of this Court alters that framework, and the Court would not summarily invalidate a state capital sentencing statute without plenary briefing and oral argument.
