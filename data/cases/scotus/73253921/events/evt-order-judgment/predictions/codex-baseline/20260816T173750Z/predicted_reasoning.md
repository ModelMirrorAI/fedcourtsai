# Forecast

The Court will reverse the Fourth Circuit, likely 5-4. Justices Sotomayor, Kagan, Gorsuch, Barrett, and Jackson will vote to disturb the judgment; Chief Justice Roberts and Justices Thomas, Alito, and Kavanaugh will dissent.

The majority will hold that an action under 28 U.S.C. § 2241 challenging civil immigration detention falls within EAJA's phrase "any civil action." It will rely on habeas's longstanding classification as a civil proceeding, the concededly civil nature of immigration detention, the breadth supplied by "any," and Congress's decision to identify tort cases—but not habeas cases—as the express exception. On that reading the statutory text is unambiguous, so the canon requiring waivers of sovereign immunity to be construed narrowly will have no residual ambiguity to resolve.

The holding will be categorical for immigration-detention habeas but expressly reserve whether habeas arising from criminal custody is covered. The Court will distinguish *Schlanger v. Seamans* as a context-specific decision about the reach of a venue provision and habeas's special procedural rules, not a definition that removes habeas from the category of civil actions. It will remand for consideration of the remaining EAJA requirements, including substantial justification and the fee amount.

A dissent will treat habeas as a sui generis proceeding that does not fall unequivocally within "civil action" and will invoke the sovereign-immunity clear-statement rule. No DIG or equally divided disposition is expected: the preserved fee dispute is live, the lower judgment rests entirely on the statutory question, and both parties regard the case as a clean vehicle.
