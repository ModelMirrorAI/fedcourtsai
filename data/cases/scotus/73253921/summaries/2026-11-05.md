## In brief

A noncitizen who won his immigration detention habeas case asks the Court to decide whether he may recover attorney's fees from the government under a federal fee-shifting law.

## What happened

Kevin Isaac Montoya Palacios, held by immigration officials after years on supervised release, won a federal habeas case ordering his release, based on his argument that his detention had become unlawfully prolonged. After the government initially failed to release him and nearly deported him, a court again ordered his release. He then sought attorney's fees under the Equal Access to Justice Act. The district court denied fees, citing Fourth Circuit precedent that a habeas case is not a "civil action" eligible for such fees, and the Fourth Circuit summarily affirmed. Other circuits have reached the opposite conclusion.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked whether a law letting prevailing parties recover attorney's fees in lawsuits against the federal government also applies to habeas cases brought by immigration detainees challenging their detention, or whether such cases fall outside that law's reach.

## Where it stands

The Court granted review on June 29, 2026. The case has been fully briefed and is scheduled for argument on January 11, 2027.

## What each outcome would mean

Montoya Palacios, who is appealing the fee denial, is asking the Court to reverse the lower court's ruling that he cannot recover fees. If the Court rules for him, he and similarly situated detainees who win their habeas cases could recover attorney's fees from the government, resolving a split among federal appeals courts. If the Court rules for the government, the lower court's denial of fees would stand, meaning habeas challenges to immigration detention remain ineligible for fee awards in the circuits that have reached that conclusion. The federal government, as respondent, has told the Court it agrees the question should be decided, while defending the lower court's reasoning on the merits.
