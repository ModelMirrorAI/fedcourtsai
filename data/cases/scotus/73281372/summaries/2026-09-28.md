## In brief

Liquidators for Madoff-linked investment funds ask the Court to revive foreign investors' claims against banks that the lower courts said were barred by a bankruptcy law safe harbor.

## What happened

Fairfield Sentry and related funds, organized in the British Virgin Islands, invested with Bernard Madoff and collapsed when his Ponzi scheme was exposed. Their liquidators sued banks and other entities that had redeemed fund shares before the fraud came to light, seeking to recover the payments under BVI law. The liquidators used Chapter 15 of the Bankruptcy Code, which lets foreign representatives seek help from American courts. The bankruptcy court, district court, and the Second Circuit Court of Appeals all ruled against the liquidators, holding that a bankruptcy law "safe harbor" protecting certain securities payments barred their claims, including claims based on foreign common law.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked whether a bankruptcy law provision that shields certain securities-related payments from being clawed back applies to foreign liquidators pursuing claims under foreign law over conduct abroad, and whether that shield blocks not just claims based on statutes but also claims based on common law.

## Where it stands

The petition has been fully briefed, including an opposition brief and a reply, and the docket shows it was distributed for the Court's conference on September 28, 2026. No decision on whether to hear the case has yet been recorded.

## What each outcome would mean

Fairfield Sentry and the other liquidators, as petitioners, asked the Court to take the case. If the Court grants the petition, it will hear full arguments and decide whether the safe harbor applies here; a grant alone would not decide who wins. If the Court denies the petition, the Second Circuit's ruling stands, leaving in place the dismissal of the liquidators' claims against Citibank, HSBC, and other banks and investors. A denial would not mean the Court agrees the lower court was right; it simply leaves that decision undisturbed.
