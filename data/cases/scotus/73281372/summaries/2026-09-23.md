## What happened

Fairfield Sentry and related funds, investment vehicles based in the British Virgin Islands, lost billions of dollars in Bernard Madoff's Ponzi scheme. Their liquidators sued in United States courts to recover redemption payments that some investors received before the fraud came to light, relying on foreign liquidation proceedings recognized under Chapter 15 of the Bankruptcy Code, which lets foreign representatives seek help from American courts. The bankruptcy court and district court rejected most of these claims, and the Second Circuit Court of Appeals ruled that a Bankruptcy Code "safe harbor" protecting certain securities-related payments barred all of the liquidators' remaining claims, including those based on foreign common law. The liquidators asked the Supreme Court to review that ruling; the banks and other defendants oppose review.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked whether a rule that normally shields certain securities payments from being clawed back in domestic bankruptcy cases can also block foreign liquidators from pursuing separate claims under foreign law, in a proceeding that exists to help foreign bankruptcies get assistance from American courts. A related question is whether that same rule can block claims based on foreign common law, not just claims based on specific statutes. The liquidators argue the rule was wrongly stretched beyond its intended reach and conflicts with how other appeals courts have treated similar claims; the opposing parties argue there is no real disagreement among courts and that the appeals court read the law correctly.

## Where it stands

The petition was filed in March 2026 after two rounds of deadline extensions, both sides have now filed their main briefs along with amicus briefs supporting the liquidators, and the case was distributed for the Court's conference on September 28, 2026, where the Justices will decide whether to take up the case.
