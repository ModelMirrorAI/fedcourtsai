## In brief

Liquidators for Madoff-linked investment funds asked the Supreme Court to revive foreign claims against banks that a federal appeals court had barred; the Court declined to hear the case.

## What happened

Fairfield Sentry and related funds, based in the British Virgin Islands, invested heavily with Bernard Madoff and collapsed into liquidation when his Ponzi scheme was exposed. Their liquidators sued banks and investors in American courts, using a bankruptcy tool called Chapter 15, to recover over $6 billion in redemption payments made before the fraud came to light. The United States Court of Appeals for the Second Circuit ruled that a Bankruptcy Code "safe harbor" for securities transactions barred all of these claims, reversing a lower ruling that had allowed some claims to proceed.

## What the Court is being asked

The liquidators asked the Court to decide whether a bankruptcy law provision that shields certain securities-related payments from being clawed back, written for ordinary domestic bankruptcies, also applies to block foreign liquidators from pursuing claims under foreign law over foreign transactions. They argued that doing so improperly stretched an American legal protection beyond the country's borders and conflicted with rulings from other appeals courts.

## Where it stands

The petition was distributed for the Court's conference on September 28, 2026, and on October 5, 2026, the Court denied it; Justice Alito did not take part in the decision.

## What each outcome would mean

Fairfield Sentry and the other liquidators asked the Court to take up the case and reverse the Second Circuit's ruling. The banks and other investors, including Citibank and HSBC entities, opposed review and defended the lower court's decision. By denying the petition, the Court leaves the Second Circuit's ruling in place, meaning the liquidators' claims against the banks and investors remain barred by the securities safe harbor. The denial does not mean the Court agreed with the lower court's reasoning, only that the case will not be reviewed.
