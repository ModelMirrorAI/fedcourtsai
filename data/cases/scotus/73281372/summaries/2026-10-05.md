## In brief

The Court declined to take up foreign liquidators' bid to revive billion-dollar claims against banks over Madoff-linked redemption payments.

## What happened

Fairfield Sentry and related investment funds, based in the British Virgin Islands, lost heavily when Bernard Madoff's fraud collapsed in 2008. Their liquidators sued banks and investors in American courts, using Chapter 15 bankruptcy proceedings, to claw back over $6 billion in redemption payments made to investors before the fraud was discovered. The bankruptcy court, district court, and the Second Circuit Court of Appeals all ruled against the liquidators, holding that a bankruptcy law safe harbor for securities-related payments barred these claims, including those based on foreign law.

## What the Court is being asked

The liquidators ask the Court to decide whether a bankruptcy safe harbor that shields certain securities payments from being clawed back can be used to block foreign liquidators from pursuing claims under foreign law over foreign transactions, and whether that shield also stops common-law claims, not just claims based on specific bankruptcy statutes. The banks respond that no other court has disagreed with the Second Circuit's approach and that the case is a poor vehicle for deciding these issues.

## Where it stands

The petition was distributed for the Court's conference on September 28, 2026. The Court denied the petition on October 5, 2026, with Justice Alito not taking part.

## What each outcome would mean

Fairfield Sentry and the other liquidators asked the Court to hear the case. The denial leaves the Second Circuit's ruling in place, meaning the liquidators' claims against Citibank, HSBC, and other banks and investors remain barred, and the banks' win below stands. Had the Court instead granted the petition, it would have heard full arguments on whether the safe harbor could reach foreign claims this way, without yet deciding who was right.
