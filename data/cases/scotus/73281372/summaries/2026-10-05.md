## In brief

The Court declined to hear liquidators' bid to revive billions of dollars in claims against banks that received redemption payments before the Madoff fraud collapsed.

## What happened

Fairfield Sentry and related investment funds, based in the British Virgin Islands, invested heavily with Bernard Madoff and collapsed when his Ponzi scheme was exposed. Their liquidators sued banks and other investors in U.S. courts, using Chapter 15 of the Bankruptcy Code, to claw back over $6 billion in redemption payments made before the fraud came to light. The bankruptcy court and district court dismissed most claims, and the Second Circuit affirmed, ruling that a Bankruptcy Code safe harbor for securities transactions barred both the foreign statutory and common-law claims the liquidators were pursuing.

## What the Court is being asked

The case asks whether a bankruptcy-law shield that protects certain securities payments from being unwound applies to foreign liquidators pursuing claims under foreign law in these cross-border cases, and whether that shield blocks not just claims based on foreign statutes but also claims based on foreign common law. The liquidators argued the lower court's reading improperly stretched the shield beyond its intended domestic reach and conflicted with how other courts had treated similar claims.

## Where it stands

The petition was distributed for the Court's conference on September 28, 2026, and on October 5, 2026, the Court denied the petition. Justice Alito did not take part in the decision.

## What each outcome would mean

Fairfield Sentry and the other liquidators asked the Court to take up the case and reverse the Second Circuit's ruling, which favored Citibank, HSBC, and other banks and investors that received the disputed payments. By denying the petition, the Court leaves the Second Circuit's decision in place, meaning the liquidators' claims seeking to recover the redemption payments for Madoff-fraud victims remain dismissed. The denial does not mean the Court agreed with the lower court's reasoning, only that the case will not receive further review.
