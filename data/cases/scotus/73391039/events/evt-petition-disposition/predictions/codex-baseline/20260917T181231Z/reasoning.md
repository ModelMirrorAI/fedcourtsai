## Why 0.6%, below the anchor

The questions presented ask whether Rule 60(b)(4) requires vacatur of a judgment deciding matters outside the pleadings and prevents law of the case from avoiding that inquiry. The strongest feature is a potentially general procedural issue: a genuinely void judgment and an ordinary mistaken decision are not interchangeable, and the interaction with an earlier appellate mandate can matter beyond this litigant.

The particular showing is much weaker than that abstract framing. The petition, printed pages 2–10 and 15–17, argues that earlier courts confused a claim about toucans and bird sales with an aviary dispute and failed to recognize a later accrual date. Deciding whether that actually occurred requires examining the pleadings, the earlier litigation, and the scope of preclusion. It therefore looks substantially like a disagreement with the application of settled doctrines, notwithstanding the petition's jurisdictional terminology.

The alleged split is also unpersuasive on the material supplied. Pages 20–21 cite other circuits for mandatory relief once a judgment is void; they do not demonstrate opposing holdings on the precise question whether an earlier appellate decision necessarily resolved the asserted defect. The petition itself, at pages 11–12, says the conflict is with this Court's precedent rather than circuit versus circuit. I do not treat the general propositions or the petition's assertion of unanimity as verification of a square, entrenched split.

I checked the actual Rule 60(b)(4) discussion in United Student Aid Funds, Inc. v. Espinosa, 559 U.S. 260, 270–72 (2010), through CourtListener MCP, opinion 1727. It confines voidness relief to exceptional jurisdictional defects or due-process failures depriving a party of notice or an opportunity to be heard, and distinguishes voidness from mere error. My inference is that the petition needs to establish that threshold before its authorities requiring relief from a void judgment can carry the case. Multiple adverse rulings do not establish that the litigants lacked an opportunity to be heard, and a disputed description of their claim does not automatically establish jurisdictional nullity. This is an assessment of vehicle quality, not a definitive merits ruling without the appendices.

The petition describes the decision under review as unreported and reports appellate sanctions for a frivolous appeal. I treat those descriptions as further indications of a case-bound repeat-litigation posture, not proof that the petitioners are wrong. Together with the lack of a demonstrated exact conflict, the waivers and absence of a response request, these features justify reducing the 5.12% anchor substantially. I retain 0.6%, rather than zero, because the claimed failure to consider a distinct voidness objection could be more substantial than this one-sided record establishes.
