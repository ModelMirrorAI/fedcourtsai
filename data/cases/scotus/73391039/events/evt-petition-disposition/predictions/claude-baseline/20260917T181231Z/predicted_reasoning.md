# Forecast: what the Court will do with No. 25-1308, Foley v. Orange County

**Disposition.** The petition will be denied at or shortly after the September 28, 2026 conference for which it was distributed on July 1, 2026, with the order issuing on the October 5, 2026 order list. P(any grant) = 0.005.

**Relists.** The docket shows exactly one distribution (for the 9/28/2026 long conference) and no relist. I expect no further distribution: a paid pro se petition on which every respondent has waived a response, arising from an unreported Eleventh Circuit affirmance that also imposed Rule 38 sanctions, is the profile the Court disposes of on first consideration. The residual 8% covers a reschedule off the long conference (which the corpus counts as an added distribution entry) rather than a substantive relist.

**CVSG.** None. No federal party, no federal program, no statute the Solicitor General administers; the question is the interplay of Rule 60(b)(4) and the law-of-the-case doctrine in a suit against a Florida county and its officers. P = 0.005.

**Which question, if any.** If the Court took anything, it would not be the question as written. The petition's QP folds a contested premise (that a judgment "outside the issues pled" is void under Reynolds v. Stockton) into the question; the only version with a plausible appellate life is a narrowed one: whether a court may deny a Rule 60(b)(4) motion on law-of-the-case grounds without deciding voidness. The Court will not reach it here because the panel below did make a voidness-adjacent determination (it held the prior panel had "necessarily determined" the property-interest identity), so the petition attacks the application of the doctrine rather than a categorical rule.

**Summary disposition versus plenary review.** Conditional on a grant, the likelier route is a summary one, most plausibly a vacatur aimed at the Rule 38 sanction rather than the merits, with P(cert-order disposition | grant) = 0.55. A plenary grant of a pro se paid petition presenting a fact-bound Rule 60(b)(4) dispute is close to inconceivable, and no intervening decision of this Court exists to drive a GVR.

**Dissent from denial.** None expected. No Justice has an announced interest in the void-judgment doctrine's reach, and nothing in this record invites a statement respecting denial. P = 0.01.
