## In brief

The Court declined to hear a Florida couple's bid to void a years-old judgment they said wrongly addressed a claim they never brought.

## What happened

David and Jennifer Foley sued Orange County, Florida, and various officials over a dispute involving their toucan-breeding business and a permit enforcement action. A federal district court dismissed their complaint on grounds that it duplicated earlier litigation, and the Eleventh Circuit affirmed. The Foleys then asked the district court to void that judgment, arguing it had actually decided a different claim than the one they pleaded. The district court denied that request, and the Eleventh Circuit affirmed again, relying on its earlier ruling in the case to reject the challenge. The Eleventh Circuit also ordered the Foleys to pay the other side's attorney's fees for pursuing the appeal.

## What the Court is being asked

The Foleys ask the Court to decide whether a rule allowing judges to void judgments that go beyond the claims a plaintiff actually raised can be sidestepped by a court's reliance on its own earlier ruling in the same case. They argue that once a prior ruling is challenged as having decided the wrong issue entirely, courts must examine that question directly rather than treating the earlier ruling as settling it automatically.

## Where it stands

The petition was distributed for the Court's conference on September 28, 2026, after the opposing parties waived their right to respond. The Court denied the petition on October 5, 2026.

## What each outcome would mean

The Foleys asked the Court to hear their case. With the petition denied, the Eleventh Circuit's ruling against the Foleys stands: the original dismissal of their lawsuit remains in place, their later motion to void that judgment stays denied, and the fee award against them remains in effect. The denial does not mean the Court agreed with the lower court's reasoning, only that the case will not be reviewed further.
