## In brief

The Foleys ask the Court to revive their long-running lawsuit against Orange County by reopening a judgment they say decided the wrong claim entirely.

## What happened

David and Jennifer Foley, representing themselves, sued Orange County, Florida, and various officials over disputes stretching back years involving their toucan-breeding business and a related permitting and enforcement dispute. A federal district court dismissed their complaint on the ground that it duplicated earlier state-court litigation, and the Eleventh Circuit affirmed. The Foleys then asked the district court to void that judgment, arguing it had ruled on a different claim than the one they actually filed. The district court denied that request, and the Eleventh Circuit affirmed again, relying on the earlier appellate ruling as settled for purposes of the new motion. The Eleventh Circuit also imposed sanctions against the Foleys for pursuing the appeal.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked whether a rule requiring judges to set aside judgments that rule on issues never actually raised in the case bars a court from instead relying on an earlier ruling in the same case to avoid deciding that question. In other words, can a court sidestep checking whether it ruled on the right claim by simply pointing to what an earlier court in the same litigation already assumed.

## What each outcome would mean

The Foleys, as petitioners, are asking the Court to take up their case. If the Court grants the petition, it would agree to hear the case and decide the legal question itself, without yet deciding who is right. If it denies the petition, the Eleventh Circuit's ruling against the Foleys stands, leaving the earlier dismissal and the sanctions award in place.
