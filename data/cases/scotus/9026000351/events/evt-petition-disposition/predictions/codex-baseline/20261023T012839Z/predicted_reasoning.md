# Forecast

The Court will deny certiorari without a separate writing, most likely on the orders list following the November 6, 2026 conference. The snapshot records one distribution, dated October 21, and therefore no relist yet. I predict zero additional distributions and no call for the Solicitor General's views. The United States has already waived its response; the Court will most likely dispose of the petition without requesting one.

The jurisdictional posture prevents this petition from being an attractive vehicle for resolving the constitutional limits of judicial discipline. The Federal Circuit summarily affirmed a dismissal for lack of Court of Federal Claims jurisdiction, not a judgment sustaining the judicial-discipline statute on its merits. The petition's recusal contention does not by itself supply the missing money-mandating claim.

If review is granted, the Court will most likely take the third question, concerning the Tucker Act's jurisdictional scope, potentially together with the second question's recusal issue. It is less likely to reach the broad first question about the constitutionality of 28 U.S.C. § 354(a)(2)(A)(i). Conditional on any grant, plenary review is more likely than a summary disposition: I assign the latter a 15% probability. The supplied record identifies no intervening decision that would make a GVR the natural route. Conditional on denial, I assign only a 0.5% probability to a noted dissent or statement respecting denial and predict no separate writing from any Justice. No individual certiorari votes are forecast.
