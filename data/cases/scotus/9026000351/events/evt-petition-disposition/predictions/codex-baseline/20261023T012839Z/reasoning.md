## Anchor and adjustments

I read the committed statpack's modern discretionary-cert, originating-circuit, paid-segment relist, CVSG, and sal-v4 band sections. Pooling every displayed Term strictly before 2026 (2017–2025), the baseline band's reached risk sets contain 644 grant-family outcomes over 12,883 weighted resolved petitions, or 4.9988%. I computed that ratio from metrics/statpack.json's prefix_est_grant_rate and prefix_weighted_resolved fields, not from rounded markdown percentages. This is the applicable class/band anchor, rather than baseline's terminal-band rate. No live corpus was queried or refreshed: these figures describe the committed statpack, not current corpus state, and I did not independently establish its corpus-wide pull vintage.

The paid-segment terminal relist cuts show much higher grant rates among repeatedly distributed petitions than among petitions ending without relists; the CVSG cut is also strongly selected. Neither supplies the forward probability of an additional distribution or a new CVSG. I use their shape, not their terminal rates, for the incremental claims. The Federal Circuit aggregate mixes many subjects and does not outweigh this case's poor vehicle.

The strongest downward adjustment is jurisdictional. The supplied appendix reproduces a nonprecedential May 22, 2026 Federal Circuit order affirming dismissal because petitioner seeks no money-mandating relief. It states that petitioner acknowledged the absence of such claims, cites United States v. Navajo Nation, 556 U.S. 287, 290 (2009), and treats the governing rule as settled. The Court of Federal Claims order likewise identifies only nonmonetary challenges to council composition and disciplinary authority. Thus the broad constitutional question is not reached by the judgment under review. Petitioner's demand to reject longstanding Tucker Act precedent does not establish a conflict among courts.

The recusal question is a potential independent interest, but the panel expressly addressed it: the named defendant is the United States, and council membership does not, in its view, reasonably call impartiality into question when deciding this jurisdictional issue. The petition argues otherwise without identifying a developed inter-circuit conflict on this precise posture. Its constitutional argument relies extensively on constitutional structure and historical sources; importance of the subject is not evidence that this vehicle will obtain review.

The government's October 16 waiver and the absence of an observable response request further support routine denial. A waiver is not an adjudication of merit, and one distribution is not a relist. I retain a small positive grant probability for unusual institutional attention or a procedural correction. The 2% additional-distribution and 0.1% CVSG forecasts are judgmental, not estimated hazards. A CVSG is particularly unlikely when the United States is already the respondent. The conditional summary-route probability leaves room for a procedural remand while favoring plenary consideration if the Court undertakes the broader questions.
