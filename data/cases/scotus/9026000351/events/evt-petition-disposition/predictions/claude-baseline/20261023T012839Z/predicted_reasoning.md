# Predicted course — Dreiling v. United States, No. 26-351

**Disposition: denied**, most likely on the order list after the November 6, 2026
conference. That is the first distribution, and it came after the United States
waived its right to respond.

- **Relists:** the docket shows one distribution (for 11/6/2026) and no relist
  so far. I expect no further distribution. The Court will not call for a
  response, and without a response it has no reason to put the petition on a
  second conference. P(another distribution) ≈ 2%.
- **CVSG:** none. The United States is the respondent, so the Solicitor General
  is already a party and a CVSG is structurally inapt.
- **Question presented, if granted:** the only question with any traction is
  QP 3, whether the Court of Federal Claims has Tucker Act jurisdiction over a
  non-money-mandating claim. The Federal Circuit decided it summarily under
  *Navajo Nation*, so a grant is not realistic. QP 1 (the constitutionality of
  28 U.S.C. § 354(a)(2)(A)(i), which governs the Judicial Council's suspension
  of a judge's case assignments) and QP 2 (§ 455 recusal of Judicial Council
  members) do not fit this jurisdictional posture.
- **Summary route:** in the very unlikely event of a grant, the one realistic
  shape is a summary disposition, not plenary review: a GVR or a per curiam
  correction of a jurisdictional point. I put that at about 25% of the
  conditional mass. Plenary review stays the larger share.
- **Dissent from denial:** I expect no separate writing. Judge Newman's
  suspension has drawn attention elsewhere, but this pro se Tucker Act vehicle
  does not present it cleanly. P ≈ 1%.
