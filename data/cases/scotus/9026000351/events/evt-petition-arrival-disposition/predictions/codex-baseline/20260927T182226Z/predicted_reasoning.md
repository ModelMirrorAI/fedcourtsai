# Court forecast

The Court will deny the petition for certiorari, leaving the Federal Circuit's disposition in place without deciding the constitutionality of the judicial-discipline statute. P(any grant), including a partial grant, GVR, or summary reversal, is **0.8%**.

The arrival record shows **zero distributions and no CVSG**. I expect a first conference distribution after the October 16, 2026 response deadline, followed by denial without a further relist. My central timing forecast is November–December 2026, allowing for extensions; timing is not a scored claim. The **97%** distribution-increment probability measures at least one distribution beyond today's zero, not a further conference after initial consideration.

I expect **no CVSG**; the probability of a new call is **0.1%**. The United States' position will be available through the ordinary respondent process rather than a separate invitation for views.

If the Court grants, it is more likely to take a narrowed version of the second question—whether judicial-council members must recuse from this collateral challenge—than to reach the full first-question attack on § 354. The Tucker Act jurisdictional issue would remain an obstacle to a decision on judicial discipline. I do not forecast adoption of the petition's theory that its invocation of the Tucker Act alone establishes jurisdiction.

Conditional on a grant, **plenary consideration is the likelier route (75%)**, compared with a **25%** chance of disposition in the cert order itself. The most plausible summary path would concern recusal and reconsideration below, not an identified intervening-decision GVR. Conditional on denial, there is a **1.5%** probability of a noted dissent or statement respecting denial; I expect none and predict no author or vote lineup.
