## In brief

The Supreme Court declined to hear Justin Dreiling's challenge to a Federal Circuit ruling that dismissed his lawsuit against the judicial council that restricted a judge's participation in his case.

## What happened

Dreiling, representing himself, sued the United States in the Court of Federal Claims, arguing that a law letting the Federal Circuit's Judicial Council temporarily bar a judge from hearing cases is unconstitutional, and that this had improperly kept Judge Pauline Newman from voting on one of his appeals. The Court of Federal Claims dismissed his case, finding it lacked jurisdiction because his claims did not seek money from the government, which that court generally requires. The Federal Circuit summarily affirmed, rejecting his arguments that the panel should have stepped aside and that the lower court's jurisdiction was wrongly limited. Dreiling then asked the Supreme Court to review that outcome.

## What the Court is being asked

Dreiling asked the Court to decide three things: whether the law letting a judicial council restrict a judge from hearing cases is constitutional; whether judges on a panel deciding an appeal against that council must step aside because they also sit on the council; and whether his underlying claims fit within the specialized court's limited power to hear only claims seeking money from the government.

## Where it stands

The United States waived its right to respond to the petition. The case was distributed for the Court's November 6, 2026 conference, and on November 9, 2026, the Court denied the petition.

## What each outcome would mean

Dreiling, who brought the case, asked the Court to overturn the Federal Circuit's decision against him. The denial leaves that decision in place: the Court of Federal Claims' dismissal of his lawsuit, and the Federal Circuit's summary affirmance of that dismissal, stand as the final word in his case. The denial does not mean the Supreme Court agreed with the lower courts' reasoning; it simply ends his attempt to have the Supreme Court review it.
