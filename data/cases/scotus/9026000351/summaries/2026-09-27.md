## What happened

Justin P. Dreiling, an Army sergeant, sued the United States in the Court of Federal Claims over the Army's vaccination requirement for his son to play Army-sponsored sports. That court dismissed the case, and Dreiling appealed to the Federal Circuit. Judge Pauline Newman did not take part in ruling on his request for a full-court rehearing because the Federal Circuit's Judicial Council had barred her from hearing cases. Dreiling then filed a new suit arguing that order was unconstitutional and that the judges reviewing his appeal should have stepped aside because his claims targeted the Judicial Council itself, of which they are members. The Court of Federal Claims dismissed this suit too, and the Federal Circuit affirmed without written opinion. Dreiling, representing himself, now asks the Supreme Court to review that outcome.

## What the Court is being asked

The case raises three issues: whether the federal law letting a judicial council temporarily stop assigning cases to a judge is constitutional, given that only Congress can impeach and discipline judges; whether judges who are themselves members of a judicial council being sued must recuse from an appeal challenging that council's actions, even though the government is the named defendant; and whether Dreiling's underlying claims fit within the law that lets the Court of Federal Claims hear certain money-related claims against the government.
