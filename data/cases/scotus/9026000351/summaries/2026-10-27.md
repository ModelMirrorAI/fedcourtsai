## In brief

A military servicemember asks the Court to hear his pro se case claiming a judicial council order and a lower court's refusal to recuse violated the Constitution.

## What happened

Justin Paul Dreiling, an Army servicemember representing himself, sued the United States in the Court of Federal Claims over a dispute involving a vaccination requirement for his son. That court dismissed the case for lack of jurisdiction, holding it could only hear claims seeking money from the government. Dreiling then sued again, this time challenging an order of the Federal Circuit's Judicial Council that had barred Judge Pauline Newman from voting on one of his earlier appeals. The Court of Federal Claims dismissed this second suit too, and the Federal Circuit summarily affirmed that dismissal, rejecting Dreiling's argument that its judges should have recused themselves.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked to decide three things: whether the law letting a judicial council temporarily bar a judge from hearing cases is constitutional, whether judges on a panel must step aside when the case concerns their own judicial council's actions even though the United States is the named defendant, and whether Dreiling's underlying claims fit within the limited types of claims the Court of Federal Claims may hear.

## Where it stands

The government waived its right to respond to the petition. The petition was distributed for the Court's November 6, 2026 conference, and the docket shows no action on it since.

## What each outcome would mean

Dreiling, the petitioner, is asking the Court to take up his case and reverse the Federal Circuit's ruling, which went against him. If the Court grants the petition, it will hear the case fully and decide these questions; a grant alone would not determine who wins. If the Court denies the petition, the Federal Circuit's decision affirming dismissal of Dreiling's claims stands, and his case does not proceed further in that court.
