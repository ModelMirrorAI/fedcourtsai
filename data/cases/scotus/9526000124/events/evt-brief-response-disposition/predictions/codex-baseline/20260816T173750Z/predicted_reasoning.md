# Forecast

The Court is more likely than not to grant an unqualified stay of the district court's injunction pending appeal. The application will probably be referred to the full Court and disposed of within days, rather than remaining with Justice Jackson for an in-chambers ruling. The likely ground is that the States' pre-enforcement challenge is premature while the Executive Order requires lawful implementation and the agencies have not finalized the disputed policies. The recent parallel appellate ruling against a similar justiciability theory reinforces that route.

The principal denial path is that the Court treats the asserted election-administration harms and imminent November election as concrete enough for review, then finds that a stay would disrupt rather than preserve the electoral status quo. A narrower or mixed order is also possible; because a grant in part and denial in part resolves as ungranted under this event's denial-first convention, the forecast is specifically for an unqualified grant.

The frozen context already shows that Justice Jackson requested a response, so no post-prediction response-request increment remains. It shows no referral to the full Court; referral is highly likely before disposition. The frozen context records six amicus briefs, and the briefing cycle has progressed through a reply and supplemental submissions, so little additional amicus activity is expected beyond that recorded count.
