# Rationale

I assign a 0.68 probability to an unqualified grant. The committed statpack's strictly prior pool for an OT2026 interim cell contains 30 substantive grants among 225 resolved applications (16/178 in OT2025 and 14/47 in OT2024), a 13.3% baseline that clears the 50-resolution floor. That pool is only a starting point: it is unconditioned on the escalation ladder, while predicted applications are deliberately selected higher on that ladder, and its two contributing Terms have sharply uneven parse coverage.

I adjust substantially upward because Justice Jackson affirmatively requested a response on the day of filing; the applicant is the federal government; the matter concerns implementation of an Executive Order before the November 2026 election; and the docket has extensive amicus participation. The application and reply frame a strong Article III/ripeness route under *Trump v. New York* and the Court's recent stay in *Trump v. American Federation of Government Employees*. They also identify a unanimous D.C. Circuit decision rejecting preliminary relief in a parallel challenge to the same Executive Order. The respondents' opposition provides meaningful counterweights: the district court injunction is limited to the plaintiff States and the imminent election, the States claim immediate administrative injury, and Purcell-like concerns favor preserving existing election procedures. Those features, along with the chance of mixed or partial relief that the event resolver treats as ungranted, keep the estimate well below certainty.

The provisioned snapshot shows a requested response filed August 3, an applicant reply on August 4, and supplemental submissions from both sides on August 12. The frozen context has `response_requested=true`, `referred_to_court=false`, and `amicus_briefs=6`. Referral is therefore the main unresolved procedural rung, and a nationally significant federal application of this kind is very likely to reach the full Court. The response-request increment is already vacuous. Additional amicus filings are unlikely after the response, reply, and supplemental briefing cycle.

No extracted filing text was provisioned under `record/documents/`. I therefore read the application, consolidated opposition, reply, and both supplemental submissions from the public Supreme Court PDF links already present in the provisioned docket. The official CourtListener MCP search for the First Circuit docket was unavailable because its daily rate limit returned HTTP 429; no direct CourtListener REST fallback was used.

There is a data-quality mismatch in the frozen amicus state. The context records six amicus briefs, while the snapshot contains 13 amicus filing entries: six use the singular phrase "Brief amicus curiae" and seven use "Brief amici curiae." I use the harness-owned count of six for the increment claim, as required, and flag the apparent singular/plural parsing gap. My largest substantive uncertainty is how the Court balances the government's favorable justiciability analogy against the election-specific status-quo and reliance interests.
