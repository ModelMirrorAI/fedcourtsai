# Forecast — Trump v. California (26A124), stay application, response-filed moment

**Disposition: the Court denies the application for a stay, most likely with
one or more separate writings (a dissent from Justices sympathetic to the
government's position, or a concurrence stressing election timing).** An
unqualified grant — the injunction stayed in full pending appeal — is unlikely
(~15%). If the Court gives the government anything, the likelier shape is
partial or procedural relief: a stay of some provisions but not others, or a
grant of certiorari before judgment with expedited argument while the
injunction stays in place. Under the interim vocabulary's denial-first
collapse, a mixed order resolves as ungranted, which is folded into the number
above.

**Referral.** The application will be decided by the full Court, not by
Justice Jackson in chambers. The disposing entry will carry the standard
formula — "presented to Justice Jackson and by her referred to the Court" — so
a referral notation will appear on the docket after prediction time
(currently the record shows none). This is close to certain (~93%); the small
residual covers an entry that omits the recital.

**Response request.** Already on the record (requested July 27, filed
August 3) — the rung has fired, so the increment claim is vacuous for this
cell. No second request is coming (~2%).

**Amicus briefs.** The application is fully briefed — response, reply, and
both sides' supplemental briefs are in as of August 12, and the amicus wave
crested at the August 3 response deadline. Expect few or no further amicus
filings before disposition (~12% that the record's counted amicus number
rises).

**Timing.** The application has now been pending twenty days — commentators
note it is among the longest-pending emergency election motions ever — which
signals separate writings in progress rather than inaction. Ballot-preparation
deadlines (UOCAVA transmission by mid-September) put a practical ceiling on
useful relief, so expect a disposition within roughly one to three weeks,
very likely before mid-September. Timing resolves against nothing; it is
stated to discipline the rest.

**Why denial.** Three case-specific signals point the same way. First, the
Court declined to enter the administrative stay the government requested on
July 27 — three weeks in which the injunction has stood is revealed
preference against urgency on the government's side. Second, the Purcell
principle as this Court has applied it protects settled election
administration close to an election: a stay in mid-August would let a
March 2026 executive order rework mail-ballot delivery in 23 states and D.C.
weeks before the midterms, the precise disruption Purcell counsels against —
and election-law commentators judge the changes practically unimplementable
for 2026 regardless. Third, the merits are weak for the applicants: the
Elections Clause assigns the manner of federal elections to states and
Congress, and no statute delegates to the President authority to direct USPS
to refuse mail ballots outside a federal list; even Justices generally
receptive to executive-power claims have less doctrinal room here. Against
all that, the government's extraordinary success rate on the 2025 shadow
docket keeps the grant probability meaningfully above the raw application
base rate, but not above one in five.

**If the Court acts on the merits track instead:** a grant of certiorari
before judgment with argument in the fall (leaving the injunction in place)
is a live secondary route (~15–20% as a component of the denial-side mass);
that would resolve this application as denied or deferred-then-denied.
