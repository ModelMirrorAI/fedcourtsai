# Forecast — Trump v. California (26A124), stay application, forecast after the response was requested

**Disposition.** The Court will not enter an unqualified grant of the stay. The
likeliest outcomes are an outright denial or a partial stay — relief as to some
provisions of the challenged elections executive order while the core
provisions stay enjoined — and under the resolver's denial-first collapse both
read as ungranted. P(unqualified grant) = 0.30. If the Court does grant in
full, expect a short per curiam or unexplained order with dissents from at
least the three Democratic appointees; if it denies, expect a noted dissent or
statement from one or more of Justices Thomas and Alito.

**Timing.** Briefing closed with the applicants' reply on August 4 and both
sides' supplemental briefs on August 12. The Court has already sat on a fully
briefed emergency application for almost two weeks — unusual against the
median two-day gap from response to disposition — which signals a reasoned
order, possibly with separate writings. Expect disposition in late August or
the first half of September 2026: election-administration deadlines for the
November midterms give both sides an urgency the Court will not ignore.
Nothing scores this horizon; it disciplines the rest.

**Response requested.** Already happened — this event's own moment. Justice
Jackson called for a response the day the application was filed (July 27, due
August 3), and it was filed on time. A *further* call for response is very
unlikely (~0.03); the supplemental briefs the parties filed on August 12
already cover any late development.

**Referral to the full Court.** Not yet on the docket, but near-certain
(~0.85). No Circuit Justice resolves an application of this magnitude alone:
a presidential applicant, a multistate respondent bloc, and a question about
executive power over federal elections will be referred to (and decided by)
the full Court. The residual doubt is only whether an explicit referral entry
appears before disposition rather than the full Court simply acting.

**Amicus activity.** The docket already carries thirteen amicus filings
(the harness's frozen count reads 6 — it counts only the singular-form
"Brief amicus curiae" entries). With briefing complete and disposition
imminent, most amici who intend to file have filed; a further brief is
possible if disposition slips into September but is not the likely case
(~0.30 that the count as frozen rises further).

**Merits-shaped content, conditional.** If the Court reaches the stay factors
in a reasoned order, the likely ground for denial is that the applicants have
not shown a fair prospect that the Court would sustain unilateral executive
authority over election administration — Article I, § 4 commits the manner of
federal elections to the states and Congress — plus the equities of changing
election rules close to the midterms (the Purcell instinct cuts against the
stay, since the injunction preserves the pre-order status quo). A partial
grant, if any, would cover the order's narrower administrative provisions
rather than its core mandates.
