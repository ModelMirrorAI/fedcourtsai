# Forecast — Trump v. California (26A124), application for a stay

**Disposition.** The Court will not grant the application in full. The likeliest
outcome is a denial or a partial disposition (which the interim vocabulary
reads as ungranted): the application asks the Court to let an executive order
restructuring federal election administration take effect roughly three months
before the November 2026 midterms, over two adverse rulings below, and the
Purcell principle points the same direction as the status quo here — the
injunction preserves existing state election rules, while a stay would change
them on the eve of an election. I put the probability of an **unqualified
grant** at **0.25**: material, because this Court has granted the federal
government interim relief at a high rate in the last two Terms and often
narrows overbroad injunctions, but well below an even chance, because the
merits rest on a claim of presidential authority over election administration
that the Constitution assigns to the states and Congress, and because a mixed
"granted in part" order — a very plausible shape for a multi-provision EO
enjoined at the behest of a broad coalition — resolves as ungranted.

**Response.** Already resolved on this record: Justice Jackson called for a
response the day the application was filed (due August 3), and the response,
a reply, and supplemental briefs from both sides are all in. The
response-requested rung has fired; I expect no further call for a response
(0.02 that a new one issues after prediction time — essentially only if the
Court orders supplemental briefing framed as a response request).

**Referral.** The docket does not yet show a referral, but I expect one with
high probability (0.9): a fully briefed application from the Solicitor
General, with supplemental briefing and heavy amicus participation, is not the
kind a Circuit Justice disposes of in chambers. Justice Jackson will refer it
to the full Court — most likely the referral becomes visible in the disposing
order itself ("presented to Justice Jackson and by her referred to the
Court") or as a docket entry shortly before it.

**Amici.** The docket already carries thirteen amicus filings (the frozen
context counts six — see reasoning.md on the counting discrepancy). Briefing
closed with the August 12 supplemental briefs and disposition is likely near,
so I expect little further amicus activity: 0.15 that the count as the harness
measures it rises past the frozen six.

**Timing** (unscored, but it disciplines the rest): the Court has had full
briefing since August 4 and supplemental briefs since August 12. I expect a
disposition within roughly one to three weeks — late August to early September
2026 — as an order with at least one separate writing, given the stakes and
the three-week pendency, which suggests opinions are being written rather
than a clean in-chambers denial.

**If relief is granted** (conditional): the likelier form is a partial stay —
narrowing the injunction's scope (for example, to the plaintiff states or to
particular provisions of the order) rather than letting the entire EO take
effect nationwide. That shape resolves this event as ungranted under the
denial-first collapse.
