I expect the Court to deny the emergency application for a stay of the District Court's nationwide injunction. The application concerns an executive order that would significantly alter election administration rules (citizenship verification and mail-in ballot procedures) very close to the November 2026 elections. Enjoining the executive order preserves the status quo, whereas staying the injunction would allow the new rules to take effect immediately, likely causing confusion and violating the Purcell principle against late-stage election rule changes. Furthermore, the First Circuit noted that the administration failed to defend the underlying legality of the executive order in its emergency request, which weakens the application's prospect for success on the merits.

The application was submitted to Justice Jackson, who requested a response. Given the high stakes and national importance of the executive order, it is highly likely that she will refer the application to the full Court rather than acting alone. Expect a disposition shortly, as briefing (including replies and supplemental briefs) appears complete, and the election is fast approaching. No further response will be requested since one was already filed, and while the amicus count is already very high (13 on the docket), there is a small chance a few more could trickle in before the Court rules.
