## In brief

The Supreme Court granted the Trump administration's request to pause a district court injunction while appeals over an elections executive order continue.

## What happened

A district court in Massachusetts issued an injunction blocking enforcement of a Trump administration executive order relating to elections, in a case brought by California and other parties. The administration appealed to the First Circuit and, while that appeal proceeded, asked Justice Jackson for an emergency stay of the injunction. She referred the application to the full Court. California and a wide range of amici, including former judges, former governors, election officials, and advocacy groups, urged the Court to leave the injunction in place. The administration filed a reply and two supplemental briefs before the Court acted.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court was asked whether to pause the lower court's order blocking the executive order while the legal fight over its validity continues in the appeals court and, potentially, in a future petition to the Supreme Court itself.

## Where it stands

The Court granted the stay on August 24, 2026. The injunction is paused until the First Circuit decides the appeal and, if a timely petition for review is filed with the Supreme Court, until that petition is resolved; the stay ends automatically if review is denied, or upon final judgment if review is granted. Justices Sotomayor, Kagan, and Jackson dissented.

## What each outcome would mean

The administration, as applicant, asked the Court to pause the injunction that blocked its executive order. By granting the stay, the Court allowed the executive order to take effect while the case continues, a result favoring the administration over California and the other parties who had won the injunction below. The pause is temporary: it lasts through the appeal and any further Supreme Court review, and will lift automatically if review is later denied, or once a final judgment issues if review is granted. The underlying legal dispute over the order's validity remains unresolved.
