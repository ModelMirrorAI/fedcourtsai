## In brief

The Trump administration asked the Supreme Court to pause a lower court order blocking parts of an election-related executive order from applying before the November 2026 election.

## What happened

President Trump issued an executive order directing federal agencies to compile citizenship lists for voters and to have the Postal Service propose mail-ballot rules, among other provisions. California and other states sued, arguing the order was unconstitutional and exceeded presidential authority. A federal district court agreed, ruling two sections of the order unlawful and blocking federal agencies from enforcing them against the plaintiff states for the November 2026 election. The First Circuit Court of Appeals declined to pause that injunction while the government's appeal proceeded. The federal government then asked the Supreme Court to step in.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked whether the lower court's order blocking the executive order should be paused while the case continues on appeal. The government argues the states cannot yet sue because the order has not been fully carried out and any harm is speculative. The states respond that they face immediate, concrete costs and burdens in preparing for upcoming elections.

## Where it stands

The application was referred to the full Court, which granted the stay on August 24, 2026, pausing the injunction pending the appeal and any later Supreme Court review. Justices Sotomayor, Kagan, and Jackson dissented.

## What each outcome would mean

The government, as applicant, asked the Court to pause the injunction obtained by California and other states. By granting the stay, the Court allowed federal agencies to proceed with implementing the executive order's citizenship-list and mail-ballot provisions ahead of the November 2026 election while the First Circuit appeal continues, meaning California's injunction no longer blocks that implementation for now. The stay is temporary: it ends automatically if certiorari is denied, or upon this Court's judgment if certiorari is granted.
