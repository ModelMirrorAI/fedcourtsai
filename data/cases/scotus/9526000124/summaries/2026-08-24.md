## In brief

The Supreme Court stayed a lower court's injunction that had blocked parts of a Trump executive order affecting mail voting in the 2026 election.

## What happened

President Trump issued an executive order directing federal agencies to compile citizen lists for states and to have the Postal Service propose rules for mail-in ballots, ahead of the November 2026 election. California and many other states sued, arguing the order was unlawful and unconstitutional. A federal district court in Massachusetts ruled for the states, declaring parts of the order void and blocking federal agencies from implementing it for that election. The First Circuit Court of Appeals declined to pause that injunction while the government's appeal proceeded. The federal government then asked the Supreme Court to step in.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked whether the states' lawsuit was filed too early to be decided, because the order mainly directs agencies to study and consider future action, consistent with the law, rather than itself change election rules. The government argues no court should block the order until agencies take concrete steps that actually harm the states. The states argue the order already forces them to spend resources and creates real, immediate legal risks.

## Where it stands

The application for a stay was referred to the full Court and granted on August 24, 2026. The Court's order stays the district court's injunction while the appeal and any later petition for review continue.

## What each outcome would mean

The Trump administration, as applicant, asked the Court to pause the injunction. By granting the stay, the Court has allowed federal agencies to proceed with implementing the disputed parts of the executive order for the November 2026 election while the First Circuit appeal continues, and while the government may still seek further Supreme Court review. The stay will end automatically if certiorari is later denied, or when the Court's judgment issues if certiorari is granted. California and the other states' injunction is paused, not permanently overturned.
