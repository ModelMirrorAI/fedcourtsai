# Forecast of the Court's action

The Court will deny the application for a stay of execution. The probability of granting relief in whole or in part, conditional on a non-moot disposition, is **3%**. The expected order will not contain a substantive majority opinion.

From the arrival baseline, which shows no requested response and no full-Court referral, the Circuit Justice will likely call for a response (**80%**) and refer the application to the full Court (**95%**). Those acts will represent additional review after the November 14 submission, not a favorable decision on the request. An uninvited response alone would not satisfy the response-requested claim.

The amicus count will most likely remain at **zero**. There is a **12%** chance that at least one additional qualifying entry or distinct submitted amicus brief raises that count before disposition. A later acceptance of the same submitted brief will not count twice.

The expected disposition window is **November 16–19, 2026**, before the execution time identified in the application, **6:00 p.m. on November 19**. Timing is an unscored forecast, and neither a response nor referral necessarily implies a delay beyond that window.

The modal order will be unsigned and unaccompanied by separate writings. The per-Justice authorship probabilities are Roberts 0.3%, Thomas 0.6%, Alito 0.4%, Sotomayor 8%, Kagan 1.5%, Gorsuch 0.8%, Kavanaugh 0.4%, Barrett 0.4%, and Jackson 4.5%. These are independent probabilities of writing, not probabilities of joining a writing or a predicted vote split.

If the Court explains a denial, the expected distinction is between the conceded irreparability of execution and an insufficient showing of likely review and reversal on the clemency or warrant challenges. This doctrinal statement is conditional: an unexplained denial will not establish that the Court adopted a particular view of the constitutional questions. If relief is instead granted, the likely form is a stay preserving consideration of the pending petition, not a final merits ruling invalidating Florida's process.
