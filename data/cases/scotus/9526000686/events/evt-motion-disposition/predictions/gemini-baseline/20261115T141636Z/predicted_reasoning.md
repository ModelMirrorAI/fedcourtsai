# Forecast of the Court's Action on Application 26A686

At the arrival moment on November 14, 2026, Application No. 26A686 — seeking a stay of the execution of Thomas M. Overton scheduled for Thursday, November 19, 2026, at 6:00 p.m. EST, pending disposition of his linked petition for a writ of certiorari in No. 26-6154 — stands submitted to Justice Thomas with no response requested, no referral to the full Court, and zero amicus briefs recorded on the docket.

1. **Response request vs. uninvited state filing:** Neither Justice Thomas nor the Clerk is likely to enter a formal docketed call for a response (`Response to application requested ...`). Under established capital-warrant practice in Florida, the State of Florida will almost certainly file its brief in opposition to the stay application (and to certiorari in No. 26-6154) automatically by November 16 or November 17, 2026, without a formal response-request entry appearing on the docket, followed by a brief reply from applicant's counsel. A docketed response request will occur only in the unlikely event that the State does not file on its standard warrant schedule and the Clerk sets a formal deadline.
2. **Referral to the full Court:** Justice Thomas will refer Application No. 26A686 to the full Court ahead of the scheduled November 19 execution so that the full bench disposes simultaneously of both the stay application in No. 26A686 and the linked certiorari petition in No. 26-6154, rather than acting in chambers alone.
3. **Amicus curiae filings:** No amicus curiae brief (accepted or submitted) is expected to arrive during the five-day window between docketing (Saturday, November 14) and disposition. The application presents late-stage, post-warrant Florida clemency and public-records claims that have not drawn amicus participation in comparable recent Florida execution-warrant dockets.
4. **Timing and form of disposition:** The Court will dispose of Application No. 26A686 by an unsigned order issued between Wednesday, November 18, and the afternoon of Thursday, November 19, 2026, prior to the 6:00 p.m. execution window. The order will state in standard form that the application for stay of execution of sentence of death presented to Justice Thomas and by him referred to the Court is denied, accompanied by a concurrent denial of the petition for a writ of certiorari in No. 26-6154.
5. **Separate writings on the order:** The denial order is very likely to issue without any recorded separate writing. If any Justice authors a separate dissent or statement respecting the denial, Justice Sotomayor or Justice Jackson is the likeliest writer, though neither is expected to write on repetitive, procedurally barred post-warrant clemency claims.

## Conditional Doctrinal Basis

To obtain a stay of execution pending certiorari under *Barefoot v. Estelle*, 463 U.S. 880, 895 (1983), an applicant must establish a reasonable probability that four Justices will vote to grant certiorari, a significant possibility of reversal, and a likelihood of irreparable harm, alongside equitable diligence in seeking relief. While the irreparable harm of execution is undisputed, the application cannot clear the certiorari or merits thresholds for three independent reasons:

- **Independent and adequate state-law procedural bars:** In its unanimous November 13, 2026 decision below (*Overton v. State* / *Overton v. Secretary, Department of Corrections*, Nos. SC2026-1724 & SC2026-1725), the Supreme Court of Florida held that Overton's successive postconviction claims were untimely under Florida Rule of Criminal Procedure 3.851(d)(1) (relying on a 2021 public remark by the Governor raised years out of time only after an October 2026 warrant), procedurally barred as successive under Rule 3.851(e)(2), and partially forfeited on appeal, while his post-warrant public-records demands failed state-law standards under Rule 3.852. Those independent and adequate state procedural grounds bar plenary federal review under *Coleman v. Thompson*, 501 U.S. 722, 729 (1991).
- **Absence of a certworthy split or viable federal claim under *Woodard*:** Conditional on reaching the federal merits, the Court would conclude that a governor's general policy statement declining to commute murder sentences does not render state clemency proceedings a Due Process or Equal Protection violation under *Connecticut Board of Pardons v. Dumschat*, 452 U.S. 458 (1981), and *Ohio Adult Parole Authority v. Woodard*, 523 U.S. 272 (1998), nor does the Constitution compel post-warrant discovery into executive clemency or warrant-selection deliberations.
- **If a stay were unexpectedly granted:** Relief would take the form of a stay pending disposition of the certiorari petition in No. 26-6154 only if five Justices concluded that systematic executive pre-commitment against murder commutations implicates Justice O'Connor's minimal-safeguards concurrence in *Woodard* notwithstanding state timeliness bars. That outcome is overwhelmingly unlikely on this vehicle.
