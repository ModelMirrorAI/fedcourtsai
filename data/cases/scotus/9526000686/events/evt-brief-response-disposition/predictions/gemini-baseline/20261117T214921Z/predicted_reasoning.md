## Disposition Rationale and Conditional Merits Framing

The Court will conclude that Applicant fails to satisfy the requirements for a stay of execution pending certiorari — specifically, a reasonable probability that four Justices would vote to grant certiorari, a significant possibility of reversal, and a balance of equities favoring last-minute intervention.

- **Independent and adequate state procedural bar.** Below, the Florida Supreme Court affirmed the circuit court's ruling that Applicant's challenge to his post-2013 clemency process was untimely and procedurally barred under Florida Rule of Criminal Procedure 3.851(d), because Applicant participated in his clemency interview in December 2013 and waited nearly thirteen years — until after the October 20, 2026 death warrant issued — to challenge the absence of formal interim notice or updated clemency proceedings. That state-law timeliness bar forecloses Supreme Court review of the judgment below regardless of the alternative merits holding.
- **Absence of certworthy conflict under *Woodard*.** Even reaching the federal question, the Court has consistently declined to revisit Florida's executive clemency procedures under *Ohio Adult Parole Authority v. Woodard*, 523 U.S. 272 (1998), including in near-identical Florida warrant postures within the past month (*Guardado v. Florida*, No. 26A623; *Reaves v. Florida*, No. 26A524). Because Applicant received appointed clemency counsel and a Commission interview in 2013, retained the ability to submit supplemental written materials under Florida's Rules of Executive Clemency, and received an express determination in the signed death warrant that executive clemency was not warranted, four Justices will not view the record as presenting the kind of arbitrary denial of minimal procedural safeguards contemplated by Justice O'Connor's controlling concurrence in *Woodard*.
- **Equitable delay.** Under *Hill v. McDonough*, 547 U.S. 573 (2006), and *Bucklew v. Precythe*, 587 U.S. 119 (2019), a challenge to a thirteen-year-old clemency interview brought only after a death warrant is signed carries heavy equitable headwinds against emergency stay relief.

If, contrary to expectation, the Court were to grant a stay and reach the underlying question on certiorari, review would be confined to the sole question presented in the application — whether a thirteen-year gap between a clemency interview and a death warrant without separate pre-warrant notice or a renewed hearing violates minimal procedural due process under the Fourteenth Amendment — and would turn on whether *Woodard*'s minimal-safeguards standard requires contemporaneous notice and periodic updating once a state establishes a capital clemency process.

Because the application rests solely on the procedurally barred Florida clemency claim — omitting the nonunanimous-jury issue litigated below — and recent indistinguishable Florida clemency stay denials generated no separate writings, even Justices Sotomayor and Jackson are unlikely to author a dissent or statement respecting the denial.
