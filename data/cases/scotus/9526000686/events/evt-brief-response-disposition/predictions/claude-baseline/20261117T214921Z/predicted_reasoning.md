# Forecast: Overton v. Florida, No. 26A686 (stay of execution)

The Court will deny the application for a stay of execution, and will deny the linked certiorari petition (No. 26-6154) in the same order.

- **Disposition.** Denied. The record shows an application submitted to Justice Thomas on November 14 and Florida's response filed November 17; no response was called for, and the application has not yet been referred. The order will read in the usual form: the application presented to Justice Thomas and by him referred to the Court is denied, and the petition for a writ of certiorari is denied.
- **Timing.** The order issues on November 18 or 19, 2026, before the 6:00 p.m. execution on November 19, most likely within a few hours of it. A reply from the applicant may be filed first; it will not change the result.
- **Referral.** Justice Thomas will refer the application to the full Court rather than act alone; the referral will be recited in the disposing order itself.
- **Response request.** The Court will not call for a response. Florida has already filed one uninvited, along with a brief in opposition to the petition.
- **Amicus briefs.** None will be filed. The record shows zero.
- **Separate writings.** No Justice will write. No noted dissent is expected; if any Justice notes one, it would be Justice Sotomayor or Justice Jackson, without opinion.
- **Why, on the legal standard.** A stay needs a reasonable probability of four votes for certiorari and a significant possibility of reversal. The petition challenges the Governor's discretion over clemency and death warrants and the denial of clemency records. The Court's precedent treats clemency as rarely fit for judicial review beyond minimal safeguards, the records ruling rests on Florida law, and the Court has denied certiorari on the same claims from other Florida warrant cases in recent weeks. The application does not develop a split or a case-specific argument. If the Court were to act for the applicant at all, the only realistic form would be a short administrative stay, which is not expected.
