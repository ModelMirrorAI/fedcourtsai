# Forecast: Blanche v. Ozurumba, No. 25-1343 (evt-petition-disposition)

**Disposition.** P(any grant) = 0.38; the single most likely outcome is a denial, probably on the
October 5, 2026 order list or after one relist. The petition was distributed once, for the
September 28, 2026 long conference, and was **not** among the grants the Court released from that
conference on October 1, 2026. The first-tranche route is the one on which most Solicitor General
petitions distributed for the long conference are granted, so what remains is a relist-and-grant
path (roughly a third of the probability) against denial.

**Relists.** The docket shows one distribution (9/28/2026) and no relist yet; relist entries from
the long conference are posted after the first Monday order list, so the silence as of October 3
is uninformative between relist and denial. I expect a relist for the October 9 conference with
probability 0.55. If the Court relists, I expect one or two further distributions at most: either a
grant by late October / early November, or a denial with a separate writing.

**CVSG.** None. The United States is the petitioner, so a call for the Solicitor General's views
is effectively impossible (0.01).

**Question presented, if granted.** The Court would take the government's question as framed:
whether the material-support bar, 8 U.S.C. 1182(a)(3)(B)(iv)(VI), requires that support be
"significant" or tied to the organization's terrorist activities (the Fourth Circuit's reading) or
reaches any knowing support that sustains a terrorist organization, including food, as the BIA held
in Matter of A-C-M- and as the Second and Third Circuits have accepted. It would not take the
duress question, which the Fourth Circuit expressly left open, nor the Loper Bright stare-decisis
question the panel flagged in dicta.

**Summary disposition.** Conditional on a grant, a summary route (GVR or per curiam reversal) is
unlikely, 0.15: no intervening decision of this Court bears on the split, and the question is a
legal one about the meaning of "material," which fits plenary review. The residual mass is a
summary reversal on the theory that the panel disregarded the substantial-evidence standard, or a
hold-and-GVR if a related petition is granted this Term.

**Dissent from denial.** Conditional on denial, 0.25 that a Justice notes a dissent or files a
statement respecting denial. Judge Richardson's dissent frames the majority as reading "terrorist
organization" out of the statute, a framing that would appeal to Justices Thomas or Alito, and a
relist-then-denial of a federal petition is the shape that most often draws a writing. Should the
Court deny outright on October 5, expect no separate writing.

**If granted (conditional only).** The likely ground would be textual: the statute distinguishes
support "for the commission of a terrorist activity" from support "to a terrorist organization,"
and Holder v. Humanitarian Law Project read "material support" broadly, so the Court would most
likely reverse the Fourth Circuit's "significant to terrorist activities" gloss while leaving
room for a genuinely de minimis floor. That is a forecast of the merits only in the branch where
the Court takes the case.
