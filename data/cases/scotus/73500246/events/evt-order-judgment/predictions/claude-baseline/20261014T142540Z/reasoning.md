**Adjustments up.**
- The SG is the petitioner. Government-as-petitioner merits wins run well
  above the docket average, historically in the high 70s to 80s percent.
- The decision below is a divided panel. The dissent states the government's
  reading, contrasting subclause (dd) with (aa) and citing HLP.
- The panel's test reads an activity nexus into "to a terrorist
  organization", which is in tension with HLP's fungibility reasoning. The
  current Court has generally read terrorism and immigration bars as written
  rather than narrowly.
- A grant on an SG petition in a fact-sympathetic asylum case suggests the
  Court sees a legal error with consequences beyond one case.

**Adjustments down / uncertainty.**
- I have not seen the petition or the BIO. The BIO may have raised a vehicle
  problem, such as removal, the reserved CAT claim, or a remand that could
  still go his way. It may also have argued that the case is fact-bound under
  substantial-evidence review.
- Two distributions before grant is ordinary, not a signal either way.
- The Court could split the difference: reject the activity-nexus test but
  stop short of A-C-M-'s "de minimis" rule. That outcome is still disturbed,
  so it does not lower my number. It only shifts which label applies:
  vacated rather than reversed.
- A DIG or an equally divided affirmance would count against me. I put that
  at about 3%.
