## In brief

Families of September 11 victims ask the Court to send their case back to the lower court for reconsideration in light of a newer Supreme Court ruling.

## What happened

Thousands of family members and estates of September 11 victims, who hold court judgments against the Taliban, tried to collect from Afghan central bank assets frozen at the Federal Reserve Bank of New York. The Second Circuit Court of Appeals ruled against them, holding that because the Executive Branch still formally recognizes Afghanistan as a state, the Taliban and its instrumentalities are protected by foreign sovereign immunity. After that ruling, and after rehearing was denied, the Supreme Court decided another case, Exxon Mobil Corp. v. Corporación CIMEX, which the petitioners say clarifies that courts, not the Executive Branch, must decide these immunity questions by interpreting the relevant statute.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked whether, given its intervening decision clarifying that courts—not the Executive Branch—must independently determine whether an entity counts as a foreign state entitled to immunity, the lower court's ruling should be set aside and sent back so it can reconsider the immunity question under the correct approach.

## Where it stands

The petition was distributed for the Court's conference on November 6, 2026, and then distributed again for a conference on November 13, 2026. The docket does not show any decision yet on whether the Court will take the case.

## What each outcome would mean

The Ashton petitioners, who lost in the lower court, are asking the Court to act. If the Court grants the request and vacates the lower court's decision, the case would go back to the Second Circuit to reconsider the immunity question without a decision for either side. If the Court denies the petition, the Second Circuit's ruling favoring the Taliban's immunity from these claims stays in place, and the petitioners' effort to collect on their judgments using the frozen bank assets would not proceed on this basis.
