## In brief

Families of September 11 victims ask the Court to send their case back for reconsideration of whether the Taliban's frozen bank assets are immune from collection.

## What happened

Kathleen Ashton and more than 3,000 other family members and estates of 9/11 victims hold court judgments against the Taliban and are trying to collect from Taliban-controlled assets at Afghanistan's central bank, frozen at the Federal Reserve Bank of New York. The Second Circuit ruled against them, holding that because the Executive Branch still formally recognizes Afghanistan as a state, the bank is immune from having its assets seized, regardless of who controls it. The full appeals court denied rehearing. After that, the Supreme Court decided a different case, Exxon Mobil Corp. v. Corporación CIMEX, which the family members say changes how this kind of immunity question should be analyzed.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked whether, given its recent decision clarifying that courts—not the Executive Branch—must decide through ordinary legal interpretation whether an entity counts as a foreign state entitled to immunity, the lower court's ruling should be set aside and sent back so it can reconsider the immunity question under that approach.

## Where it stands

The petition has been distributed for conference twice, most recently November 13, 2026. On November 16, 2026, the Court invited the Solicitor General to file a brief giving the federal government's views on the case, with that response now due December 17, 2026.

## What each outcome would mean

The Ashton family members, as petitioners, are asking the Court to set aside the Second Circuit's ruling and send the case back for reconsideration. If the Court grants the petition, it would take up the case and decide the immunity question itself, deciding nothing in advance about who wins. If it denies the petition, the Second Circuit's ruling favoring the Taliban's immunity claim stays in place. Because the Court has asked the Solicitor General for input, any further action on the petition will likely wait until that brief is filed.
