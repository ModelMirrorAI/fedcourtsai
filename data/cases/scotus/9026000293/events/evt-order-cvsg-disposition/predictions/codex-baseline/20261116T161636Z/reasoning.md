## Case-specific adjustment

The petition's distinctive request is a GVR in light of Exxon Mobil v. CIMEX, decided June 23, 2026 according to the petition and independently located CourtListener metadata. It characterizes that intervening decision as requiring courts to decide sovereign-immunity questions through statutory interpretation rather than treating executive recognition as dispositive. A recent, relevant Supreme Court decision supplies a plausible economical route to relief. The appended Sullivan panel dissent confirms a substantial dispute about recognition and statutory interpretation. The petition also reports dissenting writings concerning en banc rehearing, although the retained portion of Appendix B does not include those writings.

However, the appended majority opinion materially limits the petition's presentation. At Appendix 6a–7a and 35a–43a, the panel expressly holds that TRIA overrides both jurisdictional and execution immunity and independently supports jurisdiction when its requirements are met. It denies these judgment creditors relief because DAB was not an instrumentality of the Taliban **when the assets were blocked**. Appendix 50a–51a restates both propositions. Consequently, the petition's contention that the panel never analyzed TRIA's immunity override is not an adequate account of the retained opinion. Even if CIMEX warrants reconsideration of the recognition framework, the timing ground remains a substantial obstacle to changing the judgment. I do not treat the petition's account of CIMEX as an independently verified holding about diplomatic recognition.

The companion petitions keep a grant-family outcome quite plausible despite that weakness. The Court's own `Vide` link identifies **Havlish, No. 26-292**; its staged docket shows the same judgment, relist, and CVSG, plus a state amicus brief and an accepted supplemental brief. The petition expressly supports Havlish's challenge to TRIA's blocking-date rule and requests consolidation if review is granted elsewhere. **Owens, No. 26-294** is staged under `vided-amicus`, with one shared brief: that relationship alone is weak, but its docket identifies the same lower-court judgment and coordinated distributions/CVSG, corroborating the petition's account of related litigation. These are not three independent demonstrations that the Court intends to grant. The same-question file is empty; I infer no absence of a legal conflict from that.

On balance, I move roughly three percentage points below the frozen 35% anchor. The narrow GVR theory has a real outcome-relevance problem; the linked challenge to the timing ground and the substantive panel dissent prevent a much larger discount. A CVSG is also quite understandable solely to obtain the government's position on central-bank reserves and foreign relations, without four Justices having settled on review. I have no basis for assuming what the Solicitor General will recommend.
