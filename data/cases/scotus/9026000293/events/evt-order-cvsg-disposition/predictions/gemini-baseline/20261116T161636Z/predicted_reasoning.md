# Predicted Court Action: *Kathleen Ashton, et al. v. The Taliban, et al.* (No. 26-293)

## Procedural Trajectory from the CVSG Order

The docket currently records **two conference distributions** (distributed October 21, 2026, for the November 6 conference, and relisted November 9, 2026, for the November 13 conference) and an **invitation to the Solicitor General (CVSG)** entered on November 16, 2026, across this petition and its two companion petitions (*Havlish v. The Taliban*, No. 26-292, vided with No. 26-293; and *Owens v. Taliban*, No. 26-294), with Justice Kagan recused in Nos. 26-292 and 26-293 under 28 U.S.C. § 455(b)(3).

- **Further distributions (`relist-increment`):** Once the Solicitor General files the brief for the United States (expected in spring 2027), the Clerk will distribute the petition, the Government's brief, and supplemental briefs for at least one post-CVSG conference, raising the total distribution count from 2 to at least 3 barring an improbable pre-conference Rule 46 dismissal. Because the Court must coordinate three petitions with differing statutory postures and a recusal split across Nos. 26-292/26-293 (8 Justices) and No. 26-294 (9 Justices), one additional relist after the initial post-CVSG conference (to 4 distributions) is also a live possibility before final disposition.
- **CVSG (`cvsg-increment`):** The Court has already called for the Solicitor General's views on November 16, 2026; no further CVSG order will issue.

## Expected Disposition: Certiorari Denied

The Solicitor General will recommend **denial of certiorari** across all three companion petitions, and the eight participating Justices will **deny the petition**.

In its invited brief, the United States will defend the President's exclusive constitutional authority under *Zivotofsky v. Kerry*, 576 U.S. 1 (2015), to maintain formal recognition of the State of Afghanistan without recognizing the Taliban as its government, urge protection of Executive Branch control over the $3.5 billion in blocked Da Afghanistan Bank (DAB) central bank reserves held at the Federal Reserve Bank of New York, and note the absence of any circuit split on the Second Circuit's sui generis application of § 201(a) of the Terrorism Risk Insurance Act (TRIA). Specifically as to *Ashton* (No. 26-293), the Solicitor General will point out that the petition's sole Question Presented—seeking vacatur and remand under *Exxon Mobil Corp. v. Corporación CIMEX, S.A. (Cuba)*, 146 S. Ct. 1909 (2026)—rests on a mistaken premise, because Part II.C.1 of the Second Circuit's opinion already held that TRIA § 201(a) abrogates both jurisdictional and execution immunity under the Foreign Sovereign Immunities Act (FSIA), while ruling against the *Ashton* judgment creditors solely on an independent TRIA timing and control ground that *Cimex* does not address. With defaulted foreign respondents, an 8-Justice bench, and Executive Branch opposition on a sensitive foreign-reserves matter, the Court will deny review.

## Conditional Route and Question Presented If Granted

- **Summary disposition vs. plenary review (`summary-disposition-route`):** Conditional on *Ashton* (No. 26-293) receiving any form of grant, **summary vacatur and remand (GVR) in the certiorari order is more likely than plenary review**. That is not because a standalone *Cimex* GVR is likely on its own terms, but because *Ashton* is a 12-page companion filing that framed *only* a *Cimex* GVR question while expressly leaving the dispositive TRIA § 201(a) "blocking-date" question to vided lead petition *Havlish* (No. 26-292). If four Justices vote for plenary review of the Second Circuit's TRIA ruling, the Court is more likely to grant *Havlish* (No. 26-292) alone as the clean Rule 14.1(a) vehicle, **hold *Ashton* (No. 26-293)** pending the merits decision in *Havlish*, and ultimately **GVR *Ashton*** if the judgment in *Havlish* is reversed or vacated (alongside a smaller chance of an immediate GVR across all three dockets if the Executive Branch alters its sanctions or recognition posture toward DAB while preparing its brief).
- **Question taken if set for plenary argument:** If the Court instead grants plenary review directly in No. 26-293, it will not grant *Ashton*'s standalone FSIA/*Cimex* Question Presented in isolation. Rather, as petitioners requested in the alternative, the Court will consolidate No. 26-293 with No. 26-292 (*Havlish*) and confine review to the TRIA § 201(a) question whether DAB's blocked reserves qualify as "blocked assets of [an] agency or instrumentality of [the Taliban]" assessed at the time of turnover rather than blocking (potentially paired with the narrow FSIA § 1603(b)(2) present-ownership question, while declining to disturb *Zivotofsky* recognition doctrine). If granted and argued on the merits before an 8-Justice Court, petitioners would have a narrow edge on the textualist reading of TRIA § 201(a), though a 4–4 affirmance by an equally divided Court would remain a substantial hazard.

## Separate Writings on Denial

Should the Court deny certiorari, **no separate writing is the baseline outcome**, as Justices ordinarily refrain from writing at the certiorari stage when the Executive Branch warns against disturbing blocked sovereign central bank reserves. There is, however, a modest (~10%) chance of a dissent from the denial of certiorari or a statement respecting the denial—most plausibly authored by Justice Gorsuch or Justice Thomas (potentially joined by Justice Alito, or a brief statement respecting denial by Justice Kavanaugh)—highlighting Judges Sullivan and Menashi's textualist critique of the Second Circuit's TRIA "blocking-date" rule. Justice Kagan is recused and will not participate or write.
