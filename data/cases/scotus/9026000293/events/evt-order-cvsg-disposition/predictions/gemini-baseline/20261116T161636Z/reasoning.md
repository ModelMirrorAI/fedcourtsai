## Substantive Adjustments from 35.0% to 25.0%

Starting from the **35.0%** CVSG anchor, I adjust downward by 10 percentage points to **`0.25`** based on three converging considerations:

### 1. Expected Solicitor General Opposition (~72% probability SG recommends denial)
In CVSG cases, the Solicitor General's recommendation is the dominant predictor of certiorari, and institutional Executive Branch equities strongly point toward a recommendation to deny:
- **Article II Recognition Exclusivity:** Both *Ashton* and *Owens* invite judicial second-guessing of the Executive's formal recognition of the State of Afghanistan without recognizing the Taliban. Across administrations, the Department of Justice and State Department vigilantly defend *Zivotofsky*'s holding that formal state recognition belongs exclusively to the President—a point even Judge Menashi's en banc dissent endorsed below.
- **Sovereign Central Bank Reserves and FRBNY Safe-Harbor Policy:** The $3.5 billion at issue consists of sovereign central bank reserves of Afghanistan—a state never designated as a state sponsor of terrorism—held at the Federal Reserve Bank of New York, alongside $3.5 billion already transferred under Executive Order 14064 to the Swiss "Afghan Fund" for the Afghan people. In the Second Circuit, the United States participated as amicus curiae opposing attachment/turnover, warning that liquidating pre-existing foreign central bank reserves at FRBNY whenever an unrecognized terrorist group captures a foreign capital would undermine Executive foreign-policy leverage, deplete reserves needed for future diplomatic or macroeconomic stabilization, and chill foreign central banks' reliance on FRBNY custody.
- **No Genuine Circuit Split and Defaulted Respondents:** Despite `Ashton`'s passing citation to *Ungar v. PLO*, 402 F.3d 274 (1st Cir. 2005) (an *unrecognized* entity), and *OI European Group v. Venezuela*, 73 F.4th 157 (3d Cir. 2023), no circuit has declined to treat formal Presidential recognition of a foreign state as conclusive under the FSIA, nor has any other circuit confronted TRIA § 201(a)'s application to pre-existing central bank reserves frozen upon a regime's collapse. Moreover, neither the Taliban nor DAB has appeared, meaning plenary review over SG opposition would require the Court to appoint an amicus curiae to defend the judgment below before an **8-Justice Court** (with Justice Kagan recused in Nos. 26-292 and 26-293, creating a 4–4 affirmance hazard).

### 2. Severe Defects in *Ashton*'s Standalone *Cimex* GVR Argument
Reading the 12-page *Ashton* petition alongside the Second Circuit's full opinion reveals a striking mismatch:
- *Ashton*'s **sole Question Presented** asks for a GVR in light of *Cimex*, asserting (Pet. 8, 10) that the Second Circuit *"never asked whether TRIA's ... 'notwithstanding any other provision of law' language, or any other feature of its 'architecture'—the very inquiry Cimex directs—displaces ordinary FSIA principles."*
- In reality, **Part II.C.1 of the Second Circuit's opinion (152 F.4th at 358–60, slip op. 38–44) already held the exact proposition *Cimex* later adopted for Helms-Burton**: reversing the district court on jurisdictional immunity, the panel held that TRIA § 201(a)'s `"notwithstanding"` clause supersedes **both** FSIA jurisdictional immunity and FSIA execution immunity without requiring any FSIA exception.
- The *Ashton* judgment creditors lost below **solely in Part II.C.2**, where the panel held—as a matter of non-FSIA TRIA construction—that DAB was not an *"agency or instrumentality of [the Taliban]"* **as of the date the assets were blocked** and never controlled the New York-frozen reserves. *Cimex* (which involved indisputably Cuban state-owned corporations and addressed only FSIA § 1604 immunity) says nothing about TRIA's blocking-date rule or Executive state recognition. The Solicitor General is virtually certain to flag this disconnect, making a standalone *Cimex* GVR highly unlikely (~1%).

### 3. Companion-Vehicle Mechanics Between *Havlish* (No. 26-292) and *Ashton* (No. 26-293)
Why, despite that fatal flaw in *Ashton*'s standalone *Cimex* theory, is `probability` still **0.25** rather than <0.10, and why is `summary-disposition-route` **0.56**?
- **Upside on the broader controversy (~25% chance of plenary review in *Havlish*):** $3.5 billion in 9/11 victim relief, forceful textualist en banc dissents by Judges Sullivan and Menashi attacking the panel's "blocking-date" gloss on TRIA § 201(a), the 17-State amicus brief in *Havlish*, and a real (~20–22%) possibility that the Trump Administration's Solicitor General breaks with the prior Administration on TRIA § 201(a) (just as the Government backed statutory recovery in *Cimex*) keep a plenary grant in lead petition *Havlish* (No. 26-292) genuinely live (~25%), alongside a ~3–4% chance of an SG-recommended GVR across all three dockets if the Executive modifies E.O. 14064 or updates its formal policy toward DAB.
- **Hold-vs.-Consolidate Split for *Ashton* (No. 26-293):** Because *Ashton* deliberately omitted the TRIA blocking-date question from its Question Presented under Rule 14.1(a)—framing only the *Cimex* GVR question while requesting consolidation if another petition is granted—there are two distinct paths if the Court grants plenary review on TRIA:
  1. **Consolidation (~44% of *Havlish* grant worlds -> ~`0.11` plenary `granted`):** Because Nos. 26-292 and 26-293 are formally `Vide` and represent the major 9/11 MDL plaintiff coalitions on a single consolidated judgment, the Court grants both petitions, consolidates them, and limits review to *Havlish*'s TRIA question(s).
  2. **Hold behind *Havlish* (~56% of *Havlish* grant worlds -> ~`0.15` held):** The Court grants *Havlish* (No. 26-292) alone as the clean vehicle and **holds** *Ashton* (No. 26-293). If *Havlish* then succeeds on the merits (~63–65% conditional rate, tempered slightly by 4–4 tie risk on an 8-Justice bench), held petition *Ashton* receives a **post-merits GVR** (~`0.10` unconditional); if *Havlish* is affirmed or splits 4–4 (~35–37%), *Ashton* is **denied** (~`0.05` unconditional).
- Summing ~`0.11` plenary `granted` and ~`0.14` `gvr` (`0.10` post-hold GVR + `0.03` SG policy-update GVR + `0.01` direct *Cimex* GVR) yields **`probability = 0.25`** and **`summary-disposition-route = 0.14 / 0.25 = 0.56`**.
