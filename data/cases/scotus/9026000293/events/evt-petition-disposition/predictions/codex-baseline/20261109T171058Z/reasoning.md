## Case-specific adjustments

**Reasons to remain well above ordinary-petition odds.** The case concerns billions of dollars in Afghan central-bank reserves and more than 3,000 terrorism victims and estates. The panel divided, and the petition identifies dissents from denial of rehearing. Sovereign recognition and the relationship between two federal statutes have significance beyond an individual collection dispute. The actual first relist is a meaningful sign of attention. These facts support a live review possibility, although none proves four votes. Sources: petition pages iv, 2–4; appendix 52a–53a; own snapshot.

**Why I nevertheless reduce the anchor modestly to 22%.** The narrow question specifically requests reconsideration in light of *Exxon Mobil Corp. v. Corporación CIMEX*. I independently checked that precedent through CourtListener: opinion 11346053, docket 24-699, decided June 23, 2026, syllabus and opening majority analysis. Its holding concerns the Helms-Burton Act's abrogation of sovereign immunity for Cuban agencies and instrumentalities, not the effect of recognizing Afghanistan on DAB's status. Extending its statutory method to this case is an argument, not an identical controlling holding.

More importantly, the petition's own appendix expressly says that TRIA overrides FSIA immunity and supplies jurisdiction, but the judgment holders lose because DAB did not qualify as a terrorist party's agency or instrumentality when the assets were blocked. The panel therefore already performed the statutory-displacement inquiry that the petition says was omitted; see appendix 6a–7a and 35a–38a. On my reading, correcting the broad FSIA/recognition premise alone would not necessarily remove the independent TRIA timing barrier. This makes the narrow GVR request less straightforward than the petition's description suggests. This is my vehicle assessment, not a claim that the petitioners' broader arguments have been adjudicated by the Supreme Court.

The Havlish petition can matter because Ashton expressly adopts its TRIA arguments, including the claimed conflict with other circuits. But I have not independently confirmed that conflict or read Havlish's petition, and do not treat advocacy describing it as proof of a square split. `record/related/related.json` classifies both 26-292 and 26-294 as the Court's own **Vide** links, not shared-amicus matches. Their staged snapshots show the same underlying judgment and initial conference; Havlish also has a supplemental-brief motion and a state amicus entry. Neither staged companion shows a grant or hold. Their distribution histories are not updated identically to Ashton's, so I do not infer differing outcomes from that silence. The empty `same-question.json` list adds no identified lead case.
