## Case-Specific Adjustments from the `0.2481` Anchor

Starting from the `0.2481` `elevated` band anchor, I adjust downward by approximately 12 percentage points to **`0.13`** for four concrete reasons grounded in the provisioned filings (`record/documents/petition.txt`, `record/documents/questions-presented.txt`) and the related vided dockets (`record/related/26-292.json` and `record/related/26-294.json`, both carrying `relation = "Vide"`):

1. **Fatal analytical flaw in the *Ashton* petition's GVR theory (`record/documents/petition.txt`, `record/documents/questions-presented.txt`):**
   - Unlike the companion petitions in *Havlish* (No. 26-292, which seeks plenary review of the Second Circuit's TRIA § 201(a) blocking-date rule) and *Owens* (No. 26-294, brought by the Pre-Judgment Plaintiffs who could not invoke TRIA § 201(a) because they lacked a judgment at the time of attachment and therefore lost solely on baseline FSIA immunity), the *Ashton* petitioners (who *are* Judgment Plaintiffs alongside *Havlish*) ask only for a GVR in light of *Exxon Mobil Corp. v. Corporación CIMEX, S.A. (Cuba)*, 146 S. Ct. 1909 (2026), or consolidation if *Havlish* or *Owens* is granted.
   - Specifically, the *Ashton* petition contends (Pet. 8–11) that the Second Circuit panel failed to conduct the textual abrogation analysis required by *Cimex* and failed to ask whether TRIA § 201(a)'s "notwithstanding any other provision of law" clause overrides ordinary FSIA immunity principles.
   - However, reading the Second Circuit's opinion in Appendix A of `petition.txt` (App. 6a–7a, 35a–42a, Part II.C.1, titled *"The TRIA Abrogates Jurisdictional and Execution Immunity and Independently Provides Subject Matter Jurisdiction"*) shows the exact opposite: the panel *did* analyze TRIA § 201(a)'s "notwithstanding any other provision of law" text and **expressly held in the Judgment Plaintiffs' favor** that TRIA § 201(a) supersedes *both* jurisdictional immunity and execution immunity under the FSIA and independently confers subject-matter jurisdiction.
   - The Judgment Plaintiffs (*Havlish* and *Ashton*) lost below solely in Part II.C.2 (App. 42a–50a) because the panel held that to attach blocked assets under TRIA § 201(a), the assets must be "the blocked assets of that terrorist party (including the blocked assets of any agency or instrumentality of that terrorist party)," and that DAB was not an "agency or instrumentality" of the Taliban as of the August 15, 2021 blocking date.
   - Consequently, *Cimex* (which held that Title III of the Helms-Burton Act abrogates FSIA immunity) neither conflicts with the panel's TRIA abrogation holding (which already agreed with petitioners) nor bears on the panel's dispositive TRIA blocking-date holding. Nor did *Cimex* address whether Executive recognition of a foreign state establishes statehood under 28 U.S.C. § 1603(a) (Cuba's status as a foreign state was undisputed in *Cimex*). A standalone GVR under *Lawrence v. Chater*, 516 U.S. 163 (1996), is therefore highly improbable (`P(gvr) = 0.03`).
