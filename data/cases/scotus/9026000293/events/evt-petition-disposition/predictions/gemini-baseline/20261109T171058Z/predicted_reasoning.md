- **Call for the Views of the Solicitor General (`cvsg-increment = 0.08`):** No CVSG has been issued on the docket. A CVSG is unlikely (`P = 0.08`) even though the dispute concerns foreign sovereign immunity and $3.5 billion in blocked Afghan central bank assets: the United States already participated as amicus curiae in the Second Circuit opposing turnover of Da Afghanistan Bank's (DAB's) assets, and the Second Circuit affirmed the denial of turnover. Because the decision below already protects the foreign-policy and central-bank-immunity interests advanced by the Executive Branch, the Court has little need to invite a brief from the Solicitor General before denying review. If the Court does order a CVSG, it would issue following the November 13 or November 20, 2026 conference across all three vided dockets.

## Disposition Route and Questions Presented

The Court will most likely **deny** the petition for a writ of certiorari (`P(denied) = 0.87`, `P(any grant) = 0.13`).

- **Summary disposition vs. plenary review conditional on a grant (`summary-disposition-route = 0.23`):** Even conditional on a grant, plenary review consolidated with No. 26-292 and/or No. 26-294 (`P = 0.77` conditional on grant; `0.10` unconditional) is much more likely than a summary GVR in light of *Exxon Mobil Corp. v. Corporación CIMEX, S.A. (Cuba)*, 146 S. Ct. 1909 (2026) (`P = 0.23` conditional on grant; `0.03` unconditional). Although the *Ashton* petition asks primarily for a GVR in light of *Cimex*, *Cimex* held that Title III of the Helms-Burton Act abrogates FSIA immunity without requiring a separate FSIA exception—and the Second Circuit panel below *already held* in Part II.C.1 of its opinion that Section 201(a) of the Terrorism Risk Insurance Act (TRIA) abrogates both jurisdictional and execution immunity under the FSIA for the Judgment Plaintiffs (*Havlish* and *Ashton*). The Judgment Plaintiffs lost below solely because the panel held in Part II.C.2 that DAB was not an "agency or instrumentality" of the Taliban under TRIA § 201(a) as of the August 15, 2021 asset-blocking date—a TRIA timing question that *Cimex* does not address.
- **Question presented if granted:** If the Court grants plenary review across the vided petitions, it would consolidate No. 26-293 with No. 26-292 (and potentially No. 26-294) and focus on the dispositive TRIA § 201(a) question raised in *Havlish* (No. 26-292)—whether an entity's status as an "agency or instrumentality" of a terrorist party under TRIA § 201(a) is frozen as of the date its assets were initially blocked rather than assessed when the writ of execution or turnover motion is adjudicated—together with the threshold FSIA question whether Executive recognition of a "notional state" of Afghanistan establishes DAB as an agency or instrumentality of a "foreign state" under 28 U.S.C. §§ 1603(a)–(b) and 1609. If granted for plenary review, the Court would likely affirm the judgment below or affirm on the ground that DAB remains the central bank of the recognized State of Afghanistan rather than an agency or instrumentality of the unrecognized Taliban under TRIA § 201(a).
