### 2. Deep Intra-Circuit Fracture Below (Panel Dissents and 5-Judge En Banc Dissents)
- **Divided Panel Decision (`152 F.4th 339`):** On August 26, 2025, a divided Second Circuit panel (Judges Cabranes and Calabresi in the majority; Judge Sullivan concurring in part and dissenting in part) affirmed the Southern District of New York orders denying turnover and attachment of the DAB funds (`petition.txt`, pp. 1a–71a). The panel majority held that:
  1. Because the Executive Branch formally recognizes a "notional state" of Afghanistan (while refusing to recognize the Taliban as its government), Afghanistan is conclusively a "foreign state" under the Foreign Sovereign Immunities Act (FSIA), 28 U.S.C. § 1604, under the President's exclusive recognition power (*Zivotofsky ex rel. Zivotofsky v. Kerry*, 576 U.S. 1 (2015)), and DAB remains an "agency or instrumentality" of that notional state under 28 U.S.C. § 1603(b) based on pre-2021 Afghan central-bank ownership statutes, entitling DAB's funds to execution immunity under 28 U.S.C. § 1609 (`petition.txt`, pp. 24a–36a, 51a).
  2. Although Section 201(a) of the Terrorism Risk Insurance Act of 2002 (TRIA), 28 U.S.C. § 1610 note, abrogates FSIA execution immunity and provides an independent grant of subject-matter jurisdiction over "the blocked assets of [a] terrorist party (including the blocked assets of any agency or instrumentality of that terrorist party)," whether an entity qualifies as an "agency or instrumentality" of a terrorist party under TRIA must be assessed as of the date the assets were initially blocked (August 15, 2021, the day Kabul fell to the Taliban), rather than when turnover is sought (`petition.txt`, pp. 36a–51a). Because the Taliban did not install its loyalists at DAB until August 23, 2021, DAB was not a Taliban instrumentality on August 15, 2021 (`petition.txt`, pp. 49a–50a).
- **Five-Judge En Banc Dissent (`170 F.4th 100`):** On March 19, 2026, the Second Circuit denied rehearing en banc over two forceful dissenting opinions joined by five active circuit judges: Judge Sullivan (joined by Chief Judge Livingston and Judges Bianco and Park) and Judge Menashi (CourtListener `opinion_id=11277807`; `petition.txt`, pp. 72a–74a).
  - Judge Sullivan (joined by Livingston, Bianco, and Park) argued that the panel majority contravened *Republic of Austria v. Altmann*, 541 U.S. 677, 701 (2004), *Samantar v. Yousuf*, 560 U.S. 305, 313 (2010), and Second Circuit precedent (*Klinghoffer*, *Kirschenbaum*) by conflating diplomatic recognition with the judicial task of interpreting "foreign state" and "agency or instrumentality" under the FSIA, and further erred by inventing an extra-textual "time-of-blocking" rule under TRIA § 201(a) that had never been briefed or argued by any party (`petition.txt`, pp. 52a–63a; CourtListener `opinion_id=11277807`, chunks 0–4).
  - Judge Menashi agreed with the panel majority that the President's recognition of Afghanistan as a foreign state is conclusive on the courts, but agreed with Judge Sullivan that (1) DAB is no longer an "agency or instrumentality" of the recognized State of Afghanistan under 28 U.S.C. § 1603(b) because the Taliban captured and controls DAB in present political reality (*Bartlett v. Baasiri*, 81 F.4th 28, 33 (2d Cir. 2023)), and (2) under TRIA § 201(a), an entity's status as an agency or instrumentality of a terrorist party must be evaluated at the time of the turnover order, not the date of blocking (CourtListener `opinion_id=11277807`, chunks 4–10).

### 3. Absence of a Circuit Split and Analytical Weakness of the *Cimex* GVR Argument
- **Sui Generis Factual Context Without a Circuit Conflict:** Despite the sharp intra-circuit division in the Second Circuit, neither Question Presented implicates a genuine split among the federal courts of appeals. No other circuit has addressed whether a central bank captured by an unrecognized terrorist regime remains an "agency or instrumentality" of the recognized state under 28 U.S.C. § 1603(b), and no other circuit has squarely decided whether TRIA § 201(a)'s "agency or instrumentality" status is measured at the time of asset blocking versus the time of execution when a terrorist group captures a state bank *after* an emergency blocking order.
- **Why *Exxon Mobil Corp. v. Corporación Cimex, S.A. (Cuba)* Does Not Warrant a GVR:** Unlike the companion petitions (`26-292` and `26-294`), the *Ashton* petition (`26-293`) devotes Question Presented 2 to requesting either plenary review or a summary grant, vacatur, and remand (GVR) in light of *Exxon Mobil Corp. v. Corporación Cimex, S.A. (Cuba)*, 609 U.S. ___ (June 23, 2026) (No. `24-699`) (`questions-presented.txt`; `petition.txt`, pp. ii, 14–16, 30–36). In *Cimex*, the Supreme Court held 6–3 (per Justice Kavanaugh) that Title III of the Helms-Burton Act itself abrogates the foreign sovereign immunity of Cuban agencies and instrumentalities, so plaintiffs suing under Title III need not also satisfy an exception to immunity in the FSIA (CourtListener `opinion_id=11346053`, chunks 0–1). However, the Second Circuit panel majority in *Havlish/Ashton* **already held** that:
  - "(5) The Terrorism Risk Insurance Act abrogates any jurisdictional or execution immunity granted by the Foreign Sovereign Immunities Act; [and] (6) The Terrorism Risk Insurance Act provides district courts with an independent basis to exercise subject matter jurisdiction over the blocked assets of a terrorist party's agency or instrumentality" (`petition.txt`, p. 51a).
  - The Second Circuit also explicitly declined to import the FSIA's definition or timing test of "agency or instrumentality" into TRIA (`petition.txt`, pp. 44a–47a). Rather, the panel denied relief under TRIA solely on its statutory construction of TRIA § 201(a)'s phrase "blocked assets of that terrorist party (including the blocked assets of any agency or instrumentality of that terrorist party)" as requiring instrumentality status at the date of blocking (`petition.txt`, pp. 47a–50a). Because the Second Circuit already recognized that TRIA independently abrogates FSIA immunity, *Cimex* does not undermine the panel's dispositive TRIA holding, making a GVR in light of *Cimex* unlikely.
- **Executive Branch Foreign-Policy Opposition:** As reflected in the record below, the Executive Branch froze DAB's $7 billion in reserves under IEEPA (Exec. Order 14,064), licensed the transfer of $3.5 billion to the Swiss-based "Fund for the People of Afghanistan," and filed Statements of Interest in the district court asserting that DAB is an agency or instrumentality of the State of Afghanistan whose central-bank reserves are immune under the FSIA (`petition.txt`, pp. 7a–11a; CourtListener `opinion_id=11277807`, chunk 6 n.1–3). That posture makes the Solicitor General unlikely to support certiorari if invited to file a brief (CVSG) and counsels judicial caution at the cert stage.
