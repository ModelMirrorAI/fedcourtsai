# Forecast of the Court's action

The Court will most likely deny the petition. Any grant, including a grant that sends the case back without full argument, has a 28% probability.

## Procedural course

The observed state is **two distributions and one completed relist**, with no CVSG. The November 9 distribution already schedules the November 13, 2026 conference; it is not a future increment. I put a further distribution beyond that state at 48%, so the narrow modal call is no additional relist. If another occurs, one or two more are the most likely short trajectory. Absent a government invitation or a companion hold, disposition is most likely during November or December 2026. A CVSG would instead move resolution well into 2027. Timing is contextual rather than a scored claim.

The Court is more likely not to seek the Solicitor General's views: a future invitation has 22% probability. If one issues, it will likely accompany action on the related petitions in a forthcoming conference cycle, reflecting the federal interest in frozen Afghan reserves and recognition policy.

The Court will likely handle 26-292, 26-293, and 26-294 together procedurally. The most likely denial will be unsigned and unexplained. Conditional on denial, there is a 20% chance of a noted dissent or statement respecting denial. If a Justice writes, Thomas or Alito is a more likely author than the Chief Justice, but no named writing is the central prediction; joining a writing is not authorship.

## Conditional grant route and scope

Conditional on any grant, a disposition in the cert order is more likely than full briefing and argument, at 65%. A GVR, rather than a summary merits reversal, is the principal summary route because the petition specifically requests reconsideration under the intervening *CIMEX* decision. Plenary review remains a meaningful alternative if the Court takes the related TRIA timing dispute and consolidates this petition or includes its recognition question.

If the Court takes this petition's own question, it will most likely address whether *CIMEX* warrants reconsideration of the FSIA recognition framework, rather than treat the petition as a freestanding request to award the funds. If it instead takes the companion timing issue, relief here could follow that vehicle rather than a decision accepting this petition's characterization of the panel.

If plenary review reaches the recognition issue, the likely doctrinal dispute will distinguish the Executive's recognition of a state from courts' application of the FSIA's statutory classifications. But denial produces no majority opinion, and a GVR would leave the substantive result for the lower court to reconsider. Neither route entails immediate turnover of DAB's assets to petitioners.
