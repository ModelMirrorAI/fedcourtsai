## Why the petition is stronger than routine cert, but not a likely grant

The petition, printed pp. i–ii and 2–12, expressly asks for a GVR in light of the intervening *Exxon Mobil Corp. v. Corporación CIMEX, S.A. (Cuba)* decision, with consolidation if a companion receives plenary review. Its political-branch/judicial-role issue, divided panel and rehearing objections, exceptional stakes, and genuine relist keep review meaningfully live. I move modestly upward from the 24.81% anchor rather than applying the whole-docket rate.

The strongest contrary evidence is in the petition's own appendix. The petition argues that the panel failed to examine whether TRIA overrides ordinary FSIA immunity. But the appended majority opinion, App. 35a–40a, expressly performs that inquiry and holds that TRIA overrides both execution and jurisdictional immunity and supplies an independent jurisdictional basis. App. 46a–51a then makes the blocking-date test decisive: DAB did not satisfy the terrorist-instrumentality test when the assets were frozen. App. 34a n.97 also distinguishes the Executive's recognition of Afghanistan from the judicial determination of DAB's statutory instrumentality status. The opinion is therefore less susceptible to the petition's methodological criticism than its introductory characterization suggests.

For the intervening precedent, I consulted only the Supreme Court's official *CIMEX* opinion, No. 24-699, June 18, 2026, syllabus and opinion pp. 4–8, including p. 5 n.2. It concerns Helms–Burton Title III's displacement of FSIA **jurisdictional** immunity, not recognition of states; footnote 2 expressly separates execution immunity. I infer that it does not itself displace this case's TRIA blocking-date ground. This is a reason to discount the requested GVR, not a claim that the Court has adjudicated this petition. The companion's alleged timing split could still bring this petition along, but the companion briefs and cited circuit decisions are not before me, so I do not treat the alleged split as independently verified.

My 28% is judgmental, not the output of an estimated feature model. It balances genuine conference interest, interrelated petitions, and an intervening precedent against the weak fit of that precedent to the operative ground below. A denial remains substantially more probable than relief.
