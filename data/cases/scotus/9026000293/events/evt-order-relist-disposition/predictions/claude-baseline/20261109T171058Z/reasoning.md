## Adjustments
- **Up:** The QP is a narrow GVR ask tied to a fresh decision of this Court, and GVRs are cheap for the Court. The case concerns about $3.5B in DAB reserves and 9/11 victims, with obvious federal foreign-relations interests (EO 14064, the Afghan Fund). That makes a **CVSG** a realistic next step, and a CVSG carries roughly a 35% grant-family rate in the CVSG cut. The coordinated relist of all three petitions suggests the Court is engaged rather than processing routinely.
- **Down:** No response exists, and the Court almost never grants plenary review without one. The respondents are in default, so a plenary grant needs either a call for a response or a CVSG first, and that lowers the near-term odds. The TRIA blocking-date holding may independently support the judgment, which weakens the case for a *Cimex* GVR. Executive non-recognition and foreign-policy sensitivity also push toward caution.
- **Rough decomposition:**
  - CVSG (~0.30) followed by an eventual grant or GVR (~0.4): about 0.12.
  - A *Cimex* GVR without CVSG, possibly after holding for Havlish: about 0.13.
  - A plenary grant via a call for response: about 0.05.
  - Total: about 0.30, a little above the anchor.

## Uncertainty
I did not read the full *Cimex* opinion and rely on the petition's characterization of it. I cannot tell why the Court relisted. It may have been the pending Havlish supplemental-brief motion, a reschedule, or real interest. The relist and band pools also count reschedules, so they read low for a petition the Court truly relisted. Retrieval was light: one corpus query and one web search.
