The Supreme Court will probably turn down this appeal, with roughly a one-in-four chance (23%) of granting review or sending the case back to the lower court. Families of September 11 victims seek roughly $3.5 billion in frozen Afghan central-bank funds, and the Justices have asked both the opposing side and the Solicitor General, the federal government's top courtroom lawyer, to weigh in. Still, the government will likely oppose review to protect presidential foreign-policy authority and central-bank reserves, and this specific filing relies on a weak follow-up argument to a companion appeal.
