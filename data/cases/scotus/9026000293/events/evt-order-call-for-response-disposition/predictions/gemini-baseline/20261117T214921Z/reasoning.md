## 2. Case-Specific Adjustments Below the ~35% Anchor

Three structural factors push `probability` for *Ashton* (No. 26-293) meaningfully below the ~35% CVSG baseline, to **0.23**:

1. **The Solicitor General is substantially likely (~78%) to recommend denial.**
   Although at least four of the eight participating Justices wanted the Executive Branch's views — understandably given $3.5 billion in blocked reserves, thousands of 9/11 and 1998 embassy-bombing victims, and a sharp 5-judge en banc dissent in the Second Circuit (Judge Sullivan joined by Chief Judge Livingston and Judges Bianco and Park, plus Judge Menashi dissenting separately) — the institutional equities of the United States point strongly toward opposing certiorari:
   - The Second Circuit's primary FSIA holding (endorsed at the en banc stage by Judges Cabranes, Calabresi, Nardini, Lohier, and Menashi) entrenches *conclusive judicial deference* to the President's exclusive Article II power to recognize foreign states (*Zivotofsky v. Kerry*) — a separation-of-powers victory the Solicitor General has strong institutional reason to preserve.
   - As noted in Judge Menashi's en banc dissent (`170 F.4th 100`, slip op. at 8 nn.1–3), the United States previously filed Statements of Interest in the SDNY explicitly asserting that DAB is an agency or instrumentality of the recognized State of Afghanistan entitled to FSIA execution immunity. And as the panel noted (Pet. App. 23a n.67), the State Department under the current Administration continued in March 2025 to list Afghanistan as an independent recognized state while refusing to recognize the Taliban.
   - Allowing private judgment creditors to attach ~$3.5 billion in foreign central-bank reserves held at the FRBNY threatens both diplomatic leverage over Afghan assets and foreign central banks' confidence in holding reserves at the New York Fed under 28 U.S.C. § 1611(b)(1).
   - There is **no genuine circuit split** on either issue: prior cases examining international-law statehood criteria (*Ungar*, *Knox*, *Klinghoffer*) involved unrecognized Palestine/PLO rather than overriding an affirmative Presidential recognition, and the Second Circuit's TRIA "blocking date" timing rule addressed a question of first impression created by the simultaneous August 15, 2021 fall of Kabul and OFAC freeze. When the SG recommends denial in a splitless foreign-affairs CVSG, the Court follows that recommendation roughly 80%–85%+ of the time.

2. ***Ashton*'s standalone *Cimex* GVR premise is doctrinally flawed, making No. 26-293 an inferior tag-along vehicle.**
   Reading *Exxon Mobil Corp. v. Corporación CIMEX, S.A. (Cuba)*, No. 24-699 (U.S. June 23, 2026), directly alongside the Second Circuit's opinion exposes a critical flaw in *Ashton*'s sole standalone Question Presented. *Cimex* held (6–3, per Justice Kavanaugh) that Title III of the Helms-Burton Act itself abrogates Cuban instrumentalities' FSIA immunity without requiring an FSIA exception. Yet in Part II.C.1 of the opinion below (Pet. App. 36a–42a), the Second Circuit panel majority **already held in petitioners' favor** that TRIA § 201(a)'s "notwithstanding" clause abrogates both jurisdictional and execution immunity under the FSIA and independently confers subject-matter jurisdiction. Judgment creditors lost below not because the panel failed to treat TRIA as overriding the FSIA, nor because *Cimex* said anything new about who defines a recognized "foreign state" (Cuba's statehood was undisputed in *Cimex*), but because in Part II.C.2 (Pet. App. 42a–56a) the panel held that DAB was not yet an "agency or instrumentality" of the Taliban on the August 15, 2021 blocking date. *Ashton* relegated that dispositive TRIA blocking-date issue to a one-sentence footnote joining *Havlish*'s arguments (Pet. 5 n.2). Thus *Ashton* has virtually no standalone *Cimex* GVR traction; its fate rides almost entirely on whether the Court grants plenary review on *Havlish* (No. 26-292) and/or *Owens* (No. 26-294). Even if the Court grants plenary review in *Havlish*/*Owens*, a nontrivial fraction (~20%–22%) of those cluster-grant worlds result in *Ashton* being *held* rather than consolidated and then *denied* if the Second Circuit's judgment is affirmed on the merits.

3. **Justice Kagan's recusal leaves an 8-Justice Court on Nos. 26-292 and 26-293.**
   Because Justice Kagan is recused in *Ashton* and *Havlish* (though not in *Owens*, No. 26-294), granting requires 4 of 8 participating Justices (50%) and carries a real risk of a 4–4 affirmance by an equally divided Court on novel FSIA/TRIA questions.
