The Court will most likely **deny** the petition for a writ of certiorari after receiving the response requested for December 17, 2026, and the invited amicus brief of the Solicitor General.

- **Further distributions (relists):** The docket currently records **two** distributions (for the November 6, 2026 and November 13, 2026 Conferences), followed by a CVSG order on November 16, 2026 and a Call for Response on November 17, 2026 across all three companion petitions (*Havlish*, No. 26-292; *Ashton*, No. 26-293; and *Owens*, No. 26-294). Unless petitioners voluntarily dismiss the petition under Rule 46 while the CVSG is pending, the petition is virtually certain to be **distributed at least once more** once the response stage closes and the Solicitor General files the brief for the United States (likely in late spring 2027), with one to two post-CVSG Conference distributions before disposition (or further distributions if held behind a companion grant).
- **CVSG:** The docket already records a CVSG issued on November 16, 2026, so no initial CVSG remains to be called. The Solicitor General's invited brief will likely recommend denying certiorari across all three petitions to preserve Executive Branch recognition prerogatives, protect foreign central-bank reserves held at the Federal Reserve Bank of New York, and avoid plenary review of a splitless, sui generis post-2021 Afghanistan record.
- **Question presented and merits posture (if granted):** If the Court grants plenary review of the Second Circuit's judgment, it will not adopt *Ashton*'s standalone *Exxon Mobil Corp. v. Corporación CIMEX* framing as written. Instead, the Court would center merits review on the comprehensive statutory questions presented in vided companion *Havlish* (No. 26-292) (and/or *Owens*, No. 26-294, where Justice Kagan is not recused): (1) whether Da Afghanistan Bank ("DAB"), now controlled by the unrecognized Taliban, qualifies as an "agency or instrumentality" of a recognized "foreign state" entitled to execution immunity under 28 U.S.C. §§ 1603(b) and 1609, and (2) whether Section 201(a) of the Terrorism Risk Insurance Act ("TRIA") requires assessing an entity's status as an "agency or instrumentality" of a terrorist party on the date assets were blocked rather than when attachment or turnover is sought. If the Court reaches the merits, the likeliest ground for disturbing the judgment below would be rejecting the Second Circuit's extra-textual "blocking date" limitation under TRIA § 201(a) (and/or holding that Taliban-controlled DAB fails § 1603(b)'s present-tense ownership-or-organ test) without questioning the President's exclusive Article II recognition authority.
- **Summary disposition vs. plenary review (conditional on a grant):** Conditional on *Ashton* receiving any grant, a cert-order **GVR** is slightly likelier than consolidating *Ashton* for plenary briefing and oral argument. A standalone GVR in light of *Cimex* is unlikely because the Second Circuit already held that TRIA § 201(a) abrogates FSIA immunity. However, because *Ashton* frames only a *Cimex* GVR question in its text and incorporates *Havlish*'s dispositive TRIA challenge solely by footnote, there is a substantial chance that if the Court grants plenary review in *Havlish* (or *Owens*), it holds *Ashton* rather than consolidating it and then GVRs *Ashton* if the Second Circuit's judgment is reversed or vacated — supplemented by a small chance of a coordinated post-CVSG GVR across all three dockets.
- **Separate writings on the order:** Should the Court deny certiorari, the order will note that Justice Kagan took no part in the consideration or decision of the petition. Most likely no separate writing will accompany the denial, though there is a meaningful (~11%) chance that one or more Justices — most plausibly Justice Thomas, Justice Gorsuch, or Justice Alito — author a dissent from or statement respecting the denial criticizing the Second Circuit's TRIA "blocking date" rule or its treatment of Taliban-controlled DAB under the FSIA.
