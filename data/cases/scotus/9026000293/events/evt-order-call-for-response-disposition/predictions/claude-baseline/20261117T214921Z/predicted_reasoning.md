# Forecast — Ashton v. The Taliban, No. 26-293

The docket shows two distributions (conferences of Nov 6 and Nov 13, 2026), a CVSG on Nov 16, and a call for a response on Nov 17 (due Dec 17, 2026).

- **Disposition.** The Court will most likely deny this petition in the end (about 68%), with the denial coming on the same order list as its siblings, Havlish (26-292) and Owens (26-294). The three will be handled together throughout.
- **Timing.** Nothing happens until the Solicitor General files. The invited brief will arrive in roughly May 2027 (possibly December 2027 if it slips), and the petition will be distributed again after it. A response from the absent respondents may never be filed; the time will simply run.
- **Further distributions.** At least one more distribution is nearly certain; expect one or two after the SG's brief.
- **CVSG.** Already issued; no second invitation.
- **Solicitor General.** Expect the SG to recommend denial, defending the Executive's recognition authority and pointing to the absence of a square conflict on the FSIA question; the Court will follow it more likely than not.
- **If the cluster is granted.** The Court would take Havlish as the lead vehicle, on the TRIA "agency or instrumentality" timing question and possibly the FSIA "foreign state" question. Ashton would then be either granted and consolidated or, more likely, held and disposed of after the Havlish decision — a GVR if the judgment below is disturbed. A stand-alone GVR in light of *Cimex*, the only relief this petition's question presented asks for, is unlikely (a few percent): *Cimex* does not speak directly to who decides foreign-state status.
- **Route if granted.** A cert-order disposition (GVR) is somewhat likelier than plenary briefing for this docket, about 60/40.
- **Separate writings.** On a denial, no separate writing is expected (about 8% that some Justice notes a dissent or statement, most plausibly Thomas, Alito or Gorsuch). Justice Kagan will remain recused.
