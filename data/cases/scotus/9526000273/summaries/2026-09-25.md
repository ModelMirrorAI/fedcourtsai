## What happened

Stacey Ian Humphreys, a Georgia death row prisoner, sued in Fulton County Superior Court to stop a member of the state parole board from taking part in his clemency proceedings because of a conflict of interest; that board member had been part of the prosecution team at his trial and joined the board the day his execution warrant was signed. The court agreed a conflict existed and initially ruled clemency required five board members, but later reversed itself, holding a smaller quorum was legally sufficient. Georgia's Supreme Court declined review. Humphreys then asked the U.S. Supreme Court to hear his case, arguing this left him with fewer voting board members than any past applicant, and asked for a stay of his execution while that request was considered.

## What the Court is being asked

The application asked the Court to pause the execution temporarily so it could first decide whether to take up Humphreys's underlying claim: whether Georgia violated his rights to fair treatment and equal treatment under the law by letting a reduced, differently-composed parole board decide his clemency request, using a procedure no earlier applicant had faced.

## Where it stands

After the application for a stay was fully briefed, with both sides filing responses, Humphreys withdrew the stay application on September 25, 2026, and the docket shows it as withdrawn.
