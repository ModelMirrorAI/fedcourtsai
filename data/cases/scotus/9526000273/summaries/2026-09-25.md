## In brief

A death row inmate asked the Court to pause his execution while it considered his appeal, then withdrew that request.

## What happened

Stacey Ian Humphreys, sentenced to death in Georgia, sued in Fulton County Superior Court arguing that a member of the state's Board of Pardons and Paroles had a conflict of interest and should be barred from voting on his clemency application. The court agreed the member was conflicted but ultimately ruled that only a quorum, not all five board members, was legally required to decide clemency, leaving the case to proceed with four members. The Georgia Supreme Court denied review. Humphreys then asked the U.S. Supreme Court to hear the case, arguing the procedure violated his rights to due process and equal protection, and asked for a stay of his September 16, 2026 execution while that petition was considered.

## What the Court is being asked

The application asked the Court to pause the execution temporarily so it would have time to decide whether to hear Humphreys's underlying appeal, which argues that letting fewer than five board members decide his clemency bid, after one member was found conflicted, treated him unfairly compared to other applicants and denied him a fair process.

## Where it stands

Georgia filed an opposition and Humphreys filed a reply, but on September 25, 2026, Humphreys's counsel filed a letter withdrawing the stay application, and the docket records the application as withdrawn that same day.

## What each outcome would mean

Humphreys, as the applicant, had asked the Court to pause his execution. Because he withdrew the request, the Court did not grant or deny a stay, and no order paused the execution on this application. The underlying petition asking the Court to hear his broader constitutional challenge, filed separately, is not resolved by this withdrawal and continues on its own track before the Court.
