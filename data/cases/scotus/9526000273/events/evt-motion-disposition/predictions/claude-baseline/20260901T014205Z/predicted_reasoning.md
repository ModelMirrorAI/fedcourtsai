# Forecast: application 26A273 (Humphreys v. Georgia Board of Pardons and Paroles)

Application for a stay of execution pending certiorari, filed August 28, 2026 and
submitted to Justice Thomas; the state respondents filed an opposition, uninvited,
on August 31. The record I forecast from shows: no response requested by the Court,
no referral to the full Court, zero amicus briefs.

**Disposition.** The Court will **deny** the application, most likely in a single
unexplained order. The application rides a state civil suit against the Georgia
Board of Pardons and Paroles (Fulton County Superior Court, 25CV017069), which
reads as a challenge to Georgia's clemency process. Under *Ohio Adult Parole
Authority v. Woodard*, clemency proceedings carry only minimal due-process
protection, so the certiorari prospect the stay standard requires is weak, and the
Court's recent practice on eleventh-hour execution stays is stringent — every
capital stay application in the corpus's recent window (Silvia 26A209, Hines
26A175, Duckett 26A119, Occhicone 26A121, Sochor 26A54) was denied. P(unqualified
grant) = **0.03**.

**Referral.** Justice Thomas will **refer the application to the full Court**
rather than act alone — that is the near-universal modern practice for capital
execution stays, whose disposition orders read "presented to Justice Thomas and by
him referred to the Court." P(referral after prediction time) = **0.90**.

**Response request.** The Court will **not** call for a response: the state has
already opposed voluntarily, so there is nothing left to request, and the execution
timeline leaves no room for one. P(request after prediction time) = **0.02**.

**Amicus briefs.** None expected. The claim is a fact-bound clemency-procedure
challenge with days to disposition, not the kind of application that draws
organized amicus attention. P(count rises past 0) = **0.05**.

**Timing** (unscored, but it disciplines the rest): with the opposition filed
August 31 and the linked petition 26-5211 pending, expect disposition within days —
almost certainly before or on the scheduled execution date, likely the first week
of September 2026.

Should the Court deny, a noted dissent or statement from one or more of the
Justices who regularly write in capital cases (Justice Sotomayor, Justice Jackson)
is possible but not the likeliest outcome; most capital stay denials issue without
writing. If the Court instead granted, the likely form would be a stay pending the
disposition of 26-5211, signalling at least four Justices see a cert-worthy
question in the clemency challenge — a shape the record gives little reason to
expect.
