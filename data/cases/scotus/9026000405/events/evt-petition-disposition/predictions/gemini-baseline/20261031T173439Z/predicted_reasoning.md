## Question Presented and Conditional Route

- **Question Presented (if granted):** The petition presents a single question: whether the Federal Employees' Compensation Act (FECA), 5 U.S.C. § 8101 *et seq.*, requires a federal district court to defer to the Secretary of Labor to determine FECA coverage over a federal employee's Federal Tort Claims Act (FTCA) suit when the employee alleges neither "disability," "death," nor injury "sustained while in the performance of his duty" under 5 U.S.C. § 8102(a). If the Court were to grant certiorari, it would take that single question presented, likely reformulating it to address whether FECA's exclusive-liability and administrative-finality provisions (5 U.S.C. §§ 8116(c), 8128(b), 8145) require federal courts to stay or dismiss an FTCA action whenever a "substantial question" of FECA coverage exists.
- **Plenary Review vs. Summary Disposition (`summary-disposition-route`: 0.15):** Conditional on the unlikely event of a grant, the Court would almost certainly set the case for plenary merits briefing and oral argument (~85% conditional probability) rather than disposing of it summarily in the cert order (~15% conditional probability). There is no pending Supreme Court case or intervening decision on FECA that would trigger a routine grant, vacate, and remand (GVR), and the Court would not summarily reverse a sixty-year-old consensus across ten circuits (*Daniels-Lumley v. United States*, 306 F.2d 769 (D.C. Cir. 1962)) without full merits briefing. A GVR would occur only if, after a Call for Response, the Solicitor General modified the government's position on FECA exhaustion or scope.
- **Conditional Merits Ground (if granted):** If granted and argued on the merits, the Court would likely scrutinize whether the judge-made "substantial question" primary-jurisdiction doctrine comports with federal courts' obligation to adjudicate their own subject-matter jurisdiction under the FTCA (*Arbaugh v. Y&H Corp.*, 546 U.S. 500 (2006)), while distinguishing between pure statutory scope-of-coverage questions (such as whether non-disabling emotional harm is a covered injury category) and fact-bound "performance of duty" determinations committed to the Secretary of Labor under 5 U.S.C. §§ 8128(b) and 8145.
