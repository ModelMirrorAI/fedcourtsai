# Forecast: He v. Rubio, No. 26-405

**Disposition: denied, at the first conference (November 6, 2026).**

**Relists.** The docket shows one distribution (for the 11/6/2026 conference). The respondents (the United States, through the Solicitor General) waived their right to respond, and nobody has called for a response. I expect no further distribution: the petition will be denied without a response on its first conference. The only plausible route to another distribution is a Justice asking for a response. I put that at about 5%, because the petition alleges a circuit split and the federal government is the respondent.

**CVSG.** None. The United States is already a party, so the Court has no reason to invite the Solicitor General's views.

**Question, if granted.** The Court would take the single QP essentially as written: whether a district court must send a federal employee's FTCA claim to the Secretary of Labor whenever FECA coverage is a "substantial question," or whether the court decides FECA's scope itself (the Ninth Circuit's *Moe*/*Figueroa* approach). It would probably restate it more neutrally as a question about the substantial-question doctrine.

**Summary disposition.** Unlikely. No intervening decision supports a GVR, and the government won below, so a summary reversal in the government's favor is impossible and one against it is improbable.

**Dissent from denial.** I expect no separate writing. If one appears at all, it is most likely from Justice Gorsuch, who has written about agency displacement of judicial jurisdiction.
