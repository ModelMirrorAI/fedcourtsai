## In brief

A former State Department employee asks the Court to decide who—courts or the executive branch—gets to rule first on whether a federal pay-and-benefits law blocks his lawsuit.

## What happened

Gengshu Scott He, a former State Department employee, and his family sued the government, alleging that a State Department law enforcement officer harassed He and then, during a confrontation at the family's Virginia home, assaulted him and frightened his family. The district court dismissed He's own tort claims, ruling that the Department of Labor had to decide first whether a federal workers' compensation law covered them; once He chose not to pursue that route, the court dismissed those claims entirely. It separately dismissed the family's assault claim for failing to state a legal claim. The court of appeals affirmed the dismissal of He's claims but revived the family's assault claim.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked whether a federal employee who sues the government for a personal injury, but does not claim disability, death, or an on-the-job injury, must still let a federal agency decide first whether a workers' compensation law covers his claim before a court can hear it. The petition says federal appeals courts disagree on how much deference courts owe the agency on this question.

## Where it stands

The petition was filed in September 2026, the government waived its right to respond, and the case was distributed for the Court's November 6, 2026 conference. The docket shows no action since that distribution.

## What each outcome would mean

He and his family, as petitioners, are asking the Court to take up the case and rule that courts, not the Department of Labor, should decide whether the compensation law bars his claims. If the Court grants the petition, it will hear the case and decide that question; a grant alone would not decide who wins. If the Court denies the petition, the appeals court's ruling stands, meaning He's claims remain dismissed unless he pursues the agency process, while his family's revived assault claim continues in the lower courts regardless.
