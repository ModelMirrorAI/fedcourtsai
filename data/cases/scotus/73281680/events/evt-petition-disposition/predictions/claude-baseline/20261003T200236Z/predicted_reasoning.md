# Forecast: what the Court will do with No. 25-1154, Fofana v. Mullin

**Disposition.** The petition will be **denied**. P(any grant) = 0.08.

**Relists.** The docket shows **one** distribution, for the Conference of
October 9, 2026, and no relist. Forecasting from that state: the most likely
path is a denial on the order list following that conference. I give a 0.30
chance of at least one further distribution — the question presented is a
genuine open one that the Court itself reserved in *Bouarfa v. Mayorkas*
(2024), the Solicitor General filed a brief in opposition rather than waiving,
and one or two Justices who dissented in *Patel v. Garland* (2022) may want a
look or a writing. If it is relisted, expect one relist, not a string of them.

**CVSG.** None. The United States is the respondent, so there is no Solicitor
General to invite; P(CVSG after prediction time) = 0.01.

**Which question, if taken.** If the Court grants, it will take the clean
jurisdictional question the Eighth Circuit decided: whether
8 U.S.C. § 1252(a)(2)(B)(ii) strips district-court jurisdiction to review a
non-discretionary eligibility determination (here, that the applicant is
inadmissible on terrorism-related grounds) that the Secretary must make before
exercising the discretion § 1159(b) confers to adjust an asylee's status. It
would not take any fact-bound question about ULIMO or Fofana's knowledge, and
it would not reach the merits of the inadmissibility finding. If granted, the
likely ground is textual: whether "any other decision or action ... the
authority for which is specified ... to be in the discretion of" the Secretary
reaches threshold determinations the way *Patel* read "any judgment regarding"
in clause (i); the Court's recent trajectory (*Patel*, *Bouarfa*, and
*Mullin v. Doe* (June 2026)) points toward affirmance if it did take the case.

**Summary disposition.** Unlikely. Conditional on a grant, P(disposition in
the cert order itself) = 0.10: no intervening decision favours the petitioner
(*Mullin v. Doe* cuts the government's way), so there is nothing to GVR in
light of, and a per curiam reversal of a published, carefully reasoned
Colloton opinion is implausible. A grant would mean plenary review.

**Dissent from denial.** Probably none; P(some Justice notes a dissent from or
statement respecting denial | denial) = 0.10. The closest precedent, *Shaiban
v. Jaddou*, No. 24-183 (same statute, same asylee-adjustment posture, same
terrorism bar), was held for *Bouarfa* and then denied in January 2025 without
a noted dissent. If anyone writes, the likeliest voices are Justice Gorsuch
(who dissented in *Patel* on the presumption of reviewability) or Justice
Sotomayor or Jackson, as a statement rather than a dissent.
