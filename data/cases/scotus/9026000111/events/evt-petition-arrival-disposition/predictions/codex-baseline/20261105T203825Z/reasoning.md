## Reasons favoring review

1. **The issue is legal and preserved.** The question presented asks whether Bivens extends to excessive force during execution of an arrest warrant outside a home. The petition, pp. 3–5 and 15, and appendix, pp. 1a–3a and 58a–73a, identify district-court rejection of the action and appellate reversal on this threshold issue. Factual disputes over the force used need not be resolved to decide whether a damages cause of action exists. The failure-to-intervene claim was rejected below and is not the question presented.
2. **The split is more than counsel's unsupported assertion.** The petition, pp. 12–15, identifies Snowden, Logsdon, Byrd, Mejia, and Orellana. The appended majority and Lynch dissent expressly dispute the significance of warrants, public locations, and alternative remedies. CourtListener's pre-petition First Circuit opinion, docket 23-1618, opinion ID 11121515, independently confirms acknowledgment of conflicting circuit approaches. The split is not a clean tally of identical outcomes: Logsdon rejected relief on other grounds, and the majority distinguishes some opposing cases by officer category or legal mandate. Nonetheless, there is a real recurring disagreement about the new-context test.
3. **The current doctrinal direction favors scrutiny.** The petition's discussion of Egbert, Abbasi, and Goldey emphasizes the Court's resistance to extending implied damages actions. The First Circuit majority responds that this is an existing Bivens context rather than an extension; its appendix discussion of Goldey makes that distinction explicit. I treat the government's reading as an argument with force, not a proven merits conclusion. No external official-opinion text was successfully returned by the attempted web retrievals, so this assessment rests on the staged filing and lower-court material.
4. **There is a concrete hold-and-remand path.** Petition pp. 15–16 requests a hold for Nielsen v. Watanabe, No. 25-417, which was granted June 22, 2026 according to the petition. A targeted corpus lookup independently returned that grant, with no merits decision or judgment recorded. Its later merits-brief metadata is permissible forward information but adds little to this forecast. Nielsen concerns the Carlson/Eighth Amendment context, not the Fourth Amendment arrest setting. Its treatment of alternative remedies and what counts as a new context could nevertheless justify reconsideration here without another full merits proceeding.

## Reasons for restraint

This is an appellate remand rather than the end of the damages litigation. Further proceedings, including previously disputed qualified immunity, can reduce the Court's appetite for immediate intervention, even though the cause-of-action question itself is legal. The majority gives a substantial explanation for treating ordinary DEA arrest-force allegations as within Bivens itself. The petition acknowledges that review was denied in Snowden in 2024, demonstrating that an asserted warrant/location split does not compel a grant. Finally, Nielsen might be decided narrowly on prison-specific grounds and provide no useful basis for a GVR here. The absence of an opposition limits assessment of vehicle objections and the government's characterization of the split. These considerations leave 28% on non-grant outcomes and keep the forecast below a near-certain government win.

## Procedural claims and stakes

The paid-segment statpack's terminal relist buckets have resolved n=10,204, 2,532, 495, and 482 for 0, 1, 2, and 3+ respectively. Their grant-family shares are approximately 1.8%, 13.5%, 41.2%, and 37.1%. These are terminal distributions, not arrival hazards; I do not use the zero bucket as the arrival anchor. Here `relist-increment` compares the resolution count against **zero**, so **0.99** principally forecasts a first distribution, not a 99% chance of a genuine relist. I separately expect a hold to make a later redistribution plausible, but no long run of repeated active conferences is necessary.

The CVSG cut shows grant-family shares of 6.4% without a call (resolved n=13,549) and 34.8% with one (resolved n=164), again terminal conditioning rather than an estimated forward call probability. Because the Solicitor General already advocates the petition, a further invitation to the same office has little purpose: **P(new CVSG)=0.005**. The amicus-zero bucket has resolved n=11,109 and a 3.8% grant-family share; I do not make an independent downward adjustment from that terminal, marginal comparison at this early filing moment. No unknown amicus bucket is displayed in this pack.

Conditional on denial, **P(a noted dissent or statement)=0.08**; silence is the strong modal prediction. The unconditional `order-writers` numbers allow conservative concern about preserving the narrowed Bivens boundary on denial, and liberal concern about restricting remedies on a summary grant. Accordingly, they are not per-Justice probabilities conditional on denial. The forecast of no writing in the modal order is compatible with these small marginal probabilities.

The **0.70 stakes score** measures the significance of remedies for unconstitutional federal arrests and geographic variation in their availability, not the grant probability. The petition's precise warrant/public-location boundary is narrower than a direct demand to overrule every Bivens remedy.
