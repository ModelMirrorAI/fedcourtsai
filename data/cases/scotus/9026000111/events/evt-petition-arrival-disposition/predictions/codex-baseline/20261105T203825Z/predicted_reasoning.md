# Forecast of the Court's action

The Court will most likely hold this petition for Nielsen v. Watanabe, No. 25-417, and then grant certiorari, vacate the First Circuit's judgment, and remand for reconsideration in light of that decision. The hold itself is not a disposition and is not counted as a grant. The ultimate grant-family probability is **72%**; GVR is the most likely individual disposition.

The starting docket shows **zero conference distributions** and **no CVSG**. The petition will almost certainly receive at least its first distribution: **P(distribution count rises above zero)=99%**. The central procedural path is an initial distribution followed by a hold and a later redistribution when Nielsen is decided, rather than many successive active relists. I put roughly two-thirds probability on at least two total distributions and roughly one-fifth on three or more. These are separate from the registered increment claim, whose arrival-stage reference count is zero. On the hold path, resolution will follow Nielsen's merits judgment rather than the first conference; the record supplies no basis for an exact decision date.

The Court will not call for the Solicitor General's views. **P(new CVSG)=0.5%**: the Solicitor General already filed this petition and has stated the federal position.

Conditional on any grant, **65%** is on disposition in the cert order itself, principally a GVR following Nielsen, rather than full additional briefing and argument here. Immediate summary reversal is a smaller possibility because the parties dispute whether this is a new Bivens context at all, and the pending lead case offers a less categorical route. If Nielsen turns out to be narrowly prison-specific, the Court may instead grant plenary review of the warrant/location split or deny this petition.

If plenary review is granted, the likely question is the petition's existing Fourth Amendment question: whether a Bivens damages action encompasses excessive force during execution of an arrest warrant outside the home. The Court could formulate it as whether those circumstances are a new context, rather than as an invitation to overrule Bivens altogether. The failure-to-intervene claim will not be the focus. If relief is by GVR, the order need not finally decide whether the location or warrant alone defeats the action; it can direct reconsideration under Nielsen's framework.

If the Court denies review, it will most likely do so without explanation or separate writing. **P(a noted dissent or statement | denial)=8%**. Thomas, with Gorsuch a secondary possibility, is the likelier source of a statement objecting to preservation of an allegedly extended Bivens remedy. On a summary order restricting the damages action, Jackson or Sotomayor is more likely to write separately. No majority opinion or merits vote split is predicted for a denial or a routine GVR.
