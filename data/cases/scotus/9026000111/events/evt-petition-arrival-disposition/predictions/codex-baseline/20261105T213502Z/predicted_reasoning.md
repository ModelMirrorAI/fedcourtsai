# Forecast of the Court's action

The Court will most likely hold this petition for Nielsen v. Watanabe, No. 25-417, and then grant the petition, vacate the First Circuit's judgment, and remand for reconsideration in light of that decision. A separate argument in Herzon is less likely than action through the certiorari order itself. The grant-side probability is 78%; conditional on grant, the summary-route probability is 75%.

The snapshot shows **zero distributions and no CVSG**. An initial distribution is expected. The modal subsequent path is a hold rather than repeated consideration at successive conferences, followed by approximately one additional distribution when Nielsen supplies the governing framework. In the claim's literal definition, a distribution increment from zero has probability 99%; this is not a prediction that a true post-conference relist is virtually certain.

No CVSG is expected: the Solicitor General already represents the petitioners. A request for the government's position would add little. The probability of a new CVSG is 0.2%.

If the Court instead grants separate plenary review, it will take the presented threshold question substantially as written: whether Bivens permits this Fourth Amendment excessive-force action arising from execution of an arrest warrant outside a home. It will not need to review the abandoned failure-to-intervene claim or decide whose account of the arrest is true. Conditional on such review, the likely ground is that these circumstances constitute a new context and do not justify judicial creation of a damages remedy, rather than a finding that the alleged force was reasonable. A GVR will instead leave application of the intervening framework to the First Circuit and will not itself determine ultimate liability.

The order will most likely carry no separate writing. Conditional on denial, a dissent or statement has probability 8%. If summary restriction of the remedy prompts an objection, Jackson or Sotomayor is the likeliest author; the unconditional per-Justice probabilities remain low. No majority opinion is forecast for a denial, and no specific Justice's cert vote is predicted.
