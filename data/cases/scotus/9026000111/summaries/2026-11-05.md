## In brief

Federal drug agents ask the Court to block a damages lawsuit over a warrant-based arrest, arguing courts may not extend a Fourth Amendment damages remedy to this situation.

## What happened

DEA task-force members arrested Robert Arias in a shopping mall parking lot under an arrest warrant. Arias sued six agents, claiming they used excessive force and that others failed to stop it, seeking damages under a Supreme Court precedent, Bivens, that allows such suits in some circumstances. A federal district court ruled against Arias, partly because a federal law lets people file misconduct complaints against agents administratively. The First Circuit reversed on the excessive-force claim, holding it was similar enough to the original Bivens case to proceed, but upheld dismissal of the failure-to-intervene claim. The agents, represented by the federal government, now ask the Supreme Court to review that ruling.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked whether a damages lawsuit against federal officers for excessive force, originally allowed only for a warrantless home arrest, should also be allowed when officers use a warrant and the arrest happens outside the home. The petition also asks, if the Court does not decide this now, that it instead wait to act until it decides a related case about this same type of lawsuit in a different setting.

## Where it stands

The petition for review was filed in July 2026. The respondent's deadline to reply has been extended twice, most recently to December 7, 2026, and the Court has not yet acted on the petition.

## What each outcome would mean

The federal officers are asking the Court to take up the case and reverse the First Circuit's ruling, which allowed Arias's excessive-force claim to go forward. If the Court grants review and later rules for the officers, Arias's claim would be cut off. If it rules for Arias, his lawsuit could proceed. If the Court denies review, the First Circuit's decision favoring Arias stands, and his case continues in the lower courts. The Court could also delay deciding on this petition until it resolves the related case it mentioned.
