# Forecast: Crowther v. Board of Regents of the University System of Georgia (No. 25-183)

**Event:** the judgment the Court enters on the merits, following the May 18,
2026 cert grant. Question presented: whether Title IX provides employees of
federally funded educational institutions a private right of action to sue for
sex discrimination in employment.

## Judgment class

The Court **affirms** the Eleventh Circuit, holding that Title IX's implied
private right of action does not extend to employees' claims of sex
discrimination in their own employment. P(judgment below disturbed) = **0.35**
— reversal is a live outcome, not a remote one, but affirmance is the modal
result.

Why affirmance is more likely than the ~70% historical disturbed rate
suggests:

- **The United States is against the petitioners on the merits.** The
  Solicitor General's CVSG brief (filed April 9, 2026) recommended a grant
  while supporting the university's position — questioning whether it is for
  the Court, rather than Congress, to extend Title IX's implied right of
  action. The SG's merits position is a strong predictor of outcomes, and the
  federal government will argue for affirmance as amicus.
- **Affirmance requires no overruling.** The Court need only decline to extend
  Cannon and Jackson one step further — precisely the move its modern
  implied-right-of-action cases (Sandoval; Ziglar v. Abbasi; Egbert; Cummings
  in the Spending Clause anti-discrimination context) prescribe. Title VII's
  reticulated scheme supplies the displacement rationale the Eleventh Circuit
  used.
- **The grant coalition points the same way.** With eight circuits already
  favoring employees, petitioners had little to gain from review; the natural
  votes to grant were Justices ready to nationalize the Fifth/Seventh/Eleventh
  Circuit rule, with the SG's grant-and-affirm recommendation in hand.

Why reversal stays at 0.35 rather than lower: the textual syllogism for
petitioners is tight (North Haven held Title IX's "no person" reaches
employment; Cannon makes the statute privately enforceable; Jackson let an
employee invoke it), eight circuits stand behind it, and this Court has
repeatedly ruled for employees in recent statutory discrimination cases
(Bostock, Muldrow, Ames) when text pointed there — a Gorsuch-led textualist
crossover is a genuine path to reversal.

## Vote lineup

**6–3**, conventional ideological split: Roberts, Thomas, Alito, Gorsuch,
Kavanaugh, and Barrett in the majority; Sotomayor, Kagan, and Jackson in
dissent. The likeliest defection to a reversal majority is Gorsuch (text-first
method, Bostock), with Roberts and Kavanaugh conceivably following to keep the
opinion narrow; a 7–2 or 8–1 affirmance is less likely than a crossover
reversal. Authorship is unscored context: I expect the majority from Alito
(author of Fitzgerald v. Barnstable, the Court's closest Title IX
parallel-remedies precedent) or Thomas, with Sotomayor writing the principal
dissent.

## Ground and breadth

If the Court affirms, the ground is the implied-right-of-action doctrine plus
Title VII displacement, stated categorically for all employees — see the two
semantic claims in `prediction.json`. Jackson's retaliation holding and
students' Title IX rights are distinguished, not disturbed. If the Court
instead reverses, expect a similarly categorical textual holding that "no
person" includes employees and that Title VII's separate scheme does not
implicitly narrow Title IX — likely authored by Gorsuch.

## Procedural path

- No DIG expected (~3–4%): the BIO's vehicle objections (petitioner-specific
  merits defects, parallel Title VII claims already rejected) were before the
  Court when it granted after a CVSG and four distributions.
- An equally divided affirmance is negligible — no recusal is apparent on the
  docket.
- Timing (unscored context): merits briefing is on the extended schedule
  ordered June 2, 2026 (respondents' brief due August 17, 2026); expect
  argument in the fall 2026 sitting (November–December 2026) and a decision in
  the spring of 2027, more likely toward the end of the Term (May–June 2027)
  given the ideological stakes.
