# Forecast of the Court's decision

The Court will affirm the Eleventh Circuit, most likely by a 6-3 vote. Chief Justice Roberts and Justices Thomas, Alito, Gorsuch, Kavanaugh, and Barrett will vote with the majority; Justices Sotomayor, Kagan, and Jackson will dissent. A dismissal as improvidently granted or an equally divided affirmance is not a serious procedural prospect.

The majority will answer the sole question presented and hold that Title IX's implied private right of action does not extend to an employee's claim of sex discrimination in employment. It will distinguish substantive coverage from a private remedy: *North Haven* may establish that Title IX's prohibition reaches employment, but it does not establish that every covered employee has an implied damages action. The Court will read *Cannon* narrowly under *Sandoval*, *Gonzaga*, and *Medina*, and it will distinguish *Jackson* as protecting an employee retaliated against for reporting discrimination against students.

The majority's central ground will be that Title IX does not unambiguously confer this additional private remedy and that Congress's detailed Title VII scheme confirms the absence of a parallel, unrestricted action. Title VII's extension to educational institutions shortly before Title IX, together with its exhaustion rules, limitations periods, and remedial limits, will make it unlikely that Congress silently authorized employees to evade that scheme through Title IX.

The holding will be categorical rather than confined to the allegations against Crowther and Joseph or to public universities. It will cover employees of federally funded educational institutions generally while leaving *Jackson*'s student-discrimination retaliation rule intact. The dissent will argue that the majority artificially separates right from remedy after Congress ratified *Cannon*, and that Title IX's protection of "no person," *North Haven*, and *Jackson* already encompass employees rather than requiring a new implied action.
