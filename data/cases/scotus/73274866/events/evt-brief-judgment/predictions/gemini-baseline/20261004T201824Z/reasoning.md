The base rate for merits reversal/vacatur (disturbed) over the past ten Terms is approximately 69.8% (360 disturbed out of 516 parsed cases). However, several case-specific factors strongly suggest this case will buck the base rate and result in an affirmance.

The Eleventh Circuit below held that Title IX does not provide an implied private right of action for employees claiming sex discrimination, breaking from eight other circuits and joining only the Fifth and Seventh Circuits (which rely on pre-*Jackson* precedent). When the Supreme Court grants certiorari to review a minority position on a high-profile civil rights statute, it often does so to endorse the outlier view, especially when that view aligns with the current majority's jurisprudential priors.

The current 6-3 conservative majority is famously hostile to implied private rights of action (e.g., *Alexander v. Sandoval*, *Egbert v. Boule*). While *North Haven* established that Title IX substantive protections extend to employees, the remedy here is an implied right of action established by *Cannon*. The Court will likely decline to extend *Cannon*'s implied remedy to employees because Congress has already provided an express, comprehensive remedial scheme for employment discrimination in Title VII. Allowing a Title IX suit would permit plaintiffs to circumvent Title VII's administrative exhaustion and damages caps. This textual and structural argument—avoiding implied remedies where express ones exist—is highly appealing to the Court's conservative block.

I adjusted the probability of disturbance down to 35%, reflecting the likelihood that the Court granted this case specifically to cabin Title IX's implied right of action and resolve the deep circuit split in favor of the Eleventh Circuit's narrower approach. The main uncertainty is whether textualists like Justice Gorsuch might find that because Title IX's text covers "any person," the implied right of action must necessarily follow, though his general opposition to implied remedies makes this less likely.
