The Court will affirm the Eleventh Circuit's judgment, resolving the circuit split by holding that Title IX's implied private right of action does not extend to employment discrimination claims.

Expect a 6-3 decision, with the six conservative Justices (Roberts, Thomas, Alito, Gorsuch, Kavanaugh, Barrett) in the majority and the three liberal Justices (Sotomayor, Kagan, Jackson) in dissent. 

The Court will reach the single question presented and rule that employees of federally funded educational institutions cannot sue for sex discrimination under Title IX. The majority's doctrinal ground will likely rest on the separation of powers and the strict limitation of implied rights of action. While *North Haven* established that Title IX protects employees, and *Cannon* recognized an implied right of action, the majority will refuse to extend *Cannon* to the employment context. The Court will reason that Congress provided a comprehensive and express remedial scheme for employment discrimination in Title VII of the Civil Rights Act of 1964. Implying an overlapping remedy under Title IX would allow plaintiffs to bypass Title VII's carefully calibrated administrative exhaustion requirements and damages caps. The ruling will be a categorical holding barring all Title IX employment discrimination suits by employees, relegating them exclusively to Title VII.

A procedural exit (such as a DIG) is highly unlikely, as the circuit split is deep (8-3) and squarely presented. The dissent will likely argue that the text of Title IX broadly covers "any person" and that Congress ratified the implied right of action when it amended Title IX without altering *Cannon* or subsequent enforcement practices. Expect separate concurring opinions from Justices Thomas or Alito emphasizing their broader view that *Cannon* itself was wrongly decided.
