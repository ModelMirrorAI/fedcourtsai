# Forecast of the Court's judgment

The Court will **affirm** the Eleventh Circuit's judgment, **6–3**. Roberts, Thomas, Alito, Gorsuch, Kavanaugh, and Barrett will form the majority; Sotomayor, Kagan, and Jackson will dissent. This is the predicted lineup on the judgment, not a claim that every Justice will join every passage. I do not forecast an opinion author.

The majority will answer the question presented against a private Title IX action for employees' own sex-discrimination claims in employment. It will distinguish a statute's substantive protection from congressional authorization of a particular private remedy. Applying Sandoval's remedial-intent analysis, the Court will treat Title VII's express, administratively structured employment remedy as strong evidence against inferring an additional employment cause of action under Title IX. That forecast adopts respondents' principal theory, not a factual finding about either petitioner's conduct. [1]

The opinion will preserve Cannon's established student-facing private action, North Haven's recognition that Title IX substantively reaches employment discrimination, and Jackson's protection against retaliation for advocating students' rights. It will distinguish those holdings rather than overrule them. The holding will nevertheless be categorical for employees' own employment-discrimination claims, not confined to Georgia's public universities or these plaintiffs' disciplinary circumstances. The majority will not need separately to decide whether Title VII precludes an otherwise existing Title IX cause of action, because its conclusion will be that the asserted cause of action does not exist. [1]

The dissent will read the existing, congressionally ratified private action together with Title IX's protection of persons and North Haven's employment holding. It will reject the characterization of these suits as a newly created remedy and invoke Jackson against separating the recognized prohibition from private enforcement. It will regard overlapping employment remedies as permissible rather than as grounds for a judicially created exclusion. [2]

I expect a merits decision during the October 2026 Term, most likely by June 2027. This is an estimated horizon, not a retrieved argument or decision schedule. Neither a DIG nor an equally divided affirmance is the modal route; the provisioned record identifies no recusal. A procedural exit remains a small alternative because the plaintiffs have distinct underlying claims and some adverse rulings outside the question presented. [3]
