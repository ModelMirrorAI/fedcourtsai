## Why move below the reversal-oriented anchor?

**The dispute falls directly on the Court's distinction between a right and a remedy.** Respondents concede substantive Title IX coverage of employment but contest the additional private enforcement route. They connect that distinction to Title VII's detailed express scheme and to spending-law notice. This supplies a relatively narrow way to affirm without overruling the existence of Title IX private enforcement generally. The preserved holdings would still do substantial work: administrative enforcement against employment discrimination, private student suits, and Jackson retaliation suits. [3]

**The current doctrinal direction is stronger evidence than the abstract grant-to-reversal tendency.** I checked Medina's discussion of Cannon through CourtListener. It both recognizes Congress's ratification of Cannon's holding and rejects carrying forward its broader rights-creating method. I also checked Cisco's June 23, 2026 decision, cited by respondents: its majority sharply restricts judicial creation of causes of action, with Roberts, Thomas, Alito, Gorsuch, Kavanaugh, and Barrett together. Those are legal-method and coalition analogies, not controlling holdings on Title IX. In particular, Cisco's foreign-affairs and ATS context differs substantially from a longstanding domestic discrimination remedy. Still, the combination makes respondents' characterization of the requested action as an extension a serious obstacle. [5–6]

**The best argument for reversal is substantial.** Petitioners are not asking for a private remedy under a previously unenforceable statute. They combine Cannon and congressional ratification with North Haven's employment coverage, Title IX's person-focused language, and Jackson's recognition of an employee's retaliation action. Their overlapping-remedies argument is not answered merely by observing that Title VII exists: Congress can provide concurrent remedies, and the briefs identify statutory and remedial differences between the two laws. If the Court regards these employment claims as applications of an established cause of action, rather than a new extension, the respondents' separation-of-powers argument loses much of its force. That distinction is why my disturbance probability remains 35%, rather than a near-certain conservative-bloc affirmance. [2,5]

**Vehicle issues principally affect the form of an upset.** Joseph's other claims and Crowther's different procedural posture leave work for the lower courts even if petitioners win the question presented. Respondents specifically request a remand to consider Title VII preclusion if the Court recognizes a Title IX cause of action. Thus a vacatur or partial result belongs in P(disturbed), not only outright reversal. The BIO's vehicle objections support a small DIG allowance, but a completed grant and two merits briefs make a merits resolution more plausible than abandoning the case. [1,3]

An illustrative allocation consistent with my committed number is: affirmed **0.62**, reversed **0.28**, vacated **0.06**, affirmed-in-part-reversed-in-part **0.01**, DIG **0.025**, and equally divided affirmance **0.005**. The three disturbance labels sum to **0.35**. The small equal-division allowance is residual participation uncertainty, not evidence of an actual recusal. These are judgmental probabilities, not fitted outputs. In particular, the large downward adjustment from 69.77% is my substantive assessment, not a measured likelihood ratio.

## Votes, breadth, and stakes

The forecasted six-Justice majority follows the remedial-restraint coalition visible in Cisco, while the three dissenters would preserve a broader reading of established private enforcement. The uncertain issue is whether one or more members of that majority instead adopts petitioners' existing-remedy framing; a switch by one Justice would narrow the split, while two could change the result. The vote block describes the modal judgment lineup and does not assert certainty about each vote. The semantic claims forecast a general employment-claim exclusion with student and student-protective retaliation remedies preserved, not a factual disposition or an overruling of all Title IX private actions. [2–3,6]

The **0.72 significance score** reflects nationwide access to an employment-discrimination remedy in education and the boundary between two major federal civil-rights laws. It is not a grant-likelihood score. The continued availability of Title VII and other Title IX enforcement channels keeps this below a wholesale elimination of educational nondiscrimination protection. [2–3]
