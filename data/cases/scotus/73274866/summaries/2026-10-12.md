## In brief

The Supreme Court heard whether employees of federally funded schools can sue under Title IX for sex discrimination in employment.

## What happened

MaChelle Joseph, Georgia Tech's former women's basketball coach, and Thomas Crowther, a former Augusta University art professor, both sued their universities claiming sex discrimination in employment under Title IX, the federal law barring sex discrimination at schools receiving federal funds. Joseph was fired after complaints about her treatment of players; Crowther was let go following a Title IX investigation into alleged harassment of students. Lower courts dismissed their Title IX employment claims, reasoning that Title IX does not let employees sue for job discrimination because Title VII, a different federal law, already covers that ground. The Eleventh Circuit agreed, splitting from several other appeals courts that allow such claims.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked whether a federal law barring sex discrimination at schools that receive federal money lets employees, not just students, bring private lawsuits for discrimination in their jobs, or whether another federal employment-discrimination law is the only avenue available to them.

## Where it stands

The Court granted review, received a brief from the federal government supporting the schools' position, and heard oral argument on November 30, 2026. The case was fully briefed and argued; the docket does not yet show a decision.

## What each outcome would mean

Crowther and Joseph, as the employees who petitioned the Court, are asking it to revive their Title IX claims against the Board of Regents of the University System of Georgia and the Georgia Tech Athletic Association. If the Court rules that Title IX allows such employment-discrimination suits, their claims would return to the lower courts for further proceedings rather than being decided outright. If the Court rules that it does not, the Eleventh Circuit's dismissal of their Title IX employment claims stands, leaving Title VII as their only possible route for such claims.
