## In brief

The Court heard whether Title IX lets employees of federally funded schools sue for sex discrimination in employment, and it granted review before deciding the case.

## What happened

Thomas Crowther, a former art professor, and MaChelle Joseph, a former basketball coach, separately sued Georgia public universities claiming sex discrimination and retaliation. Crowther says he was fired after a Title IX investigation into his own conduct; Joseph says her program got fewer resources than the men's team and that she was fired for complaining. The Eleventh Circuit ruled that Title IX does not let employees sue for employment discrimination at all, reversing a lower court that had allowed Crowther's claim and affirming dismissal of Joseph's. Crowther and Joseph asked the Supreme Court to review that ruling, saying it conflicts with decisions in most other circuits.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked whether a law barring sex discrimination in federally funded education programs also gives employees, not just students, a right to sue their employer directly for job discrimination, or whether a separate federal employment-discrimination law is the only avenue for such claims.

## Where it stands

The Court granted the petition, received briefing and multiple amicus filings, and the case is awaiting a decision on the merits after full briefing was completed.

## What each outcome would mean

Crowther and Joseph asked the Court to take the case after losing on this issue below. If the Court rules for them, it would hold that Title IX allows employees to sue for job discrimination, reviving Crowther's claim and potentially Joseph's, sending the case back for further proceedings. If it rules for the Board of Regents and the Athletic Association, the Eleventh Circuit's decision denying any such right stands, leaving Crowther and Joseph without a Title IX employment claim, though they could still pursue claims under the other employment-discrimination law. A ruling to vacate would set the lower decision aside without resolving the question for either side.
