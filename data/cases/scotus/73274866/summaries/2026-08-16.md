## What happened

Thomas Crowther, a former art professor at Augusta University, and MaChelle Joseph, a former women's basketball coach at Georgia Tech, both sued their universities under Title IX, a federal law barring sex discrimination at schools that receive federal money. Crowther said he was let go amid a disputed misconduct investigation; Joseph said she was fired after complaining about unequal resources for her team and after an investigation into her treatment of players. The Eleventh Circuit consolidated their cases and ruled that Title IX does not let employees sue for sex discrimination in employment at all, disagreeing with several other appeals courts. Joseph's related claims under a different law, Title VII, were separately rejected on the merits. Both sides sought Supreme Court review of the Title IX employment-discrimination question.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked whether Title IX gives employees of federally funded schools their own right to sue over sex discrimination in their jobs, or whether that kind of claim can only be brought under a separate federal employment-discrimination law, Title VII, which has different procedures and requirements. The university argues there is no real disagreement among lower courts once the details are examined, and that ruling for the employees would not even change the outcome in these particular cases. The employees argue courts are split and that the appeals court's rule breaks with earlier Supreme Court decisions recognizing employee lawsuits under Title IX.
