## In brief

Two former university employees ask the Court to decide whether Title IX lets workers sue their schools directly for sex discrimination on the job.

## What happened

MaChelle Joseph, fired as Georgia Tech's women's basketball coach after complaints about her treatment of players and staff, and Thomas Crowther, an Augusta University art professor let go following a sexual-harassment investigation, each sued the Board of Regents of the University System of Georgia alleging sex discrimination in their employment. Joseph brought claims under both Title VII and Title IX; Crowther brought only Title IX claims. The district courts split on whether Title IX allows such employment-discrimination suits at all, given Title VII's separate framework for workplace bias claims. The Eleventh Circuit, consolidating both cases, held that Title IX does not provide employees a right to sue over sex discrimination in employment, affirming dismissal of Joseph's Title IX claims and ordering dismissal of Crowther's.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked whether a federal law barring sex discrimination by schools that receive federal money lets employees of those schools sue directly under that law, or whether employees must instead rely only on the separate federal law that specifically covers workplace discrimination.

## Where it stands

The Court granted review, and the United States, invited to weigh in, sided with the schools. The case was argued on November 30, 2026, and now awaits decision.

## What each outcome would mean

Crowther and Joseph, the employees who sought review, are asking the Court to reverse the Eleventh Circuit's ruling against them. If the Court rules for them, employees of federally funded schools nationwide could sue directly under this law for sex discrimination in their jobs, and their cases would return to the lower courts for further proceedings. If the Court rules for the Board of Regents, employees would remain limited to suing under the workplace-discrimination law instead, and the Eleventh Circuit's dismissal of both employees' claims would stand.
