## In brief

Two former university employees ask the Court to decide whether Title IX lets employees sue schools for sex discrimination in their jobs, separately from Title VII.

## What happened

MaChelle Joseph, fired as Georgia Tech's women's basketball coach, and Thomas Crowther, let go as an Augusta University art professor after a sexual-harassment investigation, each sued their universities, bringing claims under Title IX, the federal law barring sex discrimination at schools that receive federal money. The lower courts dismissed their Title IX sex-discrimination claims, reasoning that Title VII, the law that directly covers workplace discrimination, forecloses using Title IX for the same kind of claim. The Eleventh Circuit agreed, becoming the first appeals court since a 2005 Supreme Court case on this topic to hold that Title IX gives no employees any right to sue over job discrimination, splitting from several other circuits.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked whether Title IX's implied right for people to sue over sex discrimination extends to employees suing over discrimination in their employment, or whether that kind of claim belongs only under Title VII, the law written specifically to cover workplace discrimination.

## Where it stands

The Court granted review, and the case was argued on November 30, 2026; it now awaits decision on the merits.

## What each outcome would mean

Crowther and Joseph, as the petitioners, are asking the Court to revive their Title IX claims. If the Court affirms, the Eleventh Circuit's ruling stands: employees cannot sue schools under Title IX for job discrimination, and both petitioners' Title IX claims stay dismissed. If the Court reverses, it would hold that Title IX does allow such suits, reviving Crowther's and Joseph's claims and sending the cases back for further proceedings on them. A ruling vacating the decision below would set it aside and return the case without deciding the question for either side.
