## In brief

Two former university employees ask the Court whether Title IX, the federal sex-discrimination law for schools, lets employees sue for job discrimination.

## What happened

MaChelle Joseph, Georgia Tech's former women's basketball coach, and Thomas Crowther, a former Augusta University art professor, each sued their universities claiming sex discrimination and retaliation in their employment, bringing claims under Title IX. Joseph's employer fired her after an investigation into her treatment of players and staff; Crowther's contract was not renewed after a Title IX investigation found he had sexually harassed students. Lower courts dismissed their Title IX sex-discrimination claims, reasoning that Title VII, which already covers workplace sex discrimination, forecloses a separate claim under Title IX. The Eleventh Circuit agreed, holding that Title IX does not let employees sue for job discrimination at all, splitting from several other appeals courts.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked whether a federal law barring sex discrimination at schools that receive federal money lets an employee of such a school sue for discrimination in her job, or whether a separate federal employment-discrimination law is the only avenue for that kind of claim.

## Where it stands

The Court granted review in May 2026. The United States, invited to weigh in, sided with the schools on the merits but still urged the Court to take the case; briefing and oral argument followed, with argument held November 30, 2026, and the case now awaits decision.

## What each outcome would mean

Crowther and Joseph, who brought the suit, are asking the Court to reverse the lower court and revive their Title IX claims. If the Court affirms, the lower court's rule stands: employees of federally funded schools could not sue under Title IX for sex discrimination in employment, leaving Title VII as their only option, and Joseph's and Crowther's Title IX claims would stay dismissed. If the Court reverses, employees nationwide could bring such Title IX suits, and Joseph's and Crowther's claims would return to the lower courts for further proceedings. A vacate-and-remand would set the lower ruling aside without deciding the question for either side.
