## In brief

The Court is deciding whether employees of federally funded colleges can sue for sex discrimination in employment under Title IX, or whether Title VII is the only avenue.

## What happened

MaChelle Joseph, Georgia Tech's former women's basketball coach, and Thomas Crowther, a former Augusta University art professor, were each fired after misconduct investigations. Both sued, claiming sex discrimination under Title IX, the law barring sex discrimination at schools receiving federal funds. Joseph's Title IX discrimination claims were dismissed as precluded by Title VII, the law governing workplace discrimination claims generally; her related claims later failed at summary judgment. Crowther's Title IX claims survived a motion to dismiss, and the district court certified the preclusion question for immediate appeal. The Eleventh Circuit, consolidating both cases, ruled that Title IX does not let employees sue for employment discrimination at all, rejecting Crowther's claims and upholding the ruling against Joseph.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked whether a law barring sex discrimination at federally funded schools implicitly lets employees — not just students — sue their employers directly for job-related sex discrimination, given that a separate federal law already provides a detailed system for employment-discrimination claims.

## Where it stands

The Court granted review, received briefing including from the federal government (which supported the schools), held oral argument on November 30, 2026, and the case now awaits decision.

## What each outcome would mean

Crowther and Joseph, who asked the Court to overturn the Eleventh Circuit's ruling, would be allowed to pursue their Title IX employment-discrimination claims if the Court reverses; Joseph's case would return to the lower courts for further proceedings on that claim, and Crowther's dismissed claim would be reinstated. If the Court affirms, employees of federally funded schools nationwide would be unable to sue under Title IX for job discrimination and would need to rely on Title VII instead, leaving the rulings against both petitioners in place.
