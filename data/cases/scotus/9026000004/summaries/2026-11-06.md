## In brief

Samsung asks the Court to decide whether Georgia can treat business registration as consent to general jurisdiction without clearer notice to companies.

## What happened

Jordan Brewer, badly burned when a battery in his vape pen exploded, won a $10.9 million default judgment against Samsung in Georgia state court after Samsung failed to answer the complaint. Samsung spent years trying to undo that judgment, arguing among other things that Georgia courts lacked personal jurisdiction over it. Georgia's courts repeatedly rejected Samsung's attempts to set aside the judgment, relying on Georgia Supreme Court precedent holding that a company's registration to do business in the state subjects it to general jurisdiction there. The Georgia Supreme Court denied further review. Samsung then asked the U.S. Supreme Court to take the case.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked whether requiring out-of-state companies to answer lawsuits in Georgia, based solely on their registration to do business there, violates constitutional due process when Georgia's registration law itself does not say that registering counts as agreeing to be sued there — that rule comes only from Georgia court decisions, some issued after Samsung first registered.

## Where it stands

The petition was distributed for conference once, after which the Court asked Brewer to respond; Brewer filed an opposition brief, and Samsung filed a reply. The case was then distributed for a second conference, which has not yet occurred as of this summary.

## What each outcome would mean

Samsung, which lost in the Georgia courts, is asking the Court to take up its case and ultimately overturn the ruling against it. If the Court grants review, it would hear full arguments and decide the jurisdictional question itself, deciding nothing yet about who is right. If it denies review, the Georgia Court of Appeals ruling stands, leaving Samsung subject to the judgment and Brewer's win intact. Brewer's brief also argues the Court lacks authority to hear the case at all because Samsung did not properly raise the federal question in the Georgia courts, a separate ground on which review could be denied.
