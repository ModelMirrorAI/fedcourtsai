The docket currently records two conference distributions (`distribution_count: 2`) and no call for the views of the Solicitor General (`cvsg_date: null`): the petition was first distributed on September 29, 2026, for the October 16, 2026 conference, rescheduled on October 15, 2026 (one day before that conference), and redistributed on October 19, 2026, for the October 30, 2026 conference. Thus, the October 30, 2026 conference will be the first conference at which the Justices actually consider the petition rather than a post-conference relist.

- **Further relists (`relist-increment`: 0.12):** The Court will most likely deny the petition without any further distribution following the October 30, 2026 conference. There is a modest (~12%) chance of at least one additional distribution—most plausibly a single relist if a chambers takes an extra week to examine the relationship between Georgia's judicial consent-by-registration doctrine and *Mallory v. Norfolk Southern Railway Co.*, 600 U.S. 122 (2023), or to consider a brief statement respecting denial.
- **Call for the Views of the Solicitor General (`cvsg-increment`: 0.005):** The Court will not invite the Solicitor General to file a brief. This is a state-court personal-injury default-judgment dispute raising a Fourteenth Amendment Due Process challenge to Georgia's corporate registration regime, implicating no federal statute, treaty, or regulatory program.
- **Disposition (`disposition`: 0.03):** The Court will deny the petition for a writ of certiorari (`denied`). It recently upheld consent-by-registration general jurisdiction under the Due Process Clause in *Mallory*, denied review of the very Georgia Supreme Court precedent challenged here in *Cooper Tire & Rubber Co. v. McCall*, No. 21-926 (cert. denied June 30, 2023), and denied subsequent post-*Mallory* petitions in *Syngenta Crop Protection, LLC v. Nemeth*, No. 24-1190 (cert. denied Oct. 6, 2025), and *Norfolk Southern Railway Co. v. Mallory*, No. 25-1208 (cert. denied Oct. 5, 2026).

## Conditional Route and Question Presented if Granted

- **Question presented:** If the Court were to grant certiorari, it would take the single question presented in the petition—whether the Fourteenth Amendment's Due Process Clause permits a state to subject a foreign corporation to general personal jurisdiction based on business registration where notice of that consequence comes from authoritative state judicial decisions issued after the corporation's initial registration rather than express statutory text.
- **Summary disposition vs. plenary review (`summary-disposition-route`: 0.10):** Conditional on a grant, the Court would almost certainly set the case for plenary briefing and oral argument rather than disposing of it summarily in the cert order. No pending merits case on the Court's OT2026 docket involves corporate registration jurisdiction that would support a hold-and-GVR, and the close 5–4 division in *Mallory* makes a summary reversal on the papers improbable. If granted for plenary review, the likely focus would be whether annual registration renewals after *Allstate Insurance Co. v. Klein*, 262 Ga. 599 (1992), and *Cooper Tire & Rubber Co. v. McCall*, 312 Ga. 422 (2021), provided constitutionally sufficient notice under *Pennsylvania Fire Insurance Co. v. Gold Issue Mining & Milling Co.*, 243 U.S. 93 (1917), and *Mallory*.

## Separate Writings at Denial

- **Dissent or statement respecting denial (`dissent-from-denial`: 0.02):** Expect the denial order to issue without any noted dissent or separate statement. No Justice noted a dissent or statement when the Court denied certiorari in *Cooper Tire* (No. 21-926), *Syngenta* (No. 24-1190), or *Norfolk Southern v. Mallory* (No. 25-1208). In the unlikely event a Justice writes separately at the cert stage, it would most plausibly be Justice Alito (who concurred in part in *Mallory*) or one of the *Mallory* dissenters (such as Justice Barrett) or Justice Thomas/Gorsuch, though the default-judgment posture and intrastate facts here make this an unattractive vehicle for a separate statement.
