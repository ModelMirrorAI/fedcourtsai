# Forecast — Samsung Electronics America, Inc. v. Brewer, No. 26-4

**Call: certiorari denied, most likely at the conference of November 13, 2026.**

**Docket state forecast from.** The docket shows two distributions (for the
9/28/2026 and 11/13/2026 conferences), no CVSG, one amicus brief (Atlantic
Legal Foundation), and a response called for on August 14, 2026 after the
respondent waived. The first distribution was overtaken by the call for a
response, so the petition has not actually been relisted after a conference
considered it on full briefing. The 11/13 conference is the first real look
at the complete papers (petition, BIO, reply).

- **Relisting.** I expect the Court to deny at the 11/13 conference without a
  further distribution (about 78%). A relist, or a reschedule, would most
  likely mean a Justice is writing a dissent from denial, or the Court is
  checking the preservation question before granting.
- **CVSG.** Not expected. Both parties are private, the case comes from a
  state court, and the federal government has no institutional stake in how a
  State reads its own business-registration statute. The Georgia Attorney
  General was served under 28 U.S.C. § 2403(b) and may file, but the Court
  would not need the Solicitor General.
- **Question the Court would take, if it granted.** It would take the
  petition's single question as written: whether due process allows general
  jurisdiction based on consent-by-registration when the statute gives no
  express notice of that consequence and the judicial gloss supplying the
  notice came after the defendant registered. The Court might reformulate the
  question so it does not depend on the retroactivity point.
- **Summary disposition.** Unlikely, even if the Court grants. No intervening
  decision would justify a GVR, and a summary reversal on an issue the
  respondent says was never presented to the Georgia Supreme Court is
  implausible.
- **Why denial.** (1) The BIO raises a serious threshold problem under § 1257:
  the respondent says Samsung never raised the federal due-process question in
  either of its two Georgia Supreme Court certiorari petitions. It also says
  the argument made to the Court of Appeals was in a conditional cross-appeal
  that Samsung disclaimed. The Georgia courts never decided the issue. (2) The
  posture is a set-aside fight over a default judgment, with the BIO pointing
  to admissions in the trial court, so the federal question may not be
  outcome-determinative. (3) The Court already declined Georgia's
  registration-as-consent regime once: *Cooper Tire v. McCall* (No. 21-926)
  was held for *Mallory* and then denied.
- **Dissent from denial.** Unlikely (about 6%). If anyone writes, the most
  likely candidates are Justice Alito, whose *Mallory* concurrence flagged
  other constitutional limits on registration jurisdiction, or Justice
  Barrett, who wrote the *Mallory* dissent.
