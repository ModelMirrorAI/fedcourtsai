# Why P(grant) = 0.06

**Inputs read.** Snapshot `record/snapshots/2026-10-29.json`; `context.json`
(forward mode, band `elevated` under sal-v4, distribution_count 2, no CVSG,
term 2026); `questions-presented.txt`; `petition.txt` (front matter, QP,
introduction, table of contents; the file is truncated at the pipeline cap);
`brief-in-opposition.txt` (questions presented, statement of the
post-judgment proceedings, and Parts I–II on preservation and vehicle). The
reply brief was submitted on Oct 28 but was not provisioned, so I have not
read it.

**Anchor.** I pooled the sal-v4 band table's bracketed `reached` rate for
`elevated` over Terms 2017–2025 (all shown Terms before OT2026). The Term
rates run from 13.4% to 20.8%, and the pool is roughly 17% on about 3,100
weighted petitions. For context, the relist-count cut's bucket 2 reads 28.3%
granted plus 12.9% GVR. I discount it because this petition's two
distributions include one that a call for a response displaced, and the cut's
caption notes that the count is an upper bound on true relists.

**Adjustments down, from ~17% to 6%.**
- *Preservation / § 1257 (large).* The BIO says, with record citations to
  Samsung's own Georgia filings, that the due-process question was never
  presented to the Georgia Supreme Court in either certiorari petition. It
  also says the only Court of Appeals briefing was in a conditional
  cross-appeal Samsung told that court it lacked standing to pursue, and that
  no Georgia court decided the issue. The petition itself concedes that the
  Court of Appeals "did not address" the argument. A
  jurisdictional/presentation problem of this kind is close to disqualifying
  as a vehicle. I have not read Samsung's reply.
- *Prior denial.* A corpus lookup shows Cooper Tire v. McCall (21-926),
  the Georgia decision the petition attacks, was denied after four
  distributions (held for Mallory). The Court has already passed on the
  Georgia regime directly.
- *Posture.* The case is a default-judgment set-aside dispute with alleged
  admissions in the trial court, and state-law grounds are intertwined.

**Adjustments up (modest).** The Court called for a response after a waiver,
so someone looked at the petition. The legal question is real, and the
petition alleges a multistate split. Petitioner's counsel is an experienced
appellate firm, and there is an amicus. Calls for a response are common for
paid petitions with an amicus, and most such petitions are still denied.

**Increments.** Relist 0.22: a denial at first full conference is the modal
path, but a dissent or a closer look at preservation would add a
distribution. CVSG 0.03: private parties and a state-law-based regime.
Summary route given a grant 0.08. Dissent from denial 0.06.

**Uncertainty.** The biggest gap is that I have not read the reply, which may
answer the preservation objection (for example, by arguing that discretionary
denial by the Georgia Supreme Court makes the Court of Appeals the relevant
court under *Chesapeake & O. Ry. v. Mihas*). If the reply clearly defeats the
preservation argument, my number is too low. I did not use the CourtListener
MCP or the web.
