## 2. Why the Court Denies Review After Receiving the Response

### A. Procedural and Finality Obstacles Under 28 U.S.C. § 1257(a) and State Law
Although the petition frames a clean constitutional question under *Mallory v. Norfolk Southern Railway Co.*, 600 U.S. 122 (2023)—whether Georgia may treat corporate registration to do business as consent to general personal jurisdiction without explicit statutory notice, particularly where the corporation first registered in 1989 before the Georgia Supreme Court's decision in *Allstate Insurance Co. v. Klein*, 262 Ga. 599 (1992)—the record below presents formidable threshold barriers that make this case an unsuitable vehicle:

1. **Trial-Court Waiver Finding:** In its July 30, 2025 order granting in part Respondent's motion to compel jurisdictional discovery (Pet. App. 5a–6a & n.1), the State Court of Fulton County explicitly concluded that Samsung Electronics America, Inc. (SEA) "previously waived any objection to personal jurisdiction in its Motion for Protective Order" by acknowledging that the court could compel discovery to determine whether minimum contacts existed and raising its *Mallory* general-jurisdiction objection only in a reply brief.
2. **State Term-of-Court Finality Rule:** Rather than appealing the July 30, 2025 order immediately, SEA filed a Motion to Set Aside on August 15, 2025—after the July–August term of court had varies/expired—seeking to vacate both the July 30, 2025 order and an earlier March 4, 2025 protective-order ruling entered during the March–April term of court. In its October 7, 2025 order (Pet. App. 3a–4a), the trial court denied the Motion to Set Aside primarily on the ground that under Georgia law (`O.C.G.A. § 15-1-3(6)` and *Moon v. State*, 287 Ga. 659 (2010)), a trial court's plenary power to revise or vacate its orders ends with the term of court in which the order was entered, and SEA's motion had not been filed within the March–April term and concerned a discovery motion rather than the merits. Only in a secondary footnote did the trial court add `See also Cooper Tire & Rubber Co. v. McCall, 312 Ga. 422 (2021)` (Pet. App. 4a n.2).
3. **Unreasoned Discretionary Denials by the Georgia Appellate Courts:** Both the Georgia Court of Appeals (Pet. App. 2a) and the Georgia Supreme Court (Pet. App. 1a) denied discretionary/interlocutory review in one-sentence orders without addressing the merits of SEA's *Mallory* or 1989 pre-*Klein* registration arguments.

Because the trial court's refusal to set aside its prior discovery orders rests on an independent and adequate state procedural ground (the expiration of the term of court and SEA's prior waiver in discovery briefing), and because the Georgia appellate courts merely declined discretionary interlocutory review of a discovery-stage order, the Court will conclude that it likely lacks jurisdiction under 28 U.S.C. § 1257(a) (failing the fourth *Cox Broadcasting Corp. v. Cohn*, 420 U.S. 469 (1975) exception) or, at a minimum, that these threshold disputes would obstruct reaching Either Question Presented.

### B. Absence of a Deepened Post-*Mallory* Split Requiring Immediate Intervention
Even setting aside the vehicle flaws, the substantive split identified in the petition does not compel immediate plenary review in this case:
- The vast majority of state high courts have construed their business-registration statutes *not* to confer general personal jurisdiction as a matter of state statutory interpretation.
- Georgia remains an isolated outlier in relying on judicial construction (*Klein* and *Cooper Tire*) rather than explicit statutory text like Pennsylvania's (`42 Pa. Cons. Stat. § 5301(a)(2)(i)`), and the Court has already denied certiorari in *Cooper Tire* itself (142 S. Ct. 2000 (2022)) as well as in post-*Mallory* petitions challenging *Cooper Tire* on clean records.
- Should the Court wish to revisit the constitutional limits of consent-by-registration (or the Dormant Commerce Clause question flagged in Justice Alito's *Mallory* concurrence), it will await a clean final judgment or a reasoned state supreme court decision squarely passing on the federal question without state-law waiver and term-of-court complications.

## 3. Contingency Forecast: Route and Scope If Certiorari Were Granted

In the unlikely event (`probability: 0.08`) that the Court grants review:
- **Plenary Review Over Summary Disposition (`summary-disposition-route: 0.15`):** A grant would almost certainly (~85% conditional probability) set the case for full merits briefing and oral argument rather than a summary disposition or GVR. *Mallory* was decided in June 2023—two years before the trial court orders in this case—so there is no intervening Supreme Court decision on which to base a GVR, and the Court would not summarily reverse a state court's application of *Mallory* in the face of contested § 1257(a) finality and state procedural waiver questions.
- **Questions Reached:** If granted for plenary review, the Court would grant on **both Questions Presented** (which are two facets of a single Fourteenth Amendment Due Process challenge to Georgia's registration-jurisdiction regime: (1) whether a statute without explicit text can establish valid consent via judicial decisions, and (2) whether a registration predating those judicial decisions can supply valid consent), while also confronting at the threshold whether the trial court's interlocutory discovery orders and term-of-court ruling satisfy 28 U.S.C. § 1257(a).
- **Separate Writings on Denial (`dissent-from-denial: 0.04`):** Given the procedural vehicle defects that will surface in the Brief in Opposition, even Justices skeptical of Georgia's *Cooper Tire* regime (such as Justice Alito or the *Mallory* dissenters) are unlikely to write separately at the denial stage, leaving only a ~4% chance of a noted dissent or statement respecting the denial of certiorari.
