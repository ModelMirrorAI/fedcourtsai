# Predicted course: Samsung Electronics America, Inc. v. Brewer (No. 26-4)

**Call: denied.** The petition was distributed once (for the 9/28/2026 long
conference) after the respondent waived a response; on Aug 14, 2026 the Court
requested a response, due Sept 14, 2026. No relist has occurred and no CVSG is
on the docket.

- **Further distribution.** Near-certain: once the brief in opposition is in
  (or its time runs out) the petition will be distributed again, most likely
  for an October or November 2026 conference. I expect it to be decided at
  that first post-response conference, most likely by a plain denial; a
  further relist after that is unlikely (roughly one in eight).
- **CVSG.** Not expected. The question is a Fourteenth Amendment limit on a
  State's own courts in a private tort suit; no federal interest invites the
  Solicitor General's views.
- **Question the Court would take, if it grants.** The QP as written, likely
  narrowed to whether a State may treat registration as consent to general
  jurisdiction when the registration statute itself gives no notice of that
  effect — with the retroactivity strand (Georgia decisions post-dating the
  registration) folded in or dropped.
- **Summary disposition.** Unlikely. No intervening decision supports a GVR,
  and a summary reversal on a contested due-process question left open in
  *Mallory* is implausible; a grant would mean plenary review.
- **Dissent from or statement respecting denial.** Unlikely. If one appears,
  the likeliest authors are Justices who wrote separately in *Mallory* (Alito,
  Gorsuch) or its principal dissent (Barrett), but I expect a silent denial.

If granted, the likely ground is whether *Mallory*'s reliance on
Pennsylvania's explicit statutory notice is a constitutional requirement, so
that implied-consent regimes like Georgia's fail due process.
