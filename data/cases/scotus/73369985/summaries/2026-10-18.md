## In brief

Two police officers ask the Court to revive the qualified immunity defense a lower court rejected in a fatal shooting lawsuit brought by a victim's widow.

## What happened

Lansing, Michigan police officers Donovan Moore and Jeff Kurtz responded to a reported domestic disturbance and shot Stephen Romero twice, the second time after he had already been wounded and was on the ground. Romero's wife, Ashly Romero, sued the officers for excessive force. A federal district court dismissed her claims, ruling the officers were protected by qualified immunity, a doctrine shielding officials from suits unless they violated clearly established rights. The Sixth Circuit Court of Appeals reversed in part, allowing the excessive-force claim over the second round of shots to proceed, while affirming dismissal of her other claims. The full Sixth Circuit declined to rehear the case, though several judges dissented from that decision.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked whether, when police use force in stages during one encounter, courts must judge each stage by looking at everything that happened before it, from the very start of the encounter onward, rather than treating later stages separately from earlier ones. The Court is also asked whether, when body-camera video clearly shows what happened, courts deciding a case at the earliest stage of litigation—before evidence-gathering—should apply the same legal standard for qualified immunity they would use later, after full evidence-gathering, rather than a standard more favorable to the suing party.

## Where it stands

The petition has been distributed for three consecutive conferences, most recently October 16, 2026, and the docket does not yet show a decision on whether the Court will hear the case.

## What each outcome would mean

The officers, Moore and Kurtz, are asking the Court to take up the case. If the Court grants the petition, it would hear full arguments and decide the legal questions itself, deciding nothing about who is right simply by agreeing to hear it. If the Court denies the petition, the Sixth Circuit's ruling stands, and Ashly Romero's excessive-force claim over the second round of shots proceeds in the lower courts toward evidence-gathering, without the Supreme Court deciding whether that ruling was correct.
