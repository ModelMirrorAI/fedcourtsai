## In brief

Two police officers ask the Court to revive the dismissal of a fatal-shooting lawsuit after an appeals court let part of it proceed.

## What happened

Officers Donovan Moore and Jeff Kurtz shot and killed Stephen Romero during a domestic-disturbance call after he reached for a holstered gun. His wife, Ashly Romero, sued, claiming excessive force, failure to intervene, and inadequate city training. A federal district court dismissed all claims, ruling the officers had qualified immunity, a legal protection that shields officials from suits unless they violated clearly established rights. The Sixth Circuit Court of Appeals affirmed dismissal of most claims but reversed as to the second round of shots, allowing that part of the excessive-force claim to proceed. The full appeals court declined to rehear the case, over several judges' dissents.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked to decide two things: first, when officers use force in stages during one encounter, whether courts must weigh the reasonableness of each stage by looking at everything that happened before it, from the start of the encounter onward, rather than treating later stages in isolation; and second, whether courts reviewing an early motion to dismiss a case, where video evidence shows the key events, must apply the same legal standard for qualified immunity that would apply later at trial preparation, rather than a standard more favorable to the suing party.

## Where it stands

The petition has been distributed for five separate conferences, most recently November 13, 2026, after full briefing including amicus briefs and a reply. The docket does not yet show a decision on whether the Court will hear the case.

## What each outcome would mean

Officers Moore and Kurtz, who lost the relevant part of the ruling below, are asking the Court to take up the case and reverse it. If the Court grants review, it would hear full arguments and decide the legal questions about how to assess multi-stage police shootings and how video evidence should factor into early-stage immunity rulings. If the Court denies review, the Sixth Circuit's decision stands, and Ashly Romero's excessive-force claim over the second round of shots continues in the lower courts; a denial would not mean the Court agrees with that ruling, only that it leaves it in place.
