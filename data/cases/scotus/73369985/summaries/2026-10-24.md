## In brief

Two police officers ask the Court to revive the qualified immunity defense a lower court rejected in a lawsuit over their fatal shooting of a man during a domestic-disturbance call.

## What happened

Lansing, Michigan officers Donovan Moore and Jeff Kurtz responded to a 911 call reporting a domestic disturbance and, en route, a possible shooting. They found Stephen Romero in a driveway near his wife's car. After Romero reached toward a holstered gun, the officers shot him; after he fell and reached again, they shot him a second time, killing him. Romero's wife, Ashly Romero, sued on his behalf, alleging excessive force. The district court dismissed the case, finding the officers protected by qualified immunity, a legal shield for officials unless they violated a clearly established right. The Sixth Circuit affirmed dismissal of most claims but revived the excessive-force claim concerning the second round of shots, allowing it to proceed to evidence-gathering.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked two things: first, when officers use force more than once during an encounter, whether courts must judge each use of force by looking at everything leading up to it, from the start of the encounter onward, rather than treating later moments in isolation; second, whether courts reviewing an early motion to dismiss a lawsuit, where video footage captures the whole event, must apply the same legal standard for qualified immunity that would apply later in the case, after evidence-gathering, rather than a standard more favorable to the suing party.

## Where it stands

The petition has been fully briefed, including an opposition brief, a reply brief, and amicus briefs supporting the officers. It was distributed for three conferences, most recently October 16, 2026, and the docket shows no action on it since.

## What each outcome would mean

The officers, Moore and Kurtz, are asking the Court to reverse the Sixth Circuit's decision and restore the full dismissal Romero's estate lost on appeal. If the Court grants review, it would hear the case and decide these questions itself, deciding nothing yet about who wins. If it denies review, the Sixth Circuit's ruling stands, letting Ashly Romero's excessive-force claim over the second round of shots proceed to evidence-gathering in the lower courts. A denial would not mean the Court agrees with that ruling, only that it lets it stand.
