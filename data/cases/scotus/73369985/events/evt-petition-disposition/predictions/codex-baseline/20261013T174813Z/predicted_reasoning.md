# Forecast of the Court's action

The Court will grant the petition and summarily reverse on qualified-immunity grounds, rather than set the entire dispute for plenary argument. P(any grant), including a GVR or summary reversal, is 0.58; denial remains a substantial alternative. Conditional on a grant, the probability of disposition in the cert order is 0.80. Within that route, a reasoned per curiam reversal is more likely than a bare GVR.

The snapshot records **three distributions**: for September 28, October 9, and October 16, 2026. Thus two redistributions are already observed; the October 16 conference remains in the future. I expect **one or two additional distributions** beyond these three before disposition, with probability 0.62 of at least one further distribution. Additional consideration would fit preparation of a short disposition and possible separate writings. I expect resolution during the fall or early winter, not a hold extending through the entire Term.

The Court will **not call for the Solicitor General's views**. No CVSG has occurred, and the forecast probability of a new call is 0.015. This dispute concerns municipal officers and an established constitutional-immunity framework rather than an unresolved federal administrative position.

If the Court grants relief, the likely controlling question is a narrowed version of **QP 2**: whether the specific facts that can properly be taken from the complaint and body-camera evidence establish a violation of clearly established law, notwithstanding the pleading-stage posture. The Court is more likely to reject an overly general description of the right than to announce that every video-supported motion to dismiss is equivalent to summary judgment. It can leave the broader constitutional question under QP 1 undecided. If it instead orders plenary briefing, I expect emphasis on this pleading-stage immunity issue, with the successive-force question secondary.

The predicted summary reversal would hold that the lower court did not identify sufficiently specific law barring the officers' response to the renewed movement toward a firearm in this rapid encounter. This is conditional merits reasoning, not a claim that the Court will adjudicate liability if it denies certiorari. A narrower vacatur for reconsideration of the immunity analysis is the alternative grant route. Barnes alone is a less natural basis for a routine GVR because the panel expressly considered it.

If review is denied, I expect **no noted dissent or statement respecting denial**, assigning 0.25 to the existence of either. If a writing does appear, an objection focused on qualified immunity from Justice Alito or Justice Thomas is more plausible than a writing proposing to abolish immunity. This identifies a possible author only as context, not as a per-Justice vote prediction.
