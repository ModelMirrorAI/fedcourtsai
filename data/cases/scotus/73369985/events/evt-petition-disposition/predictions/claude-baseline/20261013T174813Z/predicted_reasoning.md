# Forecast — Moore v. Romero, No. 25-1295 (petition for certiorari)

**State of the docket.** Distributed three times: for the 9/28/2026 long
conference, then for 10/9, then for 10/16 — two relists so far, no CVSG.

**Disposition.** Roughly even odds that the Court acts in the officers' favor,
and if it does, most likely by **summary reversal** (a per curiam holding the
officers entitled to qualified immunity on the second volley) rather than plenary
review. The repeated relisting of a qualified-immunity denial that drew a panel
dissent and five dissents from denial of rehearing en banc (Griffin, Thapar,
Hermandorfer, Bush, Readler) fits the Court's pattern of relisting police-QI
petitions while a per curiam or a separate writing is drafted.

**Relists.** Expect at least one further distribution past 10/16 (≈60%): a
per curiam reversal or a dissent from denial typically takes several weeks of
relisting to circulate. If the Court simply denies on 10/16 without writing,
there is no further distribution.

**CVSG.** No. Private parties on both sides; no federal program at stake.

**Which question, if granted.** The Court would not take QP 1's proposed
"progressive" Barnes v. Felix framework as written; it would decide on the
clearly-established prong (the core of QP 2), holding that no precedent clearly
established that firing a second burst at a suspect who re-grabbed a visible
handgun after a "Stop!" command violated the Fourth Amendment. The
12(b)(6)-vs-summary-judgment generality framing in QP 2 would be left aside.
If plenary review were granted instead, it would likely be limited to QP 1 or a
reformulated Barnes question.

**Summary route.** Conditional on any grant, ~65% that it rides the cert order
(summary reversal) rather than briefing and argument. No intervening decision
calls for a GVR.

**If denied.** Expect a separate writing (≈65%) — most plausibly a dissent from
denial by one or more Justices who favor summary reversal (e.g., Justice Alito
or Justice Thomas), or conceivably a statement respecting denial noting the
interlocutory posture.
