# Forecast — Basso v. Rodriguez, No. 26-121 (evt-petition-arrival-disposition)

**Bottom line: the Court denies certiorari without comment, most likely on the
first order list following the opening long conference of October Term 2026.**

The docket shows **zero distributions** — the petition was filed July 20, 2026
and docketed July 27, 2026, with the response due August 26, 2026. Everything
below is forecast from that pre-distribution state.

## Distribution and relists

The petition will be **distributed once** — for the late-September 2026 long
conference or the one immediately after, depending on whether a brief in
opposition is filed or the response is waived. I expect **no relists**: nothing
in the petition marks it for a second look. That single future distribution is
why my `relist-increment` probability is high (0.96) even though I expect a
routine denial — the claim resolves on any distribution beyond the zero the
snapshot shows, and an undistributed death (Rule 46 dismissal, withdrawal) is
the only realistic path to a false resolution.

## CVSG

**No CVSG** (probability 0.005). This is a private state-court dispute over
statutory attorney's fees and a retired state judge's recall eligibility. No
federal statute is construed, no federal-government interest is implicated, and
the United States has no stake a call for the Solicitor General's views would
serve.

## Disposition

**Denied**, on the order list following its first conference — likely early
October 2026. I expect **no separate writing**: no dissent from denial and no
statement respecting denial. The Caperton line draws occasional separate
writings, but only where the factual predicate of judicial bias is undisputed
and stark; here the state appellate court found the predicate itself (that the
retired judge was practicing law when he signed the order) unproven on the
record, which is exactly the posture the Justices decline without comment.

## Summary disposition

**No GVR and no summary reversal.** QP 2 asks for summary reversal in light of
the Maryland Supreme Court's own Sugarloaf decision — a state-law ruling by a
state court, which cannot ground a GVR from this Court and does not present a
federal question at all. No intervening decision of this Court bears on either
question.

## If granted (conditional)

In the unlikely event of a grant, the Court would take a **reformulated QP 1
only** — something like *whether due process requires disqualification or an
evidentiary hearing where the judgment was entered by a judge alleged to be
statutorily ineligible to sit* — stripped of the petition's fact-laden framing.
QP 2 would be left behind as purely state-law. A grant would more plausibly
follow a record developed elsewhere; this vehicle's procedural posture (a
state-ground dismissal below, an unreported intermediate opinion, a disputed
factual predicate) makes even a narrow grant unattractive.
