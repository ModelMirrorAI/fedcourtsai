I move from the approximately 5% class anchor to **1.2% P(any grant)** because this petition has substantial vehicle and issue-selection weaknesses:

- **The federal theory depends on unresolved predicate facts.** Question one alleges that the recalled judge was categorically ineligible because he was practicing law. The petition's reproduction of the appellate decision, printed pages 35–37, says the LinkedIn material did not establish when the new job began. The petition later describes a November 17, 2023 employment agreement and a December 14 order, while also allowing that daily county-attorney work began after the order, printed pages 38 and 44. An agreement preceding an order is not itself proof of concurrent practice. This is a factual uncertainty, not my finding that the judge was qualified or disqualified.
- **The requested constitutional step is not compelled merely by a state eligibility violation.** Caperton v. A. T. Massey Coal Co., Inc., 556 U.S. 868, 876, 889–90 (2009), distinguishes the federal constitutional minimum from broader judicial-conduct rules and treats constitutionally compelled recusal as exceptional. I checked the opinion through CourtListener. The petition invokes that doctrine, but connecting the alleged employment overlap to an objectively intolerable risk of bias requires an additional argument. I see no developed interjurisdictional conflict in the reasons for review, printed pages 39–44; the petition instead emphasizes a question of first impression.
- **The second question is a weak independent reason for federal review.** The petition describes Sugarloaf as an intervening Maryland ruling requiring an adequately explained statutory-fee determination, printed pages 45–53. That may support the petitioner's state-law position, but my assessment is that correcting a state-law fee ruling is less likely to attract this Court than a clean, independently established federal issue. I did not independently retrieve Sugarloaf and do not characterize its holding beyond the petition's account.
- **The procedural presentation complicates selection.** The petition describes an unreported intermediate appellate decision, a state high court grant on a different question followed by procedural dismissal, and disputes about the mandate and reconsideration, printed pages 3–8 and 34–35. I do not resolve federal finality, preservation, or adequate-state-ground questions from this one-sided account. Their uncertainty reduces confidence that this is a clean vehicle, rather than establishing a jurisdictional bar.
- **The visible attention signals are weak.** There is one distribution, no recorded CVSG, no recorded request for a response after waiver, and no Supreme Court amicus filing in the snapshot. None establishes denial; together they give little reason to lift this case above the class prior. References in the petition to state-court amici are not Supreme Court amicus support.

The nonzero residual reflects the seriousness of judicial neutrality and the possibility that the underlying employment documents support a cleaner constitutional question than the petition's presentation reveals. It also accommodates a limited remand rather than plenary review. The low probability is a cert-selection forecast, not an endorsement of the fee ruling or a factual judgment about the judge.
