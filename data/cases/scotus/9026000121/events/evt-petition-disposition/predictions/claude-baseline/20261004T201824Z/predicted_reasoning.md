# Forecast: what the Court will do with No. 26-121, Basso v. Rodriguez

**Disposition.** The petition will be denied, most likely on the order list
following the September 28, 2026 long conference, for which it has been
distributed once. P(any grant) = 0.006.

**Relists.** The docket shows a single distribution and no relist. I expect no
further distribution (P(at least one more) = 0.07). The only plausible route to
one is a call for a response: the respondents waived, and the Court does not
grant on a waived record without first requesting a brief in opposition, so a
response request would itself add a second distribution cycle. I do not expect
that call. A reschedule off the long conference is the other small residual.

**CVSG.** None (P = 0.002). The dispute is between private parties over
Maryland attorney's-fee rules; no federal statute, program, or agency interest
is implicated, and the Solicitor General has nothing to say about a Maryland
senior-judge recall rule.

**Which question, if any.** If the Court took anything it would be a narrowed
version of Question 1: whether due process is violated where a judgment is
entered by a recalled retired judge who was simultaneously engaged in the
practice of law in violation of state disqualification rules. It would not take
Question 1 as written, which bundles an evidentiary-hearing demand and the
"discovered on appeal" posture into one sentence. It would not take Question 2,
which asks for summary reversal on the strength of a later Maryland Supreme
Court decision (Sugarloaf Alliance) about the specificity required of fee
memoranda under Maryland Rule 2-703(g). That is a question of state law and
presents nothing for this Court to review.

**Summary disposition versus plenary review.** Conditional on any grant, the
likelier form is a summary disposition rather than argument (P = 0.5): the
petitioner himself asks for summary reversal and remand, and the Court's recent
judicial-bias interventions in fact-specific state cases have come as short per
curiam opinions rather than argued cases. Against that, a GVR needs an
intervening decision of this Court, and there is none; a state court's later
ruling is not a basis for a GVR. So within the grant branch the two routes
are close to even.

**Dissent from denial.** None expected (P = 0.01). No Justice has signalled an
interest in extending Caperton to recall-eligibility defects, the record on the
dispositive fact (when the judge began practicing) is thin by the intermediate
court's own account, and the Maryland Supreme Court's dismissal rests on a
state procedural rule, so a statement respecting denial would have little to
attach to.

**If granted (conditional only).** The ground would be that a structural
disqualification of the adjudicator, where the defect could not have been
discovered before judgment, states a due process claim that a state appellate
court may not dispose of by demanding proof the petitioner had no means to
obtain; the remedy would be a vacatur and remand for an evidentiary inquiry,
not a holding on the fee award itself.
