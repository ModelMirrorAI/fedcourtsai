# Forecast — Basso v. Rodriguez, No. 26-121 (evt-petition-disposition)

The docket as of the 2026-08-14 snapshot shows **zero distributions**: the
petition was filed July 20, 2026, docketed July 27, and the response is due
August 26, 2026. Everything below forecasts forward from that state.

**Disposition.** The Court denies certiorari. Expect the brief in opposition
(or a waiver) around the August 26 due date, a first distribution for the
late-September long conference of OT2026, and a denial on an early-to-mid
October 2026 order list.

**Relists.** The petition will be distributed **once** — the first
distribution the docket does not yet show — and will not be relisted after
it. It is a fact-bound, state-court civil dispute with no alleged split and a
procedural-dismissal posture below; nothing in it invites a second look.

**CVSG.** No CVSG. The case is a purely private fraud/attorney-fee dispute
under Maryland law with no federal-government interest of any kind; there is
no plausible reason to call for the Solicitor General's views.

**Which QP, if taken.** If the Court granted (it will not), it would take
only a narrowed form of QP 1 — whether due process required disqualification
or a hearing where the final judgment was entered by a retired judge
allegedly ineligible for recall — and would leave QP 2 entirely: QP 2 asks
for reversal on the basis of a Maryland Supreme Court decision about
state-law attorney-fee memoranda, which presents no federal question the
Court could review.

**Summary disposition.** No GVR and no summary reversal. The only intervening
decision the petition invokes (Sugarloaf Alliance v. Frederick County) is a
state-law ruling of the Maryland Supreme Court, which cannot ground a GVR;
no intervening federal decision bears on QP 1.

**Separate writings.** No dissent from denial and no statement respecting
denial. Caperton-type recusal claims occasionally draw a statement, but only
on egregious, cleanly presented facts; this record — an unreported
intermediate decision and a procedural dismissal under Maryland Rule 8-602 —
is not the vehicle any Justice would choose to write about.
