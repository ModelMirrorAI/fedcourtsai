## In brief

The Court declined to hear a homeowner's bid to revive his claim for attorney's fees after a Maryland court ruled against him in a long-running fraud lawsuit.

## What happened

Joseph Basso sued two real estate agents, Jose Rodriguez and Javier Szuchman, over a home he bought that flooded repeatedly; a jury found them liable for fraud and consumer-protection violations. Basso then sought attorney's fees and sanctions. A retired judge assigned to decide the fee request denied it in a one-page order. Basso's petition says this judge may have already begun practicing law as a county attorney when he signed that order, which he argues raises due-process concerns. Maryland's intermediate appellate court affirmed the denial, and the Maryland Supreme Court granted review on one issue but dismissed that part of the case on procedural grounds, later denying reconsideration.

## What the Court is being asked

Basso asks the Court to decide whether a judge's apparent, undisclosed ineligibility to serve, discovered only after the fact during a state appeal, violates due-process protections against biased decision-making, and separately whether the Court should summarily send the fee dispute back to Maryland courts in light of a later Maryland Supreme Court ruling about how detailed fee-award explanations must be.

## Where it stands
