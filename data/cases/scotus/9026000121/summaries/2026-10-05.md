## In brief

The Supreme Court declined to hear Joseph Basso's challenge to a Maryland attorney-fee ruling, leaving the lower court's decision in place.

## What happened

Joseph Basso sued two real estate sellers, Jose Rodriguez and Javier Szuchman, over an undisclosed flooding defect in a home he bought, and won a jury verdict finding fraud and consumer-protection violations. Basso then sought statutory attorney's fees and separate sanctions against the sellers. A retired judge denied the fee request in a one-page order, and the Appellate Court of Maryland affirmed. Basso later argued the judge may have been practicing law as a county attorney around the time he signed that order, which he says made the retired judge ineligible to serve. The Maryland Supreme Court granted review on one issue, dismissed it on procedural grounds, and denied reconsideration.

## What the Court is being asked

Basso asks the Court to decide whether due process required a hearing or disqualification when evidence surfaced, only after the state appeal, suggesting the judge who decided his fee request was not legally eligible to sit at the time. He separately asks the Court to summarily send the case back to Maryland's courts in light of a new Maryland Supreme Court ruling about how detailed a trial court's written explanation for a fee award must be.

## Where it stands

The petition was distributed for the Court's September 28, 2026 conference, and the respondents waived their right to respond. On October 5, 2026, the Court denied the petition.

## What each outcome would mean

Basso, who asked the Court to take up the case, sought to overturn the Maryland Appellate Court's ruling upholding the denial of his attorney's fees. The denial means that ruling stands, so Rodriguez and Szuchman are not required to pay the statutory fees or face further review of the judge's eligibility or the fee memorandum's adequacy in this case. The denial does not mean the Court found the lower court's decision correct; it simply leaves that decision in place without deciding the questions Basso raised.
