## What happened

Joseph Basso sued two real estate agents, Jose Rodriguez and Javier Szuchman, after buying a home that flooded, and a jury found them liable for fraud and consumer-protection violations. Basso then sought attorney's fees. A retired judge assigned to decide the fee request denied all fees in a one-page order years later. Basso argues that judge had, by then, gone to work as a government attorney, making him ineligible to serve as a recalled judge. Maryland's Appellate Court affirmed the fee denial, finding insufficient evidence of the judge's outside work. The Maryland Supreme Court took up one narrower fee issue but dismissed it on procedural grounds and denied reconsideration.

## What the Court is being asked

The petition raises two issues. First, it asks whether a judge's due process rights are violated, and a hearing is required, when evidence surfaces only during an appeal suggesting the judge who decided a case was actually ineligible to serve because he was practicing law at the time. Second, it asks the Court to send the case back to Maryland's courts in light of a separate, later Maryland Supreme Court decision about how detailed a trial court's written explanation for an attorney's fee award must be, which Basso says supports his position.

## Where it stands
