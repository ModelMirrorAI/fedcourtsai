## In brief

A homeowner asks the Court to review whether a Maryland judge's alleged undisclosed law practice tainted a ruling denying him attorney's fees.

## What happened

Joseph Basso sued two real estate agents, Jose Rodriguez and Javier Szuchman, over a home he says they sold him while concealing serious flooding. A jury found fraud and consumer-protection violations and awarded him damages. Basso then sought statutory attorney's fees and sanctions. A retired judge assigned to decide the fees request denied them in a one-page order. Basso later learned, he says, that this judge may have begun practicing law as a county attorney around the same time, which he argues made the judge ineligible to sit. Maryland's appellate courts affirmed the denial, and the Maryland Supreme Court granted review on a different question, then dismissed that part of the appeal on procedural grounds and denied reconsideration.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked whether requiring a hearing or disqualification is necessary when evidence surfaces on appeal suggesting the judge who decided a case was, at the time, not legally eligible to serve because he was practicing law. Separately, Basso asks the Court to send the fee dispute back to Maryland's courts in light of a new Maryland Supreme Court decision about how detailed a trial court's fee-award explanation must be.

## Where it stands

The petition for review was filed July 20, 2026, after the Court granted an extension of time to file it. The respondents' response is due August 26, 2026; the docket does not yet show any Court action on the petition itself.

## What each outcome would mean

Basso, as petitioner, is asking the Court to take up his case. If the Court grants review, it would hear the case in full and decide the judicial-eligibility and fee-explanation questions, without yet deciding who wins. If the Court denies review, the Maryland courts' decisions affirming the denial of fees and dismissing part of Basso's appeal would remain in place, leaving Rodriguez and Szuchman's position as the prevailing one on those issues.
