## In brief

The Republican National Committee asks the Supreme Court to overturn rulings blocking Arizona from requiring proof of citizenship for state voter registration and purging noncitizens from rolls near elections.

## What happened

After the 2020 election, Arizona passed laws requiring voters who register using the state's own form to show documentary proof of citizenship, and directing county officials to check citizenship status and cancel registrations of people found not to be citizens, including close to elections. Mi Familia Vota, the Democratic National Committee, the United States, and other groups sued, arguing the laws violated federal voting law, a 2018 consent decree, and constitutional protections. A federal district court sided largely with the challengers after a trial, and the U.S. Court of Appeals for the Ninth Circuit affirmed, holding that federal law and the earlier consent decree barred Arizona's proof-of-citizenship requirement and that federal law barred removing noncitizens from voter rolls within 90 days of a federal election. The Republican National Committee, which had intervened to defend the laws, appealed.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked two questions. First, whether a federal law governing voter registration, or an older court-approved settlement, stops Arizona from requiring people who use its state registration form to show documentary proof that they are U.S. citizens. Second, whether that same federal law stops Arizona from canceling the registrations of people found not to be citizens if that cancellation happens within 90 days of a federal election.

## Where it stands

The Court granted review, and the case was argued on December 8, 2026. Briefing is complete, including replies from the petitioner and from Arizona, and the case now awaits a decision on the merits.

## What each outcome would mean

The Republican National Committee asks the Court to reverse the Ninth Circuit's rulings, which had favored Mi Familia Vota and the other challengers below. If the Court affirms, Arizona would remain barred from requiring proof of citizenship on its state form and from removing noncitizens from the rolls within 90 days of an election, leaving the challengers' win in place. If the Court reverses, Arizona could enforce both measures, a result the Republican National Committee and Arizona's Attorney General (in part) support and the challengers oppose. The Court could also vacate the rulings and send the case back without deciding the questions for either side.
