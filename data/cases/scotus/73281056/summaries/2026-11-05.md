## In brief

The Republican National Committee asks the Supreme Court to let Arizona require proof of citizenship for voter registration and to purge suspected noncitizens near elections.

## What happened

After Arizona passed laws in 2022 requiring documentary proof of citizenship for state-form voter registration and directing officials to remove suspected noncitizens from voter rolls, including near elections, multiple groups sued, including the Democratic National Committee, Mi Familia Vota, and the United States. A federal district court sided largely with the challengers, and the Ninth Circuit affirmed, holding that a 2018 consent decree and the National Voter Registration Act barred the proof-of-citizenship requirement, and that a federal law barring systematic voter-roll purges within 90 days of a federal election barred Arizona's removal program. The Republican National Committee, which had intervened to defend the laws, appealed.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked whether a past settlement agreement or federal voter-registration law stops Arizona from requiring documentary proof of citizenship on its state registration form, and separately, whether that same federal law stops Arizona from removing people it has identified as noncitizens from voter rolls in the 90 days before a federal election.

## Where it stands

The Court granted review, and the case was argued on December 8, 2026. It now awaits a decision on the merits.

## What each outcome would mean

The Republican National Committee asked the Court to reverse the Ninth Circuit's decision, which had sided with Mi Familia Vota, the Democratic National Committee, and other challengers to Arizona's laws. If the Court affirms, the Ninth Circuit's rulings stand: Arizona could not require proof of citizenship on its state form or purge suspected noncitizens from the rolls close to an election. If the Court reverses, Arizona could enforce both measures. The Court could also vacate the decision and send the case back without resolving the questions for either side.
