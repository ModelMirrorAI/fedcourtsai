## In brief

The Court agreed to hear the Republican National Committee's challenge to Ninth Circuit rulings blocking two Arizona voter-registration and voter-roll laws.

## What happened

Arizona passed laws requiring proof of citizenship for state-form voter registration and directing officials to remove suspected noncitizens from voter rolls, including within 90 days of a federal election. Mi Familia Vota and other groups, along with the Democratic National Committee and the United States, sued, arguing the laws violated federal voter-registration law and, for the registration requirement, an earlier consent decree. A federal district court and a divided Ninth Circuit panel agreed with the challengers and blocked the provisions. The Republican National Committee, which had intervened to defend the laws, asked the Supreme Court to review both rulings.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked whether federal voter-registration law, or an earlier settlement agreement, stops Arizona from requiring people who register using the state's own form to show documentary proof of citizenship, and separately, whether that same federal law stops Arizona from removing suspected noncitizers from its voter rolls in the 90 days before a federal election.

## Where it stands

The Court granted the petition on June 29, 2026. Briefing is underway: the petitioner's brief and joint appendix are due August 28, 2026, and respondents' briefs are due October 13, 2026. The case has not yet been argued or decided.

## What each outcome would mean

The Republican National Committee asked the Court to take up the case after losing below. If the Court eventually reverses the Ninth Circuit, Arizona's citizenship-verification and roll-removal laws would be upheld, siding with the RNC and Arizona legislators who intervened alongside it. If the Court affirms, the injunctions blocking those laws stay in place, a result Mi Familia Vota, the Democratic National Committee, and the United States (which filed in support of the challengers) sought. If the Court vacates, the Ninth Circuit's decision would be set aside and the case sent back without resolving which side is right.
