## What happened

Arizona passed laws requiring voters who register using the state's own form to show documentary proof of citizenship, and directing election officials to periodically check voter rolls and cancel the registrations of people believed to be noncitizens, including in the weeks before a federal election. Mi Familia Vota and other groups, along with the Democratic National Committee and the United States, sued Arizona officials, arguing these measures conflict with federal voter-registration law and, for the proof-of-citizenship requirement, with an earlier settlement Arizona had agreed to. A federal district court and the Ninth Circuit Court of Appeals sided with the challengers on both points. The Republican National Committee, which had intervened to defend the laws, asked the Supreme Court to review both rulings.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked whether a federal law governing voter registration, or an earlier court-approved settlement, stops Arizona from requiring people who use the state's own registration form to provide documentary proof of citizenship. It is also asked whether that same federal law stops Arizona from running a program, within 90 days of a federal election, to cancel the registrations of voters believed not to be U.S. citizens.
