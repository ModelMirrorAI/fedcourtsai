## In brief

The Republican National Committee asks the Supreme Court whether Arizona can require proof of citizenship for voter registration and remove noncitizens from voter rolls.

## What happened

After Arizona passed laws in 2022 requiring documentary proof of citizenship for state voter-registration forms and directing removal of suspected noncitizens from voter rolls, Mi Familia Vota and other groups, along with the Democratic National Committee, the United States, and others, sued. A federal district court and the Ninth Circuit largely sided with the challengers, ruling that a 2018 consent decree and the National Voter Registration Act barred Arizona's proof-of-citizenship requirement for state forms, and that the Act's 90-day rule against systematic voter-roll purges barred Arizona's removal program for alleged noncitizens. The Republican National Committee, which had intervened to defend the laws, appealed.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked two questions: first, whether a federal consent decree or a federal voter-registration law stops Arizona from requiring people who register using the state's own form to show documentary proof of citizenship; and second, whether that same federal law stops Arizona from canceling the registrations of people identified as noncitizens in the 90 days before a federal election.

## Where it stands

The Court granted review, and the case has been argued through full briefing; oral argument was scheduled for December 8, 2026. The case awaits decision.

## What each outcome would mean

The Republican National Committee, which asked the Court to take the case, is seeking to reverse the Ninth Circuit's rulings against Arizona's laws, which were sought by Mi Familia Vota and the other challengers. If the Court sides with the Committee, Arizona could require proof of citizenship on its state registration form and could remove suspected noncitizens from voter rolls even close to an election, reversing the lower court's rulings. If the Court sides with Mi Familia Vota, the Democratic National Committee, and the other challengers instead, the injunctions against those Arizona laws would stand, leaving the proof-of-citizenship requirement and the near-election removal program blocked.
