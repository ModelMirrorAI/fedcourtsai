## In brief

The Republican National Committee asks the Supreme Court to revive Arizona laws requiring proof of citizenship for voter registration and allowing removal of noncitizens from voter rolls near elections.

## What happened

Arizona enacted laws in 2022 requiring state-form voter-registration applicants to show proof of citizenship and directing county officials to periodically check databases and cancel the registrations of voters confirmed to be noncitizens. Mi Familia Vota and other groups, along with the Democratic National Committee, the Arizona Democratic Party, and the United States, sued, arguing these laws were preempted by the National Voter Registration Act and, for the registration requirement, barred by an earlier consent decree. The district court and the Ninth Circuit largely agreed, ruling that the National Voter Registration Act and the consent decree blocked the proof-of-citizenship requirement, and that the law's removal provisions violated a federal rule against systematically purging voter rolls within 90 days of a federal election. The Republican National Committee, which had intervened to defend the laws, appealed.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court must decide two questions. First, whether federal voter-registration law or an older consent decree prevents Arizona from requiring people who register using the state's own form to show documentary proof of citizenship. Second, whether federal law prevents Arizona from removing people confirmed to be noncitizens from its voter rolls in the 90 days before a federal election, or whether that timing restriction applies only to voters who were once eligible and later became ineligible, not to those who should never have been registered.

## Where it stands

The Court granted certiorari, and the case has been fully briefed and argued; it was set for argument on December 8, 2026, and now awaits decision.

## What each outcome would mean

The Republican National Committee is asking the Court to reverse the Ninth Circuit's decision, which upheld rulings in favor of Mi Familia Vota, the Democratic National Committee, and other challengers. If the Court reverses, Arizona could enforce both the proof-of-citizenship requirement for state-form applicants and its program for removing confirmed noncitizens from voter rolls, including within 90 days of an election. If the Court affirms, the lower court's injunctions would stand, barring enforcement of both measures. The United States and some Arizona officials support reversal; other respondents, including the Democratic National Committee, defend the Ninth Circuit's ruling.
