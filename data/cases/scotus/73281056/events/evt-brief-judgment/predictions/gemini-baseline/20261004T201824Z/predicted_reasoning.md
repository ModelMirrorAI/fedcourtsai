The Court will reverse the Ninth Circuit's judgment. The majority (likely 6-3, split along ideological lines) will hold that the National Voter Registration Act (NVRA) does not preempt Arizona's requirement that applicants provide documentary proof of citizenship when using state-created registration forms. While the NVRA requires states to "accept and use" the federal form, which relies on a sworn attestation, the Court will clarify that this mandate does not restrict states from enforcing stricter requirements on their own forms. Furthermore, the Court will rule that the NVRA's 90-day "quiet period"—which restricts states from systematically removing voters within 90 days of a federal election—applies only to the removal of eligible voters who have changed residence, not to the removal of individuals like noncitizens who were never legally eligible to register in the first place. The Court will also likely determine that the 2018 consent decree entered by a former state official cannot prospectively bind the state legislature from enacting valid voter qualifications. The conservative bloc will form the majority, with a likely dissent from Justices Sotomayor, Kagan, and Jackson arguing for a broader preemptive scope of the NVRA.
