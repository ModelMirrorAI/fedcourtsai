# Forecast of the Court's judgment

The Court will reverse the judgment below and remand. The predicted split is 6–3: Roberts, Thomas, Alito, Gorsuch, Kavanaugh, and Barrett in the majority; Sotomayor, Kagan, and Jackson dissenting. This is a forecast of the ultimate judgment coalition, not a claim that every paragraph will command the same six votes. The probability of some disturbance of the judgment is 0.84; reversal is the most likely individual judgment class.

## Ground and breadth

The majority will hold that the NVRA permits Arizona to require documentary proof of citizenship with its state registration form. It will distinguish the statutory obligation to accept the federal form from the authority to design a state form, and read Inter Tribal Council's federal-form backstop as preserving that distinction. It will reject the view that the statutory necessity and equivalent-form provisions categorically require the state form to accept the same evidence as the federal form.

The resulting state-form rule will apply beyond Arizona, rather than rest on a unique factual showing about its registration system. The Court will preserve the federal form as an alternative route to federal-election registration, not overrule Inter Tribal Council or authorize documentary conditions on every federal-form applicant. These are the ground and breadth propositions committed in the structured semantic claims.

On the consent decree, the Court will reject its use as a sufficient basis for the new statewide injunction without resolving its scope, the intervening legislation, and the distinction between decree parties and other officials. It will leave decree modification and any surviving party-specific obligations for further proceedings, rather than announce that state officers may simply disregard federal judgments.

On the second question, the Court will favor the RNC's distinction between people never eligible to register because they were noncitizens and voters who subsequently became ineligible. It will reject the Ninth Circuit's categorical application of the 90-day provision to the former category. That holding will not authorize removal merely because a registrant lacks documentary proof: establishing actual noncitizenship and compliance with other applicable safeguards will remain separate questions. This second statutory issue is the likelier source of disagreement within the predicted majority and of a separate writing.

## Procedural path

The case will proceed through the remaining adversarial merits briefing and oral argument to a reasoned judgment, most likely in the first half of 2027 and by the end of the ensuing merits term. The timing is a forecast, not an observed argument setting. Neither a DIG nor an equally divided affirmance is the expected route. No authorship prediction is committed.
