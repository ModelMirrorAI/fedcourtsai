The Court is likely to reverse the Ninth Circuit's judgment. The fact that the Court already granted an emergency stay on these exact issues strongly signals that a majority of the Justices believe the Ninth Circuit erred in enjoining Arizona's proof-of-citizenship requirement. I expect a 6-3 decision along ideological lines, with the conservative majority holding that the National Voter Registration Act (NVRA) does not preempt Arizona's requirement for satisfactory evidence of citizenship. They will likely conclude that states retain constitutional authority to determine voter qualifications and that the NVRA's mandate regarding federal forms does not preclude states from enforcing citizenship checks on their own state forms, nor does the 2018 consent decree prevent the current enforcement. Chief Justice Roberts is likely to write the majority opinion, emphasizing state sovereignty in election administration, while Justice Sotomayor will likely write the principal dissent for the liberal wing, arguing that the NVRA's plain text preempts such state-level documentary requirements.
