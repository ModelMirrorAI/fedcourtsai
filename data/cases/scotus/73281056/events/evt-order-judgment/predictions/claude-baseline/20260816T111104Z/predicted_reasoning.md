# Forecast: what the Court will do with the judgment below

**Judgment class: reversed.** The Court will reverse the Ninth Circuit's
judgment on both questions presented — holding that neither the NVRA nor the
2018 LULAC consent decree bars Arizona from requiring documentary proof of
citizenship from state-form registration applicants (QP1), and that the NVRA's
90-day quiet period, 52 U.S.C. §20507(c)(2), does not prohibit Arizona's
program for cancelling the registrations of confirmed noncitizens (QP2). A
straight "reversed" entry is the modal shape; "reversed in part" or
"vacated and remanded" are the nearest alternatives if the Court resolves QP1
solely on the consent-decree ground and remands the NVRA overlay, but I expect
the disposing entry to read as a reversal.

**Vote lineup: 6–3.** Roberts, Thomas, Alito, Gorsuch, Kavanaugh, and Barrett
in the majority; Sotomayor, Kagan, and Jackson in dissent. The August 2024
partial stay in this very case (No. 24A164) restored the state-form
proof-of-citizenship requirement, and the October 2024 Beals stay lifted an
injunction against Virginia's 90-day-window noncitizen removals over three
noted dissents — both cut the same way. Barrett is the most plausible
defection on QP2 (a narrower reading of "ineligible voters" is textually
available), so a 5–4 disturbed outcome or a 6–3 split with a
concur-in-part is the main variant; a full affirmance would require both
Roberts and Barrett to cross, which the stay history makes unlikely.

**Which question, and how.** The Court will decide both granted questions
rather than DIG-ing either. On QP1 it will confine Arizona v. Inter Tribal
Council of Arizona (2013) to the federal form and hold the state form is the
State's own instrument; it will also hold, siding with the Eighth Circuit's
Carson v. Simon against the Ninth Circuit here, that a Secretary of State's
consent decree cannot permanently override later-enacted legislation
regulating federal elections. On QP2 it will read §20507(c)(2)'s "program ...
to systematically remove the names of ineligible voters" as addressed to
list-maintenance sweeps of once-eligible registrants, not to removal of
persons never eligible at all — or alternatively hold the provision must be
read narrowly to avoid the constitutional question of forcing States to keep
known noncitizens registered.

**Standing off-ramp: unlikely.** The AAANHPI respondents lead with an Article
III challenge to the RNC's standing (the State did not appeal). Expect the
Court to resolve it briefly against respondents — intervenor status below plus
a concrete competitive interest, with the companion Petersen petition
(25-1019) as backstop — rather than dismiss. P(DIG or standing dismissal
leaving the judgment intact) is small, on the order of 5%.

**Separate writings.** Expect at least one dissent (most likely Jackson or
Sotomayor writing) framing the case as a rollback of NVRA protections, and a
possible short concurrence (Thomas or Gorsuch) urging a broader
Elections-Clause / qualifications-power rationale than the majority adopts.

**Timing (unscored, stated for discipline).** Merits briefing was just
extended: petitioner's brief is due August 28, 2026, respondents' October 13,
2026. Expect argument in the December 2026 or January 2027 sitting and a
decision near the end of the Term, April–June 2027 — a case this salient
rarely comes down early.
