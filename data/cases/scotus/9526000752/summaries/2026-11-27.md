## In brief

A Wisconsin man asks a Supreme Court justice to pause his appeals-court fee deadline and sanctions while he seeks review of his racketeering lawsuit's dismissal.

## What happened

William Armstrong sued Alter Trading Corporation and others, alleging they were part of a scheme involving stolen vehicles, falsified vehicle titles, and money laundering, and that his own car was taken and destroyed through this scheme. The district court dismissed his case, finding his claims factually frivolous and his further amendment futile, and separately fined him $500 for language in a filing, barring other civil filings until he paid. Armstrong appealed to the Seventh Circuit, which denied him permission to proceed without paying fees and denied his request for appointed counsel, finding no potentially meritorious argument that the dismissal was wrong. He was ordered to pay the docketing fee within 14 days or have his appeal dismissed.

## What the Court is being asked

Armstrong asks a single justice to pause the fee deadline and further appeals-court proceedings while he pursues Supreme Court review, and to appoint him a lawyer. He argues the lower courts made legal errors in dismissing his claims and imposed the sanction without the notice and procedures he says were required, and that these errors make the appeals court's refusal to waive fees and deny counsel wrong.

## Where it stands

The application for a stay was submitted to Justice Barrett on November 17, 2026, and the case was docketed on November 25, 2026. The docket shows no ruling yet on the request.

## What each outcome would mean

Armstrong, the applicant, is asking for the pause. If granted, the fee deadline and further proceedings in his appeal would be put on hold while he seeks Supreme Court review, keeping his appeal alive without immediate payment. If denied, the Seventh Circuit's order stands, meaning Armstrong must pay the docketing fee or have his appeal dismissed for failure to prosecute, and Alter Trading and the other defendants keep the benefit of the dismissal below. Either way, the decision would be temporary and would not resolve the underlying claims.
