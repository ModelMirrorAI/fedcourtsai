# Forecast of the Court's Action — *The Nielsen Company (US), LLC v. Cumulus Media New Holdings Inc.* (No. 26-370)

- **Current state:** The docket shows **1 distribution** (`DISTRIBUTED for Conference of 11/13/2026` on October 28, 2026) and **no CVSG**. Respondent Cumulus Media New Holdings Inc. filed a waiver of its right to respond on October 19, 2026, while two cert-stage amicus briefs (Washington Legal Foundation and Law and Economics Scholars) were filed in support of Petitioner on the same date.
- **Further distributions (`relist-increment: 0.25`):** Most likely (75% probability), the Court will deny certiorari at the November 13, 2026 Conference without calling for a response or distributing the petition again. There is roughly a 25% chance that the petition is distributed at least once more—most likely because a Justice calls for a response (CFR) prior to or at the November 13 Conference, leading to a second distribution after the brief in opposition is filed (and potentially 1–2 relists thereafter if a chamber scrutinizes the vehicle).
- **Solicitor General invitation (`cvsg-increment: 0.03`):** A CVSG is unlikely (~3% probability). Although the Court called for the Solicitor General's views twenty-two years ago in *3M Co. v. LePage's Inc.*, 542 U.S. 953 (2004), this appeal arises in an interlocutory preliminary-injunction posture with ongoing district-court enforcement and second-appeal proceedings in the Second Circuit, making it an unattractive vehicle for soliciting the government's views.

## Conditional Merits / Disposition Route

- **Question Presented if granted:** If the Court were to grant certiorari, it would take the single Question Presented in the petition as written—whether a seller offering products both separately and in a bundle at prices not alleged to be below cost may be held liable under Section 2 of the Sherman Act for a "constructive tie" based on a standalone price deemed exorbitant relative to the bundle.
- **Summary disposition vs. plenary review (`summary-disposition-route: 0.10`):** Conditional on a grant, plenary review is far likelier (~90%) than summary disposition (~10%). No intervening Supreme Court decision calls for a GVR, and resolving the doctrinal tension between *Cascade Health* / *Collins Inkjet* and *LePage's* / *Cumulus* would typically require full merits briefing and oral argument rather than a per curiam summary reversal.
- **Separate writings on denial (`dissent-from-denial: 0.025`):** Should the Court deny certiorari, the denial order will almost certainly issue without separate comment (~97.5% probability). If any separate statement or dissent from denial is filed (~2.5% probability), it would most likely come from Justice Gorsuch or Justice Thomas (potentially joined by Justice Alito) highlighting the tension between the Second Circuit's "constructive tying" standard and *Pacific Bell Telephone Co. v. linkLine Communications, Inc.*, 555 U.S. 438 (2009), while noting that the interlocutory posture counsels awaiting a final judgment.
