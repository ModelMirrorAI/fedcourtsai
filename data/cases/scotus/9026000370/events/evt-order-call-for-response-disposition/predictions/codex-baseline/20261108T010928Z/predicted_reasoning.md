# Forecast of the Court's disposition

The Court will most likely **deny certiorari** after receiving Cumulus's requested response and considering the petition on a later conference list. The chance of a grant, including a summary grant, is **22%**. The legal dispute over pricing-based tying will attract consideration, but the preliminary-injunction posture and the case-specific findings about Nielsen's continuing exclusionary policy will make this a less attractive vehicle than a final judgment resolving a fully developed pricing record.

The baseline records **one distribution**, October 28 for the November 13, 2026 conference, and no completed-conference relist. The November 5 response call, with a December 7 deadline, will almost certainly produce at least one later distribution entry: **99%** probability of an increment beyond the present count. My central path is one additional distribution after the response and any reply, followed by denial, with a second additional distribution a plausible alternative. That routine return to the list is not evidence that the Court has already considered and relisted the petition. Extended serial relisting is not the modal path.

The Court will probably **not invite the Solicitor General's views**: I assign an **18%** chance of a new invitation. If an invitation occurs, the likely point is after the requested response and ensuing conference consideration, in winter or spring 2027 rather than before the December response deadline. It would lengthen the timetable substantially.

If the Court grants review, it will likely take a focused version of the petition's single question: whether Section 2 permits liability for constructive tying through standalone-versus-bundled pricing without requiring a showing that the differential is economically equivalent to below-cost pricing. The Court is more likely to address the governing liability standard than independently determine a commercially reasonable radio-data rate. This is a conditional question forecast, not a prediction that the petition will be granted or that a merits opinion will issue.

**Conditional on any grant**, plenary briefing and argument are the likely route, at **90%**, versus **10%** for a disposition accompanying the cert order. No identified intervening authority supplies a natural grant-vacate-remand rationale. If granted and ultimately decided on the merits, the likely focus would be an administrable boundary between legitimate discounts and coercive tying, rather than a blanket prohibition on all constructive-tying claims.

**Conditional on denial**, I expect no dissent or statement: **92%** chance of silence and **8%** chance of some noted dissent or statement respecting denial. Thomas or Gorsuch would be the leading possible writers, but no named writer is the central forecast. The prediction does not assert a cert vote tally or a majority-opinion author.
