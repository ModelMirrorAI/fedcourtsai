## Why denial remains the modal result

The appealed judgment affirms a **preliminary injunction**, leaving a developing factual record. The Second Circuit repeatedly frames its analysis in terms of abuse-of-discretion review, continuation of a preceding express tying policy, foreclosure of a local-data competitor, and an inadequately supported cost justification. See decision below, pp. 29–34 and 39–42. It is therefore possible to understand the holding more narrowly than the petition's proposed universal rule about above-cost prices. The petition, pp. 26–27, argues that the controlling legal question is clean and preserved; I credit that argument without treating it as an established answer to the vehicle objection.

I independently checked **Collins Inkjet Corp. v. Eastman Kodak Co., 781 F.3d 264, 272 (6th Cir. 2015)** through CourtListener, combined opinion 2982827. It expressly requires the economic equivalent of below-cost tied-product pricing for a tie enforced solely by differential pricing. That substantiates a doctrinal tension. But Collins also affirmed a preliminary injunction, and the relevant test concerns the economics of the differential, not simply whether a bundle's sticker price exceeds total costs. The petition's absence-of-below-cost-allegation premise and its assertion that the competing tests necessarily dictate opposite results remain advocacy, not independently established cost evidence. Collins arose under Section 1; this petition concerns Section 2, another distinction the petition itself acknowledges. These qualifications keep my estimate well below the odds I would assign to an unambiguously dispositive split on a final judgment.

The lower-court summary reports rehearing en banc sought and denied, with no dissent noted. That supports exhaustion of circuit review but supplies no additional dissent-based urgency signal. No government petitioner, invited government recommendation, or companion grant appears in the baseline. The opinion discusses Cumulus's intervening bankruptcy and rejects an automatic stay of the appeal; I do not infer that bankruptcy has mooted this petition or predict a settlement without evidence.

The 22% final probability balances those considerations. It is an assessment, not a mechanical pooling of the correlated response-call, amicus, and split signals. Non-grant probability is 78%; denial is overwhelmingly the most likely non-grant route.
