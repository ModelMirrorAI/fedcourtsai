## 2. Call for the Views of the Solicitor General (`cvsg-increment`)
- **Current Docket State:** No CVSG has been issued (`cvsg_date: null`).
- **Forecast Increment:** `0.04`. While private Sherman Act Section 2 petitions with multiple business amici occasionally attract a CVSG when the Court is uncertain whether a circuit split warrants review, a CVSG is unlikely here. The case arrives in an interlocutory preliminary-injunction posture, the underlying district court proceedings are stayed due to Respondent Cumulus's Chapter 11 bankruptcy, and the Second Circuit's decision turns heavily on factual findings that Nielsen's September 2025 standalone price quote was an unquantified, pretextual continuation of an unchallenged express tying policy ("Network Policy"). Once Cumulus's Brief in Opposition details those vehicle flaws, the Court is likely to deny certiorari outright rather than expend Solicitor General resources on an interlocutory, fact-bound record.

## 3. Scope of Review & Questions Presented (Conditional on Grant)
- **If Granted:** The Court would take the single Question Presented in the petition—whether a monopolist that offers two products both separately and in a discounted bundle, at prices not alleged to be below cost, may be held liable under Section 2 of the Sherman Act for "constructive tying" without satisfying the *Brooke Group* / *Linkline* / *PeaceHealth* below-cost discount-attribution standard. The Court would not reformulate the question to add the unappealed express tying issue or the Second Circuit's threshold Bankruptcy Code § 362(a)(1) automatic-stay holding.
- **Conditional Merits Ground:** If the Court granted plenary review, a majority would likely vacate the preliminary injunction and hold that where a monopolist offers a standalone product alongside a bundled discount, any claim that the price differential coerces buyers must be evaluated under an objective cost-based standard (such as the discount-attribution test of *PeaceHealth* and *Linkline*) unless the standalone offer is proven to be a sham on a developed record, and that a federal court may not impose an open-ended "commercially reasonable rate" price-cap injunction without a cost-based anchor.

- **Forecast Conditional Probability:** `0.05`. Conditional on the Court granting certiorari, plenary briefing and oral argument would be overwhelmingly more likely (95%) than a summary reversal or GVR (5%). Resolving the doctrinal relationship between express tying remedies (*United States v. Loew's, Inc.*, 371 U.S. 38 (1962)) and bundled-discount safe harbors (*Pacific Bell Telephone Co. v.
