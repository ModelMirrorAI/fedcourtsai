## In brief

Nielsen asks the Court to review a ruling that let Cumulus pursue an antitrust claim over Nielsen's pricing of bundled and standalone radio-ratings data.

## What happened

Nielsen sells radio audience data separately for local markets and nationally. Cumulus, a broadcaster, wanted to buy Nielsen's national data plus only some local data, buying the rest from a competitor, Eastlan. Nielsen's policy tied full national data to buying local data everywhere Cumulus operates; Nielsen later offered national data alone, but at a much higher price. Cumulus sued under antitrust law, and a district court issued a preliminary injunction against Nielsen's pricing practices. The Second Circuit affirmed, holding that an exceptionally high standalone price can amount to an unlawful "constructive tie" even without a formal bundling requirement.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked whether a seller that offers its products both separately and bundled, without pricing either below its own costs, can still be held liable for illegally forcing customers to buy both together, simply because a court views the separate price as too high compared to the bundled discount.

## Where it stands

The petition was distributed for the Court's November 13, 2026 conference. After that conference, the Court requested a response from Cumulus, due December 7, 2026; the docket shows no further action since.

## What each outcome would mean

Nielsen, which lost in the lower courts, is asking the Court to take up the case. If the Court grants review, it would hear the case and decide whether Nielsen's pricing can count as an unlawful tie, with no outcome assumed in advance. If the Court denies review, the Second Circuit's decision affirming the injunction against Nielsen stands, and the case continues in the lower courts under that ruling.
