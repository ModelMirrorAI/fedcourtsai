## In brief

Nielsen asks the Court to review a ruling that let Cumulus pursue an antitrust claim over Nielsen's pricing of bundled and standalone radio ratings data.

## What happened

Cumulus, a radio broadcaster, buys audience ratings data from Nielsen, including a national report and local market data. Nielsen adopted a policy linking purchases of its national report to local data purchases, then offered Cumulus a standalone price for the national report after Cumulus objected. A federal district court found this standalone price so high that it functioned as a disguised tie-in and issued a preliminary injunction against Nielsen under federal antitrust law. The United States Court of Appeals for the Second Circuit affirmed, holding that such "constructive tying" through pricing can violate the law even without proof that any price fell below Nielsen's costs. Nielsen appealed to the Supreme Court.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked whether a seller that offers products both separately and bundled, at prices nobody claims are below its own costs, can still be found to have illegally forced a customer to buy both products together simply because a court decides the standalone price was unreasonably high compared to the bundled price.

## Where it stands

The petition was distributed for the Court's conference on November 13, 2026, after the respondent waived its right to respond and amicus briefs were filed supporting Nielsen. The docket does not yet show any decision on whether the Court will hear the case.

## What each outcome would mean

Nielsen, which lost in the lower courts, is asking the Court to take up the case and reverse the Second Circuit's ruling. If the Court grants review, it will decide the legal question itself, with no outcome assumed in advance. If it denies review, the Second Circuit's decision stands, leaving in place the preliminary injunction restricting Nielsen's pricing practices while the underlying lawsuit between Nielsen and Cumulus continues in the lower courts.
