# Forecast: what the Court will do with No. 25-1316, Pearson v. Guerrero

**Disposition.** The petition will be denied, most likely on the order list following the September 28, 2026 long conference, without a call for a response and without a written dissent.

**Relists.** The docket shows **one** distribution (July 15, 2026, for the conference of September 28, 2026). No response was filed and none has been requested. I expect no further distribution: the petition is disposed of at its first conference. The one realistic path to a second distribution is a call for a response (CFR) issued at or shortly after the long conference, which would put the case back on a conference list after Texas answers; I put that at roughly one chance in ten, and a bare relist or reschedule without a CFR at a few percent more. Overall P(at least one more distribution) about 0.12.

**CVSG.** No. This is a state prisoner's Sixth Amendment habeas case against a Texas official; no federal interest is implicated and the Court does not invite the Solicitor General on questions of this kind. P about 0.005.

**Which question the Court would take, if any.** The petition presents a single question: whether a juror who was a victim of the same type of crime as the charged offense is impliedly biased where she does not unequivocally state she can be fair. If the Court took anything, it would not be that question as written, because the case arrives under 28 U.S.C. § 2254(d) and the Fifth Circuit resolved it by assuming arguendo that implied bias is clearly established law. A grant would have to be either (a) a reformulated question on whether the implied-bias doctrine is "clearly established Federal law" for AEDPA purposes, or (b) a hold for a pending direct-review implied-bias case followed by a GVR. Neither is likely; (b) is the more plausible of the two.

**Summary disposition versus plenary review.** Conditional on any grant, I put the summary route (a GVR after a hold, or a per curiam) at about 0.3. No intervening decision now on the books bears on the question, so a GVR would require the Court to grant plenary review in some other implied-bias case first. A summary reversal in a habeas petitioner's favor is rare; the Court's summary AEDPA reversals run almost entirely the other way.

**Dissent from denial.** Conditional on denial, about 0.03 that any Justice notes a dissent or writes a statement. The facts are sympathetic (a childhood molestation victim sat on a child-sexual-assault jury; life without parole), and Justice Sotomayor occasionally writes on juror-impartiality and ineffective-assistance denials, but the AEDPA posture, the absence of a response, and the juror's affirmative disclosure and statement that she thought she could be fair make a written dissent improbable.

**If granted (conditional only).** The likely ground would be that Justice O'Connor's Smith v. Phillips concurrence does not constitute clearly established law binding state courts under § 2254(d)(1), an outcome that would affirm rather than help the petitioner; a merits holding announcing a crime-victim implied-bias test on federal habeas is very unlikely because § 2254(d) forbids relief on law the Court has not itself clearly established.
