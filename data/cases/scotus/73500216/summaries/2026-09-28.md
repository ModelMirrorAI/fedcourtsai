## In brief

Donnie Pearson asks the Court to hear his case arguing his lawyer should have removed a juror who disclosed being molested as a child.

## What happened

Pearson was convicted in Texas state court of a sex offense against a child. After jury selection questioning ended but before final jury picks were made, a juror sent the judge a note saying she had been molested as a child but thought she could be fair. Neither lawyer responded, and she served on the jury. Pearson later argued in state and federal habeas proceedings that his trial lawyer was ineffective for not challenging or striking her, saying that under the circumstances she should be presumed biased. Texas courts and a federal district court rejected the claim, and the Fifth Circuit affirmed, holding this was not the kind of extreme situation in which courts presume a juror is biased.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked to decide when a juror who was previously the victim of the same type of crime as the one on trial must be treated as biased as a matter of law, even if she says she believes she can be fair, rather than requiring proof that she was actually unable to be impartial. The petition says federal appeals courts use inconsistent approaches to this question.

## Where it stands

## What each outcome would mean

Pearson, who lost in the lower courts, is asking the Court to take up his case. If the Court grants the petition, it would agree to hear the case and decide the legal question itself, without yet deciding who wins. If it denies the petition, the Fifth Circuit's ruling against Pearson stands, and his conviction remains in place.
