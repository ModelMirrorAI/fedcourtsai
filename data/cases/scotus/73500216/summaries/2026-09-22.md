## What happened

Donnie Ray Pearson was convicted in Texas state court of a sex offense against a child. During jury selection, a juror sent a note saying she had been molested as a child but thought she could be fair; no one questioned her further, and Pearson's lawyer did not challenge or remove her. Pearson later argued in state and federal habeas proceedings that his lawyer's failure to remove that juror amounted to ineffective assistance. The federal district court and the Fifth Circuit both rejected this claim, finding that the juror's history did not present the kind of extreme circumstance that would require presuming she was biased.

## What the Court is being asked

The petition asks the Court to say when a juror who was previously the victim of the same kind of crime being tried should automatically be treated as biased, even if she says she believes she can still be fair. Pearson's petition argues that federal appeals courts disagree sharply about how to answer that question, with some finding automatic bias in similar situations and others not, and that the Fifth Circuit's approach was too narrow.
