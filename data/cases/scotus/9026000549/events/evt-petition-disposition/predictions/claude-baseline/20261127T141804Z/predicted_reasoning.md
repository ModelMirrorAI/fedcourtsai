# Forecast — No. 26-549, Allyn-Feuer v. Doe

The docket shows one distribution (for the December 11, 2026 conference) after the United States waived its response.

- **Disposition:** The Court will most likely deny certiorari, either on the December 14 order list without requesting a response, or after a called-for response in spring 2027.
- **Further distribution:** About a one-in-three chance that a Justice calls for a response, in which case the petition will be redistributed once after the government's brief in opposition (likely after one or two extensions). I do not expect true relists beyond that; if a response is called for and the petition is then relisted, the grant chance rises sharply.
- **CVSG:** None. The United States is the respondent.
- **Question presented:** If the Court grants, it will take the single question as written — whether 28 U.S.C. § 2680(c)'s detention-of-goods exception bars assault and false-imprisonment claims that fall within § 2680(h)'s law-enforcement proviso merely because property was being detained at the same time. If granted, the likely ground is textual ("arising in respect of ... the detention of ... property" reaches claims about the property, not injuries to the person), with reversal more likely than not.
- **Summary disposition:** Unlikely. No intervening decision supports a GVR; a grant would be plenary.
- **Separate writing:** Should the Court deny, expect no noted dissent or statement; the small residual chance sits with Justice Sotomayor, Justice Gorsuch or Justice Jackson.
