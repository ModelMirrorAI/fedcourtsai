Allyn-Feuer alleges that an agent pushed him onto a bench after he had stepped out of his car for a checkpoint inspection. The Fifth Circuit held that his assault, battery and false-imprisonment claims otherwise came within the Federal Tort Claims Act's law-enforcement proviso. The government's motion to dismiss those claims turned on the separate exception for detention of property, 28 U.S.C. § 2680(c). That gives the Court an opportunity to decide a statutory question that can change whether the suit proceeds. The petition also identifies a California district court, *Castellanos*, that allowed an assault claim to proceed past this exception because the officers' use of force concerned the person being detained. The practical stakes are substantial in the two circuits covering much of the southwest border. [Question presented](../../../../../record/documents/questions-presented.txt) · [Petition pp. 16–20, 26–27 and App. 1a–8a](../../../../../record/documents/petition.txt)

The decisive uncertainty is whether the two appeals courts would treat the same use of force differently. In *Gasho*, the Ninth Circuit separated distress attributable to the seizure of an airplane from distress attributable to the owners' arrests. The government conceded that the exception allowed a claim arising from the arrests. In *Jeanmarie*, the Fifth Circuit expressly agreed with *Gasho* and treated force used to restrain a traveler during an ongoing inspection as incident to the inspection. The panel here likewise said the claims arose out of the car's detention. A Justice could question whether that reasoning sweeps too broadly yet see this unpublished decision as a factual application of existing law. Judge Oldham concurred only in the judgment, and no judge requested a poll on rehearing en banc. The petition's appendix supplies those details and matches the case number and dates in the staged lower-court docket. If the Court grants review, I assign 8% to a decision in the grant order. *Martin v. United States* drew plenary review on a neighboring FTCA issue. It established that other statutory exceptions can independently bar an officer-misconduct claim. The panel already applied that rule, and *Martin* left the scope of the property exception open. That favors argument over a remand to reconsider under *Martin*. On denial I assign 1.4% to a noted dissent or statement. The consequences for checkpoint travelers give a Justice a reason to speak, but the record shows little public judicial disagreement over this particular application.
