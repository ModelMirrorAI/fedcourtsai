## Case-Specific Adjustments (`2.69%` / `5.18%` → `4.0%`)

### Upward Factors (Moving Above the `2.69%` Zero-Amicus Reference)
1. **Specialist Supreme Court Clinic Counsel:** Petitioner is represented at the certiorari stage by repeat Supreme Court practitioner Lori Alvino McGill and the University of Virginia School of Law Supreme Court Litigation Clinic (joining trial/appellate counsel). Clinic-screened petitions present cleaner statutory framing and draw CFRs and grants at substantially higher rates than typical zero-amicus private paid petitions. Notably, *Ali v. Federal Bureau of Prisons*, 552 U.S. 214 (2008) — the Court's leading decision construing § 2680(c)'s law-enforcement phrase — was itself granted out of an unpublished per curiam circuit decision.
2. **Direct Adjacency to *Martin v. United States* (2025) and Cross-Ideological Hooks:** Just last Term in *Martin*, 605 U.S. at 403–07, Justice Gorsuch wrote for a unanimous Court holding that § 2680(h)'s law-enforcement proviso modifies only subsection (h) and does not override § 2680's other exceptions, while observing that legislators may have seen no need to extend the proviso across § 2680 if other exceptions would not naturally shield wrong-house raid torts; Justice Sotomayor (joined by Justice Jackson) wrote separately to caution against expansive readings of neighboring § 2680 exceptions that nullify Congress's intentional-tort remedy. Petitioner's textual argument — that § 2680(c)'s phrase "arising in respect of ... the detention of any goods, merchandise, or other property" reaches property-detention injuries (*Kosak*, *Ali*) rather than distinct personal-injury assaults or false imprisonment committed alongside a vehicle search — speaks directly to both textualist skepticism of overbroad statutory exceptions and post-*Egbert v. Boule* concerns over leaving Border Patrol checkpoint misconduct without either a *Bivens* or FTCA remedy.
3. **Panel Friction Below:** Although the Fifth Circuit panel (Stewart, Graves, Oldham, JJ.) affirmed per curiam under binding circuit precedent (*Jeanmarie v. United States*, 242 F.3d 600 (5th Cir. 2001); *Davila v. United States*, 713 F.3d 248 (5th Cir. 2013)), the panel explicitly described Petitioner's "carte blanche" accountability argument as "troubling" (`Pet. App. 7a`), and Judge Oldham concurred *only in the judgment* (`Pet. App. 1a n.*`).

### Downward and Restraining Factors (Keeping `P(grant)` at `0.04`, Below `5.18%`)
1. **Solicitor General Waiver Hurdle:** Because the Solicitor General waived a response, the Court will almost certainly not grant plenary review without first calling for a response (CFR). Thus `P(grant)` must clear two sequential gates: `P(CFR) × P(grant | CFR)`. Even crediting UVA Clinic authorship and *Martin* salience with an elevated ~16–17% CFR probability, the Solicitor General's Brief in Opposition typically defeats certiorari in roughly four out of five CFR'd cases.
2. **Shallow, Stale, and Concession-Based Circuit "Split":** Inspection of the Ninth Circuit's opinion in *Gasho v. United States*, 39 F.3d 1420, 1433–34 (9th Cir. 1994), shows that the conflict is far shallower than an active multi-circuit division. *Gasho* is a 32-year-old decision in which **the Government conceded** in a single sentence that § 2680(c) did not bar an arrest-related intentional tort claim because it covered goods rather than persons, on facts involving arrests days after an aircraft seizure — and the Ninth Circuit affirmed summary judgment for the Government on that claim anyway. In the three decades since *Gasho* (and 25 years since *Jeanmarie*), no third circuit has squarely joined either side on simultaneous checkpoint vehicle-detention torts (*Davinci Aircraft*, 926 F.3d 1117 (9th Cir. 2019), and *Angulo v. Brown*, 978 F.3d 942 (5th Cir. 2020), confirm the narrow footprint). Moreover, the decision below is **unpublished** (`2026 WL 1269833`), and **not a single active Fifth Circuit judge requested an en banc poll** (`Pet. App. 24a`) — including Judge Oldham.
3. **Vehicle Imperfections Exploitable in an SG BIO:** The Fifth Circuit opinion reveals significant pre-clinic framing and factual complications. First, in the Fifth Circuit, Petitioner primarily argued (1) that § 2680(h)'s proviso overrides § 2680(c) — an argument squarely foreclosed by *Martin* — and (2) that Border Patrol agents were conducting an immigration rather than customs inspection (`Pet. App. 3a, 6a`), giving the Solicitor General strong preservation/framing objections against the clean "goods vs. persons" textual theory now emphasized by clinic counsel. Second, on the facts, a K-9 alerted to Petitioner's car at an interior checkpoint (`Pet. App. 2a`), supplying probable cause for the 25-minute secondary detention and vehicle search, and Petitioner's complaint intertwined property-damage allegations (spilled cleaning solution damaging belongings, conversion, property loss) with being pushed onto a bench without alleged lasting physical injury (`Pet. App. 2a, 13a–14a`), alongside potential alternative defenses under § 2680(a) or state-law privilege.
4. **Absence of a GVR Anchor:** Corpus verification of OT2025/OT2026 grants confirms there is no pending SCOTUS FTCA merits case for which this petition would be held, and *Martin* (June 2025) was already applied by the Fifth Circuit below (`Pet. App. 4a–5a`). Consequently, the routine GVR component that accounts for nearly half of `baseline` / zero-amicus grant-family base rates is compressed to near zero (`P(GVR/summary) ≈ 0.005` unconditional; `summary-disposition-route = 0.12` conditional on grant).

Combining `P(plenary grant) ≈ 0.035` (`~0.165 CFR × ~0.21 conditional grant`) with `P(summary/GVR) ≈ 0.005` yields **`probability = 0.04`**.
