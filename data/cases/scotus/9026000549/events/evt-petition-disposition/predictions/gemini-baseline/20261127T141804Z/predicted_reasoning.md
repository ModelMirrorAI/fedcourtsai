# Predicted Court Action — *Boaz Allyn-Feuer v. John Doe, Border Patrol Agents, et al.* (No. 26-549)

## Disposition and Procedural Trajectory

The Court will **deny** the petition for a writ of certiorari (`P(any grant) = 0.04`, `P(denied) ≈ 0.96`).

- **Current state and further distributions (`relist-increment = 0.18`):** As of the November 27, 2026 snapshot, the petition has been **distributed once** (on November 24, 2026, for the Conference of December 11, 2026) following the Solicitor General's November 19, 2026 waiver of the right to respond, and has never been relisted. Most likely (~82%), the Court will deny certiorari on the December 14, 2026 order list immediately following that first Conference without further distribution. There is a meaningful minority chance (`0.18`) that the petition is distributed at least once more — primarily (~16%) because a single Justice calls for a response (CFR) from the Solicitor General before or at the December 11 Conference, which would produce a Brief in Opposition in early 2027 and a second (and possibly third) distribution, plus a small (~2%) chance of a standalone relist or conference reschedule without a CFR. Even if the Court calls for a response, denial after receipt of the Solicitor General's Brief in Opposition remains the most probable outcome.
- **Call for the Views of the Solicitor General (`cvsg-increment = 0.001`):** The Court will **not** issue a CVSG. No CVSG is on the docket, and the United States of America is already a named Respondent represented by the Solicitor General; if the Court seeks the Government's views, it will request a response to the petition (CFR) rather than inviting an amicus brief via CVSG.

## Scope of Review and Disposition Route (Conditional on a Grant)

- **Question presented (if granted):** If the Court grants plenary review (after first calling for a response from the Solicitor General), it will take the petition's **single question presented** substantially as framed — whether the detention-of-goods clause of 28 U.S.C. § 2680(c) bars intentional-tort claims against the United States for personal injuries (assault, battery, and false imprisonment) otherwise actionable under § 2680(h)'s law-enforcement proviso when committed during a detention and inspection of personal property. If granted on the merits, the Court would likely narrow its analysis to distinguish claims arising out of injury to or loss of detained property from claims arising out of physical force or restraint directed against the person, remanding any remaining FTCA defenses (such as § 2680(a) discretionary-function immunity or state-law tort privilege) to the Fifth Circuit.
- **Plenary review vs. summary disposition (`summary-disposition-route = 0.12`):** Conditional on any grant, **plenary briefing and oral argument** (~88% of conditional grant mass) is far likelier than a summary disposition in the cert order (`0.12`). *Martin v. United States*, 605 U.S. 395 (2025), was decided eleven months before the Fifth Circuit's May 2026 decision and was already applied below, and no pending OT2026 merits case presents the § 2680(c)/§ 2680(h) question as a hold-and-GVR anchor. A cert-order disposition would essentially require an unexpected Government confession of error or partial vacatur request in a called-for Brief in Opposition; summary per curiam reversal against the United States over an established Fifth Circuit rule without merits briefing is highly unlikely.

## Separate Writings on Denial

- **Dissents or statements respecting denial (`dissent-from-denial = 0.03`):** Conditional on denial, the overwhelmingly likely outcome (~97%) is a bare, unsigned order-list denial with **no separate writing**. In the small tail (`0.03`) where a separate writing accompanies denial — almost certainly only after an intervening CFR and Brief in Opposition — the likeliest authors are **Justice Sotomayor** (`0.014`, potentially joined by Justice Jackson) or **Justice Gorsuch** (`0.009`), highlighting how the combination of *Egbert v. Boule*, 596 U.S. 482 (2022), and the Fifth Circuit's expansive reading of § 2680(c) extinguishes both *Bivens* and FTCA remedies for physical assaults at Border Patrol checkpoints.
