The Supreme Court is very likely to deny review in *Allyn-Feuer v. John Doe, Border Patrol Agents*, with only a 4% chance (one in twenty-five) of taking the case. Petitioner asks whether a federal sovereign-immunity exception covering detained goods also blocks lawsuits against the government when Border Patrol agents physically push or detain a driver during a checkpoint car search. Although Supreme Court clinic lawyers represent the petitioner and two appeals courts disagree, the government waived its response, no outside groups filed supporting briefs, the thirty-two-year-old split is shallow, and the unpublished decision below has factual flaws that make Supreme Court review unlikely.
