## In brief

A motorist asks the Court to revive his lawsuit claiming Border Patrol agents assaulted and falsely imprisoned him during a vehicle search at a checkpoint.

## What happened

Boaz Allyn-Feuer was stopped at a Border Patrol checkpoint in Texas, directed to secondary inspection, and, while agents searched his car, was pushed onto a bench by one agent. He sued the United States under the Federal Tort Claims Act for false imprisonment, assault and battery, and related claims, and sued unnamed agents directly over the search itself. The district court dismissed the suit, and the Fifth Circuit affirmed, holding that a legal exception covering detained property barred his claims because the mistreatment happened while his car was being searched, even though another part of the same law specifically allows such claims against law enforcement officers. The court of appeals called this result "troubling" but said it was bound by its own prior decisions.

## What the Court is being asked

The law that lets people sue the federal government for wrongs by its employees carries an exception protecting the government when a claim concerns property that customs or law enforcement officers have detained. A separate provision specifically allows lawsuits against law enforcement officers for assault and false imprisonment. The Court is asked whether the property exception blocks those assault and false-imprisonment claims whenever the mistreatment happens to occur while the person's belongings are also being searched or held, even though the claims are not actually about the property itself.

## Where it stands

The petition was distributed for the Court's conference on December 11, 2026, after the government waived its right to respond. No action on the petition has yet been recorded.

## What each outcome would mean

Allyn-Feuer, who lost in the lower courts, is asking the Court to take up his case. If the Court grants review, it would hear full arguments and decide the legal question itself, deciding nothing yet about who should win. If it denies review, the Fifth Circuit's ruling stands, leaving Allyn-Feuer's claims dismissed and leaving in place that court's approach, which differs from how another federal appeals court handles the same issue.
