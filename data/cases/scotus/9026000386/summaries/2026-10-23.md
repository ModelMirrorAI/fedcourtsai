## In brief

A former school bus driver asks the Court to review his unsuccessful lawsuit claiming Michigan wrongly denied him unemployment benefits during summer breaks.

## What happened

Marlin Lowery drove a bus for Cheboygan Area Public Schools and was laid off each summer. Michigan's unemployment agency denied him benefits for the summers of 2021 and 2022 under a state law that blocks summer benefits for school employees who have assurance of being rehired in the fall. After losing before an administrative judge and in state court, Lowery sued the school district and state agency in federal court, arguing the denials violated his constitutional due process rights and federal unemployment law. A federal district court dismissed his complaint for failing to state a valid claim, and the Sixth Circuit Court of Appeals affirmed that dismissal, also denying rehearing.

## What the Court is being asked

Lowery asks the Court to decide whether the state law's summer-benefits restriction conflicts with an older federal law meant to extend unemployment coverage to school employees, whether a later state law amendment undercuts the restriction used to deny him benefits, and whether he was treated differently because of his sex in violation of equal protection guarantees.

## Where it stands

The respondents, Michigan's unemployment agency and related state officials, filed a waiver stating they will not respond to the petition. The petition has not yet been distributed for a conference, and the docket shows no action by the Court on it since Lowery filed it.

## What each outcome would mean

Lowery, the petitioner, is asking the Court to take up his case and reverse the lower court's decision. If the Court grants the petition, it would agree to hear the case fully and decide the legal questions, though granting review alone would not decide who is right. If the Court denies the petition, the Sixth Circuit's ruling affirming dismissal of Lowery's lawsuit would stand, leaving the denial of his unemployment benefits claims in place.
