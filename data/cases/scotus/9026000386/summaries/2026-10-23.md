## In brief

A former school bus driver asks the Court to revive his lawsuit claiming Michigan's denial of his summer unemployment benefits violated federal law and his constitutional rights.

## What happened

Marlin Lowery drove a bus for Cheboygan Area Public Schools. When his hours were cut each summer, Michigan's Unemployment Insurance Agency denied him benefits under a state law that blocks summer unemployment pay for school employees who have assurance of returning to work in the fall. After losing before an administrative judge and in state court, Lowery sued the school district and state agency in federal court, arguing violations of his due process rights. A magistrate judge recommended dismissal, the district court agreed, and the Sixth Circuit affirmed, finding Lowery had no protected property interest in benefits he was never found eligible to receive and had not plausibly alleged a constitutional violation. The appeals court also denied rehearing.

## What the Court is being asked

Lowery asks the Court to decide whether Michigan's law denying summer unemployment benefits to school employees with reasonable assurance of returning to work conflicts with, and is overridden by, a 1970 federal law extending unemployment coverage to school employees. He also argues a later state amendment undercut the basis for denying his benefits, and that he was treated differently because of his sex, in violation of equal protection principles.

## Where it stands

Michigan and the other respondents have waived their right to file a response to the petition. The petition remains pending; the docket does not show that it has been distributed for a conference or otherwise acted on.

## What each outcome would mean

Lowery, the petitioner, is asking the Court to take up his case and reverse the Sixth Circuit's ruling against him. If the Court grants the petition, it would agree to hear the case and decide the legal questions raised, without yet deciding who wins. If it denies the petition, the Sixth Circuit's decision affirming dismissal of Lowery's lawsuit stands, leaving the denial of his unemployment claims and his lawsuit's dismissal in place.
