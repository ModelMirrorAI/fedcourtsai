## What happened

Marlin Lowery, a school bus driver for Cheboygan Area Schools in Michigan, applied for unemployment benefits during summer breaks in 2021 and 2022. The school and the Michigan Unemployment Insurance Agency denied his claims, citing a state law provision allowing denial when an employee has a "reasonable assurance" of returning to work the next school year. Lowery, representing himself, argued that a federal law expanding unemployment coverage to school employees should have controlled, and that a female coworker received benefits he was denied. Michigan state courts rejected his claims, and a federal district court and the Sixth Circuit Court of Appeals also ruled against him. The Sixth Circuit denied rehearing, and Lowery is now asking the Supreme Court to review that decision.

## What the Court is being asked

Lowery is asking the Court to decide whether the state law that let his employer deny him benefits based on a promise of reemployment conflicts with, and improperly narrows, a federal law meant to extend unemployment coverage to school employees. He also asks whether a later state amendment removing that reemployment-assurance basis for denial means agencies should no longer be allowed to rely on the earlier rule. Finally, he asks whether denying him benefits while a female coworker received them amounted to unlawful sex discrimination.
