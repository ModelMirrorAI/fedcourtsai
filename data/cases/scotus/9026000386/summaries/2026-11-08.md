## In brief

A former school bus driver asks the Court to revive his lawsuit claiming Michigan wrongly denied him unemployment benefits during summer breaks.

## What happened

Marlin Lowery drove a school bus for Cheboygan Area Public Schools. When his hours were cut each summer, Michigan's Unemployment Insurance Agency denied his benefits under a state law that blocks summer unemployment pay for school employees who have assurance of returning to work in the fall. After losing before an administrative judge and in state court, Lowery sued the school district and state agency in federal court, arguing his constitutional due process rights were violated. A federal district court dismissed his complaint for failing to state a valid claim, finding he had no protected property interest in benefits he was never found eligible to receive. The Sixth Circuit Court of Appeals affirmed that dismissal, and later denied his request for rehearing.

## What the Court is being asked

Lowery's petition raises several issues: whether the Michigan law denying summer benefits to school employees conflicts with an older federal law meant to extend unemployment coverage to school workers, whether a later Michigan amendment should have changed the outcome, and whether he was treated differently because of his sex in violation of equal protection guarantees, since he says a female coworker received benefits he was denied.

## Where it stands

The respondents waived their right to file a response. The petition was distributed for the Court's December 4, 2026 conference, where the justices will decide whether to take up the case.

## What each outcome would mean

Lowery, the petitioner, is asking the Court to hear his case and reverse the Sixth Circuit's decision. If the Court grants the petition, it would agree to hear the case and decide the legal questions Lowery raises, without yet deciding who is right. If the Court denies the petition, the Sixth Circuit's ruling affirming dismissal of Lowery's lawsuit stays in place, and his claims remain dismissed.
