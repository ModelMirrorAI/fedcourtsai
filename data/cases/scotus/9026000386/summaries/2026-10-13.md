## In brief

A former school bus driver asks the Court to revive his lawsuit over denied unemployment benefits during summer layoffs.

## What happened

Marlin Lowery drove a bus for Cheboygan Area Public Schools. When his hours were cut each summer in 2021 and 2022, Michigan's unemployment agency denied his benefit claims under a state law that withholds summer benefits from school employees who have assurance of returning to work in the fall. After losing before the agency and in state court, Lowery sued the school district and state agency in federal court, claiming the denial violated his due process rights and, he later argued, treated him differently because of his sex. The district court dismissed his complaint for failing to state a valid claim, and the court of appeals affirmed, also finding he had forfeited some arguments by not raising them earlier or by raising them for the first time on appeal.

## What the Court is being asked

Lowery asks the Court to decide whether the Michigan law denying summer unemployment benefits to school employees conflicts with an older federal law extending unemployment coverage to school workers, whether a later amendment to the state law undercuts the basis for denying his benefits, and whether denying him benefits while a female coworker received hers amounted to unlawful sex discrimination.

## Where it stands

The petition was filed in June 2026, and the respondents' response is due in October 2026. The docket does not yet show the Court taking any action on the petition.

## What each outcome would mean

Lowery, the petitioner, is asking the Court to take up his case. If the Court grants the petition, it would hear the case and decide these questions itself, without yet deciding who wins. If the Court denies the petition, the lower court's decision affirming dismissal of Lowery's lawsuit stays in place, and the school district and state agency keep the win they secured below.
