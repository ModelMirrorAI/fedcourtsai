# Predicted reasoning — what the Court will do with No. 26-386

**Disposition: denied**, by an unsigned order on the first conference at which the
petition is considered, with no separate writing.

## Procedural path

- **Distributions.** The docket shows zero distributions — this is the arrival
  moment, so that is the state I forecast from. Expect the respondents (a
  Michigan school district, school officials, the State of Michigan, and its
  Unemployment Insurance Agency) to **waive** their response rather than file a
  brief in opposition; the Court will not call for one. The petition will then
  be distributed once, for a conference in late October or November 2026, and
  denied at that conference. I expect **exactly one distribution and no
  relist**: nothing in the petition would prompt a Justice to hold it over.
- **CVSG.** None. No federal party is involved and the petition raises no
  question on which the Solicitor General's views would be sought; a CVSG for a
  pro se § 1983 unemployment-benefits claim is essentially unheard of.
- **Which question the Court would take, if any.** None as written. The three
  questions presented are not framed as legal questions the Court could answer:
  QP 1 and QP 2 ask whether a Michigan statute "dismantled" or "corrected" the
  Employment Security Amendments of 1970, and QP 3 asks whether the defendants
  violated *Reed v. Reed*. If the Court were somehow to grant, it would have to
  reformulate the case entirely as a question about whether the Federal
  Unemployment Tax Act's conditions on state law are privately enforceable
  against a state agency via § 1983 — a question no court below decided and
  the petition does not present.
- **Summary disposition.** In the improbable event of any grant, it would be a
  GVR rather than plenary review; there is no intervening decision that bears
  on the case, so even that route is only nominally open. Plenary review of this
  petition is not a realistic outcome.
- **Dissent from denial.** None. The case presents no issue any Justice has
  signaled interest in, and the pleading is not one a Justice would write
  separately about.

## Merits-shaped content, conditional only

If the Court were to take the case in some reformulated form, the likely ground
of decision would be that the federal unemployment-compensation statutes (26
U.S.C. §§ 3301–3311 and the Social Security Act's Title III conditions) impose
conditions on state law as a term of federal tax credits and grants, not
individually enforceable rights, so a § 1983 claim against a state agency's
benefits determination does not lie. That would be an affirmance. But this is
conditional on a grant I put at well under one percent.
