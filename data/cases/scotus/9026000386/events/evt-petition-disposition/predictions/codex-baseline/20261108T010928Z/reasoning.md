## Questions presented and actual decision below

I read `questions-presented.txt`, the petition's account and reasons for review, the April 1 appellate order and June 24 panel-rehearing denial in `appendix.txt`, and `lower-court-docket.txt`. The appendix matches the petition's case number, parties, judgment date, and rehearing date. It is the substantive lower-court source: the provisioned docket summary has no opinion body and says its panel is not recorded, whereas the appendix identifies Norris, Bloomekatz, and Hermandorfer. The summary's failure to identify an en banc petition does not mean there was no panel rehearing; the appendix expressly supplies the latter.

QP1 challenges Michigan's reasonable-assurance unemployment exclusion as incompatible with Public Law 91-373. QP2 argues that Michigan's higher-education provision corrected that exclusion more broadly. QP3 invokes Reed v. Reed against alleged sex-based differential treatment. The petition says a female comparator received benefits and asserts that its federal issues were preserved. It requests at least vacatur and remand for consideration of federal law. Its estimate of affected Michigan support staff is an advocacy claim, not independently established evidence of national scope.

The April 1 order, appendix A, especially pages 3–5, gives several reasons why those questions are not cleanly presented:

- The action was a section 1983 procedural/substantive due-process suit. The Sixth Circuit rejected the jurisdictional challenge and recognized federal jurisdiction; the adverse judgment is not simply a refusal to hear any federal claim.
- It affirmed dismissal for failure to state a claim. It said Lowery did not specifically contest the district court's due-process reasoning on appeal, and treated those arguments as forfeited.
- It said the complaint had not asserted Title VII or equal-protection sex-discrimination claims, so those claims could not be newly introduced on appeal. That separates QP3 from a preserved decision contradicting Reed.
- It addressed section 421.27(i)(5), concluding that its higher-education language did not apply to this public-school bus driver. It also said citing section 104 of Public Law 91-373 did not explain how the defendants violated due process or cure the pleading deficiencies. This conflicts with the petition's characterization of an entirely ignored federal-law issue.

The unpublished order therefore supplies procedural and pleading obstacles independent of petitioner's preferred statutory interpretation. The petition identifies no developed disagreement among federal circuits on a preserved dispositive question, and the appendix shows no dissent or contested en banc disposition. These are substantial vehicle defects, not a prediction that the petitioner's underlying fairness concerns lack merit. Self-representation is not itself a legal disqualification; the actual mismatch between the questions and the judgment drives the discount.
