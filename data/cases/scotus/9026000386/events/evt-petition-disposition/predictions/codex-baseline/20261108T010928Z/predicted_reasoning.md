# Forecast of the Court's action

The Court will deny the petition, most likely in the ordinary orders following the December 4, 2026 conference. The baseline contains **one distribution and zero relists**. I expect **zero additional distributions**, with a 2.5% chance of at least one. If the petition is distributed again, one further distribution is more likely than an extended series.

The Court will not call for the Solicitor General's views. A future invitation has a 0.1% probability; there is no predicted invitation date. The federal-state benefits framework will not overcome the procedural defects in this individual vehicle.

A denial will most likely carry no explanation, dissent, or statement respecting denial. Conditional on denial, I assign 0.4% to any such dissent or statement. I predict no particular Justice will write; the small residual writing risks are recorded in the distribution rather than treated as a forecast of public votes.

If review is granted, the most plausible question would narrow QP1 to whether federal unemployment law creates an enforceable entitlement for noninstructional public-school employees that can support this section 1983 due-process action despite the reasonable-assurance exclusion. That is a conditional reformulation, not a claim that the complaint preserved it. QP2's higher-education exception and QP3's sex-discrimination theory are less likely candidates, given the appellate order's conclusions that the exception does not cover this employment and that the discrimination claim was not pleaded.

Conditional on any grant, I place 25% on a GVR or another judgment accompanying the cert order, and 75% on plenary review. No identified intervening decision makes a GVR the expected route; the petition's alternative request for one does not establish a basis for it. Any substantive reconsideration would first have to confront forfeiture and the adequacy of the pleaded federal claim. A denial will leave those lower-court conclusions undisturbed without resolving the broader statutory or discrimination questions on their merits.
