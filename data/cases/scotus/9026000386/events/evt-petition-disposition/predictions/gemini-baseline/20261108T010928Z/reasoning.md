## Case-Specific Adjustments from the Provisioned Record
Starting from the 5.15% `baseline` reached anchor, strong downward adjustments are warranted based on the snapshot (`record/snapshots/2026-11-08.json`) and the provisioned filings (`record/documents/questions-presented.txt`, `petition.txt`, `appendix.txt`, and `lower-court-docket.txt`):

1. **Pro Se Paid Filing and Waiver of Response:** Although docketed on the paid docket (`sJsonCaseType: "Paid"`), the petitioner, Marlin D. Lowery, is self-represented (`pro se` on the petition cover and in the Sixth Circuit and district court captions; contact details scrubbed in `context.json` and `documents.json`). On October 22, 2026, the State respondents (Michigan Unemployment Insurance Agency, state officials, and the State of Michigan, represented by the Michigan Department of Attorney General) filed a waiver of their right to respond, and the school district respondents filed no response before the petition was distributed on November 4, 2026, for the December 4, 2026 conference. Under Supreme Court practice, the Court virtually never grants plenary review or summary relief without first calling for a response.
2. **Severe Vehicle and Preservation Defects:** As documented in `appendix.txt` (which reproduces the Sixth Circuit's unpublished order of April 1, 2026, in No. 25-1514, the June 24, 2026 order denying panel rehearing, and the Eastern District of Michigan's January 23, 2025 Magistrate Judge Report & Recommendation and February 26, 2025 and July 21, 2025 orders in No. 1:24-cv-11604), the decision below is a four-page, unpublished, non-precedential order entered without oral argument under Fed. R. App. P. 34(a). More importantly:
   - In the district court, Lowery brought only four counts under 42 U.S.C. § 1983 alleging Fourteenth Amendment procedural and substantive due process violations (plus a passing reference to the CARES Act) arising from the denial of summer unemployment benefits in 2021 and 2022 while employed as a school bus driver with reasonable assurance of returning in the fall.
   - On appeal to the Sixth Circuit, Lowery **forfeited** his procedural and substantive due process claims by failing to address the district court's reasoning for dismissing them under Rule 12(b)(6).
   - Lowery's gender-discrimination arguments under the Equal Protection Clause (*Reed v. Reed*) and Title VII (Question Presented 3) were **never pleaded in his complaint** and were rejected by the Sixth Circuit as improperly raised for the first time on appeal (*Tchankpa v. Ascena Retail Grp., Inc.*, 951 F.3d 805, 817 (6th Cir. 2020)).
   - Prior to filing his federal § 1983 action, Lowery appealed the administrative denial to the Cheboygan County Circuit Court, which affirmed the agency on March 28, 2024, and Lowery did not appeal that decision to the Michigan Court of Appeals.
3. **Lack of Merits Substance or Circuit Conflict:** Questions Presented 1 and 2 rest on a misreading of federal and Michigan unemployment statutes. While Pub. L. No. 91-373 (1970) extended unemployment coverage to certain educational employees, Congress subsequently enacted and mandated the "between terms" / "reasonable assurance" denial rules in 26 U.S.C. § 3304(a)(6)(A) for educational employees, which Michigan implemented in Mich. Comp. Laws § 421.27(i)(2). As the Sixth Circuit noted, Mich. Comp. Laws § 421.27(i)(5) expressly applies to services performed "for an institution of higher education" and adds the proviso "unless a denial is required as a condition for full tax credit against the tax imposed by the federal unemployment tax act, 26 USC 3301 to 3311." There is no circuit conflict, no federal preemption issue, and no cognizable constitutional violation.

Combining these factors, the probability of a grant (or GVR) is far below the 5.15% `baseline` reached anchor and well below the 1.8% single-distribution (`relist_bucket = 0`) base rate. I set `probability = 0.002` (0.2%).
