# Rationale

I assign a 0.004 probability to any grant and predict denial. The cell is a forward, paid-petition arrival forecast: the snapshot shows zero distributions and no CVSG because no conference signal can yet exist at this moment. Under the matching sal-v3 statpack, pooling the baseline band's bracketed `reached` rate over OT2017 through OT2025 gives the appropriate arrival anchor: 863 grant-family dispositions among 13,163 weighted resolved petitions, or 6.556%. I adjust sharply downward from that whole-paid-population rate based on the petition itself.

The strongest negative is the absence of a certworthy conflict. The four questions presented repeat two claims as errors by the Fourth Circuit and the district court; the petition does not identify a circuit split or a conflicting precedential rule. The Fourth Circuit's unpublished, one-paragraph per curiam opinion simply found no reversible error and dispensed with oral argument. The district court, meanwhile, expressly applied *Groff v. DeJoy* and treated the safety risk to elderly, medically vulnerable residents as a contextual, non-economic burden. Its opinion cited numerous health-care vaccination decisions reaching similar conclusions. This posture makes the petition look like fact-bound error correction rather than a vehicle for resolving an acknowledged legal disagreement.

The second issue is still weaker. The district court found that the petitioner supplied no Rule 56(d) affidavit or declaration, had more than 18 months of discovery, knew the employer's undue-hardship theory well before the corporate depositions, and delayed seeking relief. That case-specific diligence ruling is an independent vehicle problem. The petition also cites 28 U.S.C. § 1257 for review of a federal court of appeals judgment, another sign that the vehicle and presentation are unlikely to attract review.

The auxiliary modern Fourth Circuit cut has a 2.579% grant-family rate, but it is not the arrival anchor and does not overcome these petition-specific defects. No brief in opposition was provisioned, so I could not test whether the respondent disputes the factual characterization or raises additional preservation problems. The petition and all appended lower-court decisions were available in full and were not truncated.

From zero distributions, I put 0.975 on at least one later distribution because this is an accepted, paid petition with a response deadline; that claim prices the ordinary first distribution, not an actual relist. I put only 0.002 on a later CVSG because the private Title VII dispute presents no split and the record offers no reason the Court would seek federal views. Conditional on the rare event of a grant, I put 0.40 on disposition in the cert order, slightly above the prior-Term paid conditional rate of 300/863 (34.76%): the petition is framed as correcting a claimed misapplication of existing precedent, although the absence of an intervening decision makes plenary clarification modestly more plausible than a GVR or summary reversal. I put 0.015 on a dissent from or statement respecting denial; the religious-liberty subject creates a small possibility, but the weak vehicle makes separate writing unlikely.

Live retrieval did not improve the record. Two corpus-prior queries did not return before the local corpus service had to be abandoned, and therefore produced no result or ranged-read statistic. A CourtListener MCP search for pre-petition decisions applying *Groff* to COVID-19 vaccine disputes was rate-limited. I therefore relied on the committed statpack and the provisioned snapshot, petition, questions presented, and lower-court appendices. The failed retrievals increase uncertainty about whether a newly developed conflict exists, but the petition's own failure to allege one remains strong evidence against review.
