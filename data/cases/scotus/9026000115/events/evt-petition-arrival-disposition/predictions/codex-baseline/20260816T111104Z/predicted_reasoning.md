# Forecast

The petition will be distributed once from its present zero-distribution arrival state and then denied, most likely at the first conference at which it is considered. It will not draw a further conference after that initial distribution. The Court will not call for the views of the Solicitor General.

The petition presents the case as an application of *Groff v. DeJoy* to a health-care employer's COVID-19 vaccination policy, but the judgment below is unpublished and the Fourth Circuit affirmed in a short per curiam disposition without creating a precedential rule. The petition identifies no conflict among courts of appeals. Its separate Rule 56(d) issue turns on the petitioner's diligence in identifying an expert and complying with the discovery schedule. Those features make plenary review and summary intervention alike unlikely.

If review were granted, the Court would take a narrowed version of the first question: whether a health-and-safety risk to vulnerable residents can establish undue hardship under *Groff* without proof of a monetary cost. It would not take the duplicative third question or the case-specific expert-discovery questions. Conditional on a grant, a disposition in the certiorari order is possible if four Justices view the lower courts as having plainly misapplied *Groff*, but plenary review to clarify the governing rule is modestly more likely; the conditional probability of a summary-disposition route is 0.40.

The expected denial will carry no noted dissent and no statement respecting denial.
