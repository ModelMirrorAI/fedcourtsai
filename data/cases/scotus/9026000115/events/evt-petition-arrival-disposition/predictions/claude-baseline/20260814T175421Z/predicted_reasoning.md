# Forecast — Dodson v. The Lutheran Village at Millers Grant (No. 26-115)

The docket shows **zero distributions** — the petition was docketed July 24,
2026 and the only entry is the petition itself, with a response due August 24,
2026. Everything below forecasts forward from that state.

**Distribution and disposition.** The petition will be distributed for
conference once the brief in opposition or a waiver arrives — most likely for
the long conference at the end of September 2026 or one of the first October
2026 conferences. The Court will **deny certiorari at the first conference the
petition is considered at, without a relist**, with the denial appearing on an
order list in October or early November 2026. The respondent, a private
retirement community, has little incentive to do more than waive or file a
short BIO, which will not slow the schedule much.

**Relists.** Beyond the first distribution, expect **no further relists**. The
petition presents no circuit conflict — it argues the Fourth Circuit and the
district court misapplied *Groff v. DeJoy* to this summary-judgment record —
and error-correction petitions from unpublished per curiam affirmances do not
draw the second look a relist reflects.

**CVSG.** No call for the views of the Solicitor General. The dispute is
between private parties under Title VII; the legal question is framed as
evidentiary sufficiency rather than a statutory-interpretation question on
which the government's views would be sought, and the petition is too weak to
prompt one.

**Which question, if granted.** In the unlikely event of a grant, the Court
would not take any QP as written — all four are "did the court below err"
formulations, and QPs 3 and 4 duplicate QPs 1 and 2 at the district-court
level. It would reformulate around QP 1: whether *Groff*'s "substantial
increased costs" standard permits an undue-hardship defense resting entirely
on non-economic (health-and-safety) grounds without evidence of cost. QP 2's
Rule 56(d) issue would be left behind as case-specific procedure.

**Summary disposition.** Unlikely. A GVR requires an intervening decision
bearing on the case and I identify no pending merits case on *Groff*'s
application whose decision would issue before this petition is resolved; a
summary reversal of an unpublished, factbound affirmance is not this Court's
practice.

**Separate writings.** Expect a **quiet denial** — no dissent from denial and
no statement. There is a modest chance (roughly one in ten) of a short
statement or dissent respecting denial from Justice Thomas, Alito, or Gorsuch,
who have written around religious-objector vaccine-mandate denials before, but
those writings have attached to constitutional free-exercise claims rather
than record-bound Title VII sufficiency disputes like this one.
