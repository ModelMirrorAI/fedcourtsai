# Forecast — Dodson v. The Lutheran Village at Millers Grant, Inc. (No. 26-115)

Forecast at the arrival moment: the docket shows the petition filed (entry
dated June 26, 2026, docketed July 24, 2026, response due August 24, 2026) and
nothing else — zero distributions, no CVSG, no response yet.

**Disposition.** The Court will deny certiorari. The petition asks for pure
error correction of an unpublished, unreasoned Fourth Circuit per curiam
("we have reviewed the record and find no reversible error") in a single
employee's Title VII religious-accommodation suit over a COVID-19 vaccine
mandate. It alleges no circuit split, and its authorities are chiefly
district-court decisions. Expect a routine denial, most likely on an order
list in October or November 2026.

**Distribution and relists.** The petition sits at zero distributions. The
respondent, a private Maryland nonprofit, will likely waive a response or file
a short brief in opposition; the petition will then be distributed once — for
the late-September 2026 long conference if a waiver comes quickly, otherwise
an early-October conference — and denied without a relist. I do not expect a
second distribution: nothing here fits the profile (a split, a federal
respondent, a record-vetting hold) that draws one.

**CVSG.** No CVSG. This is a private-party dispute applying settled Title VII
law; no federal party or program is implicated, and the Court does not invite
the Solicitor General into fact-bound accommodation disputes.

**Question presented, if taken.** If the Court granted anything, it would not
take the QPs as written — QPs 3 and 4 are addressed to the district court's
judgment, which is not the judgment under review, and QPs 1 and 2 are
"did the court err" error-correction framings. It would have to reformulate
around the application of Groff v. DeJoy's substantial-cost standard to
employer undue-hardship showings at summary judgment. I do not expect it to.

**Summary disposition.** Conditional on this petition joining the grant family
at all, the likelier route is a disposition in the cert order — a GVR in light
of an intervening religious-accommodation decision, should the Court hand one
down this Term — rather than plenary review, for which the unreasoned,
unpublished decision below is a poor vehicle. That conditional is what the
`summary-disposition-route` number (0.55) states.

**Dissent from denial.** None expected. Although religious-liberty denials
occasionally draw statements (Thomas, Alito, Gorsuch), those have come in
constitutional free-exercise cases with reasoned opinions below; a fact-bound
Title VII summary-judgment affirmance without a published opinion is not the
vehicle a Justice writes on.
