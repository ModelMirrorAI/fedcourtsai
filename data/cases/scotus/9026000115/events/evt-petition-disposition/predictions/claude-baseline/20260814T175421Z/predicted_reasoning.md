# Predicted reasoning — Dodson v. The Lutheran Village at Millers Grant, Inc. (No. 26-115)

Forecast of what the Court will do with this petition.

## Disposition and timeline

The Court will **deny certiorari**. The docket shows the petition filed June 26,
2026 and docketed July 24, 2026, with the response due August 24, 2026. Expect
the respondent — a Maryland nonprofit represented against a solo-practitioner
petition — to waive its response or file a short brief in opposition. The
petition will then be distributed for the late-September 2026 "long conference"
that opens October Term 2026, and denied in an order list in early-to-mid
October 2026. If the Court requests a response after a waiver, that pushes
disposition to November–December 2026 without changing the outcome.

## Distributions and relists

The docket currently shows **zero distributions**. I expect the petition to be
distributed exactly **once** — the long conference — and **not relisted**.
Nothing about this petition (no circuit split, no separate-writing bait, no
capital or recurring-issue posture) marks it for the relist track.

## CVSG

**No CVSG.** This is a private employment dispute between an individual and a
nonprofit employer; no federal program or federal-party interest is implicated,
and the legal standard (Groff v. DeJoy's undue-hardship test) was settled by
the Court itself three Terms ago. A CVSG would be pointless here.

## Question presented, if taken

The four QPs as written are pure error correction — QPs 1/3 and 2/4 are
near-duplicates asking whether the Fourth Circuit and the district court
"erred" in granting summary judgment and in denying a Rule 56(d) motion. If the
Court took the case (it will not), it would have to reformulate around a
generalizable question, e.g. what record an employer must make to establish
undue hardship under Groff at summary judgment. The Rule 56(d) question would
be left behind entirely.

## Summary disposition

**No GVR and no summary reversal.** A GVR requires an intervening decision to
reconsider under; the Fourth Circuit decided this case in March 2026 applying
Groff, and no later decision of this Court bears on it. The unreported,
fact-specific ruling below is not a summary-reversal candidate.

## Separate writings

Expect a clean denial with **no dissent from denial and no statement
respecting denial**. The Justices most attentive to religious-liberty claims
(Thomas, Alito, Gorsuch) have used dissents from denial to flag open legal
questions in this area; this petition presents none — it concedes the Groff
framework and disputes only its application to one summary-judgment record.
