# Forecast — Dodson v. The Lutheran Village at Millers Grant, No. 26-115

**Disposition: denied**, most likely on the order list following the
November 6, 2026 conference, with no further distribution.

- **Relists.** The docket shows one distribution (for the 11/6/2026
  conference) and no relist. I expect no further distribution. The respondent
  waived its right to respond, and a waived petition is almost always denied at
  its first conference without the Court calling for a response. The one path to
  another distribution is a call for a response, which would put the petition
  back on a later conference. I put that at roughly 4%.
- **CVSG.** None expected. This is a private employer, there is no federal
  party, and the dispute is a fact-bound application of Title VII's
  undue-hardship standard. The SG's views add nothing here.
- **Question presented, if granted.** The only question with any certworthy
  shape is QP 1: whether, after *Groff v. DeJoy*, an employer can establish undue
  hardship on non-economic health-and-safety costs at summary judgment without
  evidence of increased costs to its business. QPs 2–4 (the Rule 56(d) denial and
  repeating the same error claims against the district court) are error
  correction and would be left behind.
- **Summary route.** If the Court acted at all, a GVR or a summary disposition
  would be about as plausible as plenary review, given the unpublished per curiam
  below. Conditional on a grant, I put the cert-order route at about 30%.
- **Separate writing.** I expect no dissent from denial or statement respecting
  denial (about 1%). COVID-mandate religious-accommodation cases have
  occasionally drawn interest from some Justices, but a waived, unpublished,
  fact-bound vehicle is a poor place for that interest to surface.
