## In brief

A former nursing home employee asks the Court to revive her lawsuit claiming her employer wrongly denied a religious exemption from its COVID-19 vaccine mandate.

## What happened

Cara Dodson worked at The Lutheran Village at Millers Grant, a skilled nursing facility. The facility granted her a religious exemption from its vaccine policy in early 2021 but denied her renewed exemption request later that year, citing the risk of spreading COVID-19 to vulnerable residents and staff. She was fired after refusing the vaccine. Dodson sued under Title VII, the federal law barring religious discrimination in employment. The district court granted summary judgment to Lutheran Village, finding the accommodation would have posed an undue hardship, and denied Dodson's request to submit a late medical expert's affidavit opposing that finding. The Fourth Circuit affirmed without issuing a published opinion.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked whether the lower courts were wrong to rule, without a trial, that accommodating Dodson's religious objection would have placed too heavy a burden on the employer given health and safety concerns at a nursing facility. It is also asked whether the trial court should have let Dodson introduce a late-disclosed expert's opinion challenging the employer's reasoning before deciding the case on paper filings alone.

## Where it stands

The petition was distributed for the Court's conference on November 6, 2026. Lutheran Village has waived its right to file a response, and the docket shows no decision yet on whether the Court will hear the case.

## What each outcome would mean

Dodson, as petitioner, is asking the Court to take up her case. If the Court grants review, it would hear full arguments on whether the lower courts wrongly ruled against her, and a grant alone would not decide who wins. If the Court denies review, the Fourth Circuit's decision affirming summary judgment for Lutheran Village stands, leaving Dodson's lawsuit over.
