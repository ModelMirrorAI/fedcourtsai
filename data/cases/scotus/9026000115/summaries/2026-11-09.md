## In brief

The Supreme Court declined to hear a former nursing-facility employee's bid to revive her religious-accommodation lawsuit over a COVID-19 vaccine mandate.

## What happened

Cara Elizabeth Liberto Dodson worked at The Lutheran Village at Millers Grant, a skilled nursing facility. After it adopted a COVID-19 vaccine mandate, it denied her request for a renewed religious exemption and terminated her when she remained unvaccinated. She sued under Title VII, the federal law barring religious discrimination in employment. The district court granted the employer summary judgment, finding accommodating her would have caused undue hardship, and separately denied her request to submit a late medical expert's affidavit opposing that motion. The Fourth Circuit affirmed both rulings.

## What the Court is being asked

The case asks the Court to decide whether the lower courts were wrong to rule, without a full trial, that exempting Dodson from the vaccine mandate would have placed too heavy a burden on the employer, and whether the trial court wrongly refused to let her introduce a medical expert's opinion that she says was essential to opposing that conclusion, even though she identified the expert after the discovery deadline had passed.

## Where it stands

The petition was distributed for the Court's November 6, 2026 conference, and on November 9, 2026, the Court denied the petition.

## What each outcome would mean

Dodson, as petitioner, asked the Court to overturn the Fourth Circuit's decision, which upheld summary judgment for Lutheran Village. By denying the petition, the Court leaves the Fourth Circuit's ruling in place, meaning Lutheran Village's win stands and Dodson's lawsuit remains dismissed. The denial does not mean the Court agreed with the lower court's reasoning; it simply leaves that decision as the final word in this case.
