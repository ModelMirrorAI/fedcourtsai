## In brief

A former nursing-facility employee asks the Court to revive her religious-discrimination lawsuit over her employer's COVID-19 vaccine mandate.

## What happened

Cara Elizabeth Liberto Dodson worked as an occupational therapist at The Lutheran Village at Millers Grant, a skilled nursing facility. After initially granting her a religious exemption from its COVID-19 vaccine mandate, the facility later denied her renewed exemption request, citing risks to residents and staff, and terminated her when she remained unvaccinated. She sued under Title VII, the federal law barring religious discrimination in employment. The district court granted summary judgment to the employer, finding the accommodation would have caused undue hardship, and separately denied Dodson's request to submit a late-disclosed medical expert's affidavit opposing that finding. The Fourth Circuit affirmed both rulings.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked whether the lower courts were wrong to rule, without a full trial, that accommodating Dodson's religious objection to the vaccine mandate would have imposed an undue hardship on her employer, and whether the trial court was wrong to refuse to let her use a medical expert's affidavit—submitted after the discovery deadline—to challenge the employer's hardship claim.

## Where it stands

The petition for a writ of certiorari was filed on June 26, 2026, and a response from the employer is due August 24, 2026. The docket does not yet show any further action.

## What each outcome would mean

Dodson, as petitioner, is asking the Court to take up her case. If the Court grants the petition, it would hear the case and decide the questions about undue hardship and the expert-affidavit ruling, without yet deciding who wins. If it denies the petition, the Fourth Circuit's decision affirming judgment for Lutheran Village would stand, leaving Dodson's lawsuit dismissed and the exclusion of her expert's affidavit in place.
