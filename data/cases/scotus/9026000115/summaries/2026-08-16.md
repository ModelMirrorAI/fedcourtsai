## What happened

Cara Elizabeth Liberto Dodson worked as an occupational therapist at The Lutheran Village at Millers Grant, a nursing facility. She received a religious exemption from the facility's COVID-19 vaccine mandate in early 2021, but the facility later denied her a continued exemption, citing the risk to residents and staff, and terminated her when she remained unvaccinated. She sued under the federal civil rights law barring religious discrimination in employment, arguing the facility failed to reasonably accommodate her beliefs. The district court granted the facility summary judgment, finding accommodating her would have created undue hardship, and separately denied her request to submit a late medical expert's opinion challenging that hardship claim. The Fourth Circuit affirmed both rulings without a published opinion.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked to decide whether the lower courts were wrong to rule, without a trial, that accommodating Dodson's religious objection would have imposed too heavy a burden on her employer's operations given the health risks it cited. It is also asked whether the courts were wrong to keep out an expert's report she wanted to use to counter the employer's claims about those risks, after she disclosed the expert late in the case.
