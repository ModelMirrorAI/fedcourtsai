## In brief

A former nursing-home employee asks the Court to revive her lawsuit claiming her employer wrongly denied a religious exemption from its COVID-19 vaccine mandate.

## What happened

Cara Elizabeth Liberto Dodson worked as an occupational therapist at The Lutheran Village at Millers Grant, a skilled nursing facility. The facility granted her a religious exemption from its vaccine mandate in early 2021 but denied her renewed request later that year, citing the risk unvaccinated staff posed to residents and coworkers. She was terminated and sued under Title VII, the federal law barring religious discrimination in employment. The district court granted summary judgment to the employer, also denying Dodson's request to submit a late medical expert's affidavit opposing that motion. The Fourth Circuit affirmed, and later denied rehearing.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked whether the lower courts were wrong to rule, without a full trial, that accommodating Dodson's religious objection would have placed too heavy a burden on her employer, and whether the trial court should have let her present late-disclosed expert evidence challenging the employer's reasons before deciding the case on paper alone.

## Where it stands

The Lutheran Village waived its right to respond to the petition. The case was distributed for the Court's conference on November 6, 2026, and the docket shows no action since.

## What each outcome would mean

Dodson, as petitioner, asked the Court to take up her case. If the Court grants review, it would hear full arguments and decide whether the lower courts applied the right standards; a grant would not itself decide who wins. If the Court denies review, the Fourth Circuit's decision affirming judgment for Lutheran Village stands, and Dodson's lawsuit remains dismissed. A denial would not mean the Court agrees the lower courts were correct, only that their rulings remain in place.
