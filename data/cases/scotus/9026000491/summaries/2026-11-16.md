## In brief

The federal government asks the Court to decide whether courts may void agency rules entirely, rather than only for the parties who sued.

## What happened

Healthcare and air-ambulance providers sued federal health agencies over rules implementing the No Surprises Act, which protects patients from unexpected medical bills. The providers argued the rules improperly let insurers calculate reimbursement rates in ways that favored insurers. A district court agreed in part and struck down several provisions entirely, not just for the parties in the case. After a panel reversed in part, the full Fifth Circuit, sitting en banc, upheld that broad approach, ruling that voiding a regulation completely is the normal remedy under the relevant federal statute governing judicial review of agency action. The government now asks the Supreme Court to review that ruling.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked whether the law that lets courts throw out unlawful agency rules allows them to eliminate those rules for everyone, including people and businesses not part of the lawsuit, or only for the parties actually before the court. If broader relief is allowed, the Court is also asked when that would be appropriate.

## Where it stands

The petition was filed in October 2026. The deadline for the providers to respond was extended to December 14, 2026, and two outside groups have since filed briefs supporting the government's position. The docket shows no decision yet on whether the Court will hear the case.

## What each outcome would mean

The federal health agencies, as petitioners, are asking the Court to take up the case and reject the broad remedy the lower court allowed. If the Court grants review, it will hear full arguments and decide the legal question, without yet deciding who wins. If it denies review, the Fifth Circuit's ruling stands, leaving in place the voiding of the challenged rules for everyone, not just the providers who sued, in that circuit.
