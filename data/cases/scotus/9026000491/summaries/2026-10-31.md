## In brief

The federal government asks the Court whether courts may cancel an unlawful agency rule for everyone, not just the parties who sued.

## What happened

Healthcare and air-ambulance providers sued several federal agencies over rules implementing the No Surprises Act, a law limiting surprise medical bills. A district court agreed that parts of the rules were unlawful and canceled them nationwide, not just for the parties in the case. A panel of the Fifth Circuit partly disagreed, but the full Fifth Circuit, sitting en banc, sided mostly with the providers and upheld the nationwide cancellation, treating it as the standard remedy. The government now asks the Supreme Court to review that remedy question.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked whether, when a court finds a federal agency's rule unlawful, the law lets the court erase that rule for everyone affected, or whether the court's power is limited to the parties actually before it. If such broad relief is allowed, the Court is also asked to say when it is appropriate.

## Where it stands

The government's petition was filed and the providers' response was due November 13, 2026. The Court granted a request to extend that deadline to December 14, 2026. The Court has not yet decided whether to hear the case.

## What each outcome would mean

The federal government, as petitioner, is asking the Court to take up the case and limit remedies under this law. If the Court grants review, it will hear full arguments and decide the legal question itself, without yet deciding who wins. If the Court denies review, the Fifth Circuit's decision stands, leaving in place the nationwide cancellation of the rule provisions and a win for the providers, Texas Medical Association and others.
