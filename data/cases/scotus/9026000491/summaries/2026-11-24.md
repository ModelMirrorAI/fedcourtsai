## In brief

The federal government asks the Supreme Court to decide whether courts may wipe out an unlawful agency rule for everyone, not just the parties who sued.

## What happened

Healthcare and air-ambulance providers sued federal health agencies over rules implementing the No Surprises Act, which protects patients from unexpected medical bills. A district court agreed the rules were unlawful and vacated them entirely, applying to everyone, not just the providers who sued. The Fifth Circuit, sitting en banc, upheld that nationwide vacatur, calling it the standard remedy for unlawful agency action under the Administrative Procedure Act. The federal government now asks the Supreme Court to review that ruling, arguing the law does not permit courts to erase a rule for people who were never part of the lawsuit.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked whether a law letting courts "set aside" unlawful agency rules allows a judge to cancel the rule for the whole country, including people and businesses who were not part of the case, or whether relief should normally be limited to the parties actually before the court, and if broader relief is sometimes allowed, when.

## Where it stands

The petition was filed in October 2026. The Court granted the respondents an extension to respond, now due December 14, 2026, and amicus briefs have been filed. The docket does not yet show the petition distributed for a conference or any ruling on whether the Court will hear the case.

## What each outcome would mean

The federal government, as petitioner, is asking the Court to take up the case and reverse the nationwide vacatur. If the Court grants review and eventually sides with the government, nationwide vacatur would be curtailed and the providers, Texas Medical Association and others, would likely see relief limited to themselves rather than applied industry-wide. If the Court denies review, the Fifth Circuit's decision upholding broad vacatur stands, leaving the rules struck down for everyone. A grant without a final ruling would not yet resolve who prevails.
