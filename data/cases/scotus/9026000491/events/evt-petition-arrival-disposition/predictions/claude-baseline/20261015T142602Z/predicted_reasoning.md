# Forecast — HHS v. Texas Medical Association (No. 26-491)

**State forecast from:** docketed October 14, 2026; zero distributions, no CVSG,
no response yet (response due November 13, 2026).

- **Disposition: granted (P ≈ 0.72).** The Solicitor General asks the Court to
  decide whether APA § 706(2) authorizes universal vacatur of agency rules. That
  is the remedial question *Trump v. CASA* (2025) set aside, and several Justices
  have written on it from both sides (Gorsuch in *United States v. Texas*, and
  Kavanaugh's *Corner Post* concurrence on the other). Expect plenary review.
- **Distribution.** The petition will be distributed for conference, most likely
  after the brief in opposition is filed or the response deadline is extended in
  the usual way, which puts the first conference in December 2026 or January
  2027. A grant could come at the first conference. One relist before a grant is
  also plausible.
- **CVSG: no.** The federal government is the petitioner, so there is no
  Solicitor General's view to invite.
- **Question taken:** the QP as written, possibly limited to the first clause
  (whether § 706(2) authorizes universal vacatur at all). The "in what
  circumstances" clause would ride along as the fallback.
- **Summary disposition: very unlikely.** No intervening decision answers the
  question. A GVR in light of *CASA* is not a natural fit because *CASA*
  expressly did not reach APA vacatur.
- **If denied:** a denial would most likely mean the Court prefers another
  vehicle, for example one where the remedy changes outcomes for non-parties more
  starkly. In that case a statement respecting denial (most plausibly from
  Gorsuch, possibly joined by Thomas) is reasonably likely, but it is not the
  modal outcome.
- **If granted, likely ground (conditional):** the Court would at least reject
  universal vacatur as an automatic "default" remedy. It would then either
  confine § 706(2) "set aside" relief to the parties or require courts to apply
  traditional equitable principles before vacating a rule universally.
