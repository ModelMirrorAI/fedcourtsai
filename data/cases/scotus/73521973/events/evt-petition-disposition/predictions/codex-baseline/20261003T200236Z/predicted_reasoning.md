# Forecast

The Court will deny certiorari. The snapshot records one distribution, on September 23, for the October 9, 2026 conference; it records no relist and no invitation to the Solicitor General. The modal path is denial without another distribution, on an order list in October 2026. If the petition receives further consideration, one or two additional distributions are more likely than a prolonged hold. The probability of at least one additional distribution is 24%.

The Court will not call for the Solicitor General's views (0.5% probability of a new CVSG). This is a state-prisoner capital habeas dispute over sentencing prejudice, not an apparent occasion to obtain the federal government's institutional position.

If review is granted, the most likely focus is a narrowed version of the first question: whether the prejudice analysis may discount an on-record initial jury division when evaluating substantial omitted mitigation under Strickland and the applicable habeas standard. The second question, invoking Rompilla and Williams, is more likely to supply the asserted error-correction basis than an independently accepted broad question. This is not a forecast that the Court will reconsider the constitutionality of capital punishment or nonunanimous sentencing generally.

Conditional on any grant, the probability of disposition in the cert order itself is 60%, versus 40% for plenary review. A summary reversal or vacatur directing renewed consideration of cumulative mitigation is the likeliest intervention; the record supplies no identified intervening decision that makes a routine companion-case GVR the central forecast. If the Court instead takes the jury-hesitation question for argument, its treatment of the state-court standard of review will be integral to the question.

Conditional on denial, there is an 18% probability of a noted dissent or statement respecting denial. The modal denial has no separate writing. No individual Justice's vote or authorship is forecast. The substantive gravity and forceful lower-court dissents create a meaningful possibility of a statement without making four votes for review likely.
