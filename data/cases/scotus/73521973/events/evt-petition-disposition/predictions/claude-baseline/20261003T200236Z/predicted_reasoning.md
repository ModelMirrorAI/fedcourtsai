# Forecast: Davis v. Lovelace, No. 25-1410 (capital case)

**Bottom line.** The Court will deny certiorari. P(any grant) = 0.08.

**Distributions so far.** One. The petition was distributed on September 23, 2026 for the Conference of October 9, 2026, after the reply brief was filed. No relist yet, no CVSG, no amicus briefs.

**Relist.** Modestly more likely than not that the petition is decided off the first conference, but I expect a real chance (about one in three) of at least one further distribution. The usual driver for a relist in this posture is not a grant in the making but a Justice preparing a dissent from denial or statement respecting denial in a capital Strickland case with striking facts (a 7-5 jury note, and a state appellate court that once wrote the omitted mitigation was "powerful" and would have "compelled" relief). If it is relisted, expect one or two relists at most, then a denial.

**CVSG.** None. This is state-prisoner habeas against a state corrections commissioner; no federal interest is implicated and the Solicitor General's views would add nothing. P(CVSG after prediction time) = 0.02.

**Which question, if any.** If the Court took the case it would take a reformulated QP 1: whether, under AEDPA, a state court unreasonably applies Strickland's prejudice prong when it disregards record evidence of jury hesitation. It would not take QP 2 (the Rompilla/Williams "materially indistinguishable facts" argument), which is fact-bound error correction. I think the Court will instead find QP 1 a poor vehicle: respondent's brief in opposition shows the argument was first articulated in the panel dissent, was never raised in state court or in the en banc petition, and was met by an alternative holding in the panel's footnote 25 that the hesitation here (a one-hour deliberation ending 11-1) would carry little weight anyway.

**Summary disposition versus plenary review.** Conditional on a grant, plenary review is the likelier route (about 70/30). There is no intervening decision to GVR in light of, and the Court's summary per curiams in AEDPA Strickland cases run almost entirely in the State's favor (reversing grants of habeas relief), not the prisoner's. The 30% residual covers a summary vacatur on the Rompilla parallel in the style of Andrus v. Texas, which I regard as unlikely on an AEDPA record.

**Dissent from denial.** Conditional on denial, about a 30% chance that a Justice writes. The likeliest author is Justice Sotomayor, possibly joined by Justice Jackson, pointing to the ACCA's earlier statement that it would have been compelled to grant relief and to the jury's 7-5 note. A statement respecting denial from any other Justice is unlikely. (Author identity is context only; the existence claim is what resolves.)

**If granted (conditional only).** The likely ground would be that Strickland's "totality of the evidence" instruction already clearly establishes that on-the-record jury conduct is part of the prejudice inquiry, so a state court that ignores it applies Strickland unreasonably; the opposing view, that no holding of this Court requires weighing hesitation, is the one the Eleventh Circuit majority adopted and the one the current majority is more likely to share.
