## In brief

The Supreme Court declined to hear a death-row inmate's challenge to how courts should weigh jury hesitation when assessing whether his trial lawyers' errors harmed his case.

## What happened

Jimmy Davis, Jr. was convicted of capital murder in Alabama and sentenced to death after a jury recommended death by an 11-1 vote, following an initial split in which five jurors favored life imprisonment. Davis later argued in state and federal courts that his trial lawyers were ineffective for failing to investigate and present evidence of childhood abuse and the nonviolent nature of his only prior conviction. State courts and a federal district court rejected his claims, and a divided panel of the U.S. Court of Appeals for the Eleventh Circuit affirmed, finding the state court's decision was not unreasonable under the deferential standard that applies to such claims. The full Eleventh Circuit declined to rehear the case, over dissents.

## What the Court is being asked

Davis asked the Court to decide two things: first, whether courts evaluating whether a defendant was harmed by his lawyer's poor performance must take into account a jury's reluctance or division over imposing a death sentence; and second, whether the appeals court's ruling conflicted with two of the Supreme Court's prior decisions addressing similar mitigating evidence and ineffective-assistance claims.

## Where it stands

The petition was distributed for the Court's October 9, 2026 conference, and on October 13, 2026, the Court denied the petition.

## What each outcome would mean

Davis, as the petitioner, asked the Court to take up his case and reverse the Eleventh Circuit's ruling against him. By denying the petition, the Court leaves the Eleventh Circuit's decision in place, meaning Davis's conviction and death sentence stand and his ineffective-assistance claims do not succeed. The denial does not mean the Court agreed with the lower court's reasoning, only that it chose not to review the case further.
