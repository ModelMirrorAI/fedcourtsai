## In brief

The Supreme Court declined to hear a death-row inmate's challenge to how courts should weigh jury hesitation in assessing whether his trial lawyers' errors prejudiced his sentencing.

## What happened

Jimmy Davis, Jr. was convicted of capital murder in Alabama and sentenced to death in 1993, after a jury that initially split 7-5 toward life imprisonment ultimately recommended death 11-1. Davis argued in state and federal courts that his trial lawyers were ineffective for failing to investigate and present evidence of childhood abuse and the non-violent details of his only prior conviction. State courts and a federal district court rejected his claims, and a divided panel of the Eleventh Circuit Court of Appeals affirmed, finding Davis had not shown prejudice under the governing legal test. The full Eleventh Circuit declined to rehear the case, over dissents.

## What the Court is being asked

Davis asked the Court to decide whether judges evaluating whether a defendant was harmed by his lawyer's mistakes must take into account signs that the jury was reluctant to impose a death sentence, such as an initial vote favoring life imprisonment. He also asked the Court to decide whether the appeals court's ruling conflicted with the Court's own precedents on evaluating such harm in death penalty cases.

## Where it stands

The petition was distributed for the Court's October 9, 2026 conference, and on October 13, 2026 the Court denied the petition.

## What each outcome would mean

Davis, the inmate, asked the Court to take up his case and reverse the Eleventh Circuit's decision, which had ruled against him and in favor of Lovelace, the Alabama prison official named as respondent. By denying the petition, the Court leaves the Eleventh Circuit's decision in place, meaning Davis's conviction and death sentence stand and his claim that his trial lawyers' errors prejudiced his sentencing does not succeed.
