## In brief

Jimmy Davis, Jr., sentenced to death in Alabama, asks the Court to revive his claim that his trial lawyers' failure to present mitigating evidence prejudiced his sentencing.

## What happened

Davis was convicted of capital murder in Alabama for killing Johnny Hazel during a 1993 robbery and sentenced to death after a jury recommended death by a vote of 11 to 1. Davis later argued in state and federal courts that his trial lawyers were ineffective for failing to investigate and present evidence of childhood abuse and the non-violent nature of his only prior conviction. State courts and a federal district court rejected this claim, and a divided panel of the Eleventh Circuit Court of Appeals affirmed, with one judge dissenting. The full Eleventh Circuit declined to rehear the case, over further dissents.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked whether courts evaluating whether a defendant was harmed by his lawyer's poor performance must take into account a jury's reluctance or hesitation to impose a death sentence, something Davis says some appeals courts require and others do not. Davis separately argues the appeals court's decision cannot be squared with the Supreme Court's own past rulings on when missed mitigating evidence requires a new sentencing hearing.

## Where it stands

## What each outcome would mean

Davis, as petitioner, is asking the Court to take up his case. If the Court grants review, it would hear full arguments and decide whether the jury's hesitation must be weighed in assessing prejudice, deciding nothing in advance about who wins.
