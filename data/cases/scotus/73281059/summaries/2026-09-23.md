## What happened

Arizona lawmakers Warren Petersen and Steve Montenegro (leaders of the state Senate and House) are challenging rulings against Arizona voter-registration laws. Arizona lets people register using either a federal form or a state form, and a 2022 law required officials to reject state-form applications lacking proof of citizenship and residency, and barred voters who registered without proof of citizenship from voting by mail. Civic organizations, tribes, the Democratic National Committee, and the federal government sued. A federal district court ruled much of this unlawful under a federal voter-registration statute and an earlier consent decree, and also rejected a claim that a related law was passed with discriminatory intent. The Ninth Circuit Court of Appeals largely affirmed but sent the discriminatory-intent question back to the district court for another look. The full Ninth Circuit declined to rehear the case, over several dissents.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked whether federal voter-registration law, or an earlier court-approved settlement, stops Arizona from requiring proof of citizenship and residency on its state registration form; whether that same federal law stops Arizona from excluding these registrants from mail voting; and whether the lower court was wrong to find no proof that state lawmakers acted with discriminatory intent in passing the related law.
