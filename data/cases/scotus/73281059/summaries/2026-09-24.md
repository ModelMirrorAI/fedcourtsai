## What happened

Arizona's legislature, through H.B. 2492 and H.B. 2243, required voters using the state's own registration form to show documentary proof of citizenship and residence, and barred voters who registered without proof of citizenship from voting by mail. Groups including Mi Familia Vota, civil rights organizations, and the United States sued, arguing these rules violated the National Voter Registration Act (NVRA), a federal law meant to make registering to vote in federal elections easier, and an earlier consent decree Arizona had agreed to. A district court sided largely with the challengers after a lengthy trial, and the Ninth Circuit affirmed most of its rulings while sending the question of discriminatory intent back for further review. The Arizona Senate President and House Speaker, who intervened to defend the laws, now ask the Supreme Court to step in.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked whether federal voter-registration law, or the earlier consent decree, stops Arizona from requiring proof of citizenship and residence on its own state registration form for people registering to vote in federal elections. It is also asked whether that federal law prevents Arizona from excluding voters who lack such proof from voting by mail. Finally, the Court is asked whether the lower court was wrong to send the question of discriminatory intent back to the district court for another look under the correct legal standard.
