## In brief

Arizona legislative leaders ask the Court to review Ninth Circuit rulings that struck down state voter-registration and mail-voting restrictions and revived a discrimination claim against the state.

## What happened

Arizona passed laws requiring voters using the state's own registration form to provide proof of citizenship and residence, and barring registrants who lack proof of citizenship from voting by mail. Civic organizations, tribes, the Democratic National Committee, and the United States sued, arguing these rules violate the National Voter Registration Act and a 2018 consent decree Arizona had entered in earlier litigation. A federal district court agreed in part, and after a trial, issued an injunction. The Ninth Circuit largely affirmed, and also sent a related discrimination claim back to the district court for reconsideration under a different legal standard. The full Ninth Circuit declined to rehear the case, over several dissents. Arizona Senate President Warren Petersen and House Speaker Steve Montenegro, who intervened in the case below, then asked the Supreme Court to step in.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked whether the federal registration law or the earlier consent decree bars Arizona from requiring documentary proof of citizenship and residence on its state registration form, whether that same federal law prevents Arizona from excluding such registrants from mail voting, and whether the lower appeals court wrongly second-guessed the trial court's finding that a related law was not passed with discriminatory intent.

## Where it stands

The petition has been fully briefed, including amicus briefs and a reply, and was distributed for the Court's conferences in June 2026. The docket shows no order granting or denying the petition since then; a brief from the petitioners "in support" was later submitted in August 2026.

## What each outcome would mean

Petersen and Montenegro, the Arizona legislative leaders, are asking the Court to take up the case. If the Court grants review, it would hear the case fully and decide the disputed questions, with no outcome assumed in advance. If it denies review, the Ninth Circuit's rulings stand: the injunctions against Arizona's citizenship and residency documentation rules and its mail-voting restriction remain in effect, and the discrimination claim continues on remand in the district court. A denial would not mean the Court agrees with the lower court, only that its decision remains undisturbed.
