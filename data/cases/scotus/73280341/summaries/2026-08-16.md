## What happened

International Partners for Ethical Care and several parents sued Washington state officials over three state laws affecting runaway minors seeking certain health services, including gender-affirming care. The laws direct licensed shelters to notify the state's child welfare agency, rather than parents directly, when such a minor arrives, and require the agency to attempt to notify parents and offer voluntary family reconciliation and behavioral health referral services. The parents said fear that their children might someday be affected had already changed how they parent, and that future harm was likely. A federal district court dismissed the suit, and the Ninth Circuit unanimously affirmed, holding that the parents had not shown a current injury caused by the laws or a future injury that was imminent rather than speculative.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked to decide whether parents can sue over a law that does not directly regulate them but that they say interferes with their role in decisions about their children, based on the possibility that their child might someday be affected by the law's terms. More specifically, the question is what a parent must show to demonstrate a sufficiently real or imminent injury from such a law to get into federal court at all, as opposed to having to wait until the chain of events they fear actually happens.
