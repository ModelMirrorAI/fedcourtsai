## In brief

Parents and organizations challenging Washington laws on runaway youth seeking gender-related care ask the Court to revive their lawsuit after it was dismissed for lack of standing.

## What happened

Parents of children who have shown signs of gender dysphoria, along with two advocacy organizations, sued Washington officials over three state laws affecting runaway youth: one letting adolescents consent to outpatient mental health treatment, and two directing shelters to route notifications about runaway youth seeking certain protected health services through the state's child welfare agency rather than contacting parents directly, with the agency offering voluntary family reconciliation and referral services. The parents said fear that their own children might someday run away and be affected by these laws has already changed how they parent, and that future harm is likely. The district court dismissed the suit, and the Ninth Circuit unanimously affirmed, holding the parents had not shown a current injury or an imminent future one.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked whether parents can sue over a law that changes who makes decisions during a possible future chain of events involving their children's gender identity, even though the law does not yet, and may never, apply to their own family.

## Where it stands

The petition has been fully briefed, including many amicus briefs, and the Court granted certiorari on June 29, 2026. The case is now awaiting merits briefing, with deadlines extended into September, October, and November 2026.

## What each outcome would mean

International Partners for Ethical Care and the parent petitioners asked the Court to take the case after losing in the lower courts. Because the Court granted review, it will now decide the standing question itself rather than leave the Ninth Circuit's ruling in place. If the Court ultimately affirms, the case stays dismissed and the parents' lawsuit ends. If it reverses, the parents' suit would go forward toward addressing the laws' merits. If it vacates, the Ninth Circuit's decision would be set aside and the case sent back without resolving the standing question for either side.
