## In brief

Parents and advocacy groups who oppose Washington laws on runaway transgender youth ask the Court to revive their lawsuit after it was dismissed for lack of standing.

## What happened

Washington passed laws changing how the state handles runaway minors who seek shelter and certain health services, including gender-affirming care. Rather than shelters notifying parents directly, the state's child welfare agency now receives reports, attempts to notify parents, and offers voluntary family reconciliation and behavioral health referrals. Several parents, worried their children might someday run away and receive this care without their consent, sued along with two advocacy organizations, arguing the laws violate their parental rights. The district court dismissed the suit, finding the parents had not shown they were actually or imminently harmed. The Ninth Circuit unanimously affirmed, holding the parents' fears rested on a speculative chain of future events rather than any injury the law itself imposed on them.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked whether parents can sue over a law that does not directly regulate them, based on worry that the law might someday affect their own children if a series of uncertain events occurred — their child identifying as transgender, running away, seeking shelter, and receiving referred services without parental consent.

## Where it stands

The Court granted certiorari, and the case has been fully briefed and argued; it was argued on December 7, 2026, and now awaits decision.

## What each outcome would mean

International Partners for Ethical Care and the parent petitioners asked the Court to take up the case after losing in the lower courts. If the Court affirms, the Ninth Circuit's dismissal stands, and the parents' lawsuit remains thrown out for lack of standing. If the Court reverses, the parents would be found to have standing, and their case would return to the lower courts to proceed on the merits of their constitutional claims against Washington's laws. If the Court vacates, the Ninth Circuit's decision would be set aside and the case sent back for further proceedings without a final ruling on standing either way.
