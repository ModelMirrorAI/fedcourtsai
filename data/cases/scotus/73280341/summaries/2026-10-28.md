## In brief

Parents and organizations ask the Supreme Court to decide whether they may sue to challenge Washington laws governing how shelters and the state handle runaway children seeking gender-related health services.

## What happened

Washington amended its laws in 2023 so that when a runaway minor seeking certain health services, including gender-affirming care, arrives at a licensed shelter, the shelter notifies the state child welfare agency rather than the parents directly. The agency must then try to notify parents, offer family reconciliation services, and offer referrals for behavioral health services. Several parents and two organizations sued, arguing the scheme violates their constitutional parenting rights, even though none alleged their own children had actually run away, sought shelter, or received such care. The district court dismissed the case for lack of standing, meaning the parents had not shown a sufficiently concrete or imminent injury to bring the suit. The Ninth Circuit affirmed, and the full appeals court declined to rehear the case over several judges' dissents.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked whether parents who object to a state law restricting their control over their children's medical care have suffered the kind of injury needed to bring a lawsuit, even without showing that their own child is close to being affected by the law, because they are generally the people the law targets or because the law has already changed how they parent.

## Where it stands

The Court granted review of the petition, and the case was argued; briefing on the merits is complete and the case was scheduled for argument on December 7, 2026. It now awaits decision.

## What each outcome would mean

International Partners for Ethical Care and the parent petitioners, who lost in the lower courts, are asking the Court to reverse and rule that they have standing to sue; Washington officials, who won below, defend the Ninth Circuit's decision that the parents lack standing. The federal government has also filed a brief supporting the parents' position. If the Court affirms, the case stays dismissed and these parents cannot pursue their challenge to the laws. If the Court reverses, the parents would be allowed to proceed with their lawsuit, though the Court would not yet decide whether the laws themselves are constitutional. If the Court vacates, the Ninth Circuit's decision would be set aside and the case sent back for further proceedings without a final ruling for either side.
