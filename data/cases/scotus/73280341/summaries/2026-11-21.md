## In brief

The Supreme Court heard a case on whether parents have standing to challenge Washington laws governing how shelters notify parents when runaway children seek certain health services.

## What happened

International Partners for Ethical Care and several parents sued Washington officials, arguing that 2023 state laws violate their parental rights by changing how shelters and the state's child welfare agency handle runaway youth seeking "gender-affirming" or reproductive health care. Under the revised laws, shelters notify the state agency instead of contacting parents directly, and the agency must attempt to notify parents while also offering family reconciliation services and behavioral health referrals. The parents said this discourages them from disciplining or speaking openly with their children for fear of prompting a runaway event. The district court dismissed the suit for lack of standing, finding the alleged harms too speculative, and the Ninth Circuit affirmed, with two judges dissenting from denial of rehearing. The parents and organizations then asked the Supreme Court to review the standing question.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked to decide whether parents can sue over laws that change how the state handles their runaway children's access to certain health services, even though none of their children have actually run away to a shelter or received such services. The dispute centers on what kind of harm is needed to get into federal court: whether parents must wait until the law actually affects their family, or whether changing their own behavior out of fear of that possibility, or simply being within the group the law targets, is enough.

## Where it stands

The Court granted certiorari, and the case was argued on the merits. Briefing is complete, including merits briefs from both sides and an amicus brief from the United States supporting the parents, and the case was argued on December 7, 2026; the Court's decision is still pending as of this summary.

## What each outcome would mean

International Partners for Ethical Care and the parent petitioners are asking the Court to reverse the Ninth Circuit's ruling that they lack standing. If the Court affirms, the parents' case stays dismissed, and the Washington laws remain in effect without the parents' challenge being heard on the merits. If the Court reverses, the parents' lawsuit would be revived, sending the case back to the lower courts to consider their constitutional claims about parental rights, free speech, and free exercise. A decision to vacate would set aside the Ninth Circuit's ruling and send the case back without deciding the standing question for either side.
