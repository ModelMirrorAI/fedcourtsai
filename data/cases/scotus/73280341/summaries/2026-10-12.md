## In brief

Parents and organizations who sued Washington state over laws affecting transgender runaway youth are asking the Supreme Court to decide whether they have the right to bring that lawsuit.

## What happened

Parents of children who struggle with gender dysphoria, along with two advocacy organizations, sued Washington officials over three state laws. Two of the laws change how shelters and the state's child welfare agency handle runaway youth seeking "gender-affirming" or other protected health care, including when and how parents are notified. A third lets adolescents consent to outpatient mental health treatment without parental involvement. The parents argued the laws interfere with their right to raise their children and said fear of these laws had already changed how they parent. A federal district court dismissed the suit, and the Ninth Circuit Court of Appeals agreed, ruling the parents had not shown they were actually or soon-to-be harmed by the laws, since harm depended on a chain of uncertain future events.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked whether these parents may bring a lawsuit at all, given that the laws do not directly regulate them and any harm to them depends on their own children's choices and future circumstances, or whether that connection is too speculative to let the parents sue in federal court.

## Where it stands

The Court granted review of the case, and it was argued on December 7, 2026. The case now awaits decision.

## What each outcome would mean

International Partners for Ethical Care and the parent petitioners are asking the Court to reverse the Ninth Circuit's decision. If the Court affirms, the Ninth Circuit's ruling stands, and the lawsuit remains dismissed, with Washington's laws remaining in effect and unchallenged in this case. If the Court reverses, the parents would be found to have the right to sue, and the case would return to the lower courts to address the merits of their constitutional claims. If the Court vacates, the Ninth Circuit's decision would be set aside and the case sent back for further proceedings without the Supreme Court deciding who is right.
