# Predicted outcome — International Partners for Ethical Care v. Ferguson (No. 25-840), briefed moment

**Judgment class: reversed** (judgment below disturbed, P = 0.70). The Court
holds that at least one set of parent petitioners has Article III standing
and sends the case back to the Ninth Circuit. "Reversed and remanded" is the
likeliest label, because the Ninth Circuit affirmed a dismissal for lack of
standing and a holding that standing exists answers that question outright.
"Vacated and remanded", which is the United States' requested form, is the
main alternative label. Both count as disturbing the judgment.

**Vote lineup: 6–3.** Roberts, Thomas, Alito, Gorsuch, Kavanaugh and Barrett
form the majority. Sotomayor, Kagan and Jackson dissent and would affirm,
leaning on Clapper, Murthy v. Missouri and FDA v. Alliance for Hippocratic
Medicine: the harm depends on a long chain of contingent steps (the child runs
away, reaches a licensed shelter, turns down reconciliation, accepts a
referral). A 7–2 with Kagan joining a narrow holding is possible. A Barrett-
or Roberts-led affirmance is the main way the judgment survives.

**Which question the Court reaches, and on what ground.** The Court decides
the one question presented, standing, and does not rule on the Washington laws
themselves. It does not adopt petitioners' lead theory, that being the
"object" of a law excuses a plaintiff from showing injury. Neither the United
States nor petitioners' own former counsel backs that theory, and it sits
badly with Lujan's text. The majority instead finds a present, concrete injury
under the usual test, either as the United States frames it (the State has
changed the rule that parents decide their child's medical care, and has
removed their earlier right to direct notice) or through petitioners'
fallback theory of present harm to the family relationship. It distinguishes
Clapper and Murthy because the injury here is a present change in parental
authority, not a feared future event.

**Breadth.** The ground is narrow. The holding is tied to these parents
(chiefly 2A/2B, whose child is still a minor) and to these pleadings. It does
not adopt a categorical rule giving any parent standing against any policy
that alters parental authority. The organizational petitioner's standing and
the merits are left for remand.

**Procedural exits.** A DIG (dismissal as improvidently granted) is a real
possibility but not the likely outcome, at about 7%. Washington's merits brief
asks for one. Its grounds: most parent claims are now moot because the
children have turned or will soon turn 18, the lead theories were never raised
below, and petitioners misdescribe state law. Petitioners concede several
children have aged out, but 2A/2B's child is 16 and parents 4A/4B have
younger children. The Court took this case after three conferences, following
Justice Alito's statement (joined by two others) in Lee v. Poudre and its own
per curiam in Mirabelli v. Bonta (2026). That history makes walking away
unlikely. No recusal is expected, so an equally divided Court is not a live
outcome.

**Authorship.** This is a December 2026 sitting case. Alito is the single most
likely author, given his Lee v. Poudre statement and parental-rights writing.
Barrett (author of Murthy, and a natural writer of a narrow, standing-doctrine
majority), Gorsuch and Kavanaugh are the main alternatives. Separate writings
are likely: Jackson and/or Sotomayor in dissent, and Thomas concurring on
Article III's private-rights roots or on substantive due process. Alito or
Gorsuch could also concur to press a broader parental-rights or object-of-law
theory.
