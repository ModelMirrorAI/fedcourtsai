# Forecast of the Court's decision

## Judgment and votes

The Court will **reverse** the Ninth Circuit's standing judgment and remand for further proceedings, **6–3**. Roberts, Thomas, Alito, Gorsuch, Kavanaugh, and Barrett will be on the prevailing side; Sotomayor, Kagan, and Jackson will dissent. This is not a forecast that the Court will enjoin Washington's laws or decide their substantive constitutionality.

The probability of any disturbance is **73%**. Reversal is the largest individual judgment class, but vacatur is an important alternative, particularly if the Court asks the Ninth Circuit to reconsider the allegations under a clarified injury analysis rather than itself resolving every jurisdictional issue. Affirmance and a dismissal as improvidently granted remain meaningful alternatives; the latter would leave the judgment below undisturbed. No equally divided disposition or nonparticipation is expected.

## Ground

The majority will distinguish a parent's alleged **present loss of participation and authority** from voluntary precautions taken solely to avoid hypothetical future harm. At the pleading stage, the remaining parents' allegations concerning their minor child and the challenged parental-exclusion scheme will be sufficient to establish a concrete injury without a showing that a shelter visit is about to occur. The Court will use the parental-participation analysis illustrated by *Mirabelli* and the distinction between objects and mere bystanders to explain why the Ninth Circuit's future-runaway framing was incomplete.

The Court will not abolish injury, causation, or redressability requirements. It will distinguish the precautionary-cost rule invoked under *Clapper*, rather than overrule that decision, and it will not hold that alleged statutory targeting automatically establishes standing regardless of actual effects. Nor will it accept as an established fact that the challenged shelter provisions themselves authorize unconsented surgery or hormone treatment.

## Breadth and issues left open

The standing ruling will be limited to parents with a gender-dysphoric minor child and particularized allegations of impaired participation. It will **not adopt the United States' categorical proposition that every objecting parent may sue whenever a law alters parental control over medical care, regardless of the family's circumstances**. This limitation is distinct from the prediction about present injury: the Court could accept that ground and nevertheless issue a broader rule, in which event the breadth forecast would be wrong.

The opinion will concentrate on the remaining live parental claim rather than revive abandoned or aged-out claims. The underlying due-process, speech, and free-exercise merits will not be decided simply because standing is recognized. Originalist arguments about private-rights standing will be more likely to appear in a concurrence than supply the majority's necessary ground.

## Writings and timing

Kavanaugh is the leading author forecast, at **25%**, followed by Barrett at **18%**; authorship is much less certain than the modal lineup. Thomas is the leading potential concurrence author, with **40%** probability of any separate writing. Kagan is the leading dissent-author forecast, with **41%** probability of a separate writing. A dissent will emphasize that the law still requires agency efforts to contact parents, that the remaining family's alleged future injury depends on several independent choices, and that constitutional objections do not establish standing by themselves. Separate-writing probabilities are independent and allow multiple writings.

Given the December 7, 2026 argument date already in the snapshot, I expect a decision in spring or early summer 2027, most likely by the end of June. Timing is context, not a separately declared scored claim.
