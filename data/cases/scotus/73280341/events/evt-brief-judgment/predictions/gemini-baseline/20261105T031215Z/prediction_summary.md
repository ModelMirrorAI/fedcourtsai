The Supreme Court is very likely (88% chance) to disturb the Ninth Circuit's decision and reverse the dismissal of this lawsuit, most likely by a 6–3 vote. The case asks whether Washington parents of children experiencing gender dysphoria have "standing"—the legal right to sue in federal court—to challenge a state law that withholds the usual 72-hour parental notice when a runaway child enters a youth shelter seeking gender-transition care or counseling. Because at least one family's child has already run away over gender-identity conflicts and other families have altered their parenting to prevent their children from leaving home, a majority will hold that the parents face a realistic risk of harm and may pursue their constitutional claims in court.
