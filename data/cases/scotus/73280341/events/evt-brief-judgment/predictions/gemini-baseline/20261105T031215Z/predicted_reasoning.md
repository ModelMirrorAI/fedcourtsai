# Forecast of the Court's Judgment and Reasoning

## Judgment and Vote Lineup

The Court will **reverse** the judgment of the United States Court of Appeals for the Ninth Circuit and remand for further proceedings (`judgment: reversed`, `probability: 0.88` that the judgment below is disturbed). The modal vote lineup is **6–3**, with Chief Justice Roberts and Justices Thomas, Alito, Gorsuch, Kavanaugh, and Barrett in the majority, and Justices Sotomayor, Kagan, and Jackson in dissent (with a meaningful possibility that Justice Kagan joins the judgment or concurs separately on narrow pleading-stage grounds, producing a 7–2 vote).

A dismissal of the writ as improvidently granted (`dismissed-as-improvidently-granted`) is unlikely despite Respondent's request. Although Petitioners reordered their arguments between the petition stage and the merits stage to lead with a direct-abrogation theory of parental rights, the Question Presented in the petition squarely encompasses both the removal of the statutory right to notice and the substantial risk and chilling effects created by the statutory scheme, and the United States as amicus curiae thoroughly briefs the traditional substantial-risk and mitigation-injury theories addressed by the Ninth Circuit panel and the dissents from the denial of rehearing en banc.

## Question Presented Reached and Doctrinal Ground (`majority-ground`)

The Court will address the single Question Presented—whether the plaintiff parents have Article III standing to challenge Washington's statutory scheme (centered on the 2023 amendments in ESSB 5599 and SHB 1406 to Wash. Rev. Code § 13.32A.082, in conjunction with §§ 71.34.530 and 74.09.675)—and will decline to reach the underlying First and Fourteenth Amendment merits in the first instance.

On the doctrinal ground for standing, the majority opinion will follow the framework advanced by the United States as amicus curiae and the en banc dissents of Judges VanDyke and Tung:

1. **Substantial Risk of Future Injury at the Pleading Stage:** Accepting the Verified First Amended Complaint's factual allegations as true and drawing reasonable inferences in Petitioners' favor under *Lujan v. Defenders of Wildlife*, 504 U.S. 555, 561 (1992), at least some of the individual parent plaintiffs—and only one plaintiff couple is needed under *Biden v. Nebraska*, 600 U.S. 477, 489 (2023), and *Bost v. Illinois State Board of Elections*, 607 U.S. 71 (2026)—have plausibly alleged a "substantial risk" of future injury under *Susan B. Anthony List v. Driehaus*, 573 U.S. 149, 158 (2014), and *Clapper v. Amnesty International USA*, 568 U.S. 398, 414 n.5 (2013). In particular, Parents 5A and 5B allege that their 13-year-old child (5C) suffers from gender dysphoria, has already run away from home over gender-identity conflicts, and continues to meet with a school counselor who encourages gender transition; Parents 2A and 2B allege that their 18-year-old transgender daughter threatened to take her 13-year-old gender-dysphoric sister (2D) to a "safe place." The majority will reject the Ninth Circuit's six-step speculative-chain framing, explaining under *Department of Commerce v. New York*, 588 U.S. 752, 767–68 (2019), and *Diamond Alternative Energy, LLC v. EPA*, 606 U.S. 100, 118–19 (2025), that Article III standing may rest on the predictable choices of third parties (here, minor children and licensed shelters) responding to incentives purposefully created by the challenged law itself, and that a statute designed to conceal a child's whereabouts from parents for seeking protected health care services cannot be insulated from pre-enforcement review by requiring parents to wait until a concealed runaway event is underway.
2. **Present Mitigation and Chilling Injury:** The majority will further hold that the Ninth Circuit misapplied *Clapper* in dismissing Petitioners' present modifications to their parenting and household speech (such as refraining from using biologically accurate pronouns or ordinary discipline to avoid triggering a child's flight to a non-reporting shelter) as "self-inflicted." Relying on *Monsanto Co. v. Geertson Seed Farms*, 561 U.S. 139, 153–54 (2010), *First Choice Women's Resource Centers, Inc. v. Davenport*, 608 U.S. ___ (2026), and the standing principles discussed in *Bost*, the majority will conclude that reasonable steps and chilled constitutional exercise undertaken to mitigate a credible, state-facilitated risk of losing notice and custody of a runaway minor constitute present injury-in-fact.

## Breadth of the Holding (`ground-breadth`)

The majority will craft its holding **narrowly**, tying Article III standing to the complaint's concrete, individualized allegations that specific plaintiff parents have minor children currently experiencing gender dysphoria combined with alleged indicators of flight risk (such as prior runaway episodes or explicit threats to leave home) and resulting household behavioral changes. Because finding standing for at least one set of individual parents suffices to reverse the Rule 12(b)(1) dismissal, the majority will neither resolve organizational or associational standing for IPEC and APC nor adopt Petitioners' broadest categorical theory (drawn from *Deanda v. Becerra*, 96 F.4th 750 (5th Cir. 2024)) that the mere enactment of a statutory carveout from a parental-notice or consent requirement automatically confers Article III standing on every fit parent in the State regardless of whether their child experiences gender dysphoria or is likely to invoke the statute.

## Opinion Authorship and Separate Writings

- **Majority Author (`majority-author`):** Chief Justice Roberts (`0.190`), Justice Barrett (`0.175`), and Justice Kavanaugh (`0.160`) are the likeliest authors of the Opinion of the Court, followed closely by Justice Gorsuch (`0.145`) and Justice Alito (`0.135`). Roberts, Barrett, Kavanaugh, and Gorsuch have authored the Court's principal recent standing opinions (*Bost*, *Murthy*, *Diamond Alternative Energy*, *Alliance for Hippocratic Medicine*, and *First Choice*), and assigning the opinion to Roberts, Barrett, or Kavanaugh allows the majority to unite the six conservative Justices (and potentially attract Justice Kagan) around the Solicitor General's measured substantial-risk and mitigation rationale.
- **Separate Writings (`separate-writers`):**
  - **Concurrences:** Justices Thomas (`0.38`) and Alito (`0.35`)—and potentially Justice Gorsuch (`0.28`)—are likely to concur separately to endorse Petitioners' broader argument (echoing *Mirabelli v. Bonta*, 607 U.S. 492 (2026)) that parents are the direct objects of statutory parental-exclusion policies and suffer an immediate legal injury when state law strips them of their affirmative statutory right to notice on par with abusive parents. Conversely, if the majority leans more heavily on *Mirabelli*'s "object of the exclusion policy" language, Justice Barrett (`0.26`) is likely to concur separately to emphasize the limits of pre-enforcement standing where a statute regulates third-party shelters.
  - **Dissents:** Justice Jackson (`0.36`) or Justice Sotomayor (`0.34`) is likely to author the principal dissent (joined by the other dissenting Justices), arguing that under *Clapper* and *Murthy v. Missouri*, 603 U.S. 43 (2024), Petitioners' theory of injury rests on an unsubstantiated multi-step chain of third-party contingencies, reinforced by Washington's representation that no minor has utilized ESSB 5599's gender-affirming-care notice exception since its enactment in 2023.
