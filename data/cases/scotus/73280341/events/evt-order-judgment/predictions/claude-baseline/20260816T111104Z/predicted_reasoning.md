# Forecast: what the Court will do with the judgment below

**Judgment class: reversed.** The Court will disturb the Ninth Circuit's
judgment that petitioners lack Article III standing, most likely by an
outright reversal and remand (with a vacate-and-remand as the main
alternative disturbed route). The grant posture points hard in one
direction: the State waived its response, the Court called for one
(Feb 26, 2026), the petition was distributed three times, and certiorari
was granted on a question three Justices had already publicly flagged as
"of great and growing national importance" in statements and dissents
from denial (Lee v. Poudre Sch. Dist.; Parents Protecting Our Children v.
Eau Claire). The Court does not ordinarily assemble that record to affirm.
The majority has a ready-made roadmap in the two dissents from denial of
rehearing en banc (VanDyke joined by Bumatay on object-of-the-law and
present injury to the parental role; Tung joined by Bumatay and VanDyke on
substantial risk of future injury), and its own recent standing decision in
Diamond Alternative Energy v. EPA (2025) — raised below by Rule 28(j)
letter and, per the petition, ignored by the panel — supplies the
object-of-regulation footing.

**Vote lineup: 6–3.** Roberts, Thomas, Alito, Gorsuch, Kavanaugh, and
Barrett in the majority; Sotomayor, Kagan, and Jackson in dissent. The
closest analogue is Diamond Alternative Energy (7–2, Kavanaugh, J.), where
Kagan joined a standing reversal; she is the likeliest crossover here, so
7–2 is the nearest alternative lineup. Barrett is the likeliest defection
in the other direction — her Clapper-line rigor (Murthy v. Missouri) is
the best fit for the State's ifs-and-shoulds argument — but the grant
itself suggests the reversal-side votes were counted before taking the
case. Expect Alito to write for the Court (he has twice written on exactly
this issue at the cert stage), with a Thomas or Gorsuch concurrence
pressing the merits of the parental-rights claim, and a Sotomayor dissent
tracking the panel's Clapper analysis.

**Ground.** Injury in fact at the pleading stage: the challenged laws
deliberately operate on the parent-child relationship — displacing
parental notice and consent for "protected health services" — so the
parents are effectively objects of the scheme, and their alleged future
harms clear Susan B. Anthony List's substantial-risk standard. Clapper is
distinguished, not narrowed. The Court will not reach traceability or
redressability in any detail, and will not decide the Fourteenth Amendment
merits.

**Breadth.** Narrow. The holding will be that *these* allegations suffice,
leaving the merits open on remand and declining any categorical rule of
parental standing. The BIO's vehicle argument (a facial challenge to three
statutes on hypothetical-laden pleadings) is the strongest lever toward a
messier disposition — a partial holding or a vacatur that remands for
re-application of the correct standard — which is why vacated is my second
most likely label.

**Procedural exits are live but unlikely.** A DIG (~2%) if the pleadings
prove as hypothetical-bound at argument as the BIO paints them; an equally
divided affirmance only in the event of an unexpected recusal (~1%). No
intervening decision suggests a summary route; the Court set the case for
plenary review and has already extended merits briefing.

**Timing.** Merits briefing runs to November 18, 2026 (reply), so expect
argument in the January or February 2027 sitting and a decision near the
end of OT2026, May–June 2027. Not scored, but it disciplines the rest:
a full-briefing, late-Term decision is consistent with a signed opinion
and a divided Court, not a summary disposition.
