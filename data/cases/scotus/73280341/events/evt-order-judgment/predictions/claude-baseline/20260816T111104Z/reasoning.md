## Adjustments up from 69.7% to 0.85

1. **Grant posture.** The State waived; the Court requested a response;
   three distributions; then a plain grant. A call for a waived response
   followed by a grant is the Court reaching out for the case, not
   tolerating it.
2. **Pre-existing appetite.** Three Justices had already dissented from or
   written respecting denials on this precise question (Parents Protecting
   Our Children v. Eau Claire; Lee v. Poudre), calling standing doctrine an
   "excuse to avoid contentious constitutional questions." Having denied
   similar school-policy petitions (Littlejohn and Foote were denied in
   April 2026, per the BIO), the Court selected *this* vehicle — the one
   where the state law itself, not a school policy, displaces the parental
   role. Granting to affirm the no-standing holding would be an odd use of
   that selection.
3. **Roadmap in hand.** Two reasoned dissents from denial of rehearing en
   banc (VanDyke/Bumatay; Tung/Bumatay/VanDyke) plus Diamond Alternative
   Energy (2025), which the panel allegedly ignored, give the majority both
   an object-of-the-law and a substantial-risk path to reversal.
4. **Cheap coalition.** A pleading-stage standing reversal commits nobody
   on the merits; historically the Court's standing-friendly and
   parental-rights-friendly wings can both join (cf. Diamond's 7–2 with
   Kagan in the majority).

## Adjustments holding it below ~0.90

- **The State's Clapper argument is genuinely strong.** The alleged injury
  chain (child identifies as transgender → runs away → reaches a licensed
  shelter → declines reconciliation → accepts referral → receives care) is
  exactly the attenuated speculation Clapper and Murthy rejected, and the
  panel found the present "parenting style" harms self-imposed under
  Clapper's manufactured-injury footnote. Barrett wrote Murthy; a majority
  to affirm is constructible.
- **Vehicle risk.** A facial challenge to three statutes on
  hypothetical-laden pseudonymous pleadings gives affirmance-side Justices
  material, and makes a DIG conceivable if argument goes badly for
  petitioners.

## Label and votes

Within the 0.85 disturbed mass I split roughly reversed 0.55 / vacated
0.27 / affirmed-in-part 0.03; undisturbed is affirmed 0.12 / DIG 0.02 /
equally divided 0.01. When this Court finds standing where a circuit found
none it typically reverses outright (Susan B. Anthony List; Diamond), so
`reversed` is the modal label. The 6–3 lineup follows the Court's recent
ideological splits on gender-identity-adjacent cases (the subject valence
likely pulls Kagan out of the Diamond-style majority); 7–2 with Kagan is
the nearest alternative. Votes are banked, not scored, today.
