# Forecast

The Court will reverse the Ninth Circuit and remand, likely by a 6–3 vote. The Chief Justice and Justices Thomas, Alito, Gorsuch, Kavanaugh, and Barrett will vote to disturb the judgment; Justices Sotomayor, Kagan, and Jackson will dissent. A dismissal as improvidently granted or an equally divided affirmance is unlikely.

The majority will decide only Article III standing, not whether Washington's laws violate the First or Fourteenth Amendment. It will hold that at least one parent plaintiff—most clearly Parents 5A and 5B, whose child previously ran away, although that episode did not trigger the challenged shelter pathway—adequately alleged injury at the pleading stage. The controlling injury will be the present displacement or constraint of parental decision-making created by a scheme that allegedly allows shelters and the State to bypass nonaffirming parents, not merely the possibility that a child might someday enter a shelter.

The opinion will treat the parents as objects of the challenged legal regime under *Diamond Alternative Energy* and will extend *Mirabelli v. Bonta*'s interim conclusion that such parents were likely objects of exclusion policies and likely injured by exclusion from significant decisions about a child's mental health. It will reject the Ninth Circuit's characterization of the parents' altered behavior as wholly self-inflicted because the complaint plausibly attributes that constraint to a state-created alternative pathway for their children.

The holding will be narrower than a general relaxation of imminence. Parents of minor children plausibly exposed to a law that deliberately reallocates parental authority will not have to wait until the child enters the regulated program or receives treatment, but generalized disagreement with a law and an unparticularized chain of possible future events will remain insufficient. The Court will therefore avoid adopting the petition's broad sliding-scale theory of probabilistic injury.

A separate concurrence is likely to emphasize that the ruling applies established parental-rights precedents without deciding the underlying constitutional claims. The dissent will stress the differences from *Mirabelli*: these statutes regulate shelters and a state agency, no petitioner's child has entered that pathway, and the alleged injury depends on independent choices by children and providers.
