The main upward adjustment is the Court's March 2026 interim decision in *Mirabelli v. Bonta*, issued before certiorari was granted here. In assessing likelihood of success on an application to vacate a stay, its per curiam opinion treated the parents as likely objects of exclusion policies and treated participation in significant mental-health decisions as a likely protected parental interest. The six-Justice alignment supporting relief there maps naturally onto a reversal here. The Court then granted this petition after Washington's opposition squarely argued that *Mirabelli* was distinguishable, which makes a conventional affirmance less likely than a decision extending or clarifying the object-of-regulation principle.

The principal downward adjustment is that Washington's distinction has real force. Unlike the parents in *Mirabelli*, no petitioner alleges that a child entered a licensed shelter under the challenged provisions or that the State actually withheld information under them. The laws directly govern shelters and the Department, require a good-faith effort to contact parents, and depend on several independent choices before treatment occurs. *Clapper* and the unanimous standing analysis in *FDA v. Alliance for Hippocratic Medicine* support the Ninth Circuit's concern about attenuated chains. These facts leave a meaningful chance of affirmance or of a narrower vacatur rather than reversal.

The predicted 6–3 lineup follows the disclosed alignment in *Mirabelli*: the Chief Justice and Justices Thomas, Alito, Gorsuch, Kavanaugh, and Barrett supported relief for the parent applicants, while Justices Sotomayor, Kagan, and Jackson did not. The precise alignment is less certain than the disturbed judgment because a Justice could agree that these pleaded facts establish standing while disagreeing on parental-rights merits.
