## In brief

The Pittsburgh Post-Gazette's publisher asks the Court to overturn labor board findings that it bargained in bad faith and unlawfully imposed contract terms on its unionized newsroom.

## What happened

PG Publishing Co., which owns the Pittsburgh Post-Gazette, bargained with the Newspaper Guild of Pittsburgh for a new contract starting in 2017. After years of negotiations, the company declared an impasse in 2020 and unilaterally imposed new employment terms. The National Labor Relations Board found this violated federal labor law, ruling the company had bargained in bad faith and had declared impasse prematurely. The Board ordered the company to restore prior terms and compensate employees, including for broader financial harms caused by the changes. The United States Court of Appeals for the Third Circuit denied the company's petition for review and enforced the Board's order. The company then asked the Supreme Court to take the case.

## What the Court is being asked

The company raises three issues: whether labor law allows a bad-faith bargaining finding based only on the content of contract proposals, without other evidence of misconduct; whether the appeals court wrongly deferred to the Board's legal interpretations instead of independently judging what the law requires; and whether the Board has authority to order an employer to pay employees for broader financial losses beyond lost wages and benefits.

## Where it stands

The petition was distributed for the Court's conference on September 28, 2026, after both the federal government and the union filed briefs opposing review and the company filed a reply. The docket shows no action recorded since that distribution.

## What each outcome would mean

PG Publishing is asking the Court to take up the case. If the Court grants review, it would hear full arguments and decide the legal questions, deciding nothing yet about who wins. If it denies review, the Third Circuit's ruling enforcing the Board's order against PG Publishing stands, leaving the company bound by the Board's findings and remedies. Either way, no decision on the underlying dispute has yet been made at this stage.
