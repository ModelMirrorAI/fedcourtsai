## What happened

PG Publishing Co., which operates the Pittsburgh Post-Gazette, bargained for years with the Newspaper Guild of Pittsburgh/CWA Local 38061 over a new labor contract for newsroom employees. When talks stalled, the company declared an impasse and put some of its proposed terms into effect on its own. The National Labor Relations Board found this violated federal labor law, ruling that the company had bargained in bad faith and had wrongly declared an impasse before one truly existed. The Board ordered back pay and other compensation. The Third Circuit Court of Appeals upheld the Board's decision and denied rehearing. PG Publishing then asked the Supreme Court to review the case.

## What the Court is being asked

The company raises three issues. First, whether it is enough for regulators to conclude an employer bargained in bad faith just by looking at what the employer proposed at the table, without pointing to other misconduct. Second, whether the appeals court wrongly gave heavy deference to the Board's reading of its own authority, rather than independently judging what the labor law means. Third, whether the Board has the power to order an employer to pay employees for broader financial losses caused by a labor violation, beyond traditional back pay. The union and the Board respond that the case does not actually present these issues cleanly, since the finding of wrongdoing rested on more than just the proposals, the case turned on facts rather than legal interpretation, and the company did not properly preserve its objection to the compensation order before the Board.

## Where it stands

The petition, opposition briefs, and the company's reply have all been filed, and the case was distributed for the Court's conference on September 28, 2026, where the justices will decide whether to grant cert (agree to hear the case).
