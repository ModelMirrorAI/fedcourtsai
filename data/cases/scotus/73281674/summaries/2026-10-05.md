## In brief

The Pittsburgh Post-Gazette's publisher asked the Court to overturn rulings that it bargained in bad faith with its newsroom union; the Court denied review.

## What happened

PG Publishing, which owns the Pittsburgh Post-Gazette, spent years negotiating a labor contract with the Newspaper Guild representing its newsroom employees. After bargaining stalled, PG declared an impasse and unilaterally changed employment terms. The National Labor Relations Board found PG had bargained in bad faith and improperly declared impasse, ordering it to restore prior terms and compensate employees for resulting financial harm. The Third Circuit Court of Appeals upheld the Board's decision and denied PG's request for rehearing.

## What the Court is being asked

The case raised three questions: whether labor law allows a finding of bad-faith bargaining based only on the content of an employer's contract proposals, without separate evidence of misconduct; whether the appeals court wrongly deferred to the Board's interpretation of its own authority instead of independently judging the issue; and whether the Board has authority to order an employer to pay employees for broader financial losses caused by unfair labor practices, beyond traditional backpay.

## Where it stands

The petition was distributed for the Court's September 28, 2026 conference, and the Court denied the petition on October 5, 2026.

## What each outcome would mean

PG Publishing asked the Court to overturn the Third Circuit's decision. Because the Court denied review, the Third Circuit's ruling stands: the Board's findings that PG bargained in bad faith and improperly declared impasse remain in effect, and PG must comply with the Board's order to restore prior employment terms and compensate the Guild's members, as the National Labor Relations Board and the Newspaper Guild had urged. The denial does not mean the Court agreed with the lower court's reasoning, only that it left that decision in place.
