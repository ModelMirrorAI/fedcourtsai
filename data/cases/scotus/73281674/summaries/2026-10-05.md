## In brief

The Court declined to hear a newspaper publisher's challenge to labor board rulings that it bargained in bad faith and owes workers compensation.

## What happened

PG Publishing, which owns the Pittsburgh Post-Gazette, and its newsroom union bargained for years without reaching a new contract. The National Labor Relations Board found the company bargained in bad faith and improperly declared an impasse before unilaterally changing employees' terms of work, including pay, benefits, and subcontracting rules. The Board ordered the company to restore prior terms and compensate employees for resulting financial harm. The Third Circuit Court of Appeals upheld the Board's decision and denied rehearing. PG Publishing then asked the Supreme Court to take the case.

## What the Court is being asked

The company raised three issues: whether bad-faith bargaining can be found based only on the substance of an employer's proposals, without separate evidence of misconduct; whether the appeals court wrongly deferred to the Board's reading of its own authority instead of independently interpreting the law; and whether the Board has power to order an employer to pay employees for broader financial losses, beyond traditional backpay, caused by unfair labor practices.

## Where it stands

The petition was distributed for conference in September 2026, and the Court denied it on October 5, 2026.

## What each outcome would mean

PG Publishing, the company that lost in the lower court, asked the Court to take up the case. By denying the petition, the Court leaves the Third Circuit's ruling in place: the Board's findings of bad-faith bargaining and improper unilateral changes stand, and the company must comply with the Board's order, including compensating affected employees. The denial does not mean the Supreme Court agreed with the Third Circuit's reasoning; it simply leaves that decision as the final word in this case.
