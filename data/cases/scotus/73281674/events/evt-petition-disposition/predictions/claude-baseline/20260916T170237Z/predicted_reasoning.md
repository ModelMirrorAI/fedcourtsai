# Forecast: what the Court will do with No. 25-1192

**Disposition.** The petition will be **denied**, most likely in the order list following the September 28, 2026 long conference or, if rescheduled or relisted once, in the October 2026 lists. P(any grant) = 0.022.

**Relists.** The docket shows **one distribution** (July 1, 2026, for the conference of September 28, 2026) and no relist. I expect the petition to be disposed of without a further distribution in roughly three cases out of four. The realistic path to a second distribution is a long-conference reschedule or a single relist while a Justice considers whether to write on the Thryv remedy question; a run of several relists, the pattern that precedes a grant, is unlikely because the Solicitor General has told the Court there is no circuit conflict and the case carries two independent vehicle defects (below).

**CVSG.** None. The National Labor Relations Board is a respondent and the Solicitor General has already filed the federal brief in opposition, so there is no one whose views remain to be called for. P ≈ 0.

**Which question the Court would take, if it took one.** Not QP 2: the Loper Bright framing collapses on inspection because the panel reviewed a factual bad-faith finding for substantial evidence, which the statute commands, and did not defer to any Board reading of the Act. Not QP 3 on its own terms: the Third Circuit held the Thryv challenge forfeited under Section 10(e), the Third Circuit itself already rejected the Thryv remedy in Starbucks, and the Court denied Macy's v. NLRB (No. 25-627) on June 15, 2026 even though the government there asked for a grant-vacate-remand. If anything is taken it is **QP 1, reframed**: whether the Board may infer bad faith from the cumulative content of an employer's proposals where they would leave employees worse off than with no contract. Even there the Court would have to get past the Board's alternative finding that no valid impasse existed regardless of bad faith, which independently supports the only remedy still live after the paper's sale.

**Summary disposition versus plenary review.** Conditional on any grant, I put the summary route (a GVR) at about even odds with plenary review. The likeliest grant-shaped path is not a plenary grant on QP 1 but a hold for some other Thryv vehicle the Court later takes, followed by a GVR; the Court's refusal to GVR Macy's cuts against that path but does not eliminate it, and no intervening decision currently on the books would ground a GVR today.

**Dissent from denial.** Unlikely (about 1 in 25). The Thryv remedy and its Seventh Amendment overtones after Jarkesy are the kind of issue on which a Justice might write, but the Court let Macy's go without taking the question, and this petition's forfeiture posture makes it a poor occasion to signal interest. If any writing appears it would be a short statement respecting denial noting the Thryv question remains open for a preserved case.

**If granted (conditional only).** On QP 1 the Court would most plausibly reaffirm that the Board may not sit in judgment on substantive terms but may draw an inference of bad faith from a package of proposals whose cumulative effect strips the union of its representational role, leaving the Third Circuit's judgment intact. That is a conditional statement about a grant I do not expect.
