## Record and conditioning

This is the cert-stage petition-disposition forecast for PG Publishing Company v. NLRB, Supreme Court docket 25-1192. The event has no express stage or moment, so its petition kind and ordinary event identifier select the cert contract. I used the case-level snapshot `record/snapshots/2026-09-15.json`, `record/context.json`, `event.yaml`, the questions presented, and substantive sections of the provisioned petition and union brief in opposition. The document manifest identifies both briefs as nonempty and untruncated; extraction includes some typographical artifacts but the arguments are readable.

The frozen context is forward, Term 2025, baseline under sal-v4, with one distribution, no CVSG, and observable proceedings. The snapshot records distribution on July 1 for the September 28, 2026 conference; the July 2 reply's parenthetical distribution notation is not a second conference. The elapsed summer interval is not a relist signal. I keep the frozen band rather than upgrading the case merely because it concerns a federal agency: the petitioner is private.

I also retrieved the federal respondent's June 17, 2026 opposition directly from the exact Supreme Court PDF link already in the snapshot. That filing is not the provisioned `brief-in-opposition.txt`, which is the union's brief. The federal filing independently requests denial, rather than a hold, concession, or remand. I did not retrieve the petition's current docket, its disposition, subsequent coverage, or any evaluator output. The reply is recorded in the snapshot but its text was not read, and the appendix was not independently retrieved. References to the decision below therefore rest on the parties' descriptions and quotations, not a separately verified lower-court record.

## Anchor

The matching sal-v4 baseline risk set is the bracketed **reached** rate, not the terminal baseline rate. I pooled every displayed strictly-prior Term, 2017 through 2024, excluding 2025 and 2026. The companion `metrics/statpack.json` supplies the unrounded baseline prefix rates and weighted denominators: **593 estimated grant-family outcomes / 11,580 weighted resolved petitions = 5.1209%**. This is the private-petitioner class floor, not the whole paid segment and not the federal-petitioner rate.

The committed pack's most recent file commit is `55121cdb8`, dated September 14, 2026 at 11:02 UTC. This identifies artifact vintage, not the freshness of the remote corpus: no live corpus query or corpus-wide newest-pull/newest-snapshot measurement was obtained. The case baseline itself is the provisioned September 15 snapshot; no independent last-pulled measurement was available. The calculation uses historical counts as published, not a claim that all underlying cases were refreshed on the commit date.

I also read the paid-segment relist and CVSG cuts. Their terminal buckets show substantially more grants among repeatedly distributed petitions and among CVSG cases than among petitions without those signals. They are not forward transition probabilities. I therefore did not use the terminal relist-zero rate as an arrival or first-conference anchor, or convert the CVSG grant rate into the chance of a future CVSG. The increments below are judgments, not estimated hazards from a published conditioned table.

## Why 3.5%, below the 5.12% anchor

**Reasons to take the petition seriously.** Questions one and two connect a recurring labor-law issue with the boundary between factual review and statutory interpretation. The petition, pp. 7-17, presents concrete D.C. and Ninth Circuit language requiring evidence beyond bargaining positions and argues that the Third Circuit instead permitted substantive policing of negotiations. It also acknowledges the panel's statement of plenary legal review but argues that the cited precedent imported an older deferential approach (pp. 18-21, especially n.5). Question three concerns potentially significant monetary liability and identifies conflicting treatment of the Thryv remedy (pp. 22-26). These arguments justify a genuine, nonzero possibility of review.

**Vehicle defects outweigh those attractions.** The union opposition, pp. 5-12, disputes the factual premise of question one and identifies a separate premature-impasse finding supporting the remedial order even without bad-faith bargaining. The petition's rejoinder is that the bad-faith analysis infected the impasse finding (pp. 9-10), so the independent ground is contested, not an adjudicated conclusion of my own. Nevertheless, the union's specific account of uncompleted discussions and remaining movement makes the vehicle objection substantial. The federal opposition, pp. 11-19, describes the opinion below as unpublished and nonprecedential, disputes a square conflict, and separately argues that additional conduct supported the finding. This is less attractive than a clean precedential disagreement over the proposed legal rule.

On question two, both oppositions distinguish deference to factual findings from deference on statutory meaning (union pp. 19-22; federal pp. 19-22). The federal brief points to the panel's express plenary legal review and says petitioner identifies no actual instance of statutory deference. I find that a substantial answer to the petition's reliance on the phrase highly deferential and on language from other decisions. This assessment does not assume that Loper Bright settles the underlying labor-law question.

On question three, even the petition acknowledges that the panel did not reach the substantive remedies question because of section 10(e) preservation (pp. 26-29). The competing briefs dispute whether exception 293 adequately identified a legal objection rather than merely disputing liability. The federal opposition, pp. 23-24 and n.2, adds that reclassifying the exhaustion rule as claim-processing would not cure this case because the Board invoked it. A fact-specific preservation dispute, and potentially an additional threshold legal question not stated in the QPs, makes this a weak vehicle for the broader remedies issue.

Both oppositions report that the Court denied the separate Macy's petition, No. 25-627, on June 15, 2026 (union p. 24 n.3; federal p. 25). That is pre-snapshot information about a different petition, not this forecast's outcome. I use it modestly to reduce the chance of a hold or remand tied to that particular vehicle, not as a merits ruling endorsing Thryv. The federal brief's p. 25 n.3 expressly distinguishes its remand request there from its refusal to request remand here. This makes the petition's April suggestion of holding for Macy's less persuasive on the information actually available to this cell.

The resulting 3.5% is a judgmental adjustment, not a fitted regression or the product of independent likelihood ratios. Vehicle concerns overlap and are not counted as separate statistical observations. The union's account of the newspaper's sale is not a basis for predicting mootness: the same filing describes surviving monetary liability, and I did not verify the transaction.

## Other numbers and limitations

The 12% further-distribution probability allows closer review of the alleged conflicts but puts most weight on a routine first-conference denial. The 0.2% CVSG probability reflects that the federal respondent has already filed through the Solicitor General. The 25% summary-route probability is conditional on a grant, not 25% unconditionally; its lower weight reflects the lack of a demonstrated intervening merits ruling and the preservation obstacle. The 2% separate-writing probability is conditional on denial, and forecasts existence only. These ancillary numbers have wider judgmental uncertainty than the headline denial call.

The stakes score is **0.55**, independently of the low review probability: nationally applicable bargaining standards, agency review, and remedial authority make a decision potentially important beyond this employer, but the supplied vehicle is not a clean vehicle for a sweeping resolution.

No outcome of this petition was known or surfaced. The retrieved federal opposition also mentions the earlier, separate emergency stay application No. 25A725 being denied (p. 11). That is pre-petition procedural history, not disposition of the event being forecast; I did not use it as evidence that certiorari has been or will be denied. Web-tool calls returned no usable content; direct in-memory PDF retrieval supplied the federal brief. The absence of the reply text and of an independently read appendix is the principal remaining evidentiary limitation.
