   - However, *Barbosa da Cunha* presents unusually strong textualist and structural headwinds against the government that pull P(disturbance) substantially below the generic ~65%–71% SG merits prior:
     1. **Lopsided circuit split (7-to-2 by August 2026)**: Seven circuits (1st, 2d, 6th, 7th, 9th, 10th, 11th) rejected the government's reading of § 1225(b)(2)(A), against two (5th in *Buenrostro-Mendez* and 8th in *Avila*).
     2. **Surplusage with § 1226(c)(1)(A), (D), and the January 2025 Laken Riley Act (§ 1226(c)(1)(E))**: Congress enacted § 1226(c)(1)(E) in 2025 specifically to mandate detention of noncitizens inadmissible under § 1182(a)(6)(A) (present without admission) who are arrested for theft-related offenses — a provision largely superfluous if § 1225(b)(2)(A) already mandated no-bond detention for *all* noncitizens present without admission, and in tension with *Nielsen v. Preap*, 586 U.S. 392, 409 (2019).
     3. **Statutory text ("seeking admission") and 29 years of unbroken executive practice under *Loper Bright***: Section 1225(a)(1) deems uninspected interior noncitizens "applicants for admission" as a legal term of art, while § 1225(b)(2)(A)'s conditional clause additionally requires an examining officer's determination regarding "an alien seeking admission," and IIRIRA § 308(d)(2)(D) repealed AEDPA's broader "seeking entry and admission" deeming language. Across five administrations from 1997 (8 C.F.R. § 236.1(c)(8)) until July 2025, DHS applied § 1226(a) — a settled-practice dynamic closely echoing *Biden v. Texas*, 597 U.S. 785 (2022) (Roberts, C.J., joined by Kavanaugh, J., on § 1225(b)(2)(A)), while Justice Gorsuch frequently rejects government textual overreach in INA cases (*Niz-Chavez*, *Pereira*).
