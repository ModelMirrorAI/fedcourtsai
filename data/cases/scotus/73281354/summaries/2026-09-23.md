## What happened

Four drugmakers — AstraZeneca, Eli Lilly, Novo Nordisk, and Sanofi — sell diabetes medications at discounted prices to safety-net health clinics under a federal drug-discount program. Starting in 2020, each company adopted policies limiting the ability of these clinics to have discounted drugs shipped to community "contract pharmacies." Mosaic Health and Central Virginia Health Services, two clinics, sued, claiming the companies conspired to restrict this distribution channel in violation of antitrust law. A federal district court dismissed the case, but the Second Circuit Court of Appeals reversed, allowing the lawsuit to proceed. The drugmakers then asked the Supreme Court to review that ruling.

## What the Court is being asked

The case raises two questions about when antitrust lawsuits like this one may go forward. First, whether a rule that normally blocks companies once removed from a transaction from suing over higher prices also blocks a claim framed instead as lost business opportunities, when no extra charge was ever passed down a supply chain. Second, whether allegations that competing companies used shared lobbyists and belonged to the same trade association, communicating around the time they adopted similar policies, can help support a plausible claim of an illegal agreement among them, or whether more direct evidence of collusion is required.

## Where it stands

The petition has been fully briefed, with the clinics opposing review and the drugmakers filing a reply, and outside groups filing supporting briefs. It was distributed for the Court's conference on September 28, 2026, after being rescheduled from an earlier conference; the Court has not yet acted on whether to take the case.
