## In brief

Drug manufacturers ask the Court to review a ruling that revived an antitrust lawsuit by health clinics over restrictions on discounted-drug purchasing arrangements.

## What happened

Mosaic Health and Central Virginia Health Services, safety-net clinics that serve uninsured and underinsured patients, sued AstraZeneca and three other drugmakers, claiming they conspired to cut off a long-used arrangement letting clinics buy discounted diabetes drugs for delivery to community "contract pharmacies." The clinics say the companies acted together, partly through shared lobbyists and a trade association, to end this practice starting in 2020. A district court dismissed the case, but the Second Circuit reversed, allowing it to proceed. The drugmakers asked the Second Circuit to reconsider; it declined.

## What the Court is being asked

The drugmakers raise two questions. First, whether a longstanding rule limiting antitrust lawsuits to those who bought directly from the defendant blocks a lawsuit by an indirect buyer seeking damages for profits it lost by being unable to buy at all, rather than for having paid an inflated price. Second, whether allegations that companies communicated through shared lobbyists and a trade association, without more, can help support an inference that they illegally conspired.

## Where it stands

The petition has been distributed for conference more than once, most recently for the conference of September 28, 2026, after the clinics filed their opposition brief and the drugmakers filed a reply. The docket does not yet show any ruling on whether the Court will hear the case.

## What each outcome would mean

The drugmakers are asking the Court to take up the case. If the Court grants review, it would hear full arguments and decide the two legal questions itself, without yet deciding who is right. If it denies review, the Second Circuit's ruling reviving the clinics' lawsuit stands, and the case continues in the lower courts under that ruling. A denial would not mean the Court agrees the clinics' claims are valid, only that the Second Circuit's decision remains in place.
