## In brief

Drugmakers ask the Court to shut down an antitrust lawsuit by safety-net clinics over cuts to discounted-drug sales through pharmacies.

## What happened

Mosaic Health and Central Virginia Health Services, clinics serving low-income patients, sued five drugmakers, alleging they conspired to restrict a discount drug program for safety-net providers. A district court dismissed the suit and denied leave to amend. The Second Circuit reversed, holding the clinics had plausibly alleged an illegal conspiracy and that a rule limiting lawsuits by indirect purchasers did not bar their claims because they sought lost profits rather than overcharges. The drugmakers petitioned the Supreme Court, arguing the appeals court's rulings on both issues conflict with other circuits' decisions.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked to decide two questions. First, whether a rule barring purchasers who did not buy directly from a manufacturer from suing for antitrust damages also blocks claims for profits lost when a purchase never happened, or applies only to claims about being overcharged. Second, whether companies' joint lobbying and shared membership in a trade group can count as supporting evidence that they illegally conspired, even without proof of what was said.

## Where it stands

The petition has been distributed for conference more than once, most recently in September 2026, and on October 5, 2026 the Court invited the Solicitor General to file a brief giving the federal government's views. No decision on whether to hear the case has been recorded since.

## What each outcome would mean

The drugmakers, AstraZeneca and others, are asking the Court to reverse the Second Circuit's decision, which had ruled for the clinics and let their lawsuit proceed. If the Court takes the case and eventually rules for the drugmakers, the clinics' lawsuit could be narrowed or dismissed. If it rules for the clinics, the case would continue in the lower courts under the Second Circuit's standards. If the Court denies review, that appeals court ruling stands and the suit proceeds there.
