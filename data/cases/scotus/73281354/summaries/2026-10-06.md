## In brief

Drugmakers ask the Supreme Court to review a ruling that revived an antitrust lawsuit by safety-net health clinics over discounted drug sales.

## What happened

Four drug companies that dominate diabetes medication markets changed their policies around 2020 to stop shipping discounted drugs to outside "contract pharmacies" used by safety-net clinics and hospitals. Mosaic Health and Central Virginia Health Services, two such clinics, sued, claiming the companies conspired to cut them out of this distribution channel in violation of antitrust law. A district court dismissed the case, but the Second Circuit Court of Appeals reversed, allowing the lawsuit to proceed. The drug companies then asked the Supreme Court to step in.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked to decide two things. First, whether a longstanding rule limiting antitrust lawsuits to direct buyers of a product also blocks a claim framed as lost profits from sales that never happened, rather than overcharges on sales that did happen. Second, whether allegations that companies had chances to coordinate through shared lobbyists and a trade group can help support a claim that they illegally conspired, when combined with other circumstantial evidence.

## Where it stands

The petition has been distributed for conference multiple times, and the respondents filed a brief opposing review. The Court has now invited the Solicitor General to file a brief giving the federal government's views on the case, meaning no decision on whether to hear the case will come until after that brief is submitted.

## What each outcome would mean

AstraZeneca and the other drug companies, as petitioners, are asking the Court to take up the case. If the Court grants review, it will hear full arguments and decide the legal questions itself, deciding nothing yet about who wins. If it denies review, the Second Circuit's decision reviving the clinics' lawsuit stands, and the case continues in the lower courts. The Solicitor General's brief, once filed, may influence whether the Court takes the case but does not itself decide the dispute.
