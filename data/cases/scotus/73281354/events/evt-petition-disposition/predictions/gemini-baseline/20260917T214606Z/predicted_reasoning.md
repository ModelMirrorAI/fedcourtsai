The petition is currently distributed for the September 28, 2026 conference (the "long conference"). It has not yet been considered at a conference, as its previous distribution was rescheduled. I expect the Court to relist this petition at least once more following the initial conference, given the complexity of the antitrust pleading and indirect-purchaser issues, as well as the significant amicus interest from the Chamber of Commerce and EPSA.

The Court has a reasonable likelihood of calling for the views of the Solicitor General (CVSG) before deciding on the petition. The federal government has dual interests here: the Department of Justice frequently weighs in on the contours of the Sherman Act and *Twombly* pleading standards (especially concerning trade associations and joint lobbying), and the Department of Health and Human Services oversees the 340B drug discount program, which is the underlying setting of this dispute.

If the Court grants review, it would likely take both questions presented to address both the *Illinois Brick* standing issue and the *Twombly* conspiracy-pleading standard as they apply to parallel conduct and trade association activities. A summary disposition (such as a GVR) is highly unlikely, as there is no recent intervening Supreme Court precedent that the Second Circuit failed to apply. Should the Court ultimately deny the petition—perhaps concluding that the interlocutory posture (a remand for the plaintiffs to file a second amended complaint) makes it a poor vehicle for immediate review—I do not expect any Justice to author a public dissent from the denial.
