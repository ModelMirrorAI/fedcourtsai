## In brief

The federal government asks the Court to review a ruling that blocked a land exchange with a fertilizer company on land once ceded by the Shoshone-Bannock Tribes.

## What happened

In 2020 the Bureau of Land Management, part of the Department of the Interior, approved exchanging land once ceded by the Shoshone-Bannock Tribes of the Fort Hall Reservation for other land owned by the J.R. Simplot Company, which wanted to expand a fertilizer plant. The Tribes sued, arguing that a 1900 law approving the government's original purchase of the land limited how it could later be disposed of, and that a newer general public-lands law could not be used instead. The district court agreed and blocked the exchange. A divided Ninth Circuit panel affirmed, and the full court of appeals denied rehearing over several judges' dissents.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked whether the government may rely on a modern, general public-lands law to sell or exchange land it once acquired from a tribe, when the old law approving that original purchase said the land could only be disposed of under a specific, older set of land laws.

## Where it stands

The government's petition for review was filed and initially due a response by August 20, 2026; the Tribes' deadline to respond was later extended, at their request, to September 21, 2026. The Court has not yet acted on whether to hear the case.

## What each outcome would mean

The federal government, joined by Simplot's interests, is asking the Court to take up the case. If the Court grants review, it will hear full arguments and decide the legal question itself, without yet deciding who wins. If it denies review, the Ninth Circuit's decision blocking the land exchange stands, leaving in place the Tribes' win and preventing the government from completing the transfer under the newer law as currently interpreted.
