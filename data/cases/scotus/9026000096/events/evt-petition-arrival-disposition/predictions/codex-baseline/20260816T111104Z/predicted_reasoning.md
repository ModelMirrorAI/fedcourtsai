# Forecast

The petition currently has zero distributions and no CVSG. It will almost certainly be distributed at least once after the response arrives. If the Court is moving toward review, one follow-up relist after the first conference is more likely than a long series of relists.

The Court is very unlikely to call for the Solicitor General's views because the United States is the petitioner and the Solicitor General has already stated those views in the petition.

The likeliest disposition is a plenary grant. The Court would probably take the single question substantially as written: whether FLPMA independently authorizes disposal of this former tribal land notwithstanding the 1900 Act's direction that the land be subject to disposal under specified laws "only." The divided Ninth Circuit decision, seven dissenters from denial of rehearing en banc, and the asserted effect on millions of federal acres make the issue suitable for plenary resolution despite the absence of a circuit split.

A cert-order disposition is a secondary route. A GVR in light of intervening statutory-interpretation decisions is possible, but the recurring public-land question and the competing Indian-law and implied-repeal arguments make full briefing more likely; a summary reversal is less likely still. If certiorari is denied, the Court will most likely issue no dissent from denial or statement respecting denial.

Conditional on a grant, the Court is more likely than not to reject the Ninth Circuit's implied limitation on FLPMA, while leaving any unresolved FLPMA, NEPA, and remedial questions for further proceedings.
