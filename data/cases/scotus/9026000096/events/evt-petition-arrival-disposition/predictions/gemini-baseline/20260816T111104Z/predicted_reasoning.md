The case presents a clear question of statutory interpretation pitting the general land-exchange authority of FLPMA against a specific 1900 Act governing lands ceded by the Shoshone-Bannock Tribes. Because the Ninth Circuit struck down the federal government's (BLM's) land exchange and the Solicitor General is petitioning, the Court is likely to grant certiorari to review this constraint on federal authority over public lands. No CVSG is expected because the federal government is already the petitioner. If granted, the Court will take the single question presented as written. A summary disposition (such as a GVR) is unlikely as there is no obvious intervening decision, so plenary review is expected. Should the Court deny the petition, there may be a dissent from conservative Justices who view the Ninth Circuit as having improperly limited FLPMA.
