# Predicted reasoning — Department of the Interior v. Shoshone-Bannock Tribes (No. 26-96)

Forecast at arrival: the docket shows **zero distributions** — the petition was
docketed July 21, 2026, the respondents' time to file a brief in opposition has
been extended to September 21, 2026, and no conference, relist, or CVSG exists.
Everything below is a forecast from that state.

**Disposition.** The Court grants certiorari and sets the case for briefing and
argument, most likely during October Term 2026 with argument in the winter or
spring of 2027. This is a Solicitor General petition from a divided Ninth
Circuit panel (Bumatay, J., dissenting) with two separate dissents from the
denial of rehearing en banc (Collins, J., and Tung, J.), and the government
quantifies systemic stakes: 35+ similarly worded turn-of-the-century disposal
statutes covering more than 4 million federal acres, 2.4 million of them in the
Ninth Circuit. The Court routinely grants the SG's public-lands petitions
without a square circuit conflict (Cowpasture, Babbitt, Sturgeon), and none is
claimed here.

**Relists.** After the BIO arrives September 21, expect first distribution for
an October or early-November 2026 conference, and **one to two relists** before
a grant — the ordinary screen-for-vehicle-defects pattern on a likely grant,
not a signal of trouble. A grant order by late 2026 or January 2027 is the
modal path. I do not expect the petition to die undistributed.

**CVSG.** None — the Solicitor General is the petitioner, so there is no
outside federal interest to invite. The cvsg-increment probability is
correspondingly near zero.

**Which question.** If granted, the Court takes the QP as the SG wrote it —
whether FLPMA's disposal authority reaches land the United States acquired
from a tribe under the 1900 Act's "only"-limited disposal clause. It is a
single clean statutory question; no narrowing or reformulation is needed, and
the companion petition in No. 26-109 (J.R. Simplot Company, the
defendant-intervenor below) is likely granted alongside or held and
consolidated.

**Summary disposition.** Unlikely (~3% conditional on a grant). No intervening
decision of this Court bears on the 1900 Act/FLPMA question, so there is
nothing to GVR in light of, and the government seeks plenary review to
displace circuit-wide precedent — a per curiam reversal without argument would
be an odd vehicle for that.

**If denied.** A denial would most plausibly reflect a vehicle concern
invisible in the petition (the appeal reached CA9 on certified interlocutory
appeal). Expect a **statement or dissent respecting denial** to be possible but
not likely (~15%): public-lands separation-of-authority questions attract
Gorsuch's and Kavanaugh's interest, but most SG-petition denials pass silently.

**If granted — conditional merits note.** The likely ground would be that
FLPMA's "without regard to how the United States acquired ownership" text
supplies independent disposal authority the 1900 Act does not prospectively
preempt — but that is conditional context, not a claim this cert-stage cell
resolves.
