## Why slightly above the anchor

The strongest positive is the United States' affirmative request for review of a published Ninth Circuit decision restricting its land-disposal authority. The government asserts effects on more than four million acres under 35 analogous statutes, including 2.4 million acres in the Ninth Circuit (`documents/petition.txt`, pp. 24–28). Those are the government's estimates and legal generalization, not findings that every parcel is already barred from transfer. Even a narrower rule creates a recurring federal administrative issue rather than only a disappointed private litigant's dispute.

There is meaningful lower-court disagreement. The provisioned `documents/decision-below.txt` is the matching published August 22, 2025 opinion: same parties, Nos. 23-35543/23-35544, same FLPMA exchange and same statutory holding. It identifies Judge Friedland's majority and Judge Bumatay's dissent, and expressly leaves the district court's alternative grounds unaddressed. `documents/lower-court-docket.txt` records rehearing denied April 21, 2026 and a noted dissent; the federal reply reports **seven judges dissenting from denial of rehearing en banc** (`documents/cert-reply.txt`, pp. 1–2). The latter number is taken from the filing, not inferred from the docket metadata. These signals and the five supportive amici justify a modest upward adjustment.

The related docket is an actual **Vide** link, not merely a shared-amicus similarity: `related/related.json` and `related/26-109.json` identify Simplot's companion petition from the same lower-court judgment. It too is distributed once for December 4. That supplies a coordinated vehicle for addressing the same land exchange, but no lead-case grant or holding signal; I do not assume one petition has already been selected over the other.

## Real reasons not to be more confident

The opposition presents substantial objections, not merely a plea to affirm. There is **no demonstrated square circuit split** on this 1900 Act/FLPMA question. The government's petition principally argues importance and error, while the Tribes distinguish Simplot's National Coal Association analogy because that case involved an express FLPMA exception in different statutory text (`documents/brief-in-opposition.txt`, pp. 13–16). The panel's text-based view gives force to the 1900 Act's exclusivity language, FLPMA's anti-implied-repeal provision, and specific-over-general principles; the government responds that the statutes confer complementary authority (`documents/cert-reply.txt`, pp. 3–6). A strong disagreement about statutory meaning is not by itself certworthiness.

The Tribes also meaningfully contest geographic breadth: the 1900 Act covers about 72,000 acres, and other old statutes differ in text and ownership arrangements (`documents/brief-in-opposition.txt`, pp. 17–22). I therefore discount the asserted nationwide acreage, while still crediting uncertainty for the federal land manager. The statutory framework reproduced in the provisioned filings is that 43 U.S.C. 1702(e) defines the relevant public lands, 43 U.S.C. 1716(a) authorizes public-interest exchanges, and FLPMA section 701(f) preserves existing law against repeal by implication (`documents/petition.txt`, pp. 12–13; `documents/brief-in-opposition.txt`, pp. 2, 23–28). Those premises do not themselves decide which party wins their interaction. Attempted independent web checks returned no readable tool response, so I rely on the supplied text rather than claim successful external verification; details are in `retrieval.md`.

The case is **interlocutory**, with relief still to be determined. More importantly, the district court independently found two FLPMA procedural defects and a NEPA defect that the Ninth Circuit did not reach. Correcting the 1900 Act holding would not itself validate the exchange. The Tribes' vehicle discussion is substantial (`documents/brief-in-opposition.txt`, pp. 31–33). The government fairly responds that those grounds can be reviewed or administratively cured and that the statutory bar is a pure, conclusive legal obstacle (`documents/petition.txt`, pp. 29–30; `documents/cert-reply.txt`, pp. 10–11). I regard this as a discount, not an absolute jurisdictional impediment.

Finally, both sides identify **pending legislation** that would ratify the exchange notwithstanding other law. The opposition names the Senate FY2027 NDAA provision; the reply says the House counterpart lacks it and proposes either immediate review or a short hold through the congressional session ending January 3, 2027 (`documents/brief-in-opposition.txt`, p. 33; `documents/cert-reply.txt`, pp. 11–12). I have not independently established enactment or current negotiations and do not assume the proposal has become law. This raises the chance of delay and of a later mootness-related vacatur. Because such a grant-and-vacate outcome still counts as a grant here, legislation reduces the probability of plenary argument more than it reduces the probability of any grant. Neither the supplied docket nor anything I relied upon shows the predicted disposition.

Balancing federal sponsorship, institutional importance and lower-court division against these substantial vehicle and scope problems yields **79%**, only about six percentage points above the relevant 73.1% prior. I would not assign near-certainty or substitute a merits prediction for the grant prediction.

## Other probabilities and stakes

- **Further distribution: 0.72.** I expect one or two additional distributions, reflecting grant screening or a short legislative wait. This is a judgment about the increment beyond the current one, not a quotation of terminal relist frequencies. A hold without another distribution would not satisfy the claim.
- **New CVSG: 0.002.** The Solicitor General already represents the federal petitioners and has filed a petition and reply. Inviting another expression of the government's views adds little; the small residual accommodates unusual procedural developments.
- **Summary route given a grant: 0.32.** The salient summary possibility is a Munsingwear-type vacatur if legislation removes the live controversy; summary merits reversal and a GVR based on an identified intervening decision are much weaker theories here. Plenary treatment remains the likelier route conditional on a grant. This is a conditional number, not 32% of all outcomes; its joint probability with a grant is about 25%.
- **Writing respecting denial given denial: 0.12.** The affirmative federal petition and sharply divided lower court leave some chance of a dissent or statement, but an unexplained order is more likely. The per-Justice order-writing probabilities are unconditional, include possible writings on a grant/vacatur as well as a denial, and are deliberately small; they are not cert votes or additive shares of a categorical distribution.
- **Significance: 0.64.** If decided, the question could materially affect tribal use rights, environmental conditions, land-title security and federal western-land administration. Its legal reach is disputed and its statutory setting specialized, so it is substantial without being a maximal national constitutional controversy. This score is separate from grant likelihood.
