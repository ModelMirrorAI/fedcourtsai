# Forecast of the Court's action

The Court will grant review of the federal petition. **P(any grant) = 79%.** The likely reason for taking the case is the recurring federal land-management question and the United States' claim that the Ninth Circuit's interpretation constrains its statutory exchange authority, notwithstanding the lack of a square circuit split and the case's interlocutory posture.

The docket currently contains **one distribution**, entered November 4, 2026 for the **December 4, 2026 conference**, with **no relist and no CVSG**. I forecast **one or two further distributions** before disposition, with **72% probability of at least one**. A short wait for the congressional ratification proposal is a plausible reason for delayed action. A bare hold would not necessarily produce another distribution and is not itself the increment being forecast.

The Court will **not call for the Solicitor General's views**: **P(a new CVSG) = 0.2%**. The federal government is already the petitioner and its position is before the Court in its petition and reply.

If the Court takes a question for plenary review, it will take the federal petition's statutory question substantially as presented: whether FLPMA supplies authority to dispose of land previously acquired from a tribe where the earlier acquisition statute limited disposal to enumerated categories of land laws. It will not need to enlarge the question into a free-standing constitutional challenge or grant the distinct FLPMA-appraisal and NEPA questions that the Ninth Circuit did not decide. The companion Simplot petition will be coordinated with this case rather than treated as an unrelated controversy.

**Conditional on a grant, plenary review is more likely than a summary disposition: 68% versus 32%.** If Congress first ratifies the exchange and removes the live controversy, the likely alternative is a grant, vacatur and remand addressing mootness, rather than a summary merits ruling that the government was correct. Pending legislation is not an enacted law in this forecast. Without that development, the competing statutory readings and unresolved implications favor full briefing and argument over summary reversal. No identified intervening precedent makes an ordinary precedent-based GVR the leading route.

**Conditional on denial, an unexplained denial without a separate statement is the leading prediction.** I assign **12%** to any dissent from or statement respecting denial. If such a denial writing occurs, Thomas or Alito is a plausible critic of leaving the restriction on federal authority in place; this is a low-confidence authorship scenario, not a forecast of a recorded vote. The structured order-writing block also allows small chances of writings on a legislative-mootness vacatur, including a tribal-rights concern expressed by Gorsuch. No majority opinion or merits vote is predicted unconditionally.
