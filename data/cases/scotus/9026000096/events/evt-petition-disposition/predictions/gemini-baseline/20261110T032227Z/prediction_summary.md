The Supreme Court is likely to grant review (67% probability, or about a two-in-three chance) in this dispute over whether the federal Bureau of Land Management can swap 713 acres of public land in Idaho—ceded by the Shoshone-Bannock Tribes in 1898—to a fertilizer company under a 1976 federal land-management statute, despite a 1900 law limiting how those lands could be sold. Although the case is at an intermediate stage and Congress is considering a bill that could approve the land swap directly, the U.S. Solicitor General (the federal government's top Supreme Court lawyer) asked the Court to take the case, seven Ninth Circuit appellate judges dissented below, and historical petitions brought by the federal government are granted nearly three-quarters of the time.
