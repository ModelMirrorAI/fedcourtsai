# Predicted Court Reasoning and Procedural Trajectory: *Department of the Interior, et al. v. Shoshone-Bannock Tribes of the Fort Hall Reservation, et al.*, No. 26-96

## 1. Relist and Conference Trajectory (`relist-increment`: 0.55)

The docket currently records **one distribution** (`distribution_count = 1`), achieved on November 4, 2026, for the Conference of December 4, 2026, paired (`Vide`) with the companion petition in *J.R. Simplot Co. v. Shoshone-Bannock Tribes of the Fort Hall Reservation*, No. 26-109.

I forecast a **55% probability** that the petition is distributed at least once more after this initial distribution, most likely for one to two additional conferences in December 2026 or early January 2027. There are two concrete reasons why the Court is likely to relist or briefly reschedule this petition before acting:
1. **Standard pre-grant scrutiny and companion-case coordination:** The Court routinely relists petitions once before granting plenary review to verify vehicle questions—here, the interlocutory 28 U.S.C. § 1292(b) posture and the unreviewed district court alternative holdings under FLPMA and NEPA—and to decide whether to consolidate No. 26-96 and No. 26-109 for argument or hold No. 26-109 pending No. 26-96.
2. **Monitoring year-end FY2027 NDAA legislation:** As the Solicitor General explicitly suggests in the reply brief (Cert. Reply 11), Section 1091(b) of the Senate's pending FY2027 National Defense Authorization Act (S. 4784) would ratify the Blackrock Land Exchange, and Congress typically finalizes the annual NDAA in mid-to-late December. Relisting or rescheduling the petition through the December conferences allows the Court to see whether the final enacted NDAA retains or drops Section 1091(b) while still granting in time for spring 2027 oral argument if Congress does not enact the provision.

## 2. Call for the Views of the Solicitor General (`cvsg-increment`: 0.01)

A CVSG will **not** issue (`probability = 0.01`). The Solicitor General is the counsel of record for the federal petitioners in No. 26-96 (and has appeared as Federal Respondents supporting petitioners in No. 26-109), so the United States' position is already fully before the Court.

## 3. Scope of Grant and Question Presented

If certiorari is granted (the modal outcome at `0.67`), the Court will take the single Question Presented in the government's petition as written—whether FLPMA authorizes the Bureau of Land Management to exchange the ceded Fort Hall public lands notwithstanding Section 5 of the Act of June 6, 1900—and will likely consolidate No. 26-96 with No. 26-109 (whose question presented is substantively identical).

If granted, the Court's likely merits ground is that FLPMA's comprehensive definition of "public lands" (43 U.S.C. § 1702(e), covering "any land ... without regard to how the United States acquired ownership," with only two express exceptions) and uniform exchange authority (43 U.S.C. § 1716(a)) coexist with and are not barred by the 1900 Act's reference to disposal under the "homestead, town-site, stone and timber, and mining laws of the United States only," particularly given FLPMA's express repeal and replacement of the 1926 Act that specifically governed disposal of ceded Fort Hall lands.

## 4. Summary Disposition vs. Plenary Review (`summary-disposition-route`: 0.05)

Conditional on a grant, plenary review with full briefing and oral argument is overwhelmingly likely (`0.95`), with only a **5% conditional probability** of a summary disposition in the cert order itself (`summary-disposition-route = 0.05`). No intervening Supreme Court decision warrants a substantive GVR, and summary reversal is uncommon in complex statutory cases involving federal public-lands and Indian-law canons. The small (`0.05`) conditional probability reflects the narrow scenario in which Congress enacts Section 1091(b) of the FY2027 NDAA before the Court acts on the petition, leading the Court to grant, vacate, and remand (`gvr`) under *United States v. Munsingwear, Inc.*, 340 U.S. 36 (1950), or in light of the new statute.
