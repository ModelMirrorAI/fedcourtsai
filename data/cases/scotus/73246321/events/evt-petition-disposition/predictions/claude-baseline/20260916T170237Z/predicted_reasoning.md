# Forecast: Francis v. Allstate Insurance Co., No. 25-1218

**Disposition.** The Court will deny the petition. The likeliest date is the
order list following the September 28, 2026 Long Conference (the Monday of
the Term's opening, October 5, 2026), with a plain "petition denied" line and
nothing else.

**Relists.** The docket shows exactly one distribution (June 24, 2026, for the
September 28 conference) and has never been relisted. I expect no further
distribution: the petition will be disposed of at its first conference. The
residual chance of one more entry is administrative (a reschedule off the
Long Conference or a hold), not a sign of interest.

**CVSG.** None. This is a private insurance dispute between a Georgia
individual and Allstate with no federal party, statute, or program at issue;
nothing here would prompt the Court to call for the Solicitor General's views.

**Which question, if any.** The petition presents three questions, all
variants of one theme: that a state appellate court's strict enforcement of
filing deadlines against a pro se litigant violates due process and equal
protection, and conflicts with the liberal-construction principle of *Haines
v. Kerner*. The Court would take none of them. If it were somehow to grant, it
would have to reformulate to a narrow question about whether dismissing an
untimely discretionary application denies due process, and that question is
foreclosed by the settled rule that there is no federal constitutional right
to appellate review and that a state may enforce its own procedural time
limits.

**Summary route versus plenary review.** Conditional on any grant, a summary
disposition is far likelier than plenary review, because these questions could
not sustain briefing and argument. But no intervening decision of this Court
bears on the case, so even a GVR has no hook. The realistic forecast is that
neither route is reached.

**Dissent or statement respecting denial.** None. A pro se paid petition
raising a generalized grievance about state procedural rules, with the
respondent having waived its response, will draw no separate writing from any
Justice.
