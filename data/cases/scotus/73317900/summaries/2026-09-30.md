## In brief

Burford asks the Court to review a ruling that a federal court could not decide its arbitration motions in a dispute with financialright claims GmbH.

## What happened

Burford German Funding and related companies (Burford) had an arbitration agreement with financialright claims GmbH (FRC), a German claims-aggregation entity, covering their litigation-funding partnership. After FRC sued a law firm in Germany and sought discovery from Burford in Delaware federal court, Burford asked that court to pause its case and send the dispute to arbitration. The district court said it lacked jurisdiction to decide those requests, while still ruling on FRC's discovery request. The Third Circuit, over a dissent, agreed that the arbitration motions needed their own separate jurisdictional basis. The full appeals court declined to rehear the case.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked whether a federal court that already has jurisdiction over a case also has jurisdiction to decide requests, filed in that same case, to pause the case and send the dispute to arbitration, or whether such requests need an independent jurisdictional basis of their own.

## Where it stands

The petition has been distributed for conference, and the Court asked for a response, which FRC filed; Burford then filed a reply. The docket does not yet show any Court action on the petition itself.

## What each outcome would mean

Burford asks the Court to take up the case. If the Court grants review, it would hear the case and decide the jurisdiction question itself, without yet deciding who wins. If it denies review, the Third Circuit's ruling stands, leaving Burford unable to pursue its arbitration motions in that court and letting FRC's discovery effort proceed. Burford's petition also asks the Court to instead hold the case and send it back for reconsideration in light of a related case, Jules, without deciding the merits.
