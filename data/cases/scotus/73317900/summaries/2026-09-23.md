## What happened

Burford German Funding and related entities (BGF) and financialright claims GmbH (FRC) worked together funding litigation for German cartel victims. After FRC allegedly sold itself to a BGF competitor, it sued BGF's law firm in Germany and separately asked a Delaware federal court for discovery under a statute allowing evidence-gathering for foreign proceedings. BGF asked that court to pause the case and send the discovery dispute to arbitration, citing an arbitration clause in the parties' agreement. The district court said it lacked authority to decide BGF's request, while still ruling on FRC's discovery request. The Third Circuit Court of Appeals agreed with the district court and rejected BGF's arbitration motion.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked whether a federal court that already has authority to hear a case also has authority to decide requests, made within that same case, to pause the proceedings and send the dispute to arbitration—or whether such requests need their own separate legal basis for the court to hear them at all. BGF argues the Court should instead hold the petition until it decides a similar pending case and then send this case back to the appeals court to reconsider in light of that decision.

## Where it stands

The petition and a supplemental brief have been filed, and FRC has filed a brief opposing review after the Court requested a response and granted FRC extra time to prepare it. The case has been distributed for the Court's conference scheduled for September 28, 2026, where the justices will consider whether to take up the case.
