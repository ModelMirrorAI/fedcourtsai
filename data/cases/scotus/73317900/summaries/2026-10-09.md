## In brief
Burford German Funding asks the Court to review whether a federal court already handling a case may also decide motions to pause it and compel arbitration.

## What happened
Burford German Funding (BGF) and its co-owners are litigation funders who worked with financialright claims GmbH (FRC) on German antitrust claims. After FRC sold itself to BGF competitors, FRC sued BGF's law firm in Germany and separately asked a federal court in Delaware for discovery under a law allowing evidence-gathering for foreign proceedings. BGF asked that court to pause its own discovery proceeding and compel arbitration instead, citing an arbitration clause in the parties' agreement. The district court said it lacked jurisdiction over BGF's motions, while still granting FRC's discovery request. The Third Circuit affirmed, and rehearing was denied.

## What the Court is being asked
The Court is asked whether a federal court that already has authority over a pending case also has authority to decide requests, made within that same case, to pause proceedings and compel arbitration, or whether such requests need some separate, independent basis for the court's power to hear them.

## Where it stands
The petition has been fully briefed, including supplemental briefs addressing a related pending case and a mootness question, and was distributed for the Court's conference on October 16, 2026. The docket does not yet show any action taken at that conference.

## What each outcome would mean
Burford German Funding and its co-petitioners are asking the Court to act. If the Court grants the petition, it would hear the case and decide the jurisdiction question itself, which could result in affirming the Third Circuit's ruling against BGF, reversing it, or vacating and sending the case back without deciding who is right. If the Court denies the petition, the Third Circuit's decision stands, meaning the district court's refusal to consider BGF's arbitration motions remains in effect while FRC's discovery request proceeds.
