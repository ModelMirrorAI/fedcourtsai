## In brief

Burford asks the Court to revive its bid to arbitrate a discovery dispute with financialright claims, after the lower courts found no jurisdiction to compel arbitration.

## What happened

Burford funded German lawsuits against truck manufacturers through an agreement with financialright claims (FRC) that required arbitration of related disputes. FRC later sued Burford's law firm in Germany and sought discovery from Burford in Delaware federal court under a statute allowing discovery for foreign proceedings. Burford asked the district court to send that discovery dispute to arbitration instead. The district court said it lacked jurisdiction to compel arbitration, since the discovery proceeding was not a "civil action" of the kind the arbitration law requires, and the Third Circuit affirmed, with one judge dissenting. Burford asked the full Third Circuit to rehear the case; that request was denied.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked whether a federal court that already has jurisdiction over a pending matter can also use that same jurisdiction to decide requests to pause the case or send it to arbitration, or whether it must instead find a separate, independent basis for jurisdiction over those arbitration requests. Burford also asks the Court to send the case back to the Third Circuit for another look in light of a Supreme Court decision issued after Burford's petition was filed, which Burford argues addressed the same jurisdictional question in a related context.

## Where it stands

The petition has been distributed for conference twice, most recently for October 16, 2026, after full briefing including a request for a response, a brief in opposition, a reply, and supplemental briefs from both sides addressing a recent Supreme Court decision and a possible mootness issue. The docket does not yet show any decision on the petition.

## What each outcome would mean

Burford, the funder, asked the Court to act. If the Court grants the petition and hears the case, it would decide the jurisdictional question itself, deciding nothing in advance for either side. If it denies the petition, the Third Circuit's ruling stands, leaving in place the conclusion that the district court lacked jurisdiction to compel arbitration, a result favoring FRC. Burford has also asked the Court to grant, vacate, and send the case back for reconsideration in light of its recent ruling in another case; that path would set aside the Third Circuit's decision without deciding the question for either side, leaving it to the lower court to reconsider.
