## In brief

The Court granted certiorari, set aside a lower-court ruling, and sent back a dispute over arbitrating discovery sought for German litigation, directing reconsideration in light of a newer decision.

## What happened

Burford German Funding and related entities funded financialright claims GmbH (FRC) to pursue antitrust claims in Germany. After a dispute arose, FRC sought discovery from Burford in Delaware federal court under a statute allowing discovery for use in foreign proceedings. Burford asked the court to send that dispute to arbitration under a contract clause, but the district court said it lacked jurisdiction to compel arbitration of this kind of discovery proceeding, and it granted FRC's discovery request. The Third Circuit affirmed, holding that such discovery proceedings are not the kind of ordinary lawsuits that arbitration law allows courts to send to arbitration.

## What the Court is being asked

The case asks whether a federal court that already has jurisdiction over a pending matter also automatically has jurisdiction to decide requests to pause that matter or compel arbitration of it, or whether such requests need a separate, independent basis for jurisdiction. Burford argued that a newly decided Supreme Court case addressing related jurisdictional questions should lead the lower court to revisit its ruling. FRC argued that the new case involved different statutory provisions and had no bearing on this dispute.

## Where it stands

The Supreme Court granted the petition, vacated the Third Circuit's judgment, and sent the case back for further consideration in light of its recent decision in another case, Jules v. Andre Balazs Properties.

## What each outcome would mean

Burford, the funding entities, asked the Court to take up the case after losing below. The Court's action sets aside the Third Circuit's ruling that favored FRC and sends the case back for the lower court to reconsider its reasoning using the Jules decision as guidance. This does not decide who is right: the Third Circuit could reach the same conclusion again, or it could rule for Burford on the jurisdiction question, after which further proceedings over arbitration and discovery would continue in the lower courts.
