## In brief

Burford asked the Court to send its case back for another look after a different ruling; the Court granted that request.

## What happened

Burford, a litigation funder, and financialright claims GmbH (FRC) had a funding agreement with an arbitration clause. FRC filed a discovery request in federal court under a law allowing evidence-gathering for foreign lawsuits, seeking documents from Burford for a case in Germany. Burford asked the district court to pause that discovery request and send the dispute to arbitration instead. The district court said it had no power to do that, and separately allowed FRC's discovery request to proceed. The Third Circuit Court of Appeals agreed that this kind of discovery proceeding could not be sent to arbitration. Burford then asked the Supreme Court to review that decision.

## What the Court is being asked

The question is whether a federal court that already has authority over a case automatically has the power to decide requests tied to that same case to pause it or send it to arbitration, or whether it must find some separate legal basis for that power. Burford argued that a recent Supreme Court decision in a different case answered this question in its favor and asked the Court to send the case back for reconsideration in light of that ruling. FRC argued the other decision did not apply and that the case did not deserve further review.

## Where it stands

The Supreme Court granted the petition, set aside the Third Circuit's judgment, and sent the case back to that court to reconsider in light of the Court's intervening decision in another case, Jules v. Andre Balazs Properties.

## What each outcome would mean

Burford asked the Court to act. By granting the petition and sending the case back, the Court did not decide who is right on the question of arbitration; it directed the Third Circuit to reconsider its decision applying the new Jules ruling. The Third Circuit's prior decision, which had sided with FRC by blocking arbitration and allowing FRC's discovery request to proceed, is no longer final. The Third Circuit will now reexamine the case and could reach the same result or a different one.
