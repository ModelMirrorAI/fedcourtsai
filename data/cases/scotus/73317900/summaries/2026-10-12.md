## In brief

A litigation funder asks the Court to revive its bid to force arbitration of a discovery dispute with financialright claims, a German claims aggregator.

## What happened

Burford German Funding and related entities funded financialright claims' lawsuits against truck manufacturers in Germany. After a dispute arose over alleged conflicts involving financialright's law firm, financialright sought discovery from Burford in U.S. federal court to use in a German lawsuit. Burford asked the court to send that discovery dispute to arbitration under its funding agreement. The district court said it lacked jurisdiction to compel arbitration of this kind of discovery proceeding, and also ruled for financialright on the merits. The Third Circuit affirmed, holding that this type of discovery proceeding cannot be sent to arbitration under the relevant arbitration law provision.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked whether a federal court that already has authority over a pending matter also has authority to decide requests to pause that matter and send it to arbitration, or whether it must find some separate, independent basis for jurisdiction before it can do so. Burford argues a recent Supreme Court decision in an unrelated arbitration case supports its position and asks the Court to send the case back for reconsideration in light of that ruling; financialright argues that decision does not apply here and that the case is a poor candidate for review.

## Where it stands

The petition has been distributed for conference more than once, most recently for the October 16, 2026 conference, after full briefing including a response requested by the Court, an opposition brief, a reply, and supplemental briefs from both sides. No order on the petition has yet been recorded.

## What each outcome would mean

Burford, the funder, asked the Court to step in. If the Court grants the petition, it could hear the case and decide the arbitration-jurisdiction question, or it could grant, vacate, and remand the case for the Third Circuit to reconsider in light of the other Supreme Court ruling Burford cites, without deciding who is right. If the Court denies the petition, the Third Circuit's decision stands, meaning the discovery dispute stays in court rather than going to arbitration, and financialright's discovery efforts against Burford continue.
