## In brief

Burford entities ask the Court to review a Third Circuit ruling that a district court lacked jurisdiction to decide their arbitration motions in a discovery dispute with financialright claims GmbH.

## What happened

Burford German Funding and related companies (collectively, Burford) had a funding agreement with financialright claims GmbH (FRC) requiring arbitration of disputes between them. After FRC sued a law firm in Germany and sought discovery from Burford in a Delaware federal court under a statute allowing discovery for use in foreign proceedings, Burford asked that court to pause its own case and send the dispute to arbitration. The district court said it lacked jurisdiction to decide those arbitration motions, while still ruling on FRC's discovery request. The Third Circuit affirmed, and later denied rehearing.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked whether a federal court that already has authority over a case also has authority to decide requests, filed in that same case, to pause the case and send the dispute to arbitration, or whether such requests need some separate, independent basis for the court's authority.

## Where it stands

The petition has been distributed for conference twice, most recently for October 16, 2026, after full briefing including a response and a reply; the docket does not yet show a ruling on whether the Court will hear the case.

## What each outcome would mean

Burford, as petitioner, asks the Court to take up the case. If the Court grants review, it would hear the case fully and decide the jurisdictional question; a grant alone would not decide who wins. If the Court denies the petition, the Third Circuit's ruling against Burford's arbitration motions stands, and FRC's discovery proceeding continues without arbitration being compelled. Burford's petition also asks the Court to instead hold the case and send it back for reconsideration in light of a related case the Court has already agreed to decide, without ruling on the merits itself.
