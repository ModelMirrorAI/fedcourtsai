## In brief

A litigation funder asks the Court to review a ruling that its arbitration motions in a discovery dispute needed a separate basis for federal jurisdiction.

## What happened

Burford German Funding and related companies (Burford) had a funding agreement with financialright claims GmbH (FRC) requiring arbitration of disputes over the agreement. After FRC sued Burford's former law firm in Germany, FRC also sought discovery from Burford in Delaware federal court. Burford asked that court to pause the case and compel arbitration instead. The district court said it lacked jurisdiction over Burford's arbitration motions, though it kept jurisdiction over FRC's discovery request. The Third Circuit, over a dissent, affirmed. Burford now asks the Supreme Court to step in.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked whether a federal court that already has authority to hear a case also has authority to decide motions filed in that same case asking it to pause the proceedings and send the dispute to arbitration, or whether such motions need their own, separate jurisdictional footing.

## Where it stands

The petition was distributed for conference, and the Court then asked for a response from FRC, which filed its opposition brief. The case has not yet been scheduled for a further conference in this record, and no decision on whether to hear the case has been recorded.

## What each outcome would mean

Burford, the petitioner, asks the Court to take up the case. If the Court grants review, it would hear full arguments and decide the jurisdictional question itself, resolving nothing in advance. If it denies review, the Third Circuit's ruling against Burford's arbitration motions stands, and FRC's discovery efforts continue unimpeded. Burford's petition itself also asks, as an alternative, that the Court hold the case and send it back for reconsideration in light of a related case, Jules, without deciding the question either way.
