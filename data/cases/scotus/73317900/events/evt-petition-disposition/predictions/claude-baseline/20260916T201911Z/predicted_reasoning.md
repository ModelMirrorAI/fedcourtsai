# Forecast: what the Court will do with No. 25-1269

**Bottom line.** The Court will deny the petition, most likely at its first
conference after the reply brief is in, in October 2026. P(any grant) = 0.15,
and almost all of that mass is a GVR in light of *Jules v. Andre Balazs
Properties* (decided May 14, 2026), not plenary review.

## Distributions and relists

The docket shows **one** distribution so far (June 17, 2026, for the September
28 long conference). That distribution is superseded: on July 15 the Court called
for a response, the brief in opposition arrived September 14, and petitioners'
reply is due about September 28. Expect a fresh "DISTRIBUTED for Conference of
10/…/2026" entry once the reply is filed or its time runs, so the distribution
count will rise past one with near certainty (0.93). Beyond that redistribution
I expect at most one genuine relist: a GVR request tied to a just-decided case
is sometimes carried over one conference while the Justices confer on whether
the lower court's ground is truly Jules-dependent. Two or more relists would
signal the GVR is live; a single October distribution followed by an order is
the modal path.

## Call for the views of the Solicitor General

No CVSG (0.03). No federal party, no federal program, and a petition that asks
only for a hold-and-GVR; the Court does not invite the Solicitor General on a
GVR request, and the underlying question (whether a section 1782 discovery
application is a "civil action" under FAA section 4) has no evident federal
interest.

## Which question, if any

If the Court acts, it will not take the question presented as written. The
petition frames a broad jurisdictional question about motions "in a pending
matter"; the Third Circuit decided a narrower one, that a section 1782
application is not a "civil action" within section 4's text. A plenary grant on
either framing is very unlikely (about 0.02): no circuit conflict, a
first-impression holding the panel itself called one that "will not often
recur," and vehicle problems the brief in opposition presses (a London
arbitral seat that section 4's within-the-district clause would independently
bar, a Chapter 2 argument the panel held waived, and a second appeal now pending
in the Third Circuit on the same dispute).

## Summary disposition versus plenary review

Conditional on any grant, the route is a GVR in light of *Jules* (0.9 of grant
mass). Petitioners asked for exactly that and nothing else in substance, and
the supplemental brief filed after *Jules* renews the request. I forecast the
GVR fails because the panel's operative ground (section 4's "civil action"
requirement) is one *Jules* never touched; *Jules* held that a court that
stayed a pending suit under section 3 keeps jurisdiction over later section 9
and 10 motions, and it went out of its way to distinguish section 4's
"distinctive" text. The panel's Part IV, which reads *Badgerow* against
petitioners' "jurisdictional anchor" argument, is the only passage a GVR could
hang on, and it is an alternative response rather than the holding.

## Dissent or statement respecting denial

None expected (0.04). A denial here leaves no separate writing: the question is
narrow, the record is procedurally tangled, and no Justice has a visible stake
in the section 1782 and FAA intersection. If anyone wrote, it would be a short
statement from a Justice concerned about the "Coinbase stay" dynamics the
respondent describes, but that is a remote possibility.

## If granted

If the Court did GVR, the Third Circuit would very probably reinstate its
"civil action" holding on remand while disclaiming reliance on *Badgerow*. If
the Court took the case for argument, which I do not expect, the likely ground
would be section 4's text, and I would expect affirmance of the Third Circuit.
