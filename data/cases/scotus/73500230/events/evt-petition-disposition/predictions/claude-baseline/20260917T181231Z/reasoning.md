**Adjust up.**
- *Call for response.* The docket shows "Response Requested" on July 27, 2026, twelve days after the first distribution. A call for response is an affirmative act by at least one chambers and is the single strongest positive signal on this docket. The statpack carries no cut for it, so the size of the lift is my judgment; I treat it as roughly doubling to tripling the band rate on its own.
- *A real, acknowledged three-way split.* The petition documents D.C. (Abbas, Kavanaugh, J.), Second (La Liberte), Fifth (Klocke) and Eleventh (Carbone) holding these statutes displaced; First (Godin) applying them as enacted; Ninth applying its hybrid. The BIO does not deny the split exists for other states' statutes; it argues Nevada's statute is different and that no court has passed on Nevada's post-2013 statute. The reply's answer (Nevada copied California's "probability of prevailing" standard in 2013, and the panel itself said the two statutes are near-identical) is persuasive on the split's existence.
- *Freshness.* Berk v. Choy (2026) reaffirmed the Shady Grove / Sibbach framework and reviewed an unpublished decision, and the Gopher Media en banc concurrence (Bress, J., joined by three others) openly invited reconsideration of Newsham. The question is live inside the Ninth Circuit.
- *One amicus* (Thomas More Society) supports the petition.

**Adjust down, and these dominate.**
- *The Court's track record on exactly this question.* Both filings agree the Court has denied every anti-SLAPP-in-federal-court petition presented to it: Tobinick (2017), Center for Medical Progress v. Planned Parenthood (2019, after a call for response), Retzlaff and Clifford (2021), and Gopher Media v. Melone, denied June 15, 2026, this very Term, from the same circuit, after one conference and without a relist. The shape "call for response, then deny" is precisely this petition's shape so far, so I discount the response-requested lift heavily.
- *Forfeiture / first-view problem.* The BIO says petitioners never argued the statute's inapplicability in the district court or in their Ninth Circuit briefing, raising it first in a motion to stay the mandate after losing. The reply's answer (they won the anti-SLAPP motion below so had no occasion to challenge it, and CoreCivic foreclosed the argument anyway) is respectable but the panel never passed on the question, which the Court treats as a serious vehicle defect.
- *Unpublished memorandum disposition* with a one-sentence public-figure holding; Berk shows this is not disqualifying, but it lowers the odds.
- *Interlocutory and possibly non-outcome-determinative posture.* No fee award has been entered; the district court has a pending Lanham Act fee motion that could supply an independent basis for most fees; claims against the Mulvehill respondents were remanded and are pending. The $600,000 figure is a litigation position, not an award.
- *Small private parties, non-elite Supreme Court counsel, a YouTube defamation dispute.* Nothing here raises stakes beyond the doctrinal question.
- *Percolation argument still available to the Court.* Another Ninth Circuit panel (Concerned Jewish Parents, Sept. 4, 2026) expressly reserved whether Berk is irreconcilable with Newsham, so the Court can plausibly wait for the Ninth Circuit to revisit its own precedent.
- *Question 2* is fact-bound and would not be granted alone.

**Net.** From a roughly 5% band anchor, the response request and the genuine split push toward the low teens; the specific anti-SLAPP denial history (including a denial three months ago from the same circuit), the forfeiture problem, the unpublished opinion and the interlocutory posture pull most of that back. I land at 0.08, above the band rate the evaluator scores against, because a called-for response on a real split is worth something even when the vehicle is weak.
