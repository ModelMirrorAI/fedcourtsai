# Forecast: what the Court will do with No. 25-1329, Wealthy, Inc. v. Cornelia

**Disposition.** The petition will be denied. P(any grant) = 0.08.

**Relists.** The snapshot shows the petition set for the September 28, 2026 long conference; the harness records one distribution (the docket carries two "DISTRIBUTED for Conference of 9/28/2026" entries, the second a redistribution after the brief in opposition arrived). I forecast from the one-distribution state. I expect the petition more likely than not to be decided on the long-conference list without a further distribution, but the chance of at least one relist is materially above the ordinary petition's: the Court has already called for a response, the reply lands the split and the Berk v. Choy angle cleanly, and a petition on which a Justice is weighing a separate writing is often carried over once. P(at least one more distribution) = 0.35. If it is relisted, I expect one relist, not a string of them, ending in a denial.

**CVSG.** No call for the views of the Solicitor General. The question is a Shady Grove / Rules Enabling Act question about state anti-SLAPP statutes in private defamation litigation; no federal program, statute, or enforcement interest is implicated, and the Court has not sought the SG's views on any of the prior anti-SLAPP petitions. P(CVSG after prediction time) = 0.03.

**Which question, if any.** If the Court took the case it would take Question 1 as written, or a slightly tightened version of it ("whether a state anti-SLAPP special motion's burden-shifting standard and fee-shifting provision apply in federal court under Rules 12(b)(6) and 56"), and leave Question 2 behind. Question 2, the one-sentence limited-purpose-public-figure designation, is fact-bound error correction the Court will not grant on alone.

**Summary disposition versus plenary review.** Conditional on a grant, plenary review is the likelier route. The question is a mature, expressly three-way circuit conflict, and Berk v. Choy (2026) predates the Ninth Circuit's superseding opinion and was put before the panel on rehearing, so a GVR "in light of Berk" would be unusual though not impossible. P(cert-order disposition | grant) = 0.20.

**Dissent from denial.** Most likely a bare denial. The realistic separate writing is a statement or dissent from Justice Thomas or Justice Gorsuch on the public-figure / actual-malice question, which the petition pitches at their Berisha v. Lawson writings, or a short statement on the anti-SLAPP split urging a better vehicle. Prior anti-SLAPP denials drew no noted dissent. P(some Justice notes a dissent or statement | denial) = 0.10.

**Timing.** Order on the Monday order list of October 5, 2026 (denial), or, if relisted, in the October 2026 order lists that follow.

**If granted (conditional).** The Court would most likely hold, following the Abbas / La Liberte line, that a state special motion imposing a "probability of prevailing" burden answers the same question as Rules 12(b)(6) and 56 and is displaced, with the fee-shifting that the statute conditions on the motion falling with it; the Ninth Circuit's hybrid would be rejected as a judicial rewrite of the state statute. That is a forecast about the merits only if the Court grants, which I do not expect.
