## In brief

The Court declined to hear Wealthy's bid to revive its defamation and false-advertising suit against Spencer Cornelia and others over a YouTube anti-SLAPP dismissal.

## What happened

Wealthy, Inc. and Dale Buczkowski sued Spencer Cornelia and others over YouTube videos that allegedly made false statements about Buczkowski, including claims about his education, criminal conduct, and a death. A federal district court granted summary judgment against Wealthy, finding it a limited-purpose public figure who could not show actual malice, and denied the defendants' separate motion to dismiss under Nevada's anti-SLAPP law. The Ninth Circuit affirmed the summary judgment but reversed the anti-SLAPP ruling, reviving that motion and triggering a mandatory fee award against Wealthy.

## What the Court is being asked

The case raises two issues: first, whether state laws that let defendants get early dismissal of speech-related lawsuits, with fee awards against losing plaintiffs, can be applied in federal court at all, and if so, in what form, given that federal courts of appeals have split three ways on the question. Second, whether the appeals court wrongly labeled Wealthy a limited-purpose public figure, a status that raises the bar for proving defamation, without the detailed findings that Supreme Court precedent requires.

## Where it stands

The petition was distributed for conference twice, after the Court asked for a response from Cornelia and received a reply from Wealthy. On October 5, 2026, the Court denied the petition.

## What each outcome would mean

Wealthy asked the Court to take up its case and overturn the Ninth Circuit's ruling. By denying the petition, the Court leaves the Ninth Circuit's decision in place: the anti-SLAPP motion stays revived, and Cornelia and the other defendants keep the mandatory fee award that ruling triggered. The denial does not mean the Court agreed with the Ninth Circuit's reasoning on anti-SLAPP laws or public-figure status; it simply leaves that decision standing without ruling on the merits.
