## In brief

Tennessee asked the Supreme Court to vacate a federal appeals court's stay of Christa Pike's execution, and the Court granted that request.

## What happened

Christa Pike was convicted of murder and sentenced to death in Tennessee in 1995. After years of unsuccessful state and federal appeals, her execution was set for September 30, 2026. The day before, hours after the Supreme Court denied an earlier stay request, Pike filed a motion asking a federal court to reopen her old habeas case, arguing a state attorney's recent courtroom remarks were new evidence supporting her claim that her trial lawyers had failed to present mitigating evidence. The district court sent that motion to the Sixth Circuit as a successive habeas petition. Hours before the execution, a divided Sixth Circuit panel granted a short stay to consider the issue; one judge dissented. Tennessee's warden, Kenneth Nelsen, then asked the Supreme Court to vacate that stay.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court was asked whether the appeals court's stay should remain in place while it considered whether Pike's motion was really a disguised, barred attempt to re-raise a previously rejected claim, or whether the stay should be lifted so the execution could proceed.

## Where it stands

The Court granted the application, vacating the Sixth Circuit's stay of execution. Justice Sotomayor dissented, joined by Justices Kagan and Jackson.

## What each outcome would mean

Tennessee, through Warden Nelsen, asked the Court to vacate the stay so the execution could proceed. Granting the application, as the Court did, removed the pause the Sixth Circuit had ordered, allowing the execution to go forward and ending the lower court's additional review of Pike's motion. Had the Court denied the application instead, the Sixth Circuit's stay would have remained in effect, delaying the execution while that court examined whether Pike's claim was properly before it.
