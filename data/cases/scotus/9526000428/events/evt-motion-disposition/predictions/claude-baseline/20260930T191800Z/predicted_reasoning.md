# Forecast — Nelsen v. Pike (26A428), application to vacate a stay of execution

**Disposition.** The Court will vacate the Sixth Circuit's stay. I put the
probability of an **unqualified grant** at **0.72**. The stay is the kind this
Court has repeatedly undone: a divided panel entered it two hours before a
scheduled execution, on a motion the movant filed the day before, without
finding a significant possibility of success on the merits, and in favor of a
prisoner whose own stay application and cert petition the Court denied the day
before (Nos. 26A414 and 26-5696, Sept. 29, 2026). The underlying question is
whether Pike's Rule 60(b)(6) motion attacks the integrity of the federal
habeas proceeding or relitigates the merits of her sentencing-phase
ineffective-assistance claim. Under Gonzalez v. Crosby the motion reads as a
merits attack: its only new fact is the State's expression of sympathy at an
August 2026 state-court hearing, and the theory is that the concession
undermines the deference the federal courts gave the state court's prejudice
finding — which is a challenge to the merits ruling itself. The order will be
short, will cite Hill v. McDonough and the Bucklew/Price line on last-minute
filings, and will likely note the absence of any likelihood-of-success
finding by the panel. I expect the disposition today, late in the evening
Central time, with the execution proceeding overnight or after the Tennessee
Supreme Court resets the date.

The complement (0.28) is split between two shapes. The more likely one is a
mootness or withdrawal path: the Sixth Circuit panel, which described the stay
as short and the motion as fully briefed, could decide the remand motion
before the Court acts, and if it dismisses the successive application and
dissolves its own stay the State withdraws or the Court dismisses the
application as moot. The less likely one is an outright denial, on the view
that a brief stay to let the court of appeals decide a jurisdictional
question it was handed at 8 p.m. the night before is within its equitable
discretion. If the Court denies, the order will be a bare denial with no
noted dissent; if it grants, expect a dissent from Justice Sotomayor, Justice
Kagan, and Justice Jackson, or from a subset of them, most likely without
opinion or with a short statement.

**Response.** The record shows no call for a response. In this Court's
practice on same-day capital vacatur applications the Clerk sets a response
time informally and the inmate's response appears on the docket as a filing,
not as a "response requested" entry. I put 0.25 on a formal request being
recorded after prediction time. Pike's counsel will file a response within a
few hours either way.

**Referral.** Not yet referred. A Circuit Justice does not vacate another
court's stay of execution alone; Justice Kavanaugh will refer the application
to the full Court and the disposing order will recite the referral. I put 0.9
on a referral after prediction time; the residue is the mootness path above,
where nothing is referred because nothing is decided.

**Amici.** Zero on the record. An application filed and disposed of within a
day in a successive-habeas dispute leaves no room for amicus briefing: 0.03
that the count rises.

**Timing** (unscored). Disposition within roughly twelve hours of filing,
almost certainly today, because Tennessee's execution order fixes a single
date and the State's interest in the application expires with it.
