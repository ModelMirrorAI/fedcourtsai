# Forecast: Nelsen v. Pike, No. 26A428 (application to vacate stay of execution)

**Disposition.** The Court will vacate the Sixth Circuit's stay of execution: an
unqualified grant of the State's application, P = 0.78. The order will be a
short, unsigned Court order in the familiar form ("The application to vacate
stay of execution of sentence of death, presented to Justice Kavanaugh and by
him referred to the Court, is granted"), most likely with a noted dissent from
Justices Sotomayor, Kagan, and Jackson and possibly a brief dissenting
statement, but no majority writing. Six Justices to vacate, three to deny.

**Timing.** The record shows the application submitted, the response filed, and
the State's reply filed all on September 30, the scheduled execution date. The
Court will act within hours — tonight or, at the latest, in the small hours of
October 1 — not after a further round of briefing. Nothing resolves a timing
claim, but it disciplines the rest: the window in which anything else can
happen on this docket is measured in hours.

**Response requested (increment).** The record shows no request for a
response; the respondent answered unprompted and the applicant has already
replied. With the matter fully briefed on both sides, the Court or the Circuit
Justice will not call for a response now. P = 0.03.

**Referral to the full Court (increment).** The record shows no referral yet.
Justice Kavanaugh will refer the application to the full Court rather than act
alone, as is the Court's near-invariable practice on a State's application to
vacate an execution stay, and the disposing order will recite the referral.
P = 0.88. The residual is the chance the application never reaches a Court
disposition at all — the Sixth Circuit resolves the transferred matter and
dissolves its own stay first, or the State withdraws — plus a small chance of
single-Justice action.

**Amicus briefs (increment).** The record shows zero amicus entries. None will
be added before disposition: the matter will be decided within hours, and the
State's side of an execution-stay vacatur rarely draws amici. P = 0.05.

**Ground, stated conditionally as context.** If the Court explains itself at
all, it will be by citation rather than opinion: the panel majority entered a
stay "until further order" without finding a significant possibility of success
on the merits, which Hill v. McDonough requires even for a short stay, and the
underlying Rule 60(b) motion — filed the day before the execution on the
strength of a prosecutor's August 13 remark — attacks the merits of a
previously adjudicated ineffective-assistance claim and so is a successive
petition under Gonzalez v. Crosby. Any dissent will stress that the panel
asked only for time to decide a fully briefed jurisdictional question.
