2. **Lower-Court Opinion (`Long v. Davis`, 663 F. App'x 362 (5th Cir. Oct. 19, 2016)) and Intervening Precedent (*Moore v. Texas*, 581 U.S. 1 (Mar. 28, 2017)):**
   - In `Long v. Davis`, 663 F. App'x 362 (5th Cir. Oct. 19, 2016) (`opinion_id: 4090920`), the Fifth Circuit denied a Certificate of Appealability (COA) on Long's *Atkins v. Virginia*, 536 U.S. 304 (2002), intellectual-disability claim and his related due-process continuance claim.
   - **Upward pressure on P(grant) (+0.05 to +0.06):** In discussing Texas's *Atkins* standard, the Fifth Circuit relied on *Ex parte Briseno*, 135 S.W.3d 1 (Tex. Crim. App. 2004), and *Ex parte Hearn*, 310 S.W.3d 424 (Tex. Crim. App. 2010), explicitly noting that although Long demonstrated adaptive deficiencies (struggling in school, never living independently, workplace difficulty), he failed to show that those adaptive deficits were "related to his alleged intellectual disability as required by *Hearn*" rather than drug use, behavioral problems, and a dysfunctional family environment (`663 F. App'x at 366`). On March 28, 2017—five months after the Fifth Circuit's decision and shortly before Long filed his cert petition (`16-8909`) and stay application (`17A145`)—the Supreme Court decided *Moore v. Texas*, 581 U.S. 1 (2017), rejecting *Briseno* and specifically invalidating Texas's requirement that a defendant prove adaptive deficits were unrelated to co-occurring personality disorders, drug abuse, or a troubled upbringing. Because `16-8909` was pending over the summer recess alongside `17A145`, there is a meaningful possibility that the Court grants a stay of execution to preserve its ability to consider a GVR in light of *Moore* (or to allow lower/state court reassessment).
   - **Downward pressure on P(grant) (-0.02):** Notwithstanding its discussion of *Briseno* and *Hearn* on adaptive functioning, the Fifth Circuit's primary ground for denying a COA was Prong 1 (significantly subaverage general intellectual functioning) and the state habeas court's factual finding under 28 U.S.C. § 2254(d) that Long **malingered** on his post-arrest IQ tests (`663 F. App'x at 366–67`). Three pre-trial defense experts (Drs. Goodness, Crowder, and Lacritz) and the State's expert (Dr. Price) concluded that Long was not intellectually disabled and had malingered on post-arrest tests after scoring 91 on a childhood achievement test, and Long's state-habeas expert (Dr. Milam) conceded she had not administered malingering validity tests. Under AEDPA's deferential standard of review, a fact-bound malingering determination on Prong 1 provides Respondent with a strong independent argument that *Moore v. Texas* cannot alter the outcome, and also leaves some probability that the application resolves as ungranted via withdrawal or dismissal if state-court proceedings intervene before execution.
