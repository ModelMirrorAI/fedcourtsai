## In brief

A former border officer convicted of civil-rights and obstruction crimes asks the Court to overturn his convictions, challenging how the appeals court analyzed his conduct.

## What happened

Miguel Angel Delgado, Jr., a Customs and Border Protection officer, was convicted after a bench trial of depriving two border crossers of their constitutional rights through excessive force and of falsifying a report about one incident. The district court sentenced him to concurrent 24-month prison terms. The Fifth Circuit affirmed, finding the evidence, including testimony about his training violations, demeanor, and inaccurate statements afterward, sufficient to support the convictions. Delgado now asks the Supreme Court to review that decision.

## What the Court is being asked

The case raises questions about how courts should evaluate claims that a law enforcement officer used excessive force and whether a criminal conviction for that conduct requires proof the officer actually knew he was acting unlawfully, or merely that his actions were objectively unreasonable. It also asks how much injury must result before such a violation becomes a felony rather than a misdemeanor, and whether a related conviction for falsifying a report can stand if the underlying force findings are flawed.

## Where it stands

The petition was distributed for conference, after which the Court asked the government to respond. The government has since requested, and received, multiple extensions, most recently moving the response deadline to November 25, 2026. The Court has not yet acted on the petition itself.

## What each outcome would mean

Delgado, who lost in the Fifth Circuit, is asking the Court to take up his case and reverse his convictions. If the Court grants review, it would hear full arguments on these questions and decide them; granting review itself would not determine who wins. If the Court denies review, Delgado's convictions and sentence, as affirmed by the Fifth Circuit, would remain in place.
