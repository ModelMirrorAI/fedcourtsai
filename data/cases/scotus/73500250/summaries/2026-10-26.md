## In brief

A former border officer asks the Court to review his federal convictions for excessive force and falsifying a report after two border incidents.

## What happened

Miguel Angel Delgado, Jr., a Customs and Border Protection officer, was convicted after a bench trial of two counts of depriving people of their constitutional rights and one count of falsifying a report about a use-of-force incident. The charges arose from two encounters at ports of entry in which people Delgado physically restrained suffered injuries, and from an allegedly false account he gave of one incident. The Fifth Circuit affirmed his convictions, finding the evidence sufficient to support the jury's findings on force, intent, and falsification. Delgado now asks the Supreme Court to take up his case.

## What the Court is being asked

The petition raises four questions about how courts should evaluate this kind of case. It asks whether the appeals court wrongly limited its excessive-force review to the moments force was used rather than the full situation, contrary to a recent Supreme Court ruling. It asks what proof of intent is needed to convict someone of willfully violating constitutional rights. It asks how much injury is needed to turn this kind of violation into a more serious felony. And it asks whether the false-report conviction can stand if the underlying findings about unlawful force were themselves flawed.

## Where it stands

The petition was filed and distributed for a conference in September 2026. The government, which had initially waived its right to respond, was then asked to respond; it has since received several extensions, with its response now due in late November 2026. No decision on whether to grant review has been recorded.

## What each outcome would mean

Delgado, who lost in the lower courts, is asking the Court to take up his case and overturn his convictions. If the Court grants review, it would hear full arguments and decide the legal questions raised; this would not itself determine who wins. If the Court denies review, Delgado's convictions and sentence, as affirmed by the Fifth Circuit, would remain in place, and the government's position would prevail without further Supreme Court proceedings.
