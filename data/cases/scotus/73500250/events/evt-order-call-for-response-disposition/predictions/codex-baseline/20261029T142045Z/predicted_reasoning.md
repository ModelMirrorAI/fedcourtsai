# Forecast of the Court's action

The Court will deny the petition without a separate writing. The response request will produce ordinary adversarial briefing, rather than a grant or a sustained series of relists. Any grant, including a grant-vacate-remand or summary reversal, has probability 9%.

The starting docket has one distribution, entered July 8 for the September 28, 2026 conference, followed by the July 27 response request. No conference had yet occurred within this record. The Court will distribute the petition at least once more after the response is filed or its time expires (99% probability of a distribution increment), most likely adding one distribution rather than several. That routine redistribution is not evidence that a completed conference has substantively relisted the petition. Denial will most likely come in the autumn 2026 conference cycle after the response and opportunity for a reply, rather than on the response-request date. This timing forecast is from the staged July 27 vantage, not a claim about the docket's status on October 29.

The Court will not separately invite the Solicitor General's views (0.3% probability of a new CVSG). The United States is already the respondent and its requested response supplies the government's position directly.

If the Court takes a question, the likeliest focus is a narrowed version of the second question: the evidence needed to establish subjective willfulness under 18 U.S.C. § 242, rather than merely unreasonable force. The bodily-injury threshold in the third question is a secondary candidate. The first question's proposed application of Barnes v. Felix is less likely to justify intervention because the appended opinion expressly considers conduct preceding the physical force within its constitutional analysis. The fourth question, concerning the false-report conviction, is principally a fact-specific derivative challenge rather than the strongest independent reason for review.

Conditional on any grant, plenary briefing and argument is the likelier route: 70%, versus 30% for disposition in the cert order itself. A summary correction on the Barnes issue is possible, but Barnes already preceded the appellate judgment; the record does not identify a later intervening decision requiring a routine remand. If plenary review is granted, the likely doctrinal issue will be the distinction between circumstantial proof of criminal intent and proof of unreasonable force, not a fresh determination of the encounter facts.

Conditional on denial, a dissent from denial or statement respecting denial has probability 4%. No such writing is the modal call. Thomas, Alito, or Gorsuch are the most plausible authors if one appears concerning criminal intent or the reach of federal criminal liability; none is individually likely to write. The independent order-writer probabilities include writings on grant orders as well as on denials, and do not predict that a denial has a majority opinion.
