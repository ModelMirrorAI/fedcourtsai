- **Call for the Views of the Solicitor General (`cvsg-increment` = 0.00):** No CVSG will issue because the United States is already the named respondent represented by the Solicitor General, and the Court has already called for the government's response directly.
- **Questions Presented If Granted:** If the Court were to grant plenary review, it would limit the grant to Question 1—whether the Fifth Circuit's Fourth Amendment excessive-force analysis in a prosecution under 18 U.S.C. § 242 improperly narrowed the totality-of-the-circumstances inquiry contrary to *Barnes v. Felix*, 605 U.S. 73 (2025)—or, at most, Questions 1 and 2 (adding the *Screws v. United States* willfulness standard under § 242), while declining the fact-bound "bodily injury" (QP 3) and 18 U.S.C. § 1519 (QP 4) sufficiency questions. Conditional on a grant of plenary review, the Court would likely affirm or vacate on narrow grounds addressing how *Barnes*'s rejection of the moment-of-threat rule applies to criminal § 242 sufficiency review.
- **Summary Disposition vs. Plenary Review (`summary-disposition-route` = 0.67 conditional on grant):** Conditional on the Court granting the petition (`P(grant) = 0.06`), a summary disposition—specifically, a grant, vacatur, and remand (GVR) in light of *Barnes v. Felix*, 605 U.S. 73 (2025)—is roughly twice as likely (`0.04` unconditional, `0.67` conditional on grant) as a grant for plenary briefing and oral argument (`0.02` unconditional, `0.33` conditional on grant). The Fifth Circuit issued its published opinion nine months after *Barnes* abrogated the Fifth Circuit's own moment-of-threat doctrine yet never cited *Barnes*, relying instead on pre-*Barnes* circuit precedent (*United States v. Brugman*, 364 F.3d 613 (5th Cir. 2004)). A summary reversal is virtually ruled out because the trial record (following a three-day bench trial with video evidence and expert testimony) shows that the Fifth Circuit did examine pre-seizure events that cut against the defendant.
- **Separate Writings on Denial (`dissent-from-denial` = 0.03):** Conditional on the Court denying certiorari, the denial will very likely issue as a standard summary order without any recorded dissent or statement respecting the denial (`P = 0.03` of any separate writing). In the unlikely event a Justice writes separately (most plausibly Justice Gorsuch, Justice Thomas, or Justice Sotomayor), it would likely be a brief statement or dissent addressing either the Fifth Circuit's failure to cite *Barnes v. Felix* or the mens rea standard under 18 U.S.C. § 242.
