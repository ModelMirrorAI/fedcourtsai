# Predicted reasoning — Delgado v. United States, No. 25-1347

**Posture forecast from:** one distribution (for the 9/28/2026 conference), the
United States' waiver of response, and the Court's call for a response on
July 27, 2026 (due August 26). No CVSG is possible in the ordinary sense — the
United States is the respondent.

**Disposition.** The petition will be **denied**. The Solicitor General will file
a brief in opposition (or obtain an extension and then file), arguing that the
Fifth Circuit applied the totality of the circumstances, that the questions are
sufficiency-of-the-evidence disputes from a bench trial, and that the asserted
splits on Screws willfulness and on "bodily injury" are either illusory or not
implicated on these facts (a nose laceration and concussion-type symptoms).

**Relists.** The petition will be distributed again once the response is in
(roughly one more distribution, for a late-fall conference). I expect it to be
decided at that first post-response conference, without a further relist.

**CVSG.** None — the United States is already the party.

**If granted.** The likeliest vehicle question is QP 2 (whether § 242
willfulness under Screws can rest on evidence of training violations and
demeanor), possibly narrowed; QP 1's Barnes v. Felix argument and QP 3's
bodily-injury threshold are less likely, and QP 4 rides on the others. A
summary route (GVR) is a real share of any grant — e.g. if the government
concedes a Barnes-related error in the excessive-force analysis — but plenary
review remains the more likely grant route.

**Separate writing.** On denial, no separate writing is expected; if any, a
statement respecting denial from Justice Gorsuch or Justice Sotomayor about
the mens rea standard under Screws is the most plausible.
