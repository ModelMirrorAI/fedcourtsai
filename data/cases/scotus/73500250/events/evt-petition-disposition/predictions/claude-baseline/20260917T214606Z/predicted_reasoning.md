# Forecast: Delgado v. United States, No. 25-1347 (evt-petition-disposition)

**Disposition.** The Court denies certiorari. P(any grant) = 0.09.

**Relists.** The docket shows one distribution (for the Conference of September 28, 2026), entered before the Court called for a response on July 27. The government's brief in opposition is now due September 25, so the petition will come off the September 28 list and be redistributed once the opposition and any reply are in, most likely for a conference in late October or November 2026. Expect at least one further distribution with near certainty (P ≈ 0.95), and a modest chance of one more beyond that if a chambers is weighing a separate writing. The redistribution here is mechanical, not a signal of interest in itself; the call for a response is the signal.

**CVSG.** None. The United States is the respondent, so there is no Solicitor General to invite (P ≈ 0.01).

**The response.** The Solicitor General waived, was asked to respond, and will oppose. The opposition will lead with sufficiency-of-the-evidence deference after a bench trial, then argue that the Fifth Circuit applied Screws's "reckless disregard" formulation as every circuit does, that the willfulness finding rested on concealment (false statements to a supervisor and a false written report), which is subjective-intent evidence, and that the injuries (a nose laceration; dizziness, confusion, and tinnitus) satisfy any circuit's bodily-injury threshold, so no asserted split is implicated.

**Which question, if granted.** Only QP 2, the willfulness element of 18 U.S.C. § 242 under Screws v. United States, is cert-worthy in the abstract. The Court would reformulate it to ask whether § 242 willfulness requires subjective awareness of unlawfulness or may be inferred from objective unreasonableness alone. QP 1 (Barnes v. Felix) misfits a case where the officer was the escalating party and broader context cuts against him; QP 3 (bodily injury) fails on its own facts; QP 4 (§ 1519) is derivative.

**Summary disposition.** Unlikely. No intervening decision of this Court bears on the questions: Barnes v. Felix predates the Fifth Circuit's decision and was available to it. Conditional on a grant, P(disposed of in the cert order) ≈ 0.2, and that residual is mostly the possibility that the Solicitor General's compelled response identifies an error the government does not defend.

**Dissent from denial.** Probably none (P ≈ 0.08). A short statement respecting denial on the Screws mens-rea question from Justice Gorsuch or Justice Thomas is the plausible form if one appears; a full dissent is unlikely on a record this fact-bound.

**Timing.** Denial at the first conference after redistribution, on an order list in late October or November 2026.
