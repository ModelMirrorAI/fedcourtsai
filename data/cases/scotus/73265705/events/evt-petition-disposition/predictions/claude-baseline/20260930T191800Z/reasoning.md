# Why 0.30, and not the 0.17 anchor or something higher

**Inputs read.** The provisioned snapshot `record/snapshots/2026-09-30.json` (19 docket entries through the September 30 distribution for the October 16, 2026 conference), `record/context.json` (mode `forward`, band `elevated` under `sal-v4`, `distribution_count` 2, no CVSG, Term 2025, no cutoff), `event.yaml` (kind petition, no stage recorded, so cert stage; moment distribution), and all three provisioned documents: `questions-presented.txt`, `petition.txt` (52 pp.), and `brief-in-opposition.txt`, which carries both VirtaMove's July 13 opposition and the Solicitor General's September 11 brief for the federal respondent. None was `empty_text`. Beyond the provisioned inputs I fetched Google's September 29 reply (submitted to the docket but not provisioned) and the supremecourt.gov dockets for the three companion petitions the reply names; see `retrieval.md`. No outcome-revealing material surfaced: the petition is set for a conference sixteen days from now.

**Anchor.** Cert stage, `band: elevated`, and the statpack's segment table is computed under `sal-v4`, which matches the context, so the band's bracketed `reached` rate is the yardstick. Pooling the eight Term rows strictly before this case's Term (2017–2024) gives 484.4 / 2810 = **17.2%**. For orientation only, the paid-segment CVSG cut (29.4% granted) is not applicable because no CVSG was or can be called here; the relist-count cut is read below for the increment claim, not for the level.

**Adjustments upward (to about 0.30).**
- *The Court called for a response.* The PTO waived on May 22; the Court itself requested a response on June 12, after the first distribution. That is the Court's own signal of interest, the closest cert-stage analogue to a CVSG where the government is already a party, and it is absent from most elevated-band petitions in the pool.
- *Seven cert-stage amicus briefs*, including 35 IP law professors, the PTAB Bar Association, the Association for Accessible Medicines, Unified Patents, and industry coalitions, filed on the response due date. Amicus weight at this level is well above the elevated-band norm.
- *Importance and climate.* The petition and reply document a collapse in IPR institution for patents over six years old under the PTO's "settled expectations" policy, and the reviewability question sits in the Court's current appetite for policing agency discretion (Loper Bright is cited throughout). The Court has taken AIA-structure cases repeatedly (Cuozzo, SAS, Oil States, Thryv, Arthrex).
- *Companion petitions.* Intel v. Squires (26-73), Tesla v. Granite (26-136), and Kahoot! v. Interstellar (26-198) raise the same or overlapping questions, with government responses due October 14 and 16. A cluster this size makes it likelier the Court takes the question somewhere this Term, and Google's is the earliest and first fully briefed vehicle. A grant in a companion with a hold here also yields a later GVR, which counts as a grant on the scored binary.
- *Counsel.* Munger, Tolles & Olson with a former Assistant to the Solicitor General as counsel of record.

**Adjustments downward (keeping it well under 0.5).**
- *The Solicitor General opposes, and on posture.* The government's brief argues the petition never engages the Cheney mandamus standard, that the Federal Circuit's two-page nonprecedential order applied it correctly, and that no statute compels institution, so no "clear and indisputable right" can exist. The reply answers with Jicarilla and TC Heartland (the Court reviews legal questions arising from mandamus denials de novo), which is a fair answer, but the posture still gives the Court an easy exit.
- *Precedent favors the government.* Thryv and Cuozzo read § 314(d) to bar review of challenges "closely tied" to institution; Arthrex describes institution as committed to the Director's "unreviewable discretion." The petitioner's McNary-style policy-versus-determination distinction is plausible but untested.
- *Track record.* The Court denied Apple v. Optis (21-118), Mylan v. Janssen (21-202), Intel v. VLSI (21-888), and Intel v. Vidal (23-135), each attacking discretionary-denial practice, some with the government participating. The present policy is more sweeping than Fintiv was, which is why I do not treat those denials as dispositive, but they are the nearest priors and they all went one way.
- *Vehicle disputes.* Both opponents say the PTO's decision rested on a "holistic assessment" including Board resources, not patent age alone; the reply quotes the decision as resting on age despite other factors cutting the other way. The PTO's new practice of unexplained denials, and a pending proposed rulemaking on discretionary denial, could also lead the Court to wait or to prefer Intel's APA posture.
- *No conflict.* The Federal Circuit has exclusive jurisdiction, and the claimed § 701(a)(2) conflict with the D.C., First and Second Circuits is thin; the SG notes the order below did not even cite § 701(a)(2).

Net: roughly 0.35–0.40 that the Court takes the settled-expectations or § 314(d) rule-review question in some vehicle this Term, times roughly 0.8 that Google's petition ends as a grant, consolidated grant, or later GVR rather than a hold-and-deny, gives about 0.30.

**Claims.**
- `disposition` 0.30, equal to the top-level probability.
- `relist-increment` 0.70. The docket's two distributions overstate the true relist state: the first (June 9, for June 25) was superseded by the June 12 call for a response before any conference vote, so the petition reaches October 16 at its first fully briefed conference. The paid-segment relist cut says P(at least one relist) is about 25% and P(a second | one) about 28% across the population, but that shape is for petitions without companions arriving weeks later. Here the Court has the ordinary reason to reschedule or relist to consider the set together, and a serious petition with a call for response is rarely decided in one conference. I discount for a straight denial on October 16 and for a silent hold that adds no distribution entry.
- `cvsg-increment` 0.01. The United States is a respondent that has already filed; a CVSG is structurally foreclosed.
- `summary-disposition-route` 0.25, conditional on a grant. The hold-then-GVR path (companion granted, this petition vacated and remanded after the companion decision) is the main route by which a grant here would ride in the cert order; outright summary reversal against the government's precedent-backed position is not credible.
- `dissent-from-denial` 0.15, conditional on denial. Justice Gorsuch's prior separate writings on § 314(d) make a statement conceivable, but the four prior denials in this cluster drew none, and a post-companion denial would be routine.

**Big case score 0.55.** High stakes for the patent system and for administrative-law reviewability doctrine, with heavy institutional amicus interest; not a case with broad public salience.

**Where to discount me.** I cannot observe how the Court will sequence the four petitions; if it denies Google alone on October 16 rather than waiting for the companions, both the grant and the relist numbers are too high. I also lean on a general sense that a call for response materially raises grant odds; the statpack publishes no cut for that signal, so that adjustment is judgment, not a committed rate. The corpus `query` surface ranks by recency and returned no topical patent priors, so the priors I used are the ones named in the briefs, not corpus-retrieved rows.
