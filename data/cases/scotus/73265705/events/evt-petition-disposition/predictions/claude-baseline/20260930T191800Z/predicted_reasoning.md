# Forecast: what the Court will do with Google LLC v. VirtaMove, Corp. (No. 25-1230)

**Disposition.** More likely denied than granted, but this is a live grant candidate: P(any grant, GVR included) = 0.30. The Court has already shown interest once, calling for the Solicitor General's response on June 12, 2026 after the PTO waived, and seven amicus briefs sit on the docket. Against that, the Solicitor General opposes review squarely on posture (a mandamus denial the petition never engages under *Cheney*), *Thryv* and *Cuozzo* read § 314(d) the government's way, and the Court has denied four earlier petitions attacking PTO discretionary-denial practice (Apple v. Optis, Mylan, Intel v. VLSI, Intel v. Vidal).

**Relists.** The docket shows two distribution entries: the June 9 distribution for the June 25, 2026 conference, which the call for a response superseded before any vote, and the September 30 distribution for the October 16, 2026 conference. Functionally the petition is at its first fully briefed conference. I expect at least one further distribution (P = 0.70), for two reasons. First, three companion petitions raising the same or overlapping questions are pending with government responses due in the same fortnight: Intel v. Squires (No. 26-73, response due October 14), Kahoot! v. Interstellar (No. 26-198, due October 16), and Tesla v. Granite Vehicle Ventures (No. 26-136). The Court's ordinary practice is to reschedule or relist the lead petition so the set is considered together, which would surface as a new distribution entry in late October or November. Second, a petition the Court has already called for a response on, with this amicus weight, is rarely disposed of at a single conference when it is a serious candidate. The main path to no further distribution is a straight denial on October 16, or a silent hold without redistribution.

**CVSG.** None will issue (P = 0.01). The United States is already a respondent and has filed its brief in opposition, so there is no one to invite.

**Which question the Court would take, if it grants.** The Court would take the reviewability question (QP 2), either as written or reformulated around whether § 314(d) and the mandamus posture bar review of a PTO institution policy alleged to exceed statutory authority, and would reach QP 1 (the PTO's authority to treat patent age as a reason to deny institution) only as the merits of that reviewable claim. A plenary grant is likelier to come consolidated with, or alongside, Kahoot! or Intel than alone; Google's is the earliest-filed and first fully briefed vehicle, which favors it as the lead case if the Court takes the settled-expectations question, while the Intel petition (an APA appeal rather than a mandamus denial) is the cleaner vehicle for the § 314(d) rule-review question.

**Summary disposition versus plenary review.** Conditional on any grant, P(the disposition rides in the cert order itself) = 0.25. That mass is the hold-and-GVR path: the Court grants a companion, holds this petition, and after deciding the companion vacates the Federal Circuit's order for reconsideration. A summary reversal is not in prospect; the government's position rests on this Court's own precedents, so nothing here is clearly wrong under existing law.

**If denied.** P(some Justice notes a dissent or statement respecting denial | denial) = 0.15. Justice Gorsuch has written separately on § 314(d) reviewability (Thryv dissent) and on the IPR system generally, and a statement flagging the reviewability question as one for a better vehicle is conceivable, but the four prior denials in this cluster drew no noted dissent, and a denial after a companion grant would be a routine cleanup that draws none.

**If granted, on the merits (conditional only).** The likely ground would be that § 314(d) bars review of individual institution determinations but not of a standing agency policy alleged to exceed statutory authority (the *McNary* line the reply presses), with the settled-expectations question remanded or decided narrowly. That is a forecast about a decision that a denial will never produce, and it is stated here only to discipline the grant call.
