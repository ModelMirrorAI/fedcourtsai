## In brief

Google asks the Court to decide whether patent officials can refuse to reconsider old patents' validity, and whether courts may review that refusal at all.

## What happened

Google filed petitions asking the Patent and Trademark Office to review the validity of a VirtaMove patent issued in 2010. The agency declined to start that review, citing VirtaMove's "settled expectations" in a patent that had been in force more than fourteen years. Google asked the Federal Circuit for a mandamus order, an extraordinary form of court relief, directing the agency to reconsider without regard to the patent's age. The Federal Circuit denied relief, relying on an earlier decision holding that such denials are not subject to this kind of review. Google then asked the Supreme Court to take the case. VirtaMove and the federal government, representing the Patent Office, both oppose review.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked whether the agency overstepped its authority by treating a patent's age as grounds to refuse reconsidering its validity, when the patent laws set no age-based cutoff for such review. It is also asked whether courts have any power to check that kind of agency decision, given a law making the agency's refusal to institute review final and unappealable.

## Where it stands

The petition has been distributed for conference twice, after the government requested and received two extensions of time to respond and Google then filed a reply. The docket shows no decision yet on whether the Court will hear the case.

## What each outcome would mean

Google, as the party asking the Court to step in, would win the chance to have its case heard in full if the Court grants review; the question would then be briefed and argued before the Court decides it. If the Court denies review, the Federal Circuit's decision stands, leaving in place the agency's denial of Google's request to reconsider VirtaMove's patent and the ruling that such denials generally cannot be challenged in court.
