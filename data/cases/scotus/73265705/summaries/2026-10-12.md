## In brief

Google asks the Supreme Court to review whether patent officials may deny patent challenges based on a patent's age, and whether courts can review that choice at all.

## What happened

Google sought administrative review, called inter partes review, of a VirtaMove patent it had been sued over, asking the Patent and Trademark Office (PTO) to reconsider the patent's validity. The PTO declined to start that review, citing in part the patent's age—over fourteen years—as creating "settled expectations" for VirtaMove. Google asked the Federal Circuit for a writ of mandamus, an order directing the PTO to reconsider, arguing the agency had exceeded its authority. The Federal Circuit denied relief, relying on its own recent rulings that such denials are not reviewable this way. Google then asked the Supreme Court to take the case.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked whether patent officials have authority to refuse to review a patent's validity simply because of how long the patent has been in force, when the law allows such challenges at any point in a patent's life. The Court is also asked whether courts have any power to check that kind of agency decision when a challenger argues the agency exceeded what the law allows.

## Where it stands

The petition has been fully briefed, including responses from VirtaMove and the federal government opposing review and a reply from Google. It was distributed for the Court's conference on October 16, 2026, and the docket does not yet show an outcome.

## What each outcome would mean

Google, as the party petitioning the Supreme Court, is asking the Court to hear the case after losing before the Federal Circuit. If the Court grants review, it will hear full arguments and decide the legal questions itself, deciding nothing in advance about who wins. If the Court denies review, the Federal Circuit's decision stands, meaning the PTO's refusal to reconsider VirtaMove's patent remains in place and Google's mandamus request stays denied. VirtaMove and the federal government, as respondents, support leaving that outcome undisturbed.
