## In brief

Google asks the Court to decide whether patent officials may deny reviewing an old patent's validity based on the owner's "settled expectations."

## What happened

Google filed petitions asking the Patent Office to reexamine the validity of a VirtaMove patent through a process called inter partes review. The Patent Office refused to start that review, citing VirtaMove's "settled expectations" in a patent that had been in force over fourteen years. Google sought review in the Federal Circuit through a mandamus petition, an emergency-style request asking a higher court to order an official to act. The Federal Circuit denied relief, relying on an earlier decision holding that such denials are shielded from judicial review and that Google's claim did not qualify for the narrow exceptions to that shield.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked whether the Patent Office has legal authority to deny review of a patent's validity simply because of the patent's age, when the patent laws set no such age-based cutoff. It is also asked whether courts have any power to check the Patent Office when it denies review this way, or whether that decision is completely beyond judicial reach.

## Where it stands

The petition has been distributed for conference, and the Court asked for a response from the respondents. Both VirtaMove and the federal government, representing the Patent Office, filed briefs opposing review, and Google has filed a reply; the case awaits the Court's decision on whether to hear it.

## What each outcome would mean

Google asked the Court to take up the case after losing before the Federal Circuit. If the Court grants review, it will hear full arguments and decide whether the Patent Office's practice is lawful and reviewable, deciding nothing yet about who is right. If it denies review, the Federal Circuit's decision stands, leaving in place the Patent Office's denial of review for VirtaMove's patent and the rule that such denials generally cannot be challenged in court.
