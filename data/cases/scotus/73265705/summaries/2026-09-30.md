## In brief

Google asks the Court to decide whether patent officials may refuse to reconsider an old patent's validity, and whether courts can review that refusal.

## What happened

VirtaMove sued Google for patent infringement, and Google asked the Patent and Trademark Office (PTO) to reconsider the patent's validity through a process called inter partes review. The PTO declined, citing in part the patent's age—over fourteen years—as creating "settled expectations" that favored leaving it alone. Google sought a court order (mandamus) directing the PTO to reconsider without weighing the patent's age, but the Federal Circuit denied relief, holding that such refusals are not subject to judicial review. Google now asks the Supreme Court to take up the case.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked whether the PTO has authority to treat a patent's age as a reason to decline reconsidering its validity, given that the patent laws set no age-based cutoff for such review. The Court is also asked whether federal courts have any power to review a decision like this one, where Google says the PTO ignored limits Congress placed on the agency's authority, or whether that decision is entirely beyond judicial review.

## Where it stands

The petition has been distributed for conference twice, most recently for October 16, 2026, after full briefing including an opposition from VirtaMove, a separate opposition from the federal government, and Google's reply. The docket shows no decision yet on whether the Court will hear the case.

## What each outcome would mean

Google, as the party that lost in the Federal Circuit, is asking the Court to take up the case. If the Court grants review, it will decide the question itself, which does not by itself favor either side. If the Court denies review, the Federal Circuit's decision stands, leaving in place the rule that courts cannot review the PTO's refusal to reconsider a patent's validity on these grounds, and Google's challenge to that specific denial ends. VirtaMove and the federal government, which both urge denial, would keep that outcome.
