## What happened

Google filed petitions asking the Patent and Trademark Office (PTO) to review the validity of a patent that VirtaMove had asserted against it in infringement litigation. The PTO refused to open that review, citing VirtaMove's "settled expectations" because the patent had been in force for more than fourteen years. Google asked the Federal Circuit for a writ of mandamus—an order forcing the PTO to reconsider—arguing the agency exceeded its legal authority. The Federal Circuit denied relief, relying on its own recent precedent holding that such review-denial decisions are shielded from court review. Google then asked the Supreme Court to take up the case.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked whether the PTO has legal authority to refuse this kind of patent review simply because a patent is old enough that its owner has developed "settled expectations" in it, when the patent laws otherwise allow such review at any point in a patent's life. The Court is also asked whether courts have any power at all to check the PTO's decisions in this area, or whether Congress cut off judicial review of them entirely. VirtaMove responds that this decision is committed to the PTO's discretion, that Google has other options such as a separate court challenge or defending itself in the ongoing infringement suit, and that the case is a poor fit for the extraordinary mandamus remedy Google is seeking.

## Where it stands

The petition has been distributed for conference, and the Court asked for a response from the other side, which was filed after two extensions. The case has not yet been scheduled for the Court to decide whether it will hear the case.
