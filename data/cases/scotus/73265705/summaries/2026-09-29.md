## In brief

Google asks the Court to hear its challenge to the Patent Office's practice of refusing to review older patents' validity based on the patent holder's "settled expectations."

## What happened

Google sought inter partes review, a Patent Office process for challenging a patent's validity, against a patent VirtaMove had asserted against it in infringement litigation. The Patent Office refused to open that review, citing VirtaMove's "settled expectations" in a patent that had been in force over fourteen years. Google asked the Federal Circuit for a writ of mandamus, an order directing a lower body to act, arguing the Patent Office's practice exceeded its statutory power. The Federal Circuit denied relief, following its own recent precedent holding such denials are not reviewable by courts.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked whether the Patent Office has authority to refuse review of a patent's validity simply because of the patent's age, when the patent laws set no such time limit, and whether courts have any power to check that practice at all, given a law making the agency's institution decisions final and unappealable.

## Where it stands

The petition was distributed for conference, after which the Court asked for a response; the government, as respondent, twice requested and received extensions before filing its opposition brief in September 2026. VirtaMove separately filed a brief opposing review. The docket shows no conference action since these filings were completed.

## What each outcome would mean

Google, as petitioner, asked the Court to take up the case after losing in the Federal Circuit. If the Court grants review, it will hear full arguments and decide whether the Patent Office's practice is lawful and reviewable, without presuming either side correct. If the Court denies review, the Federal Circuit's decision stands, leaving in place the Patent Office's refusal to reconsider VirtaMove's patent and the rule that such denials generally cannot be challenged in court.
