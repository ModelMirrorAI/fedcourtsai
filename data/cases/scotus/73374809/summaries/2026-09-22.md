## What happened

Kenneth Matsumura, a California physician, developed a cancer treatment he says preserves patients' immune cells and improves outcomes. He operated clinics in Berkeley and, starting in 2017, on a Nevada Indian reservation, planning to share revenue with tribes. California's medical board revoked his license, citing poor record-keeping, incorrect chemotherapy practice on three patients, and excessive fees. Matsumura's petition says the board's real motive was protecting conventional cancer treatments and state tax revenue, and that the hearing judge ignored favorable evidence. His appeal was rejected after a court clerk allegedly refused to accept a flash drive containing lower-court records; the California Supreme Court denied review without comment.

## What the Court is being asked

Matsumura asks the Court to step in because, he argues, the license revocation interferes with a physician's ability to treat members of a federally recognized tribe and blocks a treatment that could save the federal government money on healthcare spending. He frames this as a question of whether the federal government and the Supreme Court may intervene when a state licensing action harms out-of-state citizens or tribal interests, and whether the state's action improperly reached into an area tied to interstate and tribal commerce.

## Where it stands
