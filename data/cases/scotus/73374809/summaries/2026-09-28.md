## In brief

A California doctor whose medical license was revoked asks the Court to review that revocation after state courts rejected his appeals.

## What happened

Kenneth Matsumura, a California-licensed physician, developed a cancer treatment and operated clinics in Berkeley and on a Nevada Indian reservation. California's medical board revoked his license, citing poor recordkeeping, improper administration of chemotherapy to three patients, and excessive charges. Matsumura says the revocation was unjustified and politically motivated. He appealed within the California courts; the petition says the Court of Appeal rejected his appeal after a clerk would not accept a flash drive containing lower-court records, and the California Supreme Court denied review without comment on November 13, 2024. Matsumura, representing himself, then petitioned the U.S. Supreme Court to review the state courts' decisions.

## What the Court is being asked

Matsumura asks the Court to decide whether it and the federal government may step in when a state licensing action cuts off a medical treatment, particularly one he says could save Indian tribes and the federal Medicare program large sums of money. He also raises the Constitution's Commerce Clause, arguing it protects a licensed physician's ability to treat members of Indian tribes regardless of a state's licensing decision.

## Where it stands

The petition was distributed for the Court's conference of September 28, 2026. The docket shows no action on it since that distribution.

## What each outcome would mean

Matsumura, as petitioner, is asking the Court to take up his case. If the Court grants review, it would hear full arguments and decide the legal questions he raises; a grant alone would not decide who wins. If the Court denies review, the California Supreme Court's denial stands, leaving the license revocation and the lower courts' rulings against Matsumura in place, without the U.S. Supreme Court ruling on the merits of his claims.
