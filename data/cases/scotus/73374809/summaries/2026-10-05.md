## In brief

A doctor whose California medical license was revoked asked the Supreme Court to review that decision; the Court declined to hear the case.

## What happened

Kenneth Matsumura, a California-licensed physician who developed a cancer treatment, had his medical license revoked by the California Medical Board after proceedings finding problems with his record-keeping, his treatment of three patients, and his fees. Matsumura, representing himself, says the process was unfair and that the board acted for financial reasons. His appeal was rejected by a California appeals court, which said he had not provided the lower court record; he says court staff mistakenly refused to accept it. The California Supreme Court denied review without comment. Matsumura then asked the U.S. Supreme Court to take up the case.

## What the Court is being asked

Matsumura's petition asks the Court to find that federal courts may step in when a state licensing action cuts off a medical treatment that benefits patients in another state or members of a Native American tribe, particularly where federal healthcare spending could be affected. He frames this as a matter the Constitution's provisions on federal judicial power and interstate commerce address.

## Where it stands

The petition was distributed for the Court's conference on September 28, 2026, and on October 5, 2026, the Court denied it.

## What each outcome would mean

Matsumura, the petitioner, asked the Court to review the revocation of his medical license. By denying the petition, the Court leaves in place the California Supreme Court's denial of review, which followed the appeals court's rejection of his appeal and the original license revocation by the Medical Board. This denial is not a ruling that those decisions were correct; it simply means the Supreme Court will not review them, and the license revocation stands.
