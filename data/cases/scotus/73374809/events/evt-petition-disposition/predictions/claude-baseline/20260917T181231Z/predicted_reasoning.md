# Forecast: what the Court will do with No. 25-1301, Matsumura v. Court of Appeal of California

**Disposition.** The petition will be denied at or shortly after the September 28, 2026 long conference, without comment. P(any grant) = 0.005.

**Relists.** The docket shows exactly one distribution (July 8, 2026, for the conference of September 28, 2026) and no prior relist. I expect no further distribution: the petition is disposed of on its first consideration. A rescheduling entry ahead of the long conference is the only realistic way the count rises, and that is uncommon for an unopposed pro se paid petition. P(at least one more distribution) = 0.06.

**CVSG.** None is on the docket and none will issue. No federal party is involved, no federal statute or program is actually construed below, and the Solicitor General has nothing to be asked about. P(CVSG after prediction time) = 0.002.

**Which question presented, if any.** None of the questions as written is grantable. The petition asks whether "the United States Supreme Court and the federal government have the right to intervene" in a state medical-board revocation, framed through Article III and the Indian Commerce Clause; that is not a question the state courts decided, and the state judgment under review is a Court of Appeal dismissal for failure to procure the record, which the California Supreme Court declined to review. In the remote event of a grant the Court would have to reformulate the case entirely, and the record gives it nothing to reformulate toward.

**Summary disposition versus plenary review.** Plenary review of this petition is not a realistic outcome. Conditional on a grant of any kind, the far likelier route is a disposition in the cert order itself (a GVR) rather than briefing and argument, so I put P(summary route | grant) = 0.6. No intervening decision of this Court bears on the petition, which is why the unconditional chance of a GVR is itself negligible.

**Dissent from denial.** None. No Justice has signaled interest in state medical-licensing discipline or alternative-medicine practice, and the petition presents no vehicle for a statement. P(some Justice writes respecting or dissenting from denial | denial) = 0.01.

**Merits, conditionally.** If the Court somehow granted, the only ground consistent with its practice would be a remand on a procedural point about the state appellate default, not any ruling on the Commerce Clause theory the petition advances.
