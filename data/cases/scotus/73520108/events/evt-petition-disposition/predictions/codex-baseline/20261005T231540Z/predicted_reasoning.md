# Forecast

The Court will most likely deny certiorari in Goldman Sachs Bank USA v. Brown. The probability of any grant, including a summary disposition, is 38%. The likely disposition is an unexplained denial, not a merits ruling endorsing either side's arbitration position.

The snapshot records two conference distributions: September 9 for September 28, 2026, and October 5 for October 9, 2026. Thus one apparent relist has already occurred. From that two-distribution state, I predict no further distribution before disposition, with a substantial 45% probability of at least one more. In the further-distribution branch, one or two additional distributions are more likely than a prolonged series. My central timing forecast is resolution during October or November 2026, rather than a long hold.

The Court will not call for the Solicitor General's views; the probability of a new CVSG is 6%. This is principally a private dispute over the interaction of two federal statutes, and the competing forum arguments are developed in the parties' filings.

The main reason to pass on this vehicle will be the combination of the contested circuit conflict and the unstable underlying pleading. The bankruptcy court's September 10 dismissal without prejudice, reported in both September supplemental filings, leaves the damages allegations and prospective Section 105(a) theory unsettled. The arbitration issue may survive amendment, but the Court can decline this petition without resolving that issue.

If the Court grants review, it will most likely take the petition's single question substantially as presented: whether, and under what circumstances, a bankruptcy court may refuse to enforce an otherwise valid arbitration agreement covering Section 362(k) claims. Any narrowing would focus on whether a categorical exclusion is permissible, while leaving the arbitrability of a standalone Section 105(a) contempt proceeding outside the question.

Conditional on a grant, I assign 8% to disposition in the cert order itself and 92% to plenary review. The principal grant scenario calls for briefing and argument about bankruptcy enforcement and the FAA, not a GVR tied to an identified intervening Supreme Court ruling. A summary route is possible but not the central forecast. If plenary review occurs, the likely ground is rejection of a categorical bankruptcy exception for these damages claims, rather than a ruling that all bankruptcy contempt proceedings must be arbitrated.

Conditional on denial, I assign 15% to a noted dissent or statement respecting denial. The forecast is no separate writing; I do not predict individual cert votes or an author.
