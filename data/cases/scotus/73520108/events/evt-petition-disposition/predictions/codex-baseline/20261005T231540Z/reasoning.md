## Why 38%, above the anchor but below an even chance

The upward case is substantial. The petition presents a focused federal question and an asserted direct conflict with MBNA America Bank v. Hill. Judge King's dissent independently calls out the conflict and argues that arbitrating these damages claims need not disrupt bankruptcy administration. The petition invokes the FAA framework in McMahon and Epic Systems to attack a categorical rule. This is more than a request to correct an isolated factual mistake. Validity of the parties' arbitration agreements is not the central dispute. The American Bankers Association filing indicates organized industry interest, although I infer nothing about its unexamined arguments. Sources: QP; petition pp. 2-4, 12-20, 34-35 and appendix pp. 23a-28a; snapshot's July 23 entry.

The downward case is also concrete. Respondents distinguish Hill's completed Chapter 7 estate from Brown's continuing Chapter 13 proceeding, emphasize overlapping Section 105(a) remedies, and assert that Goldman conceded discretion below. These are respondents' contested objections, not findings I have independently verified. The petition's two-plaintiff posture does include a discharged Chapter 7 debtor, which keeps the split argument meaningful, but the panel did not separately resolve the claims on that basis. Respondents also point to earlier denials involving discharge-injunction arbitration; those denials are cautionary context, not substantive precedent against this petition. Sources: BIO pp. 4, 11-20; petition pp. 34-35 and appendix pp. 23a-24a.

The September supplements matter most at the margin. Respondents report dismissal for inadequately pleaded actual damages, with leave to amend, and plan to make Section 105(a) an explicit independent ground. Goldman agrees that dismissal occurred but argues that the surviving forum rule will still govern renewed Section 362(k) claims. I credit that response enough not to treat dismissal as established mootness. Nevertheless, unsettled pleadings and the overlap between statutory damages and contempt make this a less clean vehicle than the petition portrays. Sources: respondents' supplemental brief pp. 1-2; petitioner's supplemental response pp. 1-4. These are the parties' reports of the lower-court ruling; I did not independently retrieve that order.

Starting from 17.2%, the focused split, reasoned dissent, and nationwide arbitration issue justify a large upward adjustment. Vehicle objections and the intervening pleading dismissal then materially temper it. The result, 38%, is judgmental rather than the output of a fitted model. The observed second distribution is already represented by the elevated anchor, so I do not add a separate generic relist bonus. Denial remains the most likely single disposition.

## Other elicited probabilities and stakes

The 45% further-distribution probability reflects active consideration and a potentially consequential vehicle dispute, but is not estimated from terminal bucket frequencies. A new CVSG is only 6% because the materials present a developed private statutory dispute rather than an evident need for the federal government's operational views. The 8% summary-route probability is conditional on a grant: no retrieved source identifies an intervening controlling Supreme Court decision supplying an obvious GVR, and the contested bankruptcy distinctions favor plenary review if accepted. The 15% denial-writing probability allows for visible interest in the arbitration issue without predicting a public dissent as the default. These auxiliary estimates are subjective and their uncertainty is higher than the numerical precision implies.

The stakes score is 0.46, independent of the grant estimate. A ruling could affect consumer-credit arbitration and collective remedies for automatic-stay violations across bankruptcy courts, while remaining a specialized forum-allocation question. The proposed nationwide class and competing statutes justify a moderate score, not a claim of general public salience inferred from later coverage.
