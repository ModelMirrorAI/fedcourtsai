Distributed twice so far (one relist); expect a roughly 30% chance of at least one further relist before a decision on the petition. The issue—whether bankruptcy courts can refuse to enforce arbitration agreements for automatic stay violations—is significant for consumer lenders, and the Court may be carefully reviewing the Fourth Circuit's opinion against the FAA's strong mandate. No CVSG is expected, as this is primarily a private dispute between a bank and consumers, and the federal government is not a party.

If granted, the Court would likely take the question presented as written to resolve the tension between the Bankruptcy Code and the FAA. The Court would likely reverse the Fourth Circuit, finding no "clear and manifest" congressional intent in the Bankruptcy Code to displace the FAA for § 362(k) claims. A summary disposition route (e.g., GVR) is unlikely, as there is no recent intervening Supreme Court decision directly on point; this would proceed to plenary review. 

Should the Court deny certiorari, there is a reasonable possibility (~25%) of a dissent from denial (likely authored by Justice Thomas or Justice Alito) emphasizing the primacy of the FAA and criticizing the Fourth Circuit for creating a judge-made exception in the bankruptcy context.
