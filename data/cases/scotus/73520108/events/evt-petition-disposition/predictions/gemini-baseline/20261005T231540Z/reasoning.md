P(grant) 0.35. The case involves an intersection of the Bankruptcy Code's automatic stay provisions (11 U.S.C. § 362) and the Federal Arbitration Act (FAA). The Fourth Circuit declined to compel arbitration, holding that arbitration would inherently conflict with the centralized enforcement and breathing spell of the automatic stay. Petitioner Goldman Sachs relies on a circuit split with the Second Circuit's decision in *MBNA America Bank, N.A. v. Hill*. 

The prior-Term salience band for a once-relisted (two distributions) paid petition in the `elevated` band is around 17-19%. I adjust this base rate significantly upward to 0.35 for several reasons. First, the FAA is a favored statute for this Court, which has consistently granted cert to reverse lower courts refusing to enforce arbitration agreements (*Epic Systems*, etc.). Second, the petitioner is represented by Latham & Watkins, a repeat player at the Supreme Court, and the petition drew an amicus brief from the American Bankers Association at the cert stage, signaling industry importance. 

However, there are reasons for caution that prevent P(grant) from being higher. As respondents note in their BIO, the circuit split with *Hill* is distinguishable: *Hill* involved a closed bankruptcy case where the debtor had already received a discharge, whereas Ms. Brown's Chapter 13 case is ongoing. Additionally, the Second Circuit has recently retreated somewhat from *Hill* in *Anderson* and *Belton* (cases involving the discharge injunction under § 105(a), where the Court denied cert). 

With one relist already recorded on the docket, the hazard for a grant or further relists is elevated. Given the conservative supermajority's general skepticism toward judicially created exceptions to the FAA, this case presents an attractive vehicle, despite the vehicle arguments raised in the BIO.
