# Forecast: what the Court will do with No. 25-1408

**Disposition.** The petition is more likely denied than granted, but it is a
serious candidate: P(any grant) 0.30. If the Court grants, it will be a plenary
grant on the single question presented, set for argument; a summary route is
not in play.

**Relists.** The docket shows two distributions: the first for the September 28
long conference, then a relist for the October 9 conference. So this petition
has already been relisted once. I expect the matter to be resolved at or just
after the October 9 conference, with a 0.42 chance of at least one further
distribution. The likeliest shape of a further relist is one or two more
conferences while a Justice decides whether to write, or a redistribution after
a call for the Solicitor General's views.

**CVSG.** No CVSG is on the docket. The United States is not a party, but the
question sits at the seam of two federal statutes the government administers
through the U.S. Trustee Program, and the Court has invited the SG on
bankruptcy-procedure questions before. I put the chance of a CVSG from here at
0.12. If it happens, it comes out of the October 9 conference or the one
after, and the petition would then sit until spring.

**Which question, if granted.** The Court would take the petition's question
essentially as written, trimmed to the clause that resolves the case: whether a
bankruptcy court may refuse to enforce a concededly valid arbitration agreement
as to a debtor's Section 362(k) damages claim. It would not reach the
respondents' reframing around Section 105(a) contempt power, which the
petitioner has conceded away and which no circuit disputes. The core/non-core
methodological split is background, not a separately granted question.

**Summary disposition.** Conditional on a grant, P(cert-order disposition) is
0.04. No intervening decision of this Court bears on the split, so there is
nothing to GVR in light of, and the Fourth Circuit's published, reasoned,
divided opinion is not a summary-reversal shape.

**If granted, the likely ground.** Conditional on a grant, the Court would most
likely reverse, holding under *McMahon* and *Epic Systems* that the Bankruptcy
Code's silence on arbitration, plus generalized purposes of centralization and
uniformity, do not amount to a "clear and manifest" command displacing the FAA
for a private damages claim that does not affect administration of the estate;
it might reserve the open-Chapter-13 debtor for remand.

**Dissent from denial.** Conditional on a denial, P(some Justice writes) is
0.22. The relist after the long conference, immediately after the parties'
supplemental briefs about the September 10 dismissal, is consistent with the
Court studying a vehicle problem rather than with a Justice drafting. If anyone
writes, it would be a short statement respecting denial noting that the
question is open and the vehicle imperfect, from a Justice who regularly polices
FAA displacement arguments.
