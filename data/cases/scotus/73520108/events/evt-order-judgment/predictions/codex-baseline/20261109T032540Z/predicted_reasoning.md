# Forecast of the Court's judgment and reasoning

## Judgment and lineup

The Court will **reverse** the Fourth Circuit's judgment and remand for further proceedings consistent with enforcing the arbitration agreements as to the private Section 362(k) damages claims. The probability that the judgment below is disturbed in any way is **82%**; reversal is the most likely exact judgment, with a narrower vacatur and remand the principal alternative.

The predicted split is **6–3**. Roberts, Thomas, Alito, Gorsuch, Kavanaugh, and Barrett will be in the majority; Sotomayor, Kagan, and Jackson will dissent. Gorsuch is the most likely individual author, though the assignment is substantially less predictable than the result. Sotomayor is the most likely dissent author, with Jackson the strongest alternative. Additional separate writing is possible but is not the modal expectation.

## Majority ground

The majority will hold that a private damages claim for a willful automatic-stay violation is not exempt from the FAA simply because it arises under the Bankruptcy Code or is classified as core. It will apply the demanding congressional-intent inquiry associated with McMahon and Epic Systems, rather than allowing bankruptcy courts to balance general statutory policies against arbitration.

Centralization, expertise, uniform outcomes, and public deterrence will not establish the necessary conflict for these claims. A damages award against a creditor can vindicate the statutory right without handing an arbitrator control over estate administration or permission to disregard the automatic stay. Maze's closed bankruptcy and Brown's confirmed plan will illustrate the gap between adjudicating a private recovery and deciding how the estate should be collectively administered. The Court will not deem Brown's bankruptcy closed merely because her plan has been confirmed.

The majority will distinguish the debtor's statutory cause of action from the bankruptcy court's own contempt-enforcement authority. The latter remains available notwithstanding an agreement between private parties. Potential duplication between retained contempt matters and arbitrated damages claims will not by itself justify overriding the FAA. The Court will not decide whether Goldman actually violated the stay, whether actual damages were adequately pleaded, or what damages either respondent should receive.

## Breadth

The opinion will state a generally applicable arbitrability rule for private Section 362(k) damages claims, reaching beyond discharged Chapter 7 debtors. It will not limit the rule to Maze while endorsing the Fourth Circuit's policy analysis for Brown. At the same time, it will reserve the treatment of Section 105(a) contempt proceedings and avoid announcing that every core bankruptcy proceeding must be arbitrated. It need not overrule McMahon to reject the inherent-conflict reasoning used below.

## Dissent and procedural alternatives

The dissent will argue that automatic-stay enforcement belongs to an integrated judicial process protecting both debtors and the estate. Separating contempt from the supplementary statutory remedy will, in its view, weaken that process and create needless parallel litigation. Brown's continuing Chapter 13 case will be the strongest factual support for retaining bankruptcy-court control.

The outstanding pleading and preservation disputes will not prevent a merits decision in the modal forecast. If they do constrain the Court, a vacatur directing debtor-specific analysis or treatment of the amended claims is more likely than outright affirmance. Dismissal as improvidently granted remains a smaller live alternative because the original complaint was reportedly dismissed without prejudice and the parties disagree over Section 105(a) and waiver. A DIG would leave the judgment below undisturbed, unlike the forecast reversal or an alternative vacatur.

I expect ordinary merits briefing, argument, and a signed opinion during the remainder of OT2026, most plausibly by June 2027. That timing is a forecast, not a claim about an already-set argument date. Cert-stage relists, another grant, and a CVSG are not the unresolved events here.
