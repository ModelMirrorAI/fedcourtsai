# Forecast: Goldman Sachs Bank USA v. Brown, No. 25-1408 (merits, from the grant)

**Judgment: reversed.** P(the Fourth Circuit's judgment is disturbed) = 0.79. The Court will hold that the
bankruptcy court had to compel arbitration of the debtors' Section 362(k) automatic-stay damages claim, and
will reverse the Fourth Circuit's affirmance of the order denying Goldman's motion to compel. Reversal is
likelier than vacatur because the question is a pure question of law and the answer resolves the motion.
A vacate-and-remand stays plausible (perhaps one in six) if the Court announces a standard and sends back the
Section 105(a) dispute for the lower courts.

**Vote: 7-2**, with Justices Sotomayor and Jackson in dissent. Justice Kagan is the marginal vote: she has
joined broad FAA-enforcement holdings before (she wrote Viking River), but she might side with the bankruptcy
court's control over its own stay. A 6-3 split along those lines is the main alternative, and a unanimous
narrow reversal is possible.

**Ground.** The Court takes the question presented, narrowed to Section 362(k) claims. It applies Epic
Systems and CompuCredit: the FAA yields only to a clear and manifest congressional command found in the text.
Neither Section 362(k), which simply creates a damages cause of action, nor the "core proceeding" label in
28 U.S.C. 157 contains such a command. The Fourth Circuit's search for an "inherent conflict" with the Code's
purposes (the strand of McMahon that several circuits use in bankruptcy) is rejected or confined to the text.
Respondents' Section 105(a) contempt theory is left open. The Court will treat it as not properly presented,
in part because of how the claims were pleaded. It may say a bankruptcy court's own contempt power over its
stay is a different question.

**Procedural exits.** A dismissal as improvidently granted (DIG) is live but unlikely, about 5%. The
bankruptcy court dismissed the complaint for failure to plead actual damages, with leave to amend (respondents'
supplemental brief). The Court granted over that objection after four distributions, so it knows about the
vehicle issue. The motion to compel stays live while the debtors are amending. No recusal is expected.

**Who writes.** The majority opinion is most likely from Justice Kavanaugh (Henry Schein; arbitration and
financial-regulation cases), Justice Gorsuch (Epic Systems), or Justice Barrett. Assignments will depend on
the sitting, probably February or March 2027. Expect a dissent from Justice Jackson or Justice Sotomayor
(the two may write separately). There may also be a concurrence from Justice Thomas, who has long argued
the FAA does not apply in some settings, or from Justice Gorsuch on the limits of McMahon's
"inherent conflict" test.
