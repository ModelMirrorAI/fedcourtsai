## In brief

A bank asks the Court to decide whether consumer bankruptcy debtors' claims for money damages over debt-collection calls must go to private arbitration instead of bankruptcy court.

## What happened

Rhea Ann Brown and Gregory Kevin Maze filed for bankruptcy, which triggers an automatic halt on debt collection. They say Goldman Sachs Bank, which operated their Apple Card accounts, kept demanding payment anyway, even after being told it was violating that halt. They sued Goldman in bankruptcy court for damages and other relief. Goldman asked the court to send the dispute to arbitration under the cardholder agreement. The bankruptcy court refused, and the district court and the Fourth Circuit Court of Appeals agreed, with one judge dissenting. Goldman then asked the Supreme Court to review that decision.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked whether a bankruptcy court can refuse to enforce an arbitration agreement and keep a debtor's damages claim for a stay violation in bankruptcy court, or whether federal arbitration law requires sending that claim to a private arbitrator instead. Goldman argues bankruptcy law does not displace the duty to arbitrate; Brown and Maze argue it does, because enforcing the bankruptcy court's own orders cannot be handed to a private arbitrator.

## Where it stands

The petition has been distributed for conference more than once, most recently for October 16, 2026, and the docket does not yet show a decision on whether the Court will hear the case.

## What each outcome would mean

Goldman, which lost in the lower courts, is asking the Court to take up the case and reverse. If the Court denies review, the Fourth Circuit's decision stands, and Brown and Maze's damages claims stay in bankruptcy court rather than being sent to arbitration. If the Court grants review, it would hear full arguments and decide the arbitration question itself, which would not by itself determine who wins. A later decision affirming would leave the claims in bankruptcy court; reversing would require arbitration instead; vacating would send the case back without deciding the issue for either side.
