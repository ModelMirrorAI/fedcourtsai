# Forecast — No. 26-88, Alaska Policy Forum v. Alaska Public Offices Commission

The docket shows one distribution (for the Sept. 28, 2026 conference), overtaken by the Court's call for a response on Aug. 31. The respondents' briefs in opposition will come in around the extended Nov. 30, 2026 deadline (a further short extension is possible), the petitioner will reply, and the petition will be distributed again for a conference in January 2027. It will therefore be distributed at least once more; I expect one further distribution and most likely a denial on the following order list (January–February 2027). A relist after that first post-response conference would be the sign this forecast is wrong; I put roughly one chance in four on at least one true relist.

The Court will not call for the views of the Solicitor General: the case concerns state campaign-finance regulation of ballot-measure speech, with no federal statute or programme directly involved.

The most likely outcome is denial of certiorari (about 84%). If the Court grants, it would most likely take a narrowed case: question 1 (whether the informational interest supports donor disclosure for issue speech that does not mention the election), possibly with question 3 (on-communication top-donor disclaimers), and leave behind question 2's vagueness challenge to Alaska's Furgatch-derived test. A grant would be plenary; a summary disposition in the cert order is unlikely (about 8% of grants), since no intervening decision invites a GVR, though a per curiam reversal for failure to apply WRTL II is not impossible.

If the Court denies, expect no separate writing most likely; there is about a one-in-ten chance of a noted dissent or statement, with Justice Thomas the likeliest author, followed by Justices Alito and Gorsuch.

If granted and decided, the likely ground would be that exacting scrutiny under AFP v. Bonta is not satisfied where disclosure is triggered by issue speech that is not the functional equivalent of express advocacy for or against the measure, with the judgment below reversed.
