# Forecast of the Court's action

The Court will most likely deny the petition after obtaining the requested response and redistributing it. The baseline shows **one distribution and no completed relist**, with the September 30 response deadline falling after the originally listed September 28 conference. I expect **one additional distribution** to be the modal path from this state. Further post-conference relists are possible, particularly if the Court seriously considers review, but a long series is not my central forecast. The probability of any additional distribution is 0.98; that mostly procedural increment is not a claim of sustained merits consideration.

The Court will probably not call for the Solicitor General's views. I assign 0.025 to a new CVSG. The principal dispute is over state disclosure requirements and First Amendment standards rather than the administration of a federal program. If an invitation issues, it will most likely follow the requested response and renewed consideration rather than precede that response.

If review is granted, the most likely focus is **questions 1 and 2 in a combined or narrowed formulation**: what objective standard permits a state to treat policy discussion as ballot-measure advocacy and compel donor disclosure, and whether Alaska's contextual test gives adequate notice. A grant could additionally reach question 3's donor identification on the face of communications, but taking all three questions is not necessary to decide the threshold issue. Conditional on a merits ruling favorable to APF, an objective speech-classification or tailoring ground is more likely than a categorical prohibition on all ballot-measure disclosure.

Conditional on any grant, I assign **0.90 to plenary review** and **0.10 to disposition in the cert order**, including GVR or summary reversal. No identified intervening decision makes a hold-and-GVR the central route, and the interaction between election disclosure, issue advocacy, and donor privacy favors full briefing if the Court intervenes.

On the predicted denial, the modal outcome is a routine order without explanation. I assign **0.14, conditional on denial**, to a noted dissent or statement respecting denial. If there is a separate opinion, Thomas is the leading possible writer, followed by Gorsuch or Alito; I do not predict that any of them will in fact write. A denial will leave the Alaska judgment in place without endorsing its reasoning.
