# Predicted Court Reasoning & Procedural Trajectory

## Procedural Trajectory from Current Posture

The docket currently records **one conference distribution** (August 26, 2026, for the September 28, 2026 conference), followed by the Court's August 31, 2026 **call for a response** after all three respondents waived.

1. **Redistribution and Further Relists (`relist-increment`):** Once the requested Brief in Opposition is filed (after any routine extension of time) and petitioner files its cert reply, the petition will be distributed a second time for conference (`P = 0.98`, failing only in the unlikely event of a pre-distribution Rule 46 dismissal). Beyond that mandatory post-response distribution, there is a moderate chance (~35%) of one or two additional conference relists while chambers examine the vehicle record on Question Presented 1 or consider a separate writing, before the Court denies certiorari.
2. **No CVSG (`cvsg-increment`):** The Court will not call for the views of the Solicitor General (`P = 0.01`). The dispute concerns a state administrative agency's enforcement of Alaska's pre-2021 ballot-measure campaign-finance statutes under the First Amendment, implicating no federal statute, federal regulation, or federal enforcement program.
3. **Disposition (`disposition`):** The Court will **deny certiorari** (`P(grant) = 0.11`, `P(denied) = 0.89`). Once the opposition places the full statutory and procedural context before the conference—specifically that the challenged 2020 versions of AS §§ 15.13.040, 15.13.090, and 15.13.110 were superseded in February 2021 by Ballot Measure 2, that no monetary penalty was assessed, that the Alaska Supreme Court authoritatively construed pre-2021 Alaska law to include an earmarking limit (`AS 15.13.040(e)(5)` / `AS 15.13.090(a)(2)(C)`), that `AS 15.13.400(3)` already exempted nongroup communications costing $500 or less, and that *Citizens United v. FEC*, 558 U.S. 310, 368–69 (2010), rejected confining disclosure laws to *WRTL II*'s functional equivalent of express advocacy—fewer than four Justices will vote to grant review.

## Conditional Grant Scope & Route

- **Likely Question Taken if Granted (context-only):** If the Court grants certiorari, it will limit review to **Question Presented 1**—whether a state may classify a 501(c)(3) organization's general policy publications that do not mention a ballot measure as regulable election advocacy using contextual intent-and-effect factors derived from *FEC v. Furgatch*, 807 F.2d 857 (9th Cir. 1987), rather than an objective text-based standard under *FEC v. Wisconsin Right to Life, Inc.* (*WRTL II*), 551 U.S. 449 (2007)—and leave Questions 2 and 3 behind due to state-law earmarking entanglements and the Court's recent denials on donor-earmarking and on-ad disclaimer challenges. If granted on Question 1, the likely merits ground would cabin contextual speaker-specific factors when state disclosure triggers carry vague "purpose of influencing" language applied to non-broadcast policy commentary.
- **Plenary Review Over Summary Disposition (`summary-disposition-route`):** Conditional on a grant, the Court will set the case for full merits briefing and oral argument rather than disposing of it summarily in the cert order (`P(summary-disposition-route | grant) = 0.08`). No intervening Supreme Court campaign-finance precedent warrants a GVR, and drawing a constitutional boundary between pure issue discussion and regulable ballot-measure advocacy after *Citizens United* Part IV is unsuitable for summary reversal.

## Separate Writings on Denial (`dissent-from-denial` & `order-writers`)

Conditional on a denial of certiorari, the modal outcome (~91%) is an unsigned order denying the petition without recorded dissent or separate statement. There is an ~9% conditional probability (`dissent-from-denial = 0.09`) that a Justice—most plausibly **Justice Thomas** (potentially joined by or alongside **Justice Alito** or **Justice Gorsuch**)—authors a dissent from or statement respecting the denial highlighting the First Amendment chill of applying *Furgatch*'s contextual standard to a 501(c)(3) research organization's policy commentary while noting or setting aside the superseded state statutory posture.
