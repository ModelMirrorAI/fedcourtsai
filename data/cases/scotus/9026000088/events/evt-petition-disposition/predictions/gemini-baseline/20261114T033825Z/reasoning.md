## 3. Upward vs. Downward Adjustments (`0.0517` -> ~`0.25` -> `0.11`)

### Why I adjust substantially upward from the 5.17% `baseline` anchor
The `sal-v8` `baseline` tier does not condition on cert-stage amicus count or a post-waiver Call for Response. Sixteen cert-stage amicus briefs is top-percentile institutional mobilization on the paid cert docket, and the August 31 `Response Requested` confirms that at least one chambers flagged the petition rather than letting it die on waivers at the Long Conference. Moreover, unlike interlocutory preliminary-injunction appeals (*No on E*, *Smith v. Helzer*), this petition arrives from a final judgment of a state court of last resort arising out of an actual enforcement adjudication, with a rhetorically vivid hook: APOC dismissed the complaint against the national coalition (Protect My Ballot) that produced the ranked-choice-voting video while finding petitioner in violation for spending $643.20 in staff time to email press releases, publish policy commentary, and embed the video without naming "Ballot Measure 2."

### Why I discount heavily back down to `0.11`
Two powerful considerations cut roughly in half (and more) the raw structural lift from amici and the CFR:

1. **Unbroken string of post-*AFPF* campaign-finance disclosure cert denials through October 2026:**
   Corpus and Supreme Court docket priors show that a coordinated First Amendment campaign asking the Court to extend *Americans for Prosperity Foundation v. Bonta*, 594 U.S. 595 (2021), and *NIFLA*/*Reed* to invalidate state or municipal ballot-measure donor reporting, low/non-earmarked thresholds, and on-communication top-donor disclaimers has gone **0-for-5** at the cert/emergency stage, with **zero noted dissents**:
   - *Rio Grande Foundation v. Oliver*, No. 25-1248 (`scotus/73292884`, **denied October 5, 2026**): Just five weeks before this snapshot, at the OT2026 Long Conference, the Court denied a 501(c)(3) state policy think tank's challenge to New Mexico's ballot-measure independent-expenditure donor-disclosure law—despite Judge Eid's Tenth Circuit dissent on donor earmarking, overlapping amici (AFPF, Manhattan Institute, People United for Privacy, Advancing American Freedom, Randy Elf), a state waiver followed by a **May 26, 2026 `Response Requested`**, and two distributions (`distribution_count = 2`).
   - *Americans for Prosperity Foundation v. Albence*, No. 26A349 (`scotus/9526000349`, **denied October 1, 2026**): Full-Court denial of emergency relief in a Third Circuit challenge to Delaware's non-earmarked issue-advocacy donor-disclosure regime.
   - *Smith v. Stillie* (below: *Smith v. Helzer*, 95 F.4th 1207 (9th Cir. 2024)), No. 23-1316 (`scotus/72479625`, **denied November 18, 2024**): Direct First Amendment challenge to **Alaska's own** Ballot Measure 2 donor-disclosure and on-communication top-donor disclaimer statutes; relisted **five times** (`distribution_count = 6`) and still failed to draw four votes or a single noted dissent.
   - *No on E v. Chiu*, 85 F.4th 493 (9th Cir. 2023), No. 23-926 (`scotus/72483395`, **denied October 7, 2024**): Challenge to San Francisco's on-ad top-donor/secondary-donor disclaimers backed by **nine dissenting Ninth Circuit judges** and eight amicus briefs; denied without noted dissent.
   - *Gaspee Project v. Mederos*, 13 F.4th 79 (1st Cir. 2021), *cert. denied*, 142 S. Ct. 2647 (2022).

   Doctrinally, *Citizens United v. FEC*, 558 U.S. 310, 366–71 (2010) (8–1 on Part IV), expressly rejected confining campaign-finance disclosure and disclaimers to *WRTL II*'s functional-equivalent test, and the lower appellate courts are aligned rather than split on upholding top-donor on-communication disclaimers (*Gaspee*, *No on E*, *Smith v. Helzer*, and the Alaska Supreme Court here). Even *Wyoming Gun Owners v. Gray*, 83 F.4th 1224 (10th Cir. 2023), turned on an as-applied lack of statutory earmarking and vague "related to" text (and the Tenth Circuit itself subsequently upheld New Mexico's disclosure law in *Rio Grande Foundation*, which the Court just declined to review).

2. **Severe vehicle defects that respondents' November 30, 2026 BIOs will spotlight:**
   Because all three respondents waived in August, the petition has not yet been tested by an opposition brief. Inspection of the petition's own statutory and opinion appendices reveals four acute vehicle flaws:
   - **Binding state-law earmarking construction:** Petitioner's headline claim is that Alaska forces a 501(c)(3) to unmask its donors from the first dollar and print its top three donors on its communications. Yet as the petition concedes (Pet. 29) and the statutory appendix confirms (App. 4a, 6a, 219a–220a), the Alaska Supreme Court construed AS 15.13.040(e)(5) and AS 15.13.090(a)(2)(C) as containing an **earmarking limitation**: reportable "contributors" (including the top three contributors, *"if any,"* on a communication disclaimer) include *only* donors who contributed *"for the purpose of influencing"* an election or ballot proposition, and AS 15.13.090(c) expressly provides that *"the second statement [listing top contributors] is not required if the person paying for the communication has no contributors."* Under that authoritative state-law construction, general-support 501(c)(3) donors who did not give to influence Ballot Measure 2 do not have to be reported or listed on any disclaimer at all.
   - **Fact-bound election references:** While QP 1 posits a speaker who *"does not discuss the election,"* the Alaska Supreme Court opinion quotes petitioner's July 24, 2020 Alaska press release opening with *"As Alaskans take to the polls in November, history should provide a warning for what Ranked Choice Voting would lead to"* (App. 8a) and its July 31 embedded video ending with signs reading *"SAY NO TO RANKED CHOICE VOTING"* (App. 9a) after Ballot Measure 2 was already on the November 2020 ballot.
   - **Superseded pre-2021 statutory text:** As Footnote 5 of the Alaska Supreme Court opinion notes (App. 4a n.5) and Appendix F reflects (`[Effective until February 28, 2021]`), this proceeding enforced the *2020* pre-Measure 2 version of AS 15.13 (though petitioner attempts at Pet. 30–31 to argue against post-2021 Measure 2 provisions not applied to it).
   - **Waived penalty and $500 communication floor:** APOC waived all civil penalties (`$0` assessed on `$643.20` of cumulative staff time, App. 13a–14a), and AS 15.13.400(3) already exempts nongroup communications costing $500 or less from on-communication disclaimers (App. 5a, 230a).

Weighing the 16-brief amicus coalition, final state high court judgment, and active CFR against the 0-for-5 post-*AFPF* cert-denial record (especially *Rio Grande Foundation* on October 5, 2026 and *Smith v. Stillie* on Alaska's statute) and the binding state-law earmarking construction yields **`probability = 0.11`**.
