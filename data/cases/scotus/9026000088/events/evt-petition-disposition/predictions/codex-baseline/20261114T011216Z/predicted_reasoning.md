# Forecast of the Court's action

The Court will most likely **deny certiorari without an explanatory opinion**. Any grant has a 26% probability. The substantive obstacle to review will be that the petition's proposed limits on disclosure require additional doctrinal development, while its asserted conflicts involve different statutory schemes and factual burdens rather than a uniformly clean split.

## Proceedings from the present snapshot

The docket currently records **one distribution**, for the September 28, 2026 conference. The Court requested a response before that conference, and the response is now due November 30. The Court will almost certainly distribute the petition again after the requested response and the ensuing reply opportunity: **99% probability of at least one additional distribution**. This is not a prediction that the Court has already substantively relisted the case.

The most likely path is one further distribution for a conference after briefing is complete, followed by denial. If respondents use the current deadline, that places the next consideration around late December 2026 or January 2027, subject to the Court's scheduling. A grant path is more likely to involve one or two additional substantive relists after redistribution. The central prediction is therefore **one further distribution in total**, with a meaningful tail of two or three.

The Court will **not call for the Solicitor General's views**; the probability of a new invitation is 2%. A request for federal executive input is less natural here than deciding whether to review the constitutional disclosure issues directly.

## Conditional grant forecast

If review is granted, the Court is most likely to take **questions 1 and 2 together in a narrowed formulation**: whether a state may apply campaign registration and donor-disclosure requirements to issue communications that do not mention an election or ballot measure, using contextual factors to classify them as election advocacy. The Court would not need to accept the petition's characterization that every disputed communication is necessarily pure issue speech. Question 3, concerning donor names on communications, is a plausible additional or alternative grant, but the central forecast is review focused on the trigger for regulation rather than every asserted defect in the disclosure system.

**Plenary briefing and argument will be the likelier grant route: 88% conditional on any grant.** The remaining 12% covers grant-order dispositions. A mechanical GVR is not the central alternative: the record identifies no controlling companion decision that would obviate the need to resolve the competing standards. If the Court reaches the merits, the likeliest basis for relief will be a narrower constitutional boundary for classifying issue discussion or a more demanding tailoring requirement, not wholesale elimination of election-related disclosure.

## Writings

If review is denied, the Court will most likely issue no separate writing; the probability of some noted dissent or statement respecting denial is **14% conditional on denial**. If a Justice does write, Thomas is the leading candidate, with Alito and Gorsuch secondary possibilities. This is a forecast about possible public writings, not an inference about unrecorded votes.
