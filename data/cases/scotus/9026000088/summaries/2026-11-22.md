## In brief

A nonprofit asks the Court to strike down Alaska's campaign-finance rules requiring it to register, report donors, and disclose funders after discussing ranked-choice voting before a related ballot measure.

## What happened

Alaska Policy Forum, a nonprofit, published articles, a press release, and a video criticizing ranked-choice voting before that topic became part of a 2020 ballot measure. Alaska's campaign finance agency ruled the Forum's materials counted as advocacy against the measure and required it to register, report its spending, and disclose its donors on its communications, while clearing a partner organization that made similar statements. Alaska courts upheld the agency, and the Alaska Supreme Court affirmed, rejecting the Forum's arguments that the rules were vague and violated the First Amendment.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked whether the government's interest in informing voters justifies requiring a group to disclose its donors when it speaks about a public issue connected to an election but never mentions the election itself; whether the legal test Alaska uses to separate ordinary issue discussion from regulated election advocacy is too unclear for speakers to follow; and whether requiring donor names to appear directly on a group's own communications serves a strong enough government interest to survive First Amendment scrutiny.

## Where it stands

The petition was distributed for conference on September 28, 2026, and the Court then asked the respondents to respond; that response deadline has since been extended twice, most recently to December 28, 2026. No conference has yet acted on the petition.

## What each outcome would mean

Alaska Policy Forum, the petitioner, is asking the Court to overturn the Alaska Supreme Court's ruling that upheld the disclosure, registration, and reporting requirements. If the Court grants review, it would hear full arguments and decide the First Amendment questions itself, deciding nothing yet about who wins. If it denies review, the Alaska Supreme Court's decision stands, leaving the Forum subject to the state's registration, reporting, and donor-disclosure requirements as applied to its ranked-choice-voting materials.
