## In brief

Alaska Policy Forum asks the Court to overturn a state ruling that its ranked-choice-voting commentary counted as ballot-measure advocacy requiring donor disclosure and registration.

## What happened

Alaska Policy Forum, a nonprofit, published articles, a press release, and a video criticizing ranked-choice voting before it became part of a 2020 ballot measure. A rival group complained to the Alaska Public Offices Commission, which found the Forum had engaged in advocacy requiring it to register, report its spending, and name its donors on its materials, while clearing an allied group that had produced similar content. Alaska's courts, up through the state supreme court, upheld the Commission's decision and rejected the Forum's First Amendment challenges.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked whether government can treat commentary on a policy topic as election-related advocacy, triggering donor-disclosure and registration rules, even when the speaker never mentions the ballot measure or election; whether the legal test Alaska used to draw that line is too unclear for speakers to follow; and whether requiring donor names to appear directly on the speech itself serves a strong enough government interest.

## Where it stands

The petition was distributed for conference, after which the Court asked the respondents to respond; that response was later granted an extension to November 30, 2026. The docket shows no ruling yet on whether the Court will hear the case.

## What each outcome would mean

Alaska Policy Forum is asking the Court to take up the case and reverse the Alaska Supreme Court's decision, which upheld the disclosure and registration requirements against the Forum. If the Court grants review, it will hear full arguments and decide the questions itself, deciding nothing yet. If it denies review, the Alaska Supreme Court's ruling stands, leaving the Forum subject to the state's reporting, registration, and disclosure requirements; a denial would not mean the Court agreed with that outcome, only that the state ruling remains in place.
