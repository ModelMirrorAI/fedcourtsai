# Forecast: Hamp's Construction, LLC v. Telle, No. 26-464 (arrival moment)

**Disposition.** The Court will deny the petition. Expect the Solicitor General to waive the right to respond (response currently due November 6, 2026), the petition to be distributed once for a conference in late November or December 2026, and a one-line denial on the order list following that conference, most likely in December 2026 or early January 2027.

**Distributions (from a zero-distribution record).** The docket shows no distribution; this is the arrival moment. The petition will be distributed exactly once. It will not be relisted: nothing in it (no split, no unresolved federal question, no dissent below) gives any Justice a reason to hold it over. The only route to zero distributions is a dismissal under Rule 46 or for a filing defect, which is unlikely in a Contract Disputes Act appeal where the government has already won below.

**CVSG.** None. The Solicitor General is counsel of record for the respondent, the Acting Secretary of the Army, so a call for the views of the Solicitor General is structurally unavailable.

**Which question, if any.** If the Court took the case, it would have to reformulate: the three questions presented restate the Federal Circuit's application of settled Type I differing-site-condition doctrine (Renda Marine, H.B. Mac, P.J. Maffei) to this record. Two of the three rest on arguments the Federal Circuit held forfeited (the construction-traffic drawings and the absence-of-borings implied-representation theory), and the third asks the Court to re-weigh a Board finding reviewed for substantial evidence. There is no clean legal question to take as written.

**Summary disposition versus plenary review.** Conditional on any grant, a GVR is the likelier route rather than plenary review, because the only realistic path to a grant for a petition of this kind is a confession of error or an intervening decision bearing on Federal Circuit Type I doctrine, neither of which is on the horizon. A plenary grant on a fact-bound FAR 52.236-2 question with Federal Circuit exclusive jurisdiction is close to unprecedented.

**Dissent from denial.** None. No Justice will write a statement respecting or dissenting from the denial of a routine government-contracts petition that presents no constitutional, jurisdictional, or recurring-doctrine question.

**If granted (conditional).** The only plausible ground would be a narrow holding that the Federal Circuit misapplied de novo review by deferring to the Board's characterization of the record, and even that would most likely come as a GVR rather than an opinion.
