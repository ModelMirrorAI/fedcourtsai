## Case-Specific Adjustments from the Anchor (`0.0517` → `0.005`)

Starting from `cert_anchor.rate = 0.0517` (and `cert_amicus_line.rate = 0.0268`), several cumulative structural, doctrinal, and vehicle factors drive the grant probability sharply downward by roughly an order of magnitude to **0.005 (0.5%)**:

1. **Solicitor General waiver and procedural gate:** Respondent Acting Secretary of the Army waived a response on November 2, 2026, and the petition was distributed without opposition. Under Supreme Court practice, the Court virtually never grants plenary review or GVRs a federal agency victory without first calling for a response (CFR) from the Solicitor General. Thus, to be granted, this petition must clear both (a) a low-probability CFR hurdle at or before the December 4, 2026 conference and (b) plenary/summary grant scrutiny once the Solicitor General files a brief in opposition highlighting the record defects below.
2. **Complete absence of a circuit split or Supreme Court doctrinal hook:** Because the Federal Circuit holds exclusive jurisdiction over ASBCA appeals under 41 U.S.C. § 7107(a)(1) and 28 U.S.C. § 1295(a)(10) (save narrow TVA district-court jurisdiction under § 7107(a)(2), on which no conflict is alleged), a regional circuit split on FAR 52.236-2 is structurally rare and completely absent here. Strikingly, the petition's Table of Authorities cites **only three judicial decisions in total**—the opinion below and two older Federal Circuit cases (*Renda Marine, Inc. v. United States*, 509 F.3d 1372 (Fed. Cir. 2007), and *Randa/Madison Joint Venture III v. Dahlberg*, 239 F.3d 1264 (Fed. Cir. 2001))—citing zero Supreme Court precedents and zero conflicting appellate decisions. Moreover, Petitioner sought neither panel rehearing nor rehearing en banc in the Federal Circuit (`lower-court-docket.txt`), underscoring the absence of even an intra-circuit conflict.
3. **Misalignment between the Questions Presented and the holding below (pure factbound error correction):** The petition contends that the Federal Circuit converted FAR 52.236-2's Type I differing site condition inquiry into a categorical "geographic-no-representation" rule treating the absence of a boring at the exact site of a bank collapse as dispositive. Reading `decision-below.txt` (unanimous panel opinion by Judge Cunningham, joined by Judges Lourie and Reyna) refutes that framing. The panel explicitly reiterated settled Federal Circuit law that a contract indication need *not* be explicit or site-specific and that reliance on distant borings cannot be governed by a bright-line rule, depending instead on each site's geologic and topographic features. It affirmed because substantial evidence under 41 U.S.C. § 7107(b)(2)(C)—including Petitioner's own geotechnical expert acknowledging "markedly different" east- versus west-bank soil stratification and a slurry-filled void in west-bank boring WB-1, canal cross-sections showing steeper western bank drop-offs, and pre-bid inspection photographs indicating prior slope instability—supported the Board's finding that conditions on the southwest bank were visibly worse than elsewhere on the project.
4. **Severe preservation and record-vehicle flaws:** Even if four Justices wished to address Type I differing site conditions under FAR 52.236-2, this case is an exceptionally poor vehicle:
   - Questions 1 and 3 lean centrally on contract drawings allegedly depicting "Construction Traffic" on top of the southwest bank, yet the Federal Circuit held (slip op. at 11–12) that Petitioner **forfeited** that argument by failing to raise it before the Board *and* failed even to include those drawings in the Federal Circuit appellate appendix.
   - The panel likewise held (slip op. at 8 n.6) that Petitioner **forfeited** any argument that omitting southwest-bank borings created an implied representation.
   - Question 1's reliance on the solicitation's barge terms runs headlong into the undisputed record (slip op. at 12–13) that the Army Corps rescinded the original barge prohibition by formal amendment 18 days *before* Petitioner bid—warning bidders that work might require barges or floating platforms—and that Petitioner expressly disclaimed reliance on the pre-amendment prohibition before the Board.
5. **Counsel and external signals:** Petitioner is represented by regional Tennessee litigation counsel rather than repeat Supreme Court appellate specialists, drew zero amicus briefs, and has no companion or `Vide` dockets.

I stop at `0.005` rather than `0.000`–`0.001` solely to respect base-rate tail uncertainty (e.g., unforeseen mootness/vacatur developments or an unexpected administrative-review angle).
