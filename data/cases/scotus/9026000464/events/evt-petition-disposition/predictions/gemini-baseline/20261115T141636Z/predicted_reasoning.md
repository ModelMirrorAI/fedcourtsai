# Predicted Court Action: *Hamp's Construction, LLC v. Telle* (No. 26-464)

## Disposition and Conference Trajectory

The Court will **deny** the petition for a writ of certiorari out of its first scheduled conference without calling for a response or relisting the case.

- **Current posture and relist forecast (`relist-increment`: 0.035):** The docket currently records **one distribution**—distributed on November 10, 2026, for the Conference of December 4, 2026, following the Solicitor General's November 2, 2026 waiver of the right to respond on behalf of Respondent Acting Secretary of the Army. Expect **zero additional distributions**: no pre-conference reschedule, no Call for a Response (CFR) that would trigger re-distribution after a brief in opposition, and no conference relist. The denial order will issue on the routine Monday order list following the December 4, 2026 conference (expected December 7, 2026). In the unlikely event (~3.5%) that the petition is distributed at least once more, the likeliest path is a single pre-conference administrative reschedule or an isolated CFR followed by one post-opposition distribution and denial, rather than a multi-relist grant track.
- **CVSG forecast (`cvsg-increment`: 0.001):** The Court will **not** call for the views of the Solicitor General. No CVSG is recorded on the docket, and none will issue because the United States federal government is already a party—Respondent is the Acting Secretary of the Army, represented directly by the Solicitor General. Any request for the federal government's position would take the form of a Call for a Response directed to Respondent, not an invitation to file an amicus brief.

## Conditional Grant Route and Scope of Review

- **Questions presented if granted (context-only):** If the Court were to grant certiorari (probability 0.005), it would **not** take all three questions presented as written. Question 1 and Question 3 explicitly rest on contract drawings depicting top-of-bank "Construction Traffic" and on the solicitation's barge provisions—grounds that the Federal Circuit held were respectively forfeited before the Armed Services Board of Contract Appeals (and omitted from the appellate appendix) and rescinded by solicitation amendment prior to bidding. Accordingly, if granted, the Court would restrict review to a narrowed version of **Question 2**: whether, under the Federal Acquisition Regulation's Differing Site Conditions clause (48 C.F.R. § 52.236-2), a court construing integrated construction contract documents may treat the absence of a geotechnical boring log at the specific quadrant of a latent bank failure as defeating a threshold contractual "indication" of subsurface conditions. If reached on the merits, the Court would likely hold that contract indications under FAR 52.236-2 are assessed holistically from the perspective of a reasonable contractor rather than by a per se geographic-boring rule, while concluding that substantial evidence of topographic and geologic differences across canal banks defeated reasonable reliance on this record.
- **Summary disposition vs. plenary review (`summary-disposition-route`: 0.28):** Conditional on a grant of review, **plenary review (72%) is likelier than summary disposition in the cert order (28%)**. No pending Supreme Court merits case involves the Contract Disputes Act or FAR 52.236-2 to anchor a hold-and-GVR, and the forfeiture and substantial-evidence hurdles below foreclose a summary per curiam reversal. Conditional on an unexpected grant, summary disposition would arise primarily if an intervening administrative settlement, mootness event (*Munsingwear* vacatur), or narrow government concession after a CFR prompted a GVR rather than full briefing and argument.

## Separate Writings on the Order

- **Dissent or statement respecting denial (`dissent-from-denial`: 0.003; `order-writers`):** Expect an **unsigned routine denial order with no separate writing** by any Justice. Neither a circuit conflict nor a preserved constitutional or administrative-structural issue is presented to draw a statement respecting denial or a dissent from denial. In the remote event (~0.3%) that a sitting Justice writes separately on the order disposing of the petition, Justices Thomas and Gorsuch carry slightly higher individual probabilities than their colleagues given their broader propensity to author separate cert-stage writings and occasional interest in government-contracting fairness, though the forfeited record makes any separate writing highly improbable.
