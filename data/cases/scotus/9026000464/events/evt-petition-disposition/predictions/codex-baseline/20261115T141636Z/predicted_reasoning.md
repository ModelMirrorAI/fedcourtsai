# Predicted Court action

The Court will most likely deny the petition in an unsigned order after the December 4, 2026 conference, likely on the next regular order list. That denial will not announce a view on whether the contractor deserved compensation or endorse the lower court's reasoning.

The snapshot records **one distribution and no relist**. I expect **zero additional distributions**. The probability of at least one additional distribution is **6.5%**; if another occurs, one is likelier than a prolonged sequence. This increment includes a redistribution following a requested response or a rescheduling, not only a substantive relist.

I expect **no CVSG**, assigning **0.1%** to a new invitation. The Solicitor General already represents the federal respondent and has waived a response. If the Court wants the government's substantive position, a request for a response is the natural next step instead.

The three questions presented overlap. If review is granted, the likely focus is a narrowed combination of Questions 1 and 2: when the contract's documents, read as a whole, implicitly indicate subsurface conditions despite the absence of a boring at the failure location. Question 3's more generalized ambiguity and risk-allocation theory is less likely to be taken separately. Preservation of the traffic-drawing theory will remain an obstacle to reaching that formulation.

Conditional on a grant, **plenary review is more likely than disposition in the cert order**. I assign **12%** to a summary route conditional on grant. No identified intervening decision supplies a particular GVR ground, and resolving a disagreement about this contract and its evidentiary record is unlikely to produce summary reversal. If the Court takes the merits, the dispute will concern the scope of implied contractual indications under the differing-site-conditions clause, not a general guarantee against all unexpected construction conditions.

Conditional on denial, the probability of any dissent or statement respecting denial is **0.4%**. I expect no separate writing and no announced merits vote. No particular Justice is affirmatively predicted to write; Thomas and Gorsuch receive the largest, still tiny, individual writing probabilities.
