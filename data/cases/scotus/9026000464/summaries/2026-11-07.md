## In brief

A construction company asks the Court to revive its claim for extra payment after a federal appeals court rejected its site-conditions dispute with the Army.

## What happened

Hamp's Construction contracted with the Army Corps of Engineers to improve Trapp Canal in Louisiana. While working on the southwest bank, it found bank collapses and depressions it says were unsafe for its planned equipment, forcing a costlier construction method. Hamp's Construction sought additional payment and time, arguing the government's contract documents should have warned it of these conditions. The Armed Services Board of Contract Appeals denied the claim, finding the contract contained no representation about conditions specifically where the failures occurred. The Federal Circuit affirmed, agreeing that the boring logs, drawings, and solicitation did not give a reasonable contractor grounds to expect stable conditions there.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked whether a government construction contract can be read as making no representation at all about ground conditions in an area, even though it includes broader site data, drawings, and other materials arguably suggesting the area was suitable for the planned work. It is also asked whether courts may treat the absence of a test boring at the exact failure spot as deciding the case, rather than weighing all the contract materials together, and whether the government may treat its own drawings and data as non-representative only after problems appear.

## Where it stands

The petition was filed on September 28, 2026. The respondent, the Acting Secretary of the Army, waived the right to file a response. The docket shows no further action since that waiver was filed.

## What each outcome would mean

Hamp's Construction, which lost in the Federal Circuit, is asking the Court to take up its case. If the Court grants review, it would hear full arguments and decide how courts should interpret government construction contracts for hidden site conditions; a grant would not itself decide who wins. If the Court denies review, the Federal Circuit's decision affirming the rejection of Hamp's Construction's claim stays in place, and the Army's win stands.
