## In brief

A construction company asks the Court to revive its claim for extra payment after a canal-bank collapse the Army said its contract never promised wouldn't happen.

## What happened

Hamp's Construction contracted with the Army Corps of Engineers to rebuild a canal in Louisiana. While working on one bank, the company found collapses and unstable soil it says the contract documents did not warn about, forcing costlier construction methods. It sought extra payment and time under a contract clause covering unexpected site conditions. The Armed Services Board of Contract Appeals denied the claim, finding the contract made no representation about conditions in that specific area, even though it agreed Hamp's Construction had real problems there. The Federal Circuit affirmed, agreeing the boring samples provided were too far from the trouble spot to count as a representation.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked whether a government contract can be read as making no promise at all about underground conditions in a work area, even though it included nearby soil samples, drawings, and other site information, and whether courts may treat the absence of a soil sample at the exact failure spot as deciding the question by itself, rather than weighing all the contract materials together.

## Where it stands

## What each outcome would mean

Hamp's Construction, which lost in the lower court, is asking the Court to hear its case. If the Court grants the petition, it would hear full arguments and decide the legal question itself, without yet deciding which side wins. If it denies the petition, the Federal Circuit's ruling against Hamp's Construction stands, leaving the company without the extra payment and time it sought. Either way, the decision now rests on how the Court addresses this initial filing.
