## In brief

A construction contractor asks the Court to revive its claim for extra payment after unexpected ground conditions delayed an Army Corps canal project.

## What happened

Hamp's Construction LLC contracted with the Army Corps of Engineers to perform flood-control work on a canal in Louisiana. The contract included a clause allowing extra payment if a contractor encounters site conditions that differ materially from what the contract indicates. When Hamp's began work on one section, it found a collapsed bank and other ground failures it says were not shown in the boring logs or drawings it had been given. Hamp's sought roughly $3.9 million and extra time, but the Armed Services Board of Contract Appeals denied the claim, finding the contract made no representation about conditions at that specific spot. The Federal Circuit affirmed that decision.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked whether a government construction contract that includes general subsurface data, drawings, and site information can be read as making no representation at all about ground conditions in the area where problems arose, and whether a court may reject a contractor's claim for extra payment simply because no test boring was taken at the exact spot where the failure occurred, even if other contract materials suggested stable conditions there.

## Where it stands

The petition was filed on September 28, 2026, and the government's response is due November 6, 2026. The docket shows no action yet on whether the Court will hear the case.

## What each outcome would mean

Hamp's Construction is asking the Court to take up the case. If the Court grants the petition, it will hear full arguments and decide the legal question itself, which would not by itself determine who wins. If the Court denies the petition, the Federal Circuit's ruling against Hamp's Construction and in favor of the Secretary of the Army stands, leaving Hamp's without the additional payment and time extension it sought.
