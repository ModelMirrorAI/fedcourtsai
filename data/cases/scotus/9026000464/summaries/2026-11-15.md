## In brief

A construction company asks the Court to revive its claim for extra payment after a flood-control project ran into unexpected ground conditions the government did not disclose.

## What happened

Hamp's Construction LLC contracted with the Army Corps of Engineers to improve a Louisiana flood-control canal. While working on one section, the bank collapsed and showed other signs of instability, forcing Hamp's Construction to switch to costlier, barge-based equipment. Hamp's Construction sought extra payment and more time, arguing the government's contract documents, including soil borings and drawings, should have warned of these conditions. The Armed Services Board of Contract Appeals denied the claim, finding the contract contained no representation about conditions at the specific spot where the problems occurred. The Federal Circuit affirmed, agreeing that the boring logs provided did not indicate what a reasonable contractor would expect to find at that location.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked to decide whether a government construction contract can be read as making no representation at all about ground conditions in a work area, when the contract as a whole, including drawings, boring samples taken elsewhere on the site, and other project materials, arguably suggested the area was suitable for the planned work. The case also asks whether courts may treat the simple absence of a soil test at the exact spot where problems later arose as enough by itself to defeat such a claim.

## Where it stands

The petition was filed, the government waived its right to respond, and the case was distributed for the Court's December 4, 2026 conference, where the justices will decide whether to take it up.

## What each outcome would mean

Hamp's Construction, which lost in the Federal Circuit, is asking the Court to take up the case and reverse that ruling. If the Court grants the petition, it will hear the case in full and decide the contract-interpretation question; a grant alone would not decide who wins. If the Court denies the petition, the Federal Circuit's ruling stands, leaving Hamp's Construction without the additional payment and time extension it sought, and the Secretary of the Army's win in place.
