# Forecast: what the Court will do with No. 26-152, *Adkins v. Rosslyn Syndicate, LC*

**Disposition.** The petition will be denied, almost certainly at or shortly
after the Conference of October 9, 2026, in an order list with no separate
writing. The pending motion to expedite consideration of the petition will be
denied in the same breath or left to fall with the denial.

**Relists.** The docket shows **one** distribution (September 16, 2026, for the
October 9 Conference) and no relist. I expect no further distribution: the
petition presents no question the Court could grant on, no Justice has reason
to hold it for a statement, and nothing on the docket is pending that would
prompt a call for a response. The only realistic route to a second
distribution is an administrative reschedule, not a merits relist. P(at least
one more distribution) ≈ 0.07.

**CVSG.** No. The dispute is a private tort-type claim between an individual
and a hotel operator; no federal statute's construction is in play and no
federal interest is implicated. P(CVSG called for) ≈ 0.002.

**Which question presented, if any.** None as written. All five questions ask
whether the lower courts "properly" ruled on this petitioner's own motions
(leave to file an emergency complaint, an emergency injunction, a motion to
expedite, rehearing en banc) or whether the Fourth Circuit could issue a
show-cause order on a prefiling injunction alongside its affirmance. These are
case-specific error-correction questions; none states a rule of law on which
courts divide. The one conceivably general issue, process due before a
prefiling injunction, was not ripe when the petition was filed (the show-cause
order was still pending) and is not framed as a legal question. Were the Court
to act at all, it would reformulate around that due-process point, but I do not
expect it to.

**Summary disposition versus plenary review.** Conditional on any grant, the
route would overwhelmingly be a summary one, a GVR or a per curiam
disposition, rather than briefing and argument: there is no record, no
adversarial presentation (the respondent filed nothing), and a four-page
petition. P(summary route | grant) ≈ 0.75. Unconditionally, no intervening
decision of this Court bears on the case, so a GVR has nothing to hang on.

**Dissent from or statement respecting denial.** None expected.
P(some Justice writes | denial) ≈ 0.01. The petition raises no recurring issue
that a Justice has been flagging, and pro se sanctions and access-to-courts
grievances of this shape are denied without comment as a matter of course.

**If granted (conditional only).** The only plausible ground would be a
procedural due-process holding about notice and an opportunity to be heard
before a court of appeals imposes a prefiling injunction. That is not a live
prospect here.
