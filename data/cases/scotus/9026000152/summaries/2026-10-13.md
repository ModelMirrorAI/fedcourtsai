## In brief

The Supreme Court declined to hear Dora Adkins's bid to revive a lawsuit against a hotel operator over alleged chemical exposure.

## What happened

Dora Adkins sued Rosslyn Syndicate, LC, which operates a hotel, after two stays there she says exposed her to chemical and food poisoning. The district court screened her proposed complaint under existing filing restrictions imposed because of her history of lawsuits, and denied her motion to file it, her motion to expedite, and her request for an emergency injunction, finding her claims implausible. The Fourth Circuit affirmed and separately ordered Adkins to explain why it should not bar her from further filings or impose sanctions, citing her litigation history. Adkins asked for rehearing, which was denied.

## What the Court is being asked

Adkins asked the Court to decide whether the lower courts were wrong to block her complaint before it could proceed, whether the appeals court violated her right to fair notice by raising the possibility of a filing ban at the same time it ruled against her, and whether courts can use such restrictions to turn away claims of physical injury without reaching their merits.

## Where it stands

The petition was distributed for the Court's October 9, 2026 conference, and on October 13, 2026 the Court denied it.

## What each outcome would mean

Adkins, as petitioner, asked the Court to overturn the Fourth Circuit's decision. The denial leaves that decision in place: the lower courts' refusal to let her file the complaint, their denial of emergency relief, and the appeals court's affirmance all stand. Rosslyn Syndicate faces no further proceedings in this case before the Supreme Court. The denial does not mean the Court agreed the lower courts were right, only that it will not review the case.
