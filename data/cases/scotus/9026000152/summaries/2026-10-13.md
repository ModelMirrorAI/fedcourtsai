## In brief

The Supreme Court declined to hear Dora Adkins's bid to revive her lawsuit against a hotel operator over alleged chemical exposure.

## What happened

Adkins sued Rosslyn Syndicate, LC, which operates a hotel, claiming she was exposed to chemical contamination in two guest rooms and suffered food poisoning attempts, and raised negligence, breach of contract, and emotional distress claims. Because Adkins is subject to prefiling injunctions in the Eastern District of Virginia over a long history of similar lawsuits, a district judge screened and denied her proposed complaint and related emergency motions, then closed the case. The Fourth Circuit affirmed and separately ordered Adkins to show cause why it should not impose a prefiling injunction or sanctions against her for repeated meritless appeals. The Fourth Circuit denied rehearing.

## What the Court is being asked

Adkins asks the Court to decide whether the appeals court wrongly affirmed the dismissal of her case, whether it violated her due-process rights by ordering her to explain why she shouldn't face filing restrictions at the same time it ruled against her on the merits, and whether the lower courts wrongly refused her requests for emergency relief and to amend her complaint.

## Where it stands

The petition was distributed for the Court's October 9, 2026 conference, and on October 13, 2026, the Court denied the petition.

## What each outcome would mean

Adkins, as petitioner, asked the Court to take up her case. The denial means the Court will not review it, so the Fourth Circuit's decision affirming dismissal of her complaint stands, along with its show-cause order concerning possible filing restrictions against her. Rosslyn Syndicate's win in the lower courts remains in place. The denial does not mean the Supreme Court agreed with the lower courts' reasoning, only that it chose not to hear the case.
