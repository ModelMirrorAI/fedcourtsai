## In brief

A homeowner asks the Court to revive her claim that police must pay for extensive damage they caused searching her house for a fugitive who was never there.

## What happened

Police in South Bend and St. Joseph County, Indiana, searching for a fugitive mistakenly believed to be in Amy Hadley's house, surrounded it, deployed tear gas, and searched inside, causing substantial damage. The fugitive had no connection to Hadley and was never there. Hadley sued under the Fifth Amendment's rule against taking property without paying for it, after the city and county refused to compensate her. The district court dismissed her claims, holding that circuit precedent bars compensation claims for damage done under police power rather than the power to take property outright. The Seventh Circuit affirmed, and denied rehearing.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked whether property damaged by police during a lawful search can count as property taken for public use, entitling the owner to payment, or whether such damage falls outside that rule because it results from police power rather than the government's power to acquire property for public use. The Court is also asked whether officers who intentionally destroy an innocent person's property while pursuing a suspect can be made to pay even so.

## Where it stands

The petition has been fully briefed, including several amicus briefs and a brief in opposition, and was distributed for the Court's conference on September 28, 2026, and again for October 9, 2026. No order granting or denying the petition has been recorded yet.

## What each outcome would mean

Hadley, who lost in the lower courts, is asking the Court to take up her case. If the Court grants review, it will hear full arguments and decide whether damage like this can require compensation; granting does not itself decide who wins. If the Court denies review, the Seventh Circuit's decision stands, leaving Hadley uncompensated and leaving in place that circuit's rule that such police-caused damage is not compensable.
