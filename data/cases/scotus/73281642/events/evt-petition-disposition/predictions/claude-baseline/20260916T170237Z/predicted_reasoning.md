# Forecast: what the Court will do with No. 25-1158, Hadley v. City of South Bend

**Disposition.** The petition is more likely denied than granted, but the grant
chance is well above the run of paid petitions: P(any grant) = 0.22. The most
likely path is a denial after at least one relist, accompanied by a separate
writing from a Justice who has already spoken to this question.

**Relists.** The docket shows a single distribution, for the September 28, 2026
long conference. I expect the petition to be relisted at least once (P = 0.55),
most likely to the October 9 conference, and a second relist is plausible. Five
cert-stage amicus briefs, a published Seventh Circuit opinion that itself lists
the circuit conflict, and a companion Institute for Justice petition from the
Ninth Circuit (Pena v. City of Los Angeles, No. 25-1163, filed the same week)
make this a petition the Court will look at twice rather than clear from the
long-conference list. If the Court is going to grant, it will do so in October
or early November 2026; a denial with a statement or dissent would land in the
same window.

**CVSG.** Unlikely (P = 0.07). No federal party is involved, the Solicitor
General was not invited in Baker v. City of McKinney (2024) or Slaybaugh v.
Rutherford County (2025) on the same question, and the federal interest, the
Federal Circuit's AmeriSource line on Tucker Act takings claims, is indirect.

**Which question, if granted.** The Court would take Question 1 as written or
lightly reformulated: whether the Takings Clause has a police-power exception.
It is the clean, acknowledged split (Seventh, Tenth, and Federal Circuits
against Fourth, Fifth, Sixth, and Eleventh), and the Seventh Circuit's opinion
rested on that exception alone. Question 2, the broader innocent-owner rule
with its "how do we define innocent" problems that the panel flagged, would be
left behind or folded into Question 1. A grant of Hadley and Pena together,
consolidated, is a live shape, with Hadley the cleaner vehicle on Question 1.

**Summary disposition.** Conditional on any grant, P(summary route) = 0.2. No
intervening decision supports a GVR today, and a per curiam reversal on an open
doctrinal question is unlikely. The summary route that exists is a hold: if the
Court grants the companion Pena petition alone, Hadley would be held and later
GVR'd in light of Pena, which resolves this cell as a grant on the cert-order
route. That path is a minority of the grant mass; a plenary grant of Hadley
(alone or consolidated) is the larger share.

**Dissent from denial.** Conditional on a denial, P(some separate writing) =
0.5. Justice Sotomayor, joined by Justice Gorsuch, wrote in Baker that the
question was important and would benefit from further percolation. That
percolation has since happened (Ninth, Eleventh, Seventh Circuits), so a
denial now would most plausibly draw a dissent or statement from Justice
Gorsuch, possibly joined by Justice Sotomayor or Justice Thomas. Slaybaugh was
denied in 2025 without a noted writing, which is why this is even odds rather
than better.

**If granted, on the merits (conditional only).** The likeliest outcome is a
vacatur: the Court would reject a freestanding police-power exception as
inconsistent with Arkansas Game & Fish, Horne, Sheetz, and Cedar Point, and
remand for the Seventh Circuit to analyze the claim under ordinary takings
principles, leaving open whether a lawful-search privilege or necessity
defeats compensation on these facts. An affirmance on the Cedar Point
"no right to exclude a lawful search" ground is the main alternative.
