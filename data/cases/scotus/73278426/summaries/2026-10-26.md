## In brief

Gun rights groups ask the Court to strike down Connecticut's ban on AR-15-style rifles and large-capacity magazines as a violation of the Second Amendment.

## What happened

The National Association for Gun Rights and a Connecticut resident sued to block Connecticut laws banning certain semiautomatic "assault weapons," including AR-15-style rifles, and magazines holding more than ten rounds. A federal district court denied a preliminary injunction, finding the challengers unlikely to succeed, and the Second Circuit Court of Appeals affirmed, holding the restrictions consistent with the nation's historical tradition of regulating unusually dangerous weapons even assuming the guns are commonly owned. The gun-rights groups ask the Supreme Court to review that decision.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked whether a government may ban firearms and magazines that are commonly owned by law-abiding people for lawful purposes, or whether such a ban violates the constitutional right to keep and bear arms regardless of how dangerous the government considers the weapon to be.

## Where it stands

The petition has been distributed for conference many times since January 2026 without the docket recording a grant or denial. The parties have filed supplemental briefs disputing the effect of a more recent D.C. court decision on a similar magazine ban, and the case remains awaiting the Court's decision on whether to hear it.

## What each outcome would mean

The National Association for Gun Rights and its co-petitioner, who lost in the lower courts, are asking the Court to take up the case. If the Court grants review, it would hear full briefing and argument and decide whether Connecticut's restrictions are constitutional, without yet deciding who wins. If the Court denies review, the Second Circuit's decision upholding Connecticut's ban stays in place, leaving the restrictions in effect and the challengers' suit unsuccessful.
