## In brief

Gun rights groups ask the Court to review a ruling upholding Connecticut's ban on certain semiautomatic rifles and large-capacity magazines.

## What happened

National Association for Gun Rights and a Connecticut resident sued state officials, arguing that Connecticut's restrictions on assault weapons and magazines holding more than ten rounds violate the right to keep and bear arms. A federal district court denied their request to block the laws while the case proceeded, finding they were unlikely to win on the merits. The Second Circuit Court of Appeals affirmed, holding that even assuming the weapons are commonly owned, the restrictions fit within a historical tradition of regulating unusually dangerous weapons, and separately that the plaintiffs had not shown the other requirements for such preliminary relief. The plaintiffs then asked the Supreme Court to take the case.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked to decide whether a ban on possessing certain semiautomatic rifles and magazines over ten rounds, when both are owned by many law-abiding Americans, violates the right to keep and bear arms, or whether that common ownership does not by itself bar such restrictions.

## What each outcome would mean

The gun rights groups are asking the Court to overturn the Second Circuit's decision, which left Connecticut's restrictions in place. If the Court grants review, it would hear full arguments and decide the constitutional question itself, deciding nothing yet about who is right. If it denies review, the Second Circuit's ruling upholding Connecticut's laws stands, and the challenge to the restrictions ends without a Supreme Court decision on the merits.
