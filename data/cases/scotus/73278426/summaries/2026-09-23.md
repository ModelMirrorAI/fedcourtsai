## What happened

After the 2012 Sandy Hook school shooting, Connecticut expanded restrictions on "assault weapons," including AR-15-style rifles, and on ammunition magazines holding more than ten rounds. The National Association for Gun Rights and a Connecticut resident, Toni Theresa Spera Flanigan, sued, arguing the restrictions violate the Second Amendment, and sought to block the laws while the case proceeded. A federal district court refused, finding the challengers had not shown they would likely win. The United States Court of Appeals for the Second Circuit agreed, holding that even assuming the weapons are commonly owned for lawful purposes, the restrictions fit within a historical tradition of regulating unusually dangerous weapons. The challengers also lost on other requirements for blocking a law, which they did not contest on appeal, according to the state's brief.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked whether a state may ban commonly owned semiautomatic rifles and large-capacity magazines, or whether the fact that many law-abiding people own such weapons for lawful purposes by itself makes such bans unconstitutional. The petitioners argue that common ownership should end the inquiry; Connecticut answers that courts must still ask whether the restriction matches a historical tradition of regulating unusually dangerous weapons, and that the lower courts never actually decided whether these particular weapons are commonly used for self-defense.
