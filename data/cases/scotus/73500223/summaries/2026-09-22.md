## What happened

Majestic Realty and two related companies own shopping centers in Redlands, California, that ban leafletting and other non-commercial activity on their property. Alex Salazar, an activist, asked to distribute leaflets promoting his views on child support and was refused under that policy. Salazar sued in California state court, arguing the refusal violated the free-speech guarantee of the California Constitution. Relying on the California Supreme Court's PruneYard decision (previously upheld by the U.S. Supreme Court), the California Court of Appeal sided with Salazar at the preliminary-injunction stage, ruling he was likely to succeed and rejecting the owners' arguments that being forced to host his speech violated their property rights and their right not to speak. The state supreme court denied further review.

## What the Court is being asked

The owners want the Court to decide whether a state can force owners of privately owned commercial property, like a shopping center, to allow unwanted expressive activity such as leafletting, without violating the owners' property rights or their right to not speak. They argue this old rule permitting such access is out of step with the Court's more recent rulings on when the government must pay for occupying private property and on forcing people to host others' messages. Salazar responds that the older rule still controls, that the case is a poor vehicle because it involves only a preliminary ruling with an undeveloped factual record, and that no lower courts disagree on the question.

## Where it stands

The petition has been fully briefed, with amicus briefs supporting the property owners and a brief from Salazar opposing review, and the case was distributed for the Court's conference on September 28, 2026, where the Justices will decide whether to take up the case.
