## In brief

The Court declined to hear shopping center owners' bid to overturn a ruling requiring them to allow an activist's leafletting on their property.

## What happened

Majestic Realty and related companies own shopping centers in Redlands, California, that ban leafletting and similar activity. Alex Salazar, an activist, asked to distribute leaflets about men's child-support obligations; the centers refused under their policy. Salazar sued, arguing California's constitution protected his activity, relying on a 1979 California Supreme Court case affirmed by the U.S. Supreme Court. The trial court denied him a preliminary injunction, but the California Court of Appeal reversed, ruling Salazar was likely to succeed and that the owners' federal constitutional objections had already been rejected. The California Supreme Court denied further review.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked whether a state violates property owners' rights against uncompensated takings and against being forced to host speech when it requires commercial property owners to let people conduct unwanted expressive activity, like leafletting, on their land, and whether the Court's 1980 precedent allowing this should be overturned.

## Where it stands

The petition was distributed for the Court's September 28, 2026 conference, and the docket shows the petition was denied on October 5, 2026.

## What each outcome would mean

Majestic Realty and the other property owners asked the Court to take the case and overturn the California Court of Appeal's ruling. The denial leaves that ruling in place, meaning Salazar's preliminary injunction against the centers' leafletting ban stands, and the case returns to the lower courts for further proceedings. The denial does not mean the Supreme Court agreed with the lower court's reasoning or decided the constitutional questions the owners raised; it simply leaves the existing precedent and the appellate decision undisturbed for now.
