## Base-rate anchor

The corresponding unrounded `baseline` risk-set fields in `metrics/statpack.json` give 593 estimated grants over 11,580 weighted resolved petitions, or **5.1209%**. Calculation: sum `prefix_est_grant_rate * prefix_weighted_resolved`, divided by sum `prefix_weighted_resolved`, selecting those Terms and that band. This is the bracketed reached rate for private paid petitioners, not the lower rate among petitions that ended in baseline. I excluded the 2025 and 2026 rows from the anchor. These are the committed pack's historical estimates; I did not establish a fresh live-corpus vintage, and the pack exposes no build timestamp in its top-level fields.

The modern discretionary-cert disposition section confirms the low whole-docket grant environment but is not my selected-cell anchor. I also read the paid-segment relist and CVSG cuts. Their terminal categories put most petitions in the no-relist group and concentrate grants among petitions with later distributions or a CVSG. Those pooled cuts include Terms outside my anchor and are used only for qualitative shape, not to estimate a future hazard or replace the strictly-prior band rate. In particular, a terminal relist bucket cannot tell me directly the probability of another distribution from today's count of one.

## Why 8%, rather than the 5.1% anchor

There is a genuine reason to move above the ordinary private-petition baseline. The question expressly requests reconsideration of PruneYard across two constitutional doctrines. The petition develops a right-to-exclude theory from the physical-takings cases and argues that commercial invitation is not consent to unrelated expressive use. Its compelled-speech theory emphasizes both unwanted subsidization and a right to remain silent rather than having to disavow a third party's message. These are substantial constitutional arguments, not merely an objection to the result in one preliminary-injunction dispute. The published decision and four amicus filing entries add evidence of organized interest, although I did not read the amici and do not count their filings as four independent legal confirmations. Sources: provisioned QP; petition pp. 5-9, 12-21, 29-34; snapshot proceedings.

Several constraints keep the upward adjustment modest:

- **Vehicle:** Both briefs describe an appeal from the denial of preliminary relief, followed by an appellate reversal. The opposition stresses unsettled injunction terms, burdens, attribution, and time-place-and-manner restrictions. The petition's jurisdiction section asserts jurisdiction under 28 U.S.C. 1257(a), but the excerpt does not establish why this posture warrants immediate review. I treat finality and record development as substantial risks, not as a definitive jurisdictional holding. Sources: petition pp. 6-9; opposition pp. 1-5.
- **No established federal split:** The opposition denies any conflict over the federal validity of PruneYard-style access. The petition's discussion of different state constitutional approaches does not itself demonstrate inconsistent holdings on the same federal question. I found no substantiated square federal split in the portions reviewed. Sources: petition pp. 20-25 discussion heading and argument; opposition pp. 5-7.
- **Later precedents distinguish, rather than silently erase, PruneYard:** Cedar Point Nursery v. Hassid, 594 U.S. 139, 156-57 (2021), distinguishes public shopping premises from closed agricultural property. I verified that passage in CourtListener opinion 4697835, majority slip pp. 14-15. The petition acknowledges this distinction and argues against its force, so granting here would be a deliberate reconsideration, not routine application of Cedar Point. Source: petition pp. 17-20.
- **The First Amendment analogy also faces a preserved distinction:** Moody v. NetChoice, LLC, 603 U.S. 707, 738-40 (2024), discusses the difference between interference with an entity's own expression and access to a mall whose owner asserted no expressive activity. CourtListener opinion 11066627 confirmed the majority's PruneYard discussion. The petition's objection to being compelled to associate with unwanted speech is meaningful, but does not by itself establish the expressive-editing claim that supplied the contrasting rule. Sources: petition pp. 29-33; opposition pp. 12-14.

My 8% is a judgmental adjustment, not a fitted model or an empirical likelihood ratio. The strongest positive is a substantial constitutional reconsideration request with supporting amici; the strongest negatives are preliminary posture and the later Court's express preservation of relevant distinctions. Grant includes GVR and summary reversal, not just plenary review. I do not interpret the 92% complement as an exact probability of denial, since withdrawal or dismissal also remain possible; denial is the modal label.

## Other claims and stakes

**Additional distribution, 22%:** The petition's broad reconsideration request and amicus interest make additional consideration plausible, but the existing distribution has not yet reached its scheduled conference, and no observed repeated consideration supports a high estimate. The modal forecast is zero more, with one or two if it does recur. This is an incremental, judgmental probability with no published hazard baseline.

**New CVSG, 1%:** This is a constitutional dispute between private parties over state access rules, not a supplied record of unresolved federal statutory administration or a distinctive federal operational interest. I expect the Court to decide the petition without seeking the Solicitor General's views. This probability is not the terminal CVSG frequency.

**Summary route conditional on grant, 4%:** No reviewed material identifies a newer lead decision for a GVR. Overturning the controlling precedent favors argument and plenary consideration. The small residual accommodates an unanticipated summary route; it is conditional on grant, not 4% of all outcomes.

**Dissent or statement conditional on denial, 10%:** The constitutional challenge could attract a recorded objection to leaving the precedent in place, but there is no such signal in the supplied record. I expect silence and make no per-Justice cert vote claim.

**Stakes, 0.68:** If reached, the requested reconsideration could alter state-protected expressive access to shopping centers and the property/speech boundary. This assessment concerns potential doctrinal reach, not the chance of review, the merits of the respondent's message, or post-decision publicity.

The most important unexamined material is the reply and the underlying appendix, particularly any answer to the interlocutory/finality objections. That limits confidence without preventing a conservative forecast. Tooling limitations are recorded in `tooling.json`; no outcome contamination or blocking data defect was encountered.
