# Forecast: what the Court will do with No. 25-1322, Majestic Realty Co. v. Salazar

**Bottom line.** The petition is denied, most likely on the order list following the
September 28, 2026 conference or after a single relist. P(grant) = 0.10.

## Distribution and relists

The docket shows **one distribution** (July 15, 2026, for the Conference of September 28,
2026 — the long conference). No relist yet, no CVSG. Forecasting from that state:

- **Relist.** P(at least one further distribution) = 0.35. This is a paid petition from a
  repeat Supreme Court litigant (Pacific Legal Foundation) with four cert-stage amicus briefs
  from Cato, Washington Legal Foundation, Goldwater Institute and the Manhattan Institute, and
  it asks a question at least two Justices are likely to want a second look at. That is well
  above the typical long-conference petition, but the far more likely path is still a clean
  denial on the first order list. If relisted, expect one relist (a second look or a Justice
  drafting a separate writing), not the two-plus pattern that precedes a grant.
- **CVSG.** No. The United States has no stake in a state-constitutional speech-access
  rule applied to a private shopping center; no federal program or statute is implicated.
  P(cvsg-increment) = 0.02.

## Grant or deny, and why

The petition asks the Court to **overrule PruneYard Shopping Center v. Robins, 447 U.S. 74
(1980)** on both its Takings Clause and its compelled-speech holdings. It does not allege a
split on the federal question: the "conflict" it describes is between PruneYard and the
Court's later physical-takings cases (Loretto, Nollan, Horne, Cedar Point) and later
compelled-subsidy cases (Knox, Harris, Janus, Moody). Three features make denial the
strong favourite:

1. **The Court has just re-drawn the line the petition wants erased.** Cedar Point Nursery
   (2021) distinguished PruneYard explicitly, on the ground that a shopping center is a
   business "open to the public"; Moody v. NetChoice (2024) did the same on the First
   Amendment side, treating a shopping center as a non-expressive host. Both were majority
   opinions of the current Court. A Court that wanted to revisit PruneYard had two recent
   vehicles to signal it and instead preserved it.
2. **Interlocutory state-court posture.** The decision below reverses the denial of a
   preliminary injunction in a California intermediate appellate court; there is no final
   judgment, no developed record on burden or time-place-manner limits, and 28 U.S.C.
   § 1257 finality is a live obstacle the Court would have to work around under the Cox
   Broadcasting categories. The BIO presses the posture and the thin record.
3. **The ask is overruling, not application.** A private petitioner asking the Court to
   overrule a 46-year-old precedent, with no lower-court disagreement about whether that
   precedent binds, is the shape the Court most often declines with a "the argument is
   for another day" denial.

Cutting the other way: PLF's takings petitions have an unusually strong grant record at
this Court (Cedar Point, Knick, Sheetz, Tyler), the cert-stage amicus support is substantial
for a case of this size, the state-court opinion is published, and Justices Thomas and
Gorsuch have shown appetite for per-se takings rules. Those factors take the forecast well
above the baseline-band rate but not near even odds.

## Which question the Court would take, if it took one

If granted, the Court would take the question **as written** but decide it on the
**Takings Clause** ground alone: whether a state-law right of expressive access to a
shopping center open to the public is a per se physical taking under Cedar Point, or stays
under PruneYard's Penn Central treatment. The compelled-speech half is the weaker half after
Moody and would likely be left undecided or resolved narrowly against petitioners. A grant
limited to the takings question, or a reformulated QP dropping the First Amendment, is the
likelier grant shape than a grant on both.

## Summary disposition

No. There is no intervening decision to GVR in light of, and overruling or confining
PruneYard requires plenary review. P(summary route | grant) = 0.03.

## Dissent from or statement respecting denial

P(some Justice writes | denial) = 0.15. If anyone writes, it is Justice Thomas (a
statement or dissent noting that PruneYard is hard to square with Cedar Point and that the
Court should take the question in a case with a final judgment), possibly joined by
Justice Gorsuch. The interlocutory posture gives even a sympathetic Justice a reason to
stay silent and wait for a better vehicle, which is why this number is modest.

conference), or, if relisted once, on the October 13 or October 20 order list.
