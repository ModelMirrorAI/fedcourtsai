## Why 26%, rather than the 17.2% anchor

**Upward evidence.** The QP is an intelligible, broadly applicable dispute about what an employer must prove for Title VII's undue-hardship defense: a reasonable basis for predicting hardship versus proof that the accommodation would actually impose it. The petition, particularly pages 13–20, alleges a three-to-three circuit division and identifies concrete differences in treatment of contract-loss and liability risks, not merely an objection to vaccination policy. Its account of post-Groff accommodation disputes makes further clarification plausible. The response request after waiver is an affirmative attention signal, and the range of amici makes this more consequential than a routine private employment dispute. These factors support a material increase from the band anchor. The amici's substantive arguments were not independently read; their presence is the evidence used.

**Limits on the split.** The opposition's sections I and II, pages 10–21, directly contest the petition's characterization. It quotes the lower court as requiring actual, substantial burdens and explains its result as grounded in objective medical risk, not an employer's sincere but mistaken belief. It also points to cross-citations between supposedly opposing circuits and distinguishes the health-risk cases from the beard, naming-policy, and speech cases. Those are concrete reasons the Court may regard the conflict as fact-dependent rather than a clean disagreement over a governing rule. I treat these as respondent's supported arguments, not as independently verified holdings of every cited case.

**Vehicle concerns.** The opposition's section III, pages 21–23, argues that petitioners' comparator and safety evidence suffers from hearsay, personal-knowledge, and expertise defects. If that characterization holds, answering the abstract QP may not change the judgment. The medical-risk setting also complicates any general rule: proof of a substantial prospective risk need not require allowing the feared injury to occur. The petition offers a serious response, emphasizing prior successful accommodation and comparable employers, but the Court would have to disentangle the legal standard from the quality and admissibility of the evidence. I therefore do not move toward even odds merely because the petition announces a split.

**The two distributions are not two completed substantive examinations.** The first distribution and response request were both entered June 2; the second distribution followed the requested opposition and reply. The record is consistent with a redistribution after completion of briefing rather than a classic merits relist following conference consideration. I retain the harness's count of two but discount the inference of repeated judicial interest. This is a limitation of the signal, not a reason to replace the frozen band.

The 26% final estimate is a judgmental net adjustment: substantial attention and a potentially important legal question, offset by a persuasive dispute over whether that question controls this record. It is not a fitted calculation or an assertion that any particular Justice has voted to grant.

## Other probabilities and stakes

- **Further distribution: 38%.** This means at least one distribution after the two already recorded. Attention and the disputed split make another look plausible, but denial after the September conference remains the modal path. Conditional on any further distribution, one or two additional distributions are more plausible than a long series.
- **New CVSG: 8%.** Title VII and the agency-related arguments make federal views potentially useful, but the record shows neither a federal litigant nor a prior invitation. A response request to the respondent is not a CVSG.
- **Summary route given a grant: 15%.** The record identifies no new controlling decision requiring a routine remand; Groff already underlies the litigation below. If the Court accepts the alleged legal conflict, briefing and argument seem more useful than disposing of this fact-sensitive dispute in a cert order. The residual probability allows summary correction or a remand pathway, without asserting a pending lead case.
- **Dissent or statement given denial: 22%.** The accommodation issue and amici make a public expression of concern plausible, but a silent denial remains more likely. This forecasts aggregate existence, not individual cert votes.
- **Stakes: 0.67.** A generally applicable evidentiary rule for religious accommodation could reach many workplaces. The particular health-risk record and vehicle objections constrain how broadly this petition could establish that rule. This score assesses significance if decided, not grant likelihood.

The most important uncertainty is whether the Justices view the lower court as adopting a deferential legal test or simply applying a shared test to unrebutted medical evidence. Missing reply text limits the adversarial balance of this assessment. No majority opinion, vote lineup, or unconditional merits holding is forecast for a denial.
