# Forecast: what the Court will do with No. 25-1210, Petersen v. Snohomish Regional Fire & Rescue

**State forecast from.** The snapshot (2026-09-17) shows two distribution entries: the petition was first distributed on June 2, 2026 for the June 18 conference, the Court called for a response the same day (respondent had waived), and after the brief in opposition (Aug. 3) and reply (Aug. 17) it was redistributed on Aug. 19 for the September 28, 2026 long conference. So this petition has **not yet been considered at a conference**; the count of two is a request-for-response artifact, not a relist. No CVSG is on the docket. Fourteen amicus briefs support the petition (Gerald Groff, Nebraska and 21 other states, the Seventh-day Adventist General Conference, Protect the First, the National Catholic Bioethics Center, and others), plus one in support of neither party.

**Disposition.** Denial is the single most likely outcome, but only modestly: I put a grant at about one in three. The call for a response after a waiver is the Court's own signal that at least one chambers is interested, and the petition (Lisa Blatt, First Liberty) presents a crisp post-*Groff* question with genuine percolation. What holds me back is the vehicle: the Ninth Circuit's published opinion says in terms that it considers "only actual hardships, not hypothetical ones" and rests on unrebutted expert medical evidence, so several Justices will read it as a fact-bound affirmance with one stray "reasonable concern" phrase rather than a holding adopting a good-faith standard. The Court has also been reluctant to take pandemic-vaccine accommodation cases now that the mandates are gone.

**Relists.** If the petition is going to be granted, expect it to be relisted once (from Sept. 28 to the Oct. 9 conference) before the grant, following the Court's usual one-relist practice; a grant announced directly on the Oct. 5 order list is possible but less likely. If denied, the most likely path is an outright denial on the Oct. 5 order list, with a relist or two if a Justice is preparing a dissent from denial. Net: roughly even odds that the docket shows at least one further distribution.

**CVSG.** Unlikely but live (about 15%). The federal government is not a party, but the EEOC's regulation (29 C.F.R. § 1605.02) takes petitioners' side and the Solicitor General has a standing interest in Title VII's standard; a CVSG would be the Court's way of testing respondent's "no real split" argument before committing. If it happens it would issue from the Sept. 28 or Oct. 9 conference, pushing any grant to spring 2027.

**Question taken, if granted.** The QP as written: whether an employer may defeat a religious-accommodation claim by showing a reasonable basis for believing an accommodation would impose undue hardship, or must show actual undue hardship. I would not expect the Court to reformulate around the disease-transmission context respondent proposes; if it wanted that framing it would deny and wait.

**Summary disposition.** Very unlikely (about 4% conditional on a grant). There is no intervening decision to GVR in light of, and the record is contested enough that a per curiam reversal is implausible; a grant means plenary review, with argument in early 2027 and a decision by June 2027.

**Dissent from denial.** If denied, I expect a separate writing about a third of the time. Justice Thomas is the most likely author (he dissented from the denial in *Hittle v. City of Stockton*, whose petitioner is an amicus here), plausibly joined by Justice Gorsuch or Justice Alito, or a short statement respecting denial noting the question's importance while faulting the vehicle. Most denials of this kind still issue silently.

**If granted, conditional merits view.** The Court that decided *Groff* unanimously would very likely reject a pure good-faith or reasonable-belief safe harbor as inconsistent with the statute's "demonstrates that he is unable" text and with *Garrett*'s reading of the ADA's parallel language, while leaving room for employers to rely on objective medical evidence about risk at the time of decision. Reversal or vacatur and remand for a trial-worthy factual dispute would be the likely judgment.
