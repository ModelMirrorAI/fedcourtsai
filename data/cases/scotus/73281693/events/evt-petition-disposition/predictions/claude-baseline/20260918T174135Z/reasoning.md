# Why 0.35

**Cell.** Forward mode, cert stage (event.yaml records no stage or moment, so it reads as the ordinary `evt-petition-disposition` distribution cell). Snapshot `2026-09-17.json`; `context.json`: band `elevated` under `sal-v4`, `distribution_count` 2, no CVSG, Term 2025, paid. I read the provisioned petition, brief in opposition, and questions-presented text (all extracted cleanly per `documents.json`; the reply is on the docket but was not provisioned).

**Anchor.** The statpack's "Segment base rate by salience band (sal-v4)" table, `elevated` column, bracketed `reached` rate pooled over the eight Terms shown strictly before OT2025 (2017 to 2024): roughly 484 grant-family outcomes over a weighted risk-set of about 2,810, so about **17%**. That is the yardstick the evaluator scores this cell against. I did not anchor on the relist-count cut's bucket 2 (about 41% grant family): this docket's second distribution entry is a redistribution after the Court requested a response, not a relist, so bucket 2's population is not this petition's situation. The CVSG cut does not apply (none on the docket).

**Adjustments up (to well above the anchor).**
- *Response requested after a waiver* (June 2). This is the Court's affirmative act of attention and is the strongest single feature on the record; petitions that draw a call for response grant at a multiple of the docket rate.
- *Amicus mass*: fourteen briefs at the petition stage, including Groff himself, 22 states, and the Seventh-day Adventist Church, signalling that the religious-liberty bar has coordinated around this as the intended vehicle.
- *Counsel and posture*: Lisa Blatt as counsel of record with First Liberty; a published Ninth Circuit opinion (150 F.4th 1211); a purely legal question that was outcome-determinative below; six circuits have addressed it within three years of *Groff*.
- *Subject matter*: this Court has been receptive to religious-accommodation questions (*Groff* was unanimous), and the statutory issue avoids the constitutional baggage of the Free Exercise vaccine cases it declined.

**Adjustments down (why not higher).**
- *The split is contestable, and the BIO makes the point well.* The Ninth Circuit's opinion expressly states it considers "only actual hardships, not hypothetical ones" and rests on unrebutted expert evidence; petitioners' "reasonable concern" holding hangs largely on one phrase about the DOC contract. The BIO also shows cross-citation between the two supposed camps (Kluge citing Rodrique approvingly; the Third Circuit's Bushra affirming summary judgment in a vaccine case). A Justice who reads the opinion may see a fact-bound application of *Groff* rather than a rival legal standard.
- *Vehicle*: respondent argues much of petitioners' rebuttal evidence was inadmissible hearsay, which would complicate review; the pandemic-mandate context is atypical of accommodation disputes and the mandates have lapsed.
- *Ninth Circuit process*: rehearing en banc was denied with no judge calling for a vote, so there is no dissent below flagging the standard.
- The petition-stage amicus count is partly a product of organized interest-group support and is a weaker signal than the Court's own call for response.

Netting these, I land at **0.35**: roughly double the band anchor, reflecting the call for response and amici, discounted for a real chance the Court views the case as fact-bound or waits for a cleaner (non-COVID) vehicle. `predicted_disposition` is `denied` because that remains the modal outcome; `granted` is 0 accordingly.

**Other claims.** Relist-increment 0.50: about 0.85 conditional on a grant (the one-relist norm), about 0.30 conditional on a denial (relist for a dissent or statement), plus the CVSG path, which would add a redistribution. CVSG-increment 0.15: the government is not a party and the Court has the BIO in hand, but the EEOC's regulation squarely addresses the question and a CVSG is a common move on Title VII standards. Summary-disposition-route 0.04: no intervening decision to GVR against and a contested record. Dissent-from-denial 0.35: the Court's religious-liberty wing has written on denials in this area (Justice Thomas in *Hittle*), and the 22-state amicus support raises the odds of at least a statement.

**Big-case score 0.55.** A merits decision would set the undue-hardship burden for every Title VII religious-accommodation case nationwide, but it is a statutory refinement of a three-year-old precedent rather than a headline constitutional case.

**Uncertainty and where to discount me.** My largest uncertainty is how the Justices will read the Ninth Circuit's opinion itself, which I have only through the parties' competing characterizations (the opinion was not provisioned and I did not retrieve its text). My CVSG number is intuition, not a measured conditional rate. Retrieval was thin: CourtListener's RECAP index returned no SCOTUS dockets for the related circuit cases, so I could not confirm whether any companion petition (for example from *Rodrique* or *Kluge*) is pending or was denied, which would move this forecast in either direction. The `fedcourts query` priors returned were not topically similar (no subject filter exists for SCOTUS rows) and did not inform the number. I do not know this petition's outcome; nothing I retrieved disclosed it.
