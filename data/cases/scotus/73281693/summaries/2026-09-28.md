## In brief

Firefighters denied a religious exemption from a COVID vaccine mandate ask the Court to revive their lawsuit against their employer.

## What happened

David Petersen and seven other firefighter-paramedics at Snohomish Regional Fire and Rescue (SRFR) requested religious exemptions from a 2021 COVID-19 vaccination mandate. SRFR denied them patient-care roles, placing them on unpaid leave for about seven months, though it later allowed unvaccinated firefighters back with safety protocols. The firefighters sued under Title VII, the federal law requiring employers to accommodate religious beliefs unless doing so would cause undue hardship. A district court granted summary judgment to SRFR, and the Ninth Circuit affirmed, finding SRFR faced real health, safety, and financial costs. The Ninth Circuit denied rehearing.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked whether an employer can defeat a religious-accommodation claim by showing only that it reasonably believed an accommodation would cause serious hardship, or whether the employer must show the hardship would actually have occurred. The firefighters argue the Ninth Circuit's approach lets employers avoid liability based on good-faith but mistaken beliefs, while SRFR argues the lower courts required proof of actual, not merely feared, hardship.

## Where it stands

The case was distributed for conference twice, most recently for September 28, 2026, after full briefing including a brief in opposition and a reply. The docket does not yet show any decision on the petition.

## What each outcome would mean

The firefighters, as petitioners, are asking the Court to hear the case. If the Court grants review, it would hear full arguments and decide what standard applies to employers claiming undue hardship, without yet deciding who wins. If the Court denies review, the Ninth Circuit's decision affirming summary judgment for SRFR stands, leaving the firefighters' claims dismissed. A denial would not mean the Court agrees the Ninth Circuit was correct, only that the lower court's ruling remains in place.
