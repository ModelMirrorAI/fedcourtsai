## In brief

Eight firefighters ask the Court to revive their religious-accommodation lawsuit against the fire department that placed them on leave during a COVID-19 vaccine mandate.

## What happened

David Petersen and seven other firefighters at Snohomish Regional Fire and Rescue (SRFR) requested religious exemptions from a 2021 COVID-19 vaccination mandate. SRFR denied accommodations that would let them keep working unvaccinated, citing health, safety, contract, and liability concerns, and placed them on unpaid leave for about seven months. The firefighters sued under Title VII, the federal law requiring employers to accommodate religious practice unless doing so would cause undue hardship. The district court granted summary judgment to SRFR, and the Ninth Circuit affirmed.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked to decide what an employer must show to prove that accommodating an employee's religious beliefs would cause undue hardship: whether it is enough for the employer to show it reasonably believed, even if mistakenly, that hardship would result, or whether the employer must show the hardship would actually have occurred.

## Where it stands

The petition has been distributed for conference twice, most recently for September 28, 2026, after full briefing including a brief in opposition, a reply, and supplemental briefs from both sides filed in late September 2026. The docket does not record any decision on the petition since that conference.

## What each outcome would mean

The firefighters, as petitioners, are asking the Court to take up their case. If the Court grants review, it would hear the case fully and decide which legal standard applies, without yet deciding who wins. If the Court denies review, the Ninth Circuit's decision affirming summary judgment for SRFR stands, leaving the firefighters' claims dismissed. Denial would not mean the Court agrees with the Ninth Circuit's reasoning, only that its decision remains in place.
