## In brief

The Court declined to hear eight firefighters' bid to revive their religious-accommodation lawsuit against a fire department over a COVID-19 vaccine mandate.

## What happened

Eight firefighters at Snohomish Regional Fire and Rescue sought religious exemptions from a COVID-19 vaccination mandate imposed in 2021. The department placed them on leave rather than let them continue in patient-care roles unvaccinated, though it later reversed course in 2022. The firefighters sued under Title VII, the federal law requiring employers to accommodate religious beliefs unless doing so would cause undue hardship. A district court granted summary judgment to the department, and the Ninth Circuit affirmed, finding undisputed medical evidence showed unvaccinated firefighters posed a greater risk of spreading the virus.

## What the Court is being asked

The firefighters asked the Court to decide what an employer must prove to deny a religious accommodation: whether it is enough for the employer to show a reasonable basis for fearing the accommodation would cause hardship, or whether the employer must show the hardship would actually occur. They argued this question divides the appeals courts, with some circuits requiring proof of actual hardship and others accepting a reasonable, good-faith belief even if mistaken. The department answered that no real conflict exists and that the lower court found actual, not merely feared, hardship here.

## Where it stands

The petition was distributed for conference twice, after extended briefing and numerous amicus filings and supplemental briefs from both sides. On October 5, 2026, the Court denied the petition.

## What each outcome would mean

The firefighters, as petitioners, asked the Court to take up their case and overturn the Ninth Circuit's ruling for the department. By denying the petition, the Court leaves the Ninth Circuit's decision in place, meaning the lower court's judgment against the firefighters stands and their lawsuit does not proceed further. The denial does not mean the Court agreed the Ninth Circuit was correct; it simply leaves that ruling undisturbed.
