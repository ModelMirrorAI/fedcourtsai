## What happened

David Petersen and seven other firefighters at Snohomish Regional Fire and Rescue (SRFR) in Washington asked for religious exemptions from a COVID-19 vaccine mandate. SRFR denied the requests, offering instead unpaid leave, and the firefighters sued under Title VII, the federal law requiring employers to accommodate workers' religious practices unless doing so would cause "undue hardship." A district court granted summary judgment to SRFR, finding undisputed medical evidence showed unvaccinated firefighters posed a greater risk of spreading the virus. The Ninth Circuit affirmed. The firefighters then asked the Supreme Court to review that decision.

## What the Court is being asked

The firefighters argue employers should only win this kind of case by proving an accommodation would truly have caused serious harm to the business, not merely by showing they had a reasonable, good-faith belief that it might. They say the Ninth Circuit's ruling lets employers avoid liability based on reasonable-seeming but possibly mistaken judgments, splitting from other appeals courts that require actual proof of hardship. SRFR responds that the Ninth Circuit did require proof of real, substantial harm here, based on unrebutted medical evidence, and that no genuine split exists once the cases are read carefully.

## Where it stands

The petition has been fully briefed, including numerous amicus briefs on both sides, and was distributed for the Court's conference on September 28, 2026, where the Justices will decide whether to take up the case.
