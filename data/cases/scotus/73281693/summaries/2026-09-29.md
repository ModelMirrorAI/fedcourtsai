## In brief

Eight firefighters ask the Court to revive their religious-accommodation lawsuit against their employer over a COVID-19 vaccine mandate.

## What happened

David Petersen and seven other firefighter-paramedics at Snohomish Regional Fire and Rescue (SRFR) sought religious exemptions from a 2021 COVID-19 vaccination mandate. SRFR placed them on unpaid leave rather than let them keep working patient-care roles unvaccinated, citing health, contract, and liability risks, though it reversed course about seven months later. The firefighters sued under Title VII, the federal job-discrimination law's religious accommodation provisions. The district court granted SRFR summary judgment, and the Ninth Circuit affirmed, agreeing SRFR had shown the accommodation would cause undue hardship.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked whether an employer can avoid liability for denying a religious accommodation just by showing it reasonably believed the accommodation would cause serious difficulty for its operations, or whether the employer must instead show the accommodation would actually have caused that difficulty. The firefighters argue courts of appeals are split on this question; SRFR argues the split is not real and that the lower court found actual, not just feared, hardship.

## Where it stands

The petition has been distributed for conference twice, most recently September 28, 2026, after full briefing including a reply and supplemental briefs from both sides filed September 29, 2026. The docket shows no action recorded on the petition since that conference.

## What each outcome would mean

Petersen and the other firefighters, who lost in the lower courts, are asking the Court to take up their case. If the Court grants the petition, it will hear full arguments and decide which legal standard applies, without yet deciding who wins. If it denies the petition, the Ninth Circuit's ruling for SRFR stands, leaving the firefighters' claims rejected. A denial would not mean the Court agrees the lower court was right, only that its decision remains in place.
