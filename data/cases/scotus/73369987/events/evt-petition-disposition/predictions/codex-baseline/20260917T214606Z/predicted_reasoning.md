# Forecast of the Court's action

The Court will deny the petition for certiorari. The probability of any grant,
including a GVR, partial grant, or summary reversal, is 0.5%.

The record shows **one distribution**, for the September 28, 2026 conference,
and no previous relist. I expect **zero further distributions**, with denial
on an order list in late September or early October 2026 following that
conference. I assign 4% to at least one additional distribution; within that
less likely branch, one additional distribution is more likely than a long
sequence. This timing is a forecast, not a retrieved order-list schedule.

The Court will not call for the Solicitor General's views. The probability of
a new CVSG is 0.1%. The case presents a private parenting-time dispute rather
than an identified federal governmental interest requiring the SG's advice.

If the Court grants review, the likely question is a narrowed version of the
petition's combined questions: whether applying Colorado's best-interests
parenting-time factors to reject a motion for modification violates the
Fourteenth Amendment's due-process requirement because those factors lack
objective evaluative criteria. I do not expect the Court to accept a general
guarantee of equal parenting time or resolve every asserted defect in the
family's earlier proceedings.

Conditional on a grant, **plenary review is more likely than summary
disposition**: 85% plenary versus 15% summary. The supplied record identifies
no intervening decision that makes a GVR the natural next step, and the
requested constitutional extension is not an obvious summary application of
a directly controlling holding.

Conditional on denial, I expect no noted dissent or statement respecting
denial; the probability of any such writing is 1.5%. An ordinary denial will
not produce a majority merits opinion or resolve the constitutional validity
of the parenting factors. The anticipated selection rationale is that this
unpublished, procedurally complicated dispute presents no demonstrated
appellate conflict warranting review, not an announced merits holding that
the challenged factors are constitutional.
