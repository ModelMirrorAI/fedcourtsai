# Forecast: what the Court will do with No. 25-1296, Robinson v. Freeman

**Disposition.** The Court will deny the petition on the order list following the
September 28, 2026 conference (the long conference), most likely the first order
list of October Term 2026, without calling for a response and without comment.

**Relists.** The docket shows **one distribution** (July 8, 2026, for the
September 28 conference) and no relist. I expect **no further distribution**: this
is a pro se paid petition from an unpublished state intermediate-court decision,
with no brief in opposition on file, no amicus support, and a question no Justice
has signalled interest in. The small residual chance of a second distribution is
mostly a bulk reschedule out of the long conference rather than a merits relist.

**CVSG.** None. No federal party, no federal statute, no federal program is
implicated; the challenged law is a Colorado domestic-relations statute and the
respondent is a private party. The Court will not call for the Solicitor
General's views.

**Which question, if any.** If the Court took anything it would have to
reformulate: the petition's four "Questions to Review" collapse into one, whether
Colorado's statutory best-interests factors (C.R.S. 14-10-124(1.5)(a)) are
unconstitutionally vague under the Fourteenth Amendment's Due Process Clause.
The subsidiary question (whether a motion may be summarily denied for not
addressing the factors) is a state procedural ruling under C.R.C.P. 7(b)(1) that
the Court could not reach. I expect the Court to take neither.

**Summary disposition versus plenary review.** Conditional on any grant, a
cert-stage disposition (GVR) is the less likely route here because no intervening
decision of this Court bears on the vagueness of state custody factors; a grant
would have to be plenary. But the conditional is nearly vacuous: I do not expect a
grant of either kind.

**Separate writings on denial.** None. No Justice will note a dissent or file a
statement respecting the denial. The petitioner's two earlier petitions from the
same custody dispute (Nos. 19-356 and 23-1244, both recorded in the petition as
denied) drew none, and nothing in this petition changes that.

**Conditional merits view.** If, against expectation, the Court granted plenary
review, the likely ground for decision would be that a "best interests of the
child" standard guided by enumerated factors is not void for vagueness in a civil
custody setting, affirming the judgment below, quite possibly with a threshold
holding that the state procedural ground (the particularity ruling) is adequate
and independent.
