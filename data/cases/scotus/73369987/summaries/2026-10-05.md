## In brief

A father's petition asking the Court to strike down Colorado's child-custody factors as unconstitutionally vague was denied.

## What happened

Samuel Collin Robinson and Katherine Lyman Freeman divorced, and parenting time for their children was later set by a Colorado district court under a plan giving Freeman most of the time. Robinson filed a motion to modify parenting time, arguing the controlling factors should not control because Colorado's statutory "best interests" factors lack objective standards. A magistrate denied the motion without a hearing for failing to address the statutory factors, and the district court upheld that order. The Colorado Court of Appeals affirmed, and the Colorado Supreme Court declined further review.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked whether a state custody law that lists factors for judges to weigh, without spelling out how to measure or apply them, is so vague that it denies parents fair notice of what is required, violating constitutional guarantees of fair legal process. Robinson also asks whether a modification request can be rejected simply for not restating those factors.

## Where it stands

The petition was distributed for the Court's September 28, 2026 conference, and the Court denied it on October 5, 2026.

## What each outcome would mean

Robinson, the father, asked the Court to take up his case and overturn the Colorado Court of Appeals' decision upholding the denial of his motion. The denial means the Court will not hear the case, leaving the Colorado Court of Appeals' ruling in place. That ruling upheld the lower courts' rejection of Robinson's modification request and left the existing parenting-time arrangement, favoring Freeman, undisturbed. The denial does not mean the Court agreed the statute is constitutional, only that it leaves the lower court's decision standing.
