## In brief

A father asks the Court to review a Colorado child-custody decision, arguing the state's parenting-time law is unconstitutionally vague.

## What happened

Samuel Collin Robinson and Katherine Lyman Freeman divorced in 2012, and a parenting plan has since given Freeman most parenting time with their children. In 2024 Robinson asked the Colorado district court to modify the plan; a magistrate denied the motion without a hearing because it did not address statutory best-interest factors, and the judge upheld that ruling. The Colorado Court of Appeals affirmed, rejecting Robinson's argument that the factors are too vague to apply. The Colorado Supreme Court declined further review.

## What the Court is being asked

Robinson asks the Court to decide whether Colorado's list of factors judges must weigh in dividing parenting time is so vague, for lack of clear standards, that it fails to tell parents what is required and permits arbitrary rulings, and whether a modification request can be denied simply for not addressing those factors point by point.

## Where it stands

The petition was distributed for the Court's conference on September 28, 2026, the case's current stage. No decision on the petition has yet been recorded.

## What each outcome would mean

Robinson is asking the Court to take up his case. If the Court grants review, it would hear full arguments and decide whether the parenting-time factors are unconstitutionally vague, without yet deciding who wins. If it denies review, the Colorado Court of Appeals ruling upholding the denial of Robinson's modification motion and the existing parenting plan favoring Freeman would remain in place, though a denial would not mean the Court agreed with that ruling.
