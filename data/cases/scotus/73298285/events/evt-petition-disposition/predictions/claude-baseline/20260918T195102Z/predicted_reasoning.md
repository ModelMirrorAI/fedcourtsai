# Forecast: what the Court will do with No. 25-1250, Sigalovskaya v. Braden

**Bottom line.** The petition will be denied, most likely on the order list following the September 28, 2026 conference or after one or two further relists. P(any grant, GVR included) = 0.07.

## Where the docket stands

The snapshot shows two distribution entries (for the June 25, 2026 and September 28, 2026 conferences) and no CVSG. The first distribution never produced a conference vote: the Solicitor General waived, the Court called for a response on June 17, 2026, and the petition was pulled off the June 25 conference until the brief in opposition arrived on August 26. So the petition has been relisted zero times in the true sense; the September 28 long conference is its first real consideration. I forecast from that state.

## Relists

Roughly even odds (0.50) that the docket records at least one more distribution. Two routes get there. First, the Court may hold this petition for *Nielsen v. Watanabe*, No. 25-417 (cert granted June 22, 2026, argument set for November 9, 2026), which asks whether an alternative remedial scheme places a claim in a new Bivens context at step one, the question Judge Pérez's concurrence rested on below. A held petition sits without action and is redistributed once the lead case is decided, which would register as a further distribution in spring 2027. I put the hold at about 0.30; the SG argues against a hold because only one of the two concurring judges relied on the alternative-remedy ground. Second, a call-for-response petition from the long conference with a dissent below and a possible separate writing draws a relist more often than the ordinary paid petition; conditional on no hold I put one or more relists at about 0.30. If denied without a hold, expect denial on the October 5, 2026 order list or within two conferences after.

## CVSG

None. The United States is already the respondent through the Solicitor General; the Court does not invite the views of a party. P = 0.01.

## Which question, if any

If the Court granted plenary review it would take a reformulated version of question 1, framed as the SG frames it: whether a Fourth Amendment false-arrest claim resting on fabricated evidence arises in a new Bivens context. It would not take question 2 as written, because the panel's per curiam adopted no rationale and the alternative-remedy point lives only in a single concurrence; the Court would fold that issue into *Nielsen* instead. Plenary review is the less likely grant route (see below).

## Summary disposition versus plenary review

Conditional on any grant, a summary route is likelier than argument: P(GVR or judgment in the cert order | grant) = 0.60. The realistic grant path is a hold for *Nielsen* followed by a GVR if *Nielsen* comes out in a way that undermines the alternative-remedy-at-step-one reasoning. That requires the plaintiff side to prevail in *Nielsen*, which I put near 0.25 given the Court's uniform post-*Carlson* record against Bivens plaintiffs, and then a GVR rather than a denial given the panel's fractured rationale. A plenary grant on a plaintiff-side Bivens petition, where the government prevailed below and the SG opposes, has not happened under the current Court; *Egbert*, *Hernández*, *Abbasi*, *Goldey*, and the *Nielsen* grant itself all came on the government's side. A per curiam summary reversal in petitioner's favor is not in play.

## Dissent from denial

Conditional on denial, P(a noted dissent or statement respecting denial) = 0.20. The candidates are Justice Sotomayor or Justice Jackson writing that this case is Bivens on its own facts, possibly joined by Justice Kagan; the call for response after the government's waiver is consistent with a chambers taking a closer look. Against that, the Court denied *Mohamud v. Weyker*, No. 25-760, a fabricated-evidence Bivens petition, earlier in 2026, and plaintiff-side Bivens denials have mostly gone without writing. A statement from Justice Gorsuch or Justice Thomas urging that Bivens be overruled outright is possible but less likely on a denial where the government won.

## If granted (conditional only)

If the Court took the case for argument, the likely ground would be that a claim whose gravamen is fabrication of evidence forwarded to prosecutors differs meaningfully from the warrantless entry and arrest in *Bivens*, affirming the Second Circuit, most plausibly six to three along the usual Bivens lines. A GVR would carry no reasoning beyond a citation to *Nielsen*.
