## In brief

A woman whose false-arrest lawsuit against a federal agent was dismissed asks the Court to revive her claim for damages under a judge-made remedy called Bivens.

## What happened

Karina Sigalovskaya sued a Homeland Security Investigations agent, Abigail Braden, claiming Braden fabricated a confession that led to Sigalovskaya's arrest and weeks of detention after agents searched her home without a warrant. A jury trial was coming when the Supreme Court decided a case called Egbert v. Boule, limiting when people can sue federal officers for constitutional violations under Bivens. Citing that decision, the district court dismissed her remaining claims. A divided Second Circuit panel affirmed, with three judges writing three different opinions and no majority reasoning. The full appeals court declined to rehear the case.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked whether people may sue a federal officer for damages when the officer, during a warrantless home search, fabricates evidence and uses it to make an arrest without probable cause, given that courts have allowed such suits in closely related situations before. The Court is also asked whether the mere existence of an alternative complaint or grievance process against the officer can block such a suit, even when the claim otherwise closely resembles one the Court has already allowed.

## Where it stands

The case has been distributed for conference more than once, and the federal government, representing Agent Braden, has filed a brief opposing review. The petitioner has filed a reply brief. The docket shows no decision yet on whether the Court will hear the case.

## What each outcome would mean

Sigalovskaya, as petitioner, is asking the Court to take up her case. If the Court grants review, it would hear full arguments and decide whether her lawsuit can proceed, deciding nothing about who wins until it rules. If the Court denies review, the Second Circuit's decision affirming dismissal of her claim stands, leaving her without a damages remedy in this lawsuit, though a denial would not mean the Supreme Court agrees the lower court was right. The petition also asks, as an alternative, that the Court hold the case until it decides a related pending case, Nielsen v. Watanabe.
