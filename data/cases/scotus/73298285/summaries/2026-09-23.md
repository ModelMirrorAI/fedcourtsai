## What happened

In 2013, Department of Homeland Security agents searched Karina Sigalovskaya's home without a warrant while looking for her husband, who was later convicted of child sexual exploitation crimes. Sigalovskaya alleges that Special Agent Abigail Braden falsely claimed Sigalovskaya had confessed to a crime and passed that fabricated account to prosecutors, leading to her arrest and three weeks in custody before charges were dropped. Sigalovskaya sued Braden for damages, but lower courts dismissed her false-arrest claim, holding that a lawsuit for money damages against a federal officer is not available on these facts. A divided Second Circuit panel affirmed in a brief order, with three judges writing separate opinions disagreeing on the reasoning.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked whether a longstanding line of cases lets someone sue a federal officer personally for money damages when the officer allegedly made up evidence during a warrantless home search and used it to justify an arrest without adequate grounds. A second question asks whether the fact that an alternative complaint process exists (such as an internal government review) is by itself enough to block such a lawsuit, even when the claim otherwise closely resembles one that courts have already allowed.

## Where it stands

The petition has been distributed for conference twice, most recently for September 28, 2026, after the federal government (as respondent) filed a brief opposing review and Sigalovskaya filed a reply. The case remains awaiting the Court's decision on whether to grant certiorari, the vote to hear the case.
