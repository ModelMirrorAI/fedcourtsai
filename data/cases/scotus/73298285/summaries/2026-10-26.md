## In brief

Karina Sigalovskaya asks the Court to revive her damages lawsuit against a federal agent, claiming the agent fabricated evidence to justify arresting her without a warrant.

## What happened

Sigalovskaya alleges that Homeland Security Investigations agents entered her home without a warrant in 2013, searched it, and separated her from her children while looking for her husband. She says Special Agent Abigail Braden then falsely claimed Sigalovskaya had confessed to a crime and forwarded that claim to prosecutors, leading to her arrest and three weeks of detention before charges were dropped. She sued Braden and other agents for damages under a Supreme Court precedent, Bivens, that allows such suits in limited circumstances. The district court dismissed her remaining claims, and a divided Second Circuit panel affirmed, with each judge writing a separate opinion offering different reasoning.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked whether its precedent allowing damages suits against federal officers for certain constitutional violations extends to a claim that an officer fabricated evidence to justify an arrest made without a warrant, and whether the existence of some other complaint process for misconduct by itself prevents such a suit from going forward even where the claim otherwise closely resembles one the Court has already approved.

## Where it stands

The petition was distributed for conference twice, was the subject of a request for the government's response, and has since been fully briefed, including a reply from Sigalovskaya filed in September 2026. The docket shows no decision yet on whether the Court will hear the case.

## What each outcome would mean

Sigalovskaya is asking the Court to take up her case and reverse the Second Circuit's decision, which went against her. If the Court grants review, it would hear full arguments on whether her damages claim can proceed and would decide the legal question itself; a grant would not by itself decide who wins. If the Court denies review, the Second Circuit's decision stands, leaving Sigalovskaya's claim against Braden dismissed.
