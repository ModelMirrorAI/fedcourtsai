# Forecast: F.E.B. Corp. v. United States, No. 25-1294 (cert petition)

**Disposition.** The Court will deny the petition on the order list following the September 28, 2026 conference (the first regular order list of October Term 2026). No grant, no GVR, no summary reversal.

**Relists.** The docket shows **one** distribution (June 24, 2026, for the conference of September 28, 2026) and has never been relisted or rescheduled. I forecast no further distribution: the petition is decided at its first conference. The main way this fails is a call for a response after the government's waiver, which would push the petition to a later conference; I put that plus an ordinary reschedule or relist at about one chance in seven.

**CVSG.** None. The United States is the respondent and the Solicitor General has already spoken by waiving a response, so there is no Solicitor General whose views could be called for. A call for a *response* from the SG is the only form of government-facing order available, and I do not expect it.

**Question presented, if taken.** If the Court were to grant, it would not take either question as written. QP 1 asks whether the Court "should address" Justice Blackmun's Anderson concurrence, which is not a question of law, and QP 2 asks whether "plausible" is the correct Rule 52(a) standard in a historical-document case, which is a re-description of Anderson's own holding. A grant would have to be reformulated as: whether clear-error review under Rule 52(a) applies to findings resting entirely on documentary evidence. Anderson answered yes, and the Court has reaffirmed deference to documentary findings since, so there is no realistic reformulation the Court would find worth its time.

**Summary disposition.** Not the likelier route to anything. No intervening decision of this Court bears on Rule 52(a) or Submerged Lands Act section 1313(a), so a GVR has no hook, and a summary reversal on a fact-bound clear-error dispute is not how the Court behaves. Conditional on the very unlikely grant, plenary review is the more probable route, though the base share of grants that end in the cert order itself keeps the conditional figure well above zero.

**Dissent from denial.** None expected. No Justice has signaled interest in narrowing Anderson, the case was decided per curiam in an unpublished opinion, no amicus has appeared, and the government did not bother to respond. I expect a bare denial with no separate writing.
