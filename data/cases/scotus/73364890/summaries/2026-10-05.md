## In brief

The Court declined to hear F.E.B. Corp.'s bid to overturn a ruling that the United States owns Wisteria Island near Key West.

## What happened

F.E.B. Corp. and the United States have disputed ownership of Wisteria Island, formed from dredged material in the 1920s and 1940s, across several rounds of litigation. A federal law gives states title to submerged coastal lands but excepts land the United States filled in or built up for its own use. After a trial, a district court found the government created the island intentionally, for uses including spoil storage and harbor protection, and awarded it title. The Eleventh Circuit affirmed, applying a deferential "clearly erroneous" standard of review to those factual findings and calling them "plausible" and supported by the record. F.E.B. then asked the Supreme Court to review that decision.

## What the Court is being asked

F.E.B. asked the Court to reconsider how much deference appeals courts owe to a trial judge's factual findings when a case rests entirely on historical documents rather than live witness testimony and credibility calls. F.E.B. argued that a more searching standard of review, rather than simply asking whether the findings were "plausible," should apply in deciding whether the government built up the island for its own use.

## Where it stands

The United States waived its right to respond to the petition. The case was distributed for the Court's September 28, 2026 conference, and on October 5, 2026 the Court denied the petition.

## What each outcome would mean

F.E.B. Corp., which lost in the lower courts, asked the Court to take up its case. By denying the petition, the Court leaves the Eleventh Circuit's decision in place, meaning the United States retains title to Wisteria Island under the lower court's findings and the deferential review standard it applied. The denial does not mean the Supreme Court agreed with that standard or with the lower court's factual findings; it simply leaves those rulings undisturbed without deciding the underlying legal question F.E.B. raised.
