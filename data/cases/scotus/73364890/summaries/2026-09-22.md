## What happened

F.E.B. Corp. and the United States have spent over a decade disputing who owns Wisteria Island, a small island off Key West formed from dredged material the government deposited in the 1920s and 1940s. Under a federal law, states generally own submerged coastal lands, but the government keeps land it filled in or built up "for its own use." After earlier appeals, the Eleventh Circuit sent the case back for a trial on why the government created the island. Following a bench trial, the district court found the government intentionally built up the island for its own purposes, including storing dredge spoil and protecting naval operations. The Eleventh Circuit affirmed, calling the district court's findings on this historical-document record "plausible" and applying a deferential standard of review. F.E.B.'s request for rehearing was denied.

## What the Court is being asked

F.E.B. argues that when a lower court's factual findings rest only on historical documents rather than live testimony or credibility calls, appeals courts should look more closely at those findings rather than simply deferring to the trial court under the usual deferential rule for factual findings. The Court is asked whether that ordinary deferential rule should still apply in a case built entirely on documents, and whether "plausible" is the right test for reviewing a trial court's conclusions about the government's intent in creating the island under the land-ownership exception at issue.
