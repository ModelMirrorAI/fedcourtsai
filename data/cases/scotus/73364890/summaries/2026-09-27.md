## In brief

F.E.B. Corp. asks the Court to hear its dispute with the federal government over ownership of Wisteria Island near Key West, Florida.

## What happened

F.E.B. Corp. claims ownership of Wisteria Island, an island off Key West created decades ago from dredged material. The United States sued to quiet title, arguing it built up the island for its own use, which under a federal statute would keep ownership with the government rather than transferring to the state and, eventually, to F.E.B. After a bench trial, a federal district court found the United States intentionally created the island for naval purposes, including storing dredged material and protecting the harbor. The United States Court of Appeals for the Eleventh Circuit affirmed, holding that the district court's findings were "plausible" and supported by the record, and applying a deferential standard of review to those findings. F.E.B. then petitioned the Supreme Court.

## What the Court is being asked

F.E.B. asks the Court to reconsider how much deference appeals courts must give to a trial court's factual findings when a case turns entirely on historical documents rather than witness testimony or credibility. F.E.B. also asks whether "plausible" is the right test for reviewing such findings when applying the federal law governing which lands built up by the government for its own use remain federal property rather than passing to the states.

## Where it stands

The petition and supporting documents were filed, the government waived its right to respond, and the case was distributed for the Court's conference on September 28, 2026. The docket shows no action since that distribution.

## What each outcome would mean

F.E.B. Corp., which lost in the Eleventh Circuit, is asking the Court to take up the case. If the Court grants the petition, it will hear full arguments and decide the standard of review question itself, without yet deciding who owns the island. If the Court denies the petition, the Eleventh Circuit's decision stands, leaving the island's ownership with the United States as the lower courts determined.
