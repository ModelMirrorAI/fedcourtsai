Henry Watson asked the Court to review his Wisconsin homicide conviction, but the Court declined to hear the case.

## What happened

Henry Watson was convicted in Wisconsin state court of homicide by negligent handling of a dangerous weapon, after a bullet he fired at a man who allegedly threatened him instead killed a bystander. The jury acquitted him of a related charge involving the man he says threatened him, finding he acted in self-defense, but convicted him on the count involving the bystander's death. Wisconsin courts, including the court of appeals, held that state law barred a self-defense claim for that count. Federal courts then denied Watson's request for habeas relief, a procedure that lets state prisoners challenge convictions in federal court, and the Seventh Circuit declined to let him appeal further.

## What the Court is being asked

Watson asks the Court to decide whether the jury instructions and the state law were applied in a way that unconstitutionally prevented the jury from considering his self-defense claim and relieved the state of its burden to disprove self-defense, and whether his trial lawyer's handling of the case amounted to constitutionally inadequate representation.

## Where it stands
