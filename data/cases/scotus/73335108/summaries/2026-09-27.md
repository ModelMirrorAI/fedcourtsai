A man convicted of a Wisconsin homicide charge asks the Court to review his conviction, arguing the jury instructions and his trial were constitutionally flawed.

## What happened

Henry Watson was charged after a 2020 shooting in a Milwaukee parking lot. Watson says he fired at a man who threatened and pointed a gun at him, but a stray bullet killed a bystander. A jury acquitted Watson of injuring the man he says threatened him, finding he acted in self-defense, but convicted him of a lesser homicide charge over the bystander's death. Wisconsin courts affirmed the conviction, ruling that state law barred a self-defense claim for that charge. Watson then sought federal habeas relief, arguing this violated his constitutional rights; a federal district court and the Seventh Circuit Court of Appeals both denied relief.

## What the Court is being asked

Watson asks the Court to decide whether the jury instructions given at his trial, combined with the courts' later interpretation of the self-defense statute, unfairly prevented the jury from considering his self-defense claim and improperly shifted the burden of proof to him rather than the prosecution. He also asks whether his trial lawyer's handling of post-verdict motions amounted to constitutionally inadequate representation, and whether these problems together violated his right to due process.
