# Forecast: what the Court will do with No. 25-1279, Watson v. Mason

**Disposition.** The petition will be denied at or shortly after the September 28, 2026 conference, in the order list that follows the long conference. No order beyond the one-line denial.

**Relists.** The docket shows one distribution (June 17, 2026, for the conference of September 28, 2026) and no relists. I expect no further distribution: the petition will be disposed of at its first conference. The only realistic path to a second distribution is a clerical reschedule off the long conference, not a substantive relist.

**CVSG.** None. The case is a state criminal prosecution on federal habeas; no federal statute or federal party is involved and nothing gives the Solicitor General a stake.

**Which question presented, if any were taken.** None as written. The four questions are entangled with Wisconsin law: whether Wis. Stat. § 939.48(3) bars self-defense as to an unintended third-party victim, and whether Wisconsin Jury Instruction 820 as given contradicted that reading. If the Court were to take anything, it would have to reformulate around QP 1's burden-of-proof strand (whether, once a court instructs that the State must disprove self-defense, a post-verdict state-law ruling that the defense was unavailable relieves the State of a burden due process required it to carry). Even that reformulation runs into the AEDPA posture, the unpublished COA denial, and a record that turns on how the instruction was actually worded for Count 1.

**Summary disposition versus plenary review.** Conditional on any grant, a summary route (GVR or per curiam) is about as likely as plenary review, because a case this fact-bound would not be set for argument unless the Court saw a clean legal question, and no intervening decision exists to GVR in light of. Unconditionally, a GVR is remote: I know of no pending or recently decided case on the burden of proof for self-defense or on § 939.48(3) that would prompt one.

**Dissent or statement respecting denial.** None expected. A pro se, non-capital habeas petition from an unpublished COA denial, with the State having waived its response, is the profile that draws no separate writing.

**If granted (conditional only).** The likely ground would be Mullaney/Winship burden allocation: that the State cannot obtain a conviction while the jury was told the State bore a burden it was then excused from carrying. Lockett and Eddings would play no role, since they govern capital sentencing, not guilt-phase instructions in a non-capital case.
