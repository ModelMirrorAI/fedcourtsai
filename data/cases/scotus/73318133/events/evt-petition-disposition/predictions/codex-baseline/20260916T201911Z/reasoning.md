I reduce that roughly 5.1% reference probability to 0.3% P(any grant). The strongest reason is the gap between the questions' sweeping ambitions and the apparent issue actually adjudicated. Questions 1-7 and 9 seek constitutional, criminal-penalty, corporate-structure, funding, and nationwide-injunction answers without demonstrating an outcome-determinative appellate conflict. The petition's account of the Ninth Circuit ruling instead contests reasonable cause, corroboration, declarations, and the characterization of the employee's complaints. Its objection to the description of the suit as a diversity action does not itself establish a disputed rule of federal law. The reasons-for-granting section, beginning at printed page 37, argues national importance through alleged safety and regulatory failures rather than presenting a developed circuit split tied to the judgment.

Supreme Court Rule 10, consulted through Cornell LII's reproduction, distinguishes compelling review grounds from factual-error correction or misapplication of an otherwise correctly stated rule. That is the central adverse vehicle consideration here. The unpublished decisions and absence of any affirmative attention signal beyond ordinary distribution reinforce it; neither alone decides the forecast. Pro se status is not an independent reason to deny review. The small remaining grant probability allows for a material legal error or preserved issue that the unprovided lower opinions might reveal.
