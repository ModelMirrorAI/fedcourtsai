# Forecast: what the Court will do with Trice v. Texas, No. 25-1195

**Disposition.** The Court will deny the petition, most likely on the order list following the September 28, 2026 long conference or, if relisted, within one or two conferences after it. P(any grant) = 0.10.

**Relists.** The docket shows two distributions: one for the May 28, 2026 conference (from which the Court called for a response after Texas had waived) and one for the September 28, 2026 conference, with the reply now in. The call for a response means at least one chambers was interested, and that same interest is what produces a relist, so this petition is likelier to be relisted than an ordinary once-distributed paid petition. I still expect the modal path to be a denial straight off the long conference, where the Court clears a summer's worth of CFR'd petitions in bulk. P(distributed at least once more) = 0.35; if relisted, expect one relist, occasionally two, not a long string.

**CVSG.** No call for the views of the Solicitor General. The case is a state prosecution under a state statute, no federal party or program is implicated, and the federal analogue (the continuing-criminal-enterprise statute) was already construed in Richardson. P = 0.02.

**Which question, if taken.** The petition presents a single question: whether Texas Penal Code § 21.02(d), which tells jurors they need not agree on which specific acts of sexual abuse were committed, violates the Sixth and Fourteenth Amendment unanimity right after Ramos v. Louisiana. If the Court granted, it would take that question essentially as written, perhaps reframed as whether the predicate acts of a continuous-course-of-conduct offense are elements on which the jury must be unanimous or mere means under Schad and Richardson. It would not take up the separate juror-coercion issue the state court also decided, which the petition does not present.

**Summary disposition.** Unlikely. There is no intervening decision of this Court to GVR in light of, and a summary reversal is implausible where every court to consider the question (Texas intermediate courts, a Texas federal district court, and the Hawaii Supreme Court in State v. Tran) has upheld such statutes. Conditional on a grant, P(disposed of in the cert order rather than set for argument) = 0.10; the grant scenario is plenary review of a recurring post-Ramos question.

**Separate writing on denial.** Most denials of this kind draw no writing. The one realistic author is Justice Gorsuch, who wrote Ramos and treats unanimity as a historical-tradition right, and who might note that the means-versus-elements question is open after Ramos; Justice Sotomayor is a less likely second. P(some dissent from or statement respecting denial) = 0.15.

**If granted (conditional only).** The likelier merits outcome would still be to uphold the statute, following the Schad plurality's deference to legislative element-definition and Richardson's express dictum distinguishing state child-abuse course-of-conduct statutes; a reversal would rest on Richardson's reasoning that "violations" cannot be relabeled as means where the definition lacks historical support.
