## What happened

John David Trice was convicted in Texas of continuous sexual abuse of a child, along with several related offenses against other victims, and sentenced to 40 years for that count. Texas law lets a jury convict of continuous sexual abuse without agreeing on which specific incidents of abuse occurred, so long as the jury agrees that at least two incidents happened over a 30-day period or longer. Trice argued on appeal that this violates jurors' constitutional right to reach a unanimous verdict. The Texas court of appeals rejected that argument and affirmed his conviction, and the Texas Court of Criminal Appeals declined further review. Trice then asked the Supreme Court to take up the case.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked whether a state law can let a jury convict someone without all jurors agreeing on which specific criminal acts the person committed, so long as the jurors agree the person committed a certain number of such acts over a period of time. Trice argues that a Supreme Court ruling extending jury-unanimity requirements to the states calls this arrangement into doubt. Texas responds that the law only requires unanimous agreement on the overall pattern of abuse, not on each underlying act, and that this approach is consistent with how the Court has treated similar laws in the past.

## Where it stands

The petition has been distributed for conference more than once, and Texas filed a brief opposing review after requesting more time to respond; Trice then filed a reply. The case is awaiting the Court's decision on whether to grant review.
