## In brief

The Supreme Court declined to review a Texas prisoner's challenge to his conviction under a state law letting juries convict of continuous child sexual abuse without agreeing on specific incidents.

## What happened

John David Trice was convicted in Texas state court of continuous sexual abuse of a child and other offenses, receiving a 40-year sentence. Texas law allows a jury to convict of continuous sexual abuse if it unanimously agrees the defendant committed two or more qualifying acts over 30 days, without agreeing on which specific acts occurred. Trice argued this violates the right to a unanimous jury verdict. The Texas Court of Appeals rejected this argument and affirmed his conviction, and the Texas Court of Criminal Appeals refused further review. Trice then asked the U.S. Supreme Court to take the case.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked whether a law allowing jurors to convict of a continuing pattern of child sexual abuse, without all agreeing on which specific incidents took place, violates a defendant's constitutional right to a unanimous jury verdict.

## Where it stands

The petition was distributed for conference twice, and Texas filed a brief opposing review after initially waiving its right to respond. The Court denied the petition on October 5, 2026.

## What each outcome would mean

Trice, the prisoner, asked the Court to take up his case and overturn the Texas appeals court's ruling against him. The denial means the Court will not hear the case, leaving the Texas court's decision in place: Trice's conviction and sentence stand, and the state law's approach to jury unanimity in continuous sexual abuse cases remains unchanged in Texas. The denial does not mean the Court agreed with the lower court's reasoning, only that it left that ruling undisturbed.
