## In brief

The Supreme Court declined to hear a Texas prisoner's challenge to his conviction under a state law letting juries convict without agreeing on specific acts of abuse.

## What happened

John David Trice was convicted in Texas of continuous sexual abuse of a child and other offenses, receiving a forty-year sentence. Texas law allows a jury to convict on this charge without unanimously agreeing on which specific acts of abuse occurred, so long as all jurors agree that at least two acts happened over a 30-day period. Trice argued this violates the right to a unanimous jury verdict. The Texas Court of Appeals affirmed his conviction, and the Texas Court of Criminal Appeals refused further review. Trice then asked the Supreme Court to take up the case.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court was asked whether this kind of statute, which lets a jury convict without agreeing on which specific underlying acts the defendant committed, violates a defendant's constitutional right to a unanimous jury verdict. Trice argued the law improperly treats individual criminal acts as mere means to a broader crime rather than requiring agreement on specific conduct. Texas answered that the law is a permissible way of defining the crime itself, not a unanimity problem.

## Where it stands

The Supreme Court denied the petition on October 5, 2026, after the case had been distributed for two conferences and fully briefed, including a response from Texas and a reply from Trice.
