## In brief

A landowner asks the Court to decide when a property owner can sue over a local land-use denial he says amounts to an uncompensated taking of his property.

## What happened

Garnell Walls owns undeveloped land in Prince George's County, Maryland, where he hoped to build a home using a private well and septic system. County officials told him informally that his property did not qualify for a waiver allowing this, under the county's water and sewer plan, and that he would instead need to pursue a multi-step legislative amendment process. Walls sued, claiming the county's actions amounted to a taking of his property without compensation under the Fifth Amendment. The district court dismissed his claim as not yet ready for review, and the Fourth Circuit Court of Appeals affirmed, holding that Walls had not shown the county reached a final decision because he never pursued the legislative amendment process.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked to decide two things: first, whether a property owner must first ask the local legislature to change the law before a court can hear his claim that the law's application to his land amounts to an uncompensated taking; and second, whether this requirement that a government decision be final before a claim can be heard is a rule about the court's basic power to hear the case at all, or instead a discretionary practice judges may apply or set aside.

## Where it stands

The county waived its right to respond to the petition, but the Court later asked for a response anyway, which the county filed, and Walls has since filed a reply. The case was distributed for the Court's conference of September 28, 2026, and the docket shows no action on the petition since then.

## What each outcome would mean

Walls, who lost in the lower courts, is asking the Court to take up his case. If the Court grants the petition, it will hear full briefing and argument and decide the legal questions about when such property claims may be brought; a grant alone would not determine who wins. If the Court denies the petition, the Fourth Circuit's decision affirming dismissal of Walls's claim stands, meaning his case remains dismissed unless he pursues the county's amendment process. A denial would not mean the Court agrees with the lower court's reasoning, only that the case will not be reviewed.
