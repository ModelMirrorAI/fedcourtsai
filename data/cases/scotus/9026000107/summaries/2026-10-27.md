## In brief

A property owner asks the Court to revive his Fifth Amendment takings claim, which lower courts dismissed as not yet ready for judicial review.

## What happened

Garnell Walls sought county approval to install a well and septic system on undeveloped land he owns in Prince George's County, Maryland, because connecting to public utilities would cost millions of dollars. The county told him it would not approve this without a legislative change to its water and sewer plan. Walls sued, claiming the county's regulations had taken his property without compensation. The district court dismissed the case, finding it was not ready for review because the county had not issued a final decision, and said it would also decline to hear the case for other reasons if it were ready. The Fourth Circuit affirmed, agreeing the claim was premature because Walls had not sought a legislative amendment that might still let him use his property as he wished.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked whether a property owner must first ask a local legislature to change the law before a court can hear a claim that the law took the person's property without compensation. The Court is also asked whether this timing requirement is a strict jurisdictional rule a court must enforce, or a flexible practice a court may choose to apply or not.

## Where it stands

The petition was distributed for conference in September, after which the Court asked for a response; the county filed its opposition, Walls filed a reply, and the case was distributed again for a November conference. No order on the petition has been recorded.

## What each outcome would mean

Walls, the property owner, asks the Court to take up his case and reverse the Fourth Circuit's decision. If the Court grants review and ultimately rules for Walls, his takings claim could go forward in the lower courts despite not having sought a legislative change. If it rules for the county, the requirement that he pursue that process first would stand. If the Court denies review, the Fourth Circuit's decision dismissing Walls' claim as premature remains in place, and Walls would need to pursue the county's legislative process before suing again.
