## In brief

A landowner asks the Court to revive his lawsuit claiming a Maryland county's refusal to let him build on his property amounted to an uncompensated taking of it.

## What happened

Garnell Walls owns vacant land in Prince George's County, Maryland, that he wants to build a home on. Because no public sewer line reaches the property, he needed county permission to install a well and septic system. Walls says county staff told him he likely could not get that permission without first asking the county council to change the applicable water and sewer plan. He sued, claiming the county's regulation took his property without compensation. The district court and the Fourth Circuit both dismissed the case, ruling it was not ready for court because Walls had not pursued a legislative amendment to the plan.

## What the Court is being asked

The case asks two questions. First, whether a property owner must ask a legislature to change the law behind a regulation before a court will hear a claim that the regulation took the owner's property. Second, whether that kind of readiness requirement is a strict jurisdictional rule courts must enforce, or instead a flexible doctrine courts may apply or excuse at their discretion.

## Where it stands

The petition has been distributed for conference, and the Court asked the county to respond, which it did, opposing review; Walls has since filed a reply. The docket shows no conference action on the petition yet.

## What each outcome would mean

Walls, the property owner, is asking the Court to take up his case. If the Court grants review, it would hear full arguments and decide the legal questions, deciding nothing yet about who wins. If it denies review, the Fourth Circuit's decision stands, meaning Walls's lawsuit remains dismissed as not ready for court unless he pursues the county's legislative process first. A denial would not mean the Fourth Circuit was correct, only that its ruling remains in place.
