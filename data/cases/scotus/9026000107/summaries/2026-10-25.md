## In brief

A property owner asks the Court to revive his takings lawsuit, arguing courts wrongly required him to seek a legislative change before suing.

## What happened

Garnell Walls sued Prince George's County after it would not let him install an interim well and septic system on vacant land he has owned since 1978, arguing this amounted to taking his property without compensation. A federal district court dismissed the suit as not yet ready for review, and the Fourth Circuit agreed, holding Walls had not shown the county reached a final decision because he could still seek a change in his property's water-and-sewer classification through the county council. The county disputes none of the underlying facts but defends that reasoning.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked whether a property owner must first ask a local legislature to change the law before a court can hear his claim that the law already took his property unconstitutionally. It is also asked whether this timing requirement is a strict jurisdictional rule a court must enforce, or a flexible practice a court may apply or excuse at its discretion.

## Where it stands

The petition has been distributed for conference twice, most recently for November 6, 2026, after the county filed a brief opposing review and Walls filed a reply; the docket shows no decision yet on whether the Court will hear the case.

## What each outcome would mean

Walls is asking the Court to reverse the Fourth Circuit's decision. If the Court grants review, it would hear the case and decide these questions itself, without yet deciding who wins. If it denies review, the Fourth Circuit's decision stands, leaving Walls's lawsuit dismissed unless he pursues the county's legislative process first. A decision to instead send the case back for another look, without resolving the questions, would leave the matter unsettled for further proceedings below.
