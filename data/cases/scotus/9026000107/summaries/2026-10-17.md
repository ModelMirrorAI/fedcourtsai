## In brief

A property owner asks the Court to revive his takings lawsuit after courts ruled his claim premature because he had not sought a local legislative change.

## What happened

Garnell Walls wanted to build a home using a well and septic system on land he has owned since 1978, but Prince George's County's water and sewer rules required county approval. After county staff told him approval would not be granted, Walls sued, claiming the county's regulation amounted to an uncompensated taking of his property. A federal district court dismissed the case, finding Walls had not shown the county reached a final decision, partly because he could still seek a legislative amendment reclassifying his lot. The Fourth Circuit affirmed, agreeing the claim was not yet ready for court.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked whether a property owner must first ask a local legislature to change the law before a court will hear his claim that the law took his property without compensation, and separately, whether courts may treat this "not yet ready for court" requirement as a discretionary rule they can apply even when the owner has otherwise shown a real, concrete injury.

## Where it stands

The petition was distributed for the Court's September 28, 2026 conference, and the Court asked for a response, which the county filed; Walls then filed a reply. The docket does not show any action taken since the reply was filed.

## What each outcome would mean

Walls, as petitioner, is asking the Court to take up his case. If the Court grants review, it would hear full arguments and decide the legal questions about when such property claims can go forward, without yet deciding who wins. If the Court denies review, the Fourth Circuit's decision affirming dismissal of Walls's lawsuit stays in place, meaning his claim remains blocked unless he pursues the county's legislative process first.
