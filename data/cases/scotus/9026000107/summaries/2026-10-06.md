## In brief

A Maryland landowner asks the Court to revive his lawsuit claiming a county regulation took his property without compensation, after lower courts called the claim premature.

## What happened

Garnell Walls owns vacant land in Prince George's County that he wants to build a home on. Because no public sewer line reaches the property, county rules required him to get a waiver to install a private well and septic system. The county told him he likely would not qualify without first seeking a legislative change to its water and sewer plan. Walls sued instead, claiming the regulation took his property without just compensation. The district court dismissed the case as not ready for review, and the Fourth Circuit affirmed, holding Walls first had to pursue the legislative amendment process. The county, as respondent, argues the lower courts were right and that Walls simply never applied for the waiver or the amendment.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked whether a property owner must first ask a legislature to change the rule that allegedly caused the taking before suing over it, and separately, whether courts may dismiss such a claim as premature even when the owner has otherwise shown a concrete injury sufficient to sue.

## Where it stands

The petition was distributed for conference, and the Court asked the county to respond; the county's brief opposing review has now been filed, and the case awaits the Court's decision whether to hear it.

## What each outcome would mean

Walls, as petitioner, is asking the Court to take up his case. If the Court grants review, it would hear full arguments and decide the legal questions, without yet deciding who wins. If it denies review, the Fourth Circuit's ruling stands, leaving in place the dismissal of Walls's lawsuit and the requirement that he pursue the county's legislative process before suing again.
