This dispute involves a local county water-and-sewer classification in Maryland under 42 U.S.C. § 1983 and implicates no federal statutory scheme, agency regulation, or distinct federal proprietary interest.

## Questions Presented and Vehicle Assessment

If the Court were to grant review, it would likely restrict review to the first question presented—whether *Williamson County* and *Pakdel v. City & County of San Francisco*, 594 U.S. 474 (2021), require a property owner raising an as-applied Fifth Amendment regulatory takings claim to petition the local legislature for a legislative plan amendment before the claim is ripe—and decline to take the broader second question asking the Court to abolish prudential ripeness in takings cases altogether under *Lexmark International, Inc. v. Static Control Components, Inc.*, 572 U.S. 118 (2014).

Even on the first question, however, the Court is likely to deny certiorari because of severe factual and procedural vehicle flaws exposed by the decisions below:
1. **Disputed underlying administrative application:** As District Judge Boardman detailed in her oral ruling (App. 20a–23a), petitioner failed to retain a copy of his alleged administrative waiver application to the Department of Permitting, Inspections, and Enforcement (DPIE), failed to allege that he notarized it or paid the required fee, and conceded in the district court that DPIE's February 2022 informational letter was not a formal final decision on a submitted waiver application.
2. **Unpublished decision below and alternative grounds:** The Fourth Circuit affirmed in a short, unpublished per curiam opinion (App. 1a–11a) that creates no binding circuit precedent. Moreover, the district court explicitly stated on the record that even if the claim were ripe, it would "almost certainly abstain under *Burford*" because petitioner's theory of entitlement to a waiver turns on interpreting intricate local water-and-sewer plan criteria (App. 14a–15a, 28a–29a).

## Disposition Route and Separate Writings

Conditional on a grant, there is a meaningful probability (`summary-disposition-route` = `0.25`) that the Court would dispose of the case summarily—following the exact pattern of *Pakdel*, which was a per curiam summary reversal correcting a circuit court's conflation of *Williamson County*'s modest de facto finality requirement with administrative or legislative exhaustion—rather than setting the case for full merits briefing and oral argument (`0.75`).

Conditional on the expected denial of certiorari, there is a modest chance (`dissent-from-denial` = `0.08`) that one or more Justices (most plausibly Justice Thomas, Justice Gorsuch, or Justice Alito) will author a dissent from or statement respecting the denial of certiorari criticizing lower courts' continued invocation of "prudential ripeness" and legislative-amendment hurdles to keep Fifth Amendment takings plaintiffs out of federal court after *Knick* and *Pakdel*.
