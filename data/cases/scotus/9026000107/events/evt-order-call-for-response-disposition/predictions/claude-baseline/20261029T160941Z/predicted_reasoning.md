# Predicted course of No. 26-107, Walls v. Prince George's County

**State forecast from.** One distribution on the record (for the 28 September 2026
conference), and a response called for on 2 September 2026 after the county
waived, due 2 October. No CVSG. Six amicus filings, all supporting the
petitioner, are docketed.

**Disposition: denied (P(grant family) ≈ 0.13).** The call for a response means
at least one chambers thought the petition merited a closer look, and the
property-rights amicus support signals a recurring issue. Even so, most
petitions that draw a called-for response are still denied. The vehicle is
weak. The Fourth Circuit's opinion is an unpublished per curiam. It rests on a
fact-bound finality holding: Walls never pursued the two-step water-and-sewer
category amendment the 2022 letter described, so the county had not reached a
final decision. A respondent's brief can recast the case as an ordinary
Williamson County / Pakdel finality ruling rather than a legislative-exhaustion
rule. The jurisdictional-versus-prudential ripeness question (QP 2) does not
change the result below, because the case was dismissed under Rule 12(b)(6)
either way.

**Relisting.** Once the response and reply are in, the petition will be
distributed again (the harness masks this). I expect it to be denied at its
first post-response conference. A further relist is possible, about one in
four, if a Justice is weighing a summary disposition or a statement.

**CVSG.** Not expected. The respondent is a county, no federal program is at
stake, and the Court rarely invites the Solicitor General in a local
land-use takings case.

**If granted, which question.** QP 1 (whether a property owner must pursue
legislative change to ripen an as-applied claim) as written. The Court would
leave QP 2's broader jurisdiction-versus-prudence question for another day.

**Summary route.** If the Court acts for the petitioner, a summary vacatur or
per curiam reversal is a real alternative to plenary review (about 35% of
grants). Pakdel itself was a summary per curiam correcting an over-demanding
finality rule, and a lower court requiring an owner to petition the
legislature fits that pattern.

**Dissent from denial.** Unlikely but possible (about 12%). If one comes, it
would most likely be from Justice Thomas or Justice Gorsuch, who have written
about takings ripeness and the ongoing effects of Williamson County.
