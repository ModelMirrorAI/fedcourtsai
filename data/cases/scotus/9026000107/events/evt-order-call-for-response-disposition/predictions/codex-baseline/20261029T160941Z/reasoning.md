## Why denial still dominates

The petition's first question is stronger than ordinary error correction: petition pages 13-20 assemble conflicting approaches to legislative remedies, and the Fourth Circuit expressly requires a category amendment even assuming the waiver was denied. The petition also asserts that the amendment would only enable connection to a future subdivision, not authorize the interim well and septic system. That assertion makes the finality issue substantial, but I treat it as advocacy rather than a fact independently accepted by the appellate court.

The appendix supplies the main counterweight. The unpublished appellate decision, pages 7a-10a, treats the County Council procedure as an established, periodically available process with independent power to grant relief, distinguishes Pakdel, and rejects futility because the employee's prediction of denial did not bind the Council. The resulting dispute can be viewed as a fact-specific disagreement about which local decisionmaker must act, rather than an express refusal to follow a settled no-exhaustion rule. The petition's opposing authorities span different local procedures, older cases, and several intermediate state courts, weakening the claim of a clean contemporary conflict on identical facts. Unpublished status is not dispositive, but makes this a less obvious rule-setting vehicle.

The second question has an especially concrete vehicle problem. Footnote 1 at appendix page 2a states that neither party challenged the district court's prudential treatment and agrees with Rule 12(b)(6) analysis. The petition argues an important broader doctrinal conflict, but this record leaves a preservation obstacle and uncertainty about whether relabeling finality would change this dismissal. The district court's conditional discussion of Burford abstention at pages 27a-29a creates possible further litigation on remand; it was not the appellate holding and I do not treat it as an independent dispositive ground. Together, these considerations keep denial at roughly three chances in four despite a serious first question.
