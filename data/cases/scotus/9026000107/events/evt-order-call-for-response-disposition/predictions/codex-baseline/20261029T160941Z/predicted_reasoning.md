# Forecast of the Court's action

The Court will most likely deny certiorari after considering the requested response. The probability of any grant, including a summary disposition, is 24%. The present record shows one distribution, for the September 28, 2026 conference, and a September 2 response request following the respondent's waiver. No response or CVSG appears in this snapshot.

Expect at least one additional distribution after the response arrives or its time expires (98%). The modal path is one redistributing entry and then denial, rather than a prolonged sequence of actual conference relists. One genuine post-response relist is a plausible alternative. The additional-distribution claim includes rescheduling and is not a claim that the Court has already considered this case at a conference; the harness masks it at this response-requested moment. Expect disposition within the first few conferences after the response and any reply are ready, rather than a many-month hold for federal advice. The recorded response deadline is October 2; this forecast does not assume that a response was actually filed then or that no extension occurred.

The Court is unlikely to call for the Solicitor General's views (2%). The dispute concerns a local land-use process and an existing federal finality standard, without an identified federal program whose administration requires the government's advice.

If review is granted, the strongest question is whether an owner must pursue a legislative change to the governing land-use plan after the permitting agency has rejected the requested use. Expect the Court to focus on that first question, perhaps reformulating it as the boundary between obtaining a final land-use decision and exhausting a legislative remedy. The broader question about jurisdictional versus prudential ripeness is a less likely standalone grant vehicle because the appellate opinion says neither party challenged the district court's treatment of that issue.

Conditional on a grant, plenary briefing and argument are more likely than disposition in the cert order: the summary-route probability is 20%. A narrow summary reversal applying Pakdel is the principal summary possibility. No intervening decision requiring a GVR is identified in the record. If granted and decided for the owner, the likely ground would be that a genuinely definitive governmental position cannot be made nonfinal merely because a legislature could alter the governing law; the Court need not decide whether a taking ultimately occurred.

Conditional on denial, expect no noted dissent or statement in the ordinary course (84%). If a separate writing does appear, Thomas is the leading candidate, followed by Gorsuch; that is a forecast, not an observed vote. Denial itself would not endorse the lower court's reasoning or determine the compensation claim's merits.
