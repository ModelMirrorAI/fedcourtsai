## In brief

A former Pentagon bomb squad supervisor asks the Court to revive his whistleblower retaliation claim against a federal personnel board.

## What happened

Richard Coleman worked as a supervisory explosives specialist for the Pentagon Force Protection Agency and received hazardous duty pay. The agency discontinued that pay in January 2015 and later revised his position description to remove references to it. Starting in February 2015, Coleman made a series of disclosures to agency officials, Congress, and others, arguing the pay cut was unlawful and that his position description contained errors, including a duplicated signature the agency called "sloppy work." The Merit Systems Protection Board dismissed his retaliation appeal for lack of jurisdiction, finding his disclosures came after the pay decision and so could not have caused it. The Federal Circuit affirmed, and Coleman, representing himself, now petitions the Supreme Court.

## What the Court is being asked

The petition asks the Court to decide whether an agency's repeated refusals, after an employee's disclosures, to restore pay or fix a disputed personnel document can count as their own separate retaliatory actions, rather than being treated as just the lingering effects of an earlier decision made before the disclosures occurred.

## Where it stands

The Merit Systems Protection Board waived its right to respond to the petition. The case was distributed for the Court's conference on November 13, 2026, and the docket shows no action since then.

## What each outcome would mean

Coleman, the petitioner, is asking the Court to take up his case and reverse the Federal Circuit's ruling. If the Court grants review, it would hear full arguments and decide the legal question itself, without yet deciding who wins. If it denies review, the Federal Circuit's decision stands, leaving Coleman's retaliation appeal dismissed and the Board's finding that it lacked jurisdiction in place.
