# Forecast of the Court's action

The Court will deny the petition, most likely in an orders list following the November 13, 2026 conference, approximately in mid-to-late November. The record currently shows **one distribution and no relists**. I expect **zero additional distributions**; the probability of at least one additional distribution is 4%. The Court will not call for the Solicitor General's views (0.1% probability of a new CVSG). The respondent's existing waiver will most likely stand without further briefing.

The denial will most likely be unexplained, with no separate writing. Conditional on denial, the chance of any noted dissent or statement respecting denial is 0.8%. No Justice is affirmatively predicted to write. If a separate writing occurs, Jackson or Sotomayor is slightly more likely than any other individual Justice, with Gorsuch another plausible writer. These possibilities do not forecast a public vote tally or an opinion of the Court on denial.

If review is granted, the likeliest question is a narrowed version of **QP2**: when a distinct post-disclosure refusal to restore compensation or correct a position description qualifies as a new personnel action under the federal whistleblower statute, rather than a continuing consequence of a prior decision. The Court would be less likely to take the signature and individual factual disputes in QPs 1 and 3 as freestanding questions. Any merits consideration would distinguish genuinely new retaliatory decisions from the unchanged effects of the original pay determination; this is conditional context, not a prediction that a merits opinion will issue.

Conditional on any grant, **plenary review is more likely than a summary disposition**: 85% versus 15%. No intervening decision or other identified event supplies an obvious grant-vacate-remand route. A summary reversal is also unlikely on this fact-dependent record. The most likely outcome remains an ordinary denial that leaves the lower court's judgment in place without endorsing its reasoning.
