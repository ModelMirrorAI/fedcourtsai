# Forecast — Coleman v. Merit Systems Protection Board, No. 26-427

**The Court will deny the petition at the conference of November 13, 2026, without
relisting it.** The docket shows one distribution (for the 11/13/2026 conference),
entered after the Solicitor General filed a waiver of the MSPB's right to respond;
no relist has occurred. I expect no further distribution: the Court is very
unlikely to call for a response on a pro se, fact-bound petition from a
nonprecedential Federal Circuit affirmance, and a call for response is the only
realistic route to a second conference here.

**No CVSG.** The respondent is a federal agency already represented by the
Solicitor General, so the Court would call for a response, not for the SG's views.

**Which question, if granted.** None is a plausible vehicle. If the Court took
anything it would be a narrowed QP 2 — whether a continuing refusal to restore pay
or correct a position description is a "failure to take" a personnel action under
5 U.S.C. § 2302(a)(2)(A) for IRA jurisdiction — rather than the fact-specific QPs 1
and 3. The petition identifies no circuit split, and the Federal Circuit has
exclusive jurisdiction over most such appeals, so a split is structurally hard to
show.

**Summary disposition.** In the remote event of any grant, a summary route (a GVR
in light of an intervening decision, or a summary vacatur) is somewhat more likely
than in an ordinary grant, because nothing suggests plenary review; I see no
intervening decision on point, though.

**Separate writing.** Expect none: the petition will be denied in the orders list
without noted dissent or statement.
