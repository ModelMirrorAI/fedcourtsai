## 2. Case-Specific Downward Adjustments: Severe Vehicle and Merits Flaws

Starting from the 5.19% `baseline` reached anchor and adjusting for the respondent waivers and zero amicus briefs (which already push the conditional probability toward ~1.5%–2.0%), the provisioned filings (`record/documents/questions-presented.txt` and `record/documents/petition.txt`) reveal multiple disqualifying vehicle defects that warrant adjusting `P(grant)` down to **0.01 (1.0%)**:

1. **Unpublished Summary Order That Did Not Address Petitioner's Argument:**
   The Second Circuit resolved Jack Rabbit's consolidated appeal (`No. 24-1808(CON)`, consolidated with `No. 24-1653(L)`) by a **non-precedential Summary Order** (Pet. App. 1a–11a). Worse still, as the petition explicitly admits (Pet. 8, 20), the Second Circuit's Summary Order addressed only the arguments of the "Square Sellers" (*803 Kava LLC / Lanning* plaintiffs) and **did not mention or analyze Jack Rabbit's Article III / Rule 23 argument at all**. Nor did the companion published opinion in *Old Jericho Enterprise, Inc. v. Visa Inc.*, No. 24-2678 (Pet. App. 12a–30a)—an appeal by gas-station operators in which Jack Rabbit was not a party (mentioned only in passing at Pet. App. 28a n.4)—address Jack Rabbit's constitutional claim. There is thus no lower-court holding or analysis on the Questions Presented for the Supreme Court to review.
2. **Post-Judgment Contract Enforcement, Not Class Certification:**
   Petitioner attempts to frame Question Presented 1 around the circuit split over whether a Rule 23(b)(3) damages class may be *certified* when it contains uninjured members (Pet. i, 11–16). In reality, the Rule 23(b)(3) settlement class in `In re Payment Card Interchange Fee & Merchant Discount Antitrust Litigation` (`05-md-1720`) was certified and finally approved in December 2019 (`330 F.R.D. 11`), and that final judgment was affirmed over objector appeals in *Fikes Wholesale, Inc. v. HSBC Bank USA, N.A.*, 62 F.4th 704 (2d Cir. 2023). The May 28, 2024 district court order under review (`735 F. Supp. 3d 249`, Pet. App. 31a–76a) was not a class-certification order or even a claims-payout order; it granted **Defendants' motion to enforce the 2019 Settlement Agreement's release** against separate antitrust lawsuits filed by opt-out payment facilitators (Square and Intuit) and non-opt-out merchants who used Square (*Lanning* plaintiffs). Interpreting the contract term "accepted" under New York law, the district court held that the Square Sellers (not Square or Intuit) were the entities that "accepted" Visa and Mastercard cards and were therefore *already* members of the 2019 Settlement Class bound by its release.
3. **Collateral Objector Posture:**
   Jack Rabbit, LLC is a branded gasoline retailer (Pet. App. 28a n.4), not a Square Seller or payment facilitator, and was not even a party to the district court's May 28, 2024 enforcement order between Visa/Mastercard, Square, Intuit, and the *Lanning* plaintiffs (Pet. App. 32a–35a). None of the parties directly bound by that order petitioned for certiorari. Jack Rabbit's sole claimed injury is that holding Square Sellers to be within the 2019 Settlement Class (rather than outside it) allows them to file claims that may dilute Jack Rabbit's pro rata share of the $5.6 billion settlement fund. That collateral posture raises serious threshold appellate-standing and prudential obstacles.
4. **Conflation of *Illinois Brick* Statutory Merits with Article III Standing:**
   On the merits, Square Sellers alleged classic pocketbook economic injury (higher merchant discount/processing fees traceable to Defendants' network rules). Whether they or their payment facilitators are the "direct purchasers" under *Illinois Brick Co. v. Illinois*, 431 U.S. 720 (1977), is a statutory merits question under Section 4 of the Clayton Act, not a jurisdictional Article III standing barrier (*Lexmark International, Inc. v. Static Control Components, Inc.*, 572 U.S. 118, 128 n.4 (2014)). And as both courts below noted (Pet. App. 9a, 18a–19a, 28a n.3), a primary purpose of the $5.6 billion settlement was to compromise Defendants' contested *Illinois Brick* defense without litigating direct-purchaser status to final judgment.

- **`disposition` (`0.01`) & `relist-increment` (`0.04`):** Because all respondents waived a response, any serious interest from even one Justice would first require a call for a response (CFR) and a subsequent redistribution. - **`cvsg-increment` (`0.005`):** With no federal statutory or regulatory program at issue and respondent waivers on file, a CVSG is virtually out of the question (0.5%).
- **`summary-disposition-route` (`0.15`):** Conditional on the remote event of a grant, plenary review (85%) is much likelier than a summary order (15%), as there is no pending Supreme Court merits vehicle for a GVR hold and summary reversal is extraordinarily rare where the court of appeals wrote an unpublished order that did not address the petitioner's argument.

- **`big_case_score` (`0.55`):** Decoupled from the very low likelihood of a grant, if the Court *did* decide the merits, a ruling on Article III and Rule 23 constraints on settlement-class definitions and claims administration in the landmark $5.6 billion *Payment Card Interchange Fee* MDL would be moderately high-stakes (`0.55`), though narrower than a clean Rule 23(b)(3) certification vehicle due to the post-judgment contract-interpretation posture.
