# Predicted Court Action and Reasoning: *Jack Rabbit, LLC v. Visa Inc., et al.* (No. 26-420)

The docket currently shows **one distribution** (`distribution_count: 1`, distributed on October 28, 2026 for the Conference of November 13, 2026) and **no CVSG** (`cvsg_date: null`), following waivers of the right to respond filed by all three respondents—Mastercard Incorporated, Visa Inc., and the Rule 23(b)(3) Class Plaintiffs—and zero amicus briefs.

- **Further Distributions / Relists (`relist-increment`: 0.04):** The Court will almost certainly deny the petition on its first conference without calling for a response or relisting it. There is only a small (~4%) chance that a Justice calls for a response (which would trigger a second distribution once the opposition is filed) or relists the case. Although several Justices have shown interest in whether a Rule 23(b)(3) damages class may be certified when it contains uninjured members (*Laboratory Corp. of America Holdings v. Davis*, No. 24-304), the Court recently denied certiorari without a grant in *Sunoco, Inc. (R&M) v. Cline* (No. 26-1, denied Oct. 5, 2026) and *Takeda Pharmaceutical Co. v. Painters & Allied Trades District Council 82 Health Care Fund* (No. 25-625, denied Mar. 23, 2026), and this petition does not arise from a class-certification order at all.
- **Solicitor General Invitation (`cvsg-increment`: 0.005):** The Court will not invite the Solicitor General to file a brief. The dispute concerns the post-judgment interpretation of a private antitrust class settlement agreement under New York contract law, all respondents waived a response, and no federal agency interest is implicated.

## 2. Disposition and Conditional Route

- **Disposition (`disposition`: 0.01 — `denied`):** The Court will deny the petition for a writ of certiorari without comment at the November 13, 2026 Conference (Order List of November 16, 2026).
- **Questions Presented (if granted):** If the Court were to grant review, it would take only **Question Presented 1**—whether Article III and Rule 23(b)(3) permit a district court administering a class-action settlement fund to include merchants whose direct-purchaser status under *Illinois Brick Co. v. Illinois*, 431 U.S. 720 (1977), was disputed and compromised rather than adjudicated—while declining Question Presented 2, which merely restates the same objection as a challenge to the Second Circuit's summary affirmance.
- **Summary Disposition Route (`summary-disposition-route`: 0.15 conditional on grant):** Conditional on a grant, plenary review (85%) would be much likelier than a summary disposition in the certiorari order itself (15%). No pending Supreme Court merits case presents the question whether a post-judgment settlement-enforcement order requires adjudication of *Illinois Brick* direct-purchaser status, leaving no obvious anchor for a GVR, and summary reversal is implausible on an issue the Second Circuit's unpublished summary order did not expressly analyze.
- **Conditional Merits Ground (if granted):** If the Court granted plenary review, it would likely affirm or dismiss the writ as improvidently granted once briefing highlighted that the 2019 Rule 23(b)(3) class certification is final (*Fikes Wholesale, Inc. v. HSBC Bank USA, N.A.*, 62 F.4th 704 (2d Cir. 2023)), that the May 28, 2024 order merely enforced the 2019 Settlement Agreement's release against separate suits by Square, Intuit, and the *Lanning* plaintiffs, and that *Illinois Brick* direct-purchaser status is a statutory merits issue under *Lexmark International, Inc. v. Static Control Components, Inc.*, 572 U.S. 118, 128 n.4 (2014), that settling parties may compromise without offending Article III.
