**Adjustments, all downward or neutral:**
- *Both respondents waived.* Paid petitions with no response called for are almost never granted. Granting would require a Justice to call for a response first, and I see little reason for one.
- *Vehicle.* The decision below is a non-precedential Second Circuit summary order (App. 1a; it rests on the same panel's published *Old Jericho* opinion, 175 F.4th 172). It treats class membership as a question of the settling parties' intent, a factual finding reviewed for clear error. The petition frames this as the *Tyson Foods*/*Labcorp v. Davis* uninjured-class-member split. But the Second Circuit expressly decided no antitrust-standing question, so the split is not squarely presented. The petitioner is an objector seeking a larger pro-rata share, which is an awkward posture.
- *Companion signals.* The petition presents itself as a companion to *Sunoco v. Cline* (No. 26-1) and *City of Cleveland v. Pickett* (No. 25-677). A corpus lookup shows Sunoco **denied Oct 5, 2026**, which removes the main grant-companion path. Pickett is still pending after 4 distributions, the last on May 28, 2026, a pattern consistent with a hold. That leaves a small hold/GVR tail, which is why summary-disposition-route conditional on a grant is 0.25 rather than lower. But the QPs differ: certification vs. post-judgment settlement allocation. `same-question.json` lists no petition above the 0.5 similarity threshold.
- *Counsel.* A solo practitioner, not a repeat Supreme Court advocate. Respondents are represented by elite SCOTUS counsel who chose to waive.
- *Stakes.* The $5.6B fund and the circuit (ca2 paid grant rate is a bit above average) are modestly positive, but not enough to overcome the above.

Net: about 1.2%, below the frozen anchor of 5.2%, because the anchor is a risk-set rate and this petition's visible trajectory (waivers, first conference) is the weak end of the band.
