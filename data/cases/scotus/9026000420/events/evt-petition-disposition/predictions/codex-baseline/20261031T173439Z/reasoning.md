## Reasons to depart downward, with counterweights

The petition's strongest point is its attempt to reach an unresolved class-action question at actual distribution rather than preliminary certification. It invokes Tyson Foods, TransUnion, and Justice Kavanaugh's Labcorp dissent, and argues that including allegedly ineligible recipients dilutes a fixed $5.6 billion fund. Those arguments and the asserted circuit disagreement warrant a real, nonzero grant probability. They also support the significance score independently of grant prospects. Sources: petition pp. 1–2, 8–18; questions-presented text.

The main difficulty is vehicle fit. The appealed order interprets an existing settlement's membership and release, with the earlier certification and settlement approval already affirmed. The appendix's summary order applies fact-sensitive settlement interpretation and explains that the parties settled in part to avoid adjudicating the indirect-purchaser defense (App. 6a–9a). That is not a square holding that concededly uninjured people may recover adjudicated damages. A disputed statutory direct-purchaser defense also is not, without more, an absence of concrete Article III injury. The petition seeks to move from entitlement not having been adjudicated to entitlement needing adjudication before settlement payment. That bridge is contested, rather than an established violation I assume.

The alleged circuit division principally concerns certification, whereas the proposed questions concern postjudgment settlement administration. The petition does not demonstrate opposing appellate outcomes on this exact settlement-distribution question. Its own assertion that the panel did not separately address this objector's legal argument creates an additional issue-presentation concern, although the petition says the point was preserved (pp. 19–20). The unreported order, complicated class history, and unresolved underlying antitrust defense make another vehicle plausible. Both response waivers, with no Court request yet, reduce the near-term grant signal; they are not conclusive or a criticism of petitioner's representation.
