## In brief

The Supreme Court declined to hear Jack Rabbit's challenge to how a $5.6 billion card-fee class settlement fund is being divided among merchants.

## What happened

This case grows out of a long-running antitrust settlement between Visa, Mastercard and merchants who accepted their cards, approved in 2019 for about $5.6 billion. Jack Rabbit, a settlement class member, objected when the district court later ruled that millions of sub-merchants who use payment facilitators like Square and Intuit should also share in the fund, based on what the court found the settling parties intended. The Second Circuit affirmed that ruling but said twice it was not deciding whether those added members actually have a valid claim under federal antitrust law. Jack Rabbit asked the Supreme Court to review that decision.

## What the Court is being asked

The case raised two related questions: whether a court may order money from a fund set up to pay valid antitrust claims to be shared with people no court has found actually entitled to recover under that law, just because the parties who settled meant to include them; and whether an appeals court may approve that distribution while openly declining to decide whether those people have any such claim at all.

## Where it stands

The petition was distributed for the Court's November 13, 2026 conference, and on November 16, 2026 the Court denied it. Justice Alito did not take part in the decision.

## What each outcome would mean

Jack Rabbit, the objecting class member, asked the Court to overturn the Second Circuit's decision. By denying the petition, the Court leaves that decision in place: the lower courts' rulings allowing sub-merchants to share in the settlement fund, without any court deciding whether they are entitled to recover under antitrust law, stand. Visa and Mastercard, along with the sub-merchants who benefit from the distribution, keep the result they obtained below. The denial does not mean the Supreme Court agreed the lower courts were right; it simply leaves their rulings undisturbed, and the fund's distribution can proceed on that basis.
