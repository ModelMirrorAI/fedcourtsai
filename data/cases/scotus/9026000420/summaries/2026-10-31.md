## In brief

Jack Rabbit asks the Court to review whether courts may direct a fixed antitrust settlement fund to be shared with people no court has found entitled to recover.

## What happened

This case grows out of a $5.6 billion settlement of antitrust claims that merchants accepted Visa and Mastercard cards and paid inflated fees. Jack Rabbit, a settlement class member, objected to a 2024 order letting millions of sub-merchants who use payment facilitators like Square also share in the fund. The district court and the Second Circuit found the settling parties intended to include these sub-merchants, but both courts said they were not deciding whether the sub-merchants actually have valid antitrust claims. Jack Rabbit appealed that approval and lost, then sought Supreme Court review.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked whether a court may order a fixed fund, created to pay people with valid antitrust claims, to be shared with people no court has determined are legally entitled to recover, simply because the settling companies meant to include them. It is also asked whether an appeals court may approve that kind of order while expressly declining to decide if those added people have any valid claim at all.

## Where it stands

Visa and Mastercard have both waived their right to respond to the petition. The case was distributed for the Court's November 13, 2026 conference, where the justices will consider whether to take it up.

## What each outcome would mean

Jack Rabbit, the objecting class member, is asking the Court to take up the case and reverse the lower court's approval. If the Court grants review and ultimately agrees with Jack Rabbit, courts would have to determine that added claimants are legally entitled to recover before letting them share the fund, which could reduce or delay payments to the sub-merchants. If the Court denies review, the Second Circuit's approval stands, and the fund distribution to the sub-merchants, already underway, can continue unchanged.
