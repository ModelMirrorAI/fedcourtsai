## In brief

Shipbuilders ask the Court to review a ruling reviving a wage-suppression lawsuit, while the plaintiff's own lawyer has now asked the Court to pause the case.

## What happened

Susan Scharpf and other naval engineers sued General Dynamics and other shipbuilding companies, claiming they secretly agreed not to recruit each other's engineers, suppressing wages for decades. A federal district court dismissed the case as filed too late, ruling that an unwritten agreement alone cannot excuse the delay. The Fourth Circuit Court of Appeals reversed, holding that the plaintiffs had adequately alleged the companies took active steps to hide their agreement, allowing the case to proceed. The shipbuilders then asked the Supreme Court to review that ruling.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked whether, to excuse a late-filed lawsuit, it is enough for plaintiffs to allege that companies kept an illegal agreement unwritten and secret, or whether they must show the companies took some additional active step to mislead people and prevent them from finding out.

## Where it stands

The Court asked the Solicitor General in January 2026 to submit the federal government's views on whether the case should be heard. In May 2026, the shipbuilders submitted a request asking that the petition be held in abeyance, meaning paused rather than acted on; the docket does not show whether the Court has ruled on that request.

## What each outcome would mean

General Dynamics and the other shipbuilders, as petitioners, are asking the Court to take up the case and overturn the Fourth Circuit's decision reviving Scharpf's lawsuit. If the Court grants review and later rules for the shipbuilders, Scharpf's case would likely be dismissed as untimely. If the Court denies review, the Fourth Circuit's decision stands, and the case returns to the lower courts, where Scharpf's lawsuit continues. The pending request to pause the case does not resolve these questions; it only affects when the Court acts.
