## What happened

Susan Scharpf, a former naval engineer, sued General Dynamics and other major shipbuilders and naval-engineering firms, claiming they secretly agreed for decades not to recruit each other's engineers, suppressing wages. Because her employment ended years before she sued, her claim would normally be too old under the four-year deadline for antitrust suits, unless that deadline was paused because the companies had fraudulently hidden their conduct. A federal district court dismissed the case as too late. The Fourth Circuit Court of Appeals reversed, over a dissent, allowing the case to proceed. The company defendants asked the full Fourth Circuit to reconsider; it declined. They then asked the Supreme Court to take the case.

## What the Court is being asked

The case asks whether an antitrust plaintiff can get the filing deadline paused by alleging simply that the companies kept their agreement unwritten and secret, or whether something more—active steps to trick or mislead people, not just silence—is required to show the companies were hiding their wrongdoing. The company defendants say the appeals court's rule lets almost any plaintiff revive old claims just by saying the deal was never put in writing, splitting from other appeals courts. Scharpf answers that the lower court actually required proof of deliberate concealment efforts and intent to deceive, not just an unwritten deal, and that there is no real disagreement among the appeals courts once the facts are compared.

## Where it stands

The petition has been fully briefed, including amicus briefs supporting the companies and a reply from them, and was distributed for the Court's conference in January 2026. Rather than acting then, the Court invited the Solicitor General to submit the federal government's views (a CVSG); a later docket entry shows the company defendants have asked that the petition be held in abeyance, meaning the case has not yet been decided.
