## In brief

Shipbuilders ask the Court to review a ruling letting a former employee's decades-old wage-fixing lawsuit proceed despite the usual time limit for filing such claims.

## What happened

Susan Scharpf, a former naval engineer, sued General Dynamics and other major shipbuilders and naval-engineering firms, alleging they secretly agreed for decades not to recruit each other's engineers, suppressing wages. Because her claimed injuries occurred years before she sued, her case depended on convincing the courts that the companies had fraudulently hidden the scheme, which would pause the normal four-year filing deadline. A district court dismissed the suit as filed too late. The Fourth Circuit reversed, over a dissent, holding Scharpf had adequately alleged that keeping the agreement unwritten and secret amounted to fraudulent concealment. The companies then asked the Supreme Court to step in.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked whether alleging that companies kept an illegal agreement unwritten, rather than showing they actively deceived people, is enough to pause the deadline for suing over it. The companies say other appeals courts require more than an unwritten, secretive arrangement; Scharpf responds that the lower court actually required additional proof of intent to deceive and diligence by the plaintiff, and that no real disagreement among courts exists.

## Where it stands

The petition has been fully briefed, including amicus briefs supporting the companies. The Court asked the Solicitor General for the federal government's views in January 2026, and in May 2026 the companies asked that the petition be held in abeyance; the docket does not yet show a ruling on that request or on the petition itself.

## What each outcome would mean

General Dynamics and the other companies, as petitioners, are asking the Court to take up the case. If the Court grants review, it would hear full arguments and decide the pleading standard itself, without yet deciding who wins. If it denies review, the Fourth Circuit's decision favoring Scharpf stands, and her lawsuit continues in the lower courts; that denial would not mean the Court agrees the Fourth Circuit was right. The request to hold the petition in abeyance, and the Solicitor General's still-pending views, may affect when or how the Court acts.
