# Forecast: what the Court will do with No. 25-293 (General Dynamics v. Scharpf)

**Bottom line.** The Court will not decide the question presented. The petition will
end in a **Munsingwear disposition** — certiorari granted, the Fourth Circuit's
judgment vacated, and the case remanded with instructions to dismiss as moot — most
likely at a conference in October Term 2026, after the Eastern District of Virginia
approves the class settlements with the settling petitioners and General Dynamics
files its promised suggestion of mootness. The main alternative is a plain
**denial** (the Court refusing vacatur, or ruling on the petition as a live one after
the Solicitor General files); the third is a **Rule 46 dismissal** by the petitioners
themselves.

**Where the docket stands, and what follows.** The snapshot shows one distribution
(for the January 9, 2026 conference) followed by the January 12 call for the views of
the Solicitor General. The public docket since then shows no SG brief and a single
further entry: a May 18, 2026 letter from petitioners' counsel asking the Clerk to hold
the petition in abeyance because respondent has voluntarily dismissed her claims
against General Dynamics and the remaining petitioners have settled or are settling,
subject to district-court approval "that may take months." I forecast:

- **No further merits activity by the Court on the petition while it is held.** The
  Solicitor General will not file the invited brief before the mootness question is
  resolved; if the case is later revived, the SG will file and the ordinary CVSG path
  resumes.
- **At least one more distribution** (roughly an 80% chance). A suggestion of mootness
  seeking vacatur is distributed for conference and acted on by order; so is a
  denial. The only route that skips a distribution is a voluntary Rule 46 dismissal
  entered by the Clerk, which is why the relist probability is not higher. I expect
  exactly one further distribution if the disposition is a Munsingwear GVR, with a
  modest chance of a single relist if a Justice writes.
- **No CVSG increment**: the CVSG has issued, so this claim is vacuous for this cell.
- **Summary route conditional on a grant: very likely (about 85%).** Any grant here is
  overwhelmingly a Munsingwear GVR riding the cert order; a plenary grant requires the
  case to stay live against General Dynamics, the SG to file, and the Court to take a
  petition whose co-petitioners have left the case.
- **Which question, if the Court ever took one.** If the case were revived, the Court
  would take the question as written — whether alleging an unwritten agreement
  suffices to plead an affirmative act of fraudulent concealment under 15 U.S.C. 15b
  — without reformulation; the respondent's framing that the Fourth Circuit applied a
  fact-bound consensus standard would be the merits dispute, not a reason to narrow.
- **On a denial: a separate writing is unlikely (about 12%).** If the Court declines
  Munsingwear vacatur it will most likely deny without comment. A statement is
  possible either way — Justice Jackson has written separately against routine
  Munsingwear vacaturs (Chapman v. Doe, 2023), which cuts toward a dissent from a
  *grant* rather than from a denial and is registered here as context only.
- **Timing.** Settlement notices were filed in March 2026 and the abeyance letter
  in May 2026; final approval of class settlements typically runs six to twelve
  months, so the suggestion of mootness and the Court's order are most likely
  between late 2026 and mid 2027. The Court will not act before the district court
  does.

**Conditional merits note.** If the petition were ever granted plenarily, the likely
ground would be that maintaining an unwritten agreement is "mere silence" rather
than a "trick or contrivance" (Wood v. Carpenter) and cannot alone toll 15b, with
the Court reversing. That is conditional on a path I put well under 10%.
