# Rationale

I assign a 0.37 probability to any grant and predict denial. The committed statpack supplies two relevant anchors. Pooling the `sal-v3` high-band bracketed `reached` rates over OT2017–OT2024 gives 439 grant-family outcomes over 1,074 weighted resolved petitions, or 40.9%. The paid scored-segment CVSG cut gives 57 grant-family outcomes among 163 resolved petitions, or 35.0%. I place this private-party CVSG petition between those anchors rather than using the much lower whole-docket or Fourth Circuit rates.

The upward considerations are the Court's affirmative CVSG signal after the first conference, a divided published decision, a focused federal limitations question, experienced Supreme Court counsel, two amicus filings, and the government's operational interest in the naval shipbuilding market. The petition also identifies an arguable disagreement with Fifth, Sixth, and Ninth Circuit decisions and presents potentially large consequences for stale antitrust class claims.

The downward considerations are unusually concrete. The brief in opposition argues persuasively that the Fourth Circuit required intent and relied on coded language, private calls, and other conduct rather than adopting the petition's claimed unwritten-agreement-only rule. It also identifies factual distinctions in the alleged split, additional concealment allegations that complicate the vehicle, and the interlocutory posture. Those features make a deny recommendation from the Solicitor General plausible and pull my estimate below the high-band risk-set anchor.

The 0.98 relist-increment probability reflects the procedural posture: the docket shows one distribution and a pending invitation, so the petition ordinarily must be redistributed after the Solicitor General's brief before disposition. The `cvsg-increment` claim is set to 0 because the snapshot already records the January 12 CVSG; the harness masks that claim as vacuous at this moment.

Conditional on a grant, I assign only 0.10 to a cert-order disposition. The published prior-Term paid baseline is about 0.348, but this case presents no identified intervening authority for a GVR and no sufficiently clear error for a likely summary reversal; a grant would much more naturally lead to plenary review. Conditional on denial, I assign 0.05 to any noted dissent or statement because the issue is important but the vehicle objections give interested Justices an easy reason not to write.

The petition, questions presented, brief in opposition, dated snapshot, and frozen forward-mode context were all available and complete. Two CourtListener opinion searches for general antitrust fraudulent-concealment cases returned no results and did not affect the forecast. I did not search for this case's later docket activity or disposition.
