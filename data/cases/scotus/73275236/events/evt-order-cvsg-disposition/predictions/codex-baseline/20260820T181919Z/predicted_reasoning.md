# Forecast

The petition has been distributed once, for the January 9, 2026 conference, and the Court called for the Solicitor General's views on January 12. After the Solicitor General files, the Court will distribute the petition at least once more and will most likely consider it at one or two post-brief conferences. No further CVSG is expected because the United States' views have already been requested.

The likeliest ultimate disposition is denial of certiorari. The request for the United States' views shows meaningful interest, but the opposition gives the Court substantial reasons to stop short of review: the asserted conflict depends partly on unpublished or distinguishable decisions, the Fourth Circuit described a context-dependent pleading rule rather than an express categorical rule, and the case is interlocutory after reversal of a dismissal. The federal government's interest in naval shipbuilding and federal antitrust enforcement could nevertheless produce a recommendation to grant and keeps a plenary grant a serious possibility.

If review is granted, the Court is most likely to take a narrowed or reformulated version of the petition's single question: whether deliberately keeping an agreement unwritten, together with coded communications and other alleged concealment, can constitute the affirmative concealment needed to toll 15 U.S.C. § 15b. Plenary briefing and argument are much more likely than disposition in the cert order; there is no identified intervening precedent that would naturally support a GVR, and the disputed application of the pleading standard is not an obvious summary-reversal vehicle.

If certiorari is denied, no Justice is likely to note a dissent or issue a statement respecting denial.
