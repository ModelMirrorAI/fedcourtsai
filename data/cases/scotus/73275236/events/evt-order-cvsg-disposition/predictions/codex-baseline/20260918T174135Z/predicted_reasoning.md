# Forecast of the Court's action

The Court will most likely deny certiorari in General Dynamics Corporation, et al. v. Susan Scharpf. I assign a 30% probability to any grant, including a summary grant route.

The starting record contains exactly one distribution, on December 10, 2025, for the January 9, 2026 conference, followed by the January 12, 2026 invitation for the Solicitor General's views. From that state, the Court will await the invited brief and return the petition to conference. I expect one or two additional distributions before disposition, with a 96% probability of at least one. This forecast counts the post-CVSG redistribution as an increment; it does not require an extra reconsideration after that conference. The expected disposition window is several weeks to a few months after the invited brief is filed, rather than a fixed date inferred from an unseen filing.

The CVSG is already accomplished, not a future forecast. The required cvsg-increment probability is zero and is vacuous for this moment; it is not a prediction that the existing invitation will disappear.

The likeliest reason to decline review is that the petition's categorical framing is a poor fit for the allegations and the court of appeals' treatment of them. The case concerns whether deliberately avoiding a written record, combined with allegations of coded communications and intentional concealment, sufficiently pleads fraudulent concealment. The Court is more likely to leave that pleading-stage application and further factual development to the lower courts than to treat it as an established conflict over whether mere silence alone tolls the limitations period. A denial would not endorse the lower court's merits analysis and would probably carry no explanation.

If review is granted, I expect the Court to address the petition's single limitations question, likely framed more precisely as whether intentionally maintaining an unwritten antitrust agreement supplies the affirmative concealment needed to toll the four-year period, rather than whether any unwritten agreement automatically suffices. Review would not broadly decide the substantive legality of no-poach agreements. On that conditional branch, the likely doctrinal focus is the distinction between secrecy inherent in a conspiracy and affirmative concealment pleaded with particularity.

Conditional on a grant, I assign only 8% to disposition in the cert order itself and 92% to plenary briefing and argument. The record supplies no intervening decision that makes a GVR the natural route, and the contested characterization of the allegations makes summary reversal less attractive. Conditional on denial, I assign 7% to any noted dissent or statement respecting denial; my point forecast is no separate writing, and I do not forecast an individual Justice's cert vote.
