Distributed once so far. The Solicitor General has been invited to file a brief expressing the views of the United States. Following the submission of the SG's brief and the parties' supplemental briefs, expect the petition to be distributed at least once more (likely 2 or 3 times total) before a decision is reached on the petition.

If granted, the Court will likely take the question of whether an unwritten, secret "gentlemen's agreement" can constitute an affirmative act of fraudulent concealment sufficient to toll the Sherman Act's statute of limitations, resolving the tension among the circuits regarding the "self-concealing" standard versus the "affirmative act" requirement. A summary GVR is unlikely unless there is an intervening decision directly on point, as the Court would likely prefer plenary review to clarify the standard. 

Should the Court deny review (e.g., if the SG recommends denial on vehicle grounds or lack of a deep split), expect a possible dissent from denial by one or more of the conservative Justices (such as Justice Thomas or Justice Alito) concerned about the expansion of antitrust liability and the relaxation of pleading standards for fraudulent concealment.
