## In brief

Lane Mitchell asks the Court to review whether a second trial is barred after his first trial ended in a judge-ordered acquittal.

## What happened

Mitchell was charged in Mississippi with attempted murder after stabbing a man who he says was charging aggressively at his father. After both sides presented evidence at trial, the judge found that the prosecution's failure to secure a key witness violated Mitchell's constitutional rights and entered a "judgment of acquittal," ending the case before it reached the jury. The state appealed, and a divided Mississippi Court of Appeals reversed, treating the ruling as a procedural dismissal rather than a true acquittal, and ordered a new trial. The Mississippi Supreme Court initially agreed to review the case but later dismissed its own review without deciding anything further.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked whether retrying Mitchell would violate the constitutional protection against being tried twice for the same offense. Mitchell's petition argues that once a trial judge terminates a case after both sides have presented their evidence, based on applying the law to the facts of the completed trial, that ruling counts as an acquittal barring a second trial—even if the judge did not use the specific words "insufficient evidence" and even if the judge's reasoning might have been mistaken.

## Where it stands

Mitchell filed his petition for the Court to hear the case on October 13, 2026, after receiving an extension of time. The state's response is due November 19, 2026, and the Court has not yet acted on the petition.

## What each outcome would mean

Mitchell, who lost in the Mississippi Court of Appeals, is asking the Court to take up his case. If the Court grants review, it will hear full arguments and decide whether the trial court's ruling counts as an acquittal barring retrial; granting review does not itself decide who wins. If the Court denies review, the Mississippi Court of Appeals' decision stands, and Mitchell's case proceeds toward the second trial already scheduled in Mississippi.
