## Disposition and Route

- **Disposition (`disposition`: 0.04 — `denied`):** The Court will deny the petition for a writ of certiorari. Although the trial judge titled his order at the close of evidence an "Order Granting Defendant's Motion to Dismiss and Judgment of Acquittal," both the trial transcript and the written order expressly ground the termination of the prosecution in an asserted violation of the Sixth Amendment Compulsory Process Clause and Fourteenth Amendment Due Process Clause arising from the complaining witness's failure to appear—not in any assessment of the sufficiency of the State's proof on the elements of attempted murder. Indeed, the trial court had denied Mitchell's motions for a directed verdict of acquittal on sufficiency grounds at the close of the State's case-in-chief and at the close of the defense's case-in-chief. Under *United States v. Martin Linen Supply Co.*, 430 U.S. 564 (1977), *United States v. Scott*, 437 U.S. 82 (1978), *Evans v. Michigan*, 568 U.S. 313 (2013), and *McElrath v. Georgia*, 601 U.S. 87 (2024), substance controls over labels: a defendant-sought dismissal based on a constitutional or procedural violation unrelated to factual guilt or innocence is not an acquittal for Double Jeopardy purposes, even when entered after the close of all evidence and accompanied by the label "Judgment of Acquittal."
- **Question Presented (if granted):** If the Court were to grant review, it would take the single Question Presented as framed in the petition—whether the Double Jeopardy Clause bars retrial when, after the close of all evidence, a trial court enters a judgment of acquittal premised on trial-record constitutional violations without separately finding the evidence legally insufficient—and use it to address the boundary between *Scott* and *Evans*.
- **Summary Disposition Route (`summary-disposition-route`: 0.25 conditional on grant):** Conditional on a grant, plenary review (~75%) is substantially likelier than a summary disposition in the cert order (~25%). There is no intervening Supreme Court decision warranting a GVR, and the trial judge's explicit reliance on compulsory-process grounds rather than insufficiency of the evidence prevents the decision below from being the kind of clear departure from *Evans* or *McElrath* that would invite a per curiam summary reversal, though a summary reversal cannot be ruled out entirely if a majority viewed any post-evidence "Judgment of Acquittal" as categorically final.
- **Dissent from Denial (`dissent-from-denial`: 0.03 conditional on denial):** Expect no noted dissent or statement respecting the denial of certiorari. While Justices Gorsuch, Sotomayor, or Jackson occasionally write separately on criminal procedure and Double Jeopardy protections, the idiosyncratic facts surrounding the out-of-state conservatorship and the defense's mid-trial motion to dismiss make a separate writing unlikely.
