# Forecast — Mitchell v. Mississippi, No. 26-526 (arrival moment)

**Disposition: denied.** The petition was docketed October 20, 2026 with a
response due November 19, 2026. The docket shows **zero distributions** and no
CVSG — the arrival state.

- **Distribution / relists.** Expect the petition to be distributed for
  conference once (most likely after Mississippi either waives response or the
  response period runs, i.e. a late-November or December conference) and denied
  at that first conference without a relist. A call for a response is possible
  if the Court wants the State's side, but I do not expect one to change the
  outcome. No relist.
- **CVSG.** None. This is a state prosecution; no federal party or federal
  program interest is implicated, and the Court does not CVSG on state
  criminal-procedure petitions.
- **Question the Court would take, if granted.** The single QP as written —
  whether a post-close-of-evidence "judgment of acquittal" grounded in a
  compulsory-process / confrontation violation, without an express
  insufficiency finding, is an acquittal for Double Jeopardy purposes under
  Martin Linen, Smalis and Evans v. Michigan, or a non-acquittal dismissal under
  United States v. Scott. Most likely reformulated narrowly to the
  Evans/Scott line.
- **Summary route.** Unlikely. If the Court were to act, a summary reversal
  (per curiam applying Evans) is the only plausible summary form; no
  intervening decision supports a GVR. Conditional on a grant, plenary review
  is still more likely than a summary disposition.
- **Separate writing on denial.** Not expected. A brief statement respecting
  denial is a remote possibility given the Justices' recent interest in
  acquittal-finality (McElrath v. Georgia), but I expect a bare denial.
