Favoring review are a preserved federal constitutional question, an expressly entered acquittal following completion of evidence, four appellate dissenters, and the petition's report of a scheduled March 2027 retrial. The threatened second trial makes the issue consequential regardless of ultimate guilt. These considerations keep the chance meaningfully above a negligible error-correction probability. Sources: petition pp. 2–4, 14–16; appendix A; appendix E pp. 54a–55a, 64a.

The strongest counterweight is the functional character of the dismissal. The trial order expressly rests on missing-witness/compulsory-process violations. The appellate majority treats that as distinct from finding the prosecution's proof insufficient (appendix A, paragraph 25). The petition's cross-jurisdiction comparisons involve different predicates, including limitations, jurisdiction treated as an element, and judgments entered in differing procedural settings; they do not clearly establish opposing outcomes on this same compulsory-process record (petition pp. 11–14).

Most importantly, the independent primary-source check of United States v. Scott, 437 U.S. 82 (1978), confirms that Scott itself involved dismissal after all the evidence, on a defense motion alleging constitutional prejudice. Completion of evidence alone therefore does not distinguish this case. Scott's majority preserves a distinction between culpability adjudications and dismissals for other constitutional defects. This supports treating the petition as contestable application of an existing rule rather than a clean need for a new one. The Scott passages consulted are identified in `retrieval.md`. I do not infer that denial would endorse the state court's reasoning. State-law appellate-jurisdiction questions and the record-specific imputation of a witness's conduct to prosecutors add complexity; I do not assert a definite federal-jurisdiction bar.

Those are terminal cohorts, not forward hazards. From zero distributions, a first distribution is highly likely (97%); an actual additional conference after the first is not my central forecast. The 40% summary-route probability is conditional on a grant, not an unconditional 40% chance of relief. The 6% denial-writing probability allows for the finality concern without assuming that the state dissent predicts any named Justice's reaction. None of these auxiliary probabilities is presented as an empirically measured hazard.
