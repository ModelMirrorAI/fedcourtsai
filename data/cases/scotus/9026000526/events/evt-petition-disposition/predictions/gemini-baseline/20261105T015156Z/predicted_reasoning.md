# Forecast of the Court's Action

## Procedural Trajectory (Relists and CVSG)

The docket currently records **one distribution** (distributed on October 28, 2026, for the Conference of November 13, 2026) following respondent State of Mississippi's October 21, 2026 waiver of its right to respond, with no call for a response and no amicus briefs filed.

- **Further distributions (`relist-increment` = 0.04):** The petition will almost certainly be disposed of at its first conference on November 13, 2026, without any further distribution. Because respondent waived a response, the Court would have to call for a response (or reschedule/relist the petition to examine the trial record) before any grant could occur, which would produce at least one additional distribution after the brief in opposition is filed. Given the trial court's explicit denial of petitioner's directed-verdict motions on evidentiary sufficiency and its reliance on a Sixth Amendment compulsory-process theory, there is only a small (~4%) chance that a chamber calls for a response or triggers an additional distribution.
- **Call for the Views of the Solicitor General (`cvsg-increment` = 0.001):** The Court will not invite the Solicitor General to file a brief. This is a state criminal prosecution presenting a Fifth Amendment Double Jeopardy question with no federal statutory, treaty, or administrative interest.

## Question Presented and Merits Route (Conditional on Grant)

- **Question Presented:** If the Court were to grant certiorari, it would take the single Question Presented as framed in the petition—whether the Double Jeopardy Clause bars retrial after jeopardy attached, both sides rested, and the trial court entered a judgment of acquittal based on constitutional violations evaluated in light of the trial record without separately finding the prosecution's evidence legally insufficient.
- **Summary disposition vs. plenary review (`summary-disposition-route` = 0.20):** Conditional on a grant, plenary review (80%) is substantially likelier than a summary disposition in the cert order itself (20%). There is no pending companion case for a hold-and-GVR, and because the trial judge twice denied motions for a directed verdict on evidentiary sufficiency before dismissing on Compulsory Process grounds, the decision below does not present the kind of clear, mechanical contravention of *Evans v. Michigan*, 568 U.S. 313 (2013), or *McElrath v. Georgia*, 601 U.S. 87 (2024), that typically prompts a per curiam summary reversal. If granted for plenary review, the Court would likely address the boundary between *United States v. Scott*, 437 U.S. 82 (1978), and *Evans*/*McElrath* for post-evidence dismissals resting on constitutional trial defects rather than insufficiency of proof on the elements.

## Separate Writings on Denial

- **Dissent from or statement respecting denial (`dissent-from-denial` = 0.01):** Conditional on the expected denial of certiorari, no separate writing is expected (~99% probability of an unadorned denial order).
