The Supreme Court is very likely to deny Lane Douglas Mitchell's petition for review, with only a 1% chance of taking the case. After a four-day attempted-murder trial in Mississippi, the trial judge dismissed the charge and entered a "judgment of acquittal"—a formal ruling clearing a defendant—because the injured victim did not appear to testify, which the judge viewed as a violation of Mitchell's constitutional right to compel witnesses in his defense. A divided Mississippi appeals court allowed a retrial, explaining that the constitutional ban on double jeopardy (being tried twice for the same crime) bars a new trial only when a judge finds the prosecution's evidence insufficient to prove guilt, whereas this judge twice rejected motions challenging the sufficiency of the evidence and dismissed the case over witness unavailability instead. Because the state court's ruling aligns with existing Supreme Court precedent and Mississippi waived its right to respond, the Court is unlikely to intervene.
