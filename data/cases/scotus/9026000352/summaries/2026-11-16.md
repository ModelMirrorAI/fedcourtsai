## In brief

The Supreme Court declined to hear a former RTX employee's bid to revive his whistleblower retaliation lawsuit against the company.

## What happened

Jarin Wray, representing himself, worked for Collins Aerospace, a division of RTX Corporation. He says RTX fired him in 2024 after he reported what he believed was fraud involving RTX's contractor Exiger and that contractor's ties to China, and after he filed complaints with numerous federal agencies. RTX says Wray was fired for using confidential company information to run a competing business. A federal district court dismissed Wray's lawsuit, finding most of the laws he cited do not allow private lawsuits and that his remaining claims were not adequately supported. The Fourth Circuit Court of Appeals affirmed that dismissal, and Wray's petition asked the Supreme Court to review it.

## What the Court is being asked

Wray's petition asks whether federal whistleblower protection laws apply to businesses that have ties to a foreign country, and whether an employee can take legal action against adverse treatment under those circumstances. He also raises a broader question about what legal steps a person who has taken an oath to protect the Constitution may take if they believe the federal government has been compromised by a foreign power.

## Where it stands

The petition was distributed for the Court's November 13, 2026 conference, and on November 16, 2026, the Court denied the petition. Justice Alito did not take part in the decision.

## What each outcome would mean

Wray, as the petitioner, asked the Court to reverse the lower court's ruling against him. By denying the petition, the Court leaves the Fourth Circuit's decision in place, meaning the dismissal of Wray's lawsuit against RTX stands and the case does not continue. The denial does not mean the Court agreed with the lower court's reasoning; it simply leaves that ruling undisturbed without deciding the underlying legal questions Wray raised.
