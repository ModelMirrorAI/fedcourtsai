## In brief

A former RTX employee asks the Court to revive his whistleblower retaliation lawsuit after lower courts dismissed it.

## What happened

Jarin Wray, representing himself, worked for Collins Aerospace, a division of RTX Corporation, until RTX fired him in September 2024. Wray says he was fired for reporting what he believed was fraud involving an RTX contractor's business ties to China. He sued RTX, asserting violations of numerous federal laws, including whistleblower-protection statutes. The federal district court dismissed the case, finding most of the statutes Wray cited do not allow individuals to sue and that his remaining claims did not meet legal pleading requirements. The Fourth Circuit Court of Appeals affirmed that dismissal, and later denied Wray's request for rehearing.

## What the Court is being asked

Wray asks the Court to decide whether businesses operating in the United States that have ties to a foreign country can still claim the protections of American law while taking adverse action against someone who reports wrongdoing, and what actions a former government or military officeholder may take to address a perceived threat to the Constitution from foreign influence on the federal government.

## Where it stands

The petition was distributed for the Court's conference on November 13, 2026. The docket does not yet show any decision on whether the Court will hear the case.

## What each outcome would mean

Wray, who lost in the lower courts, is asking the Court to take up his case. If the Court grants the petition, it would hear the case in full and decide the legal questions, without yet deciding who wins. If the Court denies the petition, the Fourth Circuit's decision affirming dismissal of Wray's lawsuit would stand, leaving the case over and RTX's win in place. A denial would not mean the Court agrees the lower courts were right, only that the case ends there.
