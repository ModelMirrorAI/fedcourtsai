Without a response on file or a call for a response, the petition is on the dead list unless a Justice affirmatively pulls it for discussion or requests a response, which the record gives no reason to expect.

## Conditional Merits and Route Analysis (If Granted)

- **Questions Presented:** If the Court were to grant review, it would not adopt either of the petition's two sweeping, rhetorical questions presented as written (which ask broadly whether U.S. laws apply to businesses linked to foreign adversaries and what actions a retired servicemember may take when the federal government is compromised). Instead, the Court would have to reformulate the question entirely to address the only preserved federal statutory issue discussed in the petition: whether petitioner's filings sufficed to plead administrative exhaustion or protected activity for a Sarbanes-Oxley Act retaliation claim under 18 U.S.C. § 1514A.
- **Summary Disposition vs. Plenary Review (`summary-disposition-route`):** Conditional on the remote event of a grant, a summary disposition (grant, vacate, and remand) is substantially more plausible relative to other pro se cases (though still less than half of the conditional grant probability mass) than setting an unbriefed, pro se pleading dismissal for full merits briefing and oral argument, because the Court would not grant plenary review without first calling for a response. However, no intervening Supreme Court decision—including *Murray v. UBS Securities, LLC*, 601 U.S. 23 (2024), which was decided before the district court's October 20, 2025 order and explicitly cited in that order—undermines the Fourth Circuit's unpublished affirmance. If reached on the merits, the judgment below would be affirmed because 18 U.S.C. § 1514A(b)(1)(A) requires administrative exhaustion with the federal Secretary of Labor before filing suit in federal district court, and *Murray*'s holding on retaliatory intent at trial does not excuse failure to plead statutory exhaustion at the Rule 12(b)(6) stage.
