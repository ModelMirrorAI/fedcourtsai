# Forecast: what the Court will do with Isaacson v. Moses, No. 25-1411

**Disposition.** The petition will be **denied**. P(any grant) = 0.10.

**Relists.** The docket shows **one** distribution (August 5, for the September 28, 2026 conference), which the August 19 call for a response superseded before the petition could be considered. With both briefs in opposition filed September 18 and the reply filed October 2, the petition will be **redistributed at least once more** (P = 0.97) — most likely for a late-October or early-November conference. Two further paths both add distributions rather than removing them: (a) the Court holds this petition for the same petitioner's companion petition on the identical question, *Isaacson v. National Veterans Legal Services Program*, No. 26-302 (Fed. Cir.; respondents' briefs now due November 9, 2026), and decides both together in December or January; or (b) a Justice's chambers takes a serious look, producing one or two relists of the kind that preceded the April 2023 denial in *Johnson v. Dickenson*. Expect one to three additional distributions in total, and a decision on the petition by January 2027.

**CVSG.** No call for the views of the Solicitor General (P = 0.04). The United States is a respondent in the companion No. 26-302 and has already waived its response there; the Court has passed on this question three times since 2023 without inviting the government, and nothing in a private auto-renewal settlement implicates a federal program.

**Which question, if granted.** The Court would take the question presented essentially as written — whether district courts may, consistent with *Greenough* and *Pettus* and without authorization by statute or rule, approve payments from a common fund to compensate class representatives for their personal service — since the categorical question is the only one preserved and the amount of the $5,000 award was never contested. It would not reach the Article III class-definition point the petitioner pressed below and subordinated here.

**Summary disposition.** Very unlikely even conditional on a grant (P = 0.04). No intervening decision of this Court bears on the question, so there is nothing to GVR in light of, and the Court would not summarily reverse a rule adopted by five circuits; a grant here means plenary review, and probably a grant in tandem with No. 26-302.

**Dissent from or statement respecting denial.** Conditional on denial, a separate writing is possible but not expected (P = 0.12). The three prior denials on this question drew no noted dissent. If anyone writes, it would most plausibly be a short statement respecting denial from Justice Thomas or Justice Gorsuch noting the acknowledged split and the pending Rule 23 rulemaking suggestion, rather than a dissent.

**If granted (conditional only).** The likely ground would be that *Greenough* and *Pettus* state a rule of federal common-fund equity that only this Court may revise and that Rule 23, which never mentions such awards, cannot supersede consistent with the Rules Enabling Act — i.e., a reversal favoring the Eleventh Circuit's position, probably with a narrow majority and a dissent arguing the nineteenth-century creditors' suits do not control modern Rule 23 practice.
