# Forecast of the Court's action

The Court will most likely deny the petition, without a separate writing. The forecast assigns a 22% probability to any grant, including a summary grant, and selects denial as the categorical outcome.

The record contains **one distribution**, on August 5, 2026, for the September 28 conference. It does not establish that a conference has already considered the petition. Both respondents waived a response, and the Court requested one on August 19, due September 18. From that state, expect one additional distribution after a response or expiration of the response period, followed by disposition at an ensuing conference. The probability of at least one additional distribution is 98%; that is largely a briefing-related redistribution, not a prediction of substantive repeated relists. The modal path has no additional true relist after the first post-response consideration. A grant path is more likely to include one or two additional conference distributions. These are forecasts from the provisioned moment, not assertions about what occurred between that moment and this run.

Expect no call for the Solicitor General's views: its incremental probability is 1.5%. This is a private settlement dispute about judicial authority under the common-fund doctrine and Rule 23, rather than a case in which the federal government's position appears necessary to resolve the question.

If review is granted, expect the Court to address the petition's single question in substance: whether courts may compensate a class representative's personal service from a common-fund settlement without express statutory or rule authorization, consistently with Greenough and Pettus. The Court would not need to reopen the adequacy of the entire consumer settlement or the attorney-fee award to answer that categorical question. The entrenched disagreement described in the petition supplies a credible reason to hear the case, but does not make review the modal outcome.

Conditional on a grant, plenary briefing and argument are much likelier than disposition in the certiorari order: the summary-route probability is 5%. The record identifies no intervening controlling decision that naturally calls for a grant, vacate, and remand. Reconciling nineteenth-century common-fund cases with modern Rule 23 practice would ordinarily warrant fuller consideration rather than a summary merits ruling. If granted, a likely ground would concern the continuing force and scope of those precedents; this is not an unconditional prediction of a merits opinion.

Conditional on denial, the probability of a noted dissent or statement is 8%. The central prediction is an unsigned denial with no writing. Thomas, Alito, and Gorsuch receive the largest, still small, individual writing probabilities because the petition frames the dispute around fidelity to older precedent and limits on judicially authorized payments. Those are judgmental authorship forecasts, not observed expressions of interest by any Justice. No individual certiorari votes are predicted.
