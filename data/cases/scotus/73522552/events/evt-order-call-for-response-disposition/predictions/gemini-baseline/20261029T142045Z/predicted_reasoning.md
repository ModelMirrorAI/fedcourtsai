## Conditional Merits and Scope of Review

- **Question Presented**: The petition presents a single question: whether federal courts may, despite the common-fund doctrine of *Greenough* and *Pettus* and without authorization by statute or rule, approve payments from class-action common-fund settlements to compensate litigants for their personal service as representative plaintiffs. If the Court were to grant certiorari, it would take this single question presented as written (or slightly reformulated to focus on whether Rule 23(e)(2) and equitable common-fund principles permit reasonable service awards to class representatives).
- **Summary Disposition Route (`summary-disposition-route: 0.05`)**: Conditional on a grant, the Court is overwhelmingly likely (95%) to set the case for plenary merits briefing and oral argument rather than disposing of it summarily in the cert order. A GVR or summary reversal is unsuitable where ten circuits (including the First, Second, Third, Fourth, Fifth, Sixth, Seventh, Eighth, Ninth, and Tenth Circuits, plus the D.C. Circuit) permit class-representative service awards and only the Eleventh Circuit (*Johnson v. NPAS Solutions, LLC*, 975 F.3d 1244 (11th Cir. 2020)) forbids them per se.
- **Conditional Merits Ground**: If granted and argued on the merits, a majority would likely examine whether Rule 23(e)(2)(D)—requiring that a settlement treat class members equitably relative to each other—alongside historical class-action practice either authorizes modest, court-approved service awards or leaves *Greenough*'s common-fund restriction intact absent explicit rulemaking or congressional authorization.
