## 3. Case-Specific Adjustments

### Upward Factors
1. **A Genuine, Acknowledged Circuit Split**: The petition presents a clean, pure question of law on which the circuits are openly divided: whether *Trustees v. Greenough*, 105 U.S. 527 (1882), and *Central Railroad & Banking Co. v. Pettus*, 113 U.S. 116 (1885), prohibit class-representative "incentive" or "service" awards paid out of a common-fund class settlement under Rule 23. In *Johnson v. NPAS Solutions, LLC*, 975 F.3d 1244 (11th Cir. 2020), the Eleventh Circuit held that *Greenough* and *Pettus* render such incentive awards unlawful per se. By contrast, in *Moses I*, 79 F.4th 235, 253–56 (2d Cir. 2023) (Pet. App. 45a–51a), the Second Circuit explicitly rejected *Johnson* and aligned itself with the First, Third, Fourth, Fifth, Sixth, Seventh, Eighth, Ninth, Tenth, and D.C. Circuits (`10–1` or `11–1` split including the Seventh Circuit's subsequent reinforcement in *Scott v. Dart*, 99 F.4th 1076 (7th Cir. 2024)).
2. **Single Focused Question**: Having won vacatur of the initial coupon settlement in *Moses I* on CAFA and Rule 23(e)(2) grounds, Petitioner Eric Alan Isaacson (a veteran class-action objector and retired class-action litigator proceeding pro se) narrowed his cert petition after the *Moses II* remand affirmance exclusively to the *Greenough* / *Pettus* service-award question, avoiding distraction from the fact-bound settlement valuation issues resolved in *Moses II*.

### Downward Factors
1. **Repeated Prior Denials of the Identical Split**: As the petition itself candidly catalogues (Pet. 3, 21 & nn. 5, 65), the Supreme Court has already denied certiorari in *Johnson v. NPAS Solutions* itself (`143 S. Ct. 1745 (2023)`) and in at least half a dozen subsequent petitions from the First, Second, Sixth, Seventh, and Ninth Circuits asking the Court to resolve this exact conflict over *Greenough* and *Pettus* (including *Carson v. Hyland*, 143 S. Ct. 2436 (2023), from the Second Circuit; *Murray v. Grocery Delivery E-Services*, 143 S. Ct. 2586 (2023); *Kazman v. Frontier Oil*, 144 S. Ct. 694 (2024); *Isaacson v. Toy*, 144 S. Ct. 1397 (2024); and *Dart v. Scott*, 145 S. Ct. 446 (2024)). Indeed, this very Petitioner raised the identical question in *Isaacson v. Toy* (No. 23-908), which was denied in 2024. While a justice clearly remains interested enough to trigger a Call for a Response so Respondents cannot simply waive, the Court has consistently fallen short of four votes to grant once the brief in opposition is filed.
2. **Interlocutory/Law-of-the-Case Posture and Summary Order Below**: The Second Circuit's published analysis of *Greenough* and *Pettus* occurred in *Moses I* (decided August 17, 2023, 79 F.4th at 253–56), where the panel vacated the original settlement on other grounds. After the district court approved a revised $2.375 million cash settlement on remand, the Second Circuit in *Moses II* (Dec. 17, 2025, Pet. App. 10a) issued a non-precedential summary order declining to revisit its *Moses I* holding under law of the case. Although a party may seek Supreme Court review of an earlier interlocutory ruling after final judgment (*Mercer v. Theriot*, 377 U.S. 152 (1964)), Respondents in their forthcoming BIO will emphasize that the decision directly under review (`2025 WL 3654242`) is an unpublished summary order and that zero amici have filed in support of the petition (`amicus_briefs: 0`).

Weighing the positive signal of the Call for a Response and the acknowledged circuit split against the Court's repeated, recent denials of the exact same question (including in Petitioner's own prior petition), I place `probability` at **0.06** (`granted: 0`, `predicted_disposition: "denied"`).
