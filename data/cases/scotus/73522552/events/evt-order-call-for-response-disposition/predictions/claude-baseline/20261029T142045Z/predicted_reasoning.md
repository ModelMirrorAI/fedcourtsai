# Forecast: Isaacson v. Moses, No. 25-1411 (after the call for a response)

**Disposition: denied.** The Court will deny the petition once the response is
in, at the first conference after the response and the reply have been
distributed.

**Distributions.** The docket shows one distribution, for the conference of
September 28, 2026. The Court requested a response on August 19, 2026, due
September 18, so the petition will come off that conference and be distributed
again once the response is filed or the time to file it runs out. That further
distribution is all but certain, and the harness masks the claim for this
moment. Beyond that one re-distribution I expect no real relist. The petition is
decided at its first post-response conference.

**CVSG.** None. No federal party is involved, and the question concerns the
Court's own common-fund precedents and Rule 23 practice, on which the Court
does not need the Solicitor General's views. Probability about 2%.

**Question the Court would take, if it granted.** The single question as
written: whether district courts may, without authority from a statute or
rule, pay incentive or service awards to class representatives out of a
common-fund settlement, consistent with *Trustees v. Greenough* and *Central
Railroad & Banking Co. v. Pettus*. It is possible the Court would rephrase it
around Rule 23(e)(2) rather than the nineteenth-century cases.

**Summary disposition.** Unlikely. No intervening decision of this Court calls
for a GVR, and a summary reversal of a settled equitable-practice question
backed by five circuits is implausible. If the Court granted, it would set the
case for plenary review (about 95%).

**Separate writing on denial.** Most likely none. If anyone writes, the likeliest
author is Justice Thomas or Justice Gorsuch, perhaps with a statement that the
Eleventh Circuit's reading of *Greenough* and *Pettus* deserves review in a
better vehicle. About a 5% chance of any dissent from or statement respecting
denial.
