## In brief

The Court declined to hear a class-member objector's challenge to a $5,000 incentive payment awarded to the named plaintiff in a class-action settlement with the New York Times.

## What happened

Maribel Moses sued The New York Times Company, claiming it automatically renewed California subscribers without required disclosures. The parties settled, and the district court approved the settlement, attorney's fees, and a $5,000 payment to Moses for her work as class representative. Eric Alan Isaacson, a class member, objected, arguing such incentive payments are unlawful under old Supreme Court precedent. The Second Circuit rejected that argument twice, most recently in an unpublished order affirming a second, improved settlement. Isaacson then asked the Supreme Court to take the case.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court was asked whether old rulings limiting what representative plaintiffs may be paid from a shared settlement fund still bar courts from approving modest payments to class representatives, given that most appeals courts now allow such payments while one does not.

## Where it stands

The petition was distributed for conference twice, after the Court asked for a response from the other side; both respondents and the petitioner filed briefs. The Supreme Court denied the petition.

## What each outcome would mean

Isaacson, the objector, asked the Court to overturn the Second Circuit's decision upholding the $5,000 payment to Moses. By denying the petition, the Court leaves the Second Circuit's ruling in place: the settlement, including the incentive payment to Moses, stands as approved. The denial does not mean the Court agreed the payment was lawful; it simply leaves the lower court's decision undisturbed, and the broader disagreement among appeals courts over such payments remains unresolved.
