## In brief

A class-action objector asks the Court to decide whether judges may award class representatives payments for their service out of settlement funds.

## What happened

Maribel Moses sued The New York Times, alleging it renewed subscriptions without disclosures California law requires. After one settlement was overturned on appeal, the parties reached a new $2,375,000 settlement that included a $5,000 payment to Moses for serving as class representative. Eric Alan Isaacson, a class member, objected, arguing such payments are unlawful under old Supreme Court precedent. The district court approved the settlement and payment anyway, and the Second Circuit affirmed, saying its own prior rulings already rejected Isaacson's argument.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked whether judges may approve payments to class representatives from settlement funds to compensate them for their work on the case, given older Supreme Court rulings that Isaacson says forbid paying litigants for their personal efforts, apart from reimbursing lawyers' fees and expenses.

## Where it stands

The petition was distributed for conference twice, most recently for November 6, 2026, after both respondents filed briefs opposing review and the petitioner filed a reply. The docket shows no decision yet on whether the Court will hear the case.

## What each outcome would mean

Isaacson, the petitioner, asked the Court to take up the case. If the Court grants review, it would hear full arguments and decide whether such payments are lawful, without yet deciding who wins. If it denies review, the Second Circuit's ruling upholding the $5,000 payment and the settlement stands, leaving Moses and The New York Times with their approved settlement and the question unresolved nationally, since other circuits have split on it.
