## In brief

A class-action objector asks the Court to decide whether a $5,000 payment to the lead plaintiff in a settlement was lawful.

## What happened

Maribel Moses sued The New York Times over automatically renewed subscriptions, reaching a class settlement that included a $5,000 payment to her for serving as class representative. Eric Alan Isaacson, a class member, objected, arguing such payments are categorically forbidden under old Supreme Court common-fund rulings. The Second Circuit rejected that argument twice, most recently affirming the settlement, fee award, and the $5,000 payment in an unpublished order, and denied rehearing. Isaacson then asked the Supreme Court to take up the question.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked whether judges have the authority to approve payments, out of money won for a class, that specifically compensate the named plaintiff for personally serving as class representative, or whether older Supreme Court rulings about shared litigation funds forbid such payments without specific authorization in a statute or rule.

## Where it stands

The petition has been fully briefed, with opposition briefs from both Moses and the Times, an amicus brief supporting the petition, and a reply from Isaacson. It was distributed for the Court's conference on November 6, 2026, where the justices will decide whether to take the case.

## What each outcome would mean

Isaacson, the objector, is asking the Court to hear the case. If the Court grants review, it would hear full arguments and decide whether such payments are allowed, with no outcome yet determined. If the Court denies review, the Second Circuit's decision stands, leaving the settlement, including Moses's $5,000 payment, in place and unaffected by further Supreme Court proceedings.
