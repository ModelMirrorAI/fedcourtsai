## In brief

A class-action objector asks the Court to review whether courts may pay class representatives "incentive awards" from settlement funds.

## What happened

Maribel Moses sued the New York Times over subscription renewal practices, and the case settled, with class counsel awarded fees and Moses awarded a $5,000 payment for serving as class representative. Eric Alan Isaacson, a class member, objected to the settlement and the award, arguing such payments are unlawful. The district court approved the settlement twice, after the Second Circuit sent the case back once to fix other issues. The Second Circuit ultimately affirmed, rejecting Isaacson's argument that older Supreme Court precedent bars these awards, consistent with most other circuits' rulings. Isaacson then asked the Supreme Court to take the case.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked whether a long-standing rule limiting what money class representatives can personally receive from a shared settlement fund still prevents courts from approving modest payments to compensate them for serving as representatives, or whether modern class-action practice allows such payments despite that older rule.

## Where it stands

The petition has been distributed for conference twice, most recently for the November 6, 2026 conference, after both respondents filed briefs opposing review and Isaacson filed a reply. The docket shows no decision yet on whether the Court will hear the case.

## What each outcome would mean

Isaacson, the objector, is asking the Court to take up the case and rule that these payments are unlawful. If the Court grants review, it will decide the legal question itself, with no party yet determined to be right. If the Court denies review, the Second Circuit's decision stands, leaving in place the settlement, the fee award, and Moses's $5,000 payment, and leaving other circuits' similar rulings undisturbed as well.
