## Baseline and adjustments

The frozen band is **baseline**, salience version **sal-v4**, Term **2026**. Petitioners are private companies; the state amici do not make this a state-petitioner case. The matching segment table in the committed statpack renders ten Terms, 2017–2026. Pooling every displayed strictly-prior Term, 2017–2025, using each baseline band's bracketed reached rate and its weighted resolved denominator gives **660 / 12,913 = 5.111%**. I computed this from the full-precision `segments` entries in `metrics/statpack.json`, not from rounded percentages. I excluded the 2026 row and did not use the lower terminal-baseline rate, which conditions on never progressing to a higher band. This is the committed pack's historical anchor, not a fresh corpus-wide estimate or a response-requested conditional rate.

The upward adjustment to 18% is substantial but discretionary. The Court affirmatively requested a response after waiver. The petition raises an important access-to-federal-court question, identifies express disagreement over whether third-party causation must be determinative or coercive, and comes from a divided published appellate decision. The provisioned docket also has two amicus-related entries: a states' brief and the National Shooting Sports Foundation's motion to file a brief. I count the latter once, not as both a motion and a separate filed brief. An additional organization's appearance in the attorney metadata is not evidence that another brief had been filed by this cutoff. Experienced Supreme Court counsel and multi-state interest support seriousness, not a finding that review is likely.

The paid-segment amicus cut is a useful scale check: its 2+ bucket has 1,449 resolved cases and a grant-family share of about 22.9% (18.4% granted plus 4.5% GVR). That is a terminal, all-Term, marginal cut, not a conditional estimate for this cell; I neither substitute it for the band anchor nor add its effect independently. The paid-segment relist-0 bucket is about 1.8% grant-family over 10,203 resolved cases; later terminal buckets rise to about 13.7% at one relist (n=2,538), 41.2% at two (n=495), and 37.4% at three or more (n=484). These show selection, not transition probabilities. Our one initial distribution is not evidence that a conference has already relisted this petition. The CVSG cut likewise shows about 34.8% grants/GVRs among 164 resolved invited cases, versus 6.4% among 13,556 without an invitation. No CVSG exists here. Those descriptive cuts pool Terms and do not override the strictly-prior band anchor.

## Why denial remains the central forecast

The main limiting factor is the vehicle, not lack of doctrinal importance. Petitioners describe a three-to-three circuit split, but the bound-in Fourth Circuit majority both rejects an exclusively determinative-or-coercive test and finds the complaints sufficient under that stricter formulation in the alternative (App.31–37). Choosing the strict test therefore does not by itself reverse the judgment. Petitioners must persuade the Court that the alternative application is untenable, which turns a clean-looking standards dispute into a more fact-dependent pleading dispute.

The question presented also requires an advocacy discount. The reproduced allegations include generalized claims of exposure to advertising during online purchasing and preparation for the shooting (App.18–19). Petitioners dispute their factual sufficiency and emphasize the lack of an identified advertisement the shooter actually saw. The appellate majority's answer is that individualized identification is not necessary at this pleading stage. This is not simply a conceded absence of any exposure allegation. That distinction reduces the force of a request for summary correction without full briefing.

Finally, the judgment sends the litigation back for further proceedings. Statutory defenses under the Protection of Lawful Commerce in Arms Act, causation on the merits, and the asserted speech defenses leave additional paths for narrowing or ending the case below. Importantly, the appellate court vacated the district court's alternative statutory disposition; I do not treat that vacated ruling as an intact independent basis sustaining the appellate judgment. These unresolved issues nonetheless make waiting for a more developed vehicle plausible. The broad consequences justify a stakes score of 0.73 even though review remains unlikely.

## Other claims and limitations

The 0.98 distribution-increment call largely reflects ordinary redistribution after the requested response; it does not mean a 98% chance of a substantive post-conference relist. The harness masks that claim at this moment. The 0.025 CVSG estimate reflects limited need for governmental input into this private dispute. The 0.35 conditional summary-route estimate gives real weight to the petition's express summary-reversal request, but the competing pleading interpretations favor plenary treatment if review is granted. Conditional on denial, I assign 0.14 to any dissent or statement. Individual writer probabilities are low and include possible writings on a grant-side summary order, so they need not sum to that conditional number.

External corroboration was degraded. CourtListener MCP returned HTTP 429 on a lookup of the cited FDA standing precedent. Web searches and attempts to open/search the Court's FDA opinion returned no usable results or text. I made no direct CourtListener REST fallback. The legal characterization therefore rests on the provisioned petition and its appellate appendix, not independently retrieved precedent. A required structured corpus query succeeded, but its four grant-filtered results were not close substantive analogues and cannot estimate grant frequency; I did not use their outcomes to move the number. A read-only independent reviewer identified the alternative-holding and exposure-allegation issues; I checked both against the provisioned text before using them.
