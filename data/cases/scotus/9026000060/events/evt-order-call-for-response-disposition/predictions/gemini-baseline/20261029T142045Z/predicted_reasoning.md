## 3. Disposition and Question Presented (`disposition`: 0.14; `predicted_disposition`: `denied`)

The Court will likely deny the petition for a writ of certiorari after the brief in opposition is filed (`probability` = 0.14). Although the Fourth Circuit's expansive view of Article III traceability at the Rule 12(b)(1) stage is in tension with *Murthy v. Missouri*, 603 U.S. 43 (2024), and *FDA v. Alliance for Hippocratic Medicine*, 602 U.S. 367 (2024), two threshold vehicle obstacles weigh heavily against plenary review at this stage:
1. **Interlocutory Remand with Dispositive Statutory Defenses**: The Fourth Circuit remanded the case after vacating the district court's alternative Rule 12(b)(6) dismissal under the PLCAA purely because the district court had found a lack of Article III jurisdiction first. On remand—now that the Fourth Circuit has held Article III jurisdiction exists—the district court is free to re-enter dismissal on the merits under Rule 12(b)(6) based on the PLCAA (especially following *Smith & Wesson Brands, Inc. v. Estados Unidos Mexicanos*, 605 U.S. ___ (2025)) and lack of proximate causation under Virginia law.
2. **Alternative Holding Below**: The Fourth Circuit majority explicitly held in the alternative (App. 34–41) that the complaints' allegations, including those pleaded on information and belief, satisfied *Bennett v. Spear*'s "determinative or coercive effect" test as well as *Department of Commerce v. New York*'s "predictable effect" standard. Consequently, the case does not cleanly isolate the legal choice between those two formulations.

If the Court were to grant certiorari, it would take the single question presented as written—whether plaintiffs can satisfy Article III traceability by alleging that a third party's criminal acts are traceable to marketing and social-media speech without alleging specific facts showing the third party saw or was coerced by each defendant's speech—and would likely reverse the Fourth Circuit under *Murthy* and *TransUnion LLC v. Ramirez*, 594 U.S. 413 (2021) ("standing is not dispensed in gross").
