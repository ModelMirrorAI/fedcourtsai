## In brief

Firearms manufacturers ask the Court to reverse a ruling letting shooting victims sue them over marketing, even though the shooter is not alleged to have seen it.

## What happened

A shooter opened fire near a Washington, D.C. school in 2022, wounding Karen Lowy and Antonio Harris. They sued more than a dozen firearms, accessory, and ammunition manufacturers whose products the shooter had purchased, arguing the companies' marketing foreseeably encouraged young men like him toward violence. A federal district court dismissed the suits, finding the victims lacked standing because they could not show the marketing had a determinative or coercive effect on the shooter. A divided Fourth Circuit panel reversed, holding a looser "predictable effect" standard was enough, even without allegations the shooter saw the ads. One judge dissented.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked whether people suing over a third party's criminal act can meet the constitutional requirement that their injury be traceable to the defendants' conduct, when they do not allege the third party ever saw or encountered the defendants' marketing or speech.

## Where it stands

The petition was distributed for the Court's September 28, 2026 conference. The Court then requested a response from the respondents, due October 26, 2026, after the respondents had initially waived their right to respond. The docket shows no further action since.

## What each outcome would mean

Daniel Defense and the other manufacturers, as petitioners, are asking the Court to take up and reverse the Fourth Circuit's ruling. If the Court grants the petition, it would hear the case and decide the traceability question itself; granting cert alone would not determine who wins. If it denies the petition, the Fourth Circuit's decision reviving the victims' lawsuits stands, and the case would continue in the lower courts under that court's looser standard. Either way, no outcome at this stage decides the underlying claims against the manufacturers.
