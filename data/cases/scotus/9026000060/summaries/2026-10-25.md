## In brief

Firearms manufacturers ask the Court to shut down a lawsuit by shooting victims, arguing the victims cannot show their injuries are legally traceable to the companies' advertising.

## What happened

Karen Lowy and Antonio Harris were shot and seriously injured in a 2022 mass shooting near a school in Washington, D.C. They sued more than a dozen manufacturers of firearms and related products the shooter had purchased, claiming the companies' marketing encouraged violence and caused their injuries. A federal district court dismissed the case, finding the victims lacked standing to sue because they could not show the shooter's actions were sufficiently connected to the companies' marketing. A divided Fourth Circuit panel reversed, holding the victims had alleged enough of a connection, even though they did not claim the shooter ever saw the specific marketing material at issue. One judge dissented.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked whether people suing over a third party's criminal acts can meet the constitutional requirement of showing their injuries are connected to a defendant's conduct, when they allege only that the defendant's marketing could predictably reach someone like the attacker, without claiming the attacker ever actually saw or was influenced by that specific marketing.

## Where it stands

The petition was distributed for the Court's September 28, 2026 conference. After that conference, the Court requested a response from the respondents, due October 26, 2026; the docket shows no further action since.

## What each outcome would mean

The manufacturers, as petitioners, are asking the Court to reverse the Fourth Circuit's decision, which had allowed the lawsuit to go forward. If the Court takes the case and ultimately sides with the manufacturers, the lawsuit would likely be thrown out for lack of standing. If it sides with Lowy and Harris, the case could proceed toward further litigation on the underlying claims. If the Court instead denies review, the Fourth Circuit's ruling allowing the lawsuit to continue would remain in place.
