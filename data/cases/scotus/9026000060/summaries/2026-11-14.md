## In brief

Gun manufacturers ask the Court to throw out a lawsuit by shooting victims, arguing the victims cannot show their injuries are legally traceable to the companies' marketing.

## What happened

Karen Lowy and Antonio Harris were shot in 2022 outside a Washington, D.C. school by a young man using weapons made by Daniel Defense and other manufacturers. They sued the manufacturers in Virginia federal court, claiming deceptive marketing encouraged the shooter to use these products violently. A district court dismissed the case, ruling the victims could not show their injuries were sufficiently connected to the marketing. The Fourth Circuit reversed, holding the complaints plausibly alleged that connection, under either of two legal tests it considered. One judge dissented.

## What the Court is being asked

The manufacturers ask the Court to decide what a person suing over harm caused by someone else's criminal act must show to prove the harm is legally connected to the defendant's conduct, when the complaint does not allege that the person who committed the act actually saw the defendant's marketing. The manufacturers say appeals courts disagree on how strong that connection must be; the victims respond that the asserted disagreement does not really exist and would not change the outcome here.

## Where it stands

The petition has been distributed for conference twice, most recently for December 4, 2026, after the victims filed a brief opposing review and the manufacturers filed a reply. No order on the petition has been recorded yet.

## What each outcome would mean

The manufacturers, who lost in the Fourth Circuit, are asking the Court to take up and reverse that ruling. If the Court grants review, it would hear full arguments on what showing is required and decide the question itself, without yet determining who wins. If it denies review, the Fourth Circuit's decision stands, and the victims' lawsuit continues in the lower courts, where the manufacturers could still raise other defenses, including a federal law shielding gun makers from certain liability claims.
