## In brief

Gun manufacturers ask the Court to shut down lawsuits by shooting victims who say the companies' marketing helped cause the attack.

## What happened

A gunman shot and critically wounded Karen Lowy and Antonio Harris outside a Washington, D.C. school in 2022 using weapons and accessories made by several manufacturers, including Daniel Defense. Lowy and Harris sued the manufacturers, claiming their marketing unlawfully encouraged violent use of their products and contributed to the attack. A federal district court dismissed the case, ruling the victims could not show their injuries were sufficiently connected to the companies' marketing to allow the suit to proceed in federal court. The Fourth Circuit Court of Appeals reversed that ruling, allowing the case to continue, over a dissent from one judge.

## What the Court is being asked

The manufacturers ask the Court to decide what a person suing over harm caused by someone else's criminal act must show to prove the harm is connected enough to the defendant's conduct to be heard in federal court—specifically, whether it is enough to allege that the conduct could have influenced that third person, even without alleging the person ever encountered the material said to have influenced them.

## Where it stands

The petition has been fully briefed, including a brief opposing review from Lowy and Harris, and was distributed for the Court's conference on September 28, 2026. The docket shows no record of the Court's action since then.

## What each outcome would mean

Daniel Defense and the other manufacturers are asking the Court to take up the case and reverse the Fourth Circuit's decision. If the Court grants review, it will decide the standard for this kind of lawsuit and could ultimately end the case against the manufacturers or send it back for further proceedings under that standard. If the Court denies review, the Fourth Circuit's decision stands, and Lowy and Harris's lawsuit continues in the lower courts, where the manufacturers may still raise other defenses.
