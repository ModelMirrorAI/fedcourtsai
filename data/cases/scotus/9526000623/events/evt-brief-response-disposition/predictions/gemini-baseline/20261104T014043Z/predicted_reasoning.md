## 2. Application of the Stay Standard (Conditional Merits & Separate Writings)

Under *Barefoot v. Estelle*, 463 U.S. 880, 895 (1983), *Hill v. McDonough*, 547 U.S. 573, 584 (2006), and *Nken v. Holder*, 556 U.S. 418, 434 (2009), a stay of execution pending disposition of a petition for a writ of certiorari requires a reasonable probability that four Justices will vote to grant certiorari and a fair prospect that five Justices will vote to reverse the judgment below.

Neither requirement is met:
1. **State-Law Procedural Bars:** The Florida Supreme Court held that Guardado's penalty-phase and initial-postconviction ineffective-assistance and mitigation claims (concerning the Dozier School for Boys and trial preparation) are untimely under Fla. R. Crim. P. 3.851(d) and procedurally barred under settled state law because they could have been raised in prior collateral proceedings decades ago. That holding constitutes an independent and adequate state-law ground barring federal review (*Coleman v. Thompson*, 501 U.S. 722, 729 (1991)), and sixth-amendment / *Martinez* principles do not create a freestanding constitutional right to effective postconviction counsel overriding state successive-petition bars.
2. **Executive Clemency & Warrant Litigation Schedule:** Guardado received appointed clemency counsel, a clemency interview in July 2026, and executive consideration prior to the signing of the death warrant, satisfying the minimal procedural safeguards discussed in Justice O'Connor's concurrence in *Ohio Adult Parole Authority v. Woodard*, 523 U.S. 272, 289 (1998). Likewise, Guardado received notice and a full opportunity to litigate his successive postconviction motion in the state circuit court, the Florida Supreme Court, and this Court during the 31-day warrant period.

In the unlikely event the Court were to grant a stay and certiorari, review would focus on whether the Fourteenth Amendment's Due Process Clause under *Woodard* requires formal notice of clemency denial prior to the issuance of a death warrant or constrains a governor's categorical refusal to commute murder sentences. However, consistent with the Court's uniform denial of stays without recorded dissent across the recent series of Florida capital warrant applications raising identical claims, the Court will deny the stay application by an unsigned order with no separate writings (`order-writers` probabilities ≤ 0.04 across all nine Justices, with only a small tail chance of a brief statement or dissent from Justice Sotomayor or Justice Jackson).
