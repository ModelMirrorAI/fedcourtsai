# Likely basis, if explained

The expected denial will probably carry no substantive rationale. If the Court explains it, the likely grounds are insufficient prospects of review and reversal on the asserted clemency due-process claim, potentially reinforced by the timing of the challenge. The critical distinction is between minimal safeguards ensuring access to clemency and judicial control of the executive's ultimate decision to extend mercy. The applicant's claimed categorical policy makes that distinction contestable but does not, on the supplied papers, make relief likely.

If relief is granted, it will most plausibly preserve time to examine whether the alleged categorical exclusion makes the available clemency process constitutionally meaningless under Woodard, rather than decide the validity of the conviction or sentence itself. This conditional account is not a forecast that the Court will issue a merits opinion.
