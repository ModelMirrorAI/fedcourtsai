## Why relief is unlikely

The application's principal developed claim is that Florida's clemency process violates due process because a long absence of clemency grants and the governor's asserted categorical opposition to clemency make the process predetermined. These are the applicant's allegations, not independently established facts. I do not rely on the filing's specific execution or warrant totals. The references to the Sixth and Eighth Amendments do not come with separately developed arguments in this short application.

The legal comparison is Ohio Adult Parole Authority v. Woodard, 523 U.S. 272, 288–290 (1998), specifically Justice O'Connor's concurrence in the judgment, retrieved as CourtListener opinion 9433600. That opinion recognizes limited procedural protection for clemency but rejects the particular procedural objections presented there. Its examples concern arbitrary decision machinery or denial of access; it does not promise favorable clemency outcomes. My inference is that a long run of adverse results and an executive's stated policy are not, without more, strong evidence that the minimum constitutional process was denied to this applicant. The categorical-policy allegation is nevertheless the best argument for intervention: a documented refusal to consider an individual's presentation could be materially different from ordinary executive discretion. The missing petition and lower-court reasoning leave that distinction uncertain.

The filing itself invokes Barefoot v. Estelle, 463 U.S. 880, 895 (1983), for a reasonable probability of review, a significant possibility of reversal, and irreparable harm. Execution plainly supplies irreparable harm if the legal claim is valid, but harm alone does not establish the review-and-reversal predicates. The provided application identifies no circuit conflict, intervening decision, or particular exclusion from the clemency process. I therefore expect the Court to find the merits showing inadequate for a stay. I retain a small positive probability for stronger underlying evidence or a short protective stay; a grant of requested relief in part is included in the 3%. I make no mootness discount.
