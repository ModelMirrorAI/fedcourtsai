## In brief

The Court denied Jesse Guardado's request to stay his execution and denied his related petition challenging Florida's clemency process.

## What happened

Jesse Guardado, a Florida death-row prisoner, was scheduled for execution on November 5, 2026. The Florida Supreme Court denied him relief on October 29, 2026. Guardado then asked the U.S. Supreme Court to stay his execution while it considered a petition for certiorari (a request that the Court hear his case) arguing that Florida's clemency process denies him due process because the state has not granted clemency to a death-sentenced prisoner since 1983 and the current governor has said he will not grant clemency to murderers. Florida responded that Guardado delayed raising this challenge and that his claims lack merit.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court was asked whether Florida's clemency process, which Guardado says routinely and automatically denies mercy to death-sentenced prisoners, provides so little meaningful review that it violates the constitutional minimum protections required for clemency proceedings. A stay would pause the execution while the Court decided whether to take up that question.

## Where it stands

The Court denied both the stay application and the underlying petition for certiorari on November 5, 2026, the day the execution was scheduled.

## What each outcome would mean

Guardado, as the applicant, asked the Court to pause his execution and then to review Florida's clemency process. By denying the stay and denying certiorari, the Court left the Florida Supreme Court's decision against Guardado in place and did not pause or stop the scheduled execution. The denial of certiorari means the Court will not review the merits of his due process challenge to Florida's clemency system.
