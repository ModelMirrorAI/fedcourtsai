# Forecast of the Court's action

The Court will most likely deny certiorari after receiving Mississippi's response. The October 27, 2026 snapshot records one distribution, for the November 6 conference, followed by a response request with a November 27 deadline. Expect at least one additional distribution after the response window closes, rather than a decision at that originally scheduled conference. One further distribution and denial is the modal path; a prolonged sequence of genuine post-conference relists is less likely. A redistribution needed to obtain briefing is not itself evidence that the Court seriously considered and then relisted the case.

A call for the Solicitor General's views is a substantial alternative, though slightly less likely than proceeding without one: probability 42%. The issue concerns administration of a federal drug-discount program, and the petition expressly asks for that consultation. If an invitation issues, expect it after the opposition and reply are available, in winter 2026–27 or early spring 2027; it would probably postpone final action substantially. Without an invitation or a hold, expect a disposition in early 2027 rather than in November 2026.

The petition presents an important dispute, but the claimed circuit conflict does not yet establish opposing holdings on the same state-law preemption question. The Third and D.C. Circuit decisions described in the petition concern federal agency authority; the Fifth and Eighth Circuit decisions uphold state regulation. The Court can leave that distinction to develop further. This case also arrives from denial of a preliminary injunction rather than a final merits judgment.

If review is granted, expect a question focused on whether Section 340B preempts Mississippi's restrictions on manufacturers' conditions for delivery through contract pharmacies, rather than an unrestricted inquiry into every state addition to the program. Plenary briefing and argument is much likelier than disposition in the certiorari order: the conditional summary-route probability is 8%. The record identifies no intervening Supreme Court decision that supplies a ready-made remand, and deciding this disputed preemption question summarily would be an unusual route. If granted, the likely doctrinal contest is whether Congress preserved manufacturers' non-price discretion against state supplementation or instead left drug distribution within traditional state regulatory authority.

Expect an unsigned denial without a recorded dissent or statement; the probability of any such writing conditional on denial is 8%. Thomas, Alito, and Gorsuch are the relatively more plausible writers if a separate statement appears, but no particular Justice's writing is the modal forecast. No merits opinion or merits vote lineup is predicted on the denial path.
