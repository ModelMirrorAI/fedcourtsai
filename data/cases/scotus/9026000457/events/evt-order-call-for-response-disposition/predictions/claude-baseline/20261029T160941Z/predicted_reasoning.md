# Forecast — Novartis Pharmaceuticals Corp. v. Fitch, No. 26-457

**State forecast from.** One distribution on the record (for the November 6, 2026 conference),
no relist, no CVSG. Mississippi waived its response on October 20; the Court called for a
response on October 27 (due November 27, 2026). One amicus brief (Community Oncology Alliance)
is docketed; the petitioner's letter says at least one more (Washington Legal Foundation) is coming.

**Further distribution.** Near-certain. The November 6 conference will not act on the petition with
a response outstanding; once Mississippi's brief in opposition (or an extension of it) is in and the
reply window closes, the petition is distributed again — most likely for a January 2027 conference.
I expect at least one further distribution and a real chance of several (relists or a hold).

**CVSG.** About one in four. The petition itself asks for one, the 340B program is a federal spending
program administered by HHS, and the Court asked for the government's views in no comparable
state-340B case so far. Against that, the United States has already stated its position (supporting
the manufacturers) in amicus briefs in seven courts of appeals, which makes an invitation less
informative. If a CVSG issues, expect it on the first post-response conference (early 2027).

**Disposition.** Most likely denied, or held and then denied. The cleanest split the petition
pleads — the Fourth Circuit's McCuskey and Brown decisions striking West Virginia's and Maryland's
laws — was vacated when that court granted rehearing en banc, so what remains is the Fifth and
Eighth Circuits upholding these laws against D.C. and Third Circuit decisions that address HHS's
authority rather than state preemption. The posture is interlocutory (denial of a preliminary
injunction) and the Fifth Circuit's opinion is a short panel decision bound by an earlier panel. The
Court denied the Eighth Circuit's McClain case in 2024. The likeliest course is to wait for the Fourth
Circuit en banc: a decision there striking a state law would create a clean split and draw a grant
in a later vehicle, possibly this one if it is held. A grant this Term is a live minority outcome
(roughly one in six).

**If granted.** The Court would take the question as written — whether state laws imposing added
terms on 340B manufacturers (contract-pharmacy delivery mandates) are preempted — probably focused
on obstacle/conflict preemption and Buckman-style interference with a federal-agency relationship.
Plenary review, not a summary disposition: there is no intervening decision to GVR on, and the
question is not one the Court would decide without briefing.

**Separate writing on a denial.** Unlikely. If any, the most plausible author is Justice Thomas or
Justice Gorsuch (on the preemption framework) or Justice Alito, who handled the extensions as
Circuit Justice; a statement respecting denial noting the pending Fourth Circuit en banc is the
likeliest form.
