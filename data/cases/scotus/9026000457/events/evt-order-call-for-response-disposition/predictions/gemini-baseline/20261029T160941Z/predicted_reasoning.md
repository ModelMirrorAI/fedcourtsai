# Predicted Court Reasoning & Procedural Trajectory: *Novartis Pharmaceuticals Corporation v. Lynn Fitch, Attorney General of Mississippi* (No. 26-457)

## 1. Procedural Trajectory (Relists, Response, and CVSG)

- **Current Docket State:** As of the October 27, 2026 snapshot, the petition has been distributed once (`distribution_count: 1`, scheduled for the November 6, 2026 Conference) following Respondent's October 14, 2026 waiver, and on October 27, 2026 the Court called for a response (due November 27, 2026). One cert-stage amicus brief (by Community Oncology Alliance, Inc.) is on the docket.
- **Further Distributions (`relist-increment: 0.99`):** Because the Court called for a response after the initial October 21 distribution for the November 6 Conference, the petition will be taken off the November 6 conference list until Respondent files the brief in opposition (either around the November 27, 2026 due date or in December 2026 if a brief extension is granted) and will then be redistributed for a subsequent conference (likely in January 2027). Accordingly, at least one additional distribution past the snapshot's `distribution_count = 1` is virtually certain (`0.99`). Once re-distributed with the brief in opposition and reply brief in hand, the Court will likely dispose of the petition after one or two conferences (possibly coordinating scheduling with the companion Fifth Circuit petitions in *AbbVie v. Fitch* and *AbbVie v. Murrill*).
- **Call for the Views of the Solicitor General (`cvsg-increment: 0.18`):** Although the case implicates the federal Section 340B Drug Pricing Program (42 U.S.C. § 256b) administered by HHS/HRSA, the Court previously denied certiorari in *Pharmaceutical Research & Manufacturers of America v. McClain*, 145 S. Ct. 768 (2024), on the exact same question without a grant of review, and the federal government's position on the statutory silence of Section 340B regarding contract-pharmacy delivery was already thoroughly aired in *Sanofi Aventis U.S. LLC v. HHS*, 58 F.4th 696 (3d Cir. 2023), and *Novartis Pharmaceuticals Corp. v. Johnson*, 102 F.4th 452 (D.C. Cir. 2024). There is a modest (`0.18`) chance the Court invites the Solicitor General's views to assess whether the proliferation of state contract-pharmacy mandates interferes with HRSA's administration of Section 340B or the Administrative Dispute Resolution (ADR) process, but the far likelier course (`0.82`) is that the Court denies certiorari directly after reviewing Mississippi's brief in opposition.

## 2. Disposition Route & Question Presented

- **Expected Disposition (`denied`, `probability: 0.14`):** The Court will deny the petition for a writ of certiorari. Every published court of appeals decision addressing state laws that prohibit pharmaceutical manufacturers from restricting contract-pharmacy delivery of 340B drugs—the Eighth Circuit in *McClain* (95 F.4th 1136) and *Hanaway* (165 F.4th 1168), the Fifth Circuit in this case (133 F.4th 456) and the *AbbVie* cases (152 F.4th 619; 155 F.4th 413), and the Seventh Circuit in *Eli Lilly & Co. v. Rokita* (175 F.4th 463)—has rejected field and conflict preemption. The Fourth Circuit's decision in *AstraZeneca Pharmaceuticals LP v. Morrisey*, No. 24-1939, 2026 WL 1005576 (4th Cir. Apr. 14, 2026), is unpublished, turned significantly on West Virginia's separate statutory ban on manufacturer claims-data audits (which directly implicated 42 U.S.C. § 256b(a)(5)(C)), and merely vacated and remanded for further factual development on how the replenishment model operated. Moreover, this case arrives in an interlocutory posture from the denial of a preliminary injunction sought one day after the complaint was filed, with no discovery or evidentiary record.
- **Question Presented if Granted (Context-Only):** If the Court were to grant certiorari, it would take the single Question Presented in the petition as written—whether state laws imposing contract-pharmacy delivery mandates on drug manufacturers participating in the federal 340B Program are preempted under the Supremacy Clause—potentially consolidated with or held alongside the companion *AbbVie* petitions.
- **Summary Disposition Route (`summary-disposition-route: 0.08`):** Conditional on a grant, the Court would set the case for plenary briefing and oral argument (`0.92`) rather than issuing a summary disposition (`0.08`). There is no intervening Supreme Court precedent warranting a GVR, and summary reversal is exceedingly improbable where the Fifth, Seventh, and Eighth Circuits have all ruled against preemption in published opinions.
- **Merits Ground if Granted (Conditional):** If the Court were to grant plenary review, the central merits question would turn on whether Congress's silence regarding drug delivery in 42 U.S.C. § 256b(a)(1) and the exclusive federal enforcement framework recognized in *Astra USA, Inc. v. Santa Clara County*, 563 U.S. 110 (2011), leave room for states to regulate contract-pharmacy distribution arrangements under their traditional police powers, or instead establish a closed federal bargain whose terms states may not expand.
