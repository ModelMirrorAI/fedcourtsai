# Forecast: Novartis Pharmaceuticals Corp. v. Fitch, No. 26-457 (arrival moment)

**Bottom line.** The Court will most likely deny this petition, probably after
considering it alongside PhRMA's forthcoming petition from the Fifth Circuit's
Louisiana decision (*AbbVie v. Murrill*), and after a CVSG is a live but
minority possibility. P(any grant, including a hold-and-GVR) = 0.14.

## Procedural path

- **Distributions.** The docket shows zero distributions (the moment's
  definition). The petition will reach at least one conference (P ≈ 0.96);
  the only way it does not is a withdrawal or a Rule-based dismissal, neither
  of which is signalled. Response is due November 6, 2026; the Mississippi
  Attorney General will file a brief in opposition rather than waive, very
  likely after one extension, so first distribution lands in January 2027
  rather than December 2026. I expect the Court to distribute this petition
  for the same conference as the PhRMA Louisiana petition (extension granted to
  November 3, 2026). Expect one further relist past the first conference
  (P ≈ 0.35 that the count rises to two or more), because the paired
  petitions and the United States' known interest make a CVSG or a dissent
  worth a second look; a long relist string is unlikely.
- **CVSG.** No CVSG is on the docket. P(the Court calls for the views of the
  Solicitor General) = 0.25. The petition asks for one outright, the United
  States has filed amicus briefs supporting manufacturers in seven courts of
  appeals, and a CVSG lets the Court buy time while the Fourth Circuit sits
  en banc in *McCuskey*. Against it: the Solicitor General's view is already
  public and uniform, so a CVSG adds vehicle advice more than substance, and
  the Court denied *McClain* (No. 24-118) in December 2024 at its first
  conference with no CVSG. If a CVSG issues, it comes in January or February
  2027 and the petition then resolves in the autumn of 2027.
- **Disposition.** Denial is the modal outcome (P ≈ 0.84 including a small
  dismissal/withdrawal residue), most likely at the first or second conference
  in January to February 2027 if no CVSG issues. The decision below is an
  unpublished per curiam affirmance of a preliminary-injunction denial that
  treated itself as bound by *AbbVie v. Fitch*; the Louisiana petition
  (published, summary-judgment posture, rehearing denied) is the better
  vehicle, and the Fourth Circuit's en banc *McCuskey* decision, expected in
  2027, is where any true state-law circuit split will be made or unmade. The
  Court is more likely to wait for that than to act now.

## If the Court grants

- **Question taken.** The QP as written is broad ("state laws imposing
  different and more expansive program terms"). A grant would come on the
  Louisiana vehicle with this petition granted and consolidated, or held and
  later disposed of in light of that decision; in the held case the eventual
  order is a GVR, which is why P(cert-order disposition | grant) = 0.40 rather
  than the near-zero figure a stand-alone plenary grant would carry.
  Plenary review would narrow the question to whether the federal 340B statute
  preempts state contract-pharmacy mandates (field/obstacle preemption and the
  Spending Clause bargain), not the Takings or Contracts Clause claims
  litigated in Louisiana.
- **Summary reversal** on this record is very unlikely: there is no
  intervening decision of this Court, and the issue is contested across
  circuits.

## If the Court denies

- A dissent from denial or statement respecting denial is unlikely
  (P = 0.12). *McClain* drew no noted dissent in 2024; a statement is
  plausible only if a Justice wants to flag the pending en banc proceedings or
  the United States' repeated amicus position, and the paired Louisiana
  petition gives a more natural occasion for it than this one.
