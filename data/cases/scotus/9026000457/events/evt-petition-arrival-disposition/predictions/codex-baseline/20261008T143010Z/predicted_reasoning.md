# Forecast of the Court's action

The Court will most likely deny certiorari. The probability of any grant, including a GVR or other summary grant route, is 22%.

The arrival snapshot shows **zero distributions and no CVSG**. I expect the petition to receive its first conference distribution after response-stage briefing, most likely in winter 2026–27. The modal path is one distribution followed by denial, rather than repeated conferences. One or two additional distributions beyond that first conference remain a material alternative. The structured `relist-increment` probability is 99% because, from this zero-distribution starting point, it means at least one future distribution, not a 99% probability of a true repeat conference.

A CVSG is a substantial alternative, assigned 40%, but no invitation is the more likely individual outcome. If the Court seeks the Solicitor General's views, I expect the request after initial briefing and conference consideration, approximately January–April 2027. That path would likely postpone final disposition by several months. Without an invitation or a hold for related litigation, I expect disposition in approximately January–April 2027; timing is a judgmental forecast, not a scored claim.

If review is granted, the Court will likely focus the petition's single question on whether Section 340B preempts Mississippi's restrictions on manufacturers' contract-pharmacy delivery conditions and the associated state enforcement regime. It is less likely to use this vehicle to announce a general rule about every additional state condition on participation in a federal spending program. Conditional on a grant, plenary review is substantially more likely than disposition in the cert order: **12% summary route, 88% plenary route**. The record supplies no intervening controlling decision that presently calls for a routine GVR. A later companion decision could create such a route.

Conditional on denial, I assign a 7% probability to any noted dissent or statement respecting denial. The forecast is therefore a denial without separate writing; I do not forecast a particular Justice's vote or authorship. Denial would not supply a majority rationale about preemption. If the Court instead grants plenary review, the central merits dispute will be whether federal protection of manufacturer discretion and centralized administration displaces otherwise applicable state pharmacy regulation.
