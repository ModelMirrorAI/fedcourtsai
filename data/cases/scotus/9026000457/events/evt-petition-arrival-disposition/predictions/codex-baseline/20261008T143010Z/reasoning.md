## Why 22%, rather than the roughly 5% anchor

There are substantial upward considerations. The single question presented concerns the Supremacy Clause and state expansion of obligations under the nationwide 340B program. The petition describes laws in twenty-two states and litigation across numerous circuits, supporting importance and recurrence rather than an isolated dispute (petition pp. 32–38). It also describes repeated United States amicus filings supporting manufacturers' preemption position (pp. 33, 36–39). That is a plausible reason for greater Supreme Court attention and for seeking federal views, although it is not an SG recommendation on this petition and not a federal petitioner.

The petition's reliance on Astra supplies a serious federal-administration argument, not merely a disagreement about commercial delivery terms. I checked Astra USA, Inc. v. Santa Clara County, 563 U.S. 110, 113–14, 119–20 (2011), through CourtListener opinion 213504. It rejects covered entities' third-party-beneficiary enforcement suits and discusses the disruption of centralized administration. That supports the relevance of the petition's uniformity theme, but Astra did not decide the validity of the state contract-pharmacy laws presented here. Treating the analogy as a controlling answer would overstate it.

Important counterweights keep the forecast well below 50%:

- The asserted D.C./Third versus Fifth/Eighth conflict is not plainly a square split on the same state-law question. As the petition itself explains, the D.C. and Third Circuit decisions constrained **federal agency** authority; the Fifth and Eighth decisions address **state** regulation (pp. 26–32). Different outcomes can reflect different regulatory defendants and different preemption questions, not an irreconcilable holding.
- The petition acknowledges that the Fourth Circuit decisions most directly supporting its state-preemption theory were vacated for en banc rehearing (pp. 33–35). They therefore do not establish an operative opposing circuit holding on this record. Pending litigation leaves room for further development and a stronger vehicle.
- This petition challenges denial of a preliminary injunction, not a final merits judgment. Its own panel decision is described as unreported and bound by another Fifth Circuit case (related-proceedings statement; pp. 9–11). A substantial legal issue can still be reviewed in that posture, but interlocutory review and alternative vehicles are meaningful selection costs.
- The petition reports a 2024 denial in the related McClain litigation (pp. 11, 36). That is permissible pre-prediction history about another case, not this petition's outcome and not a merits endorsement. The claimed subsequent developments weaken, but do not erase, its negative selection signal.

The resulting **22%** is a judgmental adjustment for an important and increasingly contested federal-program question with reported federal support, discounted for an imperfect split, interlocutory posture, and unsettled companion litigation. It is not an empirically fitted likelihood ratio. Lack of the BIO and independently retrieved lower-court opinions is the principal limit on confidence.
