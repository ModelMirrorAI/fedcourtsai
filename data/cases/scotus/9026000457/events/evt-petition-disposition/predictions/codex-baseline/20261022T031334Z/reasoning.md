# Rationale

## Information set

This is a forward, cert-stage distribution-moment forecast. I read `record/context.json`, the event definition, and the provisioned `record/snapshots/2026-10-22.json`. Context freezes `baseline` under `sal-v4`, Term 2026, one distribution, and no CVSG. Novartis is a private petitioner; the state attorney general is the respondent, so a state-petitioner base rate is inappropriate.

I used `record/documents/questions-presented.txt`, the petition's question, related-proceedings information, statutory background and reasons for review, especially printed pages 26–39, and the Fifth Circuit opinion in `appendix.txt`, pages 1a–7a. `documents.json` marks the petition as complete and the 117-page appendix as truncated. The appellate opinion used here is present. No brief in opposition was provisioned, and the snapshot records Mississippi's October 20 response waiver. Thus I evaluated the opposition's legal position through the opinion below, not a respondent's brief. I did not read the October 20 petitioner letter, whose text was not provisioned, and infer nothing from its submission alone.

## Anchor and adjustment

The committed statpack's `sal-v4` table is compatible with the frozen context. Pooling every displayed strictly prior Term, 2017–2025, using the JSON's baseline `prefix_est_grant_rate` and `prefix_weighted_resolved`, yields 644 estimated grant-family resolutions / 12,883 weighted resolved petitions = 4.999%. These are the bracketed reached risk sets, not the much lower rates among petitions that terminated in baseline. I excluded Term 2026. This is a committed-pack estimate, not a claim that a live corpus was refreshed during this session: the pack files supply no build timestamp or newest-pull/newest-snapshot vintage, and I made no live corpus-state query. The case information set is specifically the provisioned October 22 snapshot.

My 13% probability of any grant adjusts upward from that approximately 5% class-and-band anchor, but remains far below an already-CVSG or repeatedly-relisted case. Reasons for the upward adjustment are the nationwide practical importance of the 340B program, the number of state statutes implicated, the petition's documented reports of federal amicus support for manufacturers in related litigation, and the arguably substantial tension between preserving manufacturer discretion and allowing states to eliminate it. The petition identifies a pre-enforcement legal question that was addressed below; the panel treated its circuit's earlier decision as controlling rather than identifying a factual failure unique to this manufacturer.

The principal downward constraints are lack of a demonstrated square split on the same state-preemption question, the Court's ability to wait for pending appeals, and the unpublished, preliminary-injunction posture. The Third and D.C. Circuit cases described in the petition concern HRSA's authority; the Fifth and Eighth Circuit cases concern state regulation. Opposing outcomes do not automatically establish incompatible holdings on the identical issue. Appendix pages 5a–7a frame Mississippi's law as regulating distribution rather than changing federal pricing or enforcement obligations. The petition's invocation of a prior denial in a related Arkansas challenge is pre-decision context supplied by the petition itself, not retrieval of this petition's outcome; it weakens urgency without disposing of the new arguments. Mississippi's waiver is a modest adverse attention signal, not evidence of the Court's view and not a bar to a response request.

I independently consulted *Astra USA, Inc. v. Santa Clara County*, 563 U.S. 110 (2011), through CourtListener, opinion 213504. Its actual holding concerns covered entities' attempted third-party-beneficiary enforcement of federal pricing agreements. It supports Novartis's uniform-administration argument but does not itself decide whether independent state contract-pharmacy laws are preempted. Treating it as already deciding the present issue would overstate the petition's case for summary reversal. I did not retrieve this petition's current docket, disposition, subsequent history, or decision coverage, and encountered no outcome for this event.

## Other probabilities and uncertainty

The paid-segment statpack cuts show terminal relist-0 petitions predominantly denied and a substantially higher grant-family share among terminal CVSG cases. These cuts describe completed trajectories, not forward hazards; I did not convert them into probabilities of another distribution or a new CVSG. The 23% additional-distribution and 16% CVSG assessments are judgmental, raised above a routine petition because of the federal program and the government's reported position, but restrained because only one distribution and a waiver are observed. A CVSG could occur without an additional distribution entry, so these are not mechanically nested claims.

The conditional 10% cert-order-disposition forecast reflects the lack of an identified intervening controlling case and the need to distinguish agency-power decisions from state-preemption decisions. The conditional 7% separate-writing forecast is low because denial would permit further appellate development without endorsing the Fifth Circuit's reasoning. Neither conditional figure is multiplied by the grant or denial probability in the structured claim block.

The 0.76 significance score reflects national healthcare-financing, drug-distribution and federalism stakes, not grant likelihood. Main uncertainties are the absent opposition text, whether the Justices view the asserted conflict as sufficiently direct, and whether they seek the government's views rather than allow more circuits to decide. The numerical adjustments are forecasts, not fitted estimates from matched historical petitions.
