## 3. Question Presented and Merits Scope (Conditional on Grant)
The petition presents a single question: *"Whether state laws imposing different and more expansive program terms on drug manufacturers who participate in the federal 340B Program violate the Supremacy Clause?"*

If the Court were to grant certiorari (after first calling for a response and relisting), it would take the single question presented as framed, addressing both field/obstacle preemption under *Astra USA, Inc. v. Santa Clara County*, 563 U.S. 110 (2011), and enforcement preemption under *Buckman Co. v. Plaintiffs' Legal Committee*, 531 U.S. 341 (2001). Conditional on a plenary grant, the Court would likely affirm the Fifth Circuit's judgment holding that Section 340B's silence regarding drug delivery mechanics and contract pharmacies leaves room for traditional state regulation of pharmacy distribution, while potentially leaving open as-applied challenges where specific state requirements directly conflict with federal PPA obligations.

## 4. Summary Disposition vs. Plenary Review Conditional on Grant (`summary-disposition-route`: `0.35`)
Conditional on the Court granting review (`P(disposition) = 0.01`), the likelier route is plenary review (`0.65`), with a `0.35` conditional probability of a summary disposition (`GVR`). Because no circuit split exists on state contract-pharmacy mandates—the Fourth, Fifth, and Eighth Circuits are uniform—a GVR would occur primarily if the Court holds the petition and remands for further consideration of the as-applied preemption framework urged by the United States in its Fifth Circuit amicus brief in *Murrill*, or in light of a subsequent development in a companion 340B case. Summary reversal is virtually inconceivable given the uniform circuit authority upholding state contract-pharmacy statutes and the Court's prior denial of certiorari in *PhRMA v. McClain*, 145 S. Ct. 768 (2024).
