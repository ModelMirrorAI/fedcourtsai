# Forecast — Novartis Pharmaceuticals Corp. v. Fitch (No. 26-457)

**State forecast from:** one distribution (for the 11/6/2026 conference), made on the respondent's waiver of response (filed 10/20/2026). No relists, no CVSG, no response on file.

- **Call for a response, then relist.** I expect the Court not to deny on the waiver. A paid petition from experienced Supreme Court counsel on a Supremacy Clause question touching ~22 state laws, which the United States has backed as amicus in seven courts of appeals, is the kind of case where the Court asks Mississippi to respond. That pulls it off the 11/6 conference and means at least one more distribution, most likely in early 2027 after a BIO and reply. The 26A127 link on the docket is only the extension application, not a related merits matter.
- **CVSG: possible, but less likely than not.** The petition asks for one outright (Part III). Even so, the federal government's position is already on the record in many circuit amicus briefs, and the Court may see little to gain from asking. The likelier alternative is a hold or a denial while the Fourth Circuit's en banc rehearing in *PhRMA v. McCuskey* / *AbbVie v. Brown* is pending. If a CVSG does come, I expect it after the BIO and reply, around February–March 2027.
- **Final disposition: deny, more likely than not.** No circuit currently holds a state 340B law preempted. The Fourth Circuit panel decisions were vacated for en banc review. The D.C. and Third Circuit decisions read the federal statute without reaching state law. The Court denied the same question in *McClain* (2024). A grant becomes much likelier if the en banc Fourth Circuit holds for the manufacturers, or if the SG files urging review. A hold pending a Fourth Circuit petition is a real path too.
- **If granted:** the Court would take the single question presented as written: whether state laws imposing more expansive terms on 340B participants violate the Supremacy Clause, probably framed around obstacle and field preemption within a federal spending-clause program. The likely ground would be the uniform, HHS-administered design of the program (*Astra*).
- **Summary disposition:** very unlikely. No intervening decision supports a GVR, and the question is too large for a per curiam.
- **Dissent from denial:** unlikely if the petition is denied. A denial would most likely be read as waiting for a square split, which rarely draws a separate writing.
