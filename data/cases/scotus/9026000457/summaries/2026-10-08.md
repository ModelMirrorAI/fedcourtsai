## In brief

Novartis asks the Court to decide whether a Mississippi law regulating drug discounts under a federal program conflicts with federal law.

## What happened

Novartis Pharmaceuticals participates in the federal 340B program, which requires drug manufacturers to sell certain drugs at discounted prices to specified healthcare providers as a condition of having their drugs covered by Medicare and Medicaid. Mississippi passed a law, H.B. 728, requiring 340B-participating manufacturers to supply discounted drugs to an unlimited number of contract pharmacies, going further than Novartis says federal law requires. Novartis sued to block the law, arguing it is overridden by federal law. A federal district court denied preliminary relief, and the Fifth Circuit Court of Appeals affirmed, holding the state law was not overridden. Novartis's request for rehearing by the full appeals court was denied.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked whether state laws that impose broader or different obligations on drug manufacturers participating in the federal 340B discount program, beyond what federal law requires, are overridden by federal law because they conflict with an exclusively federal regulatory scheme.

## Where it stands

Novartis filed its petition asking the Court to hear the case on October 2, 2026, after receiving two extensions of time. The respondent's response is due November 6, 2026. The Court has not yet acted on the petition.

## What each outcome would mean

Novartis, which lost in the lower courts, is asking the Court to take up the case. If the Court grants the petition, it will hear full arguments and decide whether Mississippi's law is overridden by federal law, deciding nothing at this stage. If the Court denies the petition, the Fifth Circuit's decision upholding Mississippi's law stands, and Novartis's challenge to H.B. 728 fails, leaving the state law in effect as it applies to Novartis.
