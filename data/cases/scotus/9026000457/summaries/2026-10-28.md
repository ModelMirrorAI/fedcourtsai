## In brief

Novartis asks the Supreme Court to decide whether a Mississippi law expanding drug makers' discount obligations under a federal drug-pricing program is unconstitutional.

## What happened

Novartis sued Mississippi's Attorney General, arguing that a state law, H.B. 728, which bars drug manufacturers from restricting how they deliver discounted drugs to "contract pharmacies" working with hospitals and clinics, conflicts with a federal program called Section 340B. A federal district court denied Novartis a preliminary injunction blocking the law, and the Fifth Circuit Court of Appeals affirmed, relying on its own recent precedent upholding a similar Mississippi law. The Fifth Circuit found that states may regulate drug delivery and pharmacy practices because Congress's program did not address those topics directly.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked whether state laws that add their own, more expansive requirements onto drug manufacturers who voluntarily participate in a federal drug-discount program improperly conflict with federal law and federal authority to run that program uniformly nationwide. Novartis's petition contends that federal courts of appeals disagree on whether Congress meant to let manufacturers set reasonable limits on these discounted sales, or instead left room for states to override that choice.

## Where it stands

The petition for certiorari was filed and Mississippi waived its right to respond. After the case was distributed for the Court's November 6, 2026 conference, an amicus brief supporting Novartis was filed, and the Court subsequently requested a response from Mississippi, due November 27, 2026.

## What each outcome would mean

Novartis, which lost in the lower courts, is asking the Court to take up the case and reverse the Fifth Circuit's ruling, which left Mississippi's law in place. If the Court grants the petition, it will hear full arguments and decide whether the Mississippi law is preempted by federal law; a grant does not decide who wins. If the Court denies the petition, the Fifth Circuit's decision upholding Mississippi's law stands, and Novartis remains subject to it.
