## In brief

Novartis asks the Court to decide whether a Mississippi law expanding drugmakers' discount obligations under a federal drug-pricing program is unconstitutional.

## What happened

Novartis sells drugs at a discount to certain healthcare providers under a federal program called Section 340B. Mississippi passed a law, H.B. 728, that bars drug manufacturers from restricting how those discounted drugs reach patients through "contract pharmacies" that providers use to dispense medication. Novartis sued Mississippi's Attorney General, arguing the state law is unlawful because it conflicts with federal control over the program. A federal district court and the Fifth Circuit Court of Appeals both ruled against Novartis, finding the state law was not preempted by federal law. The Fifth Circuit relied on its own earlier decision involving a similar challenge. Novartis then asked the Supreme Court to review that ruling.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked whether states may impose their own, more expansive requirements on drug manufacturers participating in the federal discount program, when Congress gave a federal agency sole authority to run that program, or whether doing so improperly interferes with the federal scheme.

## Where it stands

Mississippi's attorney general waived her right to respond to the petition. The case was distributed for the justices' conference on November 6, 2026; the docket does not yet show any action taken at or after that conference.

## What each outcome would mean

Novartis, which lost in the lower courts, is asking the Court to take up the case. If the Court grants review, it will hear full arguments and decide whether Mississippi's law is valid, with no outcome assumed in advance. If the Court denies review, the Fifth Circuit's decision upholding Mississippi's law stands, and Novartis remains subject to that law's requirements and enforcement. A denial would not mean the Supreme Court agrees the law is valid, only that the lower court's ruling remains in place.
