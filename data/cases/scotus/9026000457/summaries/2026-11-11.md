## In brief

Novartis asks the Court to decide whether a Mississippi law requiring drugmakers to supply discounted drugs to more pharmacies than federal law demands is unconstitutional.

## What happened

Novartis sells drugs at a discount to certain healthcare providers under a federal program called Section 340B. Novartis limits which contract pharmacies can receive these discounted drugs on providers' behalf. Mississippi passed a law barring manufacturers from restricting which contract pharmacies may receive the drugs. Novartis sued Mississippi's attorney general, Lynn Fitch, arguing the law was unconstitutional because it conflicts with and is superseded by federal law. A federal district court declined to block the law, and the Fifth Circuit Court of Appeals affirmed, also denying rehearing. Novartis then asked the Supreme Court to take the case.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked whether state laws that impose broader drug-discount obligations on manufacturers participating in a federal program go further than federal law allows and are therefore invalid because federal law controls the subject.

## Where it stands

The petition was distributed for the Court's November 6, 2026 conference after Mississippi waived its right to respond. The Court then asked Mississippi to respond, with a new deadline of November 27, 2026, and Mississippi has since asked for an extension of that deadline. Two amicus briefs supporting Novartis have been filed, and the docket shows no decision yet on whether the Court will hear the case.

## What each outcome would mean

Novartis, which lost in the lower courts, is asking the Supreme Court to take up the case and overturn the Fifth Circuit's ruling. If the Court grants review, it would hear full arguments and decide whether Mississippi's law is valid; a grant would not by itself decide who wins. If the Court denies review, the Fifth Circuit's decision upholding Mississippi's law would remain in place, and Novartis would remain subject to it. The Court has not yet acted on the petition.
