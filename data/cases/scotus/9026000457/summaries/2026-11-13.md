## In brief

Novartis asks the Court to decide whether a Mississippi law expanding drugmakers' discount obligations under a federal drug-pricing program is unconstitutional.

## What happened

Novartis sells drugs at a discount to certain health providers under a federal program known as Section 340B. Novartis limits which outside "contract pharmacies" can receive these discounted drugs on providers' behalf. Mississippi passed a law barring manufacturers from restricting delivery of discounted drugs to a provider's contract pharmacy. Novartis sued Mississippi's attorney general, arguing the law is blocked by federal law. A district court and, on appeal, the Fifth Circuit both ruled against Novartis, holding the state law does not conflict with the federal program. Novartis then asked the Supreme Court to review that ruling.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked whether a state law that imposes broader drug-discount obligations on manufacturers participating in the federal program, beyond what federal law itself requires, is invalid because federal law overrides conflicting state law in this area.

## Where it stands

The petition was distributed for the Court's November 6, 2026 conference; Mississippi waived its right to respond but was then asked to respond, with that response now due December 28, 2026. No action on the petition itself has yet been recorded.

## What each outcome would mean

Novartis, which lost in the lower court, is asking the Court to take up the case and reverse that ruling. If the Court grants the petition, it will hear full arguments and decide whether Mississippi's law is valid; a grant alone would not decide who wins. If the Court denies the petition, the Fifth Circuit's decision upholding Mississippi's law stands, and Novartis remains subject to it.
