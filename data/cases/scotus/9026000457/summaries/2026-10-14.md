## In brief

Novartis asks the Court to decide whether a Mississippi law requiring drug makers to supply discounted drugs to an unlimited number of contract pharmacies conflicts with federal law.

## What happened

Novartis sells drugs at a discount to certain hospitals and clinics under a federal program known as Section 340B. Novartis limits how many outside "contract pharmacies" it will ship those discounted drugs to. Mississippi's House Bill 728 bars manufacturers from restricting delivery to any pharmacy under contract with a qualifying healthcare provider. Novartis sued Mississippi's attorney general, Lynn Fitch, arguing the state law is preempted by federal law. A federal district court denied Novartis a preliminary injunction, and the Fifth Circuit affirmed, relying on its earlier ruling in a similar case. The Fifth Circuit denied rehearing.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked whether a state can impose broader requirements on drug manufacturers participating in the federal discount program than federal law itself demands, or whether federal law overrides such state rules.

## Where it stands

Novartis filed its petition for certiorari on October 2, 2026, after receiving two extensions of time. Mississippi's response is due November 6, 2026; the Court has not yet acted on the petition.

## What each outcome would mean

Novartis, as petitioner, asks the Court to take up the case. If the Court grants the petition, it would hear full arguments and decide whether Mississippi's law is preempted, resolving a disagreement among federal appeals courts on the question. If the Court denies the petition, the Fifth Circuit's ruling upholding Mississippi's law stands, leaving Novartis subject to H.B. 728 as written. Denial would not mean the Court agrees the law is valid, only that the lower court's decision remains in place.
