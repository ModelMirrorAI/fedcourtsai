## In brief

Novartis asks the Supreme Court to decide whether a Mississippi law requiring drug makers to supply discounted drugs to more pharmacies conflicts with federal law.

## What happened

Novartis sells drugs at a discount to certain hospitals and clinics under a federal program called Section 340B. Novartis limits which outside "contract pharmacies" can receive these discounted drugs on a covered entity's behalf. Mississippi passed a law, H.B. 728, barring manufacturers from restricting delivery of discounted drugs to a covered entity's contracted pharmacy. Novartis sued Mississippi's Attorney General, Lynn Fitch, arguing the state law is unconstitutional because it conflicts with federal law. A federal district court denied Novartis a preliminary injunction, and the Fifth Circuit Court of Appeals affirmed that decision, relying on an earlier similar ruling involving another drug company.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked whether states may legally require drug manufacturers that participate in the federal discount program to provide those discounts on broader terms than federal law itself demands, or whether only the federal government may set and enforce the terms of that program.

## Where it stands

Mississippi waived its right to respond to the petition, but the Court later asked for a response, due November 27, 2026. The petition was distributed for the Court's November 6, 2026 conference; the docket does not show any decision from that conference.

## What each outcome would mean

Novartis, which lost in the lower courts, is asking the Court to take up the case and reverse the Fifth Circuit's ruling. If the Court grants review and ultimately rules for Novartis, Mississippi's law would be struck down as preempted, and similar state laws could be called into question. If the Court rules for Fitch, or denies review, the Fifth Circuit's decision upholding Mississippi's law would stand, leaving the state free to enforce it against Novartis and other manufacturers. A denial of review would not mean the Court agrees with the lower court, only that its decision remains in effect.
