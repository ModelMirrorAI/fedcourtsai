## In brief

Novartis asks the Court to decide whether a Mississippi law forcing it to supply discounted drugs to more pharmacies than federal law requires is unconstitutional.

## What happened

Novartis sells drugs at a discount to certain healthcare providers under a federal program called Section 340B. Mississippi's H.B. 728 bars drug manufacturers from restricting which contract pharmacies can receive those discounted drugs on a covered entity's behalf. Novartis sued Mississippi's Attorney General, Lynn Fitch, arguing federal law preempts H.B. 728 and the state cannot expand manufacturers' obligations under the federal program. A federal district court denied Novartis a preliminary injunction, and the Fifth Circuit affirmed, relying on its own recent precedent upholding a similar Mississippi-area law. Novartis then asked the Supreme Court to review that ruling.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked whether state laws that impose broader drug-discount obligations on manufacturers participating in the federal 340B program, beyond what federal law itself requires, are invalid because federal law overrides conflicting state law in this area. Novartis points to a split among federal appeals courts, with some circuits finding such state laws permissible and others finding them preempted.

## Where it stands

Mississippi waived its right to respond to the petition, and the petition was distributed for the Court's conference on November 6, 2026. Novartis then asked the Clerk to defer that distribution until after the deadline for amicus briefs supporting the petition had passed; the docket does not yet show the Court's action on that request, and an amicus brief from Community Oncology Alliance has since been filed.

## What each outcome would mean

Novartis, which lost in the lower courts, is asking the Court to take up the case and reverse the Fifth Circuit's ruling. If the Court grants review, it would hear full briefing and argument and decide whether Mississippi's law is preempted by federal law, deciding nothing about the outcome at this stage. If the Court denies review, the Fifth Circuit's decision upholding Mississippi's law stands, and Novartis's challenge to H.B. 728 fails.
