## In brief

Novartis asks the Court to decide whether a Mississippi law requiring drug makers to supply discounted drugs to unlimited contract pharmacies is unconstitutional.

## What happened

Novartis sells drugs at a discount to certain healthcare providers under a federal program called Section 340B. Mississippi's H.B. 728 bars manufacturers from restricting which contract pharmacies can receive these discounted drugs on a covered entity's behalf. Novartis sued Mississippi's Attorney General, Lynn Fitch, arguing federal law preempts H.B. 728 and a federal court should block it. The district court denied a preliminary injunction, and the Fifth Circuit affirmed, relying on its own recent precedent holding that the federal law does not preempt such state rules. The Fifth Circuit also denied rehearing en banc.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked whether a state can require drug manufacturers participating in the federal discount program to deal with an unlimited number of contract pharmacies, when the manufacturers argue federal law already set the terms of that program and left no room for states to add their own, stricter requirements.

## Where it stands

Novartis filed its petition on October 2, 2026. Mississippi waived its right to respond, and the case was distributed for the Court's November 6, 2026 conference. The docket shows no action since that distribution, and petitioner's counsel has since sent a letter asking that consideration be deferred.

## What each outcome would mean

Novartis, as petitioner, is asking the Court to take up the case and reverse the Fifth Circuit's ruling, which favored Fitch. If the Court grants the petition, it will hear full arguments and decide whether Mississippi's law is preempted; a grant alone would not decide who wins. If the Court denies the petition, the Fifth Circuit's decision upholding H.B. 728 stays in place, leaving Mississippi's law in effect against Novartis.
