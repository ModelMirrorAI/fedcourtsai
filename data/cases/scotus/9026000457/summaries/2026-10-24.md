## In brief

Novartis asks the Court to decide whether a Mississippi law requiring drug manufacturers to supply discounted drugs to more pharmacies than federal law demands is unconstitutional.

## What happened

Novartis sells drugs at mandatory discounts to certain healthcare providers under a federal program called Section 340B. Novartis limits which contract pharmacies it will ship those discounted drugs to. Mississippi passed a law, H.B. 728, barring manufacturers from restricting which contracted pharmacies can receive and dispense the discounted drugs. Novartis sued Mississippi's attorney general, arguing the law is preempted by federal law. The district court denied a preliminary injunction, and the Fifth Circuit affirmed, holding that federal law does not address drug delivery and so leaves room for states to regulate it, following its own recent precedent on a similar law.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked whether state laws that impose broader drug-discount obligations on manufacturers participating in the federal 340B program, beyond what federal law itself requires, are invalid because they conflict with federal law or intrude on an area Congress meant the federal government alone to control.

## Where it stands

The petition was distributed for the Court's November 6, 2026 conference after Mississippi waived its right to respond. An amicus brief supporting Novartis has been filed, and Novartis has asked the Court to defer consideration; no action on the petition has yet been recorded.

## What each outcome would mean

Novartis, which lost in the lower courts, is asking the Court to take up the case and reverse. If the Court grants review, it would hear full arguments and decide whether such state laws are preempted, resolving a disagreement among federal appeals courts; a grant alone would not decide who wins. If the Court denies review, the Fifth Circuit's ruling upholding Mississippi's law stands, and Novartis remains subject to it. The Court could also seek the federal government's views before deciding whether to hear the case.
