## In brief

A high school wrestler and her mother ask the Supreme Court to block Washington school officials from matching her against transgender opponents while her lawsuit continues.

## What happened

K.M.K., a high school wrestler, says she was sexually assaulted by a transgender opponent during a match and was not told in advance that her opponent was transgender. She and her mother, Stephanie Brown, sued the Washington Interscholastic Activities Association, state education officials, and the Puyallup School District, claiming the state's policy allowing students to compete based on gender identity violates Title IX and Brown's parental rights. A federal district court denied a preliminary injunction, finding disputed facts and no likely success on the merits. The Ninth Circuit also denied an injunction pending appeal, applying a heightened standard for the kind of order sought.

## What the Court is being asked

The applicants ask the Court to order temporary relief letting K.M.K. avoid wrestling transgender opponents while her appeal proceeds. This raises several questions: whether appeals courts must apply a tougher standard when an injunction would require someone to take action rather than simply maintain things as they are; whether a school policy allowing transgender students to compete on girls' teams treats female athletes worse than male athletes under the law barring sex discrimination in education; and whether a parent's right to direct her child's upbringing includes the right to be told in advance, and to opt her daughter out, when a scheduled opponent is transgender.

## Where it stands

The application was submitted to Justice Kagan, who requested a response from the school district and other respondents; those responses, along with several amicus briefs and the applicants' reply, have been filed. The docket's most recent entry is a letter notification from the applicants, and no order resolving the application has been recorded.

## What each outcome would mean

K.M.K. and Brown, as applicants, are asking the Court to pause the lower courts' rulings while their appeal continues. If the Court grants the injunction, school officials would be required to take some action—such as giving notice or arranging schedules—to keep K.M.K. from facing transgender opponents during that time, even though no court has yet ruled on the underlying claims. If the Court denies the application, the district court's and Ninth Circuit's rulings remain in effect, meaning no special accommodation is ordered, and K.M.K. would compete under the existing policy while her appeal proceeds in the Ninth Circuit. Either way, the outcome is temporary and does not decide the underlying Title IX and parental-rights claims.
