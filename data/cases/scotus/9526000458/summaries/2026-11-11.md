## In brief

A high school wrestler and her mother ask the Supreme Court for emergency relief to keep her from being matched against transgender opponents while their appeal continues.

## What happened

K.M.K., a Washington high school wrestler, says she was sexually assaulted during a match by a transgender opponent in December 2025, after which she left the team. She and her mother, Stephanie Brown, sued the state athletics association, state education officials, and their school district, claiming the policy allowing transgender students to compete on girls' teams violates Title IX and Brown's parental rights. A federal district court denied a preliminary injunction, finding they were unlikely to succeed, and the Ninth Circuit denied an injunction pending their appeal, applying what it called a heightened standard for the mandatory relief sought.

## What the Court is being asked

The applicants want the Court to block enforcement of the transgender-participation policy against K.M.K. while her appeal proceeds, and ask the Court to decide, among other things, whether courts must apply a tougher standard before ordering a party to take action rather than stop one, whether the policy treats female athletes worse than male athletes under the law barring sex discrimination in school programs, and whether a mother has a constitutional right to be notified of or to prevent her daughter's matches against transgender opponents.

## Where it stands

The application for an injunction pending appeal was fully briefed in October 2026, with responses from all respondents and a reply from the applicants. Both sides have since submitted supplemental letters, including one from Washington state officials noting a newly passed ballot measure and a related state-court lawsuit; the docket shows no ruling yet on the application.

## What each outcome would mean

K.M.K. and Brown, as applicants, are asking the Court to grant emergency relief that lower courts refused. If granted, the injunction would bar enforcement of the transgender-participation policy against K.M.K. during her wrestling season while her appeal continues, letting her avoid such matchups; the underlying Title IX and parental-rights claims would still be decided later by the Ninth Circuit. If denied, the policy remains in effect as applied to K.M.K., and the Ninth Circuit's refusal to intervene stands while her appeal proceeds on its normal schedule.
