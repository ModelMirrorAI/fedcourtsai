## In brief

A high school wrestler and her mother ask the Supreme Court to block Washington school officials from letting transgender athletes compete in girls' wrestling while their lawsuit continues.

## What happened

K.M.K., a high school wrestler in Washington state, says she was sexually assaulted during a match against a transgender opponent in December 2025. She and her mother, Stephanie Brown, sued the Washington Interscholastic Activities Association, state education officials, and the Puyallup School District, arguing that letting transgender athletes compete on girls' teams violates the federal law barring sex discrimination in school sports, and that it violates Brown's parental rights to not be notified in advance of such matchups. A federal district court denied a preliminary injunction, and the Ninth Circuit Court of Appeals denied emergency relief pending appeal. K.M.K. and Brown then asked the Supreme Court to step in.

## What the Court is being asked

The applicants want the Court to order officials to keep K.M.K. from being matched against transgender opponents while her appeal continues, or to notify her mother in advance so she can withdraw her daughter from such matches without penalty. They also ask the Court, as an alternative, to treat their filing as a request for full review and decide the underlying legal questions now. Those questions include whether letting transgender athletes compete on girls' teams violates the federal sex-discrimination law governing school sports, and whether a parent has a constitutional right to advance notice and an opt-out when her daughter might compete against a transgender athlete.

## Where it stands

The application has been fully briefed, with responses from all three respondents, a reply from the applicants, and several follow-up letters, the most recent on November 11, 2026. No order resolving the application has yet been recorded on the docket.

## What each outcome would mean

K.M.K. and Brown, as applicants, are asking the Court to grant emergency relief while their appeal proceeds in the Ninth Circuit. If the Court grants the injunction, officials would have to keep K.M.K. from facing transgender opponents, or give her mother notice and an opt-out, while the underlying appeal continues; the court battle over the legal questions would continue separately. If the Court denies the application, the Ninth Circuit's refusal to grant interim relief stands, and K.M.K. could face transgender opponents while her appeal proceeds. Either way, the decision would be temporary and would not resolve the lawsuit itself.
