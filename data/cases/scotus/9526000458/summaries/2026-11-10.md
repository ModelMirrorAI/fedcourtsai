## In brief

A high school wrestler and her mother ask the Supreme Court to block Washington school officials from letting transgender athletes compete against her while her appeal proceeds.

## What happened

K.M.K., a high school wrestler, was matched against a transgender opponent at a 2025 tournament; she says the opponent sexually assaulted her during the match. She and her mother, Stephanie Brown, sued the Washington Interscholastic Activities Association, school officials, and the state education office, arguing that letting transgender athletes compete in girls' sports violates Title IX and that refusing to let Brown opt her daughter out of such matchups violates Brown's parental rights. A federal district court and a Ninth Circuit panel both declined to order relief while the case continues. The applicants then asked the Supreme Court to step in.

## What the Court is being asked

The applicants want the Court to order temporary relief preventing K.M.K. from being matched against transgender opponents while her underlying appeal continues, and, alternatively, to treat the request as a petition for full review and decide the case this term. The underlying questions are whether a stricter legal standard should apply to court orders that require schools to take action rather than simply stop doing something, whether school policies letting transgender students compete on girls' teams treat female athletes unlawfully worse than male athletes under the federal sex-discrimination law governing school sports, and whether a parent has a right to be notified of, or to opt her child out of, such matchups.

## Where it stands

The application was submitted to Justice Kagan, who requested responses from the opposing parties; the school district, the state education office, and the athletic association each filed briefs opposing the request, and the applicants filed a reply. Several outside groups filed briefs supporting the applicants. The docket shows no order yet granting or denying the application.

## What each outcome would mean

K.M.K. and Brown, as applicants, are asking the Court to grant emergency relief while the Ninth Circuit appeal proceeds. If the Court grants the request, it would temporarily bar the school officials and athletic association from matching K.M.K. against transgender opponents, or would require notice allowing her to withdraw, while her appeal continues; this would not decide the underlying legal questions. If the Court denies the request, the lower courts' refusal to order such relief stands for now, and K.M.K. would remain subject to the current competition rules while her appeal proceeds in the Ninth Circuit. Either way, the underlying lawsuit continues in the lower courts.
