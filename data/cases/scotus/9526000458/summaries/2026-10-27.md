## In brief

A teenage wrestler and her mother ask the Supreme Court to block her school district and state athletic association from matching her against transgender opponents while their lawsuit continues.

## What happened

K.M.K., a Washington high school wrestler, says she was sexually assaulted during a match against a transgender opponent and later sought assurances she would not face transgender opponents again without penalty or advance notice. The school district, state athletic association, and state education officials denied those requests, citing rules against disclosing students' gender identity and scoring rules that treat withdrawal from a match as a loss. K.M.K. and her mother, Stephanie Brown, sued, raising Title IX and parental-rights claims. A federal district court denied a preliminary injunction, and the Ninth Circuit denied an injunction pending their appeal, finding the claims not strong enough and the requested relief unworkable.

## What the Court is being asked

The applicants want the Court to block enforcement of the challenged policies while their appeal proceeds, or alternatively to treat their filing as a request for the Court to take up the case directly and decide it this term. They raise three issues: what legal standard applies to court orders requiring officials to take action rather than simply stop something; whether letting transgender athletes compete on girls' teams violates federal sex-discrimination law in education; and whether a parent has a right to notice and the ability to withdraw her child from a contact-sport match against a transgender opponent.

## Where it stands

The application was filed October 2, 2026, and the Court requested a response, which the school district and other respondents filed October 13, opposing the request. The applicants filed a reply October 15. The docket shows no ruling yet on the emergency application.

## What each outcome would mean

K.M.K. and Brown, as applicants, are asking the Court to pause the lower courts' rulings while their appeal continues. If the Court grants the injunction, the district and other defendants would be barred from the practices challenged in some form while the case proceeds, though the parties dispute what that would require. If the Court denies it, the lower courts' refusal to grant an injunction stands, and the policies remain in effect while the underlying appeal continues in the Ninth Circuit. Either way, the order would be temporary and would not resolve the underlying Title IX and parental-rights claims, which continue in the lower courts.
