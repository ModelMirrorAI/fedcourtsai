## In brief

A teenage wrestler and her mother ask the Supreme Court to block schools from matching her against transgender opponents while their lawsuit against athletic officials continues.

## What happened

K.M.K., a Washington high school wrestler, says she was sexually assaulted during a match by a transgender opponent she did not know was transgender beforehand. She and her mother, Stephanie Brown, sued the Washington Interscholastic Activities Association, state education officials, and the Puyallup School District, claiming the state's gender-identity participation policy for school sports violates Title IX and Brown's parental rights. A federal district court denied a preliminary injunction, finding disputed facts about safety risks and no violation of a recognized parental right. The Ninth Circuit also denied emergency injunctive relief pending appeal, applying a heightened standard for the mandatory injunction sought.

## What the Court is being asked

The applicants want the Court to order temporary relief letting K.M.K. avoid wrestling transgender opponents while her appeal proceeds, or alternatively to treat the request as a petition for early review and decide the case this term. The underlying questions are whether courts must apply a tougher standard before ordering officials to take action rather than stop one, whether a policy letting transgender students compete on girls' teams treats female athletes worse than male athletes under the law barring sex discrimination in school programs, and whether a parent's right to direct her child's upbringing includes a right to notice and the ability to opt her daughter out of such matches.

## Where it stands

The application for emergency injunctive relief has been fully briefed, with responses from the school district and other officials, amicus briefs, and a reply from the applicants. The docket does not show that the Court has yet ruled on the application.

## What each outcome would mean

K.M.K. and Brown, as applicants, are asking the Court to block enforcement of the policy against K.M.K. while the case continues. If the Court grants the injunction, the school district and other officials would have to keep K.M.K. from facing transgender opponents, or give notice so Brown can withdraw her, while the underlying appeal proceeds in the Ninth Circuit. If the Court denies it, the lower courts' refusal to grant that relief stands, and K.M.K. would continue competing under the current policy while her appeal proceeds. Either way, the ruling would be temporary and would not resolve the lawsuit's underlying claims.
