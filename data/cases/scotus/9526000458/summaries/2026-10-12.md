## In brief

K.M.K. and her mother ask the Supreme Court to block Washington school officials from requiring her to wrestle male opponents while her appeal continues.

## What happened

K.M.K., a high school wrestler, says a male opponent sexually assaulted her during a match permitted under a Washington Interscholastic Activities Association policy letting students compete according to gender identity. She and her mother, Stephanie Brown, sued the association, state education officials, and her school district, claiming the policy violates Title IX's ban on sex discrimination and Brown's parental rights. A federal district court denied a preliminary injunction, finding factual disputes about whether male participation creates safety or competitive problems for girls. The Ninth Circuit denied an injunction pending appeal, applying a heightened standard for the type of order requested.

## What the Court is being asked

The applicants want the Court to order, while the appeal continues, that K.M.K. cannot be made to wrestle male opponents, and that her mother gets notice or a say before that happens. They argue the appeals court used the wrong, stricter legal test for this kind of order, wrongly discounted evidence about risks to female athletes, and wrongly rejected the mother's parental-rights claim. They also ask, as an alternative, that the Court treat the request as a petition for full review and hear the case this term.

## Where it stands

The application was submitted to Justice Kagan, who asked the respondents to reply by October 13, 2026. Several outside groups have filed briefs supporting the applicants, and the case has not yet been ruled on.

## What each outcome would mean

K.M.K. and Brown are asking for this emergency order. If granted, K.M.K. would not have to wrestle male opponents while her appeal proceeds in the Ninth Circuit, and her mother would get notice or an opt-out; if the Court instead grants certiorari before judgment, as the applicants request as an alternative, it would hear and decide the case fully this term rather than issuing only temporary relief. If denied, the lower courts' refusal to block the policy stands for now, and the case continues in the Ninth Circuit without the requested protections in place. Either way, any order at this stage would be temporary and would not finally decide who is right.
