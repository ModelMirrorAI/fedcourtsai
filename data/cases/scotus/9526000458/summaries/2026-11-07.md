## In brief

A female high school wrestler and her mother ask the Supreme Court to order temporary relief letting her avoid wrestling transgender opponents while her lawsuit against school officials continues.

## What happened

K.M.K., a Washington high school wrestler, was matched against a transgender opponent in December 2025 and says that opponent sexually assaulted her during the match. She and her mother, Stephanie Brown, sued the Washington Interscholastic Activities Association (WIAA), state education officials, and the Puyallup School District, claiming the state's policy letting students compete under their gender identity violates Title IX and Brown's parental rights. A federal district court denied a preliminary injunction, finding disputed facts and no clear legal violation. The Ninth Circuit also denied emergency injunctive relief pending appeal, applying what it called a heightened standard for the mandatory injunction sought.

## What the Court is being asked

The applicants ask the Court to block enforcement of the gender-identity sports policy as it affects K.M.K., or otherwise ensure she can decline to wrestle transgender opponents without penalty and receive notice when such matchups are scheduled, while her appeal proceeds. They also ask the Court, as an alternative, to treat the application as a request to hear the case immediately and decide whether courts should apply a stricter standard for injunctions that change existing arrangements, whether the gender-identity policy discriminates based on sex, and whether a parent has a right to notice and the ability to opt her child out of such matchups.

## Where it stands

The application for emergency injunctive relief was submitted to Justice Kagan, who requested a response from the respondents. The respondents filed opposition briefs, the applicants filed a reply, and the applicants later filed a supplemental letter brief; the docket does not show a ruling on the application as of the snapshot date.

## What each outcome would mean

K.M.K. and Brown, as applicants, are asking the Court to grant emergency relief. If the Court grants the requested injunction, K.M.K. could compete this wrestling season without facing transgender opponents, or with added notice and opt-out protections, while the underlying appeal continues in the Ninth Circuit; this would not decide the lawsuit itself. If the Court denies the application, the lower courts' refusal to grant such relief stands, meaning the district's current policies continue to apply to K.M.K. while her case proceeds through the normal appellate process.
