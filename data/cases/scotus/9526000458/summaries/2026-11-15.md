## In brief

A high school wrestler and her mother asked the Supreme Court to block Washington's policy letting transgender athletes compete in girls' sports while their court appeal continues.

## What happened

K.M.K., a Washington high school wrestler, says she was sexually assaulted during a match against a transgender opponent and now fears facing transgender competitors again. She and her mother, Stephanie Brown, sued the state athletic association, school officials, and her district, claiming the gender-identity participation policy violates Title IX's sex discrimination ban and Brown's parental rights. A federal district court denied a preliminary injunction, finding the claims unlikely to succeed and the harm speculative. The Ninth Circuit also denied emergency relief pending appeal, applying a heightened standard for the mandatory injunction requested and citing feasibility and privacy concerns.

## What the Court is being asked

The applicants ask the Court to block enforcement of the policy while their appeal proceeds, resolve disagreement among appeals courts over whether a stricter standard applies to court orders requiring action rather than orders preventing it, and decide whether letting transgender athletes compete on girls' teams violates sex discrimination law and parental rights. Alternatively, they ask the Court to treat the request as a petition for full review and hear the case this term.

## Where it stands

The application was submitted to Justice Kagan, who requested a response from the respondents; both sides have filed briefs and replies, along with amicus briefs supporting the applicants. Recent letters to the Court note that a Washington ballot initiative addressing the issue passed and is set to take effect December 3, and that the wrestling season's competitions begin after that date.

## What each outcome would mean

K.M.K. and Brown, as applicants, are asking the Court to pause the lower courts' rulings while the underlying appeal continues. Granting the injunction would bar enforcement of the policy against K.M.K. during her wrestling season, letting her avoid being matched against transgender opponents without penalty. Denying it would leave the district court's and Ninth Circuit's rulings in place, meaning the policy continues to apply as the case proceeds in the lower courts. Either way, the order would be temporary and would not resolve the underlying legal claims, which remain pending on appeal.
