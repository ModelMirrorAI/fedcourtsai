## In brief

A high school wrestler and her mother ask the Supreme Court to order that she not be matched against transgender opponents while her broader appeal proceeds.

## What happened

K.M.K., a Washington high school wrestler, says a transgender opponent sexually assaulted her during a match in December 2025. She and her mother, Stephanie Brown, sued the school district, state education officials, and the state athletic association, arguing that letting transgender girls compete on girls' teams violates Title IX and that refusing to let Brown opt her daughter out of such matchups, or notify her in advance, violates Brown's parental rights. A federal district court denied a preliminary injunction, finding the claims unlikely to succeed and the relief requested unworkable. A Ninth Circuit panel also denied emergency relief pending appeal, applying a heightened standard for the type of injunction sought.

## What the Court is being asked

The applicants ask the Court to block enforcement of the participation policy while their appeal continues, pending the Ninth Circuit's full review. They raise three issues: whether appeals courts should apply a tougher standard to injunctions that require someone to act rather than simply stop acting; whether allowing transgender girls to compete on girls' teams treats female athletes worse than male athletes under the federal law barring sex discrimination in school programs; and whether a parent's right to direct her child's upbringing entitles her to notice and a chance to withdraw her daughter before a match against a transgender opponent. Alternatively, they ask the Court to treat the request as a petition for full review and hear the case this term.

## Where it stands

The application was submitted to Justice Kagan, who requested a response from the respondents; both sides have since filed briefs, replies, and supplemental letters, including notice that Washington voters approved a ballot measure addressing this issue, expected to take effect in early December. The docket shows no order yet granting or denying the requested injunction.

## What each outcome would mean

K.M.K. and Brown, as applicants, are asking the Court to act. If the Court grants the injunction, the challenged participation policy would be paused for K.M.K.'s matches while her appeal proceeds in the Ninth Circuit, meaning she could not be matched against a transgender opponent without her mother's agreement in the meantime. If the Court denies the application, the lower courts' refusal to grant that relief stands, and the policy continues to apply as the case proceeds below. Either way, the order would be temporary and would not resolve the underlying lawsuit, which continues in the lower courts.
