## In brief

A student athlete and her mother ask the Supreme Court to order temporary relief letting her avoid wrestling transgender opponents while her appeal continues.

## What happened

K.M.K., a high school wrestler in Washington state, says she was sexually assaulted by a transgender opponent during a match and was not told in advance that her opponent was transgender. She and her mother, Stephanie Brown, sued the school district, state education officials, and the state athletic association, arguing that letting transgender athletes compete on girls' teams violates Title IX's sex-discrimination protections and that Brown has a parental right to know about and opt K.M.K. out of such matchups. A federal district court denied a preliminary injunction, and the Ninth Circuit denied emergency injunctive relief pending appeal, finding the merits showing too weak and the requested order impractical.

## What the Court is being asked

The applicants ask the Court to block enforcement of the challenges policies while the appeal proceeds, pausing the current rules so K.M.K. can decline matches against transgender opponents without penalty, pending the outcome of her underlying lawsuit. They also ask, as an alternative, that the Court treat this request as a petition for full review and decide now whether a stricter legal test should apply to court orders like the one they are seeking, whether letting transgender athletes compete on girls' teams violates sex-discrimination protections in school athletics, and whether a parent has a right to notice and the ability to opt her daughter out of such matchups.

## Where it stands

The application was submitted to Justice Kagan on October 2, 2026. The school district, state education officials, and athletic association have filed responses opposing the request, and the applicants have filed a reply. The docket shows no ruling yet on the application.

## What each outcome would mean

K.M.K. and Brown, as applicants, are asking the Court to act. If the Court grants the requested injunction, the challenged policies would be paused and K.M.K. could decline matches against transgender opponents without penalty while her appeal proceeds in the Ninth Circuit, though the underlying legal questions would remain undecided. If the Court denies the application, the lower courts' rulings stand, meaning no emergency relief is in place while the case continues in the Ninth Circuit. Either way, the Court's action here would be temporary and would not resolve the merits of the Title IX or parental-rights claims.
