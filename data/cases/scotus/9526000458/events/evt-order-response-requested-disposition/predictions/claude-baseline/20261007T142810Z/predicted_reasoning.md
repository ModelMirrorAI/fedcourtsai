# Forecast — 26A458, K.M.K. v. Washington Interscholastic Activities Association (application for an injunction pending appeal)

## What the record shows at prediction time

The application was submitted to Justice Kagan, Circuit Justice for the Ninth
Circuit, on October 2, 2026. On October 6 she called for a response, due at
4 p.m. on October 13 — a seven-day clock. The frozen context shows the
response-requested rung fired, no referral to the full Court, and zero amicus
entries. The applicants, a high-school wrestler and her mother represented by
Alliance Defending Freedom, lost a preliminary injunction in the Western
District of Washington (August 23), an injunction pending appeal there
(August 31), and an injunction pending appeal in a published Ninth Circuit
order (September 25; Hawkins, W. Fletcher, Mendoza). They ask this Court to
enter, in the first instance, an injunction pending appeal "that protects
K.M.K. from male competitors in girls' wrestling" — in substance a penalty-free
opt-out from matches against male athletes, with notice where needed — and, in
the alternative, to construe the application as a petition for certiorari
before judgment on three questions.

## The disposition

**The Court will deny the application — P(unqualified grant) = 0.35.** The
modal disposing entry reads: the application for an injunction pending appeal,
presented to Justice Kagan and by her referred to the Court, is denied, with
Justices Thomas, Alito, and Gorsuch (and plausibly Kavanaugh) noting they would
grant, and at least one written dissent. The Court will not construe the
application as a petition for certiorari before judgment in this interlocutory
preliminary-injunction posture.

The grant path, at roughly one in three, is a short order or per curiam
enjoining the respondents, pending disposition of the Ninth Circuit appeal and
any timely petition, from penalizing K.M.K. for declining a scheduled match
against a male athlete — relief that tracks the application's own narrow ask,
so a grant would most likely read as unqualified. I put roughly 5–8 points on a
mixed order (relief on the parental-rights opt-out, denial as to any
Title IX-based relief or notice obligation), which resolves as ungranted, and a
residual sliver on withdrawal or dismissal.

## Timing

Response due October 13; expect a reply within two or three days. No
administrative injunction from the Circuit Justice: nothing is scheduled before
the girls' wrestling season opens in mid-November, so there is no interim status
to preserve. The Court will dispose of the application before that season
begins — most likely between October 23 and November 13, with the first week of
November the center of the distribution.

## The escalation ladder from here

- **Further response request:** the rung has fired, so this claim is vacuous
  for the cell; I put 0.02 on a second call for a supplemental response.
- **Referral to the full Court: 0.88.** Justice Kagan referred Mirabelli rather
  than acting alone, and an application of this profile — movement counsel, a
  circuit split on the governing standard, a state policy at issue, and amici
  already engaged below — is one no Circuit Justice disposes of in chambers.
  The residual is a chambers denial after the response, which Justice Kagan has
  occasionally done on weaker private applications.
- **Amicus count rises past zero: 0.85.** Three amicus filings supported the
  applicants in the Ninth Circuit within a week of the motion (a State of Iowa
  multistate coalition, Women's Liberation Front, Do No Harm). Each has
  Supreme Court counsel and a standing interest in post-B.P.J. Title IX
  litigation; the three-to-five-week window before disposition is ample for at
  least one submitted brief, and the submitted form counts.

## If the Court grants

The likelier ground is parental rights under Mirabelli and Mahmoud — a fit
parent's authority to keep her daughter out of a contact-sport match against a
male — not Title IX, whose application to gender-identity participation
policies B.P.J. expressly reserved and which the district court found rests on
disputed facts. The Court would not reach the mandatory-versus-prohibitory
circuit split on an application; that question waits for a certiorari petition.
