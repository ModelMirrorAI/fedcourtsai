# Forecast: what the Court will do with application 26A458

**Record at my moment.** One entry: the application for an injunction pending
appeal, submitted to Justice Kagan on October 2, 2026. No response requested,
no referral, no amicus entries.

## Procedural claims (the `interim-v1` set)

- **Response requested — yes.** Justice Kagan will call for a response rather
  than deny in chambers. (The live docket already shows this happened on
  October 6, with the response due October 13; see `flags.json`.)
- **Referral to the full Court — very likely (~85%).** Once the response and a
  reply are in, Kagan refers the application to the Court, as she did in
  Mirabelli v. Bonta (25A810). The alternative, a single-Justice denial, is
  unlikely on a contested, ideologically salient application where a denial
  alone would simply be renewed before another Justice.
- **Amicus count rises above zero — likely (~75%).** The application presents
  the first post-B.P.J. test of whether a gender-identity participation policy
  violates Title IX, with an alleged sexual assault in a wrestling match as
  its lead fact. Expect at least one amicus entry inside the response window
  (a coalition of States, a women's-sports organization, or both), as the
  2023 B.P.J. application (22A800) drew three within four days of the
  response request. The short window and application posture keep this short
  of certain.
- **Disposition — denied, more likely than not (~80%).** The order will most
  likely read as a denial of the application, with Justices Alito and Thomas
  (and possibly Gorsuch) noting they would grant. Roughly a 20% chance of an
  order that reads as an unqualified grant. The likeliest shape of any relief
  is partial — relief on the parental-rights (opt-out/notice) theory while
  declining the Title IX theory or the alternative request for certiorari
  before judgment — and a mixed order resolves as ungranted under the
  interim resolver, so the unqualified-grant number sits below P(any relief),
  which I put near 30%.
- **Timing.** Response due October 13; reply within a few days; disposition
  in the second half of October or the first days of November, before the
  mid-November wrestling season the application cites as its deadline. A
  grant would likely come with a short per curiam or a concurrence in the
  Mirabelli mold and a dissent from Justices Sotomayor, Kagan, and Jackson; a
  denial would be a bare order with noted dissents.

## Conditional merits-shaped content

If the Court grants, the ground will be the parental-rights claim rather than
Title IX: Mirabelli supplies a recent, direct holding that the Ninth Circuit
mis-cabined parental rights, and an opt-out-with-notice injunction can be
written narrowly to K.M.K. alone. The Title IX theory asks the Court to go
beyond West Virginia v. B.P.J. (which upheld a State's exclusion policy) to
hold that a State's inclusion policy violates the statute, on a record both
lower courts called factually disputed, under the Spending Clause clear-notice
objection the district court credited. That is not the kind of question the
Court resolves on an application where no court below granted relief. The
alternative request for certiorari before judgment will not be granted.
