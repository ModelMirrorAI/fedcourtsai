## Why 30%, rather than 10% or a majority probability

The upward adjustment is substantial but judgmental, not a measured likelihood ratio. The application presents several concrete reasons for attention: a claimed cross-circuit disagreement about mandatory preliminary injunctions; Title IX arguments invoking the recently described decision in *West Virginia v. B.P.J.*; a parental-notice/opt-out theory invoking *Mirabelli v. Bonta* and *Mahmoud*; and a school sports season approaching in mid-November. These appear in application pages 1–3, 11–18, 24–25, and 34–43. A time-limited opportunity to participate in school athletics presents a more specific claimed irreparable injury than an abstract disagreement with eligibility rules. The request could also be narrowed to this applicant.

But the application seeks an injunction from the Supreme Court, not merely suspension of an injunction already entered below. *Wisconsin Right to Life, Inc. v. FEC*, 542 U.S. 1305, 1306 (2004) (Rehnquist, C.J., in chambers), retrieved through CourtListener opinion 137724, describes the demanding All Writs Act basis for such affirmative relief, including a clear legal entitlement and critical exigency. Its particular federal-election context is different, so I use it for the procedural hurdle, not as an outcome analogue or an assertion that the applicants' cited modern injunction decisions are irrelevant.

The applicants' substantive arguments also require steps beyond the propositions they attribute to their precedents. Permission for sex-based sports classifications does not automatically establish that Title IX requires exclusion under the particular policy challenged here. A parental right concerning one's own child does not automatically settle notice obligations involving other students, scheduling, or consequence-free opt-outs. The application itself reports that the Ninth Circuit distinguished the cited authorities and emphasized factual uncertainty and privacy or administrative interests. The available district-court introduction, appendix pages 1a–3a, confirms both the alleged injury and the court's contrary view of the asserted rights; it also describes requested school-transfer and transportation relief, illustrating why the scope of the proposed injunction matters. I do not assume every item sought below remains independently requested in this emergency application.

The allegations of assault and competitive disadvantage are serious but remain allegations or contested factual propositions in the supplied record, not findings I independently established. The incomplete appendix and lack of a respondent brief make it particularly important not to adopt advocacy as a complete factual account. A court can regard the underlying legal questions as important while declining immediate relief pending the ordinary appeal. A mixed, limited grant would also fail the unqualified-grant target. Together these considerations leave **denial** the modal disposition and hold P(unqualified grant) to **0.30**, despite an anchor well above the undifferentiated pool after case-specific adjustment.
