The application for a mandatory injunction pending appeal will be denied. The Court rarely grants an injunction in the first instance, especially where it would require a school district to affirmatively alter its policies to exclude transgender athletes without a clear, underlying state mandate to do so. The applicant's asserted right under Title IX is not "indisputably clear," particularly because the Court's recent ruling in *West Virginia v. B.P.J.* established only that states *may* exclude transgender girls from female sports, not that Title IX requires such exclusion. 

Procedurally, Justice Kagan has already called for a response, and the docket currently shows four amicus briefs. Given the high profile of the legal and cultural questions at issue, it is highly likely that Justice Kagan will refer the application to the full Court rather than acting alone. Expect the total amicus count to rise past the four already filed, as additional advocacy groups will likely submit briefs shortly after the response deadline. The Court will likely issue its disposition within one to two weeks, denying the injunction. If any relief were granted, it would likely provoke strong dissents from the liberal wing regarding the abuse of the emergency docket for a mandatory injunction. However, a clean denial is the most probable outcome.
