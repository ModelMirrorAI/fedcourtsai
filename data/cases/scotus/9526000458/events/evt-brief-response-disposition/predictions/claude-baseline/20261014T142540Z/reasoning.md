# Why P(unqualified grant) = 0.04

**Anchor.** The statpack's interim-docket section (*The interim docket (applications)*,
read in its current scored-base-rate caption) gives per-Term substantive counts. This
application is in Term 2026. The strictly-prior pool within ten Terms covers
2016–2025, but only 2023–2025 carry parsed rows: 18+18+17 = 53 grants over
127+196+226 = 549 resolved substantive applications, which is **≈ 9.7%**. That clears
the 50-resolved floor. All three pooled Terms show `unparsed` = 0. The pool is not
conditioned on the escalation ladder, though, and a requested response puts an
application higher on it than the cohort average.

**Adjustments.**
- *Up:* a response was requested, which is the Court's affirmative act of attention.
  Four applicant-side amicus entries and the post-*B.P.J.* subject matter make the
  issue one some Justices care about.
- *Down, heavily:* the relief is an **injunction pending appeal**, which is the most
  demanding interim ask. It requires that the right to relief be indisputably clear,
  and the Court grants it far less often than a stay. The order would be mandatory in
  substance: it would make school authorities arrange opt-outs or notice for matches
  against transgender wrestlers they say they cannot identify. Two lower courts denied
  relief, including a unanimous Ninth Circuit panel. The record is preliminary, with no
  answer filed and no discovery. Wrestling season starts in mid-November, so the
  Ninth Circuit can still act first, which weakens the claim of urgency. The Title IX
  theory goes further than *B.P.J.* held. A requested response is routine for a
  serious, well-counseled application, and on its own it moves the odds only a little.
- The applicants lead with the mandatory-versus-prohibitory circuit split. That split
  supports cert, not interim relief, and the respondents argue that the lower courts'
  rulings do not depend on it.

The net result is well below the ~10% pool rate. I put it at 4%, which leaves a tail
for the Court's evident interest in this subject. A mixed or partial grant would read
as `denied`, which also argues against a higher number.

**Increments.** Response requested: already fired (vacuous). I give it 0.03 for the
record. Referral at 0.45: high-salience applications before Justice Kagan are often
referred, and a renewed application to another Justice after an in-chambers denial is
a common route to the full Court, but an in-chambers denial that ends the matter is
equally plausible. Amicus at 0.25: the supporting amici filed early, briefing is now
closed, and disposition is likely soon. Respondent-side amici are possible but have
not appeared.
