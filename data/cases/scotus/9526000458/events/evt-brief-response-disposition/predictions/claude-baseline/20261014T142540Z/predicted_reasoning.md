# Forecast — 26A458, K.M.K. v. Washington Interscholastic Activities Association

**The Court will deny the application for an injunction pending appeal.** Expect a
one-line denial, most likely within one to two weeks of the October 13 responses
and well before the mid-November start of the wrestling season.

- **Record as of my vantage:** submitted to Justice Kagan (Oct 2); response requested
  by Justice Kagan (Oct 6) and filed by all three respondent groups (Oct 13); four
  amicus entries, all supporting the applicants (Iowa; Independent Council on
  Women's Sports et al.; Do No Harm; the Hoefers); not referred to the full Court;
  no reply yet on the docket.
- **Response requested:** already on the record, so nothing further is forecast on
  that rung.
- **Referral:** roughly even. Kagan may deny in chambers, but given the issue's
  salience after *West Virginia v. B.P.J.* and the amicus support, a referral to the
  full Court (by Kagan, or by another Justice on a renewed application after an
  in-chambers denial) is a live path. I lean slightly against.
- **Amicus:** the applicant-side amici have already filed; with briefing closed and a
  ruling likely soon, a further amicus entry is less likely than not.
- **Ground, if the Court writes at all:** the requested relief is a mandatory
  injunction against school authorities, entered in the first instance on an
  undeveloped preliminary record, and the district court and the Ninth Circuit both
  refused it. The applicants' Title IX theory goes past what *B.P.J.* decided. That
  case held that states *may* separate teams by sex, and the respondents argue it
  reserved whether Title IX *requires* it. The opt-out/notice remedy raises feasibility
  and privacy problems that the respondents document. The cert-before-judgment
  alternative will also be denied.
- **Separate writing:** a short statement or dissent from one or more Justices
  (Alito, Thomas, Gorsuch) noting the Title IX question's importance is plausible if
  the application is referred to the full Court. It is unlikely on an in-chambers
  denial.
