## In brief

A father asks the Court to revive his civil rights lawsuit over his son's gender-related surgery, which he says violated his parental rights and custody order.

## What happened

Edward Hudacko's divorce decree gave his ex-wife, Christine Underhill, authority over their son's medical care, but required Hudacko's consent for "any gender identity related surgery." Hudacko alleges a hospital implant procedure was performed on his son without his knowledge or consent, and that the doctors and others involved knew it required his approval. He sued under the federal civil rights law allowing suits against state officials. The district court dismissed his claims, and the Ninth Circuit affirmed, holding the defendants were protected by qualified immunity—a doctrine shielding officials from suits unless they violated clearly established law—because Hudacko did not cite court decisions addressing similar facts.

## What the Court is being asked

The case raises two questions about qualified immunity. First, whether courts may look only to prior court rulings to decide if a right was "clearly established," or whether they may also consider other sources, such as professional standards or an official's own knowledge, as some circuits do. Second, whether all government officials should get the same immunity test, or whether courts should first ask whether officials like the ones here historically received immunity at all under older legal traditions.

## Where it stands

The petition was distributed for the Court's October 9, 2026 conference, after which the Court requested a response from the respondents, due October 19, 2026. The respondents were later granted an extension, to November 18, 2026, to file that response.

## What each outcome would mean

Hudacko, the petitioner, is asking the Court to take up his case and reverse the Ninth Circuit's ruling, which went against him. If the Court grants review, it would hear full arguments on the qualified immunity questions before deciding anything. If it denies review, the Ninth Circuit's decision stands, leaving the dismissal of Hudacko's claims against Underhill, the university doctors, and the other defendants in place.
