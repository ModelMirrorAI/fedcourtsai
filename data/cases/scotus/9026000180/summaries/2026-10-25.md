## In brief

A father asks the Court to revive his civil rights lawsuit against university doctors and others after his teenage son had gender-related surgery without his consent.

## What happened

Edward Hudacko's custody order, issued during his divorce, let his ex-wife make most medical decisions for their son but required Hudacko's written consent or a court order before any "gender identity related surgery." Hudacko alleges a hospital and its doctors performed such a procedure on his son without telling him or seeking his consent. He sued the doctors, a hospital attorney, his ex-wife, and two attorneys under a federal civil-rights law. The district court dismissed his constitutional claim, finding the doctors were protected by qualified immunity, a legal doctrine shielding officials from suits unless they violated clearly established law. The Ninth Circuit affirmed, and the full court declined to rehear the case.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked whether, in deciding if a government official violated clearly established law, courts may look only to prior court rulings, or whether they may also consider other sources, like statutes or professional medical knowledge, as some circuits allow. The Court is also asked whether all government officials should get the same immunity test, or whether courts should first ask whether similar officials historically received immunity under common law.

## Where it stands

The petition was distributed for the Court's October 9, 2026 conference, and the Court later asked the respondents to file a response, due November 18, 2026 after an extension was granted. The docket shows no decision yet on whether the Court will hear the case.

## What each outcome would mean

Hudacko, the father, is asking the Court to take up his case. If the Court grants review, it will decide the immunity question itself, deciding nothing yet about who wins. If it denies review, the Ninth Circuit's ruling stands, leaving the dismissal of Hudacko's claims against the doctors and hospital attorney in place. Numerous outside groups have filed briefs supporting Hudacko's petition.
