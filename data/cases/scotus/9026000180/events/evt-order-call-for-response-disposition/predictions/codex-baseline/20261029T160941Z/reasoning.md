## Why 20%, rather than the 5.11% anchor

The call after waivers is affirmative judicial attention, and five pre-call amicus briefs indicate organized interest. The petition presents an articulable cross-circuit disagreement about nonjudicial materials in the clearly-established-law inquiry. Its account of recent parental-rights decisions supplies a plausible audience beyond a single Justice. In particular, the provisioned petition's pages 4–5 describe *Mirabelli v. Bonta* as supporting parental participation in mental-health decisions. These considerations move this petition substantially above an ordinary baseline-band filing.

The downward constraints are substantial:

- **The split may not control this result.** The petition compares some circuits' use of training, regulations, and professional standards with others' demands for precedent. Those propositions need not establish that a contested state custody order clearly created the particular federal damages right asserted here. The attached memorandum, Appendix A at 4a–5a, addresses both uncertainty about the order's surgery exception and the lack of a clearly established parental right under the unusual consent arrangement. It does not expressly announce the sweeping prohibition on considering nonjudicial materials that the petition attributes to it.
- **The vehicle is unusually fact-dependent.** This was an unpublished memorandum about a parent whose medical decision-making authority was otherwise removed, with the other parent and the child consenting. A case involving an otherwise intact parent's rights against a generally applicable state policy would not present the same obstacle. Private-party state-action and state-law claims have distinct grounds of dismissal; success on immunity would not reopen every dismissed claim.
- **The requested doctrinal expansion is large.** Question 2 would replace an across-the-board approach with a historical category inquiry. The petition acknowledges at page 36 that this issue was not raised below. Its futility argument may not persuade the Court to select this vehicle.
- **Present constitutional direction and past notice differ.** The parental-rights discussion in the 2026 authority described by the petition can make the case important without resolving notice at the time of the alleged 2021 conduct. It therefore supports attention more strongly than an automatic GVR or reversal.

The resulting **20% any-grant probability** is a judgmental adjustment, not an empirical response-call estimator: it is almost four times the matched prior-Term anchor, but still leaves denial clearly favored. The five briefs and call keep me from a single-digit forecast; the order-specific ambiguity, disputed split, and preservation issue keep me well below an even chance.

## Other probabilities and limits

The 98% further-distribution probability is largely mechanical after the response request and is unscored for this moment. A CVSG remains a low-probability procedural branch at 1.5%. Conditional on a grant, I place 30% on a cert-order disposition, versus 70% on plenary treatment: a limited remand is conceivable, but the Court would need more than a later broad parental-rights principle to overcome the historical notice problem. Conditional on denial, I assign 30% to a dissent or statement. The petition's account of Thomas's historical objections to immunity and Alito's parental-rights writings supports that number; those accounts do not establish a four-vote coalition for this case. Per-Justice order-writing probabilities are unconditional and allow correlated coauthorship.

The stakes score, **0.72**, prices the potential national consequences for parental consent and remedies against state clinicians, not the likelihood of review. The custody-specific posture limits how broadly a decision necessarily would reach.
