## 2. Questions Presented, Lower-Court Holdings, and Vehicle Defects

The petition raises two questions arising out of contentious California divorce and custody proceedings involving Hudacko, his ex-wife Cristina, and their minor son K.H. (who tragically died in an unrelated drowning accident in February 2021 at age 17):
- **QP1 (Qualified Immunity & Parental Rights)**: Whether traditional substantive due process parental-rights precedents clearly establish that state-affiliated medical providers may not perform surgical gender-transition procedures on a minor while keeping them secret from a parent who retains a legal right to approve or reject them, and whether *Hope v. Pelzer*, 536 U.S. 730 (2002), applies outside Eighth Amendment cases.
- **QP2 (Absolute Quasi-Judicial Immunity for Minor's Counsel)**: Whether court-appointed minor's counsel / guardians ad litem enjoy absolute quasi-judicial immunity under 42 U.S.C. § 1983, or whether the Court should re-anchor absolute immunity to the common law of 1871 as urged in *Baxter v. Bracey*, 590 U.S. 1011, 1014 (2020) (Thomas, J., dissenting from denial of certiorari).

A close inspection of the Ninth Circuit's unanimous memorandum opinion (Pet. App. 1a–5a) and the district court's dismissal order (Pet. App. 6a–33a) reveals why this case is an exceptionally poor vehicle for either question:

1. **Fatal Chronological and Custodial Defects on QP1**:
   - The petition's headline premise is that an October 3, 2019 California family-court stipulation stripped Hudacko of medical custody except for one express carve-out—"absent a court order, any gender identity related surgery requires the agreement of both parents"—and that UCSF providers violated that right by surgically inserting a subcutaneous Supprelin (histrelin) puberty-blocker implant.
   - As the unanimous Ninth Circuit panel (including Judge Bress) explicitly noted (Pet. App. 4a), **the surgical insertion of the Supprelin implant occurred on September 29, 2019—four days *before* the October 3, 2019 family-court stipulation—and was performed with the consent of the mother**, who held joint custody on September 29 and sole medical custody for all non-surgical treatment from October 3 onward.
   - Thus, the case does not cleanly present a state actor overriding unified parents (as in `scotus/72353718`, *Mirabelli v. Bonta*, No. 25A810, or `scotus/73272830`, *Foote v. Ludlow School Committee*, No. 25-77). Rather, it involves **conflicting parental preferences during a divorce where one custodial parent consented to medical treatment at a hospital** and the only "surgical" procedure predated the family-court order on which the petitioner relies.
   - Furthermore, the Ninth Circuit resolved QP1 solely on the second prong of qualified immunity under *Pearson v. Callahan*, 555 U.S. 223, 236 (2009), without deciding the underlying constitutional merits, and assumed *arguendo* that Dr. Rosenthal and NP Buchignani acted under color of state law despite the district court's finding that UCSF Benioff Children's Hospital Oakland is a **private nonprofit corporation** (Pet. App. 20a–22a). That unresolved state-action threshold issue further insulates the judgment below from plenary review.

2. **Unanimous Seven-Circuit Consensus and Independent Threshold Bars on QP2**:
   - On QP2, the petition candidly concedes (Pet. 32) that **there is no circuit split**: every federal court of appeals to address the issue (the First, Third, Fourth, Sixth, Seventh, Ninth, and Tenth Circuits) holds that guardians ad litem and court-appointed minor's counsel are entitled to absolute quasi-judicial immunity for actions taken within their statutory court-appointed roles (*Cox v. Washington*, 914 F.3d 530, 534 (9th Cir. 2019); *Cok v. Cosentino*, 876 F.2d 1, 3 (1st Cir. 1989); *Fleming v. Asbill*, 42 F.3d 886, 889 (4th Cir. 1994)).
   - Even more problematic for vehicle purposes, the district court held on an independent ground—which the Ninth Circuit did not need to reach—that court-appointed minor's counsel under Cal. Fam. Code § 3150 **does not act under color of state law** under *Polk County v. Dodson*, 454 U.S. 312, 325 (1981), and *Kirtley v. Rainey*, 326 F.3d 1088, 1092–96 (9th Cir. 2003) (Pet. App. 18a–20a). Even if the Supreme Court were inclined to revisit quasi-judicial immunity under the 1871 historical framework of *Baxter v. Bracey*, Respondent Weber would have an immediate alternative ground for affirmance under *Polk County* because a minor's appointed attorney owes an independent professional duty to the child rather than the state.

3. **Comparison to Recent Corpus Precedents**:
   - The Court has repeatedly declined plenary review in far cleaner parental-rights challenges to gender-transition policies, including `scotus/73272830` (*Foote v. Ludlow School Committee*, No. 25-77, denied Apr. 20, 2026 after 13 distributions and 16 amicus briefs) and `scotus/69447478` (*Parents Protecting Our Children, UA v. Eau Claire Area School District*, No. 23-1280, denied Dec. 9, 2024 after 8 distributions), while granting review where circuit splits or direct school-board opt-out policies were cleanly presented, as in `scotus/9026000012` (*Association for Education Fairness v. Montgomery County Board of Education*, No. 26-12) and `scotus/69437012` (*United States v. Skrmetti*, No. 23-477). A backward-looking § 1983 damages suit encumbered by maternal consent, a pre-stipulation surgery date, private-hospital / *Polk County* state-action hurdles, and zero amicus support is far weaker than the petitions the Court has already denied.
