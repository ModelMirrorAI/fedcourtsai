## 2. Disposition and Summary-Disposition Route

- **Predicted Disposition (`denied`, `P(grant) = 0.085`)**: The Court will deny the petition for a writ of certiorari. Once the UCSF Respondents file their Brief in Opposition, the Court will conclude that the unpublished Ninth Circuit memorandum disposition (`2025 WL 2965766`) is a poor, fact-bound vehicle that does not squarely present either Question Presented:
  1. Petitioner's substantive due process claim turns on the interpretation of a specific Contra Costa County Superior Court custody order that stripped Petitioner of medical custody, authorized the mother to consent to "hormone therapy, if recommended by UCSF" (Section 7a), and required joint consent only for "any gender identity related surgery" (Section 7b). Whether inserting a subcutaneous histrelin acetate (Supprelin LA) hormone-blocker implant fell within Section 7a or Section 7b is a case-specific dispute over a state family-court decree, not a genuine circuit conflict over non-caselaw sources of clearly established federal constitutional law.
  2. The second Question Presented—asking the Court to condition qualified immunity on a threshold 1871 common-law historical inquiry—was admittedly forfeited below (Pet. 36).
- **Summary Disposition vs. Plenary Review Conditional on Grant (`summary-disposition-route = 0.176`)**: Conditional on the Court granting certiorari (`0.085`), plenary review (`~82.4%` conditional share, `0.070` unconditional) is substantially likelier than a summary disposition or GVR (`~17.6%` conditional share, `0.015` unconditional). *Mirabelli v. Bonta*, 607 U.S. 492 (2026), was already decided before the petition was filed and is cited in the petition itself, and no pending Supreme Court merits case controls how qualified immunity applies to a state family-court custody decree.

## 3. Question Presented Taken and Conditional Merits Ground (If Granted)

- **Question Presented Taken (Conditional on Grant)**: If the Court were to grant certiorari, it would either **limit the grant to Question 1** or reformulate the question to address whether the Ninth Circuit erred in granting qualified immunity at the Rule 12(b)(6) pleading stage on Petitioner's Fourteenth Amendment claim. The Court would decline Question 2 because Petitioner failed to raise the 1871 common-law historical argument in the district court or the Ninth Circuit.
- **Conditional Merits Ground (If Granted)**: If granted and decided on the merits, the Court would likely address the narrow question whether non-caselaw regulatory and medical materials may inform how a reasonable official understands the scope of a court-ordered limitation on medical consent at the motion-to-dismiss stage, while leaving undisturbed the state-action dismissal of the private respondents.
