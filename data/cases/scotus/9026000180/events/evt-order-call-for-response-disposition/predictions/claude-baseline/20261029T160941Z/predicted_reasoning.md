# Forecast: Hudacko v. Regents of the University of California (No. 26-180)

**Disposition: denied** (P(grant) ≈ 0.12).

- **Posture forecast from.** The docket shows one distribution, for the
  conference of October 9, 2026, and the Court's call for a response on
  September 17, 2026 after every respondent waived. No relist and no CVSG
  so far.
- **Further distribution.** Close to certain. The petition goes back to
  conference once the response is filed or its time runs out, which is
  expected for a December or January conference. I expect one
  post-response distribution, then a denial, with no further relist in the
  most likely path. A relist after that conference would mark the petition
  as a live grant or summary-disposition candidate.
- **CVSG.** Not expected. No federal party or federal statutory scheme is
  involved: this is a § 1983 qualified-immunity dismissal against state
  university physicians and private parties.
- **Which question, if granted.** QP 1, the sources-of-clearly-established-law
  split, is the more certworthy as written. QP 2, the common-law history
  threshold that Justice Thomas raised in *Baxter v. Bracey*, is more likely
  to be left behind or folded into a narrower question. A plenary grant on QP 1
  would be a broad qualified-immunity case, and the Court has consistently
  avoided those.
- **Summary disposition.** A summary route is nearly as likely as plenary
  review. The petition leans on *Mirabelli v. Bonta*, 607 U.S. 492 (2026)
  (per curiam), which recognised the parental right not to be shut out of a
  child's mental-health decisions, gender dysphoria included. That decision
  appears to have come after the Ninth Circuit's October 2025 opinion. A GVR in
  light of *Mirabelli* is therefore a live channel, though a weak one. Whether
  law was clearly established turns on the law at the time of the conduct, so
  *Mirabelli* does not obviously change the qualified-immunity holding.
  Conditional on any grant, I put the summary route at a little under one
  half.
- **Separate writing on denial.** Possible but less likely than not. Justice
  Thomas, who has written repeatedly against the current qualified-immunity
  test, and Justice Alito, who has written on parental rights in this setting,
  are the likeliest authors. Justice Gorsuch is a distant third.
