# Forecast — Hudacko v. Regents of the University of California (No. 26-180)

**Disposition: denied** (P(any grant) = 0.09).

**Where the docket stands.** The petition is at one distribution (for the
10/9/2026 conference). Every respondent waived, but the day after it was
distributed the Court **requested a response**, which was due October 19. The
time was then extended to November 18, 2026, for all respondents. So the
petition was effectively pulled from the 10/9 conference, and nothing will be
decided before the response is filed.

- **Relisting.** I am close to certain the petition gets at least one more
  distribution (P = 0.95). Once the response and any reply are in, it will be
  re-distributed, probably for a December 2026 or early January 2027
  conference. I expect one or two further distributions in total. A third or
  later one would mean someone is drafting a separate writing, or that a grant
  is being seriously weighed.
- **CVSG: no** (P = 0.01). The respondents are a state university and
  individuals, and no federal interest calls for the Solicitor General's views.
- **If granted, which question.** QP 2 asks whether common-law history should
  decide the immunity threshold. The Court has repeatedly passed that up, and
  it would not take it. Nor would it take QP 1, whether only judicial decisions
  can clearly establish the law, in its broad form. The likelier grant
  rewrites the question around this case's facts: whether *Parham*, *Troxel*
  and the custody order clearly established that officials violate parental
  rights by performing gender-related surgery on a minor without the consent
  of a parent who holds that decision.
- **Summary route.** Conditional on a grant, I put about 0.3 on the Court
  disposing of the case in the cert order itself. That could be a GVR in light
  of *Mirabelli v. Bonta* (2026), which postdates the Ninth Circuit's October
  2025 decision, or a per curiam reversal on whether the law was clearly
  established. A GVR does not fit well, because *Mirabelli* cannot change what
  law was clearly established when the conduct happened. That is why plenary
  review remains the likelier route if the Court grants at all.
- **Dissent from denial.** Conditional on denial, I put about 0.4 on a
  separate writing. Justice Alito dissented in *Parents Protecting Our
  Children* and wrote a statement in *Lee v. Poudre*. Justice Thomas has long
  questioned clearly-established-law doctrine (*Baxter v. Bracey*). Either is
  a plausible author of a dissent or a statement respecting the denial,
  possibly joined by Justice Gorsuch.
