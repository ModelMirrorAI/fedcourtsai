# Forecast of the Court's action

The Court will most likely **deny certiorari** after receiving the requested opposition and redistributing the petition. The probability of any grant, including a summary disposition, is **16%**.

The snapshot records **one distribution**, entered September 16 for the October 9, 2026 conference, followed by a response request and an extension through **November 18, 2026**. I expect **one additional distribution**, with two a meaningful alternative, after opposition and the ensuing briefing interval. The probability of at least one further distribution entry is **94%**. That increment may reflect restoration to the conference calendar rather than a substantive relist after completed consideration. My central timing forecast is disposition in **December 2026 through February 2027**, later if additional briefing, relists, or a hold intervene.

The Court will probably **not request the Solicitor General's views**; P(new CVSG) is **2%**. Should it do so, the likely window is after receipt of the requested response, not before the current briefing deadline.

If the Court grants plenary review, it will most likely take a narrowed form of **Question 1**: whether the clearly-established-right inquiry in this public-medical setting adequately accounts for the custody order and alleged professional knowledge, rather than treating the absence of a closely analogous decision as conclusive. The Court is less likely to take Question 2's broader historical reconstruction of immunity, which the petition acknowledges was not raised below.

Conditional on any grant, I assign **40%** to disposition in the cert order and **60%** to plenary review. A summary route would most plausibly involve a narrow correction of the treatment of fair notice or the pleaded facts. I do not predict a routine intervening-decision GVR, because no such controlling development has been identified in the information used here.

Denial remains more likely because the unpublished panel decision can be understood as resolving a particular custody-order ambiguity rather than establishing the categorical rule attacked by the petition. If denied, I expect **no separate writing**, with **25%** probability of at least one noted dissent or statement respecting denial. Thomas is a plausible author on historical immunity grounds, and Alito on parental-rights concerns; these are conditional possibilities, not a forecast of either Justice's unobservable cert vote. No majority opinion or merits holding is predicted on the denial path.
