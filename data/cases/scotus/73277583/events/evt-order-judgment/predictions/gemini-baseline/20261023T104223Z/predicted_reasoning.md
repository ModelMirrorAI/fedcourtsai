# Predicted Reasoning of the Court: *Kendrick Jarrell Beaird v. United States* (No. 25-5343)

## 1. Judgment Class and Vote Lineup

- **Judgment**: **Affirmed** (`affirmed`).
- **Vote Split**: **7–2**.
  - **Majority (7)**: Chief Justice Roberts, Justice Alito, Justice Sotomayor, Justice Kagan, Justice Kavanaugh, Justice Barrett, and Justice Jackson.
  - **Dissent (2)**: Justice Gorsuch and Justice Thomas.

## 2. Question Presented Reached

The Court reaches only the single Question Presented on which certiorari was granted in the April 20, 2026 order—*"Whether Stinson v. United States, 508 U.S. 36 (1993), still correctly states the rule for the deference that courts must give the commentary to the Sentencing Guidelines"*—and answers it in the affirmative. Questions 1 and 3 of the petition (challenging 18 U.S.C. § 922(g)(1) under the Second Amendment and the Commerce Clause) were excluded by the limited grant and are not addressed.

## 3. Doctrinal Basis of the Majority (`majority-ground`)

**Proposition**: The Court holds that *Stinson v. United States*, 508 U.S. 36 (1993), continues to govern judicial deference to Sentencing Guidelines commentary without requiring *Kisor v. Wilkie*'s threshold genuine-ambiguity inquiry, grounding commentary's authority in the Sentencing Reform Act (28 U.S.C. § 994(a)(2)), *Williams v. United States*, 503 U.S. 193 (1992), USSG § 1B1.7, and the post-*Booker* advisory nature of the Guidelines rather than APA § 706 or *Seminole Rock*.

Specifically, the majority opinion rests on four interlocking pillars:
1. **Statutory and Doctrinal Foundation in the Sentencing Reform Act and *Williams***: Rather than treating *Stinson* as a derivative application of *Bowles v. Seminole Rock & Sand Co.*, 325 U.S. 410 (1945)—an analogy *Stinson* itself cautioned is "not precise," 508 U.S. at 44—the Court grounds *Stinson* in 28 U.S.C. § 994(a)(2) (which expressly authorizes the Sentencing Commission to promulgate general policy statements "regarding application of the guidelines"), 18 U.S.C. § 3742(f)(1), *Williams v. United States*, 503 U.S. 193, 201 (1992), and USSG § 1B1.7 (a congressionally reviewed guideline in force since 1987 instructing that commentary is to be treated as the legal equivalent of a policy statement).
2. **Post-*Booker* Advisory Architecture vs. Executive Agency Rules**: Following *United States v. Booker*, 543 U.S. 220 (2005), the Sentencing Guidelines do not bind sentencing courts with the force of law; they provide a uniform national starting point and non-binding recommendation under 18 U.S.C. § 3553(a)(4). Deferring to the Commission on the meaning of its own non-binding sentencing advice raises none of the Article III or APA § 706 concerns addressed in *Loper Bright Enterprises v. Raimondo*, 603 U.S. 369 (2024), or *Kisor v. Wilkie*, 588 U.S. 558 (2019), while sentencing judges retain full authority under § 3553(a) to vary from the advisory range on policy or case-specific grounds.
3. ***Stinson*'s Built-In Textual Boundary**: The majority reaffirms that *Stinson* itself forbids giving weight to commentary that violates the Constitution or a federal statute or is "plainly erroneous or inconsistent" with the guideline text, 508 U.S. at 38, 43 ("If ... the commentary and the guideline it interprets are inconsistent in that following one will result in violating the dictates of the other, the Sentencing Reform Act itself commands compliance with the guideline"). Because *Stinson* already prevents commentary from overriding unambiguous guideline text, superimposing *Kisor*'s multi-step threshold inquiry is unnecessary and would undermine the uniform baseline calculation the Sentencing Reform Act requires.
4. **Application to USSG § 2K2.1(a)(3) and Application Note 2**: Defining a "large capacity magazine" in Application Note 2 as a magazine capable of accepting "more than 15 rounds of ammunition" is a quintessential line-drawing clarification consistent with the text of USSG § 2K2.1(a)(3). The Fifth Circuit therefore properly treated Application Note 2 as authoritative under *Stinson*.

## 4. Breadth of the Majority Ground (`ground-breadth`)

**Proposition**: The holding establishes a categorical rule across the federal Sentencing Guidelines Manual that commentary is authoritative unless it violates the Constitution or a federal statute or is plainly erroneous or inconsistent with the guideline text, while expressly confining that deference regime to the advisory Sentencing Guidelines so as not to disturb *Loper Bright* or *Kisor* for executive agencies.

The ruling is broad and categorical within federal sentencing law—resolving the 6–6 circuit split in favor of the *Stinson* standard across all provisions of the Guidelines Manual—while explicitly cabined outside sentencing law so that executive-branch agencies subject to the Administrative Procedure Act cannot invoke *Stinson* to bypass *Loper Bright* or *Kisor*.

## 5. Separate Writings and Procedural Exits

- **Dissent**: Justice Gorsuch (joined by Justice Thomas) dissents, arguing that neither 28 U.S.C. § 994(a) nor 18 U.S.C. § 3553(a) ("consider") delegates binding interpretive authority to the Commission via unreviewed commentary, that *Williams* rested on pre-*Booker* 18 U.S.C. § 3553(b), and that courts should interpret the Guidelines de novo using commentary at most for its persuasive power under *Skidmore*.
- **Concurrence**: No concurrence in the judgment is expected to fracture the majority holding, though a brief concurrence (potentially by Justice Kavanaugh or Justice Barrett) may underscore that *Stinson*'s "inconsistent with the guideline" limit strictly bars commentary from expanding unambiguous guideline text and that the decision rests on the unique post-*Booker* advisory structure of the Sentencing Reform Act.
- **Procedural Exits**: A dismissal as improvidently granted (DIG) or an equally divided affirmance is not live; all nine Justices participated at oral argument, the 6–6 circuit split urgently requires resolution, and the vehicle cleanly presents the pure methodological question on which certiorari was granted.
