# Predicted Court Reasoning: *Kendrick Jarrell Beaird v. United States* (No. 25-5343)

## Predicted Judgment and Disposition

The Supreme Court will **vacate** the judgment of the United States Court of Appeals for the Fifth Circuit and remand the case for further proceedings (`judgment: "vacated"`, `probability: 0.88` that the judgment below is disturbed).

## Predicted Doctrinal Ground (`majority-ground`)

The majority opinion will hold that *Stinson v. United States*, 508 U.S. 36 (1993), no longer permits courts to treat Sentencing Guidelines commentary as authoritative whenever it is not "plainly erroneous or inconsistent" with an unambiguous Guideline. Instead, *Kisor v. Wilkie*, 588 U.S. 558 (2019), governs the deference due to Guidelines commentary under *Stinson*.

Specifically, the majority will ground its holding on three points:
1. **Text and Structure of *Stinson* Itself:** In *Stinson*, the Court considered and rejected analogies between Guidelines commentary and legislative history or advisory committee notes, and expressly elected to treat commentary "as an agency's interpretation of its own legislative rule" under *Bowles v. Seminole Rock & Sand Co.*, 325 U.S. 410, 414 (1945). See *Stinson*, 508 U.S. at 44–45. Because *Stinson*'s rule was an application of *Seminole Rock*, it did not create a freestanding, bespoke deference doctrine insulated from subsequent refinements to *Seminole Rock* and *Auer v. Robbins*, 519 U.S. 452 (1997).
2. **Application of *Kisor*'s Preconditions:** In *Kisor*, the Court reinforced the limits inherent in *Seminole Rock*/*Auer*—explicitly citing *Stinson* among the decisions applying that doctrine, 588 U.S. at 566 n.4—and held that "the possibility of deference can arise only if a regulation is genuinely ambiguous" after a court has exhausted "all the 'traditional tools' of construction," including text, structure, history, and purpose. *Id.* at 573–575. Those same limits govern Guidelines commentary: while the Guidelines themselves go through mandatory congressional review under 28 U.S.C. § 994(p) and statutory notice-and-comment under § 994(x), commentary is not required by statute to undergo either check, making *Kisor*'s insistence on genuine ambiguity in the underlying rule essential to prevent commentary from expanding or overriding the Guideline text.
3. **Rejection of the Institutional Carve-Out:** The Court will reject the Court-appointed amicus's argument (drawn from *United States v. Vargas*, 74 F.4th 673 (5th Cir. 2023) (en banc), and *United States v. Maloid*, 71 F.4th 795 (10th Cir. 2023)) that the Sentencing Commission's placement in the judicial branch and composed-of-judges structure warrant broader deference to commentary than executive agencies receive under *Kisor*. On the contrary, the Sentencing Reform Act's structural distinction between Guidelines (§ 994(p), (x)) and commentary (§ 994(p)) reinforces why commentary may only interpret genuinely ambiguous Guideline text, not supplement unambiguous text.

## Predicted Breadth of the Holding (`ground-breadth`)

The Court will decide the case on a **general doctrinal rule for Guidelines commentary paired with a remand on case-specific application**:
1. **Categorical Rule Across Guidelines Commentary:** The holding that *Kisor*'s preconditions—most notably the requirement of genuine textual ambiguity after exhausting traditional tools of construction—govern *Stinson* deference will apply categorically across all provisions of the Sentencing Guidelines and their commentary, resolving the 6–6 circuit split on that question.
2. **Restraint on Overruling *Stinson* Outright / Lenity:** Accepting the common ground shared by petitioner's fallback argument and the Solicitor General's Brief in Opposition, the majority will decline to go beyond *Kisor* to abolish deference to Guidelines commentary altogether under *Loper Bright Enterprises v. Raimondo*, 603 U.S. 369 (2024), or the rule of lenity, reserving any question about whether the rule of lenity forecloses deference even as to genuinely ambiguous Guidelines if that issue is not necessary to vacate the Fifth Circuit's *Vargas* rule.
3. **Remand on Application to U.S.S.G. § 4B1.2(b) and Texas Law:** Because the Fifth Circuit treated *Vargas* as foreclosing Beaird's challenge at the threshold and never addressed whether pre-2023 U.S.S.G. § 4B1.2(b) is genuinely ambiguous under *Kisor* or whether Beaird's Texas conviction for possession with intent to deliver otherwise qualifies as a controlled substance offense, the Court will decline to resolve those downstream questions in the first instance, vacating and remanding for the Fifth Circuit to apply the *Kisor*-cabined standard.
