# Predicted Court Reasoning — *Kendrick Jarrell Beaird v. United States* (No. 25-5343)

## 1. Judgment Class and Vote Lineup

- **Predicted Judgment:** `vacated` (the judgment of the United States Court of Appeals for the Fifth Circuit is vacated, and the case is remanded for further proceedings consistent with the Court's opinion).
- **Predicted Vote Lineup:** 9–0 in the judgment to vacate and remand, with a 6-Justice majority opinion (`John G. Roberts, Jr.`, `Sonia Sotomayor`, `Elena Kagan`, `Brett M. Kavanaugh`, `Amy Coney Barrett`, and `Ketanji Brown Jackson`) and 3 Justices concurring in the judgment (`Clarence Thomas`, `Samuel A. Alito, Jr.`, and `Neil M. Gorsuch`).
- **Procedural Exit Assessment:** A dismissal as improvidently granted (`dismissed-as-improvidently-granted`) or an affirmance by an equally divided Court (`affirmed-by-an-equally-divided-court`) is unlikely. No recusal conflict appears on the record (Justice Barrett did not sit on the Seventh Circuit panel in *White*, Justice Kagan was not Solicitor General during any stage of this prosecution, and the Fifth Circuit panel comprised Judges Richman, Graves, and Douglas). Because the Court deliberately narrowed the grant to the abstract threshold standard of deference under *Stinson v. United States*, 508 U.S. 36 (1993), any downstream dispute over how `U.S.S.G. § 2K2.1(a)(3)` applies to a 17-round magazine does not impede answering the Question Presented.

## 2. Scope of Review (Question Reached)

The Court will reach only the single reformulated Question Presented on which certiorari was granted in its April 20, 2026 order:

> *"Whether Stinson v. United States, 508 U. S. 36 (1993), still correctly states the rule for the deference that courts must give the commentary to the Sentencing Guidelines."*

The Court already declined review of Petitioner's first and third questions (challenging 18 U.S.C. § 922(g)(1) under the Second Amendment and the Commerce Clause). Crucially, the Court also declined the Solicitor General's invitation in the Brief in Opposition to frame the second question around the case-specific application ("Whether the district court correctly determined that a 17-round magazine qualified as a 'large capacity magazine' for purposes of an enhancement to petitioner's base offense level under the advisory Sentencing Guidelines § 2K2.1(a)(3) (2021)"). Following its standard practice as "a court of review, not of first view" — and mirroring the disposition in *Kisor v. Wilkie*, 588 U.S. 558, 590 (2019) — the Court will answer the doctrinal question concerning *Stinson* and leave the downstream interpretation of `U.S.S.G. § 2K2.1(a)(3)` and Application Note 2 for the Fifth Circuit on remand.

## 3. Doctrinal Basis of the Majority Opinion (`majority-ground`)

The Court holds that *Kisor v. Wilkie*, 588 U.S. 558 (2019), modified *Stinson v. United States*, 508 U.S. 36 (1993), because *Stinson* explicitly grounded deference to Sentencing Guidelines commentary on *Bowles v. Seminole Rock & Sand Co.*, 325 U.S. 410 (1945), so courts may defer to Guidelines commentary only if the Guideline text remains genuinely ambiguous after exhausting all traditional tools of statutory construction.

Specifically, the majority will reason along three lines:
1. **Textual and doctrinal link between *Stinson* and *Seminole Rock*/*Kisor*:** In *Stinson*, 508 U.S. at 44–45, the Court rejected analogy to legislative history or *Chevron* and instead held that the Sentencing Commission's commentary is "akin to an agency's interpretation of its own legislative rules," adopting *Seminole Rock*'s rule that commentary controls unless "plainly erroneous or inconsistent" with the Guideline. In *Kisor*, 588 U.S. at 568 n.3, the Court explicitly listed *Stinson* among its prior decisions applying *Seminole Rock* (*Auer*) deference and reinforced strict preconditions on that doctrine.
2. **Rejection of the Court-appointed amicus's institutional carve-out:** Court-appointed amicus curiae argues that the Sentencing Commission's placement in the judicial branch and statutory structure under 28 U.S.C. § 994(a), (p), and (x) insulate *Stinson* from *Kisor*. The majority will reject that distinction: Congress required Guidelines themselves — unlike commentary — to undergo notice-and-comment rulemaking (§ 994(x)) and 180-day congressional review (§ 994(p)). Allowing unambiguous Guideline text to be expanded by unreviewed commentary whenever there is no "flat inconsistency" would subvert the statutory distinction between Guidelines and commentary and conflict with *Kisor*.
3. **Exhaustion of traditional tools before deference:** Under *Kisor*, a sentencing court must first exhaust all traditional tools of construction — text, structure, history, and purpose — to determine the meaning of a Guideline provision. Only if the Guideline remains genuinely ambiguous after that inquiry, and only if the commentary is a reasonable reading within the zone of ambiguity, may a court defer to the commentary. Because the Fifth Circuit below relied on circuit precedent (*United States v. Martin*, 119 F.4th 410 (5th Cir. 2024), and *United States v. Vargas*, 74 F.4th 673 (5th Cir. 2023) (en banc)) applying *Stinson*'s unmodified "flat inconsistency" test without first determining whether `U.S.S.G. § 2K2.1(a)(3)` is genuinely ambiguous, its judgment must be vacated.

## 4. Breadth of the Majority's Ground (`ground-breadth`)

The Court announces a categorical methodological rule governing all official commentary to the United States Sentencing Guidelines across all federal criminal sentencings, while declining to resolve in the first instance whether `U.S.S.G. § 2K2.1(a)(3)`'s "large capacity magazine" provision is genuinely ambiguous as applied to petitioner's 17-round magazine, remanding that question to the Fifth Circuit.

## 5. Separate Writings

Justices Thomas, Alito, and Gorsuch will concur in the judgment vacating the Fifth Circuit's decision, writing separately to argue that *Stinson* should be overruled altogether rather than cabined by *Kisor*. Building on Justice Gorsuch's concurrence in the judgment in *Kisor* and the Court's decision in *Loper Bright Enterprises v. Raimondo*, 603 U.S. 369 (2024), the concurrence in the judgment will contend that judicial deference to Sentencing Commission commentary that increases a criminal defendant's sentencing range raises acute separation-of-powers, due-process, and rule-of-lenity concerns, and that courts should exercise independent judgment over the meaning of the Guidelines without binding deference to commentary even where a Guideline is ambiguous.
