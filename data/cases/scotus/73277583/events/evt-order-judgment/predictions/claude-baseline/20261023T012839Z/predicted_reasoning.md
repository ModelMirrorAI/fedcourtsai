# Forecast — Beaird v. United States, No. 25-5343 (merits, grant moment)

**Judgment class: vacated** (and remanded). The Court will hold that *Stinson v.
United States* no longer correctly states the deference owed to Guidelines
commentary, and send the case back to the Fifth Circuit to reassess the
§2K2.1(a)(3) "large capacity magazine" enhancement under the corrected rule.

**Vote: 8–1 or 7–2 to vacate.** Expect a broad majority, likely with Justice
Alito (and possibly one more Justice) dissenting or concurring only in the
judgment on the view that the Commission's commentary, as the product of a
judicial-branch body with congressional oversight, warrants Stinson-level
weight. A separate concurrence (Gorsuch and/or Thomas) arguing that commentary
gets no binding deference at all — that it is at most persuasive, in the
spirit of *Loper Bright* — is likely, but they join the vacatur.

**Question reached.** The Court limited the grant to QP II (Stinson); the
Second Amendment and Commerce Clause questions are out.

**Majority ground.** The Kisor framework (genuine ambiguity after the
traditional tools, plus the character-and-context limits) governs whether a
court may defer to interpretive commentary. Because the government agreed in
its brief in opposition that Kisor sets the standard, the judgment is defended
by court-appointed amicus; the Court rejects amicus's position that Stinson's
unqualified test survives.

**Breadth.** A categorical rule for all interpretive commentary, with the
application to "large capacity magazine" left for remand rather than decided.

**Procedural exits.** A DIG is unlikely (≈3%): the issue was preserved at
sentencing and the government's harmlessness point (every circuit applying
Kisor has still deferred on this phrase) is better suited to remand. An
affirmance — the Court deciding the phrase is genuinely ambiguous itself, or
that Stinson survives — is the main alternative (≈20%).
