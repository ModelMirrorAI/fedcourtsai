# Forecast of the Court's judgment

The Court will **vacate the Fifth Circuit's judgment and remand**, by a **6–3 vote**. Roberts, Thomas, Alito, Gorsuch, Kavanaugh, and Barrett will form the majority; Sotomayor, Kagan, and Jackson will dissent. The sentencing issue, not the validity of Beaird's felon-in-possession conviction, will drive the judgment.

The majority will displace Stinson's binding-deference rule: courts must independently interpret the Guidelines, with Commission commentary receiving persuasive rather than controlling weight, including when the guideline is ambiguous. The Commission's expertise can inform interpretation, but commentary cannot dictate a court's reading merely because it is not inconsistent with the guideline. The rule will apply generally to interpretive Guidelines commentary, not just the definition of a large-capacity magazine.

The Court will leave the first application of that rule to the lower courts. They will reconsider whether the text, structure, and history of Section 2K2.1 support the enhancement for the 17-round magazine without treating Application Note 2 as binding. Vacatur will not itself establish that a 17-round magazine falls outside the guideline or entitle Beaird to a particular sentence.

The Court will reach only the question in its April 20, 2026 limited grant. It will not decide the petition's Second Amendment or Commerce Clause challenges. The dissent will defend retaining controlling weight for the Commission's reasonable interpretive commentary, emphasizing precedent and the Commission's distinctive sentencing role. Some agreement on the result could extend beyond the forecast majority, but the committed lineup is six to three.

A decision after briefing and argument, most likely by the end of the following merits cycle in June 2027, is the expected route. A DIG or an equally divided affirmance is not the forecast; the supplied record identifies no recusal or concrete jurisdictional defect.
