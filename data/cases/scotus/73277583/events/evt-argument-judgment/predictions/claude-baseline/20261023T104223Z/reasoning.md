# Reasoning — why P(disturbed) = 0.30

**Inputs read.** I read the snapshot `2026-10-14.json`, `context.json` (forward
mode, cutoff 2026-10-14), and every provisioned document: the QPs, the petition,
the BIO, the appendix (the Fifth Circuit's unpublished summary-calendar
affirmance and the district court judgment), the petitioner's merits brief and
reply, the United States' brief "supporting vacatur" and its reply, and the full
argument transcript (`argument-transcript.txt`, 143 pages, not empty). I read the
briefs mainly through their summaries of argument and tables of contents; I read
the transcript in full. I made no CourtListener, web or corpus-query retrieval.
The court-appointed amicus's brief was not provisioned, so I know its position
only from the argument and the other briefs.

**Anchor.** The statpack's merits section publishes an `excluded` count, so it is
quotable. Certiorari was granted on Apr. 20, 2026, which is grant Term OT2025.
The pool is grant Terms 2015–2024, and the table shows parsed rows only for
2017–2024. Pooled: 360 disturbed of 516 parsed, which is **69.8%**. Coverage is
close to complete for those Terms (for example 2024: 73 parsed of 75 granted;
2017: 52 of 56). That rate is the bar.

**Why I sit well below it.** This is an unusual posture. The United States does
not defend the judgment; it asks for vacatur. The Court appointed an amicus to
defend Stinson, and that amicus is in effect the defender of the judgment.
Confessions of error usually push the disturbed rate *above* the base rate, so
the downward move here rests entirely on the argument. I read the bench like
this:

- **Toward affirmance (Stinson stands):** Sotomayor (Kisor would create "chaos";
  the application note is what makes the guideline workable); Jackson
  (guidelines and commentary are "inextricably linked"; the consistency and data
  rationale); Alito (asked how a judge could define "large" without deferring;
  raised the 10,000-resentencings concern; pointed to the district court's
  same-sentence statement); Roberts (Stinson "a more workable regime"; Loper
  Bright has little to do with a judicial-branch agency giving non-binding
  advice); Kavanaugh (Stinson plus Booker, 1B1.7 since 1987, stability, and
  "if I end up agreeing with your position" put to the amicus); Kagan (the
  express authorization to issue policy statements, Williams).
- **Toward vacatur:** Gorsuch (sustained, emphatic attack: silence is not
  delegation, Loper Bright, Booker's "topsy-turvy" result, no reliance
  interests); Thomas (called the amicus's argument "metaphysics" and asked
  whether a court would have more freedom with a statute than with a guideline;
  he has a long record against Auer-style deference).
- **Unclear:** Barrett pressed the petitioner that the dispute is "all about
  nomenclature" and asked whether the Commission was "pulling a fast one". She
  also got the SG to agree that Stinson already refuses commentary that is
  flatly inconsistent with the guideline. I read her as leaning toward
  affirmance, with a concurrence.

Even the Solicitor General said twice that the government would be "happy"
with, and on policy would prefer, the amicus's approach. That removes the usual
force of a confession of error. A question is not a vote, and Kavanaugh and
Kagan were partly testing theories. Still, six Justices showed openly
pro-Stinson, stability-first concerns, and that is a strong lean.

**What keeps me from going lower (0.30 rather than about 0.15).** There is
a real path to vacatur even with Stinson reaffirmed. The Court could adopt the
SG's "Stinson read through Kisor" synthesis as the least disruptive doctrinal
fix and remand for the Fifth Circuit to apply it. The Fifth Circuit's en banc
*Vargas* rule (commentary can control even against unambiguous text) is the
version the SG attacked. A Court that wants to correct *Vargas* and still keep
deference broad might vacate. Several Justices, Barrett and Kavanaugh among
them, may also find that middle path attractive. The base rate is 70%, and
argument-based reads are noisy.
