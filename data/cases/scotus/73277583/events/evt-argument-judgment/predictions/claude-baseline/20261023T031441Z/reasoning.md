# Reasoning — P(judgment disturbed) = 0.27

**What I read.** I read the snapshot `2026-10-14.json`, which runs through the
argument on 2026-10-13, and `context.json`, which is forward mode with a date
cut at 2026-10-14. I also read the provisioned documents: the questions
presented, petitioner's merits brief (mainly the statement of the case and the
table of contents), the summary of the United States' merits brief, and the
**argument transcript**, read in large part, with every Justice's questions to
all three advocates examined. I did not read the petition, BIO, appendix or
reply briefs in depth; the cert-stage questions on § 922(g)(1) and the Commerce
Clause fell away under the limited grant. No retrieval was made beyond the
provisioned inputs.

**Anchor.** The statpack's merits section publishes an `excluded` count, so it
is quotable. The grant was entered on 2026-04-20, which puts it in grant Term
OT2025. The pool is the ten strictly prior grant Terms, 2015–2024. Only
2017–2024 render, because 2015 and 2016 have no parsed judgments. That pool
gives disturbed 360 of parsed 516, which is **69.8%**. Coverage is near-complete
for those Terms, for example 2024 has 73 parsed of 75 granted. This is the bar
the cell is scored against.

**Why I go well below the anchor.**
1. **The posture is unusual.** The United States confessed error and argued for
   vacatur, and the Court appointed amicus (Anthony Dick) to defend the
   judgment. In confession-of-error cases with appointed amicus, the Court does
   side with amicus at a meaningful rate, so the usual reversal tilt is weaker
   here.
2. **The argument read strongly against petitioner.** Questions are not votes,
   but the pattern was consistent:
   - Roberts suggested *Stinson* is "a more workable regime" after *Booker*.
   - Jackson stressed that guidelines and commentary are inseparable.
   - Sotomayor pressed workability and denied that *Kisor* upset *Stinson*.
   - Kavanaugh cited 30 years of practice and the post-*Booker* stability
     concern. He got the SG to concede that the amicus's position better serves
     stability, and the SG said the government would be fine with it.
   - Kagan developed the express-delegation theory through policy statements and
     *Williams*, which is exactly the amicus's theory.
   - Alito pressed the practical consequences, roughly 10,000 resentencings, and
     harmlessness given the district court's alternative-sentence statement.
   - Barrett called the dispute "all about nomenclature" and asked about
     probation officers' reliance.
   - Only Gorsuch, at length, and Thomas ("metaphysics class") were clearly hostile
     to the amicus.

   I read six or seven Justices as leaning toward keeping *Stinson*.
3. **Even a clarified *Stinson* likely affirms.** The commentary's
   15-round line is not plainly inconsistent with "large capacity magazine," so
   a narrowing opinion can still affirm.

**Uncertainty.** The main risk to this forecast is a vacate-and-remand ordered
on a *Kisor*-lite clarification, the SG's position, which Barrett and possibly
Kagan could join as a compromise. The Court also has a general habit of
remanding after it announces a standard. That risk, plus the strong anchor, keeps me from going
below about 0.25. I put DIG at about 3%; it counts as undisturbed and so sits
inside the 0.73.

**Votes.** I forecast 7–2, with Gorsuch and Thomas dissenting. I am least
confident about Barrett and Thomas.
