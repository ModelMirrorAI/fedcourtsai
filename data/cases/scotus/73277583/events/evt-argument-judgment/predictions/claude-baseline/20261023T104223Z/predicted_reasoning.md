# Predicted outcome — Beaird v. United States, No. 25-5343 (argued Oct. 13, 2026)

**Judgment: affirmed. P(judgment below disturbed) = 0.30.**

**Vote: 7–2 to affirm.** Majority: Roberts, Alito, Sotomayor, Kagan, Kavanaugh,
Barrett, Jackson. Dissent: Thomas and Gorsuch, with Gorsuch writing the principal
dissent. The likeliest way the forecast fails is a narrower majority that adopts
the Solicitor General's middle path, folding Kisor's limits into Stinson and
vacating so the Fifth Circuit can re-examine the 2K2.1 enhancement. Barrett is the
swing vote on whether the opinion says Stinson "always meant" Kisor's limits. A
DIG is not a live exit: the posture was settled when the Court appointed an amicus,
and the question is cleanly presented.

**Question reached.** Only the limited question the Court granted: whether
Stinson still correctly states the deference owed to Guidelines commentary. The
Second Amendment and Commerce Clause questions were not granted and stay out.

**Ground (the `majority-ground` claim).** The majority reaffirms Stinson and
rests it on the Sentencing Reform Act itself, not on an implied-delegation theory
from Seminole Rock. The Act expressly authorizes policy statements "regarding
application of the guidelines." Williams already treats an interpretive policy
statement as an authoritative guide, and USSG 1B1.7 and Congress's own
amendments of commentary show that commentary works the same way. The Court
stresses that the guidelines are advisory after Booker: deferring only fixes what
the Commission's *advice* means, and the judge stays free to vary. Stinson's own
limit still applies, so commentary that is plainly erroneous or flatly
inconsistent with the guideline does not control. The 15-round definition of a
large-capacity magazine is a reasonable gloss on "capable of accepting a large
capacity magazine," so the enhancement for a 17-round magazine stands. The
opinion may also cite the district court's statement that it would have imposed
the same sentence anyway.

**Breadth (the `ground-breadth` claim).** The rule is categorical for Guidelines
commentary and is not limited to 2K2.1. It is also expressly fenced off: the
opinion says it does nothing to revive deference to executive agencies under
Loper Bright or Kisor. Justice Kavanaugh asked the amicus for exactly that
assurance at argument, and Justice Alito's and the Chief Justice's concerns about
stability point the same way.

**Separate writings (context, not scored).** A Gorsuch dissent, likely joined by
Thomas, arguing that 3553(a) only tells courts to "consider" the commentary,
that silence cannot work as a delegation after Loper Bright, and that binding
commentary sits oddly beside advisory guidelines after Booker. Possibly a
Barrett concurrence saying Stinson's "plainly erroneous or inconsistent" limit
already does the work Kisor's limits would. Authorship of the majority is a
guess (Kavanaugh or Kagan).
