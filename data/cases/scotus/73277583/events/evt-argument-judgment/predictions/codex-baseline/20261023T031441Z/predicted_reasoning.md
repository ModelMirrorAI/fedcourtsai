# Forecast of the Court's judgment and reasoning

## Judgment and lineup

The Court will **affirm** the Fifth Circuit's judgment, retaining Stinson's rule for deference to interpretive Sentencing Guidelines commentary. My modal lineup is **7–2**: Roberts, Alito, Sotomayor, Kagan, Kavanaugh, Barrett, and Jackson in the majority; Thomas and Gorsuch dissenting. The structured probability that the judgment below is disturbed is **0.28**, not a probability concerning certiorari, which has already been granted.

The Court will address the question specified in the April 20, 2026 grant order: whether Stinson still correctly states the rule for deference to Guidelines commentary. It will not decide the petition's separate Second Amendment and Commerce Clause questions.

## Majority ground

The majority will treat the Commission's interpretation of its own sentencing recommendations differently from an executive agency's construction of binding law. It will read the Sentencing Reform Act's authorization of Guidelines and policy statements concerning their application as supporting authoritative explanatory commentary. Stinson's connection to Williams, the role of Guidelines § 1B1.7, and the statutory sentencing framework will carry more weight than the analogy to Seminole Rock considered in isolation.

On this account, the judge must identify the Commission's recommendation accurately before independently deciding the appropriate sentence under § 3553(a). Booker makes the recommended range advisory; it does not make the contents of the recommendation whatever each sentencing judge considers best. The majority will preserve the distinction between correctly calculating a range and choosing whether to impose a sentence within it.

The Court will therefore reject both abolition of commentary deference and the government's request to make Kisor's genuine-ambiguity inquiry a prerequisite here. It will preserve Stinson's limits: commentary cannot override a statute, violate the Constitution, or contradict the Guideline it explains. Retaining that limit will not require judges to conduct Kisor's exhaustion inquiry before consulting otherwise consistent commentary. The magazine-capacity definition will remain an authoritative application of § 2K2.1, leaving the judgment below intact.

## Breadth and separate positions

The holding will govern interpretive Guidelines commentary generally, not only Beaird's 17-round magazine. At the same time, the Court will expressly cabin its rationale to the Sentencing Commission and its unusual statutory and advisory-sentencing framework. It will not retreat generally from Loper Bright or expand executive-agency deference.

Gorsuch will object that the authority to promulgate policy statements does not expressly delegate binding interpretive authority over Guidelines and that accurate range calculation still has substantial legal consequences after Booker. Thomas will join the dissenting side. A separate concurrence defending the result principally through statutory stare decisis is plausible, but the vote block does not make an authorship or writing-role prediction.

The main alternative is a vacatur and remand adopting Kisor's limits while preserving substantial deference in practice. That would count as disturbing the judgment even if the Fifth Circuit ultimately reinstated the same sentence. A DIG is a small residual possibility; the appointment of an adversarial amicus and completion of argument make a merits resolution much more likely. No nonparticipation appears in the provisioned argument, so an equally divided affirmance is not a meaningful central scenario.
