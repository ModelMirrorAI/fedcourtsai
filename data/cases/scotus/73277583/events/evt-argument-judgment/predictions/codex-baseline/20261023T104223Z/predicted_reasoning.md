# Forecast of the Court's judgment

The Court will affirm the Fifth Circuit's judgment, retaining Stinson's rule for interpretive Sentencing Guidelines commentary rather than importing Kisor's genuine-ambiguity threshold. My point forecast is 7–2: Roberts, Alito, Sotomayor, Kagan, Kavanaugh, Barrett, and Jackson in the majority; Thomas and Gorsuch dissenting. The corresponding probability that the judgment below is disturbed is 0.24.

## Ground and scope

The Court will answer the question in the April 20, 2026 grant order: the continuing validity of Stinson's commentary-deference standard. It will not decide the petition's separate Second Amendment or Commerce Clause questions. It will treat determining the Commission's recommended sentencing range as distinct from deciding what sentence the district judge should impose. The judge must correctly ascertain the recommendation, including the Commission's authoritative explanation, but remains free under Booker to select a different sentence with an adequate explanation.

The majority will ground the result in the Sentencing Reform Act's allocation of sentencing-guidance responsibilities to the Commission, Stinson's connection to Williams and Guideline 1B1.7, and statutory stare decisis. It will conclude that Kisor's treatment of executive-agency interpretations does not require a separate genuine-ambiguity inquiry before following commentary in this distinctive advisory system. It will distinguish Loper Bright without retreating from that decision's treatment of executive agencies.

This will be a general rule for interpretive Guidelines commentary, not merely a ruling that a seventeen-round magazine is large or that Beaird would have received the same sentence anyway. Commentary will remain subordinate to the Constitution, federal statutes, and the guideline it explains: retaining Stinson will not authorize an interpretation that is inconsistent with, or a plainly erroneous reading of, the guideline. The opinion will preserve those limits without adopting Kisor's full threshold framework. It will not establish a general power for executive agencies to give binding interpretations through guidance documents.

The Court will therefore leave intact the judgment sustaining Beaird's sentence. It need not independently choose a numerical definition of large-capacity magazine to do so. Alito's harmless-error concern supplies an additional reason to resist remand, but I do not predict harmless error as the majority's principal ground.

## Division and alternatives

Gorsuch will argue that the authority to issue guidance is not authority to bind courts to an interpretation, and that neither statutory silence nor the commentary's practical convenience can displace independent judicial interpretation. Thomas will join the dissenting side. The dissent will emphasize the procedural distinction between guidelines and commentary and the continuing legal consequences of calculating an advisory range. The Court's majority will instead emphasize the coherent, nationally uniform starting point Congress wanted the Commission to supply.

The strongest alternative is vacatur and remand under the government's Kisor approach, without overruling Stinson outright and without promising Beaird a shorter sentence. Even under that disposition, the Fifth Circuit could retain the enhancement or find any error harmless on remand. Such a Supreme Court vacatur would nevertheless disturb the judgment for this event. Complete abolition of commentary deference is less likely than that intermediate alternative. A DIG is possible but not the expected exit; the developed circuit disagreement and fully adversarial argument favor resolving the legal question. Nothing in the supplied record suggests a recusal making an equally divided affirmance likely.

I expect a decision during the October 2026 Term, most likely in the first half of 2027 and by the end of June. This timing estimate is context, not a scored claim.
