# Predicted Reasoning: *Kendrick Jarrell Beaird v. United States* (No. 25-5343)

## Judgment and Vote Lineup

- **Judgment Class**: **`affirmed`** (`granted: 0`, `P(disturbed) = 0.28`). The Supreme Court will affirm the judgment of the United States Court of Appeals for the Fifth Circuit.
- **Vote Split and Lineup**: **7–2** affirmance.
  - **Majority (7)**: Chief Justice Roberts and Justices Alito, Sotomayor, Kagan, Kavanaugh, Barrett, and Jackson.
  - **Dissent (2)**: Justices Gorsuch and Thomas.

## Scope of Review and Question Reached

The Court will resolve the single question on which certiorari was granted: whether *Stinson v. United States*, 508 U.S. 36 (1993), still correctly states the rule for the deference that courts must give the commentary to the Sentencing Guidelines. It will reject both Petitioner's unpreserved primary contention that commentary receives no deference at all and the Solicitor General's middle-ground position that *Kisor v. Wilkie*, 588 U.S. 558 (2019), modified *Stinson* by imposing a threshold "genuine ambiguity" gatekeeping test before sentencing courts may apply Guidelines commentary.

## Majority Ground (`majority-ground`)

The majority will reaffirm *Stinson v. United States*, 508 U.S. 36 (1993), and decline to transplant *Kisor v. Wilkie*, 588 U.S. 558 (2019), or *Loper Bright Enterprises v. Raimondo*, 603 U.S. 369 (2024), into the calculation of the advisory Sentencing Guidelines. The Court's reasoning will rest on four interlocking pillars:

1. **Statutory Structure, *Williams*, and U.S.S.G. § 1B1.7**: Unlike *Chevron* or *Seminole Rock* deference to an executive agency under the Administrative Procedure Act, *Stinson*'s rule is grounded in the Sentencing Reform Act of 1984 (28 U.S.C. § 994(a)(1)–(2), (o), (p) and 18 U.S.C. §§ 3553(a)(4)–(5), 3742(f)(1)), *Williams v. United States*, 503 U.S. 193 (1992), and the text of U.S.S.G. § 1B1.7 ("Significance of Commentary"), which has been part of the Guidelines Manual since 1987, repeatedly reviewed by Congress, and expressly instructs that failure to follow interpretive or explanatory commentary can constitute an incorrect application of the Guidelines. As *Stinson* itself recognized, the analogy to *Seminole Rock* was "not precise."
2. **Post-*Booker* Advisory Nature of the Guidelines**: Under *United States v. Booker*, 543 U.S. 220 (2005), the Guidelines Manual no longer binds sentencing judges with the force of law. Instead, a Guideline and its accompanying Application Notes operate as an integrated instruction manual for calculating the Sentencing Commission's uniform benchmark recommendation under 18 U.S.C. § 3553(a)(4), after which the sentencing court exercises independent Article III judgment under § 3553(a) to vary upward or downward. Treating commentary as authoritative in computing the Commission's recommended range therefore does not usurp the judicial power or conflict with *Loper Bright*.
3. ***Stinson*'s Built-In Textual Boundary**: *Stinson* already prohibits following commentary that violates the Constitution or a federal statute or is "inconsistent with, or a plainly erroneous reading of, that guideline." That standard already prevents commentary from overriding unambiguous Guideline text that cannot bear the construction, without inviting threshold litigation over whether open-ended Guideline terms are "genuinely ambiguous" before consulting Application Notes.
4. ***Stare Decisis* and Systemic Workability**: Overruling or modifying *Stinson* after more than three decades of congressional acquiescence—including statutes directing the Commission to amend specific Application Notes and Commission rules submitting commentary amendments to public notice-and-comment and congressional review—would needlessly destabilize roughly 66,000 federal sentencings each year and undermine nationwide uniformity in calculating the baseline Guidelines range.

Applying *Stinson* here, Application Note 2's definition of a "large capacity magazine" in U.S.S.G. § 2K2.1(a)(3) as a magazine capable of accepting more than 15 rounds of ammunition is consistent with the Guideline text and valid.

## Breadth of the Holding (`ground-breadth`)

The majority will announce a **categorical, Manual-wide rule** governing judicial reliance on interpretive and explanatory commentary across the U.S. Sentencing Guidelines generally, rather than a narrow holding confined to U.S.S.G. § 2K2.1(a)(3)'s large-capacity-magazine enhancement or the harmlessness of the district court's alternative sentence.

## Separate Writings and Procedural Exits

- **Dissent**: Justice Gorsuch (joined by Justice Thomas) will dissent, arguing that the Sentencing Reform Act distinguishes between "guidelines" (18 U.S.C. § 3553(a)(4), 28 U.S.C. § 994(a)(1), (p), (x)), "policy statements" (§ 994(a)(2)), and "official commentary" (§ 3553(b)(1)), and that *Stinson* rested on an implied delegation of interpretive authority that cannot survive *Loper Bright* or *Kisor*.
- **Concurrence**: A brief concurring opinion (likely by Justice Kavanaugh or Justice Barrett) may emphasize that affirming *Stinson* in the context of the Judicial Branch's advisory Sentencing Guidelines casts no doubt on *Loper Bright* or *Kisor* in executive-agency administrative law, and that *Stinson* itself never permits commentary to contradict unambiguous Guideline text.
- **Procedural Exits**: Neither a dismissal as improvidently granted (DIG) nor an affirmance by an equally divided Court is live. All nine Justices participated at oral argument, and the Court granted review specifically to resolve the entrenched 6–6 circuit split over *Stinson* and *Kisor*.
