# Predicted Court Reasoning: *Kendrick Jarrell Beaird v. United States* (No. 25-5343)

## 1. Judgment Class and Vote Lineup

- **Predicted Judgment:** `affirmed` (the judgment of the United States Court of Appeals for the Fifth Circuit is affirmed).
- **Predicted Vote Lineup:** **7–2** to affirm.
  - **Majority (7):** Chief Justice Roberts, Justice Alito, Justice Sotomayor, Justice Kagan, Justice Kavanaugh, Justice Barrett, and Justice Jackson.
  - **Dissent (2):** Justice Gorsuch and Justice Thomas.
  - **Likely Writing Roles (Context-Only):** Justice Kavanaugh (or Chief Justice Roberts / Justice Jackson) is a prime candidate to author the majority opinion emphasizing institutional stability, *stare decisis*, the statutory design of the Sentencing Reform Act under *Williams v. United States*, 503 U.S. 193 (1992), and the post-*Booker* advisory character of the Guidelines. Justice Gorsuch is expected to author a dissent (joined by Justice Thomas) arguing that *Stinson v. United States*, 508 U.S. 36 (1993), cannot be squared with *Loper Bright Enterprises v. Raimondo*, 603 U.S. 369 (2024), or at a minimum must be cabined by *Kisor v. Wilkie*, 588 U.S. 558 (2019).

## 2. Questions Reached and Procedural Exit

The Court granted certiorari limited exclusively to Question II of the petition: *"Whether Stinson v. United States, 508 U.S. 36 (1993), still correctly states the rule for the deference that courts must give the commentary to the Sentencing Guidelines."* (Petitioner's Second Amendment and Commerce Clause challenges to 18 U.S.C. § 922(g)(1) were excluded by the limited grant order.)

A procedural exit—such as a dismissal as improvidently granted (`dismissed-as-improvidently-granted`) or an affirmance by an equally divided Court—is unlikely. No Justice is recused, and the Circuits are squarely divided (roughly 6–6) over whether *Kisor* alters *Stinson*, making a definitive doctrinal resolution across the 66,000 annual federal criminal sentencings a high institutional priority for the Court.

## 3. Majority Ground (`majority-ground`)

The Court will hold that *Stinson v. United States*, 508 U.S. 36 (1993), continues to state the governing rule for judicial deference to official commentary in the U.S. Sentencing Guidelines Manual—namely, that commentary interpreting or explaining a guideline is authoritative unless it violates the Constitution or a federal statute, or is inconsistent with, or a plainly erroneous reading of, that guideline—without importing the threshold "genuine ambiguity" multi-step framework of *Kisor v. Wilkie*, 588 U.S. 558 (2019), or the de novo statutory-interpretation rule of *Loper Bright Enterprises v. Raimondo*, 603 U.S. 369 (2024).

The majority's reasoning will rest on four interlocking pillars developed by Court-appointed amicus curiae and embraced by a cross-ideological bench coalition at oral argument:

1. **Statutory Text and Structure of the Sentencing Reform Act (*Williams* and U.S.S.G. § 1B1.7):** Unlike executive-branch regulations governed by the Administrative Procedure Act (5 U.S.C. § 706), the Sentencing Commission is an independent commission in the Judicial Branch (28 U.S.C. § 991(a)) expressly directed by Congress to promulgate both "guidelines" (28 U.S.C. § 994(a)(1)) and "general policy statements regarding application of the guidelines" (28 U.S.C. § 994(a)(2)), and 18 U.S.C. § 3553(b) explicitly directs sentencing courts to consider the Commission's "official commentary." Moreover, U.S.S.G. § 1B1.7—itself a congressionally reviewed guideline in force since 1987—instructs that commentary explains how guidelines are to be applied and that failure to follow commentary can constitute an incorrect application of the Guidelines under 18 U.S.C. § 3742(f)(1). As *Stinson* itself recognized, its analogy to *Bowles v. Seminole Rock & Sand Co.*, 325 U.S. 410 (1945), was "not precise" (508 U.S. at 44), and *Stinson* built directly upon *Williams v. United States*, 503 U.S. 193, 201 (1992), which held that the Commission's policy statements are authoritative interpretations of the Guidelines.
2. **Post-*Booker* Advisory Nature of the Guidelines:** Since *United States v. Booker*, 543 U.S. 220 (2005), the Guidelines Manual does not bind sentencing courts with the force of law. Rather, calculating the Guidelines range is the first procedural step in which the sentencing judge determines what the Sentencing Commission *recommends* as an initial benchmark before exercising independent Article III judgment under 18 U.S.C. § 3553(a). Because the Guidelines and commentary are drafted and promulgated together as an integrated manual (typically through the same notice-and-comment and congressional-submission process) to express the Commission's recommended range, reading the guideline text in isolation from its accompanying Application Notes would distort the Commission's recommendation without advancing judicial independence, which is fully preserved by the sentencing court's authority to vary under § 3553(a).
3. **Inapplicability of *Loper Bright* and *Kisor*:** The majority will make crystal-clear that retaining *Stinson* neither retreats from *Loper Bright* nor weakens *Kisor*'s limits on executive-agency *Auer*/*Seminole Rock* deference. *Loper Bright* rested on Section 706 of the APA and the judiciary's duty to declare the meaning of binding statutes enacted by Congress; *Kisor* cabined deference to executive agencies interpreting their own binding regulations. Neither doctrine governs how sentencing courts ascertain the non-binding sentencing range recommended by a Judicial Branch commission under the Sentencing Reform Act.
4. ***Stare Decisis*, Uniformity, and Clarifying *Stinson*'s Built-In Textual Boundary:** The majority will emphasize that 33 years of settled practice, congressional acquiescence in U.S.S.G. § 1B1.7 and annual Guidelines amendments, and the practical realities of 66,000 federal sentencings per year weigh heavily against replacing *Stinson* with either de novo review or a *Kisor* threshold-ambiguity inquiry that would invite litigation over whether every open-textured Guideline phrase is "ambiguous enough." At the same time, the majority will clarify that *Stinson*'s own standard—that commentary cannot stand if it is "inconsistent with, or a plainly erroneous reading of, that guideline" (508 U.S. at 38) and applies only "if the guideline which the commentary interprets will bear the construction" (id. at 46)—already prohibits commentary that contradicts unambiguous Guideline text. Applied here, Application Note 2's specification that a magazine capable of accepting more than 15 rounds qualifies as a "large capacity magazine" under U.S.S.G. § 2K2.1(a)(3) is plainly consistent with the Guideline text, requiring affirmance.

## 4. Breadth of the Majority Ground (`ground-breadth`)

The Court will announce a **categorical rule** governing official commentary throughout the U.S. Sentencing Guidelines Manual across all federal criminal sentencings generally, resolving the circuit split over *Stinson*'s vitality rather than narrowing its decision to U.S.S.G. § 2K2.1(a)(3)'s definition of "large capacity magazine" or disposing of Beaird's appeal on case-specific harmless-error grounds.

## 5. Separate Writings

- **Dissent (Justice Gorsuch, joined by Justice Thomas):** Will argue that *Stinson* rested squarely on *Seminole Rock*'s discredited presumption that statutory or regulatory ambiguity constitutes an implicit delegation of lawmaking authority, that commentary is not subject to the mandatory notice-and-comment and 180-day congressional-waiting-period requirements of 28 U.S.C. § 994(p) and (x), and that treating commentary as binding on the Guidelines range calculation cannot be reconciled with *Loper Bright* or *Kisor*.
- **Possible Concurrence (e.g., Justice Barrett or Justice Kagan):** May write separately to underscore that *Stinson*'s "inconsistent with, or a plainly erroneous reading of, that guideline" limitation has real teeth and does not license commentary to override unambiguous Guideline text—converging in practice with *Kisor*'s rejection of reflexive deference when commentary conflicts with clear text.
