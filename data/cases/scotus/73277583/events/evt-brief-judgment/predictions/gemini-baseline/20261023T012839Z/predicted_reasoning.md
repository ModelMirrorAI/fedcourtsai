# Predicted Reasoning of the Court — *Kendrick Jarrell Beaird v. United States* (No. 25-5343)

- **Vote Lineup (9–0 in the judgment; 7–2 on the controlling rationale)**:
  - **Majority Opinion**: Chief Justice Roberts, joined by Justices Alito, Sotomayor, Kagan, Kavanaugh, Barrett, and Jackson (7 Justices).

## 2. Majority Ground (`majority-ground`)
The Court reaches the single question on which it granted certiorari—whether *Stinson v. United States*, 508 U.S. 36 (1993), still correctly states the rule for the deference that courts must give the commentary to the Sentencing Guidelines—and holds that ***Stinson* incorporated the administrative-law deference framework of *Bowles v. Seminole Rock & Sand Co.*, 325 U.S. 410 (1945), which is therefore governed and cabined by *Kisor v. Wilkie*, 588 U.S. 558 (2019)**.

Specifically, the majority reasons as follows:
1. ***Stinson* Borrowed *Seminole Rock*, Not a Standalone Rule**: In *Stinson*, 508 U.S. at 44–45, the Court rejected analogies between Guidelines commentary and legislative committee reports or *Chevron* statutory constructions, and instead held that "the guidelines are the equivalent of legislative rules adopted by federal agencies" and that commentary interpreting them "is akin to an agency's interpretation of its own legislative rules." When *Stinson* stated that commentary must be given controlling weight unless it is "plainly erroneous or inconsistent with" the Guideline, id. at 38, 45 (quoting *Seminole Rock*, 325 U.S. at 414), it invoked the classic shorthand for *Seminole Rock* deference, while recognizing that commentary is a permissible vehicle for revisions only "if the guideline which the commentary interprets will bear the construction," id. at 46.
2. ***Kisor*'s Limits Apply to Guidelines Commentary**: In *Kisor*, 588 U.S. at 574–579, the Court explained that reading *Seminole Rock*'s "plainly erroneous or inconsistent" phrasing in isolation produces a "caricature of the doctrine, in which deference is 'reflexive.'" The Court therefore reinforced the preconditions inherent in *Seminole Rock*: (a) a court may not defer unless the regulation remains "genuinely ambiguous" after the court has exhausted all "traditional tools" of construction (text, structure, history, and purpose); (b) the agency's reading must fall within the zone of reasonable constructions; and (c) the character and context of the interpretation must entitle it to controlling weight as the agency's authoritative, expert, and considered judgment. Because *Stinson* adopted *Seminole Rock* deference, *Kisor*'s clarification of *Seminole Rock* applies directly to Sentencing Guidelines commentary. The Fifth Circuit's contrary holding in *United States v. Vargas*, 74 F.4th 673 (5th Cir. 2023) (en banc)—that *Stinson* requires deference even when a Guideline is unambiguous—is rejected.

---

## 3. Ground Breadth (`ground-breadth`)
The Court announces a **categorical doctrinal rule governing all interpretive Sentencing Guidelines commentary nationwide**—holding that *Stinson* is cabined by *Kisor*'s genuine-ambiguity and character-and-context limits across the board—**while narrowing its disposition to avoid case-specific application in the first instance**.

Because the Court is "a court of review, not of first view" (*Cutter v. Wilkinson*, 544 U.S. 709, 718 n.7 (2005)), and the grant of certiorari was limited to the threshold deference standard, the majority declines to decide in the first instance: (1) whether the phrase "semiautomatic firearm that is capable of accepting a large capacity magazine" in U.S.S.G. § 2K2.1(a)(3) is genuinely ambiguous; (2) whether Application Note 2's 15-round threshold is entitled to deference under *Kisor* or represents the best reading; or (3) whether any Guidelines calculation error was harmless under Fed. R. Crim. P. 52(a) given the district court's statement that it would have imposed the same 72-month sentence regardless of the Guidelines range. Those case-specific questions are left to the Fifth Circuit on remand.

## 4. Separate Writings and Fault Lines
- **Concurrence in the Judgment (Gorsuch, J., joined by Thomas, J.)**: Argues that under the Sentencing Reform Act's text and structure (28 U.S.C. § 994(a), (p), (x); 18 U.S.C. § 3553(a)(4)), *Loper Bright Enterprises v. Raimondo*, 603 U.S. 369 (2024), *Rutherford v. United States*, 146 S. Ct. 1320 (2026), separation of powers, and the rule of lenity, courts owe no binding deference whatsoever to unreviewed Commission commentary that expands criminal punishment.
- **Concurrence (Kagan, J.)**: Emphasizes that when a Sentencing Guideline is genuinely ambiguous—such as where a qualitative phrase requires a line-drawing judgment—official commentary adopted by majority vote of the Sentencing Commission after notice-and-comment and congressional submission satisfies all three *Kisor* character-and-context markers and warrants controlling weight.
