# Predicted reasoning — Beaird v. United States (No. 25-5343), briefed moment

**Judgment class: vacated** (and remanded). The Court will vacate the Fifth
Circuit's judgment affirming the 72-month sentence and remand for that court to
interpret U.S.S.G. § 2K2.1(a)(3) ("large capacity magazine") under the correct
deference standard.

**Vote lineup on the judgment: 9–0 for vacatur is the modal outcome**, with the
rationale split across separate writings. Both parties ask for vacatur (the
United States filed a "brief supporting vacatur"), so the only adversarial
defense of the judgment below is the Court-appointed amicus defending Stinson as
written. A dissent from the judgment is possible but less likely than not — the
most plausible source is a Justice who would affirm on the ground that the
district court said it would impose the same sentence regardless of the range
(harmlessness), but the QP is limited to the deference rule and the Court's
usual practice is to leave that question for remand.

**Ground.** The Court reaches the single question it granted (whether Stinson
still correctly states the deference rule for guidelines commentary; QPs I and
III of the petition — § 922(g)(1) Second Amendment and the interstate-commerce
challenge — were not granted and are not reached). The likely majority holds
that Stinson's "plainly erroneous or inconsistent" formulation must be read
through Kisor v. Wilkie: commentary can receive deference only where the
guideline is genuinely ambiguous after the traditional tools are exhausted, the
reading is reasonable, and its character and context warrant controlling weight.
That rejects the Fifth Circuit's en banc Vargas position that Stinson survives
Kisor untouched — the position the appointed amicus defends.

**Separate writings.** Expect a concurrence (Gorsuch, likely joined by Thomas,
possibly others) arguing that after Loper Bright no deference to commentary is
owed in criminal sentencing and that lenity precedes any deference — petitioner's
lead argument. Whether lenity is a "traditional tool" in the Kisor exhaustion step
is a live fault line the majority may reserve.

**Breadth.** The rule announced is categorical across all guidelines commentary,
not confined to § 2K2.1; the application to a 17-round Glock magazine and any
harmless-error question are left to the Fifth Circuit on remand.

**Procedural exits.** A DIG is unlikely (the vehicle problem — the district
court's alternative-sentence statement — was visible at cert and the Court
granted after seven distributions and appointed an amicus). No recusal is
indicated, so an equally divided affirmance is not live.
