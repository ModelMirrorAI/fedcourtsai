## In brief

Amazon asks the Court to review a ruling that its California delivery drivers are exempt from arbitration agreements because some goods they deliver once crossed state lines.

## What happened

Delivery drivers who worked for Amazon Flex sued, claiming Amazon misclassified them as independent contractors rather than employees. Amazon sought to compel arbitration under the Federal Arbitration Act, but the drivers argued they fell under an exemption for transportation workers "engaged in interstate commerce." California trial and appellate courts agreed with the drivers, ruling that because many delivered goods originated outside California, the drivers qualified for the exemption, regardless of how long goods sat in local warehouses or whether they were locally stocked groceries. The California Supreme Court denied further review.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked whether a local delivery driver counts as engaged in interstate commerce, and therefore free from having to arbitrate disputes, simply because some delivered goods earlier crossed state lines, even when there was a lengthy break in the goods' journey or the goods were locally stocked items.

## Where it stands

The petition was distributed for conference, after which the Court requested a response from the drivers. The drivers have since received two extensions of time, with their response now due November 16, 2026. No further action has been recorded.

## What each outcome would mean

Amazon, which lost in the lower courts, is asking the Court to take up the case and reverse. If the Court grants the petition, it would hear full arguments on whether the drivers must arbitrate their claims rather than sue in court. If it denies the petition, the California Court of Appeal's ruling exempting the drivers from arbitration stands, and the drivers' lawsuit continues. Amazon separately asks that, even without full review, the Court send the case back for reconsideration in light of a more recent decision.
