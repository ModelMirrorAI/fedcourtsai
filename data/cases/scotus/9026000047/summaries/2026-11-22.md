## In brief

Amazon asks the Court to review a ruling that its local delivery drivers are exempt from arbitration agreements because some packages they deliver originated out of state.

## What happened

Amazon Flex drivers sued Amazon in California, claiming they were misclassified as independent contractors. Amazon sought to compel arbitration under the Federal Arbitration Act, but the drivers argued they fall under an exemption for transportation workers "engaged in interstate commerce." The trial court and the California Court of Appeal agreed with the drivers, reasoning that many packages they deliver originated outside California and remain in a continuous interstate journey until delivered. The California Supreme Court denied review. Amazon appealed to the U.S. Supreme Court, arguing the decision conflicts with a recent Supreme Court ruling on the same exemption.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked whether a local delivery driver counts as engaged in interstate commerce, and so is exempt from being forced into arbitration, merely because some packages he delivers once crossed state lines, even when the goods sat in a local warehouse for a time or were locally stocked items before delivery.

## Where it stands

The petition was distributed for a September 2026 conference; the Court then asked for a response, which the respondents filed in November 2026 after two extensions. The docket shows no further action since that filing.

## What each outcome would mean

Amazon, which lost in the lower court, is asking the Court to take up the case and reverse. If the Court grants review, it would hear full arguments and decide the legal question itself, deciding nothing yet about who is right. If it denies review, the California Court of Appeal's decision stands, leaving in place the ruling that these drivers are exempt from arbitration and may continue pursuing their claims in court rather than arbitration.
