## 2. Call for the Views of the Solicitor General (`cvsg-increment`: 0.01)

The Court will **not** call for the views of the Solicitor General (**1%**). The Court has already interpreted the Federal Arbitration Act's Section 1 transportation-worker exemption four times since 2019—most recently in *Flowers Foods, Inc. v. Brock*, 146 S. Ct. 1358 (May 28, 2026)—without needing the Solicitor General's views at the certiorari stage to assess whether a last-mile delivery dispute warrants review. No federal agency administers Section 1 of the FAA, and no distinct federal governmental interest is implicated by state-court PAGA arbitration litigation between private parties.

## 3. Disposition and Summary-Disposition Route (`disposition`: 0.06; `summary-disposition-route`: 0.58)

Once the requested brief in opposition is filed, the Court will **deny** the petition for a writ of certiorari (**94%** probability of denial; **6%** probability of any grant).

Conditional on the Court granting review of any kind, a **summary grant, vacatur, and remand (GVR)** in light of *Flowers Foods v. Brock*, 146 S. Ct. 1358 (2026), is slightly more likely (**58%** conditional share of grants, or ~3.5% unconditional) than setting the case for plenary briefing and oral argument (**42%** conditional share of grants, or ~2.5% unconditional):

- **Why a GVR is the primary (though still unlikely) grant pathway:** The California Court of Appeal issued its unpublished opinion on January 7, 2026—while *Brock* was pending—and explicitly defined the class of workers by borrowing the verbatim question presented from *Brock* (App. 4a n.4, 8a–9a & n.10). Amazon's petition expressly asks in the alternative for a GVR in light of *Brock* (Pet. 25–26). However, because *Brock* unanimously *affirmed* the Tenth Circuit's ruling that intrastate delivery drivers are exempt under Section 1 and cited *Rittmann v. Amazon.com, Inc.*, 971 F.3d 904, 916 (9th Cir. 2020), neutrally, the Court will most likely conclude that *Brock* provides no basis to disturb a judgment that reached the same pro-exemption result.
- **Question Presented if Plenary Review Were Granted:** If the Court were instead to grant plenary review, it would take the single question presented in the petition—whether local delivery drivers are engaged in interstate commerce under 9 U.S.C. § 1 when they deliver goods from in-state warehouses or local retail stores that previously crossed state lines without a prior customer order. If reached on the merits, the Court would likely hold that while purely local retail grocery deliveries do not qualify, Amazon Flex drivers who regularly perform last-mile deliveries of interstate customer orders from Amazon fulfillment centers remain exempt under *Southwest Airlines Co. v. Saxon*, 596 U.S. 450 (2022), and *Brock*.

## 4. Separate Writings on Denial (`dissent-from-denial`: 0.02)

Should the Court deny certiorari, expect **no dissent from or statement respecting the denial** (**2%** probability of any separate writing). All nine Justices joined the Court's opinion in *Flowers Foods v. Brock* (as well as *Saxon* and *Bissonnette v. LePage Bakeries Park St., LLC*, 601 U.S. 246 (2024)) without any separate concurrence or dissent, indicating consensus on the current case-by-case approach to FAA Section 1.
