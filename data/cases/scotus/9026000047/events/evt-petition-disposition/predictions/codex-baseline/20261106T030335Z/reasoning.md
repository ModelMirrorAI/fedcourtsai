## Legal and vehicle assessment

The petition's principal theory is that the California court treated prior interstate movement of some packages as enough to exempt Amazon Flex drivers from the Federal Arbitration Act, notwithstanding warehouse storage before orders and a predominance of grocery deliveries. The petition, pages 12–25, identifies two disputed boundaries: whether storage interrupts an interstate journey, and how a mixed local-grocery/package delivery class should be classified. Its alternative request, pages 25–26, seeks reconsideration in light of Flowers Foods, Inc. v. Brock.

I separately verified Brock through CourtListener opinion 11332974, decided May 28, 2026. It unanimously rejected a requirement that an exempt worker cross a state border or interact with vehicles that do. Its final discussion expressly left other factual limits undecided, mentioning business-entity contracts, passage of title, intended destinations, and local take-out delivery. It affirmed the worker's exemption rather than adopting Amazon's proposed warehouse-duration rule. See Brock, slip opinion pages 7–8. This supports a live unresolved boundary, not an already controlling victory for Amazon.

The appendix materially tempers the petition's characterization. At pages 12a–13a, the court below expressly reasoned that the warehouse stop remained part of the product's journey to its ultimate recipient and that the drivers' actual work completed that journey. It considered, rather than entirely overlooked, Amazon's storage and mixed-goods arguments. Amazon may be right that the court treated those considerations too categorically, but calling the reasoning merely a rule about where goods were manufactured understates the opinion's continuity-of-transport analysis. Brock rejected a categorical exclusion and did not hold that every categorical inclusion is unlawful. This is the strongest reason not to make a GVR the modal disposition.

The timing nevertheless makes reconsideration plausible: the court of appeal decided on January 7, the California Supreme Court denied review on April 1, and Brock followed on May 28. The petition's cited earlier GVR in Domino's Pizza v. Carmona after Saxon is a useful analogy, as described in the petition and appendix, not an independently retrieved disposition of this case. A GVR could invite application of Brock without deciding whether Amazon ultimately wins.

Plenary review is less attractive. The competing cases involve different delivery systems, transaction timing, title, and worker classes; the asserted split may partly be factual rather than a clean post-Brock legal disagreement. The Court may prefer lower courts first to apply its new decision. The California Supreme Court denied requests to publish the opinion (appendix 1a), limiting its precedential footprint. This is also an arbitration appeal within ongoing state litigation; I cannot resolve every possible finality or vehicle objection without the opposition and do not assert a jurisdictional defect. Although the petition presents substantial economic stakes and experienced counsel, those considerations alone do not justify a much higher grant estimate.
