# Forecast of the Court's action

The Court will deny the petition, leaving the arbitration ruling in place without endorsing its reasoning. The new Brock decision does not clearly require a different result, and the Court will leave factual limits on the transportation-worker exemption to further lower-court development rather than immediately hear another plenary case.

The snapshot contains one distribution and no CVSG. I predict one additional conference distribution after the requested opposition and any reply, followed by disposition without another substantive relist. The probability of at least one additional distribution is 98%; that includes routine redistribution after the response request, not just a true relist following consideration. I do not predict a call for the Solicitor General's views; its probability is 1.5%.

If the Court grants any relief, a summary order is likelier than full briefing and argument: 75% conditional on a grant. The principal alternative to denial is a grant, vacatur, and remand for the California court to reconsider under Brock, without deciding that Amazon is entitled to arbitration. A summary reversal prescribing the answer is much less likely.

If the Court instead takes the case for plenary review, it will take the petition's single question focused on whether pre-order warehouse storage and mixed local-grocery deliveries interrupt or attenuate the interstate journey for purposes of 9 U.S.C. § 1. It will not reopen Brock's rejection of a border-crossing requirement or take the underlying employee-classification and wage claims.

I predict no noted dissent or separate statement with the denial. The probability of a dissent from denial is 3%, conditional on denial. If there is a written objection, Alito or Kavanaugh is the likeliest writer in my low-probability residual forecast; no particular Justice's vote is predicted as known.
