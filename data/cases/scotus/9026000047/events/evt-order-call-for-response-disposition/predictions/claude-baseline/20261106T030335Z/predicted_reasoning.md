# Forecast: Amazon Logistics, Inc. v. Clinton (No. 26-47)

**Docket state forecast from.** Paid petition, docketed July 2026. The respondents waived a response on Aug 10. It was distributed once, for the Sept 28, 2026 conference (Aug 12). The Court requested a response on Aug 18, due Sept 17. It has been distributed once and never relisted, and there is no CVSG.

**Disposition.** I expect a denial, but a grant-family result is a real minority outcome, at about 27%. The likeliest form of that is a GVR in light of *Flowers Foods, Inc. v. Brock*, 146 S. Ct. 1358 (2026). Plenary review is less likely, at roughly 6–7%.

- **GVR (~20%).** The California Court of Appeal ruled on Jan 7, 2026, before *Brock*. Amazon asks in the alternative for a GVR (petition Part III). It cites *Domino's Pizza v. Carmona*, a GVR in light of *Saxon* that went to the side that lost in *Saxon*. Against a GVR: *Brock* held for the worker. It rejected the rule that intrastate drivers are never exempt, so a lower-court result exempting last-mile drivers is consistent with it. That makes "reasonable probability the court below would decide differently" a weaker fit than usual. The court below also openly followed *Rittmann*/*Carmona*, and *Brock* did not repudiate them.
- **Plenary (~7%).** The Court has now decided four Section 1 cases in about four years. *Brock* expressly left open the "break in transit" and "locally stocked goods" questions this petition raises. But the vehicle is an unpublished, interlocutory California Court of Appeal decision on a factual record that predates *Brock*, so it is not a clean vehicle. The Court is more likely to let the lower courts apply *Brock* first.

**Relist / timing.** The petition will be distributed again once the response and reply are in, probably for an October or November 2026 conference. I expect at most one relist before a decision. If a GVR is coming, it may be held for one extra conference.

**CVSG.** Unlikely (~3%). No federal interest is directly implicated, and the Court resolved the governing standard itself in *Brock*.

**Question, if granted.** If the Court grants plenary review, it would take the QP as written: whether out-of-state origin alone, without regard to a break in transit or the local character of the goods, makes a local driver "engaged in interstate commerce." The most likely grant-family route is a GVR rather than an argued case (about 72% of the grant mass).

**Separate writing on denial.** I expect none (~5%). A short statement, possibly from Thomas or Gorsuch, on Section 1 methodology or the reach of the FAA in state courts is the tail risk.
