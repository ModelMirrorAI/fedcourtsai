## Disposition and Summary-Disposition Route (`disposition`: 0.06; `summary-disposition-route`: 0.55)

The Court will **deny** the petition for a writ of certiorari (`predicted_disposition: "denied"`). Although the call for a response indicates initial interest in examining the petition's request for plenary review or a GVR in light of *Flowers Foods, Inc. v. Brock*, respondents' brief in opposition will demonstrate that *Brock* ruled in favor of the worker and affirmed that intrastate last-mile delivery drivers who never cross state lines or interact with border-crossing vehicles qualify for the Section 1 exemption. Furthermore, the California Court of Appeal expressly framed the class around the question presented in *Brock* and followed settled Ninth Circuit precedent (*Rittmann v. Amazon.com, Inc.*, 971 F.3d 904 (9th Cir. 2020), and *Carmona v. Domino's Pizza, LLC*, 73 F.4th 1135 (9th Cir. 2023)) that *Brock* did not disturb.

Conditional on the Court granting review (`summary-disposition-route: 0.55`), a **grant, vacate, and remand (GVR)** in light of *Brock* is slightly likelier than plenary review. Because the California Court of Appeal issued its unpublished decision on January 7, 2026—four months before *Brock*—and declined to stay the appeal pending *Brock*, the easiest path if four or more Justices wish to press state and lower courts to conduct a more granular "continuous carriage" inquiry is a brief GVR order, just as the Court issued in *Domino's Pizza, LLC v. Carmona*, 143 S. Ct. 361 (2022), following *Southwest Airlines Co. v. Saxon*, 596 U.S. 450 (2022).

## Scope of Review if Granted on the Merits (Conditional)

If the Court were to grant plenary review rather than a GVR, it would take the single question presented as framed in the petition—whether local delivery drivers are engaged in interstate commerce under 9 U.S.C. § 1 solely because some delivered goods previously crossed state lines, notwithstanding an extended pause in an in-state fulfillment center before customer orders are placed or the predominance of local grocery deliveries. If decided on the merits, the Court would likely articulate a multi-factor "continuous journey" standard distinguishing goods shipped across state lines to fulfill pre-existing or inevitable customer demand from general retail inventory that comes to rest in local warehouses or grocery storefronts prior to consumer purchase.
