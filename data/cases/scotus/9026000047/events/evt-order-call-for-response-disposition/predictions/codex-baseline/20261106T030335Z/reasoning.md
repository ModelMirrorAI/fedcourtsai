## Why the alternative is substantial, but denial still leads

The presented question is whether prior interstate movement alone exempts local delivery drivers under FAA section 1 despite a break in transit and the local character of deliveries (`questions-presented.txt`). The petition emphasizes warehouse dwell times and grocery deliveries, and the declarations provide record material rather than just unsupported assertions (appendix 28a–38a). The appellate court rejected the asserted warehouse break and treated mixed intrastate deliveries as insufficient to defeat the class-wide exemption (12a–13a). Its judgment predates the intervening Supreme Court opinion. These features make reconsideration plausible even without a fresh merits case.

I checked the actual intervening opinion, **Flowers Foods, Inc. v. Brock**, No. 24-935, decided May 28, 2026 (CourtListener opinion 11332974), rather than relying solely on Amazon's account. It rejects a categorical requirement that workers cross borders or interact with border-crossing vehicles. It preserves the direct-and-necessary participation standard and leaves other proposed limitations unresolved; it does not hold that warehouse storage breaks interstate movement. Its pp. 7–8 leave the significance of particular facts open. Thus it supports an argument for reconsideration, but is not a clear reversal of the rule applied below. The California court already made a continuity finding, rather than expressly announcing that every product's historical interstate origin suffices (appendix 12a–13a).

I also checked **Lopez v. Cintas**, 47 F.4th 428, 432–433 (5th Cir. 2022), using CourtListener opinion 7805030. Its reasoning categorically excludes local drivers after goods reach a warehouse. My inference is that *Brock* weakens some of that reasoning, making the petition's claimed continuing split less clean than its presentation suggests. A disagreement about warehouse inventory and local retail deliveries may remain, but the supplied materials do not demonstrate a square post-*Brock* appellate split. This counsels against immediate plenary review.

The decision below is unpublished, and California's highest court denied both review and requests for publication (appendix 1a; petition p. 3). The case also concerns an appeal from denial of arbitration in ongoing PAGA litigation, with an additional contractual-scope defense not reached (appendix 6a n.8). I treat these as vehicle and payoff uncertainties, not as an established jurisdictional defect or independent ground sustaining the judgment. The petition invokes section 1257 and *Southland* (p. 4); I have no opposition establishing a contrary jurisdictional argument.

In short, 38% is a substantial, judgmental departure from the 5.15% band anchor because the record offers both Court attention and a specific intervening-decision remand route. Denial remains likeliest because the new precedent is compatible with the judgment, the factual distinctions are contestable, and the Court need not take a second section 1 case immediately. The largest uncertainty is whether the Court sees enough possible effect from *Brock* to order reconsideration without deciding that Amazon is right.
