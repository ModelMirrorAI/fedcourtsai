# Forecast of the Court's disposition

The Court will most likely deny the petition after receiving the requested response, without a separate writing. The August 18 call for a response will produce renewed circulation, but will not itself lead to plenary review. The snapshot contains one distribution, for a conference that had not yet occurred. Expect one or two additional distribution entries, primarily to place the completed papers before a conference, and no extended sequence of genuine post-conference relists. The probability of any additional distribution is 0.99; that does not measure genuine relisting after consideration.

The Court will not call for the Solicitor General's views; that possibility is assigned 0.015. Its next substantive choice will be between declining further review and returning the case to California for reconsideration under *Flowers Foods v. Brock*, rather than seeking the government's position.

If the Court grants relief, it will most likely grant the petition, vacate the judgment, and remand for consideration of *Brock*. A disposition in the cert order has probability 0.80 conditional on any grant. Such a remand will not establish that these drivers must arbitrate or that Amazon ultimately prevails. Summary reversal deciding that issue outright is much less likely.

If the Court instead sets the case for argument, the question will concern whether goods stored in local inventory before a customer orders them remain in a continuous interstate journey for section 1 purposes, together with the relevance of mixed grocery and package deliveries. It will not revisit a categorical requirement that the drivers themselves cross state lines. The Court will take the petition's continuity/local-character question, potentially narrowed to the warehouse-interruption issue.

On denial, expect neither a reasoned Court opinion nor a noted dissent or statement. The conditional probability of any such dissent or statement is 0.035; no individual writer is predicted as the likely outcome.
