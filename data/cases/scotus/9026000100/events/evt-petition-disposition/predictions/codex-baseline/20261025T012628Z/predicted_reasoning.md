# Forecast of the Court's action

The Court will most likely deny the petition without a separate writing. The leading obstacle is vehicle quality: this unpublished employment-discrimination decision presents disputed applications of evidentiary standards rather than a cleanly isolated conflict about the governing legal rule. The Court will leave reconsideration of McDonnell Douglas at summary judgment for another petition.

## Further distributions and timing

The snapshot records exactly one distribution, on July 29, 2026, for the September 28 conference. It then records an August 13 request for a response after respondent's waiver, an extension of the response deadline, and an opposition filed October 14. It records no subsequent distribution and no CVSG.

I predict at least one further distribution with probability 0.98. Most likely there will be one additional distribution, placing the completed briefing before a later conference, followed by denial in November or December 2026. This is primarily a prediction of redistribution after the requested opposition, not a claim that the Court substantively considered the petition at the September conference and then relisted it. An additional genuine consideration-stage relist remains possible, but is not the modal path. The mechanically declared increment counts either kind of additional distribution.

I predict no call for the Solicitor General's views; the probability of a new CVSG is 0.02. The Court can evaluate this dispute about discrimination proof and summary judgment on the parties' submissions without inviting another brief.

## If review is granted

The most likely question taken would be narrower and less fact-loaded than the petition's formulation: whether applying McDonnell Douglas at summary judgment may prevent consideration of the entire record under Rule 56 in a Title VII religious-discrimination case. A grant need not reopen the distinct hostile-environment or accommodation theories or decide whether the employer actually discriminated.

Conditional on any grant, I assign 0.35 to disposition in the cert order and 0.65 to a grant opening plenary review. A narrow summary correction of the treatment of circumstantial evidence is the plausible cert-order route. A routine GVR based solely on Ames is less persuasive because Ames already preceded the judgment below, and its holding did not abolish McDonnell Douglas. If the Court elects to reconsider the framework itself, briefing and argument are more likely than summary abandonment. Any merits holding is conditional on a grant, not a prediction that this denial will produce an opinion.

Conditional on denial, the probability of any noted dissent or statement respecting denial is 0.18. No separate writing is the leading forecast; the recognized debate about the framework nevertheless makes a writing a meaningful minority possibility. I do not forecast a complete cert vote lineup.
