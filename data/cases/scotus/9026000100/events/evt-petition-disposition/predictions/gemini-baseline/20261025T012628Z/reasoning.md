3. **Severe Factual and Vehicle Defects Exposed by the Brief in Opposition**:
   - The Petition portrays petitioner Shannon Cross as a bus operator with an unblemished record who was pressured to remove her hijab and "fired, on the spot" after a bicyclist collided with her bus on May 30, 2019, based on a reason allegedly contradicted by video evidence and a post-dated police report (Pet. i, 6–12).
   - The Brief in Opposition (filed by Robert M. Loeb of Orrick) and the lower courts' opinions refute that narrative with undisputed record facts that make this case an exceptionally poor vehicle:
     - **Immediate religious accommodation**: When Cross submitted a request to wear a hijab on February 12, 2019, CCCTA approved it in writing that same day, and the Director of Transportation emailed all supervisors the next day instructing them that the head covering was approved and reminding them of religious-dress accommodation obligations (App. 10a; BIO at 3). Furthermore, Cross never pleaded a failure-to-accommodate claim in her complaint (App. 20a n.2).
     - **Police fault determination and contemporaneous written admissions**: On the day of the accident (May 30, 2019), police responded to the scene, reviewed the onboard bus video at CCCTA's office that same day, and concluded Cross violated Cal. Veh. Code § 21760(b) (requiring three feet of clearance when passing a bicyclist) (App. 11a–12a, 16a; BIO at 4, 21). Cross herself watched the video on May 30, 2019 and wrote in her occurrence report that she "clipped" the cyclist with the rear of the bus (BIO at 4), and later emailed Human Resources on June 13, 2019 stating she was "at complete fault" for the accident (App. 6a; BIO at 6).
     - **Thirty additional traffic violations and cell-phone use on video**: On May 30, 2019, Cross was not terminated on the spot, but placed on paid administrative leave with a Notice of Intent to Terminate pending a *Skelly* hearing on June 7, 2019 (App. 12a; BIO at 4–5). At that hearing, Cross claimed an alarm distracted her, and her union asked CCCTA to review additional video footage to verify the alarm. That video review revealed no alarm during the incident, and instead captured Cross running **30 stop signs and red lights**, failing to stop at BART crosswalks, and **using her cell phone while operating a public bus with passengers aboard** (App. 12a, 22a; BIO at 5, 22). Only after those findings did CCCTA issue the final Notice of Termination on June 13, 2019, which was upheld through two independent grievance steps before the union declined arbitration (App. 22a–23a).
     - **Comparator mismatches**: Cross's primary proposed comparator (Ronnette Brown) had over 25 years of tenure (compared to Cross's 10 months) and was found *not* at fault by police in her bicycle collision; the other proposed comparators did not strike pedestrians or cyclists, and at least one comparator who received a last-chance agreement was himself a practicing Muslim (App. 18a–19a; BIO at 6, 22–23).
   - Because CCCTA's decisionmakers indisputably had an overwhelming, contemporaneously documented safety basis to terminate Cross, Cross's claim fails under basic Rule 56 principles regardless of whether *McDonnell Douglas* is applied.
