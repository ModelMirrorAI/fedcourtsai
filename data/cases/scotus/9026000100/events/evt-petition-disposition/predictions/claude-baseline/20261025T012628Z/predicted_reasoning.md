# Predicted course — Cross v. Central Contra Costa Transit Authority (No. 26-100)

**Disposition: cert denied** (P(any grant) ≈ 4%).

**Where the docket stands.** One distribution so far (Jul 29, 2026, for the
Sept. 28 conference), after which the Court requested a response (Aug 13). That
request took the petition off the conference; the brief in opposition was filed
Oct 14 after a 30-day extension, and four amicus briefs supporting the
petitioner are on file. No CVSG.

- **Further distribution:** expected. With the BIO in, the petition will be
  re-distributed once the reply window closes, for a November 2026 conference.
  Most likely outcome is a denial at that first post-BIO conference, with no
  further relist; a single relist (to check for a separate writing) is
  possible but not the expectation.
- **CVSG:** none expected. The respondent is a local transit agency, not a
  federal party, and the question is a general Title VII/Rule 56 question on
  which the Court does not need the government's views to deny.
- **Which question, if granted:** the Court would not take the fact-bound QP as
  written. A grant would reformulate it to the clean legal question — whether
  the *McDonnell Douglas* burden-shifting framework applies at summary judgment
  under Rule 56 — which the Court has so far declined to take up.
- **Summary disposition:** if the Court acted at all, a per curiam summary
  reversal correcting a Rule 56 misapplication (crediting the movant's version
  of disputed video evidence) is a real alternative to plenary review; no
  intervening decision makes a GVR likely.
- **Separate writing on denial:** most likely none. The likeliest source of
  one is Justice Thomas (possibly joined by Justice Gorsuch), restating his
  view that *McDonnell Douglas* should be reconsidered, but the vehicle
  problems make a full dissent here less likely than in a cleaner case.
