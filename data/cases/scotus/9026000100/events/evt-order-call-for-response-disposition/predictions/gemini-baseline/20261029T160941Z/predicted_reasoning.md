## 3. Question Presented and Conditional Merits Ground

- **Question Presented Taken (if granted):** If the Court were to grant plenary review, it would take the single Question Presented—or reformulate it more cleanly to ask whether the three-step burden-shifting framework of *McDonnell Douglas Corp. v. Green*, 411 U.S. 792 (1973), governs motions for summary judgment under Federal Rule of Civil Procedure 56 in Title VII disparate-treatment and retaliation actions—while leaving behind the petition's fact-intensive, unpreserved arguments under *EEOC v. Abercrombie & Fitch Stores, Inc.*, 575 U.S. 768 (2015).
- **Conditional Merits Ground (if granted):** If granted, the Court would likely hold that *McDonnell Douglas* is an evidentiary tool rather than a rigid prerequisite under Rule 56, and that a Title VII plaintiff may defeat summary judgment whenever the totality of direct and circumstantial evidence in the record, viewed in the light most favorable to the nonmovant, would permit a reasonable jury to find that a protected characteristic or protected activity was a motivating factor or but-for cause of the adverse employment action (harmonizing circuit practice with *Desert Palace, Inc. v. Costa*, 539 U.S. 90 (2003), *Ames v. Ohio Department of Youth Services*, 605 U.S. 303 (2025), and *Ortiz v. Werner Enterprises, Inc.*, 834 F.3d 760 (7th Cir. 2016)).
