# Forecast of the Court's action

The Court will deny the petition after obtaining the requested response. The
August 13 record shows one distribution, entered July 29 for the September 28
conference, followed by a response request due September 14. It shows no completed
conference consideration, no true relist, and no CVSG.

Expect at least one additional distribution after the response process, usually
one or two further distributions before disposition. This forecast concerns the
distribution-count increment, including redistribution after a response request;
it does not predict that the Court has already considered and relisted the case.
The central timing forecast is disposition by the end of 2026. A further true
relist is possible if the Court examines whether this is a suitable vehicle for
revisiting the employment-discrimination proof framework.

No invitation to the Solicitor General is expected. The petition challenges how
courts evaluate evidence under an established federal employment statute, rather
than presenting a federal-party, regulatory, or intergovernmental question for
which obtaining the government's views would be a natural next step.

If the Court takes the case, expect a narrowed question: whether applying
McDonnell Douglas at summary judgment may displace consideration of the full
record under Rule 56 in a Title VII disparate-treatment action. Do not expect
review of who actually caused the bus collision or a ruling that Cross has proved
discrimination. A grant would more likely produce briefing and argument on the
framework than a disposition in the cert order: the conditional summary-route
probability is 35%. Within that smaller branch, a narrow correction and remand
for reconsideration of the evidentiary standard is more plausible than a summary
decision establishing liability. No provisioned intervening decision makes a GVR
the default route.

If review is denied, expect no separate writing, with a 20% conditional chance of
a noted dissent or statement. Thomas is the likeliest author, given his expressed
interest in this framework; Gorsuch is a more likely joiner than an independent
author. The per-Justice writing probabilities concern authorship, not joining a
colleague's opinion or an undisclosed certiorari vote.
