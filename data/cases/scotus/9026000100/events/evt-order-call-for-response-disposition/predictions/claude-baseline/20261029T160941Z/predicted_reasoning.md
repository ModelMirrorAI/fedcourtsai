# Predicted disposition: Cross v. Central Contra Costa Transit Authority, No. 26-100

**Forecast: the Court denies certiorari (P(any grant) ≈ 0.07).**

**Docket state I am forecasting from.** The petition was filed on July 20, 2026. The respondent waived a response on July 24. The petition was distributed once, for the September 28, 2026 conference. On August 13 the Court requested a response, due September 14. One distribution, no relist yet, no CVSG.

- **Further distribution.** Almost certain. Once the response (and any reply) is in, the petition will be redistributed for a fall conference. I expect it to be decided at its first post-response conference, or after at most one relist. A run of relists is unlikely unless the Court is using it as a hold or vehicle candidate.
- **CVSG.** Will not happen. The respondent is a local transit agency, and the federal government's interest in the outcome of a private Title VII suit is not what the Court seeks the SG's views on.
- **Question the Court would take, if any.** A reformulated version of the QP: whether the *McDonnell Douglas* framework applies at summary judgment under Rule 56. The petition's fact-heavy framing (video evidence, comparators) is the part the Court would drop.
- **Route if granted.** Several petitions pending this Term raise the same *McDonnell Douglas*/Rule 56 issue. If the Court takes the question in another vehicle, this petition is more likely held and then GVR'd than granted for plenary review. So, conditional on any grant, a summary route (GVR) is slightly more likely than plenary review (~0.55).
- **Separate writing on denial.** A real possibility. Justice Thomas, joined by Justice Gorsuch, has written twice against the framework (*Ames* concurrence; *Hittle* dissent from denial). The call for a response is consistent with a Justice wanting a full record before writing. Most likely, though, the denial issues without comment (~0.8). If someone writes, it is most likely Thomas, possibly joined by Gorsuch.
