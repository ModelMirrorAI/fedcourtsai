## In brief

The Court declined to hear a former bus driver's bid to revive her religious discrimination lawsuit against her transit agency employer.

## What happened

Shannon Cross, a Muslim bus driver, was fired by Central Contra Costa Transit Authority after a collision with a bicyclist and alleged traffic violations. She sued under federal and California anti-discrimination law, claiming she was really fired because of her religion, pointing to supervisors who told her she could not wear her hijab and to other drivers who kept their jobs after collisions. The district court granted summary judgment to the employer, and the Ninth Circuit affirmed in an unpublished decision, finding she had not shown a similarly situated employee was treated better or that the employer's stated reasons were a cover for discrimination.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked whether a long-standing method courts use to sort evidence in discrimination cases is fundamentally at odds with the ordinary rule for deciding summary judgment motions, and whether that method should be reconsidered or abandoned because it lets judges resolve disputed facts in the employer's favor instead of leaving them for a jury.

## Where it stands

The petition was distributed for conference twice, most recently November 13, 2026, after full briefing including an opposition brief, a reply, and several amicus briefs. On November 16, 2026, the Court denied the petition.

## What each outcome would mean

Cross, the petitioner, asked the Court to take up her case and overturn the Ninth Circuit's ruling against her. The denial means the Ninth Circuit's decision stands: the summary judgment in favor of Central Contra Costa Transit Authority remains in place, and Cross's lawsuit remains dismissed. The denial does not mean the Court agreed with the lower court's reasoning or with the employer's position; it simply leaves that outcome undisturbed without deciding the broader question Cross raised about how discrimination cases should be evaluated at summary judgment.
