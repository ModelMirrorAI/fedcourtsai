## In brief

A former transit bus driver asks the Court to revisit how judges apply a longstanding discrimination-case framework at summary judgment.

## What happened

Shannon Cross, a Muslim bus driver, was fired by the Central Contra Costa Transit Authority after colliding with a bicyclist and, the Authority says, after repeated traffic violations found on video. Cross sued under federal and California law, alleging the real reason was religious discrimination and retaliation, pointing to supervisors who told her she could not wear her hijab and to other drivers who kept their jobs after collisions. The district court granted summary judgment to the Authority, applying the McDonnell Douglas framework, a method courts use to evaluate discrimination evidence. The Ninth Circuit affirmed in an unpublished decision.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked whether applying this evidence-evaluating framework at the summary-judgment stage improperly lets judges credit an employer's disputed explanation instead of leaving that factual dispute for a jury, as the ordinary summary-judgment rule otherwise requires.

## Where it stands

The petition has been distributed for conference twice, most recently for November 13, 2026, after full briefing including an opposition brief, several amicus briefs, and a reply. The docket shows no action yet on whether the Court will hear the case.

## What each outcome would mean

Cross, the petitioner, asks the Court to take up her case and reverse the Ninth Circuit's ruling for the Authority. If the Court grants review, it would hear full arguments and decide whether the framework can be used this way at summary judgment, without yet deciding who wins. If the Court denies review, the Ninth Circuit's decision affirming summary judgment for the Authority stands, and Cross's lawsuit remains dismissed.
