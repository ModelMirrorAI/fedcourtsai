## In brief

A former bus driver asks the Court to revisit how judges apply a longstanding discrimination-case framework at summary judgment.

## What happened

Shannon Cross, a Muslim bus driver, was fired by Central Contra Costa Transit Authority after colliding with a bicyclist and other reported driving violations. She sued, claiming the real reason was religious discrimination and retaliation, pointing to supervisors who told her she could not wear her hijab and to other drivers she says were treated more leniently. The transit agency responded that police and its own investigation found her at fault, and that her discipline matched her conduct. The district court granted summary judgment to the agency, and the Ninth Circuit affirmed in an unpublished decision, applying the traditional McDonnell Douglas burden-shifting method used in discrimination cases.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked whether judges may keep using this burden-shifting method to evaluate discrimination evidence when deciding summary judgment motions, or whether doing so improperly lets courts favor the employer's version of events over disputed evidence, conflicting with the usual rule that courts must view evidence in the light most favorable to the employee at that stage.

## Where it stands

The case has been distributed for conference twice, most recently for November 13, 2026, after full briefing including an opposition brief, a reply, and several amicus briefs. The docket does not yet show any order granting or denying the petition.

## What each outcome would mean

Cross is asking the Court to take up her case and overturn the Ninth Circuit's ruling. If the Court grants review, it will hear the case fully and decide whether the framework was properly applied; a grant alone would not decide who is right. If the Court denies review, the Ninth Circuit's decision affirming summary judgment for Central Contra Costa Transit Authority stands, and Cross's claims remain dismissed.
