## In brief

A former bus driver asks the Court to take up whether summary judgment rules were misapplied in rejecting her religious discrimination lawsuit against her employer.

## What happened

Shannon Cross, a Muslim bus driver for Central Contra Costa Transit Authority, was fired after colliding with a bicyclist. She sued under federal and California employment discrimination law, claiming she was really terminated because of her religious practice of wearing a hijab and because she complained about harassment, pointing to supervisors' comments, uneven discipline compared to other drivers, and disputed facts about the collision. The district court granted summary judgment to the employer, and the Ninth Circuit affirmed in an unpublished decision, applying the McDonnell Douglas framework that courts use to evaluate discrimination claims without direct evidence.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked whether the framework courts use to sort out discrimination claims at summary judgment — before trial, when a judge decides if enough evidence exists for a jury to hear the case — was applied in a way that improperly let the courts accept the employer's disputed version of events instead of letting a jury weigh the conflicting evidence.

## Where it stands

The petition was distributed for conference, then the Court asked the employer to respond. The employer has since filed a brief opposing review, arguing the lower court's decision does not conflict with other rulings and that this case is a poor candidate for reconsidering the framework. The petition is awaiting the Court's decision on whether to hear the case.

## What each outcome would mean

Cross, the petitioner, is asking the Court to take up her case and ultimately overturn the Ninth Circuit's ruling against her. If the Court grants review, it would hear full arguments on whether the McDonnell Douglas framework was misapplied, though granting review alone would not decide who wins. If the Court denies review, the Ninth Circuit's decision affirming summary judgment for the transit authority stands, and Cross's lawsuit remains dismissed.
