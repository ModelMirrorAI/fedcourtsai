## In brief

A former transit bus driver asks the Court to take up her religious discrimination case and reconsider how courts apply a key discrimination-law precedent at summary judgment.

## What happened

Shannon Cross, a Muslim bus driver, says supervisors pressured her to stop wearing her hijab even after her employer approved the accommodation. She was fired after her bus collided with a bicyclist, following a review that also cited other traffic violations. She sued the Central Contra Costa Transit Authority for religious discrimination, retaliation, and harassment. The district court granted summary judgment to the employer, applying the McDonnell Douglas framework, a longstanding method courts use to evaluate discrimination claims without direct evidence. The Ninth Circuit affirmed in an unpublished decision, and rehearing was denied.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked whether using this framework to evaluate discrimination evidence at the summary-judgment stage, before trial, improperly lets judges credit an employer's disputed account of events rather than letting a jury weigh the evidence, and whether the framework should be reconsidered in light of the Court's other rulings on proving workplace discrimination.

## Where it stands

The case was distributed for a conference in September 2026, after which the Court asked the transit authority to respond; the authority's brief opposing the petition was filed in October 2026, and several amicus briefs have been filed. The docket shows no decision yet on whether the Court will hear the case.

## What each outcome would mean

Cross, the petitioner, asked the Court to take the case and reconsider the framework. If the Court grants review, it would hear the case in full and decide the legal question; this would not by itself decide who wins. If the Court denies review, the Ninth Circuit's decision affirming summary judgment for the transit authority stands, ending Cross's lawsuit. A denial would not mean the Court agrees the lower court was correct, only that the decision remains in place.
