## Conditional Merits and Stay Standard Assessment

Under the standard governing stay applications (*Hollingsworth v. Perry*, 558 U.S. 183, 190 (2010); *Nken v. Holder*, 556 U.S. 418, 434 (2009)), an applicant must demonstrate (1) a reasonable probability that four Justices will vote to grant certiorari, (2) a fair prospect that a majority of the Court will vote to reverse the judgment below, and (3) a likelihood of irreparable harm absent a stay.

None of these requirements is met here. Below, in *In re: Paul Schultz*, No. 26-2406 (4th Cir. Oct. 26, 2026) (unpublished per curiam), a Fourth Circuit panel (Wilkinson, King, and Rushing, JJ.) denied Schultz's petition for a writ of mandamus seeking to disqualify U.S. District Judge Leonie M. Brinkema in a pending bankruptcy appeal (*Schultz v. McCarthy*, No. 1:26-cv-02340-LMB-IDD), vacate the denial of recusal, and transfer the appeal to a judge in another district, and denied his accompanying stay motions as moot. Judicial dissatisfaction with adverse rulings in bankruptcy proceedings does not establish disqualifying bias under 28 U.S.C. § 455 (*Liteky v. United States*, 510 U.S. 540, 555 (1994)), and mandamus is a drastic remedy reserved for extraordinary circumstances where the petitioner has an indisputable right to relief (*Cheney v. U.S. Dist. Ct.*, 542 U.S. 367, 380 (2004)). There is no circuit conflict, no plausible prospect of certiorari or reversal, and no cognizable irreparable harm from proceeding before the assigned district judge with normal appellate review available after final judgment. If the Court were ever to address the merits, it would summarily reject the recusal and reassignment challenge under *Liteky* and *Cheney*.
