## In brief
A bankruptcy litigant's emergency request to pause a federal district judge's ruling in his appeal was denied by the Chief Justice.

## What happened
Paul David Schultz, a debtor in a long-running bankruptcy dispute, sought to stop the district judge handling his appeal against trustee Kevin McCarthy from ruling on the merits. Schultz had asked that judge to recuse herself, alleging she wrongly reframed his disqualification argument; she declined, and the Fourth Circuit denied a related mandamus petition while his rehearing request remained pending there. Schultz then asked the Chief Justice, as the circuit's designated justice, to temporarily block the district judge from deciding the appeal while that rehearing process continued.

## What the Court is being asked
Schultz asked for a temporary order pausing the district judge from issuing a final ruling in his bankruptcy appeal, so that his pending request asking the appeals court to reconsider his recusal argument could be resolved first. He argued that without this pause, the judge could decide the case before his challenge to her continued involvement could be reviewed, making that review meaningless.

## Where it stands
The Chief Justice denied the stay application on November 10, 2026, eight days after it was filed.

## What each outcome would mean
Schultz, as the applicant, asked the Court to pause proceedings before McCarthy's case could be decided below. With the application denied, no pause is in effect: the district judge Schultz sought to have removed remains free to rule on the merits of his bankruptcy appeal, and his related rehearing request continues before the Fourth Circuit on its own schedule. Had the stay been granted, the district judge would have been temporarily blocked from issuing a merits ruling until the appeals court acted on Schultz's pending rehearing and recusal arguments.
