# Forecast of the Court's action

The Court will most likely deny certiorari after receiving the requested response. The probability of any grant, including a partial grant or summary disposition, is 22%.

The snapshot records **one distribution**, entered September 23 for the October 9, 2026 conference, followed by a September 24 response request with an October 26 deadline. I predict **one further distribution** after response briefing, most likely in November or December 2026 if the response deadline is not extended. I assign 96% to at least one further distribution. That forecast concerns the recorded distribution increment: it need not represent a substantive relist after an actual conference discussion. The modal path is denial at the next post-response consideration, without several additional relists.

I predict **no CVSG** (3% probability of a new call). The dispute as presented concerns a state restriction and federal judicial administration, rather than asking the Court to review a federal agency's current regulatory judgment.

If the Court grants plenary review, the likelier focus is **question two**, narrowed to whether allegations that the regulated CAD/CAM files communicate information suffice to invoke First Amendment coverage notwithstanding their functional capacity. A grant on that question would not itself decide whether New Jersey's restriction survives constitutional scrutiny. Question one's transfer/comity dispute is a plausible separate route to review, but I do not expect the Court to take both questions together.

Conditional on any grant, **plenary review is more likely than disposition in the cert order**: 12% for the summary route, 88% for ordinary briefing and argument. The presented conflicts require choosing a doctrinal rule or addressing the unusually complicated transfer history; the materials consulted identify no intervening controlling decision supplying a straightforward GVR instruction.

Conditional on denial, I assign **16%** to a noted dissent or statement respecting denial. The modal prediction is an unexplained denial without separate writing; I make no individual-Justice attribution. The expected denial would leave the lower-court judgment standing, not supply a Supreme Court endorsement of its First Amendment analysis.
