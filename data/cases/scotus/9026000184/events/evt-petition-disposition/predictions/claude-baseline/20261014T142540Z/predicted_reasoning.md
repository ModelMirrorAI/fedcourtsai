# Predicted course — Defense Distributed v. Davenport (No. 26-184)

**State forecast from.** The docket shows one distribution (for the 10/9/2026
conference, on the respondent's waiver) and, the next day, a **response
requested** from New Jersey, due October 26, 2026. No CVSG. The petition was not
acted on at the 10/9 conference because the response call pulled it.

**Relist.** Once New Jersey's brief in opposition is filed (or an extension
runs out), the petition will be **distributed again** — near-certain, the
ordinary path after a response call. Expect one further distribution, likely
for a November or early-December 2026 conference; a second relist after that
only if a Justice is studying it for grant or writing.

**CVSG.** None. The respondent is a State, the statute is a state criminal law,
and nothing in either question calls for the federal government's views.

**Disposition.** Most likely **denied** after the response. The First Amendment
question (whether communicative code is covered "without more", Junger/Corley vs.
the Third Circuit's indicia-of-expressiveness rule) is an acknowledged split in a
precedential opinion, which is what drew the response call; but it arrives at the
pleading stage, entangled with a fact-specific inter-circuit venue fight and a
Second Amendment standing holding, and New Jersey will argue the split is shallow
(the Sixth Circuit's Junger is a 2000 export-control case) and that the files'
functional character would doom the claim under any test.

**If granted.** The Court would take **QP 2** (First Amendment coverage of
computer code), likely as written or reformulated around CAD/3D-printing files;
QP 1's venue standoff is unlikely to be taken on its own, though it might ride
along as a threshold question. Plenary review, not a summary disposition —
there is no intervening decision to GVR on.

**Dissent from denial.** Possible but not expected; if one comes, most likely
from Justice Thomas (possibly joined by Justice Gorsuch or Justice Alito) on the
code-as-speech question.
