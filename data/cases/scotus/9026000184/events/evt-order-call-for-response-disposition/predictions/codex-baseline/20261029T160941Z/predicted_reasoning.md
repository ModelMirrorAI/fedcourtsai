# Forecast of the Court's action

The Court will most likely deny certiorari after the requested response and any reply. I assign a 20% probability to any grant, including a summary disposition, and expect an unsigned denial without a recorded dissent or statement.

The snapshot contains **one distribution**, on September 23, 2026, for the October 9 conference, followed by the September 24 response request setting an October 26 deadline. It contains no response, no second distribution, and no CVSG. I expect at least one additional distribution after the response process, probably one fresh distribution followed by denial at the first conference that fully considers that briefing. That administrative return to conference is not necessarily a substantive relist after conference consideration. One or two substantive relists are a meaningful alternative, particularly if the Court seriously considers the computer-code question. Disposition within roughly one to three months after response/reply briefing is complete is the central timing forecast; extensions or a hold could delay it.

A CVSG is unlikely: this is a state-law challenge involving private petitioners, and the principal questions concern constitutional coverage and federal judicial administration rather than a statutory scheme administered by the federal government. The earlier export-control litigation supplies some federal background but does not make an invitation the likely next step. P(a new CVSG) is 2.5%.

If review is granted, the stronger candidate is **question 2**, narrowed to whether allegations that computer files communicate technical information suffice to invoke First Amendment coverage, or whether the Third Circuit's additional expressive-use showing is required at the pleading stage. The Court could address that threshold without deciding whether New Jersey's restriction ultimately survives constitutional scrutiny. Question 1, concerning the legal effect of the transferor circuit's vacatur and return request, offers an alternative narrow grant but carries an unusually complicated severance and jurisdictional history. A grant of both questions is less likely than a focused grant.

Conditional on any grant, I place 12% on disposition in the cert order itself and 88% on plenary briefing and argument. No identified intervening Supreme Court ruling supplies a natural GVR instruction. The genuine disagreement over expressive code and the disputed force of the transfer ruling are more naturally addressed through briefing than a summary merits correction.

Conditional on denial, there is a 14% chance of a noted dissent or statement respecting denial. The central forecast remains no separate writing. Thomas or Alito would be the most plausible individual writers, with Gorsuch another possibility; the per-Justice probabilities in prediction.json are unconditional chances of authoring or co-authoring a writing on the disposing order, not predictions of who joins another Justice's writing. No cert vote lineup is forecast.
