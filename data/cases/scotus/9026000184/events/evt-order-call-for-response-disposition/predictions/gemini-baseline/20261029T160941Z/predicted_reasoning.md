# Predicted Court Reasoning and Procedural Trajectory: *Defense Distributed v. Davenport* (No. 26-184)

## 1. Further Distributions and Relist Trajectory (`relist-increment`: 0.98)

As of the September 25, 2026 snapshot, the petition has been distributed once (`distribution_count: 1`, on September 23, 2026, for the October 9, 2026 conference), and on September 24, 2026, the Court called for a response from the New Jersey Attorney General (due October 26, 2026, subject to any extension). Because the Call for a Response removes the petition from active disposition at the October 9 conference until Respondent's Brief in Opposition (and any reply brief) is filed, the petition will almost certainly (`0.98`) be distributed at least once more for a winter 2026/2027 conference once briefing is complete. After that post-response distribution, the Court will most likely deny the petition at its first post-BIO conference, or after at most one additional relist if a Justice circulates a separate statement or dissent from denial.

## 2. Call for the Views of the Solicitor General (`cvsg-increment`: 0.02)

The Court will not call for the views of the Solicitor General (`0.02`). Although Petitioners originally joined the U.S. Department of State alongside the New Jersey Attorney General in the Western District of Texas suit, the claims against the New Jersey Attorney General were severed in April 2021, and the judgment under review from the Third Circuit solely concerns (1) inter-district retransfer between the District of New Jersey and the Western District of Texas and (2) a First Amendment challenge to a New Jersey state criminal statute (`N.J. Stat. Ann. § 2C:39-9(l)(2)`). No federal statute, treaty, or federal agency action is at issue in the Third Circuit judgment, and the pleading defects below make an invitation to the Solicitor General unwarranted.

## 3. Disposition and Scope of Review If Granted (`disposition`: 0.09; `summary-disposition-route`: 0.12)

The Court will deny the petition for a writ of certiorari (`predicted_disposition: "denied"`, `P(granted) = 0.09`). Once the New Jersey Attorney General files her Brief in Opposition, the Court will be presented with two threshold vehicle defects that foreclose plenary review on either Question Presented:
- On **Question Presented 1 (intercircuit venue conflict)**, Petitioners failed to seek a stay of the Western District of Texas's April 19, 2021 sever-and-transfer order before the case was docketed in the District of New Jersey on April 20, 2021; Petitioners had already filed their own parallel lawsuit in the District of New Jersey in 2019 raising the same claims; Petitioners failed to oppose consolidation of the transferred action with their preexisting New Jersey lawsuit in June 2021; and Petitioners never sought a writ of mandamus from the Third Circuit after the District of New Jersey denied retransfer in 2022. Both the Fifth Circuit and the Third Circuit agree that physical docketing of the transfer in New Jersey divested the Fifth Circuit of jurisdiction prior to *Defense Distributed v. Bruck*, 30 F.4th 414 (5th Cir. 2022).
- On **Question Presented 2 (First Amendment coverage of computer code)**, the District of New Jersey dismissed Petitioners' First Amendment claim *without prejudice* under Rule 12(b)(6) and twice granted leave to file a Fourth Amended Complaint explaining which files fall within `N.J. Stat. Ann. § 2C:39-9(l)(2)` and how Petitioners' CAD and CAM files operate technically. Petitioners refused to amend and stood on a complaint that the Third Circuit held deficient under *Ashcroft v. Iqbal*, 556 U.S. 662 (2009), both for failing to allege how sharing non-programmable files violates the New Jersey statute and for failing to plead basic facts about the technical properties of their CAD/CAM files.

**Conditional scope if granted:** If the Court were nonetheless to grant review (`0.09`), it would most likely limit the grant to **Question Presented 1** (whether and when a transferee district court abuses its discretion or violates comity by refusing a return request issued after the transferor circuit vacates a transfer order on mandamus), leaving behind Question Presented 2 because the unamended complaint lacks an adequate factual record to adjudicate First Amendment coverage of 3D-printing computer code. If granted on Question Presented 1, the Court would likely affirm the Third Circuit or vacate only on narrow comity grounds, holding that while a transferor circuit's post-transfer mandamus order lacks binding jurisdictional force over an out-of-circuit transferee court, transferee courts must give substantial weight to a sister circuit's determination that the original severance and transfer was a clear abuse of discretion.

**Summary disposition route (`0.12` conditional on grant):** Conditional on a grant, plenary review is far more likely (`0.88`) than summary disposition (`0.12`). There is no pending Supreme Court merits case that would support a hold-and-GVR, and summary vacatur would occur only in the unlikely event the Court wished to remand for reconsideration of intercircuit comity without oral argument.

## 4. Separate Writings on Denial (`dissent-from-denial`: 0.08)

Conditional on denial of certiorari, there is a modest (`0.08`) probability that one or more Justices (most plausibly Justice Alito, Justice Thomas, or Justice Gorsuch) will author a dissent from the denial of certiorari or a statement respecting the denial of certiorari, either highlighting the unusual intercircuit tug-of-war between the Fifth and Third Circuits over venue or noting that the First Amendment status of computer code and digital firearm files remains an important open question for a future case with a properly developed factual record.
