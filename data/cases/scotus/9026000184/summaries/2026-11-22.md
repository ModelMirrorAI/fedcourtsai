## In brief

Defense Distributed asks the Court to review a venue dispute between two appeals courts and a ruling that its 3D-printed gun file designs are not protected speech.

## What happened

Defense Distributed, a Texas company, and the Second Amendment Foundation sued New Jersey's Attorney General over efforts to stop distribution of downloadable files that let people 3D-print firearm parts. After years of litigation, a federal judge in Texas transferred the claims against the New Jersey official to a New Jersey court. The Fifth Circuit appeals court later ruled that transfer unlawful and told the New Jersey court to send the case back. The New Jersey court refused, and the Third Circuit upheld that refusal. The Third Circuit also ruled that the computer files were not shown to be protected speech under the First Amendment, and affirmed dismissal of the lawsuit.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked to decide two things: first, whether a court that received a transferred case may keep it even after the appeals court overseeing the original court ruled the transfer improper and ordered the case returned; second, whether computer code that communicates information is automatically covered by free-speech protections, or whether a person must show additional signs that the code is expressive before those protections apply.

## Where it stands

The petition was filed and distributed for a conference, after which the Court asked New Jersey's Attorney General to respond. New Jersey has since been granted more time, now until November 25, 2026, to file that response. The Court has not yet acted on whether to hear the case.

## What each outcome would mean

Defense Distributed and the Second Amendment Foundation, as petitioners, are asking the Court to reverse the Third Circuit's rulings on both venue and free speech. If the Court denies review, the Third Circuit's decisions stand: the case stays resolved in New Jersey's favor, and the dismissal of the lawsuit remains in place. If the Court grants review, it would hear full arguments on one or both questions and could ultimately affirm those rulings, reverse them in Defense Distributed's favor, or send the case back without deciding the questions itself.
