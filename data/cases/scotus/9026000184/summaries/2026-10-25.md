## In brief

Defense Distributed asks the Court to decide whether its lawsuit over 3D-printed gun files belongs in Texas rather than New Jersey, and whether its computer files count as free speech.

## What happened

Defense Distributed and the Second Amendment Foundation have fought New Jersey's attorney general for years over computer files that let users 3D-print firearm parts. A Texas federal court transferred the claims against the New Jersey attorney general to New Jersey; the Fifth Circuit later ruled that transfer unlawful and told the Texas court to ask for the case back. New Jersey's courts refused to send it back, and the Third Circuit upheld that refusal. The Third Circuit also ruled that the group's computer files did not qualify for First Amendment protection as pleaded, and affirmed dismissal of the case.

## What the Court is being asked

The case raises two questions. First, whether a court holding a transferred case may keep it after the original court's court of appeals has ruled the transfer improper and asked for the case to be sent back. Second, whether free-speech protection automatically covers computer code that conveys information, or whether courts may first require additional proof that the code is expressive before any such protection applies.

## Where it stands

The petition was distributed for the Court's October 9, 2026 conference; the New Jersey attorney general waived the right to respond but was then asked to respond, and her deadline to do so was extended to November 25, 2026. The docket shows no action since that distribution.

## What each outcome would mean

Defense Distributed, joined by the Second Amendment Foundation, is asking the Court to take up the case and reverse the Third Circuit's rulings on venue and on First Amendment coverage. If the Court grants review, it would hear full arguments on one or both questions and decide them; a grant alone would not determine who wins. If the Court denies the petition, the Third Circuit's decisions keeping the case in New Jersey and rejecting First Amendment coverage for the files as pleaded would stand, leaving the New Jersey attorney general's position intact.
