## In brief

Defense Distributed asks the Court to hear its dispute over where its lawsuit against New Jersey's attorney general belongs and whether its gun files are protected speech.

## What happened

Defense Distributed and the Second Amendment Foundation sued New Jersey's attorney general, challenging enforcement actions and a state law against distributing digital files that can be used to 3D-print firearms. The case bounced between Texas and New Jersey courts over years of litigation. A federal appeals court in Texas (the Fifth Circuit) ruled the case had been transferred to New Jersey improperly and should return, but the New Jersey court refused, and a federal appeals court there (the Third Circuit) upheld that refusal. The Third Circuit also ruled that the complaint did not sufficiently show the files were protected speech, and affirmed dismissal of the lawsuit.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked to decide two things: first, whether a court that receives a transferred case may keep it even after the court that sent the case rules the transfer was improper and asks for it back; second, whether computer files that communicate information are automatically covered by free-speech protections, or whether a plaintiff must show additional signs that the files are expressive before those protections apply.

## Where it stands

The petition was distributed for the Court's October 9, 2026 conference, and the Court asked New Jersey to respond even though New Jersey had initially waived its right to do so. New Jersey has since been given until November 25, 2026, to file its response, so the petition remains pending.

## What each outcome would mean

Defense Distributed, the company that sued, is asking the Court to take up the case. If the Court grants review, it will hear full arguments and decide the venue dispute and the free-speech question itself, without deciding in advance who wins. If the Court denies review, the Third Circuit's decision stands: the case stays decided in New Jersey's favor, with the lawsuit dismissed and the file-distribution law in effect as to these plaintiffs.
