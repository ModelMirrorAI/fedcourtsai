## In brief

Property owners ask the Court to force a federal judge to keep hearing their flooding-related takings claims instead of sending the case to state court.

## What happened

Texas property owners sued the state, claiming a highway flood barrier took their land without compensation. After years of litigation, the Supreme Court in 2024 avoided deciding whether the Takings Clause itself lets property owners sue, because Texas law offered another route, and it sent the case back for further proceedings. The property owners added a state-law claim as instructed. But the district court, adopting a magistrate judge's recommendation, ruled it lacked federal jurisdiction and sent all four underlying lawsuits back to Texas state courts. The property owners say this defies the Supreme Court's mandate and now ask the Court directly to intervene, since the law normally bars appealing a remand order.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked whether the district court disregarded the Supreme Court's earlier instructions by sending the whole case to state court, instead of letting the property owners pursue their federal claims in federal court as they say the Court's prior decision required.

## Where it stands

This is a petition for a writ of mandamus, a request that the Supreme Court order a lower court to comply with its mandate. It was distributed for the Court's conference of September 28, 2026, and the docket shows no action since.

## What each outcome would mean

The property owners (Devillier and others) are asking the Court to act. If the Court grants the writ, it would order the district court to undo the remand and keep the case in federal court, letting the property owners pursue their federal takings claims there. If the Court denies the petition, the district court's order sending the four lawsuits back to Texas state courts stays in effect, and the case would proceed there instead.
