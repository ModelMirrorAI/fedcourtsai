## Why denial remains the main path

The strongest argument for intervention is concrete. Petitioners say that the Court sent them back to pursue federal Takings Clause claims through a Texas cause of action, and the district court instead sent them out of federal court altogether. They seek recall of the remand order, invoking mandate enforcement rather than ordinary appellate correction. Their petition also argues that section 1447(d) forecloses ordinary review, making an extraordinary writ their remaining remedy. These points come from the petition, question presented and pages 1, 5–8, and 20; I do not independently treat the asserted absence of alternatives as settled.

The substantial obstacle is that the prior opinion's operative language permits use of the Texas cause of action but does not expressly guarantee a federal forum. Its concluding discussion also emphasizes state responsibility for enforcing constitutional rights. Those passages, reproduced at appendix pages 36a–38a, make it possible to distinguish preserving a claim from preserving jurisdiction over it. The district court's adopted recommendation analyzes jurisdiction claim by claim, including embedded federal-question jurisdiction for the state-law takings cause of action, rather than expressly refusing to apply the Supreme Court's holding (appendix pages 10a–18a; adopting order at 3a). Its legal analysis can be contested without necessarily establishing the clear mandate breach needed for this requested intervention.

That is a forecast about the Court's likely willingness to use this procedural vehicle, not endorsement of the district court's reasoning. The record supports meaningful upside risk: the Justices could conclude that their mandate and the contemplated amendment cannot sensibly be reconciled with the lower court's jurisdictional disposition. I leave 7% grant probability for that possibility. The absence of an observed relist, CVSG, or response request provides no independent attention signal strong enough to make intervention the main prediction.
