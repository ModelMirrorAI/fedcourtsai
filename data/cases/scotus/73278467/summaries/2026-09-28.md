## In brief

A county sheriff and jail deputy ask the Court to reverse a ruling letting a transgender former detainee's constitutional claims over jail housing policy and a strip search proceed.

## What happened

Darlene Griffith, a transgender woman, was held as a pretrial detainee at the El Paso County Jail, which assigns housing based on genitalia. She was placed in a male unit, denied women's items, and strip-searched by a male deputy despite her objections. She sued under civil rights law, raising equal protection and search claims. Lower courts dismissed her complaint, but the Tenth Circuit reversed in part, letting her equal protection claim over housing and commissary policy and her search claims proceed, while also finding a deputy not entitled to qualified immunity on an abusive-search claim. The full appeals court denied rehearing.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked whether courts must apply a demanding standard of review to jail policies that classify detainees by sex, or instead a more deferential standard that generally applies to prison rules, and whether a rule limiting searches of transgender detainees by staff of a different gender identity conflicts with the Court's search precedents.

## Where it stands

The petition was distributed for conference twice, and the Solicitor General was then invited to file a brief giving the federal government's views; that brief has been submitted, but the docket shows no decision yet on whether the Court will hear the case.

## What each outcome would mean

The sheriff and deputy, as petitioners, are asking the Court to take up the case. If the Court grants review, it would hear full arguments and decide the legal questions, without yet deciding who wins. If it denies review, the Tenth Circuit's ruling stands, letting Griffith's claims proceed in the lower courts. The Court could also grant, vacate, and send the case back for reconsideration in light of another recent decision, without resolving the questions itself.
