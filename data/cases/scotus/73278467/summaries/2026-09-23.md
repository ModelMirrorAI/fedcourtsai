## What happened

Darlene Griffith, a transgender woman, was held as a pretrial detainee at the El Paso County, Colorado jail. She was housed in a male unit under county policy, denied certain items given to female detainees, and subjected to a strip search that included a male deputy. She sued the sheriff and the deputy. Lower courts dismissed her claims, but the Tenth Circuit revived several of them, ruling that the housing and commissary policies must meet a heightened standard because they classify by sex, and that the cross-sex search claim could also proceed. The sheriff and deputy now ask the Supreme Court to review that ruling. Griffith opposes review.

## What the Court is being asked

The case raises two issues. One is what standard courts should use when a transgender detainee challenges jail policies that separate people by sex: a demanding standard applied to sex-based rules generally, or a more relaxed standard that gives prison officials leeway to run their facilities as they see fit. The other is whether it can ever be reasonable, absent an emergency, for a search of a transgender detainee to be carried out by an officer of a different gender identity than the detainee's, or whether courts must always defer to jail officials' judgment on how to conduct such searches.

## Where it stands

The petition has been fully briefed, including an amicus brief supporting the sheriff and deputy, and was distributed for the Court's conference in December 2025 and again for a conference in June 2026. Rather than acting on the petition, the Court has invited the Solicitor General to file a brief giving the federal government's views.
