## In brief

A sheriff and jailer ask the Court to overturn a ruling that let a transgender woman's jail-treatment lawsuit go forward.

## What happened

Darlene Griffith, a transgender woman, was jailed as a pretrial detainee in El Paso County, Colorado. She says the jail housed her with men based on her genitalia, denied her female commissary items, and had a male deputy strip-search her over her objection, during which he made abusive comments. She sued jail officials, alleging violations of equal protection and of her Fourth and Fourteenth Amendment rights against unreasonable searches. A magistrate judge recommended dismissing her entire case, and the district court agreed, applying a deferential legal standard. The Tenth Circuit reversed in part, allowing her equal-protection and some search claims to proceed, while ruling for the jail officials on other claims.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked whether a stricter standard for judging sex-based rules, rather than a more deferential standard normally used for prison and jail policies, should apply to a transgender inmate's challenge to being housed and treated according to biological sex. It is also asked whether a rule letting jails use same-sex staff for strip searches conflicts with the Court's past approach of giving jail officials flexibility over how searches are conducted.

## Where it stands

The government's brief recommends that the Court grant the petition, but only to vacate the Tenth Circuit's decision and send the case back for reconsideration in light of two of the Court's more recent rulings, rather than deciding the questions itself. The case was then distributed for a further conference.

Roybal and the other jail officials are asking the Court to step in. If the Court denies the petition, the Tenth Circuit's ruling reviving part of Griffith's lawsuit stands, and her case continues in the lower courts. If the Court grants the petition and rules on the merits, it could either affirm the Tenth Circuit's decision, keeping Griffith's revived claims alive under the standard that court applied, or reverse, cutting back or eliminating those claims. If the Court instead grants, vacates, and remands, as the Solicitor General's brief recommends, it would set aside the Tenth Circuit's decision without finally deciding who is right, sending the case back for that court to reconsider in light of newer rulings.
