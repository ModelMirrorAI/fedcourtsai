## In brief

The Supreme Court vacated a Tenth Circuit ruling that had let a transgender former jail detainee's civil rights claims proceed, sending the case back for reconsideration under two newer rulings.

## What happened

Darlene Griffith, a transgender woman, was jailed as a pretrial detainee in El Paso County, Colorado. She says jail policy housed her with male detainees, denied her female commissary items, and had a male deputy strip-search her, including abusive comments during the search. She sued the sheriff and jail staff. A federal district court dismissed her claims. The Tenth Circuit reversed in part, holding she had plausibly alleged an equal protection violation and a claim over the cross-sex strip search, while rejecting other claims. The sheriff and the deputy asked the Supreme Court to review that ruling.

## What the Court is being asked

The case asks whether courts must apply a more skeptical level of review to jail policies that separate detainees by biological sex, or whether courts should instead defer heavily to jail officials' security judgments. It also asks whether having a male officer search a transgender female detainee can violate her constitutional rights absent an emergency.

## Where it stands

After the petition was fully briefed, the Court asked the Solicitor General for the federal government's views. The government recommended granting the case, setting aside the Tenth Circuit's decision, and returning it for reconsideration in light of two other Supreme Court rulings decided since the lower court ruled. The Court has now done exactly that: it granted the petition, vacated the Tenth Circuit's judgment, and sent the case back for further review in light of those rulings, without deciding the underlying questions itself.

## What each outcome would mean

The sheriff and the deputy, as petitioners, asked the Court to overturn the Tenth Circuit's decision reviving Griffith's claims. By vacating that decision and remanding, the Court has not ruled on who is right; it has instead directed the Tenth Circuit to reconsider the case applying the reasoning of the newer rulings. Griffith's claims are not dismissed, but neither are they confirmed to proceed. The Tenth Circuit must now decide, with that fresh guidance, whether her equal protection and search claims can still go forward or must be dismissed.
