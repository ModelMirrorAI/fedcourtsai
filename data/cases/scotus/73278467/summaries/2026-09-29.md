## In brief

A county sheriff asks the Court to review rulings that let a transgender detainee's claims about jail housing and a strip search go forward.

## What happened

Darlene Griffith, a transgender woman held as a pretrial detainee at the El Paso County Jail, sued the sheriff and a deputy, alleging the jail's policy of housing detainees by genitalia unconstitutionally placed her in a male unit and denied her women's items, and that a male deputy's strip search of her violated her rights. A magistrate judge recommended dismissing the whole case, and the district court agreed. The Tenth Circuit reversed in part, letting Griffith's claims about the housing policy, the cross-gender search policy, and the deputy's conduct during the search proceed, while granting the deputy immunity on one claim. The full appeals court denied rehearing.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked to decide what level of judicial scrutiny applies when a transgender inmate challenges a jail's sex-based housing and search policies as discriminatory: the more skeptical standard courts use for sex classifications generally, or the more deferential standard that lets prison rules stand if they reasonably serve security and management needs. It is also asked whether a rule against cross-gender strip searches conflicts with the Court's past decisions giving jail officials flexibility over search procedures.

## Where it stands

The petition was distributed for conference twice, and the Court then invited the Solicitor General to file a brief giving the federal government's views; that brief has since been filed. The docket shows no further action since.

## What each outcome would mean

The sheriff and deputy, as petitioners, are asking the Court to take up the case. If the Court grants review, it would hear full arguments and decide the scrutiny and search questions itself, without prejudging who wins. If it denies review, the Tenth Circuit's ruling stands, and Griffith's claims continue in the lower courts toward further proceedings, including discovery. Neither outcome would resolve the underlying facts of what happened to Griffith.
