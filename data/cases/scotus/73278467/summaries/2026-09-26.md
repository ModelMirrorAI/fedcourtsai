## What happened

Darlene Griffith, a transgender woman, was held as a pretrial detainee in the El Paso County Jail in Colorado. The jail's policy assigned housing based on genitalia, so Griffith was placed in a male unit and, during intake, was strip-searched partly by a male deputy despite her objections. Griffith sued the sheriff and the deputy, raising equal protection and search-related claims. A lower court dismissed her entire complaint, but the Tenth Circuit Court of Appeals reversed much of that decision, allowing her equal protection claim about housing and commissary policies, and claims about the strip search, to go forward, while also finding the deputy could be sued individually over how the search was conducted. The county and deputy, as petitioners, now ask the Supreme Court to review that ruling. Griffith opposes review.

## What the Court is being asked

The case raises two issues about how courts should judge jail policies that separate people by sex. First, when a transgender inmate challenges such a policy, should courts apply a demanding standard that treats sex-based rules as presumptively suspect, or a more deferential standard that asks only whether the policy reasonably serves the jail's institutional needs. Second, whether jail officials may ever have a legitimate security reason to have a search of a transgender inmate conducted partly by an officer of a different sex than the inmate identifies with, or whether such searches are essentially never justified absent an emergency.

## Where it stands

The petition was distributed for conference in December 2025 and again in June 2026; the docket does not record a grant or denial at either conference. Following the second conference, the Court invited the Solicitor General to file a brief expressing the federal government's views, and that brief was submitted in September 2026.
