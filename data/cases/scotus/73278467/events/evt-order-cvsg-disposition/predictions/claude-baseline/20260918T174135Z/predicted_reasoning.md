# Forecast: what the Court will do with No. 25-442, Roybal v. Griffith

**Moment.** This is the CVSG cell. The docket shows two distributions (Conference of 12/12/2025, then Conference of 6/29/2026) and the 6/30/2026 order inviting the Solicitor General's views. The six-month gap between the two distributions was a hold for *West Virginia v. B.P.J.* (No. 24-43, consolidated with *Little v. Hecox*), decided 6/30/2026 — the same day the Court called for the SG's views here rather than GVR'ing or denying the held petition.

## Disposition

**The petition will be granted (P ≈ 0.52 for any grant).** The most likely single outcome is a plenary grant limited to, or reformulated around, Question 1 — whether *Turner v. Safley*'s reasonableness standard or *United States v. Virginia*'s heightened scrutiny governs an equal-protection challenge to sex-based jail housing, commissary and search policies. Question 2 (the cross-identified-gender strip search under *Bell* and *Florence*) is fact-bound at the pleading stage, the deputy's alleged conduct makes it an unattractive vehicle, and the petition claims no split on it; expect it to be left behind or folded into a single reformulated question about the standard of review for prison sex classifications.

The Solicitor General will support the petitioners on the merits — the federal Bureau of Prisons houses by biological sex under current executive policy and is itself a defendant in equal-protection suits over that policy — and more likely than not will recommend that certiorari be granted (roughly 0.6). The main risk to a grant is the SG recommending denial on vehicle grounds: petitioners conceded intermediate scrutiny below, the panel resolved the *Turner*/*VMI* question on party-presentation grounds rather than the merits, and the case sits at Rule 12(b)(6) with no record. If the SG counsels denial, the Court will most likely deny.

## Route, if granted

**Plenary review, not a summary disposition (P(cert-order disposition | grant) ≈ 0.12).** A GVR "in light of *B.P.J.*" was available on 6/29 and the Court did not take it, because *B.P.J.* applied intermediate scrutiny to a sex classification and upheld it, which does not undercut a Tenth Circuit ruling that only applied intermediate scrutiny at the pleading stage. The residual summary route is a GVR after the SG recommends one, or in light of the Tenth Circuit's own decision on remand in *Fowler v. Stitt*.

## Relists and timing

**Further distribution: near-certain (P ≈ 0.97).** The SG's brief will arrive in roughly December 2026 (a late-June CVSG is usually answered in time for the January conferences), possibly slipping into spring 2027. The petition will then be distributed once, likely twice, before disposition — a first conference and one relist is the modal pattern for a CVSG'd petition the Court is inclined to grant. Disposition most likely between January and March 2027, and before the end of October Term 2026 either way. A second CVSG will not issue.

## If denied

**A dissent or statement respecting denial is possible but not the likelier case (P ≈ 0.35).** If denial follows an SG recommendation grounded in vehicle problems, the Court will most likely deny silently. If a Justice writes, expect a statement from Justice Alito or Justice Thomas noting that the *Turner*/*VMI* question remains open and that the Court should take it in a case where it was preserved.

## If granted: likely ground (conditional)

If the Court grants and reaches Question 1, the likelier holding is that *Turner*'s deferential standard governs equal-protection challenges to sex-based prison classifications, with *Johnson v. California* confined to race, and a reversal of the Tenth Circuit. A narrower alternative is a holding that heightened scrutiny applies but that penological interests are weighed within it, which would affirm in substance. Which of these prevails is not a docket-resolvable claim at this stage.
