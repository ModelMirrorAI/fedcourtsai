# Forecast — Roybal v. Griffith (No. 25-442), disposition after CVSG

**State forecast from:** the snapshot (2026-07-01) shows two distributions
(Conferences of 12/12/2025 and 6/29/2026) and a CVSG issued 6/30/2026, the day
after the second distribution and the same day the Court decided West Virginia
v. B.P.J. (No. 24-43). No SG brief exists yet.

## Relists and further distributions

The petition **will be distributed again** — at least once and probably two to
three more times. The CVSG suspends conference consideration until the
Solicitor General files; expect the SG's brief between November 2026 and
January 2027 (an end-of-term CVSG is typically answered by the winter so the
case can be resolved within the following Term), redistribution within two to
four weeks of filing, and a disposition between January and April 2027. The
only route to no further distribution is a settlement-driven dismissal or
withdrawal, which is unlikely (~3%).

## No further CVSG

A CVSG is already on the docket; no second invitation will issue.

## Disposition

A close call I put slightly on the deny side: **P(any grant) = 0.45**. The
Court has invested real attention — it held this petition six months for
B.P.J./Hecox and then chose a CVSG over a GVR or denial the day B.P.J. was
decided, which signals the prison-context scrutiny question survives B.P.J.
and interests the Court on the merits. But the vehicle is genuinely flawed:
petitioners conceded intermediate scrutiny in the Tenth Circuit, the panel
resolved QP1 on party-presentation grounds, and the case sits at the Rule
12(b)(6) stage with the merits open on remand. I expect the SG's
recommendation to be decisive. If the SG says grant (more likely than not,
given the federal government's direct interest in its own prison housing and
search policies), the Court grants and sets the case for argument in OT2027.
If the SG says deny on vehicle grounds — the classic move against a
preservation problem — the Court denies.

## Which question, if granted

**QP1 only**, likely reformulated rather than taken as written — the Court
would frame the question as whether Turner v. Safley's deferential standard or
heightened equal-protection scrutiny governs sex-based prison policies
(possibly folding in how B.P.J.'s framework applies in custody), dropping
QP1's circuit-inventory framing. QP2 (the cross-gender strip-search claim) is
factbound, interlocutory, and presents no claimed split; the Court would leave
it behind.

## Summary disposition

Unlikely conditional on a grant (~0.15). The Court passed on the free
GVR-in-light-of-B.P.J. moment on 6/30/2026, so it does not regard B.P.J. as
disposing of this case; the residual summary route is a post-SG-brief GVR if
the SG argues B.P.J.'s framework should be applied first below.

## If denied

Meaningful chance (~0.35) of a separate writing — most plausibly a dissent
from denial or a statement respecting denial by Justice Thomas or Justice
Alito (the Tenth Circuit's application of intermediate scrutiny in the prison
context would remain standing law in that circuit, and Judge Tymkovich's
en-banc dissent gives the writing a ready template). A silent denial is still
the modal outcome if the SG recommends denial on vehicle grounds, since the
interested Justices could acquiesce while awaiting a cleanly preserved
vehicle.
