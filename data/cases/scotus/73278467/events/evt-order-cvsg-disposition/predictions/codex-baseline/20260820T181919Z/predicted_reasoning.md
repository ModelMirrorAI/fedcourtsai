# Forecast

The petition will most likely be denied, although a grant remains a substantial possibility after the Court's call for the Solicitor General's views. The Court has already distributed the petition for two distinct conferences. After the Solicitor General files, the petition will almost certainly be distributed at least once more; one post-brief distribution is the likeliest path, with an additional relist possible if the Court is working through the relationship between this case and its recent transgender equal-protection decisions.

No further CVSG is expected because the Court has already invited the United States' views. The Solicitor General's recommendation will likely focus on the petition's vehicle defects: the decision below arose at the pleading stage, the petitioners conceded intermediate scrutiny in the court of appeals, the Tenth Circuit expressly left the ultimate constitutionality of the policies unresolved, and the abusive-search claim will continue regardless. Those defects make denial more likely than plenary review even though the asserted constitutional question is nationally important and implicates federal correctional policy.

If review is granted, the Court is most likely to take a narrowed version of the first question: whether Turner deference or heightened scrutiny governs a transgender detainee's equal-protection challenge to sex-based prison housing and commissary rules. The Court is unlikely to take the second question as broadly framed because the strip-search ruling rests on complaint-stage allegations that a female deputy was available and the male deputy acted abusively. Plenary review is more likely than a summary disposition conditional on a grant, but a GVR remains a meaningful route if intervening decisions supply a standard for the Tenth Circuit to apply.

If the petition is denied, no separate writing is the most likely outcome. A dissent from denial or statement respecting denial is possible from a Justice concerned with prison-administration deference, but it is not the central forecast.
