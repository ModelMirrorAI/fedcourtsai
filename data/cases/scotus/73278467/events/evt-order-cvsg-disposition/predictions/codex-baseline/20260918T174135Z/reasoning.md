## Case-specific adjustment to 0.43

The considerations favoring review are substantial. The questions directly juxtapose equal-protection scrutiny and prison-management deference, and concern practices extending beyond this county jail. The petition identifies differing circuit approaches, including Harrison and Veney (petition pp. 22–25). The provisioned docket shows a multistate amicus filing and, most importantly, the Court's affirmative decision to request federal input after two distributions. The federal government's operational interests in detention offer a plausible reason for that request, although the order itself states no reason. These features support more attention than an ordinary dispute over applying settled law.

The principal brake is **vehicle quality**, not a conclusion about the ultimate constitutional merits. The opposition says the petitioners failed to present the Turner-over-heightened-scrutiny theory below and affirmatively accepted intermediate scrutiny after Fowler; it grounds that contention in the panel's discussion of party presentation (BIO pp. 6–7, 10–12). The petition passages consulted do not resolve that objection. I treat this as a serious, specifically supported contention, not as an independently audited finding from the complete lower-court record. A prominent question is not necessarily squarely presented in this vehicle.

The opposition also distinguishes the asserted circuit conflict and notes that the decision merely revived plausible claims at the motion-to-dismiss stage. It did not finally invalidate the housing policies or establish a categorical ban covering every search. A separately surviving abusive-search claim would remain even if petitioners prevailed on their questions (BIO pp. 12–19, 21–23). These points reduce the urgency of taking this case, especially Question 2. Conversely, the Court requested federal views with these objections already in the filed opposition, so I do not discount the petition as if it had attracted no judicial interest.

The **alternative GVR route materially sustains the grant probability**. Petitioners emphasize the lower court's reliance on Fowler and the Supreme Court's vacatur of that decision in light of Skrmetti (petition pp. 5, 15, 30–31). The opposition agrees about that intervening history but argues that ordinary proceedings below can absorb its implications without Supreme Court intervention (BIO pp. 23–26). My external check confirms that Skrmetti's majority analyzed classifications based on age and medical use and did not decide whether transgender persons are a suspect or quasi-suspect class. See CourtListener opinion 11076608, majority Part II.A, excerpts returned for `medical use`. It therefore does not automatically decide the constitutionality of the expressly sex-based jail policies alleged here. That distinction favors reconsideration, if anything, over assuming an obvious summary merits reversal.

Taking these points together, I move moderately above the approximately 35% high-band/CVSG reference to **43% any grant**, while retaining denial as the modal outcome. A useful coherent decomposition is approximately 23.65% cert-order grant disposition, 19.35% plenary grant, 55% denial, and 2% dismissal or withdrawal. These are judgmental weights, not fitted frequencies. The withheld government recommendation and the unavailable reply are important uncertainty sources; neither is replaced by a guess about what counsel actually filed.

## Other forecasts and stakes

The **0.95 further-distribution** forecast rests on the expected return to conference after the invited government submission, not on treating a high terminal relist bucket as a forward hazard. The **0.0 CVSG increment** simply records no second invitation forecast when the first invitation is already observed; the harness masks this vacuous claim. The **0.55 summary-route probability is conditional on a grant**, not an unconditional 55% chance of a GVR. The **0.18 denial-writing probability is conditional on denial**, allowing for the salient issue and lower-court disagreement while expecting an ordinary unexplained denial most often. No cert vote lineup is inferred.

The **0.76 stakes score** reflects the potential national consequences for detainee safety, equal protection, searches, and prison administration if the Court reaches the questions. It is intentionally much higher than the grant probability. The factual and preservation constraints limit how broadly even a granted case might resolve those issues.
