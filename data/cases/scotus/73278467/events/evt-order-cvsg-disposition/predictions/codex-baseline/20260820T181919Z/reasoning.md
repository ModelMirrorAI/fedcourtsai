# Rationale

I assign a 0.44 probability to any grant and make denial the modal disposition. My approximate distribution is 0.54 denial, 0.02 dismissal or withdrawal, 0.31 plenary grant, and 0.13 grant-family summary disposition (principally a GVR). That decomposition supports the 0.30 conditional probability of a summary route given a grant.

The committed statpack is the quantitative anchor. The context freezes this paid OT2025 petition in the `high` band under the matching `sal-v3` version, with two distributions and a June 30, 2026 CVSG. Pooling the bracketed `reached` rates for the `high` band over the displayed strictly prior Terms, OT2017 through OT2024, gives 439 grant-family outcomes over a weighted 1,074 resolved petitions, or 40.9%. The broader paid scored-segment CVSG cut has a 34.9% grant-family rate (29.4% granted plus 5.5% GVR among 163 resolved petitions). I use the frozen-band rate as the scoring anchor and the CVSG cut as a cross-check. The terminal one-relist bucket's 12.7% grant-family rate is not a forward hazard and does not capture the later CVSG, so I do not treat it as this cell's baseline.

I adjust modestly above 40.9% because the Court took the affirmative and uncommon step of requesting the United States' views after the petition had sat from its December conference until a second June distribution. The questions concern nationwide prison administration, federal facilities, and an asserted disagreement over Turner deference versus heightened scrutiny. The petition also points to a divided panel and dissents from denial of rehearing.

The brief in opposition supplies strong downward adjustments. This is an interlocutory Rule 12(b)(6) case, the panel repeatedly disclaimed any decision on the policies' ultimate validity, and petitioners conceded intermediate scrutiny rather than preserving their principal Turner argument. The respondent also disputes the asserted circuit split and identifies unusually poor facts for the search question: the complaint alleges that a female deputy was available and that the male deputy conducted the search abusively. That separate abusive-search claim remains on remand regardless of the questions presented. These defects make this petition weaker than an otherwise comparable clean CVSG case.

I read the full provisioned petition, brief in opposition, questions presented, July 1 snapshot, document manifest, and frozen context. The snapshot is a truncated forward-mode snapshot with observable proceedings; all three provisioned documents contain extractable, untruncated text. CourtListener retrieval confirmed that *West Virginia v. B. P. J.* was decided on June 30, 2026, the same date as this CVSG, but the MCP document reader reported that no opinion text was available. I therefore used only the related decision's existence and timing to preserve a meaningful GVR possibility, not any unobserved holding. Searches found no later *Fowler v. Stitt* opinion and no *Little v. Hecox* Supreme Court opinion. The unavailable related-opinion text is the main reason to discount confidence in the plenary-versus-GVR split.

The 0.98 relist-increment probability reflects the ordinary need to redistribute a CVSG petition after the Solicitor General's brief, starting from the two distributions frozen in context. The 0.01 CVSG-increment number reflects that a new CVSG after prediction is essentially inapplicable because the invitation already occurred; the harness masks this claim at the CVSG moment. I assign 0.22 to a separate writing conditional on denial because the petition is salient and ideologically charged, while recognizing that even important denials usually produce no writing.
