# Predicted course of the petition — Roybal v. Griffith, No. 25-442

**Disposition: grant, vacate and remand (GVR) in light of *West Virginia v. B.P.J.* and *United States v. Skrmetti*.** The Solicitor General's invited brief asks for exactly that, and the Court should follow it at the next conference that takes up the case.

- **Distributions.** The docket shows two distributions so far (for the conferences of 12/12/2025 and 6/29/2026), then the CVSG on June 30, 2026 and the SG's brief on September 25, 2026. Expect the petition to be distributed again shortly after the brief, once the parties' supplemental briefs are in or the 14-day window passes, for a conference in late October or November 2026. A GVR could come off that first post-brief conference. If the Court has a respondent-side writing to deal with, a relist or two is possible.
- **CVSG.** Already on the docket, so no further call.
- **Question presented.** The Court would not take either QP as written. A GVR sends the whole judgment back for the Tenth Circuit to reconsider its heightened-scrutiny holding, which rested on its since-vacated *Fowler v. Stitt*, and its search and privacy holding in light of *B.P.J.*/*Skrmetti*. If the Court instead grants plenary review (the less likely grant route), expect QP 1 (Turner deference versus *Virginia* heightened scrutiny for sex-based prison classifications) to be granted, possibly reformulated along the lines of the SG's version ("policies that classify by biological sex and apply equally to inmates who self-identify with the opposite sex"), with QP 2 left aside.
- **Summary route.** A GVR is the likelier route than plenary review, because the SG itself says plenary review is "not warranted at this time": the split is thin and old, QP 2 has no split, and the posture is a motion to dismiss. The Court has followed this pattern repeatedly (*Kadel*, *Fowler*). A summary reversal is unlikely.
- **Separate writings.** On a GVR, a short statement or dissent from the GVR by Justice Sotomayor and/or Justice Jackson is plausible. If the Court instead denies outright, expect Justice Alito and/or Justice Thomas to dissent from or write respecting the denial, since a published circuit decision applying heightened scrutiny to sex-segregated jail housing would stand.
