# Forecast of the Court's action

The Court will grant the petition, vacate the Tenth Circuit's judgment, and remand for reconsideration in light of B.P.J. and Skrmetti. The order will most likely be a short GVR rather than a plenary grant or a summary reversal deciding the underlying prison-rights questions outright. Conditional on any grant, I assign 0.96 to a disposition accompanying the cert order rather than a new briefing-and-argument proceeding.

The snapshot shows **two distributions**, the last preceding the June 30 CVSG. I expect **one additional distribution** to consider the September 25 invited brief, with a second additional distribution possible if the Court wants more time or prepares explanatory language. The probability of at least one further distribution from this baseline is 0.97. A third or longer sequence is less likely than a prompt post-brief disposition. This is a scheduling forecast from the frozen record, not a claim that the petition has already been relisted again. The modal horizon is a fall 2026 orders list following consideration of the SG brief, rather than a hold through another merits Term.

The Court has already called for the Solicitor General's views, and the requested brief has arrived. No new CVSG is expected. The Court will likely use the government's proposed intervening-decision route without resolving the entire disagreement over whether Turner or heightened scrutiny governs sex-based prison policies. A GVR can allow the Tenth Circuit to reconsider the surviving claims and the effect of the authorities it relied on without a Supreme Court holding that every alleged search abuse was constitutional.

If the Court instead takes the case for plenary review, the more plausible focus is the first question: the applicable level of scrutiny for sex-based correctional policies challenged by a transgender detainee. It may narrow that question to the treatment of a detainee under otherwise sex-segregated policies, rather than decide transgender equal-protection claims categorically. The second question, concerning strip searches, is less likely to be taken independently because its alleged conflict is weaker and the conduct allegations complicate a categorical rule. Under the predicted GVR, neither question will be adopted for plenary resolution.

If review is denied, the modal forecast is a routine denial without separate writing; I assign 0.20 to at least one noted dissent or statement respecting denial, conditional on denial. I make no Justice-specific cert-vote prediction. The expected GVR itself will not establish who ultimately wins the remanded litigation.
