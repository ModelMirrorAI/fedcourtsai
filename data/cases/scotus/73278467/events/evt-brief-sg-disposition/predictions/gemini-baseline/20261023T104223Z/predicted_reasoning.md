# Predicted Reasoning: *Roybal v. Griffith* (No. 25-442)

## Predicted Disposition and Route

The Court will grant the petition for a writ of certiorari, vacate the judgment of the United States Court of Appeals for the Tenth Circuit, and remand the case for further consideration in light of *West Virginia v. B.P.J.*, 146 S. Ct. 2356 (2026), and *United States v. Skrmetti*, 605 U.S. 495 (2025). Rather than setting the case for plenary merits briefing and oral argument, the Court will dispose of the petition in the certiorari order itself through a summary grant-vacate-remand (GVR) order, following the recommendation of the Solicitor General.

## Docket Trajectory Before Disposition

Because the Solicitor General filed the invited amicus brief on September 25, 2026, and the two prior conference distributions on the docket occurred before the June 30, 2026 call for the views of the Solicitor General (CVSG), the petition will be distributed at least once more for an autumn conference following the supplemental-briefing window under the Court's rules. No further CVSG will issue from this point forward because the Court already invited the Solicitor General's views in June 2026 and the United States has now filed its brief.

## Basis for Summary Vacatur and Remand Rather Than Plenary Review

Under *Lawrence v. Chater*, 516 U.S. 163, 167 (1996) (per curiam), a GVR order is appropriate where intervening decisions of this Court create a reasonable probability that the court of appeals' judgment rests upon premises that the lower court would reject or must reconsider if given the opportunity. Both elements are present here:

1. **Abrogation of the Tenth Circuit's Core Authorities and Reasoning:** The Tenth Circuit panel majority issued its decision in February 2025—and the full circuit denied rehearing en banc on June 10, 2025—without the benefit of either *Skrmetti* (decided June 18, 2025) or *B.P.J.* (decided in June 2026). To hold that El Paso County Jail's sex-segregated housing and commissary policies classify on the basis of sex and trigger heightened scrutiny when applied to a biologically male pretrial detainee who identifies as female, the Tenth Circuit relied repeatedly on its circuit precedent in *Fowler v. Stitt*, 104 F.4th 770 (10th Cir. 2024), as well as the Ninth Circuit's decision in *Hecox v. Little*, 104 F.4th 1061 (9th Cir. 2024). This Court has already vacated *Fowler* in light of *Skrmetti*, see *Stitt v. Fowler*, 145 S. Ct. 2840 (2025), and reversed *Hecox* in *B.P.J.*, 146 S. Ct. at 2382.
2. **Application of *B.P.J.* and *Skrmetti* to As-Applied Challenges to Sex-Segregated Facilities and Searches:** In *B.P.J.*, the Court held that where separating males and females is constitutionally justified by enduring physical differences, safety, and privacy concerns, the Equal Protection Clause permits the government to treat all biological males the same and all biological females the same, without carving out individualized or subclass exemptions based on gender identity or cross-sex hormone therapy. 146 S. Ct. at 2377–2380 & n.4. In *Skrmetti*, the Court similarly rejected sex-stereotyping arguments and emphasized legislative and administrative discretion where biological sex differences matter. 605 U.S. at 512, 516, 524. Because Respondent does not dispute that correctional facilities may generally segregate housing by sex and conduct same-sex visual body-cavity intake searches, Respondent's as-applied Fourteenth Amendment Equal Protection claims and official-capacity Fourth and Fourteenth Amendment cross-gender strip-search claims rest on premises regarding gender-identity exemptions that *B.P.J.* and *Skrmetti* rejected.
3. **Vehicle Obstacles to Plenary Review of the Questions Presented:** Although Petitioners ask the Court to grant plenary review to decide whether *Turner v. Safley*, 482 U.S. 78 (1987), displaces heightened scrutiny under *United States v. Virginia*, 518 U.S. 515 (1996), for sex-based prison classifications (Question 1) and whether the Tenth Circuit's cross-gender strip-search holding conflicts with *Bell v. Wolfish*, 441 U.S. 520 (1979), and *Florence v. Board of Chosen Freeholders*, 566 U.S. 318 (2012) (Question 2), this case is an unsuitable vehicle for plenary review. The Tenth Circuit panel majority expressly declined to resolve the *Turner* versus *Virginia* question on party-presentation grounds because Petitioners did not argue *Turner* in the district court or in their appellate briefing and conceded intermediate scrutiny in a post-argument letter following *Fowler*. Moreover, only two circuits since *Johnson v. California*, 543 U.S. 499 (2005), have issued published decisions addressing whether *Turner* applies to sex-discrimination claims, and there is no circuit split on the second question presented. Because *B.P.J.* and *Skrmetti* provide sufficient doctrinal guidance for the Tenth Circuit to resolve Respondent's challenges to the County's policies on remand without reaching the broader *Turner* question, the Court will vacate and remand rather than calendar the case for oral argument.

## Contingent Scenarios: Plenary Grant or Denial of Certiorari

- **If Plenary Review Were Granted:** Should the Court instead grant plenary review, it would focus primarily on the first question presented—whether inmates' Equal Protection challenges to sex-based correctional policies are governed by the deferential reasonable-relationship standard of *Turner v. Safley* and *Washington v. Harper*, 494 U.S. 210 (1990), rather than intermediate scrutiny under *United States v. Virginia*—or take both questions together to reaffirm correctional deference under *Turner*, *Bell*, and *Florence*.
- **If Certiorari Were Denied:** Should the Court deny the petition on account of its interlocutory Rule 12(b)(6) posture, a noted dissent from the denial of certiorari (or statement respecting/dissenting from denial) would likely be filed by one or more Justices—most notably Justice Thomas, Justice Alito, or Justice Gorsuch—objecting that the Tenth Circuit's published decision leaves binding circuit precedent in place that conflicts with *Turner v. Safley*, *Florence*, *Skrmetti*, and *B.P.J.*
