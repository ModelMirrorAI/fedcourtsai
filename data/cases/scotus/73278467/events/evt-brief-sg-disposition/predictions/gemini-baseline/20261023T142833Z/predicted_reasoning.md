## Disposition and Summary-Disposition Route

The Court will **grant the petition, vacate the judgment of the Tenth Circuit, and remand (`gvr`)** for further consideration in light of *West Virginia v. B.P.J.*, 146 S. Ct. 2356 (2026), and *United States v. Skrmetti*, 605 U.S. 495 (2025).

1. **Intervening Precedent Directly Undercuts the Tenth Circuit's Rationale:** The Tenth Circuit panel majority issued its decision on February 19, 2025, and denied rehearing en banc on June 10, 2025—eight days before *Skrmetti* was decided and more than a year before *B.P.J.* was decided. To hold that the El Paso County Jail's sex-segregated housing and commissary policies were subject to intermediate scrutiny as applied to a transgender detainee, the Tenth Circuit panel relied heavily and repeatedly on its own precedent in *Fowler v. Stitt*, 104 F.4th 770 (10th Cir. 2024), and the Ninth Circuit's decision in *Hecox v. Little*, 104 F.4th 1061 (9th Cir. 2024). This Court subsequently GVR'd *Fowler* in light of *Skrmetti* (*Stitt v. Fowler*, 145 S. Ct. 2840 (2025)) and reversed *Hecox* in *B.P.J.* (146 S. Ct. at 2382).
2. **Alignment with the Solicitor General's Recommendation and *B.P.J.* Holding Pattern:** The Court held this petition from its December 12, 2025 conference until the end of OT2025 (June 29, 2026) while *West Virginia v. B.P.J.* and *Little v. Hecox* were pending, and then invited the Solicitor General's views the day *B.P.J.* was decided (June 30, 2026). The Solicitor General now squarely recommends a GVR in light of *B.P.J.* and *Skrmetti* and opposes plenary review. Because *B.P.J.* held that when a biological sex classification is constitutionally justified, the Equal Protection Clause does not require individualized exemptions or reclassification based on gender identity (146 S. Ct. at 2377–2380), there is a clear probability that *B.P.J.* and *Skrmetti* alter or foreclose the Tenth Circuit's analysis.
3. **Why Summary Disposition (`gvr`) Dominates Plenary Review (`summary-disposition-route`):** Conditional on a grant, a summary GVR order is far likelier than setting the case for plenary briefing and oral argument. As both the Solicitor General and Respondent point out, the Tenth Circuit majority declined on party-presentation grounds to decide the first question presented—whether *Turner v. Safley*, 482 U.S. 78 (1987), displaces heightened scrutiny under *United States v. Virginia*, 518 U.S. 515 (1996), for sex-based prison policies—and the case arrives in an interlocutory Rule 12(b)(6) posture where an unchallenged Fourth Amendment claim against Deputy Mustapick individually for abusive conduct during the strip search remains pending on remand. A GVR removes the Tenth Circuit's published precedent resting on *Fowler* and *Hecox* without requiring this Court to resolve the *Turner*-versus-*VMI* question in the first instance.
