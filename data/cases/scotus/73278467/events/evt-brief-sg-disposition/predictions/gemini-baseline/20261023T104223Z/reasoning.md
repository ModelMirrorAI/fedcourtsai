## 2. The Solicitor General's Recommendation and Case-Specific Analysis

This event (`evt-brief-sg-disposition`) opened on September 25, 2026, when the Solicitor General filed the invited brief of the United States (`record/documents/sg-invited-brief.txt`).

### What the Solicitor General Recommended
In the invited brief, the Solicitor General explicitly recommends that **the Court grant the petition for a writ of certiorari, vacate the judgment of the Tenth Circuit, and remand the case for further consideration (GVR) in light of *West Virginia v. B.P.J.*, 146 S. Ct. 2356 (2026), and *United States v. Skrmetti*, 605 U.S. 495 (2025)** (and suggests at page 15 that the Court may wish to accompany the GVR with a short statement noting that the Tenth Circuit committed the same errors corrected by those intervening decisions). At the same time, the Solicitor General recommends **against plenary review** of the two questions presented.

### How Far the Forecast Follows the Solicitor General's Recommendation
My forecast closely follows the Solicitor General's recommendation, moving from the unconditional `high`-band / CVSG base rate of **35.0% / 34.8%** up to **`probability = 0.75`** (`granted = 1`, `predicted_disposition = "gvr"`) and setting **`summary-disposition-route = 0.90`** (meaning a ~67.5% unconditional probability of a GVR/summary vacatur and a ~7.5% unconditional probability of a plenary grant, against a ~25.0% probability of denial).

Four record-grounded factors justify this substantial upward adjustment from the unconditional CVSG base rate:

1. **Conditioning on a Positive SG Recommendation (GVR):** The statpack's `34.8%` grant-family rate across `n=164` resolved CVSG petitions pools all CVSG briefs, the large majority of which recommend *denial* of certiorari. When the Solicitor General instead recommends granting certiorari or GVRing, the Court's historical follow rate is more than double the unconditional CVSG average.
2. **Hold-Then-CVSG Procedural History:** The docket (`2026-09-26.json`) shows that the petition was first distributed on November 25, 2025 for the December 12, 2025 Conference and was then held by the Court for over six months while *West Virginia v. B.P.J.* and *Little v. Hecox* were pending on the merits docket. Only after *B.P.J.* was decided in June 2026 was the petition redistributed on June 29, 2026 for the end-of-Term conference, producing the CVSG order on June 30, 2026. That procedural sequence confirms the Court was already holding *Roybal* for *B.P.J.* and sought the Solicitor General's views on how *B.P.J.* and *Skrmetti* interact with the prison-administration questions (*Turner v. Safley*) and interlocutory posture of this case.
3. **Direct Abrogation of the Tenth Circuit's Controlling Authorities:** As both Petitioners and the Solicitor General demonstrate, the Tenth Circuit's February 19, 2025 panel opinion (`129 F.4th 790`) relied over a dozen times on *Fowler v. Stitt*, 104 F.4th 770 (10th Cir. 2024), and also invoked *Hecox v. Little*, 104 F.4th 1061 (9th Cir. 2024), to conclude that applying sex-segregated housing and commissary policies to a transgender woman constitutes a sex classification subject to heightened scrutiny under *United States v. Virginia*, 518 U.S. 515 (1996). This Court subsequently GVR'd *Fowler* in light of *Skrmetti* (*Stitt v. Fowler*, 145 S. Ct. 2840 (2025)) and reversed *Hecox* in *B.P.J.* (`146 S. Ct. 2356`), holding that when a sex-based separation is constitutionally permissible based on biological differences, safety, and privacy, the Equal Protection Clause does not require individualized or subclass exemptions based on gender identity or hormone therapy (`146 S. Ct. at 2377–2380`). Under *Lawrence v. Chater*, 516 U.S. 163, 167 (1996), and *Lords Landing Village Condominium Council of Unit Owners v. Continental Insurance Co.*, 520 U.S. 893, 896 (1997), vacating and remanding when the authorities underpinning the lower court's opinion have been reversed or vacated by this Court is standard practice.
4. **Why GVR Dominates Plenary Grant (`summary-disposition-route = 0.90`):** While 23 States filed an amicus brief supporting Petitioners and four Tenth Circuit judges dissented from the denial of rehearing en banc (`139 F.4th 1183`), the Solicitor General persuasively highlights a major vehicle flaw against plenary review of Question 1: the Tenth Circuit panel explicitly declined to decide whether *Turner v. Safley*, 482 U.S. 78 (1987), displaces *VMI* intermediate scrutiny for sex-based prison policies because Petitioners never raised *Turner* below and conceded intermediate scrutiny in a Rule 28(j) letter after *Fowler*. Furthermore, only two circuits post-*Johnson v. California*, 543 U.S. 499 (2005), have published decisions on the *Turner* sex-classification issue (both applying intermediate scrutiny), and there is no circuit split on Question 2. Because *B.P.J.* and *Skrmetti* defeat Respondent's as-applied claims under either standard of scrutiny, the Court has strong institutional reasons to issue a GVR rather than grant plenary review on a forfeited question.

## 3. Steelmanning the Counter-Argument (Denial of Certiorari, ~25%)

The strongest argument for denying certiorari—pressed in Respondent's Brief in Opposition (`brief-in-opposition.txt`, pp. 22–26)—is that the case is in an interlocutory Rule 12(b)(6) motion-to-dismiss posture where:
- The Tenth Circuit expressly stated that it was not opining on whether the County's policies ultimately survive heightened scrutiny on a developed factual record;
- The Tenth Circuit is already reconsidering *Fowler v. Stitt* on remand from this Court's GVR in *Stitt v. Fowler*, 145 S. Ct. 2840 (2025), and both the district court and the Tenth Circuit can apply *Skrmetti* and *B.P.J.* in further proceedings; and
- Even if Respondent's official-capacity Equal Protection and cross-gender search claims are foreclosed, Respondent's individual-capacity Fourth Amendment claim against Deputy Mustapick for the allegedly abusive manner in which he conducted the strip search (explicit sexual threats and harassment) was not challenged on that independent ground in the petition and would still proceed on remand.

While those points give denial a non-trivial probability (~25%), they are unlikely to prevail over a GVR. As Petitioners' Reply Brief (`cert-reply.txt`, pp. 9–11) and the Solicitor General (`sg-invited-brief.txt`, pp. 15–16) explain, leaving `Griffith v. El Paso County`, 129 F.4th 790, on the books as binding Tenth Circuit precedent and law of the case would trap the district court on remand—and plaintiffs in *Fowler* on remand have already cited *Griffith* as surviving post-*Skrmetti* binding circuit precedent. Moreover, this Court regularly GVRs interlocutory appellate decisions reversing Rule 12(b)(6) dismissals (e.g., *Fazaga v. FBI*, 146 S. Ct. 2160 (2026); *Atchley v. AstraZeneca UK Ltd.*, 144 S. Ct. 2675 (2024)).
