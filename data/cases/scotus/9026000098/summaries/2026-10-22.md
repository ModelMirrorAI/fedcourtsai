## In brief

An election-integrity group asks the Court to decide whether federal law requires states to disclose their statewide voter registration lists on request.

## What happened

Public Interest Legal Foundation asked Hawaii's chief election officer, Scott Nago, for a copy of the state's full list of registered voters. Nago's office declined and told the group to seek county-level lists instead. The Foundation sued, arguing federal voter-list law required Hawaii to disclose the statewide list. A district court dismissed the case, finding it not yet ready for review. The Ninth Circuit Court of Appeals ruled the case was ready for review and that the Foundation had the right to sue, but still ruled against the Foundation on the merits, holding that a statewide voter list is not the kind of record the law requires states to disclose.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked whether a law requiring states to make public the records documenting their efforts to keep voter rolls accurate also requires disclosure of the voter list itself, or only records about the process used to maintain that list.

## Where it stands

The petition was distributed for the Court's conference of September 28, 2026. The Court then asked Hawaii to respond, and the response deadline has since been extended twice, most recently to November 25, 2026. No conference has yet acted on the petition itself.

## What each outcome would mean

The Foundation is asking the Court to take up the case and reverse the Ninth Circuit's ruling against it. If the Court grants review, it would hear full arguments and decide whether statewide voter lists must be disclosed under this law, deciding nothing yet about who is right. If the Court denies review, the Ninth Circuit's ruling stands, leaving Hawaii's refusal to turn over the statewide list in place and leaving other states within that appeals court's reach free to withhold similar lists.
