## In brief

A voter-integrity group asks the Court to decide whether federal law requires Hawaii to disclose its statewide voter registration list.

## What happened

Public Interest Legal Foundation asked Hawaii's state election office for a copy of the state's full list of registered voters, citing a federal transparency law. The state declined, telling the group to request records separately from each county. The Foundation sued, and a federal district court dismissed the case, finding the claim was not yet ready for review. The Ninth Circuit Court of Appeals disagreed on that point, ruling the Foundation could sue, but it still ruled against the Foundation on the merits, holding that a statewide voter list itself is not the kind of record the federal law requires states to disclose. Other appeals courts have reached the opposite conclusion on this question.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked whether a law requiring states to make public the records documenting how they maintain accurate voter rolls also requires states to disclose the master statewide list of registered voters itself, or whether that law covers only records about the maintenance process and not the list that process produces.

## Where it stands

The petition has been distributed for conference, and Hawaii's election officer was asked to respond after initially waiving that right. The response deadline has been extended twice, most recently to November 25, 2026, so the Court has not yet acted on whether to hear the case.

## What each outcome would mean

The Foundation is asking the Court to take up the case and reverse the Ninth Circuit's ruling. If the Court grants the petition, it will hear full arguments and decide whether statewide voter lists must be disclosed under this law, deciding the issue for everyone rather than ruling for either side at this stage. If the Court denies the petition, the Ninth Circuit's decision stands, meaning Hawaii would not be required to turn over its statewide list to the Foundation, and the rule allowing states within that circuit to decline such requests remains in effect.
