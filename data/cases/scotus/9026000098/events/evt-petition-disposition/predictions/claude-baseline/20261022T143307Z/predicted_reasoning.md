# Forecast — PILF v. Nago, No. 26-98 (evt-petition-disposition)

**State the docket shows.** Distributed once (for the 9/28/2026 conference),
after Hawaii waived its response. Three amicus briefs supporting the petition
were filed (Honest Elections Project, Center for Election Confidence — counsel
Paul Clement — and Judicial Watch). On Aug 26 the Court **requested a
response**, pulling the petition off the conference; the response is now due
Nov 25, 2026 after two extensions. No CVSG.

**Relist / redistribution.** Near-certain: once Hawaii files its brief in
opposition (late Nov), the petition will be redistributed, most likely for a
conference in early-to-mid January 2027. I expect it to be considered at that
conference and, if the Court is interested, possibly relisted once more before
any grant; a hold for a companion vehicle would also generate further
distributions.

**CVSG.** Unlikely. The federal government's position on the scope of
§ 20507(i)(1) is already being litigated by DOJ in its own voter-roll suits
(e.g. *United States v. Benson*, 6th Cir. 2026), so the Court learns little by
inviting the SG; the SG may instead file on its own.

**Disposition.** More likely denied than granted, but a substantial grant
probability. The split is genuine and acknowledged by the Ninth Circuit itself
(CA1 *Bellows* and CA10 *Torrez* vs. CA9 here, with the Sixth Circuit's
*Benson* affirmance leaning with the Ninth), and further circuits (CA4, CA7,
CA8) decided related PILF cases in 2026, so the question is mature. The main
paths to denial: Hawaii's BIO identifies a vehicle problem (state-law
confidentiality, the narrow request below, the ripeness history), or the Court
prefers a different vehicle — in which case this petition is held, then denied
or GVR'd depending on how the lead case comes out.

**If granted,** the Court takes the QP as written (whether a statewide voter
registration list is a record "concerning the implementation of" list
maintenance programs), possibly consolidated with or alongside another NVRA
disclosure petition. A summary route is plausible mainly via a hold-then-GVR
behind a lead vehicle; a summary reversal on a statutory split is unlikely.

**If denied,** I expect no separate writing most likely; a statement or
dissent (e.g. from Justices Thomas or Alito, noting the split) is a minority
possibility.
