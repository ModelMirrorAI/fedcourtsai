## Conditional Merits and Order-List Forecasts

4. **Questions Presented (Conditional on a Grant):**
   If the Court were to call for a response and ultimately grant certiorari, it would likely grant on **both Questions Presented** (or limit the grant to Question 1 on Article III informational standing if it views the Ninth Circuit's standing holding on Counts I and II as a threshold barrier), because resolving whether a statewide voter registration list falls within 52 U.S.C. § 20507(i)(1) (Question 2) in a private-plaintiff suit requires confirming that private watchdogs have Article III standing to seek such records or challenge state restrictions on their use. If granted on the merits, the Court would likely clarify how *TransUnion LLC v. Ramirez*, 594 U.S. 413 (2021), applies to statutory public-inspection regimes like the NVRA and resolve the split between the First, Fourth, Sixth, and Tenth Circuits (holding statewide voter lists covered by § 20507(i)(1)) and the Seventh and Ninth Circuits (holding voter lists are the "object" of list maintenance rather than records concerning its implementation).

5. **Summary Disposition Route (`summary-disposition-route`: 0.10 conditional on grant):**
   Conditional on the Court granting review, plenary review with full merits briefing and oral argument would be far more likely than a summary disposition (`0.10`). No intervening Supreme Court decision calls for a grant, vacate, and remand (GVR), and the deep multi-circuit split on both post-*TransUnion* informational standing and the text of 52 U.S.C. § 20507(i)(1) would not be resolved by a summary per curiam reversal.

6. **Separate Writings Respecting Denial (`dissent-from-denial`: 0.03 conditional on denial):**
   Conditional on the Court denying review, the denial will almost certainly issue without a noted dissent or statement respecting the denial (`0.03`). When the Court denied PILF's prior petitions raising the same Third Circuit standing rule (No. 25-379) and Sixth Circuit NVRA question (No. 25-437) on March 2, 2026, no Justice noted a dissent or issued a separate statement, and the waiver posture here makes a separate writing even less likely. If any Justice were to write separately, it would most plausibly be Justice Thomas or Justice Alito flagging the continuing lower-court confusion over *Akins*/*Public Citizen* informational injury after *TransUnion*.
