For context, the modern-cert disposition table has 662 grants and 588 GVRs over 44,686 estimated resolved petitions, about 2.8% for the grant family. These are terminal associations, not probabilities of a future relist or CVSG, and I do not treat them as such.

I raise P(any grant) to **0.42**, a substantial but judgmental adjustment rather than a fitted empirical estimate:

- **A genuine statutory conflict.** The petition argues that statewide voter lists are covered by 52 U.S.C. § 20507(i)(1). More importantly than the advocate's characterization, the appended Ninth Circuit opinion expressly rejects the First Circuit's reading in Bellows, 92 F.4th 36, 47–49 (2024), at appendix pages 35a–37a. My separate MCP lookup of Bellows, opinion 9929645, confirms that its treatment of Maine's voter file as the product of registration and maintenance activities supports disclosure. This is a concrete disagreement on federal law, not merely divergent results on different records.
- **Actual Court attention.** The Court requested a response after waiver. That is affirmative attention not represented by the coarse baseline band. Three supporting amici reinforce that the legal issue matters beyond this litigant; neither their count nor counsel identity independently establishes certworthiness.
- **A recurring, focused question.** The access question affects administration and scrutiny of statewide election records. It is a single statutory-interpretation question, and the Ninth Circuit ordered dismissal with prejudice on that ground. That makes it substantially more reviewable than a fact-bound records dispute.

Several considerations keep the grant probability below one-half. The Ninth Circuit distinguishes the Tenth Circuit's Torrez decision because the same limiting statutory language was not contested there; I therefore count the First/Ninth conflict as the strongest one, not three equally direct circuit holdings. The Fourth Circuit's Project Vote case concerns registration applications rather than an entire voter list. The government has not yet submitted its response, so the asserted vehicle quality is not adversarially tested. The lower court resolved standing and ripeness favorably to the petitioner, but the appended opinion devotes extensive attention to those issues; a respondent can still press threshold objections. Hawaii's county-level access regime and the distinction between disclosure of process records and the resulting database give the respondent nonfrivolous arguments. These concerns, and the Court's selective docket, justify denial remaining the modal disposition despite unusually strong signals for this band. I am not treating likely correctness on the underlying statute as equivalent to likely review.

## Other probabilities and stakes

**Further distribution: 0.97.** The response request and subsequent extensions make an additional distribution highly likely once briefing is complete. This is the declared mechanical claim: one more distribution entry beyond the one already recorded. It can be satisfied by a rescheduling before first effective consideration, not necessarily a true post-conference relist. I expect one such distribution, with one additional genuine relist a plausible minority path. This high number is not inferred from the terminal relist-count table.

**Future CVSG: 0.22.** Federal NVRA enforcement and the intersection of state records practices with a federal disclosure mandate make the Solicitor General's views potentially useful. On the other hand, there is no federal party, and the core question is a comparatively clean conflict the Court can address without that detour. No invitation exists in the supplied record.

**Summary route conditional on grant: 0.06.** The strongest case for review is an unresolved circuit conflict. No controlling intervening decision or mootness basis is identified in the inputs. Briefing and argument are much likelier than either a GVR or summary reversal if review is granted. This is conditional on grant, not a 6% unconditional chance of summary relief.

**Separate writing conditional on denial: 0.15.** The acknowledged split and election-records stakes create some chance of a statement or dissent. An unexplained denial without a separate writing remains substantially likelier; I do not infer individual cert votes.

**Stakes: 0.62.** Uniform federal access to statewide voter data has meaningful national transparency and privacy implications. The question nevertheless concerns access to records, not an immediate determination of election outcomes, voter qualifications, or a constitutional allocation of election authority. This score describes significance if decided, not the chance of a grant.

The chief uncertainty is the unfiled response: it may reveal a jurisdictional obstacle or sharpen the already substantial conflict. The forecast uses a well-developed petition and its appended judicial opinion, but not a complete adversarial cert record.
