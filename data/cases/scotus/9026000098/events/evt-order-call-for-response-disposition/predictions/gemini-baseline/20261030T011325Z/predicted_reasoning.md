## 2. Disposition and Route
- **Certiorari Disposition (`disposition`: 0.22, `predicted_disposition`: `denied`):** The Court is most likely to deny the petition once Respondent's Brief in Opposition is filed. Although the Ninth Circuit's opinion explicitly acknowledged creating a circuit split with the First Circuit's decision in *Public Interest Legal Foundation, Inc. v. Bellows*, 92 F.4th 36 (1st Cir. 2024), only those two circuits have squarely adjudicated whether a state's compiled statewide voter registration list itself satisfies the "concerning the implementation of programs and activities" clause of 52 U.S.C. § 20507(i)(1). Furthermore, the Third and Sixth Circuits have recently held that Petitioner lacks Article III informational standing under *TransUnion LLC v. Ramirez*, 594 U.S. 413 (2021), in similar NVRA § 20507(i) public-disclosure suits, creating a significant threshold jurisdictional complication that counsels in favor of further percolation rather than granting review in this vehicle.
- **Question Presented if Granted:** If the Court were to grant certiorari, it would take the single Question Presented in the petition as framed (whether a state's current statewide list of registered voters is a "record[] concerning the implementation of programs and activities conducted for the purpose of ensuring the accuracy and currency of official lists of eligible voters" under 52 U.S.C. § 20507(i)(1)), while also having to satisfy itself of Petitioner's Article III standing.
- **Summary Disposition Route (`summary-disposition-route`: 0.04):** Conditional on a grant, the Court would set the case for full merits briefing and oral argument rather than disposing of it summarily in the cert order (96% plenary vs. 4% summary). There is no intervening Supreme Court decision warranting a GVR, and a published appellate opinion resolving an acknowledged circuit split on a pure question of statutory interpretation is not a candidate for summary reversal.
- **Conditional Merits Outlook:** If granted, the Court would likely affirm the Ninth Circuit's textual distinction between the "official lists of eligible voters" (the object of maintenance, governed by HAVA § 21083(a)) and "records concerning the implementation of programs and activities conducted for the purpose of ensuring the accuracy and currency of" those lists under § 20507(i)(1), or alternatively vacate for lack of Article III informational standing under *TransUnion*.
