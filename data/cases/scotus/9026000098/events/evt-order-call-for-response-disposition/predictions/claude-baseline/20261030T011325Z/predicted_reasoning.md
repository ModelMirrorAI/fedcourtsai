# Forecast: PILF v. Nago (No. 26-98), disposition after the call for a response

**State on the record.** Distributed once (for the 28 September 2026 conference), after Hawaii waived a response. On 26 August the Court called for a response. No relist and no CVSG yet. Three amicus briefs support the petition. The live docket now shows the response deadline extended twice, to 25 November 2026.

**Disposition.** Most likely denied (P(grant) ≈ 0.30). If the response is filed on 25 November and a reply follows, I expect the petition to be distributed for a conference in early-to-mid January 2027. The issue is real: the Ninth Circuit acknowledged that it broke with the First Circuit (*Bellows*), and a Tenth Circuit decision (*Torrez*) sides with the First. Even so, most called-for-response petitions are still denied. Hawaii's response will very likely raise vehicle problems and argue that the split is shallow or still developing.

**Further distribution.** Near-certain. The petition goes back to conference once the response is in. I expect at least one relist before a final decision if the Court is seriously considering a grant.

**CVSG.** Unlikely (about 14%). The United States has an obvious interest, because DOJ is pursuing statewide voter lists in its own litigation (e.g., *United States v. Benson*, 6th Cir.). But that interest makes the federal position fairly predictable, and the Court may not need to ask for it. A CVSG would most likely come at the January conference, not before.

**Which question.** If the Court grants, it would take the single question as written: whether a statewide voter registration list is a "record concerning the implementation of programs and activities" for list accuracy under 52 U.S.C. § 20507(i)(1).

**Summary route.** If the petition is granted, plenary review is much more likely than a GVR. A GVR becomes plausible only if the Court grants a companion voter-roll case (such as a federal petition out of the Sixth Circuit's *Benson* line) and holds this one for it.

**Separate writing on denial.** Possible but not expected (about 18% conditional on denial). If anyone dissents from the denial of an acknowledged split on a federal election-integrity statute, the likeliest writers are Justice Alito or Justice Thomas, possibly joined by Justice Gorsuch.
