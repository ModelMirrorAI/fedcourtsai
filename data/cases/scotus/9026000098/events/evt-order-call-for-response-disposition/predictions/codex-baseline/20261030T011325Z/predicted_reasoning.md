# Forecast of the Court's action

The most likely disposition is **denial of certiorari**, leaving the lower-court result in place without a merits opinion. The probability of any grant, including an immediate grant-and-disposition order, is **42%**. The acknowledged disagreement over access to statewide voter lists makes review a substantial alternative, but the Court will more likely decline review than take it.

The starting record contains **one distribution and no CVSG**. The Court has requested a response after a waiver. The petition will almost certainly be distributed again once the response and reply cycle concludes: **99.5%** for at least one additional distribution. The modal path is one redistribution to place the fully briefed petition before a conference, followed by a denial; another one or two distributions are a meaningful possibility if the Court seriously considers review. That first redistribution need not represent a true relist after consideration by a conference.

The Court will probably not seek the Solicitor General's views: **18%** probability of a new CVSG. If it does, an invitation is most likely after the response is considered, and final resolution will take substantially longer. Absent an invitation or a hold, expect disposition within roughly one to three months of completed cert briefing. These are prospective intervals from the record's procedural state, not assertions that the September response or any later conference action actually occurred.

If the Court grants review, it will most likely take the single statutory question substantially as presented: whether the statewide voter list is within the NVRA provision requiring disclosure of records concerning implementation of voter-list-maintenance programs. It may clarify that the question is coverage of the list, rather than an entitlement to every sensitive data field. The direct First–Ninth Circuit disagreement is the likeliest basis for taking the case; disagreement with the petitioner's policy position alone is not.

Conditional on a grant, plenary briefing and argument are substantially more likely than immediate disposition: **12%** for a cert-order disposition, **88%** for the non-summary route. A GVR lacks an identified intervening controlling decision in this record; the competing statutory analyses make a summary reversal less likely than full review. If review leads to a merits decision, the likely ground for a petitioner-favorable result would be that the maintained list is an output concerning maintenance activities, with sensitive-field questions kept distinct. That merits reasoning is conditional, not a forecast that a majority opinion will issue after a denial.

If review is denied, expect no noted dissent or statement: **15%** probability of any such writing. Thomas, Alito, or Gorsuch are the most plausible individual writers in that relatively uncommon branch. No public cert-vote lineup is predicted, and no unsigned denial is treated as evidence of a unanimous private vote.
