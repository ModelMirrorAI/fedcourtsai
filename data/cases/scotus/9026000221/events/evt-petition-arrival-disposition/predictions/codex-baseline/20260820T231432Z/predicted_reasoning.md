# Forecast

The petition starts from zero distributions. It will be distributed for conference and is likely to be carried into at least one additional conference before disposition. The Court will not call for the Solicitor General's views: the constitutional issue, extensive record, and positions of the state and affected school districts can be presented without federal-government participation.

The Court will grant review. The most likely form is a plenary grant, with a substantial but slightly smaller chance of a cert-order disposition. The Fifth Circuit squarely held that *Stone v. Graham* no longer binds after *Kennedy*, while the petition presents the recurring nationwide consequences of that holding through a mandatory, permanent classroom display. If the Court uses a summary route, it will most likely summarily reverse or vacate and remand with instructions to respect *Stone* unless this Court itself overrules it; an intervening-decision GVR is less natural because the asserted conflict is already fully framed.

For plenary review, the Court will take the first question presented substantially as written: whether a state may mandate a permanent, conspicuous, government-selected Ten Commandments display in every public-school classroom. It will leave the separate Free Exercise question for application on remand or treat it as secondary to the Establishment Clause issue.

A cert-order disposition is slightly less likely than plenary review conditional on a grant. If review is denied, a separate dissent from denial or statement respecting denial is close to even but slightly less likely than no writing; the most likely authors would come from Justices Sotomayor, Kagan, or Jackson, emphasizing vertical stare decisis and the public-school setting.
