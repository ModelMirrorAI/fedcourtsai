# Forecast: Nathan v. Alamo Heights Independent School District (No. 26-221)

Joint petition (Rule 12.4, with Cribbs Ringer v. Comal ISD) from the en banc
Fifth Circuit's 9–8 decision of April 21, 2026, which vacated a preliminary
injunction against Texas S.B. 10 — the statute mandating a Ten Commandments
display in every public-school classroom — on the express ground that Stone v.
Graham, 449 U.S. 39 (1980), is no longer good law after Kennedy v. Bremerton.

## Disposition

**The Court grants certiorari and sets the case for plenary briefing and
argument.** An en banc court of appeals declaring one of this Court's
precedents abrogated is the classic certiorari trigger; the Court will not
leave standing a regime in which Stone binds every circuit except the Fifth
while two dozen state legislatures are enacting or considering copycat
statutes. Both blocs have reasons to grant: four-plus Justices who believe
Stone was wrongly decided (or died with Lemon) will want to say so themselves,
and the Justices who believe Stone controls cannot acquiesce in a lower court
performing the overruling. I put P(grant, including any grant-family route) at
0.72 — denial is a live but minority scenario (percolation while the Eighth
Circuit's Arkansas case, Stinson v. Fayetteville, No. 26-1722, matures, or
defensive denials on both wings).

## Path to disposition

- **Docket mechanics.** The BIO is due September 21, 2026; expect the
  respondent school districts (with Texas supporting them, as the State
  litigated below) to take at least one 30-day extension, putting the BIO in
  late October or November 2026. First distribution around a December 2026 or
  January 2027 conference.
- **Relists.** The petition is currently at zero distributions (it was
  docketed August 20, 2026). It will almost certainly be distributed
  (relist-increment 0.97 — only a pre-conference dismissal or withdrawal,
  implausible in a constitutional test case, prevents it). From there expect
  **one to three relists** before action: the modern Court almost never grants
  a major case at its first conference, and it will likely coordinate this
  petition with Louisiana's conditional petition in Roake v. Brumley (No.
  25-1360, response due September 28, 2026), whose en banc decision went off
  on ripeness rather than the merits. Grant order likely January–March 2027,
  argument in the fall of OT2027 sittings (October–December 2027) unless
  granted early enough for an April 2027 argument, which is possible but not
  the base case.
- **CVSG: no.** No federal party, no federal statute, and the Solicitor
  General will not need an invitation — the United States can be expected to
  file an uninvited amicus brief (it supported the states in the parallel
  Louisiana litigation). P(CVSG) 0.03.
- **Summary disposition: no.** Petitioners lead with a request for summary
  reversal or GVR "for application of Stone." The Court will not summarily
  reverse a 9–8 en banc decision that rests on a genuinely open question about
  the Court's own recent precedent (whether Kennedy's abrogation of Lemon
  carried Stone with it), and there is no intervening decision of this Court
  to GVR in light of. Conditional on a grant, P(decided in the cert order
  itself) 0.05.
- **Questions presented.** If it grants, the Court takes **QP 1 as written**
  (whether the Establishment Clause forbids mandatory classroom Ten
  Commandments displays) and most likely QP 2 alongside it — Mahmoud v. Taylor
  makes the parents' free-exercise theory independently live, and the en banc
  court resolved both. A grant limited to QP 1 is the main alternative.
- **If denied.** Expect a written dissent from denial — most likely from
  Justice Sotomayor or Justice Jackson, plausibly joined by Justice Kagan — on
  the vertical-stare-decisis point: a lower court declared this Court's
  precedent dead and the Court let it stand. A silent denial of this petition
  is very hard to imagine. P(some dissent or statement respecting denial |
  denial) 0.85.

## Conditional merits view (context only)

If granted, the likely ground is a direct resolution of Stone's status: either
a formal overruling (holding that under Kennedy's
history-and-tradition/coercion framework a passive classroom display is not an
establishment) or a reaffirmation that Stone survives Kennedy and controls a
statute "nearly identical" to the Kentucky law it struck down. Given the
Kennedy and Mahmoud majorities, an affirmance of the Fifth Circuit —
formally interring Stone — is the modestly more likely outcome on the merits,
but that is not what this cell forecasts.
