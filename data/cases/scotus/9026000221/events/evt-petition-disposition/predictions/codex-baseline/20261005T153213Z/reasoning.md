## Why 3%, rather than the 5.016% anchor

**The positive case is real.** The question presented attacks using an accusation as investigative background and then as corroboration of guilt. The petition, pages 10–14, describes conflicting approaches in several circuits and contrasts the Sixth Circuit's AEDPA analysis with review of a state judgment here. Smith's independently checked holding rejects a non-truth label for an absent analyst's statements when their support for an expert opinion depends on their truth. That supports the petition's analogy, without itself deciding every police-background case. The petition's claimed prosecutorial use in closing makes this more substantial than an abstract evidentiary complaint. These points prevent reducing the forecast to a near-zero routine error-correction probability.

**The vehicle substantially offsets that importance.** First, the judgment under review denies state postconviction relief for ineffective assistance; it is not simply a preserved direct-appeal ruling admitting an undisputed testimonial statement. The petition argues preservation through the collateral proceedings, and I do not assume a jurisdictional bar. Nonetheless, Strickland adds a separate prejudice inquiry and a narrower challenged omission. Appendix A, pages 16a–20a, expressly reasons that the result would remain unchanged even assuming deficient performance, relying on the confession and other evidence. The petition, pages 22–24, disputes the independence and adequacy of that analysis. That disagreement is a serious vehicle problem, not something the cert question automatically eliminates.

Second, the QP calls the accusation testimonial, but that characterization is disputed. Appendix A, pages 13a–15a, focuses on the child's private statement to her grandfather; the petition argues that stationhouse statements and intervening police reports also matter. Clark, independently checked at 576 U.S. 247–248, makes the circumstances and the very young child's purposes important to the testimonial inquiry. It does not categorically immunize every child statement, but it supplies a plausible alternative obstacle even if the non-truth rationale is wrong. Resolving which layer and which statement was challenged would complicate a clean national holding.

Third, the petition itself describes the principal opposing circuit decision as governed by AEDPA. That difference may explain some outcomes without establishing a clean conflict on the same review standard. I have not independently verified every cited circuit decision and discount the petition's strongest split characterization accordingly. The reproduced opinion is a useful counterweight to advocacy, but not a substitute for an adversarial BIO.

Finally, the provisioned docket shows a response waiver and no request for a response, no relist beyond the first distribution, and no CVSG. I give the lack of affirmative Court interest modest negative weight, not dispositive weight: the Court can still request a response. The combination yields **P(any grant) = 0.03**, with denial the modal disposition.
