# Forecast: Purdy v. Kansas, No. 26-221 (cert petition)

**Disposition.** The Court will deny certiorari. The most likely path is a
denial at the October 9, 2026 conference, appearing on the order list of
Tuesday, October 13, 2026, without a request for a response from Kansas.

**Relists.** The docket shows one distribution (September 16, 2026, for the
October 9 conference) and no relist. I expect no further distribution in the
base case. The main route to a further distribution is a call for a response:
Kansas waived on September 8, and if any chambers wants to look harder the
Court will request a response rather than relist a waived petition. A call
for a response would push the petition off the October 9 conference and
redistribute it for a late-November or December conference, which is where
most of my relist-increment probability sits (roughly one in five, counting a
reschedule as a further distribution as the harness does). A relist without a
call for a response is unlikely: there is no obvious pending case to hold this
one for, and the Court does not generally relist a waived state-criminal
petition to write a dissent from denial.

**CVSG.** None. This is a state criminal prosecution under the Sixth Amendment
with no federal party and no federal statute at issue; the Solicitor General's
views would not be invited.

**Which question, if taken.** If the Court took the case it would take the
question as framed, but narrowed to the pure Confrontation Clause point:
whether an officer's repetition of an absent declarant's accusation, defended
as investigative "background," is admitted for its truth under Smith v.
Arizona when the accusation describes the charged act and the prosecution
argues it as corroboration. The Court would leave the Ohio v. Clark
"testimonial" layer and the Strickland prejudice holding for remand. A plenary
grant is the only realistic grant route: there is no intervening decision to
GVR in light of, and a summary reversal of a state postconviction court on a
Strickland-wrapped Confrontation question is not the Court's practice, so I
put the summary route at about fifteen percent conditional on a grant.

**Dissent from denial.** I expect none. The issue has drawn no separate
writing in its recent cert appearances (the Court denied Reed v. Fredrick in
2025 without noted dissent), and the record here, a child-victim case with a
repeated confession and an alternative prejudice holding, is not one a
Justice would choose to write on. A statement respecting denial is a small
tail, around four percent.

**If granted (conditional only).** The likely ground would be Smith v.
Arizona's "actual use" test: an accusation whose evidentiary value depends on
its truth is admitted for its truth whatever the state-law label, and the
Court would vacate and remand for the Kansas courts to redo Strickland
prejudice under that rule. The vote would track Smith's broad majority.
