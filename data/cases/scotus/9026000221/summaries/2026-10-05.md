## In brief

A man convicted of child rape asks the Court to review whether police testimony relaying the child's accusation violated his right to confront witnesses.

## What happened

Theodore Purdy was convicted in Kansas of rape and aggravated indecent liberties with a child after a jury heard his confession along with testimony from two law enforcement officers describing an accusation the child, who did not testify, had made against him. The prosecution said this testimony explained the investigation rather than proving the accusation true, but then argued in closing that Purdy's confession corroborated what the child said. Purdy's postconviction appeal argued his trial lawyer was ineffective for not objecting. The Kansas Court of Appeals agreed the lawyer fell short but found no resulting harm, holding the officer's testimony was not hearsay because it explained the investigation. The Kansas Supreme Court denied further review.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked whether prosecutors may present an absent accuser's detailed accusation through a police officer by calling it background information explaining an investigation, when the accusation describes the exact crime charged and is then used to argue the defendant's guilt, without giving the defendant a chance to cross-examine the original accuser.

## Where it stands

The petition was distributed for the Court's conference on October 9, 2026. Kansas waived its right to file a response. The docket shows no action since that distribution.

## What each outcome would mean

Purdy, who lost below, is asking the Court to take up his case. If the Court grants review, it would hear full arguments and decide the confrontation question itself, deciding nothing yet about who wins. If the Court denies review, the Kansas Court of Appeals' decision upholding Purdy's conviction stands, though denial would not mean the Court agrees with that decision, only that it leaves it in place.
