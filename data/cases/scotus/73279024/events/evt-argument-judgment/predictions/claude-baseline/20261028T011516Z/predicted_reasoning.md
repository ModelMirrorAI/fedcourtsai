# Forecast: Department of the Air Force v. Prutehi Guahan (No. 25-579), post-argument

**Judgment class: reversed.** The Court reverses the Ninth Circuit, holding that
the Air Force's submission of a RCRA permit-renewal application to Guam EPA is
not final agency action reviewable under the APA, and remands with instructions
consistent with dismissal of the APA claim. P(the judgment below is disturbed) = 0.87.
A vacatur-and-remand is the main alternative form of a disturbing judgment; an
affirmance is the main undisturbed risk (~12%), and a DIG is remote (~1%).

**Vote lineup: 8–1, Sotomayor dissenting** as the single most likely split. A 7–2
with Kagan or Jackson joining Sotomayor, or a unanimous Court, are the main
alternatives. Thomas, Alito, Gorsuch, Kavanaugh, Barrett and Roberts are in the
majority on finality; Jackson's own framing at argument (the application is a
proposal the regulator can rewrite) is the majority's rationale, so she is likely
in it; Kagan probed the government's line-drawing hard but closed by suggesting
it makes more sense to wait until the permit's terms are known.

**Which question the Court reaches.** QP1 only. The government itself told the
Court that a non-finality holding disposes of the case (NEPA does not apply to
non-final action), and the Court's practice is not to reach a second question
unnecessarily. Kavanaugh pressed hard for deciding QP2 too, so expect a separate
writing from him (a concurrence urging that RCRA displaces NEPA, or flagging that
the question will recur in state permit-review proceedings). On QP2 the bench
looked unfavorable to the government's specific-governs-the-general theory
(Gorsuch, Sotomayor, Jackson all pushed that the statutes can be harmonized),
which is a further reason a majority will avoid it.

**Ground and breadth.** The majority applies Bennett v. Spear: an application
initiates a back-and-forth process rather than consummating one, and the old
permit's extension flows from the regulator's not ruling and the old permit's
terms, not from the application. The holding is narrow — tied to an agency
applying to another (here non-federal) regulator — and reserves whether the
Air Force's operational decision, or its conduct once a permit issues, could be
challenged as final agency action; it does not adopt a rule that contingent
decisions are never final.

**Who writes.** The case was argued in the October sitting. Thomas, who built his
questioning around the regulated-body-as-applicant point, and Barrett, who probed
the mechanics of the Guam review scheme, are my leading candidates for the
opinion of the Court, with Kavanaugh, Roberts and Jackson close behind. Expect a
Sotomayor dissent (she disputed the framing of the finality question and the
government's displacement theory), and plausible concurrences from Kavanaugh
(QP2) and Gorsuch (alternative avenues — unlawfully withheld action — or a
reservation on QP2).
