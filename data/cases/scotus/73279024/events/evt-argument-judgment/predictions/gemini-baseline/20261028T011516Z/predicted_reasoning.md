# Forecast of the Court's Decision: *Department of the Air Force v. Prutehi Guahan* (No. 25-579)

## 1. Judgment Class and Vote Lineup

- **Predicted Judgment:** `reversed` (disturbing the Ninth Circuit's judgment and reinstating the District Court of Guam's dismissal of Respondent's complaint).
- **Predicted Vote Lineup:** **9–0 in the judgment** (8–1 on the rationale, with Chief Justice Roberts and Justices Thomas, Alito, Sotomayor, Kagan, Gorsuch, Kavanaugh, and Barrett joining the opinion of the Court in full, and Justice Jackson concurring in the judgment or, as the primary alternative, dissenting solo for an 8–1 split).
- **Procedural Exit:** Neither a dismissal as improvidently granted (DIG) nor an affirmance by an equally divided Court is likely. All nine Justices participated at oral argument, and the bench rejected Respondent's effort to recharacterize its complaint around an unpleaded challenge to the Air Force's broader operational decision rather than the 2021 RCRA permit-renewal application named in the First Question Presented.

## 2. Which Question Presented the Court Reaches and Doctrinal Ground (`majority-ground`)

The Court will resolve the case on **Question Presented 1**—holding that a federal agency's submission of a permit-renewal application to a state or territorial regulator is not "final agency action" subject to immediate judicial review under the Administrative Procedure Act (APA), 5 U.S.C. § 704—and will **decline to reach Question Presented 2** (whether RCRA displaces NEPA review via functional equivalence or irreconcilable statutory conflict).

Applying the two-prong test of *Bennett v. Spear*, 520 U.S. 154, 177–78 (1997), the majority opinion will hold that the Air Force's May 2021 RCRA permit-renewal application fails both prongs:

1. **Failure of *Bennett* Prong 1 (Not the Consummation of Decisionmaking):** A permit application does not mark the "consummation" of the permitting process; it is the *beginning* of that process. The fact that RCRA's cooperative-federalism framework divides the process between two entities—a federal applicant (the Air Force) and an authorized territorial regulator (Guam EPA)—does not transform an interlocutory step (applying for permission) into a final decision to conduct hazardous-waste disposal operations for the new 2021–2024 permit term. Moreover, under 5 U.S.C. § 551(13), a permit application to another regulator is neither a "rule" (contrary to the Ninth Circuit's characterization) nor a "license" or "order."
2. **Failure of *Bennett* Prong 2 (No Direct Legal Consequences from the Application):** The Air Force's 2021 renewal application does not itself determine legal rights or obligations for the 2021–2024 period, because future operations under the new permit are contingent on Guam EPA's decision to grant the renewal. Nor does the automatic administrative continuation of the 2018 permit during Guam EPA's review (under 22 Guam Admin. R. & Regs. § 30109(o) and 40 C.F.R. § 270.51) supply the missing finality: that interim continuation flows by operation of Guam law from the unchallenged 2018 permit, not from any legal authority exercised by the Air Force in its 2021 application. And to the extent Respondent sought to reframe its claim as a challenge to an independent "decision" to conduct open burning/open detonation (OB/OD) operations apart from the permit application, that framing is foreclosed by both Respondent's complaint and the Question Presented.

## 3. Breadth of the Holding (`ground-breadth`)

The majority's holding will be **narrow and confined to the threshold APA finality question (QP1)**. Specifically:
- The Court will hold that submitting a permit application to a separate state or territorial regulator is not itself a reviewable "final agency action" under 5 U.S.C. § 704, even where state/territorial law provides that a timely renewal application keeps an existing permit in force pending the regulator's decision.
- The Court will note the Solicitor General's representation at oral argument that once Guam EPA grants a renewal permit and the Air Force makes an operational decision (such as issuing an operational plan) to detonate munitions under that permit, that subsequent federal operational decision—not the antecedent permit application—is the locus for any APA review.
- Because the lack of final agency action under § 704 disposes of the entire suit, the majority will leave open Question Presented 2 (whether RCRA's permitting procedures or the 2023 NEPA amendments in 42 U.S.C. § 4336(a)(2)–(3) displace NEPA for RCRA permitting).

## 4. Authorship and Separate Writings (`majority-author` and `separate-writers`)

- **Majority Author:** **Justice Kavanaugh** is the most likely author (`0.180`), followed by **Justice Barrett** (`0.140`), **Chief Justice Roberts** (`0.120`), and **Justice Kagan** (`0.120`). Justice Kavanaugh authored the Court's unanimous NEPA decision in *Seven County Infrastructure Coalition v. Eagle County*, 145 S. Ct. 1497 (2025), has extensive D.C. Circuit experience with APA finality and environmental permitting, and asked targeted questions at argument designed to build a clean consensus around QP1 while avoiding divisive rulings on QP2.
- **Separate Writings:**
  - **Justice Jackson (`0.38`):** Most likely to write separately—either a concurrence in the judgment (emphasizing the Government's concession that the Air Force's post-permit operational decision to detonate munitions will constitute final agency action, so that cooperative-federalism permitting does not create a permanent NEPA enforcement gap) or a solo dissent (arguing that because Guam EPA is not subject to NEPA and the timely renewal application legally extends the Air Force's authority to detonate munitions under 22 Guam Admin. R. & Regs. § 30109(o), the application marks the Air Force's final decision).
  - **Justice Thomas (`0.18`), Justice Sotomayor (`0.18`), and Justice Gorsuch (`0.16`):** Justice Thomas or Justice Gorsuch may write a brief concurrence addressing either Article III standing/ripeness or NEPA displacement under RCRA (QP2); Justice Sotomayor may join or write a brief concurrence emphasizing that the Court's holding turns strictly on the premature timing of a suit challenging a permit application rather than immunizing federal operational decisions from NEPA.
