# Forecast of the Court's judgment

The Court will **reverse** the Ninth Circuit and remand for proceedings consistent with the absence of final agency action in the challenged permit-renewal submission. My point forecast is **9–0 in the judgment**, with all nine Justices joining the narrow finality holding. The Court will not order environmental review or decide whether open detonation should continue at Tarague Beach. I expect an opinion during the present Term, most plausibly in winter or spring 2027.

The majority will treat the Air Force's application as a request for authorization within an unfinished permitting process, not as the consummated authorization itself. Guam EPA can deny the application or require changes. The Air Force's preference for continuing operations does not eliminate that substantive contingency. The majority will also reject the proposition that continuation of the previous permit makes the renewal application independently final: the continuing authority rests on the existing permit and the governing continuation mechanism, rather than a new final determination of rights in the application.

The Court will reach the first question presented and leave the broader second question unresolved. Its holding will not establish that federal agencies acting as proprietors are never subject to APA review. It will distinguish the submission challenged here from a separately identifiable decision or order governing operations. Whether a later operational decision is reviewable, or a differently pleaded failure-to-act claim can succeed, will remain outside the holding.

That route avoids a holding that RCRA's procedural requirements categorically displace NEPA. It also avoids deciding that every federal project awaiting any external approval is necessarily nonfinal. The Court will not promise that a particular future Guam-law action provides a remedy for the federal NEPA claim; that disputed remedial question is unnecessary to identifying the action challenged in this suit.

Barrett is my leading candidate to deliver the opinion, with Thomas, Roberts, and Jackson substantial alternatives. The argument gives Barrett a plausible organizing rationale: identify the application rather than a free-floating internal decision, distinguish the permitting authority from the applicant, and locate the asserted continuation consequence in the old permit. Authorship is much less predictable than the result.

Kavanaugh is the leading candidate for a separate concurrence, pressing the costs and uncertainty of leaving the NEPA/RCRA question unanswered or explaining why he would resolve it for the government. Gorsuch is another plausible separate writer, emphasizing that statutory overlap alone does not excuse compliance with both statutes while agreeing that this application is not the proper vehicle. A Kagan or Sotomayor concurrence could reserve the availability of later APA review. No particular separate writing is more likely than not, and a single unanimous opinion is a substantial possibility.

A vacatur rather than a reversal is the principal alternative judgment class if the Court orders further examination of the precise challenged action or the continuation mechanism. An affirmance is less likely but remains possible if a majority treats the application as the concrete implementation of a completed Air Force decision with immediate legal consequences. A DIG or equally divided affirmance is remote on this record.
