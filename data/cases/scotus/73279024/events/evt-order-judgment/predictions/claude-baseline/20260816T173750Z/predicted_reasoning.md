# Forecast: Department of the Air Force v. Prutehi Guahan, No. 25-579 (evt-order-judgment)

**Judgment class: `reversed`.** The Court will disturb the Ninth Circuit's
judgment, and the modal route is an outright reversal rather than a vacatur:
a holding that the Air Force's RCRA permit-renewal application is not final
agency action ends the APA suit, leaving nothing to remand for. P(disturbed)
= 0.88.

**Which question drives the judgment.** The Court will rest the judgment on
the first question presented — APA finality under *Bennett v. Spear*, read
with *Franklin v. Massachusetts* and *Dalton v. Specter* — holding that an
agency's submission of a permit application to a separate permitting
authority (here Guam EPA) neither consummates the agency's own
decisionmaking nor itself fixes legal consequences. The second question
(whether RCRA displaces NEPA's general review procedures) will either go
unreached or be addressed briefly and secondarily; the finality holding is
logically antecedent and disposes of the case. The ground will be stated as
a categorical rule about agency-as-applicant finality, not a fact-bound
holding about this record — this Court rarely grants at the Solicitor
General's request merely to correct a one-off application of settled law,
and the government's briefing frames the rule generally.

**Vote lineup.** Unanimous or near-unanimous on the judgment: 9-0 is the
modal outcome, with 8-1 or 7-2 (Jackson, and possibly Sotomayor, dissenting
or concurring only in the judgment) the main alternative. The recent pattern
in NEPA/administrative-review cases — *Seven County Infrastructure
Coalition v. Eagle County* was unanimous in the judgment, with the three
Democratic appointees concurring separately — is the template: expect the
possibility of a concurrence in the judgment from Sotomayor or Jackson
resting on narrower, RCRA-specific grounds rather than the majority's
categorical finality rule. Predicted author: Kavanaugh (who wrote *Seven
County* and has the administrative-review portfolio), with Barrett the
runner-up. (Authorship and writings resolve against no artifact today;
stated as context.)

**Procedural exits.** A DIG is very unlikely (~2%): the case was cleanly
presented, the SG is the petitioner, and no vehicle problem surfaced between
grant and briefing. An equally divided affirmance is negligible — no recusal
is on the docket and none is apparent.

**Timing.** Argument is set for October 7, 2026 — the first week of OT2026.
A decision by June 2027 is near-certain; a largely unanimous finality
holding would likely issue in the winter or early spring (January–April
2027).

**If the Court affirms instead** (the ~12% complement), the likeliest path
is that the automatic permit-extension consequence of Guam EPA's regulation
carries *Bennett*'s second prong — the one concrete, immediate legal effect
respondent can point to, which the government concedes occurred — combined
with *Sackett*'s "mere possibility of reconsideration" language on the first
prong. Even then respondent must also win the NEPA-displacement question to
keep the judgment, which is why full affirmance is the residual outcome, not
a coin-flip.
