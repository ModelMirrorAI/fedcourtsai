# Forecast — Department of the Air Force v. Prutehi Guahan (No. 25-579), judgment after briefing

**Judgment class: `reversed`. P(judgment below disturbed): 0.84.**

The Court will reverse the Ninth Circuit's judgment outright. The likeliest path
is the threshold question: the Court holds that the Air Force's submission of a
RCRA permit-renewal application to Guam EPA is not "final agency action" under
5 U.S.C. 704, applying Bennett v. Spear's two conditions and Franklin v.
Massachusetts's treatment of submissions that merely feed another actor's
decision. On that ground the suit fails at the door and the Court does not
decide QP2 (whether RCRA displaces NEPA at the application stage). The
second-most-likely path to the same judgment is QP2 itself — a holding that
NEPA imposes no separate obligation before a RCRA permit-renewal submission —
but the 2023 codification of NEPA's displacement rules at 42 U.S.C. 4336(a)
gives respondent a hard textual target there, and this Court prefers the clean
jurisdictional exit when one question disposes of the case. An
affirmed-in-part split across the two QPs is unlikely because affirmance on
either question alone cannot sustain the judgment below without the other.

**Vote lineup: 7–2.** Majority: Roberts, Thomas, Alito, Gorsuch, Kavanaugh,
Barrett, Kagan. Dissents: Sotomayor and Jackson, on the finality question —
respondent's framing (the challenged action is the Air Force's consummated
decision to continue open-detonation operations, with the application's
automatic permit-continuation effect as its legal consequence under Sackett and
Hawkes) is the kind of access-to-review argument they have credited. Kagan
joins the majority as an APA formalist; a unanimous or 8–1 disposition with
liberal concurrences in the judgment (the Seven County pattern) is the main
alternative to this lineup. Kavanaugh is the most plausible majority author
(he wrote Seven County); authorship is unscored and offered as context.

**Ground and breadth** (the two semantic claims, in prose): the majority's
doctrinal basis will be Bennett finality — no consummation, no rights or
obligations determined by an application that begins an iterative process
before a separate regulator — and the rule will be stated categorically, for
agency permit applications as a class, not confined to RCRA, the military, or
Tarague Beach, while preserving APA review once the regulator acts.

**Procedural exits:** a DIG is unlikely (~2%) — the vehicle is clean, the panel
below divided, and the Solicitor General is the petitioner. An equally divided
affirmance is not live: no recusal is apparent and the full bench heard the
linked extension application.

**Timing** (unscored, stated for discipline): argued October 7, 2026 — the
first sitting of OT2026. A threshold administrative-law reversal from the
October sitting most likely issues between February and May 2027; modal
estimate March 2027, with June 2027 the outer bound.
