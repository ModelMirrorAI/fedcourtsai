# Rationale

I assign a 0.74 probability that the judgment below is disturbed and predict a reversal. Across the available strictly prior rows in the OT2015-OT2024 ten-Term window, the committed merits anchor is 359 disturbed judgments among 515 parsed judgments, or 69.7%; OT2015 and OT2016 have no represented row. The represented rows cover 515 of 557 grants (92.5%), clear the 30-judgment floor, and exclude 57 dispositions caught by the cert-order pool guard. I adjusted modestly above that anchor because the Court granted the federal government's petition from a divided Ninth Circuit panel and the government's first question offers a narrow route to reverse without deciding the broader NEPA issue. The petition's pre-grant distributions are spent cert-stage signals and did not affect this merits forecast.

The strongest basis for reversal is finality. The petition and questions presented show that the Air Force submitted an application to a different regulator, Guam EPA, which retains authority to demand changes and ultimately grant or deny the renewal. That structure supports the government's characterization of the application as interlocutory under *Bennett*, *Franklin*, and *Dalton*. The Court's generally restrictive treatment of NEPA in *Seven County Infrastructure Coalition v. Eagle County* modestly reinforces the reversal adjustment, although that case does not answer either question here.

Respondent's merits brief materially limits confidence. It argues that the relevant decision is the Air Force's completed selection of continued open detonation at Tarague Beach, not Guam EPA's later permitting decision. More importantly, the timely application automatically continued the 2018 permit and has allowed operations to continue for nearly five years, giving the filing an immediate legal consequence that an ordinary application lacks. On the second question, respondent has a strong textual argument that NEPA yields only to an express exclusion or a clear and fundamental conflict, while RCRA governs a later regulator-facing process and lacks NEPA's early alternatives analysis. Those points make affirmance, or a narrower vacatur, a substantial possibility and keep the forecast well below certainty.

This was a forward-mode forecast. I used the provisioned August 16 docket snapshot, the questions presented, the cert petition, and the document labeled `brief-in-opposition`, which actually concatenates the January cert opposition and the July respondent merits brief. The latter is truncated after extracted page 37, before its full final-agency-action argument; the earlier cert opposition and the merits brief's introduction and summary preserve the core of that position. No petitioner merits-brief text was provisioned, so its filing was visible only as a docket event and its position was assessed through the petition and respondent's merits brief. CourtListener MCP searches for the predecision authorities *Rico* and *Seven County* were throttled, and exact-citation corpus lookups returned no usable rows, so no outside opinion text affected the forecast.

The 0.68 confidence reflects a solid directional call but real uncertainty over whether the automatic permit continuation satisfies the second *Bennett* prong and whether textualist Justices accept respondent's distinction between a regulator and a permit applicant. The 0.70 big-case score reflects the nationwide implications for federal facilities and environmental permitting, tempered by the possibility of a fact-specific finality ruling.
