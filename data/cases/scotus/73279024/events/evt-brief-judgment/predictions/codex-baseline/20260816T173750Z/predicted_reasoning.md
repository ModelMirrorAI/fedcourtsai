# Forecast

The Court will reverse the Ninth Circuit, most likely by a 6-3 vote. Roberts, Thomas, Alito, Gorsuch, Kavanaugh, and Barrett will join the majority; Sotomayor, Kagan, and Jackson will dissent.

The majority will most likely resolve the case on final agency action. It will characterize the Air Force's renewal application as the opening submission in an iterative process controlled by Guam EPA, not the consummation of an agency process that itself fixes legal rights or obligations under *Bennett v. Spear*. It will attribute the continued force of the 2018 permit to the existing permit and governing continuation rule, and it will regard a future Guam EPA grant or denial as the operative regulatory act. On that ground, the Court can reverse without deciding whether RCRA's permitting procedures displace NEPA review for a federal permit applicant.

The dissent will emphasize that the Air Force had definitively selected continued open detonation at Tarague Beach and that filing the timely renewal application was the necessary trigger for automatic continuation of the prior permit. It will also stress that postponing review until Guam EPA acts frustrates NEPA's requirement that environmental alternatives be considered before the federal agency commits to its course.

If the Court reaches the statutory-interaction question, the majority is more likely than not to treat RCRA's specific hazardous-waste review as displacing duplicative NEPA procedures in this setting. That route is less likely than the finality holding because RCRA contains no express NEPA exemption, the two statutes can operate at different decision points, and the functional-equivalence precedents principally concern an environmental regulator rather than a federal permit applicant.

A DIG or equally divided affirmance is unlikely: the questions are cleanly presented, no participation problem appears on the record, and the parties have fully joined the two issues. A decision is most likely by the end of June 2027.
