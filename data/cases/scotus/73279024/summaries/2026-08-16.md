## What happened

Since 1982, the Air Force has destroyed hazardous-waste munitions by open burning and open detonation at a range on Tarague Beach in northern Guam, a site near an aquifer that supplies most of the island's drinking water. In 2021 the Air Force decided to continue these operations and applied to renew its hazardous-waste permit with the Guam Environmental Protection Agency, without first preparing an environmental review under a federal law requiring agencies to study environmental effects before major actions. Prutehi Guåhan, a Guam environmental group, sued. A federal district court dismissed the case, but the Ninth Circuit reversed, ruling the Air Force's decision was final enough to challenge in court and that the environmental-review law still applied even though a separate waste-permitting law was also involved.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked two questions. First, whether the Air Force's move to seek renewal of its waste-disposal permit, describing where and how it would keep destroying munitions, was a completed enough decision to be challenged in court immediately, or whether it was just a preliminary step before Guam's environmental agency acts. Second, whether the Air Force still had to study environmental impacts and alternatives before deciding on this disposal plan, even though a different, waste-specific permitting law also requires some environmental review once the permit application is being considered by Guam's regulators.

## Where it stands
