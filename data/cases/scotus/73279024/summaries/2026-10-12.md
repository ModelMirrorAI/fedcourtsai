## In brief

The Supreme Court heard the Air Force's challenge to a ruling that its application to renew a hazardous-waste permit on Guam was reviewable and required separate environmental review under NEPA.

## What happened

The Air Force has destroyed waste munitions by open burning and detonation on Tarague Beach, Guam, since the early 1980s under permits issued by Guam's environmental agency under a federal hazardous-waste law (RCRA). In 2021 the Air Force applied to renew its permit without first preparing the environmental study NEPA generally requires before major federal actions. Prutehi Guåhan, a Guam environmental group, sued, arguing the Air Force needed to complete NEPA review first. A federal district court dismissed the suit, but the Ninth Circuit reversed, ruling that the permit application was a reviewable final decision and that NEPA still applied despite the RCRA permitting process.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court must decide two things: first, whether simply applying to renew a hazardous-waste permit counts as a final decision that can be challenged in court right away, even though the permitting agency has not yet acted on it; and second, whether a federal agency must complete general environmental-impact review under NEPA before applying for a permit under a different law that has its own specific environmental-review requirements for hazardous waste.

## Where it stands

The Court granted review, heard full briefing including numerous outside groups' briefs, and held oral argument on October 7, 2026. The case now awaits decision.

## What each outcome would mean

The Air Force and other federal petitioners asked the Court to take up the case after losing below. If the Court reverses, it would mean permit-renewal applications like this one are not immediately challengeable in court, and/or that agencies like the Air Force need not conduct separate NEPA review before seeking such permits—restoring the lower court dismissal of Prutehi Guåhan's suit. If the Court affirms, the Ninth Circuit's ruling stands, allowing Prutehi Guåhan's lawsuit to proceed and requiring the Air Force to satisfy NEPA before applying for the permit. A vacate-and-remand would set the Ninth Circuit's decision aside without resolving the dispute for either side.
