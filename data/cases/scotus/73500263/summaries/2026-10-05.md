## In brief

The Supreme Court declined to hear a retired worker's dispute with his former employer's pension plan over how his court-ordered benefit share was calculated.

## What happened

David Gasper's 2013 divorce produced a court order dividing his pension benefits with his former spouse. The pension plan approved that order. When Gasper later applied for retirement benefits, the plan administrator required him to choose among benefit forms that, he says, changed how his benefit was calculated and reduced his share. Gasper challenged the change through the plan's internal appeals process, receiving what he describes as three inconsistent denial letters, then sued in federal district court. The district court ruled against him, and the Fourth Circuit Court of Appeals affirmed that ruling in December 2025.

## What the Court is being asked

Gasper asks the Court to decide two questions. First, whether a court reviewing a pension plan's benefit decision may uphold that decision based on reasons the plan administrator did not actually give during the internal appeals process, rather than limiting review to the reasons the administrator stated at the time. Second, whether a plan administrator may replace a benefit structure fixed by a court-approved domestic-relations order with a different payment form that Gasper says the plan's own documents never authorized.

## Where it stands

The petition was distributed for conference in June 2026 and again for a conference in September 2026, after the respondents waived their right to respond. The Supreme Court denied the petition on October 5, 2026.

## What each outcome would mean

Gasper, as the petitioner, asked the Court to take up his case. By denying the petition, the Court leaves the Fourth Circuit's decision in place, meaning the lower courts' rulings against Gasper stand and the plan's benefit calculation as upheld by those courts remains in effect. The denial does not mean the Supreme Court agreed with the Fourth Circuit's reasoning; it simply means the case will not receive further review, and no court will revisit the dispute at this stage.
