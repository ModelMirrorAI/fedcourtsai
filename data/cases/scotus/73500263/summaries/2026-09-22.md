## What happened

David Gasper's pension benefits were divided with his former spouse through a court-approved domestic relations order in 2013. When Gasper applied for retirement benefits in 2019, the pension plan run by EIDP (formerly DuPont) and related entities required him to elect a different benefit form than the one set out in that 2013 order, which he says reduced his share and changed how costs were calculated. Gasper challenged the change through the plan's internal appeals process, then in federal court, arguing that the plan administrator never adequately explained the basis for reducing his benefit. The district court and the Fourth Circuit Court of Appeals both ruled against him, upholding the plan's decision. Gasper, representing himself, is now asking the Supreme Court to take up the case.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked two related questions. First, whether courts reviewing a benefits decision may uphold it based on reasons and plan provisions the plan administrator never actually gave to the person appealing, rather than being limited to the reasons the administrator originally stated. Second, whether a retirement plan may replace a person's already-finalized, court-ordered pension benefit with a different type of benefit that Gasper says does not appear in the plan's own documents, effectively altering what a state court had ordered in the divorce proceedings.
