## In brief

A retiree asks the Court to review lower-court rulings upholding a pension plan's reduction of his court-ordered retirement benefit.

## What happened

David Gasper's 2010 divorce produced a court order in 2013 dividing his pension, which the plan approved as final. When he retired in 2019, the plan administrator required him to choose among benefit options that, he says, replaced the 2013 order's terms, reducing his share and shifting costs to him. He appealed within the plan, received three denial letters he says gave shifting and incomplete reasons, and then sued. The district court ruled against him, and the Fourth Circuit Court of Appeals affirmed in December 2025.

## What the Court is being asked

Gasper asks the Court to decide two things: first, whether a court reviewing a benefit denial may uphold it based on reasons and plan terms the plan administrator itself never gave during the internal appeal process, rather than only the reasons actually stated at the time; and second, whether a plan administrator may replace a retirement benefit already fixed by a court-approved divorce order with a different benefit form not authorized by the plan's own documents, changing how the divorce order divided the property.
