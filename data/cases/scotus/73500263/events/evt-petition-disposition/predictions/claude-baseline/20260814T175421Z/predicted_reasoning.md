# Predicted reasoning — Gasper v. EIDP, Inc. (No. 25-1362)

The petition sits on the docket for the Conference of September 28, 2026 —
the long conference. I forecast a **denial on the order list that follows
it, expected Monday, October 5, 2026**, without separate writing.

**Relists.** The docket shows two distribution entries: May 19, 2026 (the
sealed-appendix motion 25M82, for the June 4 conference) and June 17, 2026
(the petition itself, for September 28). The petition proper is at its
first conference distribution and has never been relisted. I expect **no
further distribution** after the long conference (P ≈ 0.10 that one more
accrues). The residual chance is mostly a routine reschedule out of the
long conference's crush or a call for a response, not a merits-interest
relist.

**Call for a response.** Respondents waived on June 11, 2026, and the Court
has not requested a response. The Court does not grant without one, so any
grant path runs through a CFR first — and I expect none: the petition is a
fact-bound plan-administration dispute pressed pro se.

**CVSG.** None, with high confidence (P ≈ 0.01). This is a private
benefits dispute between one participant and one plan; no federal program
or agency position is implicated in a way that has drawn the Solicitor
General in comparable single-plan QDRO cases.

**Question taken, if taken.** Against the odds, a grant would take a
reformulated version of QP 1 — whether ERISA § 503 and Chenery principles
confine judicial review of a benefit denial to the rationales and plan
provisions the administrator actually gave — since that is the only
question with a colorable inter-circuit dimension. QP 2 (whether the plan
could substitute a spousal-annuity form for the QDRO's structure) is
case-specific plan interpretation and would be left behind. Justice Alito
took no part in the linked motion 25M82, so expect his recusal from any
disposition of this petition as well.

**Summary disposition.** No GVR and no summary reversal: no intervening
decision of the Court bears on the Chenery-in-ERISA question, and the
record-heavy posture does not support per curiam treatment.

**Separate writings.** Expect a bare denial — no dissent from denial and no
statement respecting denial. The Chenery/§ 503 issue has circulated for
years without drawing a noted dissent in similar vehicles, and this
petition's pro se, fact-bound posture makes it an unlikely occasion for
one.
