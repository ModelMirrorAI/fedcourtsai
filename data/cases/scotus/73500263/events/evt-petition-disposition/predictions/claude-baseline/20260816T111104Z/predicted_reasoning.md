# Forecast — Gasper v. EIDP, Inc. (25-1362), petition disposition

The Court will **deny** this petition, most likely on the order list following
the September 28, 2026 long conference (i.e., in the first days of October
2026).

**Relists.** The docket shows two distributions, but only one is the
petition's: the May 19 distribution was of the linked seal motion (25M82,
granted June 8), and the petition itself was distributed once, on June 17, for
the 9/28/2026 long conference. Forecasting from that state: I do not expect a
further distribution. The most likely path is denial at the long conference
without a call for a response and without a relist. A third distribution would
most plausibly come from a call for the respondent's waived response — the
one step the Court takes when any chambers is interested — and none has issued
in the two months since the June 11 waiver. I put roughly a 12% chance on at
least one more distribution accruing past the two the record shows.

**CVSG.** None will issue. This is a private pension-benefits dispute between
an individual and a corporate plan; no federal party's interest is implicated
and the questions do not turn on an agency's regulatory scheme in a way that
has drawn CVSGs. Near-zero probability.

**Question taken, if any.** If the Court took anything, it would be a
narrowed form of QP 1 — whether ERISA § 503's specific-reasons requirement
limits a reviewing court to the rationales the administrator actually gave (a
Chenery-style rule for plan administrators) — stripped of this case's
record-specific framing. QP 2 (the replacement of a separate-interest QDRO
benefit with a spousal-annuity form) is fact-bound plan interpretation and
would be left behind.

**Summary disposition.** Unlikely in absolute terms because any grant is
unlikely; conditional on the Court acting favorably at all, a summary route
(GVR-type) and a plenary grant are roughly comparable in likelihood — there
is no obvious intervening decision to GVR in light of, which cuts against the
cert-order route, but pro se error-correction cases that move at all tend to
move summarily. I put the conditional cert-order share at about 35%.

**Separate writings on denial.** Expect none — no dissent from denial and no
statement respecting denial. The case lacks the ideological or doctrinal
salience that draws them; roughly a 1% chance some Justice writes.

**If granted** (conditional, and I give the grant family only ~1%): the likely
ground would be that ERISA § 503 and its implementing regulation confine
judicial review of a benefits denial to the reasons and plan provisions the
administrator identified in the administrative process, resolving the claimed
disagreement among the circuits over post-hoc rationales.
