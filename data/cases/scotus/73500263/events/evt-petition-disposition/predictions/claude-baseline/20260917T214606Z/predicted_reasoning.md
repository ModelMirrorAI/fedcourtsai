# Forecast: what the Court will do with No. 25-1362, Gasper v. EIDP, Inc.

**Disposition.** The petition will be denied, almost certainly on the first order list after the September 28, 2026 long conference (the October 5, 2026 list). Respondents have waived their response, no amicus has appeared, and the Court has no reason to call for a response: the questions presented do not match what the Fourth Circuit actually decided.

**Relists.** The docket shows exactly one distribution (for the 9/28/2026 conference) and no relist. I expect no further distribution. The one realistic route to a second distribution is a routine reschedule off the long conference or a hold for a pending ERISA merits case; I know of no pending case this petition could be held for, so I put the chance of any further distribution at about one in ten.

**CVSG.** None. The Solicitor General's views are occasionally sought on ERISA questions of plan administration, but not on a pro se petition whose respondent did not bother to file a brief in opposition and whose actual holding is an application of North Carolina contract law to one domestic-relations order. Probability about 1%.

**Which question, if any.** Neither as written. QP 1 (Chenery / ERISA § 503) asks the Court to review a "post-hoc rationale" problem the panel never discussed; the panel reviewed the QDRO de novo as a contract and held its language unambiguous, so there was no administrative rationale for it to substitute. QP 2 (the § 205 / § 206(d)(3) "unauthorized spousal-annuity form" theory) was, in the form the petition presses it, treated below as raised for the first time on appeal (the panel's footnote 5 declines the § 1056(d)(3)(C) argument as forfeited). If the Court took anything, it would have to reformulate the case around the standard-of-review question the panel did flag as open in the Fourth Circuit, and that question was decided in petitioner's favor (de novo), so he has no grievance on it.

**Summary disposition versus plenary review.** Conditional on any grant, a GVR is the likelier form (roughly one in three of grants), because a petition of this profile is granted, if at all, only in the wake of an intervening decision rather than on its own claimed split. I see no intervening decision on the horizon that bears on it, which is why the unconditional grant probability is so low.

**Dissent from denial.** None expected. No Justice has a stake in this fact-bound benefits calculation, and no separate writing accompanies denials of pro se petitions of this kind. Note that Justice Alito took no part in the motion to file the supplemental appendix under seal, and I expect the denial order to record the same recusal; that is a non-participation notation, not a dissent or statement.
