# Forecast

The Court will deny the petition. The provisioned record shows one distribution of the petition, for the September 28, 2026 conference, and no CVSG. The earlier distribution and grant concerned the separate motion to file a supplemental appendix under seal, not certiorari. I expect zero further petition distributions and disposition around late September or early October 2026. The probability of at least one additional distribution is 6%; if additional consideration occurs, one further distribution is more likely than a prolonged sequence.

I expect no call for the Solicitor General's views (0.8% probability of a new CVSG). The benefits-law setting gives the federal government a possible institutional interest, but this dispute does not present a sufficiently clear general conflict to make an invitation likely. An invitation, if issued, would more likely follow further consideration in fall 2026 than accompany an immediate denial.

If the Court grants review, the more plausible question is a narrowed version of QP 1: whether ERISA's administrative notice and review requirements permit affirmance on a plan provision or rationale not communicated during the benefits process. The Court would be less likely to accept QP 2's premise that the administrator replaced a vested separate-interest benefit with an unauthorized annuity. That premise is entangled with the interpretation of this particular QDRO and the plan's actuarial calculations.

Conditional on any grant, I assign 22% to disposition in the cert order itself and 78% to plenary review. The record supplies no identified intervening decision that would make a GVR the natural route. A summary correction or remand remains possible if the Court sees a clear administrative-process error, but settling the asserted general rule would more plausibly require briefing and argument.

Conditional on denial, I assign 1% to a noted dissent or statement respecting denial and expect none. I forecast no individual Justice's cert vote or authorship. A denial would leave the judgment below intact without endorsing its reasoning or announcing a merits rule.
