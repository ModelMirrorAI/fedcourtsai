## Why below the anchor

**There is a potentially general legal question, but the claimed conflict is not cleanly demonstrated.** QP 1 concerns post-hoc rationales and plan provisions under ERISA's administrative notice requirements and Chenery; QP 2 concerns allegedly replacing a qualified domestic-relations benefit with an unauthorized spousal annuity. The petition identifies contrary approaches in other circuits, but its purported permissive camp is essentially this decision, while it also invokes Fourth Circuit precedent as already requiring the procedural protection it seeks. That is consistent with an alleged misapplication within a circuit rather than an established disagreement over the governing rule. I treat the petition's statements about what its cited precedents hold as advocacy, not independently verified holdings. [Source: petition, QPs and printed pp. 35–38.]

**The actual lower opinion makes the vehicle appreciably narrower.** This was a published, unanimous Fourth Circuit opinion, not an unpublished disposition. That gives its reasoning some wider significance. Nevertheless, it adopts de novo interpretation of the QDRO in agreement with the Third and Fifth Circuits, then applies North Carolina contract principles to the distinction between permissive and mandatory cost-allocation language. The decision separately applies deferential review to the plan's benefit calculations. It does not expressly announce a contrary Chenery rule; a literal search of the opinion for Chenery returned no matches. My inference is that review would require resolving how the administrator's explanations and this order fit together before the Court could reach the broad conflict as framed. [Source: Gasper v. EIDP, Inc., No. 24-1959, Dec. 8, 2025, slip op. pp. 1–3, 9–13; CourtListener opinion 11216613.]

**A concrete preservation problem undercuts the petition's clean-vehicle assertion.** The petition says there are no waiver problems and characterizes a statutory objection as ignored. The lower opinion, however, expressly declines to consider the argument under 29 U.S.C. § 1056(d)(3)(C) because it was first raised on appeal. This does not prove that all of either QP is forfeited; it is a specific obstacle to part of the statutory framing and makes the categorical clean-vehicle claim unreliable. The record's disputed annuity characterizations and absent original administrative materials add uncertainty rather than warranting acceptance of either party's account wholesale. [Sources: petition, printed pp. 5 n.2 and 39–40; lower opinion, p. 10 n.5.]

**There is little Court-generated attention at this point.** One ordinary distribution, a waived response, no recorded request to respond, and no CVSG offer no affirmative signal that offsets these vehicle problems. The months between June distribution and the scheduled September conference are not multiple relists. I give the published decision and the potentially recurring administrative-process question some positive weight, but the narrow order interpretation, weakly established conflict, and preservation concern dominate. The adjustment from 5.12% to 1.2% is my judgment, not a fitted statistical estimate. [Sources: provisioned snapshot/context; lower opinion, pp. 9–12.]

## Other claims and limitations

- **Further distribution: 6%.** Zero additional distributions is my modal forecast. Some chance remains for a requested response or further conference consideration, but the terminal relist counts do not supply a calibrated transition model.
- **New CVSG: 0.8%.** Federal benefits administration creates a possible interest, but the record-specific dispute and lack of visible Court attention make an invitation unlikely.
- **Summary route given grant: 22%.** This is conditional, not 22% of all outcomes. It leaves room for summary procedural correction, but I identified no intervening authority making a hold-and-GVR route especially plausible. Plenary review is the more likely route if the Court perceives the asserted general conflict.
- **Dissent or statement given denial: 1%.** I see little basis for predicting a separate writing. This is an aggregate existence forecast, not a Justice-level prediction.
- **Significance: 0.24.** Pension administration and QDRO finality can matter beyond these parties, but the demonstrated dispute is technically narrow. This is a stakes assessment independent of the 1.2% grant probability.

The main limitation is the absence of original appendices and an adversarial Supreme Court response. The petition and lower opinion disagree about the administrative record and preservation; I cannot adjudicate all those disputes from these materials. I used no corpus-query priors. Two attempts to obtain general certiorari-rule context through the web tool returned no usable content, so no retrieved web material influenced the forecast. The CourtListener opinion retrieval worked. No durable data-quality or leakage flag is warranted on what I observed.
