# Forecast: St. Clair v. Quick (Pettit), Warden, No. 25-1274

**Disposition.** The Court will deny certiorari at or shortly after the September 28, 2026 long conference. The petition is a paid, counseled filing by an Oklahoma state prisoner asking whether AEDPA's one-year limitation in 28 U.S.C. § 2244(d)(1) governs a habeas petition styled under § 2241, and whether the Tenth Circuit's rule (Burger v. Scott; Dulworth v. Evans) survives Bowe v. United States (Jan. 9, 2026). The Court will not take it.

**Relists.** The docket shows one distribution (June 24, 2026, for the September 28 conference) and no relist. I expect no further distribution: the petition will be disposed of on the long-conference order list or the one that follows it. A reschedule or single relist is possible but unlikely; more than one is very unlikely.

**CVSG.** None. No federal party or federal-government interest is implicated; the respondent is a state warden and the United States has no stake in whether state prisoners' § 2241 petitions carry a limitations period. The Court has never called for the Solicitor General's views on this split and will not here.

**Response.** The record shows no brief in opposition and no waiver. If the Court had real interest it would first call for a response, which would itself produce a further distribution. I do not expect a call for a response.

**Which question, if taken.** If the Court took anything, it would reformulate the QP to the clean statutory question: whether § 2244(d)(1) applies to a habeas application by a person in custody pursuant to a state-court judgment when the application is filed under § 2241 rather than § 2254. It would drop the petition's second clause asking whether the Tenth Circuit's rule "can be squared with" Bowe, which is argument rather than a question.

**Summary disposition versus plenary review.** Conditional on any grant, a GVR in light of Bowe is a live route, since the petition itself asks for one in the alternative and the Court sometimes clears a docket that way after a new statutory-construction decision. But Bowe construed § 2244(b)(1) and § 2244(b)(3)(E) as they bear on federal prisoners' § 2255 motions, and said nothing about § 2244(d) or § 2241, so an intervening-decision GVR is a stretch. Conditional on a grant I put the cert-order route a bit above one in three; plenary review on the reformulated question is the likelier form a grant would take, and neither is likely in absolute terms.

**Dissent from denial.** None expected. No Justice has flagged the § 2241/§ 2244(d) question as one awaiting a vehicle, the underlying custody claim is weak, and the decision below is an unpublished order denying a certificate of appealability. A statement respecting denial is very unlikely.

**If granted, the likely ground.** Conditionally only: the Court would most likely hold that § 2244(d)(1) applies by its own terms to any habeas application by "a person in custody pursuant to the judgment of a State court," whatever section the petition invokes, and affirm. The textual hook the petitioner needs, a limitation tied to § 2254 rather than to state custody, is not in § 2244(d)(1), and Bowe's own reasoning stresses that § 2244's strict requirements are aimed at state prisoners.
