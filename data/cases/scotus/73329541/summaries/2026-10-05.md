## In brief

The Supreme Court declined to hear an Oklahoma prisoner's challenge to how courts calculate deadlines for certain habeas corpus petitions.

## What happened

Michael St. Clair, convicted of murder in Oklahoma in the early 1990s, later escaped and was transferred to Kentucky for prosecution there under an agreement between the two states' governors. After years of litigation in Kentucky, he was returned to Oklahoma custody in 2018. He filed a federal habeas petition arguing that agreement required his release, not challenging his original conviction. The district court dismissed his petition as filed too late, applying a one-year deadline. The Tenth Circuit denied him permission to appeal, and later denied rehearing.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked whether a one-year filing deadline that applies to prisoners challenging their underlying convictions also applies to a different, more general type of habeas petition that St. Clair used to challenge the terms of his custody transfer, rather than his conviction itself. St. Clair's petition says courts disagree on this question, and points to a recent Supreme Court decision addressing a related deadline question as supporting his position.

## Where it stands

The petition was distributed for the Court's conference on September 28, 2026, and the Court denied it on October 5, 2026.

## What each outcome would mean

St. Clair, the prisoner, asked the Court to take up this case. The denial means the Court will not hear it, leaving the Tenth Circuit's ruling in place: St. Clair's habeas petition remains dismissed as untimely, and the one-year deadline continues to apply to this type of petition in that circuit. The denial does not mean the Supreme Court agreed the Tenth Circuit was correct, only that the case will not be reviewed.
