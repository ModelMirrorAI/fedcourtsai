A state prisoner's bid to have the Supreme Court decide whether a one-year filing deadline applies to his type of habeas challenge was denied.

## What happened

Michael St. Clair, convicted of murder in Oklahoma in the early 1990s, later escaped, committed crimes in Kentucky, and was eventually sent back to Kentucky under a written agreement between the two states' governors for prosecution there. After his Kentucky cases ended, he was returned to Oklahoma custody in 2018. He then filed a federal habeas petition arguing that agreement required his release from Oklahoma custody. A federal district court dismissed his petition as filed too late, and the Tenth Circuit Court of Appeals denied him permission to appeal. Oklahoma's prison warden is the respondent.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked whether the one-year deadline that applies to habeas petitions challenging a person's underlying criminal conviction also applies to a different type of habeas petition, like St. Clair's, that instead challenges the terms of a custody-transfer agreement between states. Lower courts disagree: most courts to consider the issue say the deadline applies, but one court of appeals has held it does not. St. Clair also argues a recent Supreme Court decision about a related deadline question supports his position.

## Where it stands
