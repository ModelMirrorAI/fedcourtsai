# Forecast of Court Action

## Procedural Trajectory (Relists and CVSG)

As of the October 22, 2026 snapshot, this petition (`No. 26-310`) has been distributed once (`distribution_count: 1`, on October 21, 2026, for the November 6, 2026 Conference) and has not been relisted, with the Court issuing a Call for a Response on October 22, 2026 (response due November 23, 2026) after Respondents initially waived their right to respond.

- **Further Distributions / Relists (`relist-increment: 0.98`):** Because the October 22, 2026 Call for a Response pulls the petition off the November 6, 2026 initial waiver conference list pending the filing of Respondents' Brief in Opposition (and any subsequent reply brief), the petition is virtually certain to be distributed at least once more once briefing is complete in December 2026 or January 2027. After that post-opposition distribution, there is a moderate chance (roughly 25–30%) of one or two true post-conference relists if a Justice circulates a separate statement or dissent from denial, but the most likely path is disposition after the first or second post-BIO conference distribution.
- **Call for the Views of the Solicitor General (`cvsg-increment: 0.03`):** The Court is very unlikely to invite the Solicitor General's views. The United States is not a party, the governing Title VII "severe or pervasive" framework under *Harris v. Forklift Systems, Inc.*, 510 U.S. 17 (1993), and *Faragher v. City of Boca Raton*, 524 U.S. 775 (1998), is well-established, and the dispute turns on the fact-specific sufficiency of a single online training module pleaded in a sequential lawsuit rather than an ambiguous federal regulatory scheme.

## Disposition and Question Presented

- **Predicted Disposition (`denied`, `P(any grant) = 0.12`):** After Respondents file their Brief in Opposition, the Court will most likely deny the petition for a writ of certiorari.
- **Question Presented if Granted:** If the Court were to grant review, it would take the petition's single Question Presented as written—whether employer-mandated training that communicates derogatory racial stereotypes and directs race-conscious workplace conduct may be sufficiently severe to create a racially hostile work environment under Title VII absent repetition or additional race-based misconduct.
- **Summary Disposition vs. Plenary Review (`summary-disposition-route: 0.15`):** Conditional on a grant, plenary review with full merits briefing and oral argument is far likelier (85%) than a summary disposition (15%). No intervening Supreme Court decision postdates the Tenth Circuit's May 11, 2026 opinion in *Young II* (the Court's decision in *Ames v. Ohio Department of Youth Services*, 605 U.S. 303 (2025), was decided nearly a year earlier), making a GVR unlikely, and the fact-intensive nature of the pleading record and *Young I* preclusion issues makes summary reversal improbable.

## Separate Writings on the Order (`dissent-from-denial: 0.18`)

Conditional on a denial of certiorari, there is a meaningful (~18%) probability that one or more Justices will author a dissent from, or statement respecting, the denial of certiorari. In *Young I*, 94 F.4th 1242, 1251 (10th Cir. 2024), the Tenth Circuit panel itself remarked that the mandatory training's racial generalizations were "troubling on many levels" and "well on the way to arriving at objectively and subjectively harassing messaging," and the petition is supported by a 20-state amicus coalition led by Montana. Justice Thomas (`0.10`), Justice Alito (`0.08`), or Justice Gorsuch (`0.04`, from the Tenth Circuit) are the likeliest authors of a separate writing criticizing workplace DEI mandates or flagging the Title VII hostile-work-environment standard for a future case with a cleaner factual record.

## Conditional Merits Outlook

If the Court were to grant certiorari, it would likely reaffirm that Title VII draws no distinction between majority-group and minority-group plaintiffs (*Ames*, 605 U.S. at 309) and clarify that official, mandatory employer training materials carrying explicit racial stereotypes are weighed as formal employer policy under *Harris*'s totality-of-the-circumstances test, while leaving intact the high bar under *Faragher* for establishing a hostile work environment from a single, non-recurring instructional session.
