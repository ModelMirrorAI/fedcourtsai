## 3. Downward Pressures and Why Certiorari Remains Unlikely (`P(grant) = 0.12`)

A careful examination of the provisioned filings (`record/documents/petition.txt`, `questions-presented.txt`, `decision-below.txt`, `appendix.txt`, and `lower-court-docket.txt`) and circuit precedent retrieved via CourtListener and the corpus shows strong reasons why four Justices are unlikely to vote to grant once Colorado files its Brief in Opposition:

1. **Absence of a Genuine Circuit Split:**
   - Petitioner contends (`petition.txt` at 9–17) that the Tenth Circuit departed from the Third, Fourth, Fifth, and D.C. Circuits (*Castleberry v. STI Group*, 863 F.3d 259 (3d Cir. 2017); *Boyer-Liberto v. Fontainebleau Corp.*, 786 F.3d 264 (4th Cir. 2015) (en banc); *Woods v. Cantrell*, 29 F.4th 284 (5th Cir. 2022); *Ayissi-Etoh v. Fannie Mae*, 712 F.3d 572 (D.C. Cir. 2013)) on when a single incident is "sufficiently severe" under *Harris v. Forklift Systems, Inc.*, 510 U.S. 17, 21 (1993), and *Faragher v. City of Boca Raton*, 524 U.S. 775, 788 (1998).
   - In reality, every one of those single-incident cases involved a supervisor directing the N-word or an explicit racial epithet directly at a subordinate in person, often coupled with an explicit threat of termination. Moreover, the Tenth Circuit itself explicitly recognizes that a single extremely severe incident can establish a hostile work environment (*decision-below.txt* at 6, 13–14, citing *Young I*, 94 F.4th at 1250–51, and *Sharpe-Miller v. Walmart, Inc.*, 181 F.4th 1037, 1063–64 (10th Cir. 2026)).
   - Nor is there any split among the circuits on DEI trainings under Title VII. In *Chislett v. New York City Department of Education*, 157 F.4th 172, 191 (2d Cir. 2025), and in *Diemert v. City of Seattle*, No. 25-1188 (9th Cir. Sept. 28, 2026) (published opinion retrieved via CourtListener MCP, `opinion_id: 11452678`), the Second and Ninth Circuits explicitly stated that implicit-bias and race-conscious trainings are *not* per se unlawful under Title VII; both courts permitted hostile-work-environment claims to survive summary judgment only because the plaintiffs presented evidence of **numerous, pervasive incidents over years** that spilled over into workplace confrontations, harassment by supervisors/subordinates, and loss of duties. Indeed, the Ninth Circuit in *Diemert* (slip op. at 21) expressly distinguished cases involving only "one or two racially offensive incidents." Meanwhile, the Seventh Circuit in *Spengler v. Cooperative Educational Service Agency 7*, 180 F.4th 1015, 1017, 1019–20 (7th Cir. 2026), rejected Title VII claims where an employer required all employees to adopt an "equity mindset" and confront implicit biases. Thus, every circuit to address the question agrees that a single mandatory training session without workplace spillover or individualized harassment does not satisfy *Harris*.
2. **Severe Vehicle Defects (*Young I* Preclusion / Law-of-the-Case and Fact-Bound Pleading):**
   - As `decision-below.txt` (slip op. at 2–15) and `appendix.txt` detail, this case (*Young II*, 174 F.4th 1283 (10th Cir. May 11, 2026)) is Petitioner's *second* federal lawsuit challenging the exact same March 2021 online training module after the Tenth Circuit affirmed the dismissal of his first lawsuit in *Young I*, 94 F.4th 1242 (10th Cir. 2024), from which Petitioner did not seek certiorari.
   - Because *Young I* already held that the March 2021 training materials alone did not state a plausible hostile work environment claim, the Tenth Circuit in *Young II* treated *Young I*'s assessment of the training materials as settled baseline and evaluated only whether five thin additional allegations in the new complaint bridged the gap.
   - Furthermore, the Tenth Circuit held (`decision-below.txt` at 7–11) that the training slides attached to Petitioner's own complaint contradicted his characterizations (e.g., an introductory slide explicitly stated: *"Wait! Pause! Hang on! We're NOT saying anyone needs to change their values or beliefs"*), and that during the four months between the single March 2021 online training and his July 2021 resignation, Petitioner never alleged a single racial comment, confrontation, reprimand, or adverse action in the workplace.
3. **Recent Denial in a Related Equity-Training Cert Petition:**
   - Corpus query for `No. 26-156` (*Springfield R-12 School District, et al. v. Brooke Henderson, et al.*, `scotus/9026000156`, cited in `petition.txt` at 25 n.10) confirms that on October 5, 2026, the Court denied certiorari in another high-profile case involving mandatory employer equity and anti-racism training.
