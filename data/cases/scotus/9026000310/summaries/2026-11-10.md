## In brief

A former Colorado prison employee asks the Court to revive his lawsuit claiming mandatory diversity training created a racially hostile workplace for him.

## What happened

Joshua Young, a former Colorado Department of Corrections sergeant, sued the department and two officials after taking a mandatory Equity, Diversity, and Inclusion training he says disparaged white employees and directed staff to treat people differently by race. A federal district court dismissed an earlier version of his case; the Tenth Circuit affirmed, while suggesting more detailed allegations might succeed. Young filed a new complaint with added facts, but the district court dismissed it with prejudice, and the Tenth Circuit again affirmed, holding that one training session, without repetition or further race-based incidents, could not plausibly show a hostile work environment.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked whether a single mandatory training session containing allegedly derogatory racial messaging and instructions to act differently based on race can by itself be severe enough to support a hostile-work-environment claim, even without repeated trainings or separate incidents of race-based mistreatment.

## Where it stands

The petition was distributed for the Court's November 6, 2026 conference. The day before that conference, the Court requested a response from the respondents, due November 23, 2026; the docket shows no decision yet on whether the Court will hear the case.

## What each outcome would mean

Young, the petitioner, is asking the Court to overturn the Tenth Circuit's decision, which the Colorado Department of Corrections and the other respondents won below. If the Court grants review, it would hear full arguments on whether his claim should go forward, without yet deciding who is right. If it denies review, the Tenth Circuit's dismissal of Young's lawsuit stands, ending his case. A denial would not mean the Court agrees with the lower court's reasoning, only that the case is not taken up.
