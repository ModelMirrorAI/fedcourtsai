## In brief

A former Colorado corrections officer asks the Court to revive his workplace discrimination lawsuit over a mandatory diversity training he says targeted White employees.

## What happened

Joshua Young, who is white, worked for the Colorado Department of Corrections and had to complete a mandatory training on equity, diversity and inclusion. He later sued, claiming the training's content and aftermath created a racially hostile work environment under federal employment discrimination law. Lower courts dismissed his claims twice: first without prejudice, then, after he amended his complaint, with prejudice. The Tenth Circuit affirmed the second dismissal, reasoning that a single training session, without repetition or further incidents of harassment, was not enough to plausibly allege a hostile work environment.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked whether a single mandatory training session that an employee says conveyed negative racial stereotypes and pushed race-conscious conduct can, by itself, be considered serious enough to count as workplace harassment, even without the training being repeated or accompanied by other race-related incidents.
