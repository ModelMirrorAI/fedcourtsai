## In brief

A former Colorado prison employee asks the Court to revive his lawsuit claiming mandatory diversity training created a racially hostile workplace for white employees.

## What happened

Joshua Young, a white former sergeant at the Colorado Department of Corrections, sued after attending a mandatory training on equity and racial issues that he says disparaged white employees and directed race-conscious conduct at work. Two successive complaints were dismissed by a federal district court for failing to plausibly allege a hostile work environment, and the Tenth Circuit affirmed both dismissals, reasoning that a single training session, without repeated or additional race-based harassment, was not enough. Young now asks the Supreme Court to review that second dismissal, which was issued with prejudice.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked whether a single episode of employer-required training containing negative racial generalizations and directions to act based on race can, by itself, be serious enough to count as illegal workplace harassment, even without repeated incidents or other related mistreatment.

## Where it stands

The petition was distributed for conference and the Court asked Colorado to respond; Colorado's deadline to respond was later extended, so the petition has not yet been considered on the merits at conference.

## What each outcome would mean

Young, who lost in the lower courts, is asking the Court to take up and reverse the Tenth Circuit's ruling. If the Court grants review, it would hear full arguments and decide the legal question itself, without yet deciding who wins. If it denies review, the Tenth Circuit's decision dismissing Young's case with prejudice stays in place, and Colorado's prevailing position below stands.
