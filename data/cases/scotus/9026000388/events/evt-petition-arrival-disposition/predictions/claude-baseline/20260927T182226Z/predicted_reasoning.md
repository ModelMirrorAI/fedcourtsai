# Forecast: what the Court will do with No. 26-388, *Aubol v. Andeavor Logistics, L.P.*

**State forecast from.** Docketed September 22, 2026 (paid). Zero distributions, no CVSG,
no response yet (due October 22, 2026). Petition filed September 10 after a Kavanaugh
extension. Decision below: a published, divided Eighth Circuit panel (165 F.4th 1102), Judge
Grasz dissenting on the question presented; rehearing en banc denied April 13, 2026.

**Disposition.** More likely denied than granted, but this is a genuinely live petition: I
put the chance of a grant at about one in four. If the Court takes it, it takes the single
question presented essentially as written — whether individual Indian allottees holding a
federal-law possessory right have a federal common-law trespass action — and sets it for
plenary review; the question is case-dispositive and the facts are undisputed, so no
narrowing is needed and no reformulation is likely beyond dropping the "current right of
possession" framing into *Oneida*'s own terms.

**Response and timing.** Andeavor will not waive. Expect a request for an extension of the
brief-in-opposition deadline into late November or December 2026, a substantial BIO
contesting the split (arguing the Tenth Circuit assumed rather than held, and that the Ninth
Circuit cases are jurisdictional dicta) and the vehicle (the United States' own trespass
counterclaims on the allottees' behalf in the parallel *Tesoro* APA litigation, the
administrative trespass process, and the panel's "standing" phrasing). Petitioners reply;
first distribution for a conference in January 2027 or thereabouts.

**Relists.** The petition will be distributed at least once — near certainty, the only
realistic alternative being a settlement-driven dismissal before conference, which a decade
of litigation and a still-in-place pipeline make unlikely. Conditional on reaching
conference, I expect it to be relisted at least once (roughly even odds), because it is the
kind of petition the Court examines rather than denies on first look.

**CVSG.** A meaningful but minority probability, about 35%. The United States is the trustee
holding legal title, has already filed an amicus brief below supporting the allottees, and
is itself asserting trespass counterclaims on their behalf in *Tesoro*; the Court routinely
calls for the Solicitor General's views in Indian-law cases where the government is trustee.
Cutting against a call: the government's position is already on the record, so the Court may
feel no need to ask, and the current Solicitor General has not spoken to whether the January
2024 brief still states the government's view. If a CVSG issues, it comes at the first or
second conference (January–February 2027), the Solicitor General files in late spring 2027,
and the petition is resolved by the end of June 2027 or in the October 2027 long conference.
An uninvited cert-stage amicus brief from the United States is possible but unlikely.

**Route if granted.** Plenary review, not a summary disposition. There is no intervening
Supreme Court decision to GVR against, the panel opinion is reasoned and published with a
dissent, and the question is one the Court would want briefed and argued. If granted, the
likely ground is that *Oneida I* and *Oneida II* turn on whether the possessory right derives
from federal law and is insulated from state law, not on tribal versus individual status or
aboriginal versus statutory title, and the likely result is a reversal — a coalition of the
property-rights and Indian-law wings is plausible, with Justice Gorsuch (who joined
*Nahno-Lopez* on the Tenth Circuit) a natural author.

**If denied.** A separate writing is more likely than for an ordinary paid denial but still
unlikely — about 15%. The most plausible form is a statement or dissent from denial by
Justice Gorsuch, possibly joined, framed around the right to exclude and the *Oneida* cases;
the existence of any such writing is the claim, not who writes it.
