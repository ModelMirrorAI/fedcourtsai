## Why above the anchor, but below one-half

The strongest upward adjustment is a substantial inter-circuit tension over a recurring federal remedy, reinforced by a panel dissent and the United States' supporting position below. This is more than a claim that the panel misapplied a fact-bound rule. Petition pp. 28–31 identify competing circuit treatments of individual allotment trespass actions. I checked two older authorities through CourtListener rather than accepting the petition's characterization alone. In *Nahno-Lopez v. Houser*, 625 F.3d 1279, 1282 (10th Cir. 2010), the court construed the complaint as a federal common-law trespass claim within section 345 jurisdiction. That supports a genuine difference in treatment.

But the split is less square than the petition's introduction suggests. *Davilla v. Enable Midstream Partners L.P.*, 913 F.3d 959, 965 n.2 (10th Cir. 2019), expressly leaves uncertain whether the circuit had recognized the cause of action or merely assumed it. The Eighth Circuit relies on precisely that qualification in App.23a n.7. The petition's Ninth Circuit examples also are not uniformly the same individual-allottee cause-of-action posture. These points support review-worthy tension, not an uncontested clean split. The CourtListener checks corroborated both the petition's affirmative example and the qualification against it.

Vehicle concerns materially restrain the estimate. The appended majority describes this as a second interlocutory appeal, remands for consideration of consolidation, and identifies unresolved overlapping issues in the separate Tesoro litigation. The United States has asserted claims there as trustee. App.40a–43a thus supplies a concrete reason the Court could wait, notwithstanding the petition's claim that this is an ideal vehicle. The majority also distinguishes recognizing an existing federal common-law remedy from creating a new one; a disagreement over the reach of older precedents need not compel immediate review. None of this establishes that the allottees will ultimately receive adequate relief.

The government's trust responsibility and its participation below make a CVSG materially plausible. Its lower-court position is evidence of federal interest, however, not a Supreme Court recommendation to grant. I have no provisioned BIO, and do not invent either respondents' cert arguments or a present Solicitor General recommendation. Taking the legal significance and split evidence together, then discounting for the express assumption-of-law caveat, interlocutory posture, and one-sided briefing, yields my judgmental 28%, rather than a mechanically fitted estimate.

## Other probabilities and limitations

- **Distribution increment: 0.99.** Starting from zero, this claim needs only the first distribution. It is not a 99% forecast of a true relist after initial consideration. Withdrawal or another pre-conference termination accounts for the small remainder.
- **New CVSG: 0.40.** The United States' ownership and enforcement role make this much more plausible than on an ordinary private commercial petition, but an invitation remains less likely than no invitation.
- **Summary route conditional on grant: 0.12.** No identified intervening decision supplies a routine GVR path. Resolving the disputed remedy and the scope of prior precedent would more naturally require plenary briefing. The 12% is conditional, not joint with the 28% grant probability.
- **Separate writing conditional on denial: 0.15.** The denial could attract concern about individual Indian property remedies, but a silent denial is the clear modal forecast. I do not predict individual cert votes.
- **Significance: 0.65.** Independent enforcement of trust-allotment rights could affect many landowners and holdover easements. This is a substantial specialized issue, not simply a dispute about this pipeline's damages. The petition's acreage figures are advocacy-supported context, not independently audited measurements.
