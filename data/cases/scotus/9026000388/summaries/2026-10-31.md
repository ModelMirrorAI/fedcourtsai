## In brief

Individual Indian allottees ask the Court to decide whether they can sue a pipeline company for trespass under federal common law for staying on their land without authorization.

## What happened

Sharon Aubol and dozens of other individual Indians hold federal trust allotments on the Fort Berthold Reservation in North Dakota. Andeavor Logistics has operated an oil pipeline across their allotments since 2013, after its right-of-way expired, without a new agreement. The allottees sued for trespass, breach of contract, and unjust enrichment. The district court dismissed their claims, holding they had no federal common-law cause of action for trespass because, in its view, that right belongs only to tribes asserting aboriginal title, not individual allottees with statutory trust interests. A divided Eighth Circuit panel affirmed, with one judge dissenting.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked whether individual Indians who hold a current right to possess land under federal law can sue for trespass using a trespass rule created by federal courts, rather than needing a tribe or the federal government to bring the claim on their behalf.

## Where it stands

The petition for certiorari was filed on September 10, 2026, after the Court granted an earlier extension of time. The deadline for Andeavor's response was extended to December 8, 2026, and a group of tribal organizations has filed a brief supporting the allottees. The petition has not yet been distributed for a conference.

## What each outcome would mean

The allottees, who lost in the Eighth Circuit, are asking the Court to take up the case and reverse. If the Court grants the petition, it will hear full briefing and argument and decide whether individual allottees have this trespass claim, deciding nothing yet about who wins. If the Court denies the petition, the Eighth Circuit's ruling stands, leaving the allottees unable to pursue this trespass claim in that circuit, while similar claims remain available to allottees in the Ninth and Tenth Circuits under those courts' rulings.
