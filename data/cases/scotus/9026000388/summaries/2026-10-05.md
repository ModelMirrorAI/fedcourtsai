## In brief

Individual Indian allottees ask the Court to decide whether they can sue a pipeline operator for trespass under federal common law after its right-of-way expired.

## What happened

Sharon Aubol and other individual Indians hold beneficial title to trust allotments on the Fort Berthold Reservation in North Dakota. A pipeline owned by Andeavor Logistics and related companies has crossed their land since a right-of-way expired in 2013. The allottees sued for trespass, breach of contract, and unjust enrichment. The district court dismissed their trespass claim, holding they had no cause of action under federal common law. A divided Eighth Circuit panel affirmed, reasoning that the Supreme Court's precedent recognizing such claims applied only to tribes asserting aboriginal title, not individual allottees holding statutory trust title. A dissenting judge disagreed. The full Eighth Circuit denied rehearing.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked whether individual Indians who currently have a right to possess land under federal law can sue in trespass, using a legal rule created by judges rather than Congress, to protect that right against someone occupying their land without permission.

## Where it stands

The petition for certiorari was filed on September 10, 2026, after the Court granted an extension of time to file it. A response is due October 22, 2026, and the Court has not yet acted on the petition.

## What each outcome would mean

The allottees, as petitioners, are asking the Court to take up their case. If the Court grants the petition, it would hear the case and decide whether their trespass claim can go forward, without yet deciding who wins. If the Court denies the petition, the Eighth Circuit's ruling stands, leaving the allottees without this cause of action and their trespass claim dismissed, while Andeavor's win in the lower court remains in place.
