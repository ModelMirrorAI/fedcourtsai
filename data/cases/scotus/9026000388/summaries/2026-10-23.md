## In brief

Individual Indian allottees ask the Court to decide whether they, not just tribes, can sue under federal law when a company trespasses on their land.

## What happened

Sharon Aubol and other individual Indians hold beneficial title to trust allotments on the Fort Berthold Reservation in North Dakota. Andeavor Logistics has operated an oil pipeline across their land since its right-of-way expired in 2013. The allottees sued for trespass and other claims. The district court dismissed the trespass claim, and a divided Eighth Circuit panel affirmed, holding that a federal common-law trespass claim exists for tribes asserting aboriginal title but not for individual allottees whose rights come from a federal statute. One judge dissented. The full Eighth Circuit denied rehearing.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked whether individual Indians who currently have a right to possess land under federal law can sue in trespass under federal common law, the same way the Court has said tribes can, or whether that right belongs only to tribes claiming ancestral title.

## Where it stands

The allottees filed their petition for certiorari on September 10, 2026. The Court granted Andeavor an extension, giving it until December 8, 2026 to respond. A group of tribal and advocacy organizations has filed an amicus brief supporting the petition. The petition has not yet been distributed for a conference.

## What each outcome would mean

The allottees, who lost in the Eighth Circuit, are asking the Court to take up the case and reverse that ruling. If the Court grants the petition, it will hear full briefing and argument on whether individual allottees can bring this kind of trespass claim, without yet deciding who wins. If the Court denies the petition, the Eighth Circuit's ruling stands, leaving the allottees without this cause of action in that circuit, while the Ninth and Tenth Circuits continue to allow such claims by individual allottees.
