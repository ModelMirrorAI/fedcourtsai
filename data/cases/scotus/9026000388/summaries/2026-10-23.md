## In brief

Individual Indian allottees ask the Court to decide whether they can sue a pipeline company for trespass on their land under federal common law.

## What happened

Sharon Aubol and other individual Indians hold beneficial title to trust allotments on the Fort Berthold Reservation in North Dakota. Andeavor Logistics has operated an oil pipeline across their land since a right-of-way expired in 2013, without their consent. The allottees sued for trespass and other claims. The district court dismissed the trespass claim, and a divided Eighth Circuit panel affirmed, holding that a federal common-law trespass cause of action exists for tribes asserting aboriginal title but not for individual allottees whose possessory rights come from a federal statute. One judge dissented. The full Eighth Circuit denied rehearing.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked whether individual Indians who hold a current right to possess land under federal law can sue in trespass under federal common law, the same way courts have allowed tribes to do, or whether that right to sue belongs only to tribes claiming ancestral title.

## Where it stands

The allottees filed their petition for certiorari on September 10, 2026. The Court granted Andeavor more time to respond, extending its deadline to December 8, 2026. A group of tribes and an Indian land organization have filed an amicus brief supporting the petition. The petition has not yet been distributed for a conference.

## What each outcome would mean

The allottees, as petitioners, are asking the Court to reverse the Eighth Circuit's ruling. If the Court grants the petition, it would hear the case and decide whether individual allottees have this trespass claim, without yet deciding who wins. If the Court denies the petition, the Eighth Circuit's ruling stands, leaving the allottees without a federal trespass claim against Andeavor over the pipeline on their land.
