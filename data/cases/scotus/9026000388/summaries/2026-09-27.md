## What happened

Sharon Aubol and other individual Indians hold beneficial title to trust allotments on the Fort Berthold Reservation in North Dakota. Andeavor Logistics operated a crude-oil pipeline across their allotments under a right-of-way that expired in 2013, and the pipeline has stayed in place since without a new agreement. The allottees sued Andeavor for trespass and other claims. A federal district court dismissed the case, and a divided panel of the Eighth Circuit Court of Appeals affirmed, holding that individual allottees whose possession rights come from a federal statute, rather than from tribes' historic ("aboriginal") land claims, cannot bring a trespass claim under judge-made federal law. One judge dissented. The allottees, as petitioners, then asked the Supreme Court to review that ruling; Andeavor is the respondent.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked whether individual Indians who hold a present right to possess land, granted to them under federal law, can sue in trespass under judge-made federal law to protect that right, or whether that kind of lawsuit is available only to tribes claiming rights based on historic occupation of land. The petition says other appeals courts have allowed such suits by individual allottees, creating a disagreement among circuits, and that the federal government itself has argued individual allottees should be able to sue. Andeavor's position is not included in this record.

## Where it stands
