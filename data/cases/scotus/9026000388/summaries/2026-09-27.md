## In brief

Individual Indian allottees ask the Court to decide whether they, not just tribes, can sue in federal court for trespass on their own land.

## What happened

Sharon Aubol and other individual Indians hold beneficial title to trust allotments on the Fort Berthold Reservation in North Dakota. Andeavor Logistics operated an oil pipeline across their land under a right-of-way that expired in 2013, and the pipeline has stayed in place ever since. The allottees sued for trespass and other claims. The district court dismissed the trespass claim, and a divided Eighth Circuit panel affirmed, holding that a federal common-law trespass claim exists for tribes asserting aboriginal title but not for individual allottees whose possessory rights come from a federal statute. One judge dissented. The full Eighth Circuit denied rehearing.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked whether individual Indians who currently have the right to possess land under federal law can sue in trespass under federal common law to protect that right, or whether that kind of lawsuit is available only to tribes claiming ancestral land rights.

## Where it stands

The petition for certiorari was filed on September 10, 2026, after the Court granted an extension of time to file it. A response is due October 22, 2026, and the docket does not yet show any further action.

## What each outcome would mean

The allottees, as petitioners, are asking the Court to take up the case. If the Court grants review, it would hear full arguments and decide whether individual allottees have this trespass claim, without yet deciding who wins. If the Court denies review, the Eighth Circuit's ruling stands, leaving these allottees and others in that circuit unable to bring federal trespass suits on their own to protect their allotments, while allottees in the Ninth and Tenth Circuits, which allow such suits, would remain unaffected.
