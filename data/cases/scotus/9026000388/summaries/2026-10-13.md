## In brief

Individual Indian allottees ask the Court to decide whether they can sue a pipeline company for trespass on their land under federal law.

## What happened

Sharon Aubol and other individual Indians hold beneficial title to trust land allotments on the Fort Berthold Reservation in North Dakota. A pipeline owned by Andeavor Logistics and related companies has crossed their land since its right-of-way expired in 2013, without their consent. The allottees sued for trespass and other claims. The district court dismissed their trespass claim, and the Eighth Circuit affirmed, over a dissent, reasoning that a federal trespass claim exists only for tribes asserting rights based on long-standing historical occupancy, not for individual allottees whose rights come from a federal statute. The allottees sought rehearing, which was denied.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked whether individual Indian landowners whose right to possess their land comes from federal law can bring a trespass claim under federal common law, a body of law courts develop themselves rather than one written by a legislature, or whether only tribes may do so.

## Where it stands

The allottees filed their petition asking the Court to hear the case on September 10, 2026. Andeavor's response, originally due October 22, 2026, has been extended to December 8, 2026. The petition has not yet been distributed for a conference.

## What each outcome would mean

The allottees, as petitioners, are asking the Court to take up their case. If the Court grants the petition, it will hear full arguments and decide whether they have a trespass claim, without yet deciding who wins. If the Court denies the petition, the Eighth Circuit's ruling stands, leaving the allottees without this trespass claim against Andeavor, though other claims or proceedings involving the government as trustee may continue separately.
