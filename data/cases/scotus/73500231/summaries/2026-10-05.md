## In brief

The Court declined to hear a former VA employee's bid to revive her workplace discrimination case, which lower courts had dismissed as barred by an earlier ruling against her.

## What happened

Tatyana Drevaleva, a former probationary employee at a New Mexico VA hospital, lost an earlier discrimination lawsuit over her 2017 termination after taking leave to travel to Russia. After that case ended, she filed a new suit in the District of Columbia challenging a Merit Systems Protection Board decision in her case. The district court dismissed it, ruling it was barred because the same issues had already been decided against her. The D.C. Circuit affirmed, and later denied rehearing.

## What the Court is being asked

Drevaleva asked the Court to decide whether a prior dismissal can block a later lawsuit when, she says, the earlier court never fully examined the facts and legal rules involved, and whether courts must consider newly discovered facts before applying that bar. She also raised questions about whether the agency reviewing her firing had authority over her case and whether courts were required to reexamine the facts of her termination independently.

## Where it stands

The government waived its right to respond, and the petition was distributed for the Court's September 28, 2026 conference. A related emergency application for an injunction was denied by the Chief Justice on July 16, 2026. The Court denied the petition on October 5, 2026.

## What each outcome would mean

Drevaleva, as petitioner, asked the Court to take up her case. With the petition denied, the D.C. Circuit's ruling stands, meaning her lawsuit remains dismissed and the earlier decision against her continues to bar her claims. Had the Court granted the petition instead, it would have agreed to hear the case and decide the legal questions itself, deciding nothing about who was right merely by taking it up. The earlier denial of her emergency injunction request left her without court-ordered reinstatement while the case proceeded, a separate, temporary matter now closed along with the underlying case.
