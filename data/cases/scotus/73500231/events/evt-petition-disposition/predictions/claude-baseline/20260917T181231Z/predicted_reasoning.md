# Forecast: what the Court will do with No. 25-1328, Drevaleva v. United States

**Disposition.** The petition will be denied on the first order list after the
September 28, 2026 Conference, i.e. the Monday, October 5, 2026 order list, in a
one-line denial with no separate writing.

**Relists.** The docket shows one distribution (June 24, 2026, for the
September 28 Conference) and no relist. I expect no further distribution: the
Court disposes of a pro se, response-waived petition of this shape at its first
conference. The only realistic path to a second distribution entry is a
reschedule off the long conference, which happens to a small fraction of the
summer pile and signals nothing about the merits.

**CVSG.** None. The United States is the respondent and the Solicitor General
has already waived a response, so there is no third-party federal view to call
for. If any Justice took an interest, the Court's instrument would be a call for
a response from the SG, not a CVSG, and I do not expect that either: the
petition sat through the whole summer distribution window without one.

**Which question, if taken.** None as written. The petition states eight
questions, most of them personal to the petitioner ("my Appeal No.
DE-0752-19-0097-1-1"), and the two that gesture at a legal question (whether
Lawlor v. National Screen Service and Parr v. United States define "finality"
for claim-preclusion purposes such that a with-prejudice dismissal is not a
judgment on the merits) misread both cases and identify no circuit conflict. A
grant would require the Court to reformulate the question from scratch, which it
does not do for a vehicle like this.

**Summary disposition versus plenary review.** Conditional on any grant, the
route would be a GVR rather than argument: plenary review of an unpublished D.C.
Circuit res judicata order in a pro se federal-employment case is not a live
possibility. But no intervening decision of this Court bears on claim
preclusion after a sanctions dismissal, so there is nothing to GVR in light of,
and the conditional is priced on a grant I do not expect to occur.

**Dissent from denial.** None. No Justice has written on the finality prong of
claim preclusion in this posture, the injunction application in the linked
matter 26A37 was denied by the Chief Justice alone without referral, and the
questions are fact-bound. Expect a bare denial.
