# Rationale

I assign a 1.5% probability to an unqualified grant and predict denial. This is a forward-mode, arrival-moment forecast from the snapshot dated August 31, 2026. The snapshot shows an August 11 application for injunctive relief submitted to Justice Thomas, linked with No. 26-5339, but it does not describe the requested injunction or the underlying legal dispute. The frozen context records no response request, no referral to the full Court, and no amicus briefs.

The committed interim statpack provides a strictly-prior-Term scoring baseline: OT2024 and OT2025 together contain 31 grants among 296 resolved substantive applications, or 10.47%, clearing the 50-case floor. That denominator is selected for machine-matchable disposition text; it treats withdrawals and dismissals as ungranted and collapses mixed partial relief to denial. Its parse coverage is sharply uneven (OT2024 has 977 unparsed applications while OT2025 has none), and the predicted reserve is systematically selected higher on the escalation ladder than the pooled substantive cohort. The figure is therefore the registered scoring anchor, not a conditional estimate transferable to this selected arrival-stage cell; skill against it alone would not establish forecast skill.

I adjust far below that anchor. The applicant appears to be acting without identified counsel, the caption seeks relief involving the district court itself, and none of the escalation signals has fired. The application had also been dated twenty days before the snapshot without a response request or referral appearing. Those features make an extraordinary unqualified grant substantially less likely than in the pooled substantive-application cohort. The statpack does not publish grant rates conditioned on the escalation ladder, so this adjustment is judgmental rather than a direct conditional estimate.

The increment probabilities are 2.5% for a later response request, 10% for later referral to the full Court, and 0.5% for any increase above the current zero amicus briefs. The prior-Term statpack's terminal shares show that these rungs occur in the broader cohort, but they are right-censored, last-poll descriptive counts rather than as-at-arrival probabilities and therefore are not treated as direct anchors.

The principal limitation is missing case-specific material. No `record/documents/` content or `documents.json` was provisioned even though the snapshot links a main PDF. CourtListener searches did not locate the Eleventh Circuit No. 25-12952 record, so I could not assess likelihood of success, irreparable harm, equities, or the precise requested relief. Other same-name search results were not treated as linked to this application. That missing merits record warrants discounting confidence in the exact percentage, although the denial direction remains comparatively strong.
