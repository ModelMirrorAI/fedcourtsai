# Forecast — Application 26A256, Perkins v. U.S. District Court for the N.D. of Georgia

**Disposition.** The application for injunctive relief will be **denied**. The
likeliest form is a one-line denial entered by Justice Thomas acting alone as
Circuit Justice — "Application (26A256) for injunctive relief denied by Justice
Thomas" — with no opinion, no noted dissent, and no separate writing.

**Whether a response will be called for.** No. The record shows no response
requested as of the 2026-08-29 snapshot, and I expect none to be requested
before disposition: the Court calls for responses on applications where relief
is a live possibility, and an application by a self-represented litigant to
enjoin the district court presiding over her own civil cases presents none.
The named respondent — a federal court — will not be asked to answer.

**Whether the application will be referred to the full Court.** Probably not.
A first application of this kind is ordinarily disposed of by the Circuit
Justice alone; referral is the exception, reserved mostly for applications
with some substance or for renewed applications, which Rule 22.4 practice
sends to the full Court. If Justice Thomas does refer it, the full Court
denies it in one line on an order list. I put the chance of a referral before
disposition at roughly 10%.

**Amicus participation.** None. The record shows zero amicus briefs and I
expect zero at disposition — there is no constituency for this dispute beyond
the applicant.

**Timing** (context, not a scored claim). The application was submitted to
Justice Thomas on August 11, 2026 and docketed August 25. Applications of this
shape are usually acted on within one to four weeks of docketing; I expect a
denial by late September 2026, likely sooner.

**Knock-on.** The linked IFP petition 26-5339 proceeds on the ordinary cert
track regardless; nothing in this application's disposition will change that
docket. Should the Court deny, I expect no statement or dissent from any
Justice.
