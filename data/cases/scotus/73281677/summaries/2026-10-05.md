## In brief

The Court declined to hear an Air Force officer's bid to let military appeals courts review a post-conviction firearms ban attached to his court-martial judgment.

## What happened

Parker Myslow, an Air Force second lieutenant, pleaded guilty at a court-martial to drug-related offenses and was sentenced to dismissal from service. Attached to his judgment was a document called a "First Indorsement," which stated that his convictions triggered a federal law barring him from possessing firearms. Myslow argued military appellate courts could review and correct that firearms determination, including an argument that it violated his Second Amendment rights. The Air Force Court of Criminal Appeals, following a prior ruling in another case, held that military appeals courts lack authority to review the indorsement because it is part of the judgment itself, not a later error in processing. The Court of Appeals for the Armed Forces denied further review, leaving that ruling in place.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked whether a law giving military appeals courts power to fix errors occurring in a court-martial's processing after judgment also covers a firearms-ban notice attached to that same judgment, or whether that notice is instead considered part of the judgment and therefore outside the appeals courts' power to review.

## Where it stands

The petition was distributed for the Court's conference on September 28, 2026. On October 5, 2026, the Court denied the petition.

## What each outcome would mean

Myslow asked the Court to overturn the ruling that military appeals courts cannot review his firearms ban. The United States, representing the government, opposed review, arguing the lower courts were correct and that other avenues exist to challenge or correct the firearms restriction. Because the Court denied the petition, the ruling that military appellate courts lack authority to review the firearms notice stands, and Myslow's case ends without that review. The denial does not mean the Court agreed the lower ruling was correct, only that it left it in place.
