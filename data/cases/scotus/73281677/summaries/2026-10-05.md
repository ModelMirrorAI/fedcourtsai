## In brief

The Court declined to hear an Air Force officer's bid to have military appeals courts review a firearms ban attached to his court-martial judgment.

## What happened

Parker Myslow, an Air Force officer, pleaded guilty at a court-martial to drug-related offenses and was sentenced to dismissal from service. His judgment included an attached document noting that his conviction triggered a federal law barring him from possessing firearms. Myslow argued this notation was wrong and asked the Air Force Court of Criminal Appeals to review and correct it. That court, following a prior ruling by the Court of Appeals for the Armed Forces, held that military appeals courts lack authority to review that kind of notation. The Court of Appeals for the Armed Forces denied further review, leaving the ruling in place.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked whether military appeals courts have authority under a specific military justice law to fix a firearms restriction noted in a document attached after a court-martial judgment is finalized, when that restriction is challenged as unconstitutional.

## Where it stands

The petition was distributed for conference, considered, and denied; the Court has taken no further action on the case.

## What each outcome would mean

Myslow, the service member, asked the Court to take up his case. Because the Court denied the petition, the ruling of the Court of Appeals for the Armed Forces stands: military appeals courts still lack authority to review or correct this kind of firearms-restriction notation on a court-martial judgment. Myslow's conviction and the attached firearms restriction remain as they were, and the denial does not reflect any decision by the Court on whether the restriction itself was constitutional or whether the lower court's reasoning was correct.
