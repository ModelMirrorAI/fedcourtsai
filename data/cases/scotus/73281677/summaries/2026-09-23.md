## What happened

Parker Myslow, an Air Force servicemember, pleaded guilty at a court-martial to drug offenses and was sentenced to dismissal from the service. His case file included a document, added after the judge signed the final judgment, stating that his conviction triggered a federal ban on possessing firearms. Myslow argued this firearms flag was wrong and that the Air Force Court of Criminal Appeals should be able to fix it. That court, and then the Court of Appeals for the Armed Forces, ruled that military appeals courts have no power to review that firearms notation at all, relying on an earlier ruling in a similar case. Myslow then asked the Supreme Court to take up his case.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked whether military appeals courts have the power, under the law governing corrections to court-martial proceedings after judgment is finalized, to review and fix a firearms-possession restriction that is noted in paperwork attached to that judgment. Myslow argues that this paperwork is separate from the judgment and can be corrected as a processing error; the government responds that the paperwork is part of the judgment itself and therefore outside that reviewing power, and adds that the Air Force has since changed its practice of attaching such paperwork.
