## In brief

The Court declined to hear a challenge by businesses and advocacy groups to the Corporate Transparency Act's ownership-reporting requirement.

## What happened

The Corporate Transparency Act requires millions of businesses created under state law to report information about their owners to a federal agency. Texas Top Cop Shop and several other businesses and organizations sued, arguing the law was unconstitutional. A federal district court agreed and blocked the law nationwide, but the Supreme Court later paused that order while the case continued. Meanwhile, a federal appeals court in another circuit upheld the same law in a different case. With the appeal still pending before the Fifth Circuit, the businesses asked the Supreme Court to take up their case directly, before the appeals court ruled, so it could resolve the dispute together with the other pending case.

## What the Court is being asked

The petitioners asked the Court to decide two things: whether Congress has power to require reporting simply because a business exists, without regard to any commercial activity it conducts, and whether demanding this ownership information, without any individual suspicion of wrongdoing or advance court review, violates protections against unreasonable searches.

## Where it stands

The petition was distributed for conference twice, in June and September 2026, and the Court denied it on October 5, 2026.

## What each outcome would mean

Texas Top Cop Shop and the other businesses asked the Court to take up their challenge early, before the appeals court ruled below. By denying the petition, the Court leaves that appeal to proceed in the Fifth Circuit rather than deciding the reporting law's constitutionality itself now. The denial does not mean the Court agreed the law is valid; it simply declines to intervene at this stage, leaving the question for the lower courts to continue addressing.
