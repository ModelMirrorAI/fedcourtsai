## In brief

Companies and a political party ask the Court to strike down a federal law requiring corporations to disclose their owners' information to the government.

## What happened

The Corporate Transparency Act requires millions of corporations, LLCs, and similar entities to report information about their owners to a federal financial-crimes agency, regardless of what the company does. Texas Top Cop Shop and several other entities sued, arguing the law exceeds Congress's power and violates constitutional privacy protections. A federal district court agreed and blocked the law nationwide; the Fifth Circuit's appeal was later put on hold while a related dispute proceeded elsewhere. Meanwhile, the Eleventh Circuit upheld the same law in a different case, creating a conflict between courts. The petitioners now ask the Supreme Court to take up their case directly, before the Fifth Circuit rules, alongside that other case.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked whether Congress had the constitutional authority to require corporations to report ownership information simply because the corporations exist under state law, even without any showing that the company engages in commercial activity. It is also asked whether requiring companies to disclose private ownership information to federal law enforcement, without individualized suspicion or a chance to challenge the demand beforehand, violates constitutional protections against unreasonable searches.

## Where it stands

The petition was distributed for a June 2026 conference, then rescheduled, and was later distributed again for a September 28, 2026 conference; the government has waived its right to respond. The docket shows no decision yet on whether the Court will take the case.

## What each outcome would mean

Texas Top Cop Shop and the other entities are asking the Court to take up their case now, before the appeals court rules. If the Court grants the request, it would hear the case in full alongside the related dispute and decide whether the law is constitutional, without yet deciding who wins. If the Court denies the request, the case would continue in the Fifth Circuit, which has not yet ruled, leaving the law's status undecided for now.
