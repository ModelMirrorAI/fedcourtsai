## What happened

Congress's Corporate Transparency Act requires most corporations, LLCs, and similar entities created under state law to report information about their owners to a federal agency, FinCEN, with civil and criminal penalties for noncompliance. Texas Top Cop Shop and several other businesses and organizations sued, arguing the law is unconstitutional. A federal district court agreed and blocked the law nationwide; the Supreme Court later paused that injunction while the case continued. The appeal has been on hold at the Fifth Circuit pending further agency rulemaking. Meanwhile, the Eleventh Circuit, in a separate case, upheld the same law against similar challenges, creating a disagreement between courts. The petitioners now ask the Supreme Court to take up their case before the Fifth Circuit rules, alongside the other pending case.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked whether Congress had the constitutional authority to require companies to report ownership information simply because they were formed under state law, without regard to whether they actually engage in any business activity. The Court is also asked whether requiring companies to disclose private ownership information to federal law enforcement, without any individual suspicion of wrongdoing and without a chance to challenge the request beforehand, improperly intrudes on protections against unreasonable searches.

## Where it stands
