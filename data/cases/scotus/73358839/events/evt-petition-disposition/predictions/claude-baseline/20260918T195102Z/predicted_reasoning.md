# Forecast: what the Court will do with No. 25-1290

**Disposition.** The Court will **deny** the petition for certiorari before judgment, most likely on the order list following the September 28, 2026 conference or shortly after. The petition rises or falls with its companion, *National Small Business United v. Bessent*, No. 25-1201, and I expect that petition to be denied too: FinCEN's August 2026 final rule has exempted every domestic entity from the Corporate Transparency Act's reporting mandate, and the Solicitor General's brief in opposition in 25-1201 urges denial on that ground. The Court will not reach out, on a cert-before-judgment petition from an appeal the Fifth Circuit is holding in abeyance, to decide the constitutionality of a mandate the executive no longer applies to any of the petitioners.

**Relists.** The docket shows two distribution entries: a June 2 distribution for the June 18 conference that was rescheduled on June 5 before the case was ever considered, and a September 9 redistribution for the September 28 conference. So the petition has **never been relisted** in the true sense; it sits at its first conference, paired with 25-1201. I forecast a modest chance (about 40%) of one further distribution, for one of two reasons: a Justice preparing a statement or dissent respecting the denial (Justice Gorsuch said in January 2025 that he would have granted certiorari before judgment in this very case), or the Court holding both petitions over one conference while it settles what to do with the companion. If it relists at all, I expect a single relist, not a series.

**CVSG.** None. The Solicitor General is counsel for the respondents and has already stated the government's position in the companion case, so there is no one to invite.

**Question presented, if granted.** In the unlikely event of a grant, it would come only as a companion to a grant in 25-1201, and the Court would take Question 1 (the Commerce Clause question) as framed by the NSBU petition, with this case consolidated for argument. Question 2 (the Fourth Amendment challenge to the reporting mandate) would most likely be left behind or subsumed. The Court would not grant this petition on its own.

**Summary disposition.** Very unlikely. Petitioners prevailed in the district court, so there is no adverse judgment to vacate and remand, and no intervening decision to GVR against. A grant would be plenary and consolidated; a non-grant would be a plain denial rather than a dismissal.

**Separate writing on denial.** A real but minority possibility (about 30%). Justice Gorsuch is on record wanting the Court to resolve the CTA's validity definitively, and Justice Thomas has long pressed for enforcing Commerce Clause limits. The most likely form is a statement respecting the denial noting that the constitutional question remains open and that the exemption rests on executive grace, rather than a dissent arguing the Court should have granted a petition about a mandate that no longer applies to the petitioners. If the Court denies the companion 25-1201 with a separate writing, the same writing will most likely cover 25-1290 by reference.
