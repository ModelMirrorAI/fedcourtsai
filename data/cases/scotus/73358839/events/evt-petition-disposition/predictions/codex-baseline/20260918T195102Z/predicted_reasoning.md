# Forecast of the Court's action

The Court will most likely **deny the petition for certiorari before judgment**, without a separate writing. P(any grant), including partial or summary relief, is **8%**. The most likely timing is an order following the scheduled September 28, 2026 conference, in late September or early October; a companion-related hold is the principal longer-tail alternative.

The baseline contains **two distribution entries**, but the first was followed by a rescheduling entry before its scheduled conference. I forecast **no further distribution** as the modal path, with **25% probability of at least one more** beyond the count of two. If there is an increment, one or two further distributions are more plausible than an extended sequence, except in the companion-hold scenario.

The Court will most likely **not call for the Solicitor General's views**: P(a new CVSG) is **0.5%**. The federal government already appears as respondent and has waived its response. If the Court seeks the government's position, a request for a response rather than a CVSG is the more likely mechanism.

The likely reason to leave this petition ungranted is its procedural posture: it asks the Court to bypass an unfinished appeal while rulemaking affects the reporting obligations, and another petition described in the record already presents the constitutional questions after an appellate decision. Denial would leave those issues unresolved by this order, not endorse the Act's constitutionality.

**If review is granted**, the strongest candidate is the first question: whether the Corporate Transparency Act's reporting duties for entities formed under state law exceed the commerce power when untethered to commercial activity. I expect review to focus on that question, potentially alongside NSBU, with the Fourth Amendment reporting/search question secondary rather than necessarily granted. That is a conditional forecast, not a claim that companion review has already been ordered.

Conditional on a grant, **plenary treatment is more likely than a summary route**: **85%** versus **15%**. A summary remand would be most plausible only after a relevant companion ruling or another procedural development, neither of which is established by the provided record. I do not predict an immediate summary declaration that the Act is unconstitutional.

Conditional on denial, I assign **8%** to a noted dissent from or statement respecting denial and **92%** to no such writing. I forecast no particular Justice's cert vote. The modal outcome is a short denial order without merits reasoning.
