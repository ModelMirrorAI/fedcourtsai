## In brief

Texas parents ask the Court to revive their challenge to a law requiring every public school classroom to display the Ten Commandments.

## What happened

Texas enacted a law requiring public schools to post a specific version of the Ten Commandments in every classroom. Parents from several faiths sued, arguing the displays violate the First Amendment's protections against government establishment of religion and for free exercise of religion. In one case, a federal district court blocked the law, but the full Fifth Circuit Court of Appeals reversed, ruling the displays do not violate either protection. In a related case, a Fifth Circuit panel summarily reversed a similar injunction based on that ruling. The parents now ask the Supreme Court to review both decisions together.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked whether requiring public schools to permanently display a government-chosen version of the Ten Commandments in every classroom amounts to a prohibited government endorsement of religion, and separately, whether the displays interfere with parents' right to guide their children's religious upbringing. A related question is whether the appeals court was wrong to treat an older Supreme Court decision striking down a similar classroom display law as no longer binding.

## Where it stands

The petition has been fully briefed, including a brief opposing review, amicus briefs on both sides, and a reply from the parents. The Court distributed the case for its December 11, 2026 conference, where it will decide whether to take up the case.

## What each outcome would mean

The parents, who lost before the full Fifth Circuit, are asking the Court to take the case and reverse that ruling. If the Court grants review, it will decide the constitutional questions after full briefing and argument; granting review alone would not indicate who wins. If the Court denies review, the Fifth Circuit's decision upholding the law stands, and the displays may remain in classrooms covered by that ruling. The parents have also asked, as an alternative to full review, that the Court summarily reverse the lower court or send the case back for reconsideration without deciding the merits itself.
