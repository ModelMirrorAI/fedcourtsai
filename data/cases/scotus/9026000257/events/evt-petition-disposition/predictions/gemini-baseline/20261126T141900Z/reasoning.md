## 3. Upward vs. Downward Adjustments to `P(grant) = 0.42`

### Strong Upward Forces (pushing far above the 5.2% / 16.3% / 22.7% baselines)
1. **Explicit En Banc Defiance of Vertical Stare Decisis (*Stone v. Graham*):** Unlike cases that distinguish an older Supreme Court precedent on its facts, the 9–8 en banc Fifth Circuit expressly treated *Stone v. Graham*, 449 U.S. 39 (1980)—which struck down Kentucky's nearly identical classroom Ten Commandments statute—as no longer good law after *Kennedy v. Bremerton School District*, 597 U.S. 507 (2022), and rendered final judgment dismissing Petitioners' claims. Respondents' BIO (at 18) candidly agrees that *Stone* cannot be reconciled on its reasoning and urges the Court, if it grants review, to expressly overrule *Stone*. Under *Rodriguez de Quijas v. Shearson/American Express, Inc.*, 490 U.S. 477, 484 (1989), *Agostini v. Felton*, 521 U.S. 203, 237 (1997), and *Mallory v. Norfolk Southern Ry. Co.*, 600 U.S. 122, 136 (2023), lower courts lack authority to deem directly applicable Supreme Court precedents implicitly overruled. Letting an en banc circuit court strip a still-standing Supreme Court First Amendment protection from ~5.5 million Texas public-school children is an acute institutional provocation.
2. **Synchronized Supreme Court Docket Handling with *Brumley v. Roake* (No. 25-1360):** Corpus and official SCOTUS docket checks show that after the en banc Fifth Circuit dismissed challenges to Louisiana's classroom Ten Commandments statute as unripe in *Roake v. Brumley*, 170 F.4th 292 (5th Cir. 2026), Louisiana filed a *conditional* certiorari petition (No. 25-1360) on May 21, 2026. Although respondents there waived a response on July 6, **the Supreme Court requested a response on July 28, 2026**. Then, on **October 20, 2026**, the Clerk/Court granted Texas's second BIO extension in *Nathan* **only in part** (to November 10 rather than November 18), keeping *Nathan* inside the December 11 Conference window for OT2026 oral argument. Finally, on **November 5, 2026**, the Court rescheduled *Brumley* from the December 4 Conference to the **December 11, 2026 Conference** (`distribution_count = 3`) so *Nathan* and *Brumley* are considered side-by-side. That sequence proves active chamber attention to the pair.
3. **National Proliferation, Doctrinal Tension (*Kennedy* + *Mahmoud*/*Mirabelli*), and Elite Advocacy:** Nearly a dozen states have enacted classroom Ten Commandments statutes, and Petitioners pair their Establishment Clause claim with a substantial post-*Mahmoud v. Taylor*, 606 U.S. 522 (2025) / *Mirabelli v. Bonta*, 607 U.S. 492 (2026) Free Exercise claim, backed by prominent cross-ideological religious-liberty scholars (Laycock, Berg, Storslee, Meiser, Lund, Esbeck).

### Serious Countervailing Forces (keeping `P(grant)` below 0.50 at `0.42`)
1. **Absence of a Circuit Split, With an Eighth Circuit Vehicle Already Argued (*Stinson*):** No other circuit or state high court has yet ruled on classroom Ten Commandments displays post-*Kennedy*. Critically, the Eighth Circuit heard oral argument on September 22, 2026 in *Stinson v. Fayetteville School District No. 1* (No. 26-1722), reviewing a **final permanent injunction** against Arkansas's statute (CourtListener confirms no Eighth Circuit opinion has issued as of November 26, 2026). If the Eighth Circuit follows *Rodriguez de Quijas* and *Stone* within the next few months, the Court will immediately face a clean, post-final-judgment circuit split for OT2027. Institutionalist swing Justices (Roberts, Kavanaugh, Barrett) have strong reasons to wait one Term for that split rather than rush into a single-circuit review.
2. **The November 3, 2026 Texas Attorney General Election & Adverseness Cloud:** Notice the crucial chronology: both the July 28 *Response Requested* in *Brumley* and the October 20 partial BIO extension in *Nathan* occurred **before** the November 3, 2026 election upset in which Democrat Nathan Johnson won the Texas Attorney General race after publicly pledging (*"I will not defend the legislature's passage of a requirement that schools place the Ten Commandments in classrooms"*). Outgoing Texas SG William Peterson prominently warns the Court in the November 10 BIO (pp. 3, 29, 32–33) that the new AG takes office in **January 2027**—right when merits briefing would begin if cert were granted—threatening state-level adversarial defense. While Petitioners rightly counter (Reply 8–9) that the 24 named Respondents are independent school districts capable of retaining private counsel (and the Court also has Louisiana's AG right beside it in *Brumley*, No. 25-1360, albeit behind a ripeness hurdle), conservative and institutionalist Justices alike are understandably reluctant to grant a blockbuster vehicle whose lead state counsel warns of an impending representation rupture four weeks later when Arkansas's *Stinson* case has none of those defects.
3. **Rule-of-Four Asymmetry & Standing Split:** Because Texas **won** below, the six conservative Justices face no urgency to intervene now—especially over outgoing Texas SG Peterson's explicit plea to wait for *Stinson*. Meanwhile, for Justices Sotomayor, Kagan, and Jackson, even if all three vote to grant (because *Stone* is already dead across the Fifth Circuit and Petitioners have a credible path to five merits votes on compulsory K–12 classroom displays), they still require a **fourth vote** from Chief Justice Roberts, Justice Barrett, or Justice Kavanaugh, each of whom must weigh vertical-stare-decisis enforcement against pre-enforcement posture (3 majority judges below rejected standing) and the imminent January 2027 Texas AG transition.
