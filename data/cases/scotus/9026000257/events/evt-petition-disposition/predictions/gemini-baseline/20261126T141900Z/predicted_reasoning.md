The petition currently sits at a single distribution—distributed on November 24, 2026, for the Conference of December 11, 2026, where it is scheduled alongside Louisiana's rescheduled conditional certiorari petition in *Brumley v. Roake* (No. 25-1360). Expect the petition to be **relisted at least once** beyond this initial distribution, most likely drawing one to three additional conferences into January 2027 as the Justices weigh immediate plenary review against awaiting impending sister-circuit action in the Eighth Circuit (*Stinson v. Fayetteville School District No. 1*, argued September 22, 2026), evaluate the representation posture following Texas's November 2026 Attorney General transition, and potentially circulate separate writings. Ultimately, the Court will most likely **deny certiorari** at this juncture, leaving the constitutional question to return once a square circuit conflict and cleaner state-level adversarial posture materialize, though a plenary grant this Term remains a close alternative.

The Court will **not call for the views of the Solicitor General (CVSG)**. The petition presents a pure First and Fourteenth Amendment challenge to a state public-school statute (Texas S.B. 10) implicating no federal statute, agency program, or federal spending condition, and the United States may participate as amicus curiae as of right at the merits stage if certiorari is granted.

**If the Court grants review**, it will set the case for **plenary briefing and oral argument** rather than disposing of it summarily in the certiorari order. A summary reversal or grant, vacatur, and remand (GVR) instructing the Fifth Circuit to apply *Stone v. Graham*, 449 U.S. 39 (1980), under *Rodriguez de Quijas v. Shearson/American Express, Inc.*, 490 U.S. 477 (1989), is unlikely: five Justices will not summarily enforce a 1980 *Lemon*-era *per curiam* ruling without merits argument—particularly where an incoming Texas Attorney General who has stated he will not defend S.B. 10 takes office in January 2027 and might decline to seek further review afterward. If granted for plenary review, the Court will likely take **both Questions Presented** (potentially neutrally reformulated) so it can resolve both (1) whether *Stone v. Graham* remains good law or whether permanent, state-mandated Ten Commandments displays in compulsory K–12 classrooms violate the Establishment Clause under *Kennedy v. Bremerton School District*, 597 U.S. 507 (2022), and (2) whether non-opt-outable scriptural classroom displays unconstitutionally burden parents' and children's Free Exercise rights under *Mahmoud v. Taylor*, 606 U.S. 522 (2025), after confirming Article III standing under *School District of Abington Township v. Schempp*, 374 U.S. 203 (1963). If plenary review is granted, the Court would either hold *Brumley v. Roake* (No. 25-1360) or grant it alongside *Nathan* to ensure uninterrupted state-level adversarial defense.

**Should the Court deny certiorari**, there is a substantial chance of a separate writing accompanying the denial order—most likely a dissent from the denial of certiorari (or statement respecting denial) authored by Justice Sotomayor (or Justice Jackson) rebuking the en banc Fifth Circuit's unilateral abrogation of *Stone v. Graham* in violation of vertical stare decisis, or alternatively a brief statement respecting denial by Justice Kavanaugh (or Justice Alito) noting pending percolation in the courts of appeals and vehicle considerations.
