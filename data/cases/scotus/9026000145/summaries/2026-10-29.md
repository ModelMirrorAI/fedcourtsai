## In brief

The federal government asks the Court to review whether a law letting people collect attorney's fees from the government applies to immigration detention habeas cases.

## What happened

Adolph Michelin and Adewumi Abioye, immigration detainees, each won habeas corpus challenges to being held without bond hearings, and each was then awarded attorney's fees from the government under the Equal Access to Justice Act. The government appealed the fee awards, arguing that habeas cases do not count as "civil actions" under that law's limited waiver of the government's immunity from such awards. The Third Circuit Court of Appeals disagreed and upheld both fee awards, joining some other appeals courts while other circuits have ruled the opposite way. The government's request to reconsider that ruling before the full Third Circuit was denied, with several judges dissenting.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked whether a federal law allowing prevailing parties to recover attorney's fees from the government in civil lawsuits also covers habeas corpus cases brought by immigration detainees challenging their detention, or whether that fee-shifting law's limited waiver of the government's immunity from such claims does not reach habeas cases at all.

## Where it stands

The petition was distributed for the Court's October 9, 2026 conference; the Court then asked Michelin and Abioye to respond by November 4, 2026, and a motion for more time to do so was later filed. The government's petition itself asks the Court to hold the case pending its decision in a related case already granted, Montoya Palacios v. Liggins.

## What each outcome would mean

The government is asking the Court to take up the case and reverse the Third Circuit's decision, which favored Michelin and Abioye. If the Court grants the petition, it would hear the case and decide whether habeas cases qualify for fee awards under this law; a grant would not itself decide who wins. If the Court denies the petition, the Third Circuit's ruling favoring Michelin and Abioye's fee awards stands. The government's own petition also asks the Court to simply hold the case until it decides the related Montoya Palacios case and then act accordingly.
