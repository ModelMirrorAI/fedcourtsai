## In brief

The federal government asks the Court to review whether a fee-shifting law covering "civil actions" also covers immigration detainees' habeas challenges to their confinement.

## What happened

Adolph Michelin and Adewumi Abioye, immigration detainees, separately won habeas corpus rulings ordering bond hearings after they argued their prolonged detention violated due process. Both then won awards of attorney's fees against the government under the Equal Access to Justice Act, a law letting prevailing parties recover fees from the government unless its position was "substantially justified." The government appealed, and the Third Circuit, consolidating the cases, affirmed both fee awards, holding that habeas challenges to immigration detention count as "civil actions" covered by the fee-shifting law, joining some circuits while other circuits have held the opposite.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked whether a federal law letting people recover attorney's fees from the government in civil lawsuits also covers habeas corpus cases brought by immigration detainees challenging their detention, or whether such fee awards are barred because the government has not clearly agreed to allow them in this type of case.

## Where it stands

The petition was distributed for a conference, after which the Court requested a response from the detainees, due December 4, 2026 after an extension; the government's own petition asks the Court to hold the case pending its decision in a separate case already granted on the same question, rather than to rule now.

## What each outcome would mean

The government, as petitioner, is asking the Court to reverse the Third Circuit's decision allowing the fee awards. If the Court grants review and ultimately rules for the government, the fee awards to Michelin and Abioye would be at risk of being overturned. If it rules for the detainees, the Third Circuit's decision allowing such fees would stand. If the Court denies review, the Third Circuit's ruling favoring the detainees remains in effect. The government's petition also requests simply holding the case until a related pending case is decided.
