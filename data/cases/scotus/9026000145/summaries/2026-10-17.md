The government asks the Court to hold its appeal of fee awards to two immigrants until it decides a related case on the same legal question.

## What happened

Adolph Michelin and Adewumi Abioye, immigrants detained during removal proceedings, each won habeas corpus cases in federal district court after arguing their prolonged detention without bond hearings violated due process. Both then sought attorney's fees from the government under the Equal Access to Justice Act, which allows fee awards against the government in civil cases unless its position was "substantially justified." The district courts granted the fees, and the Third Circuit Court of Appeals, consolidating both cases, affirmed, holding that habeas cases challenging immigration detention count as "civil actions" under the fee-shifting law. The government now asks the Supreme Court to review that ruling.

## What the Court is being asked

The question is whether a federal law letting people recover attorney's fees from the government in civil lawsuits also applies to habeas corpus cases challenging immigration detention, or whether that fee law's limited waiver of the government's immunity from such awards does not reach habeas cases at all.
