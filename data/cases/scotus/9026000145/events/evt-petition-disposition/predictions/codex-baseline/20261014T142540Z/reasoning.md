## Why this petition is different

The decisive fact is in the provisioned petition itself, at pages 5–6: it reports that certiorari was granted in **Montoya Palacios v. Liggins, No. 25-1223, on June 29, 2026**, on the same EAJA question, and expressly asks for a hold followed by an appropriate disposition. I take that pre-snapshot representation as input, not as independent verification of the lead case's current status. It makes this principally a follower-petition forecast rather than a fresh contest for an argument slot. The companion information is disclosed in flags because it is decisive; it reveals no outcome of this petition.

The Third Circuit affirmed attorney-fee awards in both consolidated cases, holding that the EAJA clearly covers immigration-detention habeas proceedings. The petition describes disagreement with the Fourth and Fifth Circuits and dissents from denial of rehearing. The provisioned appellate opinion supplies the counterargument: the breadth of the word “any,” the express tort exception, and the civil nature of immigration detention support treating these proceedings as covered civil actions. This is a substantial textual answer, not merely a policy plea. It leaves meaningful probability that a lead decision will sustain the fee awards and this petition will be denied.

For independent, preexisting legal context, I checked **Obando-Segura v. Garland, 999 F.3d 190 (4th Cir. 2021)** through CourtListener: the opinion rejects EAJA eligibility for immigration habeas and discusses the clear-waiver and hybrid-proceeding reasoning. That confirms a genuine opposing position, but a circuit disagreement does not itself prove statutory ambiguity or dictate how the Supreme Court will resolve it. My 0.66 is a judgment that an adverse or qualifying lead decision requiring reconsideration is moderately more likely than a decision leaving these awards intact. It allows for a GVR even if the ultimate entitlement to fees remains contested on remand. It is not an empirically estimated conditional rate for this exact posture.

The government's requested hold, the overlapping question, and the response request justify the large upward adjustment from the frozen baseline. The strong competing textual reading and absence of the respondents' brief keep the estimate well below certainty. The provisioned petition is marked truncated; pipeline notes identify omissions within the appendix, while the short petition body and its hold request are present. No BIO is provisioned or recorded as filed in this snapshot, so I cannot assess the respondents' vehicle or hold arguments. I do not infer acquiescence from that absence.
