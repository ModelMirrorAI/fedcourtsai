# Predicted reasoning — Warden, Moshannon Valley Correctional Center v. Michelin (No. 26-145)

**This is a hold petition.** The Solicitor General does not ask for plenary review.
The petition says the Court already granted certiorari on the same EAJA question in
*Montoya Palacios v. Liggins*, No. 25-1223 (granted June 29, 2026). It asks the Court
to hold this case until that decision and then dispose of it in light of it. The Court
will do that.

- **Distributions / relists.** The docket shows **one** distribution (for the
  October 9, 2026 conference). The Court then requested a response on October 5,
  due November 4. Expect the petition to be **redistributed** after the response
  comes in. After that, expect it to sit unacted-on through repeated conferences
  while it is held, and to come up again after *Montoya Palacios* is decided,
  most likely in June 2027. Several more distributions are expected, not just one.
- **CVSG.** None. The federal government is the petitioner, so there is no
  Solicitor General's view to invite.
- **Disposition.** This tracks the merits of *Montoya Palacios*. That case comes
  up from a decision denying EAJA fees in immigration-detention habeas, so the
  noncitizen is the petitioner there.
  - If the Court holds that EAJA's "any civil action" waiver does **not**
    clearly reach immigration-detention habeas, it will **GVR** this case. The
    Third Circuit held the opposite, so its fee awards cannot stand on its
    reasoning. I put this branch slightly ahead: about 55-58%. The
    clear-statement rule for waivers of sovereign immunity cuts this way, and so
    do *Ardestani*'s narrow reading of EAJA and the acknowledged 4th/5th Circuit
    view.
  - If the Court holds that EAJA covers these habeas petitions, it will **deny**
    the petition. The Third Circuit's judgment would then be correct.
- **Route if granted.** Almost certainly a GVR in the cert order. Plenary review
  of a second vehicle on a question already being decided is very unlikely, and
  a summary reversal outright is unlikely too. A remand still leaves the Third
  Circuit to apply the rule, and the case may raise other issues, such as
  substantial justification.
- **Question presented.** Context only: the Court would not take this case's QP
  separately. Any grant is "in light of *Montoya Palacios*".
- **Dissent from denial.** Not expected. A denial here would just follow a merits
  ruling for the noncitizens, and Justices rarely write separately on a held
  petition's routine disposition.
