We predict that the Supreme Court is slightly more likely than not (about a 58% chance) to grant the federal government's petition, vacate (set aside) the lower court's ruling, and send the case back for a new decision. This case asks whether the Equal Access to Justice Act—a federal law that requires the government to pay a winning party's attorney's fees in "any civil action"—applies to habeas corpus petitions (lawsuits challenging unlawful detention) brought by immigration detainees. Because the Supreme Court has already agreed to decide that exact question in another pending case, *Montoya Palacios v. Liggins*, the Court will almost certainly hold this petition until *Montoya Palacios* is decided and then set aside the Third Circuit's ruling if the government wins the lead case.
