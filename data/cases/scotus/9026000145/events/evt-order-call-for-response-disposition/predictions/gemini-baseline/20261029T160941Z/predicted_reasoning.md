3. **Question Presented:** The petition presents a single question: whether the Equal Access to Justice Act's limited waiver of the United States' sovereign immunity in "any civil action (other than cases sounding in tort)," 28 U.S.C. § 2412(d)(1)(A), unambiguously and unequivocally encompasses petitions for writs of habeas corpus challenging immigration detention. That is the exact threshold question already granted for plenary review in *Montoya Palacios v. Liggins*, No. 25-1223.
4. **Summary Disposition vs. Plenary Review (`summary-disposition-route`: 0.98):** The Solicitor General explicitly asks the Court to hold this petition pending *Montoya Palacios* and dispose of it in light of that decision rather than to grant plenary review. Conditional on a grant in this docket, the Court will almost certainly dispose of the petition in the cert order itself via a **grant, vacate, and remand (`gvr`)** to the Third Circuit for further consideration in light of *Montoya Palacios v. Liggins*, rather than setting No. 26-145 for separate merits briefing and oral argument.
5. **Ultimate Disposition (`predicted_disposition: gvr`, `probability`: 0.58):** If the Court holds in *Montoya Palacios* that 28 U.S.C. § 2412(d)(1)(A) does not unambiguously waive sovereign immunity for 28 U.S.C. § 2241 immigration-detention habeas petitions (affirming the Fourth Circuit in *Montoya Palacios*), it will grant this petition, vacate the Third Circuit's judgment affirming the EAJA fee awards to Michelin and Abioye, and remand (`gvr`). Conversely, if the Court reverses the Fourth Circuit in *Montoya Palacios* and holds that § 2412(d)(1)(A) unambiguously covers immigration habeas petitions, the Court will deny this petition once *Montoya Palacios* issues (because the Solicitor General did not petition on the Third Circuit's secondary case-specific "substantial justification" ruling in *Abioye*, and *Michelin* involved only the threshold sovereign-immunity issue on appeal).
6. **Separate Writings on the Disposing Order (`dissent-from-denial`: 0.04):** Whether the petition ends in a post-*Montoya Palacios* GVR order or a post-*Montoya Palacios* denial order, the Court will have just addressed the merits in signed opinions in *Montoya Palacios*. Conditional on a denial, a separate dissent or statement respecting denial is unlikely (4%), with only a small chance that Justice Thomas or Justice Alito notes a brief dissent or statement referencing their separate writing in *Montoya Palacios* or the case-specific substantial-justification record below.
