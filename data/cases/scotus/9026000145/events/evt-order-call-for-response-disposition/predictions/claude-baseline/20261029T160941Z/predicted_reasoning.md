# Predicted course — Warden, Moshannon Valley Correctional Center v. Michelin (No. 26-145)

**State forecast from:** one distribution (for the 9 October 2026 conference), then a
Response Requested order on 5 October 2026 (due 4 November 2026). No CVSG, no relist,
no response yet on the record.

**The petition will be held, not decided on its own.** The Solicitor General does not
ask for plenary review: the petition expressly asks the Court to hold it pending
*Montoya Palacios v. Liggins*, No. 25-1223 (cert granted 29 June 2026, from the Fourth
Circuit), which presents the same question — whether EAJA's "any civil action" waiver
reaches habeas petitions challenging immigration detention. Once the requested response
is in (or its time runs), the petition will be distributed again and then carried on the
held list, conference after conference, until *Montoya Palacios* is decided — most
likely in spring or early summer 2027. Expect many further distributions.

**No CVSG.** The United States is the petitioner; there is no one to invite.

**Disposition depends on the companion case.**
- If the Court in *Montoya Palacios* holds that EAJA's waiver does not unambiguously
  cover immigration-detention habeas (affirming the Fourth Circuit), the Third Circuit
  judgment here is on the wrong side, and the Court will GVR this petition — grant,
  vacate and remand for reconsideration in light of *Montoya Palacios*. A summary GVR,
  not plenary review, is the route on a grant.
- If the Court reverses the Fourth Circuit and holds habeas fees available, the Third
  Circuit is right and this petition will be denied.
- I put these close to even, leaning slightly toward denial: the Court reverses most
  judgments it reviews and took the case on the detainee's petition, against the
  pull of the clear-statement rule for sovereign-immunity waivers that favors the
  government.

**QP if taken:** the single question as written; in practice the Court decides it in
*Montoya Palacios*, not here.

**Separate writing:** unlikely either way. A held petition disposed of after the lead
case rarely draws a dissent; a dissent from a GVR by a Justice who would simply have
denied or reversed is the more plausible of the rare cases.
