# Forecast: NRCC, et al. v. Brown, et al. (26A274) — emergency stay application

**Disposition: the Court grants the stay** of the Fourth Circuit's judgment
setting aside the FCC Media Bureau's March 2026 lowest-unit-charge public
notice, pending disposition of a petition for certiorari. P(unqualified
grant) = 0.60. If the Court denies, I expect a noted dissent or a short
statement from at least one Justice rather than a bare one-line denial —
the political stakes and the government's alignment with the applicants
make a silent denial less likely than usual.

**Timing.** The response the Chief Justice requested is due at noon on
September 3, and the general-election advertising window the application is
aimed at opens September 4. The Court acts on this application fast — I
expect a disposition on September 3 or 4, and almost certainly within a week
of the response deadline. (Timing resolves against nothing; it is stated
because it disciplines the rest.)

**Referral.** The application was submitted to the Chief Justice as Circuit
Justice for the Fourth Circuit and has not yet been referred. A politically
charged application on which a response has been called for is not the kind
a Circuit Justice disposes of alone in the current practice: I expect the
Chief Justice to **refer it to the full Court** before disposition, and the
disposing order to issue in the Court's name. P(referral after prediction
time) = 0.85.

**Further response request.** A response has already been requested (Aug 31,
due Sept 3), so this rung has fired; nothing further of that kind is left to
happen. P = 0.02 (the claim is vacuous on this record and the harness will
mask it).

**Amicus activity.** The docket shows zero amicus briefs as of the snapshot.
The window before disposition is very short (roughly three days), which
suppresses filings, but this is exactly the kind of nationally salient
application that draws fast-turnaround amici — Campaign Legal Center is
already active on the respondents' side of the underlying litigation, and
broadcaster interests (NAB) have a direct compliance stake. Slightly less
likely than not that any brief lands before the Court rules.
P(amicus count rises past 0) = 0.45.

**Shape of the order.** If granted, I expect a brief order staying the
Fourth Circuit's judgment pending the timely filing and disposition of a
petition for certiorari, possibly with noted dissents from two or three of
Justices Sotomayor, Kagan, and Jackson. A grant of certiorari before
judgment in the same order is possible but not the modal outcome. If denied,
the likely ground is equitable rather than merits-based: the pre-March-2026
status quo was candidate-only LUC, so denial merely restores decades-old
practice — a framing the respondents will press hard.
