# Forecast

The Court will refer the application to the full Court and enter an unqualified grant of a stay pending the filing and disposition of a petition for certiorari. An order is likely on September 3 or September 4, 2026, near the response deadline and before the challenged FCC guidance is due to take effect.

At prediction time the Chief Justice had already requested a response, a response from the United States and FCC had been filed, the application had not yet been referred to the full Court, and the docket showed no amicus briefs. No further response request is expected. Referral is very likely, and at least one amicus filing is more likely than not despite the compressed schedule.

The stay will rest primarily on a fair prospect that the Court will reject the Fourth Circuit's jurisdictional treatment of an FCC order still undergoing Commission review, with the merits reinforced by the dissent's reliance on the Court's recent protection of coordinated party-candidate political speech. The imminent election-advertising window supplies the irreparable-harm and timing component. A denial remains plausible because the Fourth Circuit majority treated the statutory text as clear and because the requested relief would restore an agency policy whose legality was sharply contested.
