# Forecast — 26A274, NRCC et al. v. Brown et al. (stay application)

**Disposition: the Court grants the stay (P = 0.62).** I expect an order
staying the Fourth Circuit's judgment pending the timely filing and
disposition of a petition for certiorari, issued within days of the
September 3 response deadline — most likely September 3–8, and quite
possibly before September 4, when the 60-day general-election
lowest-unit-charge window opens. The drivers: the Solicitor General, for
the United States and the FCC, has already filed a response supporting
the applicants on standing, on Hobbs Act finality (a Media Bureau public
notice as a non-final, staff-level action), and on the merits; the panel
below was divided, with Judge Wilkinson dissenting on statutory
ambiguity; the Fourth Circuit compounded the emergency by making its
ruling effective immediately; and a stay restores the regulatory status
quo that had governed ad sales since March 2026 rather than changing
rules mid-election. The jurisdictional arguments (competitor standing,
finality) give the Court a route to relief that does not commit it on
the statute. The main path to denial is the statutory text itself — the
majority below read §315(b)'s lowest-unit-charge guarantee as
unambiguously limited to candidates and their authorized committees —
plus the classically economic character of the harm.

**Referral (P = 0.85).** The Chief Justice, as Circuit Justice for the
Fourth Circuit, requested a response on a three-day clock. On an
application of this profile — a national-election stakes dispute with
the federal government supporting the applicant — I expect him to refer
it to the full Court rather than act alone, and the disposing order to
issue by the Court with any dissents noted.

**Response request (P = 0.02).** Already on the record — the request is
this event's opening moment, so the increment is vacuous by
construction; the number is nominal.

**Amicus briefs (P = 0.50).** The record shows zero. The window is very
short (likely under a week to disposition), which suppresses filings,
but the affected constituencies are organized and fast: broadcasters
(NAB), the Democratic campaign committees, and campaign-finance groups
on both sides. I put it at a coin flip that at least one amicus brief
lands before disposition.

**Timing (unscored).** Disposition by roughly September 8; the tight
response deadline signals the Court intends to resolve the application
before the general-election advertising window has meaningfully run.

**Shape of the order.** If granted: a short order staying the Fourth
Circuit's judgment pending certiorari, likely with three noted dissents
(Sotomayor, Kagan, Jackson), possibly with a short dissenting statement.
A grant here strongly signals a later cert grant on the Hobbs Act
finality question or the §315(b) merits. If denied: a one-line denial,
possibly with a concurring statement noting the applicants' ability to
press the question via certiorari after final judgment.
