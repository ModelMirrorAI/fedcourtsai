# Rationale

I assign a 0.76 probability to an unqualified grant. The committed statpack supplies a valid OT2026 baseline from the strictly prior application Terms: OT2024 and OT2025 contain 296 resolved substantive applications and 31 grants, a pooled grant rate of about 10.5%. That is an unconditioned application-docket baseline, not a response-requested rate. I adjust sharply upward because this cell is frozen at the strongest recorded escalation rung: the Chief Justice has affirmatively requested a response. The statpack's signal counts are terminal/right-censored and do not support a numerical conditional rate, so they inform the direction rather than the size of that adjustment.

The provisioned snapshot shows a stay application submitted to the Chief Justice, a response request with a September 3 noon deadline, a response already filed by the United States and FCC, no referral to the full Court, and zero amicus briefs. No filed-document text was provisioned under `record/documents/`. I therefore used CourtListener MCP material from the related Fourth Circuit proceeding. The lower-court opinion granted review by a 2–1 vote and set aside the FCC notice; the dissent disputed jurisdiction and defended the notice. The applicants' lower-court stay motion identifies contrary finality decisions from the D.C., Third, and Eleventh Circuits, federal support for a stay, reliance interests in advertising contracts, and the September 4 effective date. The Fourth Circuit denied its own stay 2–1, which strengthens both the need for Supreme Court relief and the clean vote split.

The most important additional signal is *Trump v. California*, issued August 24, 2026. There the Court granted a stay on justiciability grounds and emphasized that courts should not act on contingent, nonfinal executive measures. It is not identical—the FCC notice arguably has more immediate external effect—but it supports the applicants' central theory and shows a recent majority willing to grant emergency relief on finality grounds. Election timing cuts the same way because a stay would preserve the asserted historical practice rather than impose the Fourth Circuit's change just before the general-election advertising window.

I leave 0.24 for denial, partial or administrative-only relief that ultimately expires, withdrawal, or dismissal. The main weaknesses are that the FCC notice governs external conduct now, the candidates face immediate competitive effects, and the Fourth Circuit treated Commission review as constructively exhausted. Because the interim resolver counts mixed partial relief as denied, the 0.76 is specifically P(an unqualified grant), not P(any temporary accommodation).

The response-request increment is 0.00 because that monotone rung already fired and the harness masks it as vacuous. Referral is 0.96 because a nationally significant, response-requested application presenting a circuit split and an imminent deadline is very likely to go to the full Court. The amicus increment is 0.55: the dispute has organized political, campaign-law, and broadcast-industry stakeholders, but the filing window is extremely short. My principal uncertainty is that the actual Supreme Court application and all responses were not provisioned; the CourtListener material includes the substantially similar lower-court stay motion but not any refinements made in the Supreme Court papers.
