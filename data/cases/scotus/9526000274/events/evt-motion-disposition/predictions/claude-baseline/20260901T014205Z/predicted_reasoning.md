# Forecast — 26A274, NRCC et al. v. Brown et al. (application for stay)

**Disposition.** The Court grants the stay, more likely than not (P = 0.60), as an
unqualified grant pending disposition of a petition for certiorari. The Fourth
Circuit struck down the FCC's March 2026 public notice extending lowest-unit-rate
eligibility to party and joint fundraising committees, refused its own stay, and
made the ruling effective immediately with the 60-day general-election window
opening in early September. Three things point toward a grant: the Solicitor
General — respondent in caption but not in posture — filed the same day the Chief
Justice asked, defending the FCC's own guidance and so effectively supporting the
applicants; Judge Wilkinson dissented below on jurisdiction, giving the Court a
clean non-merits ground (a staff-level public notice as unreviewable non-final
agency action) on which a fair prospect of vacatur is easy to find; and the
equities run through unrecoverable ad-rate differentials in an election window
that opens within days of the response deadline. The countervailing risk, and
why this is 0.60 rather than higher, is the merits text: § 315(b)'s
lowest-unit-charge applies to "use" of a station by a legally qualified
candidate, and a textualist Court may think the panel majority simply read the
statute correctly, making certiorari and reversal less than fairly probable.

**Escalation ladder from the frozen record.** The record already shows a
response requested (by the Chief Justice, day of docketing, due at noon
September 3 — that rung has fired; my stated probability is for the record's
sake, and the harness masks it as vacuous). **Referral to the full Court:
expect it (P = 0.90).** A nationally salient, government-supported election
application is not the shape a Circuit Justice disposes of alone; the Chief
will refer it after the September 3 response. **Amicus briefs: expect at least
one (P = 0.60), past the zero the record shows.** Broadcasters (the NAB), the
DNC-side committees, and campaign-finance groups all have direct stakes; the
compressed schedule — likely under two weeks end to end — is the only thing
holding this number near a coin flip.

**Timing** (unscored, but it disciplines the rest): disposition within roughly
one week of the September 3 response deadline — by mid-September 2026 — because
the relief only matters while the general-election window is running. Expect a
short order, possibly with a brief explanation or noted dissents from the
Democratic-appointed Justices if granted, or a noted dissent from one or more
Republican-appointed Justices if denied.
