# Forecast

The Court will grant an unqualified stay of the Fourth Circuit's judgment pending a timely petition for certiorari, probably after a short administrative stay preserves the pre-judgment advertising-rate regime while the application is considered. The requested relief is likely to issue shortly after the challengers' September 3 response deadline and before or very near the September 4 start of the statutory 60-day general-election window.

The record already shows that the Chief Justice requested a response on August 31, so no response-request increment remains to occur. After the response arrives, the Chief Justice will almost certainly refer the application to the full Court. At least one amicus filing is more likely than not because the lower-court proceeding attracted campaign-finance and media-industry amici, the national party committees claim immediate advertising-contract harm, and the ruling affects campaign spending nationwide; the compressed schedule limits how many briefs can arrive.

On the disposition, a majority will likely find a fair prospect that the Court will grant certiorari and reverse on one or more threshold grounds. The Fourth Circuit's rule allowing review of FCC staff action while Commission review remains pending conflicts with the agency-review sequence applied in other circuits. The Court's August 24 per curiam in *Trump v. California* reinforces the applicants' objection to judicial intervention before agency action becomes final. The Solicitor General and FCC support the stay, and Judge Wilkinson dissented from the merits ruling and voted to stay it below.

The equities also favor a stay. Applicants assert that broadcast contracts were negotiated under a longstanding treatment of coordinated advertisements and that the Fourth Circuit changed that treatment days before the lowest-unit-charge period begins. The Court is likely to view preservation of that status quo, pending review, as less disruptive than allowing the new rule to take effect during the election. The principal contrary argument is that the FCC notice already had concrete operative consequences and that the Fourth Circuit majority regarded both jurisdiction and the statutory text as clear, but that is unlikely to prevent interim relief from this Court.
