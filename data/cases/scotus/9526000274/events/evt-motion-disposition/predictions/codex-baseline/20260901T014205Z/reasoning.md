I assign a 0.82 probability to an unqualified grant. The strictly prior application-Term pool in `metrics/statpack.md` contains 31 grants among 296 resolved substantive applications from Terms 2024 and 2025, a 10.5% pooled grant rate that clears the 50-resolution floor. That is the correct published anchor for this Term 2026 interim cell, but it is unconditioned on the escalation ladder and comes from a broader population than the selected prediction reserve.

The case-specific adjustment is large. The dated August 31 snapshot shows that the Chief Justice had already requested a response, the United States and FCC filed a same-day response, the matter had not yet been referred to the Court, and no Supreme Court amicus brief was shown. The government's retrieved response expressly supports the application. It identifies asserted conflicts with Supreme Court standing precedent and multiple circuits' treatment of FCC staff action, relies on the Court's August 24, 2026 per curiam in *Trump v. California*, and argues that immediate relief is needed before the lowest-unit-charge window begins September 4. The Fourth Circuit ruled 2-1, Judge Wilkinson dissented, and he also voted to grant a stay below. Those facts make this application much stronger than an undifferentiated substantive application.

I set `response-requested-increment` to 0.0 because the frozen record already shows that rung as fired; the harness should treat it as vacuous. I assign 0.98 to referral after prediction because a contested, nationally consequential application with a requested response and Solicitor General participation is overwhelmingly likely to be sent to the full Court. I assign 0.62 to an increase above the frozen zero amicus briefs: the lower litigation drew amici and the stakes are national, but the response and likely disposition schedule is extremely compressed.

The main uncertainty is the strict interim target. A temporary administrative stay would not necessarily mean the Court ultimately grants the requested stay pending certiorari, and a mixed or partial order resolves as ungranted under this event's convention. The challengers can also distinguish *Trump v. California*: the FCC notice purportedly governed near-term private advertising rates, while the executive directive there imposed no external legal obligation. The Fourth Circuit majority additionally found the statutory text clear. Those considerations keep the forecast below 0.9 despite the government's support and the recent analogous order.
