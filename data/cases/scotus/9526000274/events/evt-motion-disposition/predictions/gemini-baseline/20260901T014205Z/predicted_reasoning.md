# Predicted Reasoning of the Court

Because the application raises urgent questions concerning nationwide campaign advertising rules on the eve of the 60-day election window, the Court's disposition will be driven by the tight timeline and the alignment of the applicants and the federal government.

## Procedural Escalation and Timing
*   **Response Requested:** A response has already been requested by the Chief Justice, reflecting the Court's immediate attention to the application.
*   **Referral to the Full Court:** The Chief Justice is extremely likely (95% probability) to refer this application to the full Court rather than acting alone, given the national political implications and the impact on the upcoming midterm elections.
*   **Amici Curiae:** Given the high stakes for political advertising, there is a moderate likelihood (60% probability) that additional amicus briefs will be filed by campaign finance groups or political organizations before the Court rules.
*   **Timing:** The Court will almost certainly rule before September 4, 2026, when the "lowest unit charge" 60-day window begins. An administrative stay could be issued immediately while the Court finalizes a substantive ruling.

## Disposition
*   **Unqualified Grant:** The Court will likely grant the stay (85% probability). The order will reflect that the applicants have demonstrated both a fair prospect of reversal and irreparable harm, largely deferring to the Solicitor General's and the FCC's assessment that the Fourth Circuit's vacatur of the agency's guidance disrupts settled expectations immediately before an election.
*   **Merits Outlook (Conditional):** If the stay is granted, the Court will likely treat the application as a petition for certiorari before judgment or signal that the underlying jurisdictional and administrative law questions warrant full review in the regular course, given the split over the FCC's authority and interpretation of the lowest unit charge provision.
