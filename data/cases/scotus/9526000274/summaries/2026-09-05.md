## In brief

The Supreme Court stayed a Fourth Circuit ruling that had struck down FCC guidance on discounted political ad rates, keeping that guidance in effect for now.

## What happened

An FCC staff notice said political parties and joint fundraising committees, not just candidates, could qualify for broadcasters' discounted "lowest unit charge" on campaign ads. Democratic candidates Sherrod Brown, Jon Ossoff, Roy Cooper, and Kristen McDonald Rivet challenged that notice, arguing it unfairly benefited Republican committees that spend more through coordinated ads. The Fourth Circuit agreed with them and set the notice aside, over a dissent arguing the court lacked jurisdiction because the FCC itself had not yet finished reviewing the notice. The National Republican Congressional Committee and National Republican Senatorial Committee, who had intervened in that case, asked the Supreme Court to pause the ruling before new election-season ad rates took effect.

## What the Court is being asked

The application asked the Court to pause the Fourth Circuit's decision while the Republican committees pursue further review, on the grounds that the appeals court acted before the FCC had finished its own review of the guidance and wrongly interpreted which political spending qualifies for discounted ad rates.

## Where it stands

The Chief Justice asked for responses, which were filed along with amicus briefs and a reply. On September 4, 2026, the Court granted the stay, recalling and pausing the Fourth Circuit's mandate until a petition for certiorari is filed and resolved; Justice Jackson dissented.

## What each outcome would mean

The Republican committees asked for the stay, and the Court granted it. That means the Fourth Circuit's decision striking down the FCC guidance is paused, so the guidance allowing discounted ad rates for party-coordinated and joint fundraising committee ads remains in effect while the committees seek Supreme Court review. If certiorari is later denied, the stay ends automatically and the Fourth Circuit's ruling against the guidance would take effect; if certiorari is granted, the stay continues until the Court decides the case.
