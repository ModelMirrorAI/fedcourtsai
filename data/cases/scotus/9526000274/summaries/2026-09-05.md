## In brief

The Supreme Court stayed a Fourth Circuit ruling that had struck down FCC guidance letting political parties and joint fundraising committees get discounted broadcast ad rates alongside candidates.

## What happened

The FCC's Media Bureau issued a public notice in March 2026 saying discounted "lowest unit charge" broadcast ad rates apply not just to candidates but also to party committees and joint fundraising committees coordinating with them. Democratic candidates Sherrod Brown, Jon Ossoff, Roy Cooper, and Kristen McDonald Rivet challenged that notice, arguing it improperly extends the discount beyond candidates themselves. The Fourth Circuit agreed with them and set the notice aside, over a dissent arguing the court lacked jurisdiction because the FCC had not yet finished reviewing the notice itself. The National Republican Congressional Committee and National Republican Senatorial Committee, who had intervened to defend the notice, asked the Fourth Circuit for a stay; it was denied. They then asked the Supreme Court to step in before the rate change took effect on September 4, 2026.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court was asked to pause the lower court's ruling while the committees pursue further review, and, separately, the case raises questions about whether a court could review the FCC notice at all before the agency itself had finished considering a challenge to it, and whether the rate discount extends to party and joint committee ad spending or only to spending directly by candidates.

## Where it stands

The Chief Justice referred the stay application to the full Court, which granted it on September 4, 2026. The Fourth Circuit's mandate is recalled and stayed pending a timely petition for certiorari; the stay ends automatically if certiorari is denied, and ends when judgment issues if certiorari is granted. Justice Jackson dissented.

## What each outcome would mean

The Republican committees asked for the stay. By granting it, the Court has put the Fourth Circuit's ruling on hold, so the discounted rates for party and joint-committee-coordinated ads remain available while the committees seek Supreme Court review. Brown, Ossoff, Cooper, and McDonald Rivet, who won in the Fourth Circuit, do not get the benefit of that ruling for now. If certiorari is later denied, the stay ends and the Fourth Circuit's decision against the discount takes effect; if certiorari is granted, the stay lasts until the Court decides the case.
