## What happened

The Federal Communications Commission's Media Bureau issued a notice saying candidates, political parties, and joint fundraising committees are all entitled to discounted "lowest unit" broadcast advertising rates before elections. Democratic candidates Sherrod Brown, Jon Ossoff, Roy Cooper, and Kristen McDonald Rivet argued only candidates themselves should get that discount, since party-coordinated ads benefit their opponents more. While their request that the full Commission review the notice was still pending, they asked the Fourth Circuit to strike it down. The Fourth Circuit agreed and set the notice aside, over a dissent arguing the court acted before the agency finished its own review. The Republican National Congressional Committee and National Republican Senatorial Committee, who intervened to defend the notice, asked the Supreme Court to pause that ruling.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked whether the Fourth Circuit was allowed to review and cancel the Commission's notice at all, given that the full Commission had not yet finished considering the challenge itself, and separately whether the notice's interpretation—extending discount ad rates to parties and joint committees, not just candidates—was correct.

## Where it stands

The Chief Justice asked the government to respond, and both sides, along with amici, filed briefs. On September 4, 2026, the Court granted the stay, recalling and pausing the Fourth Circuit's mandate until a certiorari petition is filed and resolved; Justice Jackson dissented.
