## In brief

Harvey Trust asks the Court to review a Nevada ruling rejecting its claim that a state agency's sale of nearby land left part of its property without access and uncompensated.

## What happened

Judith Harvey, as trustee and estate representative, sued the City of Reno, Washoe County, Nevada's transportation department, and Green Acres Storage Partners, claiming that a 2020-2021 sale of state-owned land cut off access to the northwest corner of the Harvey property, which had used a road across that land for decades. A Nevada trial court dismissed the claims, finding Harvey Trust had no property interest in the sold parcel and that other claims were time-barred. The Nevada Supreme Court affirmed, and later denied rehearing.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked whether the government must pay compensation when it sells land that had provided the only practical access to someone else's property, even though the landowner never formally owned or held a recorded easement over the access route.

## Where it stands

The petition has been fully briefed, with respondents' opposition and the petitioner's reply filed, and the case was distributed for the Court's conference of September 28, 2026. The docket shows no action recorded since that distribution.

## What each outcome would mean

Harvey Trust, as petitioner, is asking the Court to take up the case. If the Court grants review, it would hear the case in full and decide whether the Takings Clause required compensation here, without yet deciding who wins. If the Court denies review, the Nevada Supreme Court's decision stands, leaving in place the rulings that Harvey Trust had no compensable property interest in the sold parcel and that several of its claims were time-barred, a result favoring the city, county, state transportation department, and Green Acres.
