## In brief

The Supreme Court declined to review a landowner's claim that Nevada owed compensation for selling land she used to reach part of her property.

## What happened

Judith Harvey, representing a family trust, sued the City of Reno, Washoe County, Nevada's transportation department, and Green Acres Storage Partners over access to part of her property near Interstate 580. Harvey Trust said a 1987 highway project removed a bridge it had used to reach one corner of its land, forcing it to rely on a state-owned access road instead; when the state later sold that road to Green Acres, the corner became landlocked. Nevada courts dismissed the claims, ruling that Harvey Trust never owned or held a legal easement over the access road and that related claims were filed too late.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked whether the Constitution's requirement that government pay for property it takes applies when a state agency sells the only practical route to someone's land, even if that person never held formal legal rights to use that route.

## Where it stands

The petition was distributed for the Court's September 28, 2026 conference, and the Court denied the petition on October 5, 2026.

## What each outcome would mean

Harvey Trust, the petitioner, asked the Court to overturn the ruling against it. By denying the petition, the Court leaves the Nevada Supreme Court's decision in place, meaning Harvey Trust's claims remain dismissed and it receives no compensation. The City of Reno and the other opposing parties, who had urged the Court not to take the case, keep the win they secured in state court. The denial does not mean the Supreme Court agreed the lower court was correct; it simply ends the case without a ruling from the Court on the merits.
