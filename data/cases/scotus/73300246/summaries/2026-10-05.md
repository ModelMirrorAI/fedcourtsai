## In brief

The Court declined to hear a landowner's bid to revive a lawsuit claiming a state agency's sale of land took away her property's only access.

## What happened

Judith Harvey, representing a family trust, sued the City of Reno, Washoe County, Nevada's transportation department, and a private buyer, Green Acres Storage Partners, after the state sold a parcel her family had long used to reach part of their property. A bridge once provided that access but was removed decades earlier during highway construction; afterward the family used a road on state-owned land next door. When the state auctioned that land to Green Acres in 2021 without reserving access for the Trust, the Trust claimed this amounted to an uncompensated taking of its property. Nevada's trial court dismissed the claims, and the Nevada Supreme Court affirmed, finding the Trust never owned or held a legal easement over the access road and that related claims were filed too late.

## What the Court is being asked

The case asks whether the constitutional requirement of just compensation for government takings of property applies when a state agency sells land that was someone's only practical way to reach part of their property, leaving that portion unreachable and without value, even though state courts found the family had no formal legal right to use that access road.

## Where it stands

The petition was distributed for the Court's conference on September 28, 2026, and the Court denied it on October 5, 2026.

## What each outcome would mean

Harvey asked the Court to take up her case. With the petition denied, the Nevada Supreme Court's ruling stands: the Trust's claims remain dismissed, and the City of Reno, Washoe County, the state transportation department, and Green Acres prevail as they did in the lower courts. The Trust cannot pursue its takings claim further in this case. A denial does not mean the Supreme Court agreed with the Nevada court's reasoning, only that it chose not to review it.
