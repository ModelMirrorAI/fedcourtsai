# Forecast: Alabama, et al. v. California, et al. (26A139)

Application for a stay of the D. Mass. injunction in *State of California v.
Trump* (the multistate challenge to the President's elections executive order;
CA1 Nos. 26-1774, 26-1779), filed by twelve intervening states and submitted
to Justice Jackson on July 29, 2026. As of the August 4 snapshot: a response
was requested the day of filing and filed on the August 3 deadline, and five
amicus filings — all supporting the respondent states — arrived the same day.

**Disposition: the Court denies the application.** Justice Jackson will refer
it to the full Court rather than act alone — a contested, fully briefed,
twelve-state application over a presidential election order is not
single-Justice material — and the Court will deny the stay in a short order.
The likeliest form is a bare or briefly reasoned denial; a noted dissent from
one or more of Justices Thomas, Alito, or Gorsuch is plausible but not the
modal outcome, and I do not expect four votes for the applicants. If the Court
is moved at all, it is likelier to be by the injunction's scope than by the
order's legality, and that path produces a *partial* stay — which this event's
denial-first collapse records as a denial. An unqualified grant requires the
Court to (a) overlook that the applicants are intervenors whom the injunction
does not restrain, (b) credit the executive order's legality against a
district court and a Court of Appeals panel that both refused a stay, and
(c) change the operative federal election rules roughly three months before
the November 2026 midterms — each independently unlikely.

**Ladder claims, from the frozen record.** The record already shows a
response requested (July 29) and filed (August 3), so the
response-requested rung is spent: no further call for a response will issue
(the claim is vacuous on this record; my nominal probability covers only an
unexpected supplemental request). Referral to the full Court: very likely
(~0.85) — the residual is the chance Justice Jackson denies in chambers or the
disposing entry never carries referral language the record parser can read.
Amicus count: the frozen count is 2 (the record's counter reads the two
entries styled "amicus curiae"; three more styled "amici curiae" sit outside
it). I expect little further amicus activity — the interested amici filed with
the response, and the window to disposition is short — so P(the counted number
rises) is modest (~0.15).

**Timing** (not scored at any stage): the application became fully briefed on
August 3; I expect disposition within one to three weeks of that date — by
roughly the end of August 2026 — with no administrative stay in the interim.

**If the Court grants** (conditional, against my expectation): the likelier
form is a stay limited to particular provisions of the executive order or to
particular plaintiff states, over three or more noted dissents from the
Court's Democratic appointees, with the case then moving quickly on the CA1
merits calendar or via a grant of certiorari before judgment.
