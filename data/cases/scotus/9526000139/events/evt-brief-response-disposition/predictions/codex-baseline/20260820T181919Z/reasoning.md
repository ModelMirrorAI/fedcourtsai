# Rationale

I assign a 0.22 probability to an unqualified grant. The committed statpack supplies a valid interim baseline for this OT2026 cell: OT2025 and OT2024 together contain 225 strictly prior resolved substantive applications and 30 grants, a 13.3% grant rate, clearing the 50-case floor. This is an unconditioned baseline, while this application sits high on the selection ladder because Justice Jackson requested a response and the matter attracted substantial amicus participation. Those signals justify moving upward from 13.3%.

The case-specific record pulls the estimate back down. The provisioned snapshot identifies a stay application by Alabama and other intervenor states after the First Circuit denied relief. No application or response text was provisioned. In pre-cutoff CourtListener material, the First Circuit's July 25 order shows a 2–1 denial of both stay motions. Its majority reasoned that the district-court injunction applies only in the plaintiff states through the November 2026 election, leaving implementation in the applicant states untouched; it treated the applicants' asserted injury as weak, found concrete harms to the respondent states, and observed that the applicants had not presented a public-interest argument. The dissent would have stayed only Section 2(a) and part of Section 2(b), which raises the prospect of limited Supreme Court relief but also makes an unqualified grant less likely. Because this event resolves mixed relief denial-first, the 0.22 estimates only a clean grant, not any favorable relief.

The response-request claim is effectively spent: the frozen context already records `response_requested: true`, so I state 0.01 for another post-prediction request and expect the harness to mask the claim as vacuous. I put referral at 0.88 because a requested response in a nationally significant election dispute is likely to precede full-Court consideration. I put a further amicus increase at 0.08 because the response-filed moment is normally very close to disposition and the visible amici had already filed by August 3.

There is a conditioning-data discrepancy. The frozen context records two amicus briefs, while the provisioned snapshot lists five separate August 3 amicus filings. I treated the harness-owned frozen count as the claim baseline but did not count the three already-visible filings as future increments. This may cause the mechanical amicus-increment resolution to disagree with the actual prediction-time record, so I surfaced it in `flags.json`.

Main uncertainty is whether the Court views the standing objection and the panel dissent as sufficient for partial emergency relief despite the applicant states' attenuated injury. The CourtListener searches were date-bounded to material filed before August 4 and did not surface this application's disposition.
