# Predicted reasoning — Alabama, et al. v. California, et al. (26A139)

Application by twelve intervenor states to stay the District of Massachusetts'
final judgment permanently enjoining the March 2025 elections executive order,
after a divided First Circuit panel denied a stay pending appeal on July 25,
2026. Submitted to Justice Jackson July 29; she called for a response the same
day, due August 3.

**Disposition.** The Court will deny the application. The likeliest form is a
single order disposing of this application together with the federal
defendants' parallel application in the same matter, without a full opinion,
possibly with a short explanation invoking the equities and the proximity of
the November 2026 midterms. If the Court grants any relief, the likelier shape
is a partial stay — narrowing the injunction to the plaintiff states or to
particular provisions of the order — rather than an unqualified stay of the
whole judgment; a partial grant resolves this event as ungranted under the
denial-first convention. The states' application faces a hurdle the federal
one does not: intervenors must show their own irreparable harm from an
injunction that binds federal officials, and this Court has been skeptical of
state standing built on indirect effects. A denial of the states' application
for want of standing or as duplicative is live even in the scenario where the
federal application succeeds.

**Referral.** Justice Jackson will refer the application to the full Court
rather than act alone — a twelve-state application in a case of this
magnitude, paired with a presidential application in the same matter, is not
single-Justice business. Expect the disposing order to issue from the full
Court, with the referral visible on the docket or in the order's text.

**Further response requests.** None. The response was requested the day the
application was docketed; that rung has fired and will not recur.

**Amici.** Further amicus filings will land: the snapshot's attorney roster
already carries counsel for five amicus groups (Society for the Rule of Law,
56 Former Judges, Bipartisan Former Governors, National Security Leaders for
America, and an Asian Law Caucus-led voting-rights coalition), all of which
filed against a stay in the First Circuit, and the companion federal
application had drawn six amicus briefs by mid-August. Expect the count on
this docket to rise from zero to at least four or five.

**Timing.** The response deadline was August 3 and the companion application
was still pending in mid-August, so the Court is taking the pair on a
deliberate schedule rather than a same-week turnaround. Expect disposition by
mid-to-late September 2026 — before state registration deadlines make the
question academic and before the October Term opens. Nothing scores this
horizon; it disciplines the rest.

**If relief is granted** (the ~15% branch): expect it to issue in tandem with
a grant on the federal application, over noted dissents from three or four
Justices, with the order emphasizing the unitary character of the federal
form. A grant for the states alone, with the federal application denied, is
implausible.
