# Rationale

I assign a 0.38 probability to an unqualified grant and predict `denied`. The committed statpack's strictly prior pool for this OT2026 application contains 225 resolved substantive applications from OT2024 and OT2025, of which 30 were granted, for a 13.3% baseline. That pool clears the 50-case floor. It is not conditioned on the escalation ladder, and the predicted reserve preferentially selects response-requested applications, so I adjust substantially upward after Justice Jackson's response request. I do not treat the statpack's response-request count as a conditional rate because it is terminal, right-censored, and covers all substantive applications rather than their as-at-prediction states.

The retrieved pre-application record supports both directions of the adjustment. The district court enjoined implementation of Sections 2 and 3 of Executive Order 14399 for elections through November 3, 2026, finding the provisions ultra vires and unconstitutional. The First Circuit denied stays sought by the federal defendants and twelve intervenor states, but one judge would have stayed the injunction as to Section 2(a) and part of Section 2(b). A response request and a reasoned dissent make some relief considerably more plausible than the unconditioned interim baseline. Recent Supreme Court emergency treatment of presidential-control disputes also makes the applicants' Article II and prematurity arguments non-frivolous.

I stop below 50% because this application is brought by the intervenor states, while the injunction does not prevent implementation in those states. The First Circuit therefore viewed their claimed harm as weak, whereas the plaintiff states supplied concrete evidence of election-administration costs and risks to eligible voters. The applicants also sought a full stay even though the lower-court dissent's strongest theory supports only partial relief. Because this resolver classifies a mixed grant-in-part/deny-in-part order as denied, the probability is specifically for an unqualified grant, not for any relief at all.

The frozen context shows `response_requested: true`, `referred_to_court: false`, and zero amicus briefs. I use 0.00 for the already-fired, vacuous response-request increment; 0.96 for later full-Court referral; and 0.90 for an increase above zero amici. The last estimate is supported by the snapshot's top-level identification of counsel for several interested groups, while the frozen procedural count remains zero. The snapshot is truncated and contains no provisioned application or response text, so my main uncertainty is whether the Supreme Court filing narrows the requested relief or supplies a stronger applicant-specific injury than the First Circuit materials reveal.
