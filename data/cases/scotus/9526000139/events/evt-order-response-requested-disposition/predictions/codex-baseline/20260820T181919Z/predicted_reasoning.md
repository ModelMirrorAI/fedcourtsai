# Forecast

The Court will most likely deny the application for a stay. A narrower order granting relief only as to part of Executive Order 14399 is a meaningful alternative, but a mixed grant-in-part/deny-in-part order would still resolve as denied under this event's denial-first convention. An unqualified grant is less likely.

The record already shows that Justice Jackson requested a response, due August 3, 2026, so no further response-request increment remains. The application has not yet been referred to the full Court; given the response request, the constitutional stakes, and the divided First Circuit order, referral is highly likely. The frozen record shows zero amicus briefs. At least one later amicus filing is likely, particularly because the snapshot identifies counsel for several interested groups.

The decisive weakness is the applicant states' own claim to emergency relief. The injunction operates only in the plaintiff states, not in Alabama or the other applicant states, and the First Circuit found their asserted mail-ballot-security injury insufficient to outweigh the plaintiff states' concrete election-administration costs and disenfranchisement risks. The Court's conservative majority may be receptive to the First Circuit dissent's view that the plaintiff states lack standing to challenge Section 2(a) and part of Section 2(b), but that supports at most a partial stay more naturally than the unqualified grant scored by this event.

Disposition should follow shortly after the response and any reply, most likely by a full-Court order. If the Court grants any relief, the likeliest route is a narrow stay limited to the citizenship-list provisions rather than reinstatement of the entire executive order.
