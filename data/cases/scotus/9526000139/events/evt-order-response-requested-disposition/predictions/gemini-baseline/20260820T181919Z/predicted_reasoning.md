This application for a stay arises from a highly salient legal conflict over election administration and executive power, just months before the 2026 midterm elections. The applicants (Alabama and other Republican-led states) are seeking to stay a district court injunction that blocked a presidential executive order mandating citizenship verification for mail-in ballots. 

The docket shows that Justice Jackson has called for a response, confirming that the application is being taken seriously. Given the national significance of the issues—implicating federalism, election integrity, and the scope of executive authority—and the fact that it involves a district court issuing a nationwide injunction against a federal executive order, Justice Jackson is almost certain to refer the application to the full Court. The intense political interest in the case also makes it highly likely that multiple amicus briefs will be filed.

As for the disposition, the current Court has shown skepticism toward nationwide injunctions issued by district courts (as reflected in recent precedent such as *Trump v. CASA, Inc.*). The conservative majority is likely sympathetic to the arguments raised by the intervening red states regarding election integrity and the limits of district court authority. While the *Purcell* principle—counseling against late judicial interventions in election rules—could weigh against disturbing the status quo, the Court may view the district court's injunction itself as the improper disruption. Therefore, an unqualified grant of the stay is the most likely outcome, though not a certainty due to the complex procedural posture and timing.
