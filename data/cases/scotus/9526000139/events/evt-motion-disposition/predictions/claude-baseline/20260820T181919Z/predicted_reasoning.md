# Predicted reasoning — Alabama, et al. v. California, et al. (26A139, stay application)

## What the application is

Twelve intervenor-defendant states led by Alabama ask the Court to stay the
District of Massachusetts's preliminary injunction barring implementation of
the President's executive order restricting mail-in voting (the USPS
citizenship-list regime), pending appeal to the First Circuit. It runs in
parallel with the Solicitor General's own application in the same litigation
(26A124, filed two days earlier); the two will be resolved together, almost
certainly in one order.

## Procedural forecast

- **Referral.** The record shows the application submitted to Justice Jackson,
  who requested a response the same day but has not referred it. She will not
  act alone on an application of this magnitude: expect referral to the full
  Court, with the disposition issued by the full Court (referral probability
  0.92; the residual is the chance the docket never records an explicit
  referral entry).
- **Response.** Already requested at prediction time (due August 3, 2026), so
  the response-requested increment cannot fire again from this vantage; expect
  the respondent states' consolidated opposition on that date and a prompt
  reply from applicants.
- **Amici.** Five sets of amicus counsel had already entered appearances by the
  snapshot cutoff (former judges, bipartisan former governors, national
  security leaders, a rule-of-law group, and a voting-rights coalition) with
  zero briefs yet docketed. Expect several amicus briefs to land within days —
  the amicus count will almost certainly rise past zero (0.97).
- **Timing.** With briefing complete in early August, expect a disposition in
  mid-to-late August or early September 2026 — the Court will not let this sit
  near the November midterms. Timing is unscored; it disciplines the rest.

## Disposition forecast

Most likely outcome: **the full stay is denied** (probability of an
unqualified grant: 0.30). Three shapes dominate the outcome space:

1. **Outright denial (~40%).** The merits are weak — no constitutional or
   statutory text gives the President authority over state election
   administration, the First Circuit called the order "unprecedented levels of
   involvement by federal officials in how states administer elections," and
   Purcell-style equities cut *against* the applicants here: staying the
   injunction would inject a new federal citizenship-list regime into mail
   voting months before the midterms, disrupting rather than preserving the
   status quo.
2. **Partial relief (~25-30%).** The strongest applicant argument is scope,
   not merits: after Trump v. CASA, a nationwide injunction reaching the
   twelve applicant states — who want the order to operate in their own
   elections — is vulnerable. A stay limited to non-plaintiff states, or to
   discrete provisions, is a very live route. Note the resolver reads a mixed
   "granted in part and denied in part" order denial-first, so this shape
   resolves as ungranted.
3. **Unqualified grant (~30%).** The Court's recent record on the
   administration's emergency applications is strongly favorable, the
   Solicitor General is co-applicant, and a majority skeptical of universal
   injunctions could stay the injunction wholesale and let the appeal proceed.

Withdrawal or dismissal is negligible (~2%). A companion possibility is that
the Court treats the applications as cert petitions and grants certiorari
before judgment with expedited argument while leaving the injunction largely
in place — that route resolves the stay ask as a denial or partial denial.

Expect separate writings either way: dissents from Justices Sotomayor, Kagan,
and Jackson if the stay is granted; a dissent from Justices Thomas and/or
Alito, possibly Gorsuch, if it is denied. (No interim vote is scored; this is
context.)
