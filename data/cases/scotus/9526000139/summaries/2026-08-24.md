## In brief

The Supreme Court stayed a lower court's injunction against a federal election executive order, letting it take effect while appeals continue.

## What happened

President Trump issued an executive order directing federal agencies to compile citizenship lists for states, prioritize prosecution of election officials who issue ballots to ineligible voters, and have the Postal Service draft rules on mail-ballot security. California and other states sued, arguing the order was unconstitutional and exceeded executive power. A federal district court in Massachusetts agreed, blocking parts of the order for the 2026 elections. Missouri and other states, who had intervened to defend the order, asked the First Circuit Court of Appeals to pause that injunction while they appealed; the appeals court refused. They then asked the Supreme Court to stay the injunction.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked whether the states challenging the order had the legal right to sue over it at all, given that the order mainly tells federal agencies to study or propose future actions rather than immediately requiring anyone to do anything, and whether the harms they claim are concrete enough, or too speculative and premature, to justify a court blocking the order now.

## Where it stands

The Court has acted: it denied this stay application as moot, while granting a similar stay application in a related case (26A124), pausing the district court's injunction until the appeals process, including a potential cert petition, concludes. Justices Sotomayor, joined by Justice Kagan, and Justice Jackson separately dissented.

## What each outcome would mean

Alabama and the other states defending the executive order had asked the Court to pause the injunction. By granting the stay in the related case and denying this one as moot, the Court allowed the executive order to take effect while the First Circuit appeal and any further Supreme Court review proceed, a win for Alabama's position. California and the other states challenging the order do not get the order blocked during this period. The pause is temporary: it ends automatically if certiorari is denied, or upon final judgment if the Court takes up the case.
