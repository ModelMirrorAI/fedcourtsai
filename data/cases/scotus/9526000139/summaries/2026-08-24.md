## In brief

The Supreme Court stayed a lower court injunction that had blocked parts of a presidential election-integrity executive order, while Alabama's related stay request was denied as moot.

## What happened

An executive order directed federal agencies to compile citizen voter lists for states, prioritize prosecuting election-related crimes, and have the Postal Service write rules on mail ballot security. California and other states sued, arguing the order was unconstitutional; Alabama and other states intervened to defend it. A federal district court in Massachusetts blocked parts of the order covering the November 2026 election, and the First Circuit refused to pause that injunction pending appeal. Alabama and the other defending states then asked the Supreme Court to stay the injunction while their appeal continued.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court was asked whether the states challenging the executive order had legal standing to sue over it now, given that key details of how it would be carried out were still undecided, or whether their case was too premature for a court to decide. The states defending the order argued the challengers could not show a concrete, immediate harm.

## Where it stands

The Court denied Alabama's stay application as moot and instead granted a related stay application (No. 26A124) referred by Justice Jackson, pausing the district court's injunction pending the appeal and any later petition for Supreme Court review. The decision included a per curiam opinion and two dissents.

## What each outcome would mean

Alabama and other states defending the executive order had asked the Court to pause the injunction against it. By granting the stay in the related application, the Court allowed the executive order's challenged provisions to take effect while the First Circuit appeal and any further Supreme Court review proceed, a result favoring the defending states and the federal government over California and the other challenging states. The stay is temporary: it ends automatically if certiorari is later denied, or when judgment is issued if the Court takes up the case.
