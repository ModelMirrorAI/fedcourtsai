## In brief
The Court denied Alabama's request to pause a lower court's elections-policy injunction as moot after granting a stay in a related case.

## What happened
This application arose from litigation over a federal policy affecting elections, challenged by California and other states. A federal district court in Massachusetts entered an injunction against the policy, and the case was on appeal before the United States Court of Appeals for the First Circuit. Alabama and other states, as applicants, asked the Supreme Court to stay that injunction while the appeal proceeded. California and other states opposed the request, and several groups, including former judges, former governors, and civil-rights and legal organizations, filed briefs supporting California's opposition.

## What the Court is being asked
The Court was asked whether to temporarily pause the district court's injunction against the elections policy while the underlying appeal and any further Supreme Court review continued, without deciding the merits of the dispute itself.

## Where it stands
The Court denied this application as moot on August 24, 2026, the same day it granted a stay in a related application (No. 26A124) covering the same district court injunction. The order included a per curiam opinion and dissenting opinions from Justice Sotomayor, joined by Justice Kagan, and separately from Justice Jackson.

## What each outcome would mean
Alabama and other states, as applicants, sought to pause the injunction against the elections policy. Because the Court granted a stay of that same injunction through the related application, this application was denied as moot, meaning it no longer needed a separate ruling. The practical effect is that the injunction is stayed while the appeal proceeds in the First Circuit and while any petition for Supreme Court review is pursued, with the stay ending automatically if review is denied or upon judgment if it is granted.
