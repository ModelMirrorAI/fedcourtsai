## In brief

TMX Finance asks the Court to decide whether a Pennsylvania enforcement proceeding can block it from raising federal constitutional claims in federal court.

## What happened

TMX Finance Corporate Services provides back-office support for companies that make short-term loans in states like Texas and Georgia. Pennsylvania's banking regulator began a multi-year enforcement proceeding claiming TMX and related companies made loans to Pennsylvania residents that exceeded the state's interest-rate caps, even though the petition says the loans were made in stores in other states under those states' laws. TMX sued in federal court in Texas, arguing the proceeding violated the Constitution's limits on regulating across state lines and on reaching companies with no local ties. The district court dismissed the suit under a doctrine called Younger abstention, which lets federal courts step back when a state proceeding touches an important state interest. The Fifth Circuit affirmed.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked whether a state's broadly described interest in enforcing its laws is enough to let it keep a dispute out of federal court, even when the dispute involves regulating deals that out-of-state companies made with the state's residents while physically located in other states.

## Where it stands

The petition was distributed for the Court's conference of September 28, 2026. The respondent's deadline to respond has since been extended, most recently to November 2, 2026, so the docket does not yet show further conference action.

## What each outcome would mean

TMX Finance, which lost in the lower courts, is asking the Court to take up the case and reverse. If the Court grants review, it would hear the case fully and decide the legal question, deciding nothing yet about who wins. If it denies review, the Fifth Circuit's decision stands, leaving TMX Finance unable to pursue its federal claims in federal court while Pennsylvania's proceeding continues.
