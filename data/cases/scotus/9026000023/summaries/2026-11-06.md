## In brief

TMX Finance asks the Court to let it pursue federal claims against a Pennsylvania enforcement action rather than having federal courts step aside under a doctrine called abstention.

## What happened

Pennsylvania's banking regulator investigated TMX-affiliated companies over car-title loans allegedly made to Pennsylvania residents at interest rates exceeding state caps, then brought an administrative enforcement action seeking over $52 million in penalties. TMX Finance Corporate Services, which says it only provided back-office support and never made loans itself, sued in federal court in Texas, arguing the proceeding violates constitutional limits on a state regulating activity outside its borders and improperly targets a company with no ties to Pennsylvania. The district court dismissed the suit under a rule requiring federal courts to step back when important state proceedings are already underway. The Fifth Circuit affirmed. Related companies raising similar claims lost in the Third and Fourth Circuits as well.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked whether a state's general interest in enforcing its own laws is strong enough to justify a federal court stepping back from a company's constitutional challenge, when the challenged conduct is a state trying to regulate business dealings that happened across its border in other states. TMX argues courts should weigh this federalism question more carefully rather than accepting any broadly stated state interest; Pennsylvania's regulator responds that courts correctly look only to the general importance of the type of proceeding, not the specifics of the dispute.

## Where it stands

The petition was distributed for the Court's September 28, 2026 conference, and the same day the Court asked for a response, which Pennsylvania's regulator filed in November 2026 after two extensions. The docket shows no action yet on the petition itself.

## What each outcome would mean

TMX Finance, which lost before the Fifth Circuit, is asking the Court to take up its case and reverse that ruling. If the Court grants the petition, it would hear full briefing and argument and decide the abstention question itself, deciding nothing yet about who prevails. If it denies the petition, the Fifth Circuit's ruling stands, and TMX's federal suit remains dismissed while Pennsylvania's enforcement proceeding continues. Either way, no decision on the underlying constitutional claims occurs at this stage.
