# Forecast: what the Court will do with No. 26-23

**Disposition.** The Court will deny the petition, most likely at a January 2027 conference, without a noted dissent. Probability of any grant: 0.07.

**Where the docket stands.** The snapshot shows one distribution, entered Aug 12 2026 for the Sep 28 conference, superseded the same day by a call for a response after Pennsylvania had waived. The response is now due Nov 2 2026 after two extensions. The petition has therefore been considered at zero conferences, and the frozen count of 1 is the state I forecast from.

**Relists.** At least one further distribution is near-certain (0.96): once the brief in opposition and a reply are in, the Clerk will re-distribute the petition, most likely for the Jan 9 or Jan 16 2027 conference, paired with the companion petition No. 25-1359 whose response is due the same day. Beyond that re-distribution I expect at most one relist: the pair raises a real federalism question and will have a state amicus (South Carolina already filed in 25-1359), which can earn one extra look, but the Fourth Circuit's intervening decision makes a long relist chain unlikely.

**CVSG.** No (0.05). No federal party, no federal statute, and the Younger question does not implicate a program the Solicitor General administers. The SG's amicus participation in Suncor concerned substantive extraterritoriality, not abstention.

**Which question, if any.** The Court would not take the question as written; it is argumentative ("evade federal court") and framed around New Orleans Public Service's "generic" language. A grant would come with a reformulated question asking whether a State's interest in enforcing its laws against out-of-state transactions with its residents is an "important state interest" under Middlesex and Sprint. If the Court is interested in the issue at all, the likelier lead vehicle is No. 25-1359, which carries the actual lenders and six petitioners from the Third Circuit; this petition, from a back-office affiliate on an unpublished Fifth Circuit opinion, would be consolidated with it or held behind it.

**Summary disposition.** Conditional on a grant, I put the summary route (a GVR rather than plenary review) at 0.40. The main grant path for this petition is being held behind a plenary grant in 25-1359 and then GVR'd if the Court reverses there. The alternative is a consolidated plenary grant of both, which same counsel, parallel filings, and aligned response dates make roughly as likely. A GVR in light of Suncor is unlikely: Suncor, argued Oct 5 2026, concerns federal preemption of state-law climate claims, not Younger.

**Dissent from denial.** Unlikely (0.08). Younger denials rarely draw separate writings, and the Fourth Circuit's published affirmance gives any Justice inclined toward the federalism concern an easy reason to wait for a cleaner split.

**If granted, the merits (conditional only).** The Court would most likely reverse narrowly, holding that the important-state-interest inquiry must account for the territorial reach of the State's asserted interest and remanding for that analysis, rather than holding Younger categorically inapplicable to extraterritorial enforcement.
