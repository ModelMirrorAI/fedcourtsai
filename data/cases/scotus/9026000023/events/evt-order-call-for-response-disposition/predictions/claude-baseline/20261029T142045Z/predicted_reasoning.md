# Predicted course of the petition — TMX Finance Corporate Services v. Spicher (No. 26-23)

**State forecast from.** One distribution on record (for the 9/28/2026 conference), entered the same day the Court called for a response, after Pennsylvania had waived one. No CVSG. No amici on this docket.

**Disposition: denied (P(any grant) ≈ 9%).** This petition travels with a companion, *TitleMax of Virginia v. Spicher*, No. 25-1359: same counsel, same Pennsylvania Banking Department enforcement proceeding, same question (how generally a State may describe its "important interest" to trigger *Younger* abstention), arising from the Third Circuit instead of the Fifth. The Court called for a response there too, and that response is now due November 2, 2026. Expect the Court to handle the two petitions together. Most likely it denies both. If it takes the question, it is likelier to take the Third Circuit vehicle, which comes with a South Carolina amicus, and to hold this one, than to grant this petition on its own.

**Relists.** The petition will be distributed again once Pennsylvania's response and the reply are in. That should come late in 2026, roughly in step with the companion, quite possibly after a response-deadline extension like the ones the companion received. I expect one or two more conferences before a decision, not a long relist run.

**CVSG.** Not expected. A private lender is suing a state official over a state enforcement proceeding, and no federal program or federal party is implicated.

**Which question, if granted.** The single QP as written: whether a State's "generic" interest in regulating transactions its residents enter into with out-of-state companies outside its borders (*NOPSI*, 491 U.S. at 365) is important enough to support *Younger* abstention under the *Sprint* framework.

**Summary route.** If this petition reaches the grant family, the most likely path is a hold behind the companion and then a GVR after the companion is decided for the petitioners. Hence the conditional summary-route probability of about 0.6. A plenary grant of this petition itself, alone or consolidated, makes up the rest.

**Dissent from denial.** Unlikely. A denial here should come without separate writing. If anyone writes, Justice Thomas or Justice Gorsuch are the likeliest, on federal courts' obligation to exercise their jurisdiction.
