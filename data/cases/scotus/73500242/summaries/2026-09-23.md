## What happened

Blessing Nwosu, a Black woman, was dining at the Bloom & Bee restaurant in Houston's Post Oak Hotel when a hostess placed a wool shawl on her, saying her dress was too revealing under the restaurant's dress code. Nwosu says the shawl was forced on her without warning or consent, while other, similarly or more casually dressed non-Black patrons were left alone; the restaurant disputes some of these details. Nwosu sued the restaurant's owner under a federal civil rights law protecting equal contract rights, along with other claims. A federal district court granted summary judgment against her, and the Fifth Circuit Court of Appeals affirmed, reasoning that she was not denied any right protected by the statute because she was never forced to leave. The Fifth Circuit denied rehearing.

## What the Court is being asked

The case asks whether a customer who suffers discriminatory treatment at a restaurant, but chooses to stay and finish the meal rather than leave, can still sue over that treatment under the civil rights law protecting equal enjoyment of contract benefits. Nwosu argues the law protects the full experience of dining, not just whether a customer is allowed to complete the meal, and that the lower courts wrongly resolved disputed facts in the restaurant's favor at the summary-judgment stage. The restaurant maintains that enforcing a published dress code did not deny her any contractual benefit.

## Where it stands
