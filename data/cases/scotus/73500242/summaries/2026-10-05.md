## In brief

The Court declined to hear a restaurant patron's bid to revive her civil-rights lawsuit over a shawl an employee placed on her at dinner.

## What happened

Blessing Nwosu, a Black woman, was dining at the Bloom & Bee restaurant when a hostess placed a wool shawl on her, saying her dress violated the dress code. Nwosu says the shawl was forced on her without warning and that white patrons dressed similarly or more casually were not stopped. She sued the restaurant's owner under a federal civil rights law protecting contract rights, along with other claims. The district court granted summary judgment to the restaurant, finding her claim did not involve a type of harm the law covers. The Fifth Circuit affirmed, reasoning that a restaurant guest must show treatment severe enough to force her to leave, and Nwosu stayed for the meal.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked whether a patron can still sue over discriminatory treatment at a restaurant even if she chose to stay and finish her meal rather than leave, or whether staying defeats her claim as the lower court held. Nwosu also argues the appeals court wrongly resolved disputed facts against her instead of in her favor, as is required when courts decide cases without a trial.

## Where it stands

The petition was distributed for the Court's conference on September 28, 2026. On October 5, 2026, the Court denied the petition.

## What each outcome would mean

Nwosu, who lost in the lower courts, asked the Court to take up her case. With the petition denied, the Fifth Circuit's ruling against her stands, and her discrimination claim against the restaurant's owner remains dismissed. The denial does not mean the Court agreed with the lower court's reasoning; it simply leaves that decision in place without the Supreme Court reviewing it further.
