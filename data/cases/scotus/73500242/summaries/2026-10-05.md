## In brief

The Supreme Court declined to hear a Houston restaurant patron's bid to revive her race discrimination lawsuit over a shawl an employee placed on her.

## What happened

Blessing Nwosu, a Black woman, was dining at a Houston restaurant when a hostess placed a wool shawl on her, saying her outfit was too revealing. Nwosu says the shawl was forced on her without warning while white patrons dressed similarly or less formally were left alone. She sued the restaurant's owner under a federal civil rights law protecting contract rights, along with other claims. A federal district court granted summary judgment to the restaurant, finding her discrimination claim did not involve a type of harm the law covers. The Fifth Circuit Court of Appeals affirmed, and later denied rehearing.

## What the Court is being asked

The case asks whether a restaurant customer who experiences discriminatory treatment, but stays and finishes her meal rather than being forced to leave, can still sue under the civil rights law at issue. Nwosu's petition argues the lower court wrongly required proof she was pushed out of the restaurant, and separately argues the appeals court ignored disputed facts that should have been resolved in her favor at this stage of the case.

## Where it stands

The petition was distributed for the Court's conference on September 28, 2026, and on October 5, 2026, the Court denied the petition.

## What each outcome would mean

Nwosu, who lost in the lower courts, asked the Court to take up her case. By denying the petition, the Court leaves the Fifth Circuit's ruling in place: the restaurant's win stands, and Nwosu's civil rights claim over the shawl incident remains dismissed. The denial does not mean the Court agreed with the Fifth Circuit's reasoning, only that it chose not to review the case, leaving that decision as the final word in this litigation.
