# Forecast: what the Court will do with No. 25-1340, Nwosu v. 1600 West Loop South, L.L.C.

**Disposition.** The Court will deny the petition on the order list following the September 28, 2026 conference (the Term-opening long conference), most likely on October 5, 2026, without comment.

**Relists.** The docket shows exactly one distribution (July 15, 2026, for the September 28 conference), so the state I forecast from is "distributed once, never relisted". I expect no further distribution: the petition is unopposed (the response deadline of July 1 passed with no brief in opposition or waiver on the record and the Court distributed anyway), so the only paths to a second distribution are a call for a response or a hold/reschedule out of the long conference. Neither is likely for a fact-bound petition from an unpublished per curiam affirmance. I put the chance of at least one more distribution at about 15 percent, almost all of it a routine long-conference reschedule or a call for a response rather than a merits relist.

**CVSG.** None. Both parties are private, no federal statute's administration is at stake in a way that implicates the United States, and section 1981 public-accommodation claims are not an area where the Court solicits the Solicitor General. Essentially zero chance.

**Which question the Court would take, if any.** The petition presents one question, written around this plaintiff's facts. If the Court granted plenary review it would reformulate it as the general question of whether a section 1981 plaintiff alleging discriminatory treatment during a restaurant meal must show harassment severe enough to force her to leave, or whether the "benefits, privileges, terms, and conditions" language of section 1981(b) reaches discriminatory indignities short of expulsion. The summary-judgment argument (Part I of the petition) would not be a plenary question.

**Summary route.** Conditional on any grant, the summary route is the likelier one, roughly 60 percent. The petition expressly asks for a Tolan v. Cotton-style summary reversal for misapplying the summary-judgment standard, and the plenary vehicle is poor (unpublished, one-plaintiff, no brief in opposition, no amici). No intervening decision of this Court bears on the section 1981 restaurant standard, so a GVR is unlikely; a summary disposition would be a per curiam reversal on the Rule 56 point, which the Court does perhaps once or twice a Term and almost never in a private civil-rights case of this profile.

**Dissent from denial.** I expect none. The fact pattern (a Black diner singled out for a shawl in an otherwise white dining room) is the kind that could draw a short statement from Justice Sotomayor or Justice Jackson about appellate courts resolving factual disputes against non-movants, but such statements attach overwhelmingly to published opinions with broader consequences or to qualified-immunity cases. About 4 percent.

**If granted (conditional only).** The Court would hold that section 1981(b)'s "enjoyment of all benefits, privileges, terms, and conditions of the contractual relationship" is not limited to the ability to complete the transaction, reject a "forced to leave" threshold as a categorical requirement, and remand for application of the ordinary McDonnell Douglas framework to the third prima facie element. That holding would be textual and modestly broad (a standard for in-progress service contracts generally, not just restaurants).
