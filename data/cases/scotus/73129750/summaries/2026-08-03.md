## In brief

The Supreme Court upheld President Trump's executive order denying automatic citizenship to children born in the U.S. to parents who were undocumented or only temporarily present.

## What happened

President Trump signed an executive order in January 2025 stating that children born in the U.S. are not automatically citizens if their mother was in the country unlawfully or only temporarily, and their father was not a citizen or lawful permanent resident. Several families, including a woman identified as Barbara, sued, arguing the order violates the Constitution's Citizenship Clause and a related federal statute. A New Hampshire federal district court certified a nationwide class of affected children and blocked the order. The First Circuit Court of Appeals had ruled against the government in a related case. The government asked the Supreme Court to hear this case before the appeals court ruled on it.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked whether a rule that grants citizenship to nearly everyone born on U.S. soil, regardless of their parents' immigration status, is required by the Constitution and by a federal law using similar language, or whether that guarantee instead depends on parents having lived in the country with authorization to stay. The government argued citizenship should depend on parents owing primary allegiance to the United States, shown through lawful permanent residence; the families argued the constitutional guarantee covers all children born here except for narrow historical exceptions like children of foreign diplomats.

## Where it stands

The Court granted the government's petition, received full briefing and oral argument, and on June 30, 2026 affirmed the lower court's decision, with several justices writing separately in concurrence or dissent.

## What each outcome would mean

The Trump administration, as petitioner, asked the Court to uphold its executive order. The Court's affirmance means the lower court's injunction blocking the order stands, so the families and the certified class of children prevail: children born to parents who are undocumented or only temporarily present continue to receive automatic birthright citizenship under the Constitution's Citizenship Clause, and the executive order cannot be enforced against them.
