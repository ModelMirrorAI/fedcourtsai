## What happened

President Trump signed Executive Order 14,160, which would deny automatic citizenship to children born in the United States if their mother lacked permanent immigration status and their father was not a citizen or lawful permanent resident. Several affected families, identified by pseudonyms including "Barbara," sued in New Hampshire federal district court. The district court certified a nationwide class and blocked the order, agreeing with challengers that it violated both a federal citizenship statute and the Constitution's Citizenship Clause. Rather than wait for the First Circuit to rule, the government asked the Supreme Court directly to review the case, and a related First Circuit panel separately ruled the order unlawful too.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked to decide whether children born in the United States to parents without permanent immigration status are citizens at birth. That requires deciding whether a federal statute defining citizenship, and separately the Constitution's guarantee of citizenship for those born in the country and subject to its authority, cover such children, or whether the government may read in a requirement that a parent be permanently and lawfully settled here for a child to qualify.

## Where it stands

The Court granted review before any appeals court judgment, heard full briefing and argument on April 1, 2026, with many amicus briefs filed on both sides, and on June 30, 2026 affirmed the lower court's ruling against the executive order, with multiple separate opinions among the justices.
