# Reasoning for my numbers

**What I read.** The snapshot `2026-11-19.json` (one entry: the stay application submitted to Justice Alito on 18 Nov 2026; capital case; linked with 26-6187; from the Texas Court of Criminal Appeals, WR-34,627-03), `context.json` (forward mode, arrival-position cut, band null as expected for an interim cell, no response requested, not referred, zero amicus), and `application.txt` (6 pages, full text, not truncated). No response or reply was provisioned; none exists on my record.

**The application.** A five-page stay request filed the day before a scheduled 19 November 2026 execution. Its only ground is a Wiggins v. Smith ineffective-assistance claim (trial counsel's thin mitigation case), raised in a subsequent state habeas application that the TCCA dismissed as an abuse of the writ under Article 11.071 § 5 without reaching the merits. The application argues likelihood of success in one paragraph, by reference to the petition, and does not address the state procedural bar or the timing of the claim.

**Base rates.** From the statpack's interim section I computed the scored pool for an OT2026 application: ten prior application-Terms, 156 granted of 1,337 resolved, 11.7%, well above the 50-resolved floor. That is the yardstick, but it is the wrong population for this case. The applicant-class table's private/capital cell is 10 of 288, 3.5%, and that cell is the one I leaned on. The caption class (private) matches my own reading of the applicant.

**Adjustments downward from 3.5%.** 
- The judgment below rests on a state procedural ground (the § 5 bar), which the Court ordinarily treats as adequate and independent; that is a jurisdictional obstacle to certiorari, not only a merits weakness.
- The claim is a fact-bound trial-IAC claim on a decades-old conviction, raised in a successive application on the eve of execution; the Court's stay cases weigh last-minute filing heavily against the applicant.
- No circuit split, no novel legal question, and no method-of-execution or religious-liberty issue of the kind that has drawn the rare recent stays.
- The private/capital cell's 10 grants are concentrated in cases with a distinct legal hook; an ordinary successive-habeas Wiggins claim from Texas sits in the bulk of that cell that is denied.

I land at 0.015. I did not go lower because the application is counselled by a federal defender's capital habeas unit and I have not seen the certiorari petition, which may be stronger than the stay application's summary.

**Ladder claims.** Referral 0.93: in capital stay applications the Circuit Justice almost always refers to the full Court; the statpack's referred count (877 of 1,619 substantive, right-censored and all asks) is consistent with referral being the norm for the capital subset, but that inference is mine, not a published cut. Response requested 0.03: the State answers execution-day applications without being asked, and there is no time for a formal call. Amicus 0.01: one day, no sign of organised interest.

**Order writers.** Small numbers throughout; Sotomayor (0.04) and Jackson (0.03) are the Justices who most often write or note dissents from capital stay denials, though usually on method or procedure claims rather than a barred Wiggins claim.

**Corpus.** One priors query (recent SCOTUS denials including applications) returned a same-week capital stay denial (Overton v. Florida) among mostly unrelated rows; it did not move my number. A docket-number lookup of the linked petition 26-6187 showed it docketed 18 Nov 2026 with no distribution or disposition. I made no web or CourtListener lookup and did not check the live docket, so I do not know whether the Court has already acted; the execution date is today and the outcome may exist by the time this is read.

**Where to discount me.** I have not read the certiorari petition, the TCCA order or the State's opposition. If the TCCA order mixed a merits assessment into its § 5 ruling, the independent-state-ground obstacle is weaker than I assumed.
