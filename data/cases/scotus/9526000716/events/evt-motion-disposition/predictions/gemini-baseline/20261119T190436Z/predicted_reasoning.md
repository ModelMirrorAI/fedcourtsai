# Predicted Court Trajectory and Disposition (`26A716`)

At the arrival baseline, Application `26A716` sits solely as submitted on November 18, 2026 to Justice Alito as Circuit Justice for the Fifth Circuit, with no response requested, no referral to the full Court, and zero amicus briefs docketed.

## Procedural Path Before Disposition

- **Response request (`response-requested-increment`): Unlikely.** Because this is an execution-eve capital stay application filed the night before a scheduled November 19, 2026 Texas execution, the Office of the Attorney General of Texas will file a response in opposition voluntarily on November 19 without awaiting a formal briefing call from Justice Alito or the Clerk. The docket will record a filed State opposition rather than a formal `"Response ... requested"` entry.
- **Full-Court referral (`referral-increment`): Very likely.** Consistent with settled Supreme Court practice on capital stay-of-execution applications—as distinct from routine non-capital applications—Justice Alito will refer `26A716` to the full Court rather than denying a stay of execution in chambers alone.
- **Amicus participation (`amicus-increment`): None expected.** Fewer than twenty-four hours separate the November 18 evening filing from the scheduled execution, and the application turns on a fact-bound subsequent state habeas bar rather than an institutional or method-of-execution question attracting organized amici. Zero amicus briefs will be docketed before disposition.

## Disposition and Order Form

The full Court will **deny** the application for a stay of execution on November 19, 2026 in a single-sentence, unsigned order (`"The application for stay of execution of sentence of death presented to Justice Alito and by him referred to the Court is denied."`). The Court will neither enter an administrative stay nor treat the application as a petition for a writ of certiorari and grant review.

The Court will leave the Texas Court of Criminal Appeals' ("TCCA") dismissal in `WR-34,627-03` undisturbed because the TCCA's abuse-of-the-writ dismissal under Texas Code of Criminal Procedure art. 11.071, § 5 rests on an independent and adequate state procedural ground barring Supreme Court certiorari jurisdiction on linked petition `26-6187`, and because Dennes cannot show four votes for certiorari, a significant possibility of reversal on his successive *Wiggins v. Smith* trial-mitigation claim nearly thirty years after his 1996 offense, or equitable entitlement to last-minute relief.

No Justice is expected to author a separate dissent, concurrence, or statement on the order. If any separate writing appears at all, it would be a brief dissent from denial of a stay authored by Justice Sotomayor or Justice Jackson.

If the Court were unexpectedly to grant a stay, it would grant a stay of execution pending the disposition of the linked petition for a writ of certiorari (`No. 26-6187`)—terminating if certiorari is denied or upon issuance of the judgment if granted—exclusively to examine the threshold jurisdictional question whether a boilerplate TCCA dismissal under Art. 11.071, § 5(a)(1) and § 5(a)(3) necessarily incorporates an antecedent federal-law determination under *Strickland*/*Wiggins* or *Sawyer v.
