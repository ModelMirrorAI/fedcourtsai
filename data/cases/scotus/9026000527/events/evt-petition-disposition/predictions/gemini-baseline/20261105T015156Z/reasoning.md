3. **Interlocutory Posture**:
   The Second Circuit's June 8, 2026 judgment vacated the Southern District of New York's partial summary judgment on issue preclusion and remanded the case for the district court to adjudicate the merits of Cesari's Lanham Act trademark infringement claim (`App. 2a, 10a`). Even without issue preclusion from the 2004 TTAB registration refusal, Cesari remains free on remand to prove likelihood of confusion under the *Polaroid* factors, and the Second Circuit affirmed dismissal of Peju's counterclaims (`App. 10a–14a`). The Supreme Court routinely denies interlocutory petitions where the petitioner may still prevail on remand or seek review after final judgment.

4. **Non-Precedential Summary Order Below and Fact-Bound Application of *B&B Hardware***:
   - The decision below (`App. 1a–14a`, Judges Wesley, Sullivan, and Bianco) is an unpublished Summary Order that expressly bears "NO PRECEDENTIAL EFFECT" under Second Circuit Local Rule 32.1.1 (`App. 1a`).
   - On the merits, the Second Circuit did not defy *B&B Hardware, Inc. v. Hargis Industries, Inc.*, 575 U.S. 138 (2015). In *B&B Hardware*, this Court held that TTAB likelihood-of-confusion decisions have issue-preclusive effect "[s]o long as the other ordinary elements of issue preclusion are met, when the usages adjudicated by the TTAB are materially the same as those before the district court" (575 U.S. at 160), while cautioning that "for a great many registration decisions issue preclusion obviously will not apply" because the TTAB typically analyzes marks and goods only as set forth in the application regardless of actual marketplace usage (575 U.S. at 156–57; see also *id.* at 160 (Ginsburg, J., concurring)).
   - In the 2004 TTAB opposition (`App. 31a–34a`), the TTAB sua sponte converted a motion for judgment on the pleadings into summary judgment and refused Peju's application to register "LIANA" for "wine" based strictly on the four corners of the unrestricted application, expressly declining to consider Peju's evidence that it used LIANA on a California late-harvest dessert wine whereas Cesari used LIANO on an Italian red table wine (`App. 34a`).
   - In the subsequent infringement suit, Judge Buchwald granted partial summary judgment applying issue preclusion (`App. 15a–28a`), but the Second Circuit vacated because the 2004 TTAB decision never examined the parties' actual marketplace usages—including the distinct varieties of wine, direct-to-consumer winery/website sales channels, and Peju's prominent house mark (`App. 7a–10a`). Although the panel cited *Jim Beam Brands Co. v. Beamish & Crawford Ltd.*, 937 F.2d 729 (2d Cir. 1991), and *Levy v. Kosher Overseers Ass'n of America, Inc.*, 104 F.3d 38 (2d Cir. 1997), alongside *B&B Hardware*, its holding turned on the fact-specific gap between the abstract registration refusal in 2004 and the marketplace context in the infringement suit.
   - Petitioner's attempt to link this case to *RiseandShine Corp. v. PepsiCo, Inc.*, No. 24-1016 (cert. granted June 29, 2026) (`Pet. 16–18`), is unpersuasive: *RiseandShine* concerns whether appellate review of summary judgment on trademark strength/likelihood of confusion is de novo and how inherent conceptual strength is evaluated on summary judgment, not TTAB issue preclusion (`same-question.json` likewise shows zero matching petitions).
