# Forecast: Wilson v. Watts, No. 25-1304 (evt-petition-disposition)

**Disposition.** The Court will deny the petition on the order list following the September 28, 2026 long conference, most likely the October 5, 2026 order list, without comment.

**Relists.** The docket shows one distribution (July 8, 2026, for the conference of September 28, 2026) and no relist. I expect no further distribution: the petition will be considered once and denied. The residual chance of a second distribution entry comes mainly from a reschedule or a clerical redistribution at the long conference rather than from any Justice holding the case.

**CVSG.** None. Both sides are private Alabama litigants, the judgment rests on a state-court jury verdict, and no federal statute or federal-government interest is implicated. The Solicitor General has no stake the Court would ask about.

**Which question, if any, the Court would take.** Neither as written. Question 1 asks whether a state supreme court may affirm a punitive award without a written opinion; the TXO plurality already said an unexplained denial of remittitur is not itself a due-process violation, and the Court does not police state appellate courts' no-opinion affirmance practice. Question 2 restates the same complaint about the trial court. If the Court were somehow interested in punitive-damages excessiveness again, it would wait for a counseled petition presenting a large ratio and a reasoned lower-court opinion, not a 2.2-to-1 award affirmed without opinion on a pro se petition.

**Summary disposition.** No grant of any kind is expected. Conditional on the very unlikely event of a grant, a summary route (a GVR in light of some intervening punitive-damages decision, or a short per curiam) is somewhat likelier than plenary review, because nothing about this record recommends it as a vehicle for argument. No intervening decision that would prompt a GVR is known to me as of the snapshot date.

**Dissent from denial.** None expected. Justices who have questioned the Gore line have done so to argue for *less* federal constitutional supervision of punitive awards, which points toward denial, not toward writing in the petitioner's favor. A statement respecting denial is very unlikely on a pro se petition of this shape.
