## What happened

Robert T. Wilson, Jr., a disbarred lawyer, was sued by Charles Randall Watts and by Mark E. Wilson after Watts learned that Wilson had taken trust funds meant for Watts. A Walker County, Alabama jury awarded Watts $200,000 in compensatory damages and $800,000 in punitive damages, on top of an earlier $160,696.46 summary judgment. Wilson asked the trial court to reduce the punitive award, arguing it was constitutionally excessive; the court held a hearing, explained its reasoning on the record, and denied the request. The Alabama Supreme Court affirmed without issuing a written opinion, citing an appellate rule allowing affirmance when an opinion would serve no precedential purpose. Wilson, representing himself, then asked the U.S. Supreme Court to review the case.

## What the Court is being asked

Wilson argues that a state supreme court cannot simply affirm, with no opinion or explanation, a punitive damages award challenged as excessive, and that doing so denies meaningful review of whether the punishment fits constitutional limits. He also argues the whole process denied him due process because courts never substantively explained their reasoning. Mark Wilson responds that the trial court did meaningfully review the award at a hearing, explaining its reasoning on the record, and that no prior ruling requires an appellate court to issue a written opinion explaining that review.
