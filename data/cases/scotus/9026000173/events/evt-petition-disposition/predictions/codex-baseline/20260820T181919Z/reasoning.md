# Rationale

I assign P(any grant) = 0.0015 and predict denial.

The committed statpack's sal-v3 table is compatible with the frozen `baseline` band. Pooling the bracketed `reached` rates over OT2017 through OT2025, all strictly before this OT2026 petition, gives an estimated paid-segment grant-family rate of about 6.5% over a weighted denominator of 13,163. That is the risk-set anchor, not my case-specific forecast. The terminal baseline band is much weaker (0.8% plenary grants plus 0.4% GVRs across the live/historical slice), and the modern Fourth Circuit cut is also below the broad paid risk set (1.3% granted plus 1.3% GVR). These terminal cuts describe population shape rather than the petition's forward hazard.

The case-specific evidence warrants a sharp downward adjustment. The petition is pro se and presents compound, fact-intensive questions about the handling of an administrative record and one military-installation debarment. It principally alleges that the lower courts committed reversible error. Its asserted circuit split is stated as a list of circuits said to apply the record rule differently, without identifying conflicting holdings on a common legal question. The petition also mixes the APA issue with vehicle-specific constitutional theories and relies on strained authorities, including treating *State Farm Mutual Automobile Insurance Co. v. Campbell* as a record-rule case. The Fourth Circuit disposition is unpublished and described in the petition as a conclusion that there was no reversible error, making this a poor vehicle for broad clarification.

The snapshot and provisioned documents were complete enough to assess the petition: I read the 49-page petition and extracted questions presented. No brief in opposition was provisioned. Instead, the federal respondent waived its right to respond on August 19, a negative attention signal, though the Court could still request a response. A CVSG is especially implausible because the Solicitor General already represents the federal respondent.

The 0.98 relist-increment number needs a data-quality qualification. `record/context.json` freezes `distribution_count` at one, but the only recorded distribution was May 19, before this paid petition was received and docketed on August 6; it appears linked to the earlier veteran-status motion denied June 8. I therefore expect the mechanical count to rise when the petition receives its first actual cert conference distribution. This is not a substantive forecast of a true relist. From that first actual conference, I expect no further distribution.

The summary-route probability is conditional on the very unlikely event of a grant. I set it at 0.12 because no intervening precedent suggests a GVR, while the alleged record error would require engagement with a case-specific procedural history. The conditional probability of a separate writing on denial is 0.001 because the case offers neither a clean split nor a recurring question in a suitable vehicle.

Two CourtListener MCP searches supplied no additional merits material. An opinions search for Fourth Circuit docket 24-1166 returned no results; a RECAP search confirmed the docket caption but exposed no filing or decision text. I therefore relied on the provisioned petition's account of the proceedings and discount that one-sided characterization.
