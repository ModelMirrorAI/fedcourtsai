The Court will deny the petition. The docket snapshot records one distribution, but that May 19 distribution predates the paid petition's August 6 docketing and appears to concern the linked veteran-status motion that was denied June 8. From the harness-frozen count of one, I assign a 98% probability that the count rises: the paid petition should receive its own initial conference distribution after the respondent's waiver. I expect no genuine relist after that conference and a denial on the ensuing order list.

The Court will not call for the views of the Solicitor General. The federal agency is already represented by the Solicitor General, and it has waived response. If the Court takes any preliminary interest, a request for a response is the procedurally natural step; even that is unlikely on this record.

If review were granted, the Court would most plausibly narrow the first question to whether an APA reviewing court may rely on material outside the administrative record that was not before the agency. It would leave the petition's fact-bound debarment, jurisdiction, and constitutional questions aside. A cert-order disposition is unlikely even conditional on a grant: no intervening decision supplies an evident GVR ground, and the alleged error is contested and record-dependent rather than an obvious candidate for summary reversal.

The denial will carry no noted dissent and no statement respecting denial.
