# Forecast: what the Court will do with No. 26-173, Jones v. Defense Supply Center, DLA

**Disposition.** The petition will be **denied**, without comment, on the order list following the September 28, 2026 long conference — most likely the October 5, 2026 order list.

**Relists.** The docket shows two `DISTRIBUTED` entries, but they belong to two different filings: the May 19 distribution (for the June 4 conference) was of the petitioner's motion for leave to proceed as a veteran, filed under the linked miscellaneous docket 25M81 and denied June 8; the August 26 distribution (for the September 28 conference) is the petition's own first distribution. So the petition itself sits at a **single distribution and has never been relisted**. I forecast **no further distribution**: the petition will be disposed of at the long conference. The residual chance of one more distribution is a reschedule off the long conference or a request for a response, neither of which this record suggests.

**CVSG.** None. The respondent is the United States (Defense Logistics Agency, represented by the Solicitor General), so there is no non-party federal interest for the Court to invite; the Solicitor General has already spoken by waiving.

**Response.** The Solicitor General waived the right to respond on August 19. The Court will **not** call for a response.

**Which question presented, if any.** If the Court took anything, it would be a reformulated version of QP 1 — the scope of the exceptions to the APA record rule (extra-record supplementation in a 5 U.S.C. § 706 review). It would not take QP 2 (the compound commander-jurisdiction / record-rule question), which is fact-bound and rests on a contested reading of a single declaration in the administrative record. This is context only; I expect neither to be taken.

**Summary disposition versus plenary review.** Conditional on any grant, the likelier route is a **GVR** rather than plenary review — a pro se, unpublished-below APA case is not a plenary vehicle, and a grant here would almost certainly be a remand in light of some intervening decision. No such intervening decision is presently visible, which is one reason the unconditional grant probability is so low.

**Dissent from denial.** None expected. No Justice will note a dissent from or a statement respecting the denial: the case presents no clean legal question, the decision below is an unpublished affirmance, and the federal respondent did not engage.

**Timing.** Denial on the first October order list (October 5, 2026), roughly 60 days after docketing — the ordinary long-conference cadence for a waived paid petition.
