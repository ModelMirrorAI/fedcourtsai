# Forecast — Jones v. Defense Supply Center, DLA (No. 26-173)

**Disposition: the petition will be denied without comment, most likely at or
shortly after the fall long conference.** The respondent (represented by the
Solicitor General) waived its right to respond on August 19, 2026, so the
petition is ready for distribution now. Expect it to be distributed once for
an early-OT2026 conference and denied on the order list that follows.

**Relists.** The snapshot records one distribution (May 19, 2026, for the
June 4 conference — a distribution of the miscellaneous-docket motion phase,
before the petition was re-docketed as 26-173 on August 6). From that state, I
expect exactly one further distribution: the routine conference distribution
of the now-complete petition. I do not expect a relist beyond that single
further distribution — nothing here is the kind of petition the Court carries
over. So: at least one more distribution, near-certain (~0.97); two or more
further distributions, unlikely (~0.10).

**CVSG: none will issue.** The United States is already the respondent and the
Solicitor General is counsel of record; a CVSG is an invitation to a
non-party government, so the mechanism has no application here. The SG's
views, if the Court wanted them, would arrive as a called-for response — and
even that is unlikely (~0.05) given the waiver and the petition's weakness.

**Which question presented, if taken.** If the Court took anything (it will
not), it would be a cleaned-up version of QP 1 — whether a reviewing court may
uphold agency action on materials outside the administrative record — not the
compound, fact-entangled QP 2 about the commander's jurisdiction. But this
vehicle is poor: pro se presentation, an apparently unpublished Fourth Circuit
affirmance, and a split asserted in broad strokes without developed authority.

**Summary disposition.** Conditional on any grant-family action, a GVR or
summary order is the far likelier route than plenary review (~0.75
conditional) — the Court would not set a case in this posture for argument.
Unconditionally it is negligible, since no intervening decision of the Court
bears on record-rule supplementation in a way that would prompt a GVR.

**Dissent from denial: none expected.** A fact-bound pro se APA case against a
federal agency draws no separate writing; probability of any noted dissent or
statement respecting denial ~0.01.
