## In brief

A former defense agency contractor asks the Court to review his court battle over being barred from a military installation.

## What happened

William David Jones, a former employee at a Defense Logistics Agency facility, was barred from the installation by its commander. He challenged that debarment in federal district court under the law governing review of agency actions, arguing the agency's decision could not stand because the commander herself stated under oath that he had broken no law and that she had no authority over him. The district court upheld the debarment, relying partly on documents Jones says the agency never used when it made its original decision. The Fourth Circuit Court of Appeals affirmed that ruling, and later denied rehearing.

## What the Court is being asked

The case raises two questions. First, whether courts reviewing an agency's action may rely only on the file the agency actually used to make its decision, or whether they may also consider other documents added later, and whether adding those documents here was a harmful mistake. Second, whether the commander had the authority to bar Jones from the installation at all, given her own sworn statement that he had violated no law and that she had no authority over him.

## What each outcome would mean

Jones, the petitioner, is asking the Court to take up his case. If the Court grants the petition, it will hear full arguments and decide the record-rule and authority questions itself, deciding nothing yet about who is right. If it denies the petition, the Fourth Circuit's decision affirming the debarment and the agency's actions stands, and Jones's challenge ends without further Supreme Court review.
