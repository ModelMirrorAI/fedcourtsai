## What happened

William David Jones, a former civilian employee of the Defense Logistics Agency's Defense Supply Center in Richmond, was barred from the installation by its commander. Jones sued the agency in federal district court, arguing the debarment was improper. The district court reviewed the agency's decision under the federal law governing court review of agency actions and granted summary judgment for the agency, relying in part on documents the agency added to its official record during the litigation. Jones appealed to the United States Court of Appeals for the Fourth Circuit, which affirmed. The Fourth Circuit later denied rehearing. Jones, representing himself, now asks the Supreme Court to review that outcome.

## What the Court is being asked

The case raises two issues. First, whether the lower courts wrongly allowed the agency to rely on documents and records that were added after the fact, rather than limiting review to the material the agency actually used when it made its original decision. Jones argues that letting agencies supplement the record this way undermines a rule that is supposed to keep judicial review of agency actions confined to what the agency actually considered, and that federal appeals courts are divided on when such additions are permitted. Second, whether the base commander exceeded her authority by barring Jones when, according to Jones, she stated under oath that he had not violated any law and that she had no jurisdiction over him.
