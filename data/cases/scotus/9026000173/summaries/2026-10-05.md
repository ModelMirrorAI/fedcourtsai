## In brief

A former defense agency employee asked the Court to review his barment from a military installation, but the Court declined.

## What happened

William David Jones, a former employee of the Defense Logistics Agency's Defense Supply Center in Richmond, was barred from the installation after an investigation into alleged threats against agency employees. He sued in federal district court under the law governing review of agency actions, arguing the agency's barment decision was unsupported and improperly reviewed. The district court ruled against him, and the Fourth Circuit Court of Appeals affirmed that decision. Jones, representing himself, then asked the Supreme Court to take up his case.

## What the Court is being asked

Jones raised two issues. First, he argued the lower courts wrongly let the agency rely on documents outside the official record it had used to make its decision, when review of agency action is supposed to be limited to that original record. Second, he argued the military commander who barred him lacked authority to do so, since she stated under oath that he had broken no law and that she had no jurisdiction over him.

## Where it stands

The petition was distributed for conference in June 2026; a motion filed alongside it was denied. The agency waived its right to respond, the petition was distributed again for a later conference in September 2026, and the Supreme Court denied the petition on October 5, 2026.

## What each outcome would mean

Jones, as the petitioner, asked the Court to take up his case and overturn the Fourth Circuit's ruling against him. The Court's denial means it will not hear the case, leaving the Fourth Circuit's decision in place. That decision upheld the lower court's ruling against Jones and left his barment from the installation standing. The denial does not mean the Supreme Court agreed the lower courts were correct; it simply leaves their rulings as the final word in this case.
