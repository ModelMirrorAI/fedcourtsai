## In brief

A former defense agency employee's petition asking the Court to review his barred-from-base order and how the lower courts handled the agency's evidence was denied.

## What happened

William David Jones, a former employee of the Defense Logistics Agency's Defense Supply Center in Richmond, was barred from the installation by its commander in 2018. He sued in federal district court, arguing the agency's decision violated his constitutional rights and that the commander lacked authority over him since he no longer worked there. The district court granted the agency summary judgment, relying in part on documents the agency submitted beyond its original case file. The Fourth Circuit Court of Appeals affirmed that decision, and the full appeals court later denied rehearing.

## What the Court is being asked

Jones asked the Court to decide two things: first, whether courts reviewing agency decisions may consider documents an agency adds later, beyond the file it used when making its original decision, and whether doing so here was a harmful error; second, whether the commander exceeded her authority by barring Jones from the installation after she stated under oath that he had broken no law and that she had no authority over him.

## Where it stands

The petition was distributed for conference twice, in June and September 2026. The government, representing the Defense Logistics Agency, waived its right to respond. On October 5, 2026, the Court denied the petition.

## What each outcome would mean

Jones, as the petitioner, asked the Court to take up his case. By denying the petition, the Court leaves the Fourth Circuit's decision in place, meaning the agency's barment decision and the summary judgment against Jones stand. The denial does not mean the Court agreed with the lower courts' reasoning; it simply means the case ends there, without the Supreme Court reviewing the record-handling or authority questions Jones raised.
