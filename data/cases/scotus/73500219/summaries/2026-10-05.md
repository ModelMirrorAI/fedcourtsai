## In brief

The Court declined to hear Erik Maund's bid to restore a new trial after jurors saw evidence never admitted at his murder-for-hire trial.

## What happened

A jury convicted Erik Maund of conspiracy to commit murder-for-hire. Afterward, the district court discovered that jurors had received exhibits that were never admitted into evidence, including a recording in which a co-defendant said Maund's alleged accomplice "didn't know any of this shit" — information that undercut part of Maund's defense. The district court held a hearing, found the jury had considered this material, and granted Maund a new trial, calling the error "fundamental unfairness." The government appealed, and the Sixth Circuit reversed, holding the error was harmless because it believed the evidence against Maund was strong enough that the outcome would not have changed.

## What the Court is being asked

The question is what a defendant must show to get a new trial after jurors are exposed to information outside the evidentiary record. One approach asks only whether the outside information probably swayed the jury against the defendant. Another approach goes further, asking whether the defendant would have been convicted anyway even without that information. Maund's petition says courts are split on which approach applies.

## Where it stands

The petition was distributed for the Court's conference on September 28, 2026, after the government waived its right to respond. On October 5, 2026, the Court denied the petition.

## What each outcome would mean

Maund, who lost in the Sixth Circuit, asked the Court to take up his case. By denying the petition, the Court leaves the Sixth Circuit's ruling in place: Maund's conviction stands, and he does not get the new trial the district court had ordered. A denial does not mean the Court agreed with the Sixth Circuit's reasoning — it simply leaves that decision as the final word in this case, without the Supreme Court resolving the disagreement among lower courts that Maund's petition described.
