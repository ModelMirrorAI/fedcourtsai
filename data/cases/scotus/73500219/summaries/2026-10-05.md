## In brief

The Court declined to hear a convicted man's bid to overturn an appeals court ruling that reinstated his murder-for-hire conspiracy conviction despite a jury's exposure to improper evidence.

## What happened

Erik Maund was convicted of conspiracy to commit murder-for-hire. After the verdict, the trial court discovered the jury had received evidence exhibits that were never formally admitted, including a statement bearing on a codefendant's knowledge of the crime. The district court held a hearing, found the jury had considered this material, and ordered a new trial, calling the error "fundamental unfairness." The government appealed, and the Sixth Circuit reversed, holding the error was harmless because, in its view, the evidence against Maund was strong enough that the outcome would not have changed. Maund then asked the Supreme Court to review that decision.

## What the Court is being asked

Maund asked the Court to decide what standard applies when a jury considers evidence it should never have seen. He argued a new trial should be required whenever such information influenced the jury's deliberations against the defendant, while the Sixth Circuit's approach lets a court instead excuse the error if it concludes, in hindsight, that the result would have been the same anyway. Maund's petition described lower courts as split on which approach governs.

## Where it stands

The government waived its right to respond to the petition. The Court considered the petition at its September 28, 2026 conference and denied it on October 5, 2026.

## What each outcome would mean

Maund, as the petitioner, asked the Court to take up his case. With the petition denied, the Court will not hear the case, and the Sixth Circuit's decision reversing the new-trial order stands, leaving Maund's conviction in place. A grant would instead have meant the Court would hear full briefing and argument and decide which legal standard applies to jury exposure to improper evidence, without itself determining who should ultimately win.
