## In brief

A man convicted of conspiring to commit murder-for-hire asks the Court to decide what standard applies when a jury sees evidence it should never have received.

## What happened

After a jury convicted Erik Maund of conspiring to commit murder-for-hire, the trial court discovered that jurors had received unadmitted exhibits, including material both sides had agreed to keep out because it undercut Maund's defense. The district court held a hearing, found the jury had considered this material, and ordered a new trial, calling the error a fundamental unfairness. The government appealed, and the Sixth Circuit reversed, holding that because the evidence against Maund was otherwise substantial, the error was harmless and the conviction should stand. Maund now asks the Supreme Court to review that reversal.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked to resolve how judges should decide whether a new trial is required when jurors were exposed to information outside the trial record that hurt a defendant's case. One approach asks only whether that outside information affected the jury's discussion and decision. Another approach asks whether, additionally, the defendant would likely have been convicted anyway based on the properly admitted evidence. Maund's petition says courts of appeals are split on which approach applies.

## Where it stands

The petition was filed and the government waived its right to respond. The case was distributed for the Court's September 28, 2026 conference, where the justices will consider whether to take it up; the docket does not yet show any decision on the petition.

## What each outcome would mean

Maund, the petitioner, is asking the Court to hear his case and overturn the Sixth Circuit's ruling. If the Court grants review, it would hear full arguments and decide which legal standard applies, without yet deciding who wins. If it denies review, the Sixth Circuit's decision stands, meaning Maund's conviction remains reinstated and no new trial occurs on this basis.
