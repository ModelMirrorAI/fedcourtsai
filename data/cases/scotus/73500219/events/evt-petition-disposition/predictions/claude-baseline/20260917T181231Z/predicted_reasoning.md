# Forecast: Maund v. United States, No. 25-1318 (evt-petition-disposition)

**Disposition.** The Court will deny the petition, most likely in the order list
following the September 28, 2026 long conference (October 5, 2026) or, if it is
held over once, within the following two or three conferences. P(any grant) = 0.035.

**Relists.** The docket shows one distribution (June 17, 2026, for the
September 28 conference) and no relist. I expect the petition to be decided off
that single distribution. The one path that adds a distribution is a request for
a response: the United States waived on June 8, and the only realistic route to a
grant runs through the Court calling for a brief in opposition, which would pull
the petition off the September 28 list and redistribute it later. I put roughly a
one-in-eight chance on a response request and a smaller chance on an ordinary
hold-over relist or reschedule, for P(at least one more distribution) = 0.20. If
relisted at all, expect one or two further distributions, not a long string.

**CVSG.** None. The United States is the respondent, so the Solicitor General is
already a party; the Court's instrument here is a response request, not a call for
the views of the Solicitor General. P(CVSG) is effectively zero (0.003).

**Question the Court would take, if it takes one.** The question presented as
written, perhaps trimmed to its core: whether a Remmer violation entitles the
defendant to a new trial upon a showing that extraneous information detrimentally
influenced deliberations, or whether the government may defeat relief by showing
the verdict would have been the same. The Court would not add the burden-of-proof
question the petition dangles in a footnote; the Sixth Circuit assumed the most
defendant-favorable burden and that framing keeps the case clean.

**Summary disposition versus plenary review.** Conditional on a grant, plenary
review is the likelier route (about 3 to 1). The petition alleges a genuine and
long-percolating disagreement over the meaning of prejudice under Mattox, Remmer
and Olano, and the Court would want argument on it. The petition's alternative
request for summary reversal, on the theory that the Sixth Circuit acted as a
court of first view and never found harmlessness beyond a reasonable doubt, is a
plausible but minority path. A GVR is unlikely because no intervening decision of
this Court bears on the question. P(cert-order disposition | grant) = 0.25.

**Dissent or statement on denial.** Probably none. The record is sympathetic to a
criminal-procedure dissent (a court that inadvertently sent unadmitted exhibits to
the jury, a trial judge who found fundamental unfairness, an appellate court that
reweighed a cold record), and Justices Sotomayor, Gorsuch or Jackson are the ones
who might write. But the Sixth Circuit's opinion is unanimous, assumed the
strictest standard, and rests on a harmlessness finding against a murder-for-hire
defendant with a recorded self-incriminating call and a $150,000 same-day payment,
which dampens the appetite for a separate writing. P(some dissent or statement |
denial) = 0.05.

**If granted.** The likely ground would be that Remmer and Olano ask whether the
intrusion affected the jury's deliberations, so an outcome-would-not-differ
inquiry misstates the prejudice question; a reversal or vacatur and remand for the
Sixth Circuit to apply the correct standard would be the expected judgment. That
is conditional on a grant I think unlikely.
