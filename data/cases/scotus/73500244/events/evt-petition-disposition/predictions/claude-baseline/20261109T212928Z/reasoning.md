# Reasoning — Brown v. United States, No. 25-1342 (P(grant) = 0.33)

**Anchor.** `record/context.json` freezes band `high` (sal-v8), `distribution_count` 3, no CVSG, and `cert_anchor.rate` = 0.346, the pooled `reached` rate for `high` over the ten prior Terms. The statpack's relist-count cut agrees on shape: in the bucket with 2 relists, about 28% of petitions were granted and about 13% GVR'd (n≈495). A GVR is implausible here because no intervening decision bears on the question, so the plenary-grant share (~28%) is the relevant part of that bucket.

**The docket.** The snapshot (2026-11-09.json) and the live docket page agree. The petition was distributed for the 10/16 conference. It was not decided there and was redistributed on 11/2 for the 11/6 conference, then on 11/9 for 11/13. So it has been relisted twice, and it is still pending as of today. There is no CVSG, and none is possible because the United States is the respondent. Two amicus briefs support the petition: Cato and Professor Randy Barnett.

**Documents read.** I read the petition, the QP, the government's BIO, the cert reply, the decision below and the lower-court docket record. The QP asks whether an automobile is per se an "instrumentality of interstate commerce" under 18 U.S.C. § 1201(a)(1). Arguments for a grant:
- The split is real-looking. The Tenth Circuit in *Chavarria* (2025) held that motor vehicles are not per se instrumentalities. The published Eleventh Circuit decision in *Bryan*, which the unpublished decision below follows, drew a dissent from Judge Calvert saying the circuit was on the wrong side of a developing split.
- The question has federalism stakes that interest Justices Thomas and Gorsuch.
- The petition comes from an experienced Supreme Court clinic (Banner, UCLA).

Arguments against a grant:
- The SG says there is no split. In its view, *Chavarria* concerned an indictment charging a generic "motor vehicle," while this one specifies a Toyota Sequoia. Older circuit cases (3d, 4th, 6th, 7th, 9th) treat cars as instrumentalities.
- The SG also argues the "dominant use" argument was not raised below.
- The decision below is unpublished, and the facts (murder of a three-year-old) make the case unattractive.

**The companion case moved me down.** McMichael v. United States, No. 25-7371, is the IFP petition from *Bryan* raising the same question. According to its live docket and the corpus row, it was denied on 10/19, out of the very 10/16 conference at which Brown was held back. If the Court meant to grant Brown, it would ordinarily hold McMichael for it rather than deny it outright. Denying the companion while relisting the better vehicle fits two explanations about equally well: a grant on the cleaner vehicle, or a Justice writing a dissent from denial in Brown. The 10/16 → 11/6 gap also looks more like time for a writing or a closer look than an immediate grant track. Netting these, I stay at the anchor or slightly below: **0.33**.

**Uncertainty.** I give near-even odds to a decision at the 11/13 conference against a further relist. If it is denied, a written dissent is likely, given two relists after the conference where the companion was denied.

No flags. Retrieval was the live dockets and one corpus lookup (see retrieval.md).
