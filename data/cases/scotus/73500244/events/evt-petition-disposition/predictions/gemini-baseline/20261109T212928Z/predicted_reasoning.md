## 2. Disposition and Separate Writings on Denial
- **Disposition (`disposition` = 0.12, `predicted_disposition` = `denied`)**: The Court will deny the petition for a writ of certiorari. Three weeks earlier, on October 19, 2026, the Court denied certiorari in *Travis McMichael v. United States* (No. 25-7371), which sought review of the Eleventh Circuit's published decision in *United States v. Bryan*, 159 F.4th 1274 (11th Cir. 2025)—the very precedent the panel below applied in two paragraphs on the Non-Argument Calendar. Additionally, the Tenth Circuit's decision in *United States v. Chavarria*, 140 F.4th 1257 (10th Cir. 2025), expressly confined its holding to an indictment charging generic "motor vehicles" and distinguished cases involving "automobiles" or "cars," leaving no square circuit conflict on whether an automobile is an instrumentality of interstate commerce under 18 U.S.C. § 1201(a)(1).
- **Separate Writing on Denial (`dissent-from-denial` = 0.28)**: Given the two relists, the amicus brief filed by Professor Randy E. Barnett, and the petition's direct invocation of Justice Thomas's concurrence in *United States v. Hemani*, 146 S. Ct. 1677, 1695 (2026), there is a meaningful likelihood (~28%) that Justice Thomas (potentially joined by or writing alongside Justice Gorsuch) issues a statement respecting the denial of certiorari or a dissent from the denial of certiorari highlighting the tension between categorical "per se instrumentality" rules and the original meaning of the Commerce Clause, while noting vehicle flaws or the nascent posture of the circuit split.

## 3. Conditional Forecast if Certiorari Is Granted
- **Question Presented Taken**: If the Court grants certiorari, it will take the single Question Presented in the petition as written—whether an automobile is *per se* an instrumentality of interstate commerce under 18 U.S.C. § 1201(a)(1).
- **Summary Disposition Route (`summary-disposition-route` = 0.05)**: Conditional on a grant, the Court will overwhelmingly likely set the case for plenary briefing and oral argument rather than a summary disposition (GVR or summary reversal), as there is no intervening Supreme Court decision on point to trigger a GVR and the Court would not summarily reverse an eleven-circuit consensus without argument.
- **Conditional Merits Ground**: If granted, the Court would likely address whether 18 U.S.C. § 1201(a)(1)'s phrase "uses ... any means, facility, or instrumentality of interstate or foreign commerce" requires proof that the vehicle was used with an interstate or commercial nexus, invoking constitutional avoidance and federalism principles (*United States v. Lopez*, 514 U.S. 549 (1995); *United States v. Bass*, 404 U.S. 336 (1971)) to avoid reading the Federal Kidnapping Act to federalize every local abduction involving a personal car.
