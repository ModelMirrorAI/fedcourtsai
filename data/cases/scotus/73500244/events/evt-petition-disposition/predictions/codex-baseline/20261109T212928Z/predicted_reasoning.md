# Predicted disposition

The Court will deny the petition. The probability of any grant is 41%; denial is the most likely disposition. The denial will not decide whether the Eleventh Circuit's interpretation is correct and will most likely carry no explanation.

# Procedural path

Three conference distributions are already recorded, most recently for November 13, 2026. I expect one further distribution beyond those three, followed by disposition, rather than a long hold. The probability of any further distribution is 58%. The November 13 conference itself is not the predicted increment.

The Court will not call for the Solicitor General's views. The United States has already supplied its position as respondent; the probability of a new CVSG is 0.5%.

# If review is granted

The Court would take the single question presented substantially as written: whether an automobile is per se an instrumentality of interstate commerce for purposes of the federal kidnapping statute. It would focus on wholly intrastate use of an identified automobile, not on whether every device that could be called a motor vehicle qualifies. Both statutory construction and the constitutional limits underlying that construction would remain relevant.

A grant would most likely lead to full briefing and argument. Conditional on any grant, summary disposition has an 8% probability. There is no identified intervening controlling decision supporting a routine GVR, and the disagreement is not an obvious candidate for summary reversal. If granted, the likely central dispute would concern the scope of the instrumentalities category and whether the statute can be read narrowly to avoid constitutional difficulty; this is conditional merits context, not a predicted merits holding.
