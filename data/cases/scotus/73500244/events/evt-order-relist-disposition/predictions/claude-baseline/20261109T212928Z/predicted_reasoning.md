# Forecast — Brown v. United States, No. 25-1342

**State forecast from.** The docket shows three distributions: one for the 10/16/2026 conference, a second (entered 11/2) for 11/6, and a third (entered 11/9) for 11/13. The petition was taken off the 10/16 conference and then relisted after 11/6. This is the first relist that opens this cell. There is no CVSG, and the United States is the respondent and opposes the petition.

**Disposition.** It is roughly a coin flip, leaning slightly toward denial (P(grant) ≈ 0.42). The current Court almost always relists a petition at least once before granting it. It also relists petitions it means to deny when a Justice is writing a dissent from the denial. Both paths fit this docket. A decision is most likely in the order lists after the 11/13 conference, around 11/16–11/17.

**Further relists.** One more relist is plausible (≈0.30). That would happen if a Justice is still drafting a dissent from denial, or if the Court wants another look at the vehicle. More than two further relists are unlikely.

**CVSG.** None. The United States is already a party and has filed its brief in opposition.

**Question if granted.** The Court would take the question presented as written: whether an automobile is per se an "instrumentality of interstate commerce" under 18 U.S.C. § 1201(a)(1). It might rephrase it to cover both the statutory question and the Commerce Clause question, the two questions the government's brief sets out. Plenary review (full briefing and argument) is the expected route. A GVR (grant, vacate and remand) or summary reversal is unlikely (≈0.05 conditional on a grant), because no intervening decision bears on the question and the court below applied settled circuit precedent (Bryan).

**If denied.** I expect a dissent from denial about half the time. Justice Thomas is the most likely author, given his long-running Commerce Clause writing, and Justice Gorsuch is next. A dissent or statement is the most common reason the Court relists a petition it then denies.
