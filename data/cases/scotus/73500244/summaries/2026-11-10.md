## In brief

Derick Brown asks the Court to decide whether using an ordinary car makes a kidnapping a federal crime, even when the car never crosses state lines.

## What happened

Derick Brown was indicted on federal kidnapping charges after a three-year-old girl in her care died. Because the alleged crime happened entirely within Alabama, prosecutors relied on a law that applies when a defendant uses something that counts as an instrumentality of interstate commerce, pointing only to her use of a Toyota Sequoia. The district court rejected her argument that this made the law unconstitutional as applied, and a jury convicted her. The Eleventh Circuit affirmed, holding that cars count as such instrumentalities automatically, even when driven only within one state. The full appeals court declined to rehear the case.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked whether an ordinary car, driven entirely within one state, automatically counts as a connection to interstate commerce that lets Congress treat the conduct as a federal crime, or whether the government must show some actual link to interstate activity. Brown argues other appeals courts disagree on this question; the government argues there is no real conflict and that the lower court's reading is correct.

## Where it stands

The petition has been distributed for three conferences, most recently November 13, 2026, after full briefing including an opposition brief, a reply, and amicus briefs. The docket does not yet show a decision on whether the Court will hear the case.

## What each outcome would mean

Brown, who lost in the lower courts, is asking the Court to take up her case. If the Court grants review, it would hear full arguments on whether cars automatically satisfy this jurisdictional requirement, without yet deciding who is right. If it denies review, the Eleventh Circuit's ruling stands, leaving Brown's convictions and life sentence in place and leaving automobiles treated as automatic instrumentalities of interstate commerce in that circuit.
