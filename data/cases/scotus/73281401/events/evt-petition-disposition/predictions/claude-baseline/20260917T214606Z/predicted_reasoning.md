# Forecast: what the Court will do with No. 25-1108, Zook v. Fuqua

**Disposition.** The petition will most likely be **denied** at or shortly after the September 28, 2026 long conference, with the denial appearing on the October 5 order list. I put the chance of any grant (plenary grant, GVR, or summary reversal) at **10%**.

**Relists.** The docket shows **two distributions**: May 5 (for the May 21 conference, superseded when the Court called for a response on May 15) and July 29 (for the September 28 conference). No conference has yet actually considered the petition, so this is functionally a first-conference petition, not a relisted one. I expect it to be decided off the long conference without a further distribution; probability of **at least one more distribution: 22%**. If it is relisted, the likelier reason is a Justice preparing a statement or dissent, or the Court checking the vehicle before a summary disposition, rather than a hold for a pending merits case (none is on point).

**CVSG.** No call for the views of the Solicitor General. The case is a section 1983 suit against county deputies; no federal statute, program, or party is implicated, and the Court does not ordinarily invite the SG on qualified-immunity petitions. **2%**.

**Which question, if taken.** If the Court grants, it would take **Question 1 only** (whether a court may consider video evidence that blatantly contradicts the complaint at the Rule 12(b)(6) stage), probably reformulated to strip the "may, or must" framing and the fact-bound qualifiers. Questions 2 and 3 are error-correction questions that depend on Question 1 and would be left behind. The most plausible grant is a plenary grant to resolve the Sixth/Eleventh versus Tenth Circuit methodological disagreement, framed as an extension of *Scott v. Harris* to the pleading stage.

**Summary disposition.** Conditional on a grant, a summary route (per curiam reversal or GVR) is the less likely path: **30%**. The Court's qualified-immunity per curiams have come at summary judgment on a video record the lower court was entitled to consider; here the threshold question is whether the video may be consulted at all, and the Tenth Circuit held in the alternative that the videos would not blatantly contradict the complaint even under the Sixth Circuit's rule. A summary reversal would have to overturn both holdings on a pleading-stage record. No intervening decision of this Court supports a GVR.

**Dissent from denial.** Conditional on denial, I expect **no separate writing**; probability of a dissent or statement respecting denial **7%**. The published dissent below and the police-shooting facts give a Justice sympathetic to officers material to write from, but the alternative holding and the weak vehicle make a silent denial the norm.

**If granted (conditional).** The Court would likely hold that a court may consider indisputably authentic video that is central to the complaint and blatantly contradicts its allegations, aligning Rule 12(b)(6) practice with *Scott v. Harris*, and would vacate and remand for the Tenth Circuit to apply that standard rather than resolving qualified immunity itself.
