## What happened

Jason Roybal led three Santa Fe County sheriff's deputies on a nighttime car chase, then fled on foot after stopping. The officers shot and killed him. Scott Fuqua, representing Roybal's estate, sued the officers for excessive force. The officers asked the trial court to dismiss the case, arguing that video recordings of the incident showed a different story than the complaint described. The trial court declined to view the videos and let the case proceed, finding the complaint plausible on its own terms. A divided Tenth Circuit appeals court panel agreed, holding that its circuit's rules do not allow video evidence to be considered at this early stage, even evidence both sides had submitted. A dissenting judge disagreed. The officers, now petitioners, ask the Supreme Court to review that ruling.

## What the Court is being asked

The officers ask the Court to decide whether courts may look at video recordings when deciding, at the earliest stage of a lawsuit, whether a complaint should go forward, when that video contradicts what the complaint alleges. They say other appeals courts allow this and the Tenth Circuit does not. They also ask whether a plaintiff can make a complaint sound plausible by leaving out known facts that would otherwise undercut it, and whether a single past Supreme Court ruling about shooting a fleeing suspect was enough to show the officers clearly violated the law, given the specific circumstances here. Fuqua responds that the complaint was properly pled, that the videos could not be considered at this stage under settled procedural rules, and that considering them would not change the outcome anyway.

## Where it stands

The petition was distributed for conference twice, most recently for September 28, 2026, after the Court requested a response from Fuqua and later received a supplemental brief from the officers. The docket shows no action yet on the petition since that conference.
