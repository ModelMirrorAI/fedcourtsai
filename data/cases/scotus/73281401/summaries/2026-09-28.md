## In brief

Sheriff's deputies ask the Court to review a lawsuit over a fatal shooting, arguing lower courts wrongly refused to consider video evidence and let an implausible complaint proceed.

## What happened

Santa Fe County deputies shot and killed Jason Roybal after a car chase. Roybal's estate, represented by Scott Fuqua, sued the deputies under a federal civil-rights law, alleging they used excessive force by shooting Roybal while he was unarmed and fleeing on foot. The deputies asked the district court to dismiss the case, urging it to consider video recordings of the encounter, which they say show Roybal had fired a BB gun at officers moments earlier and was running toward an occupied vehicle. The district court declined to consider the video and denied dismissal, and a divided Tenth Circuit panel affirmed, with one judge dissenting. The Tenth Circuit denied rehearing.

## What the Court is being asked

The deputies raise three questions: whether courts may look at video recordings when deciding a motion to dismiss a complaint, if the footage clearly contradicts what the complaint alleges; whether a plaintiff can make a complaint sound plausible by leaving out known facts that would otherwise undercut it; and whether one past ruling on shooting unarmed, non-threatening suspects, by itself, is enough to strip officers of legal protection from suit without a closer factual match to precedent.

## Where it stands

The petition has been distributed for conference twice, most recently for September 28, 2026, after the Court requested and received a response from Fuqua and a supplemental brief from the deputies. The docket does not yet show any decision on the petition.

## What each outcome would mean

The deputies, as petitioners, are asking the Court to take up the case. If the Court grants the petition, it would hear the case and decide the questions about video evidence, pleading standards, and legal protection for officers, without yet deciding who wins. If the Court denies the petition, the Tenth Circuit's decision letting Fuqua's lawsuit proceed against the deputies would stand, and the case would continue in the lower courts.
