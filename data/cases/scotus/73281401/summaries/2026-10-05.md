## In brief

The Supreme Court declined to hear sheriff's deputies' appeal seeking dismissal, on qualified immunity grounds, of a lawsuit over a fatal shooting.

## What happened

Deputies Christopher Zook, Jacob Martinez, and Leonardo Guzman shot and killed Jason Roybal after a vehicle chase in Santa Fe, New Mexico. Scott Fuqua, representing Roybal's estate, sued the deputies, alleging excessive force. The deputies asked the district court to dismiss the case early, citing qualified immunity, a legal protection shielding officers from suits unless they violated clearly established rights, and urged the court to review video footage of the shooting. The district court declined to consider the video and let the case proceed; the Tenth Circuit Court of Appeals affirmed, over one judge's dissent, and denied further review.

## What the Court is being asked

The deputies asked the Court to decide whether judges may look at video evidence when deciding, at an early stage, whether a lawsuit should go forward, if the video appears to directly contradict what the complaint says happened. They also asked whether a plaintiff's complaint can be considered adequate when it leaves out known facts that might otherwise defeat the claim, and whether a single past case about shooting an unarmed fleeing suspect was enough, by itself, to show the officers' conduct clearly violated the law.

## Where it stands

The case was distributed for conference twice, and the Court requested a response from Fuqua after the first conference. The petitioners later filed a supplemental brief. On October 5, 2026, the Court denied the petition.

## What each outcome would mean

The deputies asked the Court to take up the case. With the petition denied, the Tenth Circuit's ruling stands: Fuqua's lawsuit against the deputies continues in the lower courts, and the question of whether video evidence may be considered at this early stage was not decided by the Supreme Court. The denial does not mean the Court agreed with the Tenth Circuit's reasoning, only that it left that decision in place.
