## What happened

Jason Roybal led three Santa Fe County deputies on a nighttime chase in a stolen car, then fled on foot after allegedly firing a BB gun at officers. He was shot and killed. Scott Fuqua, representing Roybal's estate, sued the officers for excessive force, alleging Roybal was unarmed and posed no threat when shot. The officers asked the district court to dismiss the case and to consider video footage of the encounter, arguing it contradicted the complaint. The district court declined to consider the video and let the case proceed; a divided Tenth Circuit panel affirmed, with one judge dissenting. The officers then asked the Supreme Court to review that ruling.

## What the Court is being asked

The officers want the Court to decide whether judges may look at video recordings when deciding, at an early stage, whether a lawsuit can go forward — even if the lawsuit itself never mentions the video — when the footage seems to sharply contradict what the complaint alleges happened. They say other appeals courts allow this while the Tenth Circuit does not. They also ask whether a plaintiff can meet the legal standard for a plausible claim by leaving out facts they knew about, and whether a single past Supreme Court ruling about shooting fleeing suspects was enough, by itself, to show the officers clearly broke the law. Fuqua responds that the complaint was properly pled, that the video could not be considered at this stage under settled rules, and that considering it would not change the outcome anyway.

## Where it stands

The petition has been distributed for conference more than once, most recently for September 28, 2026, and the Court has received the petition, an opposition brief, and a supplemental brief from the officers; no decision on whether to hear the case has yet been recorded.
