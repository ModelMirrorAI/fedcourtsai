# Rationale for the numbers

**Stage and moment.** `event.yaml` records `stage: interim`, `moment: arrival`, so this is the ordinary interim cell, opened at the application's submission entry. `record/context.json` carries `mode: forward`, `band: null`, `cut_kind: arrival-position` with `cut_anchor_index: 0`, `response_requested: false`, `referred_to_court: false`, `amicus_briefs: 0`. Since `band` is null and this is an interim cell, I did not derive a band or use the cert band table or the caption-class floors.

**What the provisioned record shows.** I read `record/snapshots/2026-07-23.json`, the file `context.json` names. It carries one proceedings entry: "Jul 22 2026 — Application (26A163) for a stay, submitted to Justice Alito." Docketed August 5, 2026. Applicant Bridget Gilmore, Texarkana, TX, listed as her own counsel of record (pro se). Respondent Walmart, Incorporated. Lower court: Fifth Circuit, No. 26-30022. No `record/documents/` directory was provisioned, so I had no application text; that absence is the ordinary state for an application docket, not a gap I am flagging. `snapshot_provenance` is `truncated`, consistent with the arrival-position cut at the opening entry.

**Anchor.** I anchored on the statpack's "The interim docket (applications)" section. Its caption already describes the row as grounding the scored base rate (not descriptive-only). The application Term is 2026 (docket 26A163), so the pool is application-Terms 2016 through 2025. Only two of those Terms carry parsed substantive rows:

| Term | resolved (subst.) | granted | unparsed |
| --- | --: | --: | --: |
| 2025 | 226 | 17 | 0 |
| 2024 | 70 | 14 | 972 |

Pooled: 31 / 296 = **10.5%**, which clears the 50-resolved floor, so a published baseline exists and that is the yardstick I am scored against. Caveats I carry from the section: the 2024 row rests on a Term the poller reached only in part (972 unparsed), and the pooled cohort is not conditioned on the escalation ladder while the predicted population is selected on it, so the number is shape before it is an anchor.

**Adjustment, and why it is large.** The pooled rate blends every substantive application, including the government and political-committee applications that account for nearly all grants. This applicant's class is the opposite end of that distribution. From `fedcourts query --court scotus --include-applications --limit 300` I filtered the 49 substantive rows returned (44 denied, 4 granted, 1 withdrawn). The four grants were all institutional applicants (a federal agency, a national party committee, a ballot-initiative committee, the President). Every individual-versus-private-company stay application in the set (Arsenis v. M&T Bank, Evans v. Freddie Mac, Golden v. Transunion, Robalino v. U.S. Bank, Nicholson v. BNY Mellon, Lamell v. U.S. Bank, Perez v. JPMorgan Chase, Arnold v. Huntington Ingalls, and others) was denied. That slice is small and recency-ranked, but it matches what I know of the Court's practice: a Circuit Justice denies pro se stay applications against private parties essentially without exception.

The Fifth Circuit docket (retrieved through the CourtListener MCP server; every entry predates my snapshot) sharpens the posture further. The appeal, from W.D. La. No. 5:20-cv-1589, was docketed January 20, 2026; Walmart moved to dismiss on February 10 and the court granted the motion on February 26, before any briefing, which is the profile of a jurisdictional dismissal (most likely an untimely notice of appeal from a 2020 case). Rehearing en banc was denied without poll on March 31. Two motions to stay the mandate pending certiorari were denied on May 7, and the applicant's subsequent filings alleged fraud on the court and clerk misconduct, with several returned as "no action taken." A stay from this Court requires a reasonable probability of certiorari and a fair prospect of reversal; a procedurally dismissed appeal with these filings behind it meets neither. I therefore moved from the 10.5% anchor to **0.01**. I did not go lower because the interim resolver classifies text mechanically and an unusual order form could be misread, and because I am reasoning from the docket, not from the application itself.

**The three increments.** Response requested at 0.03: a Circuit Justice calls for a response when the application has some chance of relief or the respondent's position is needed; nothing here suggests either. Referral at 0.12: Justice Alito refers a minority of even meritless applications to the full Court, and pro se applicants who renew or file supplements sometimes prompt a referral, so this is the least lopsided of the three. The statpack's referral count (172 of 352 substantive applications) is much higher, but it is dominated by the high-profile applications that fill the substantive slice and is right-censored, so I read it as shape rather than as this case's hazard. Amicus at 0.01: no amicus would file on this dispute.

**Stakes.** `big_case_score` 0.02: a pro se employment or civil-rights dispute with no question of general importance.

**Uncertainties and where to discount me.** I have not read the application, so I am inferring its ask (a stay of the mandate or judgment pending certiorari) from the Fifth Circuit docket. I did not fetch the last handful of Fifth Circuit entries after May 18, 2026, and I deliberately did not look up the Supreme Court docket 26A163 itself, to avoid retrieving this case's own disposition. The corpus prior slice I read was recency-ranked, not similarity-ranked, so the "individual versus corporation" pattern rests on about a dozen rows plus general knowledge of the Court's practice. I did not read the prior run's predictions on this event; this forecast is formed from this cell's record alone.
