# Forecast: Gilmore v. Walmart, Inc., No. 26A163 (application for a stay)

**Disposition.** The application will be **denied**, by Justice Alito acting alone as Circuit Justice, in a one-line order ("Application (26A163) denied by Justice Alito."). P(unqualified grant) = 0.01.

**Response requested.** No. The Circuit Justice will act on the papers without calling for a response from Walmart. P(a response is called for after prediction time) = 0.03.

**Referral to the full Court.** Unlikely. A pro se stay application against a private party, arising from an appeal the court of appeals dismissed on motion and whose mandate that court twice declined to stay, is the paradigm case a Circuit Justice disposes of alone. Referral is a live but minority path: Justice Alito occasionally refers even weak applications, and a denial "by the Court" after referral would still resolve this event as a denial. P(referred after prediction time) = 0.12.

**Amicus filings.** None. The record shows zero amicus entries and nothing about the case would draw one. P(the amicus count rises) = 0.01.

**Timing and shape.** The application was submitted July 22, 2026 and docketed August 5, 2026 (the two-week gap suggests a deficient first submission that had to be corrected). Expect disposition within roughly one to four weeks of docketing, i.e. in August or early September 2026, before the Court's October sitting. If the applicant then files a petition for certiorari, it will be a separate docket and is not part of this event. A renewed application to a second Justice, which pro se applicants sometimes attempt, would likewise be a separate entry and would be referred to the Court and denied.

**Merits, conditionally.** The application presumably asks the Court to stay the Fifth Circuit's mandate (or the underlying Western District of Louisiana judgment) pending a petition for certiorari. A stay requires a reasonable probability that four Justices would grant certiorari, a fair prospect of reversal, and irreparable harm. The Fifth Circuit disposed of the appeal by granting the appellee's motion to dismiss five weeks after docketing, before any briefing, which points to a jurisdictional defect such as an untimely notice of appeal; a dismissal of that kind on a civil-rights employment suit presents no certworthy question. None of the three requirements is plausibly met, and the Court will not write.
