## In brief

Bethany Hall asks the Court to overrule a 2004 precedent that lets states exclude ministry-degree students from otherwise available tuition grants.

## What happened

Bethany Hall, a Liberty University student, received a Virginia tuition grant but lost it after changing her major to ministry-related fields, which Virginia's grant program excludes from funding as "religious training or theological education." Hall sued the SCHEV director, arguing the exclusion violates her right to free exercise of religion. The district court dismissed her claim, and the Fourth Circuit affirmed, holding that Hall's case is squarely controlled by the Supreme Court's 2004 decision in Locke v. Davey, which upheld a similar Washington state scholarship restriction. A concurring Fourth Circuit judge agreed the lower courts were bound by Locke but wrote separately to argue the precedent was wrongly decided.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked whether it should overturn its own prior ruling that allows states to withhold general scholarship or grant funding from students specifically because they are pursuing degrees meant to train them for ministry, even while allowing that funding to be used at religious schools generally.

## Where it stands

The petition has been fully briefed, including a brief in opposition, a reply, and numerous amicus briefs. It was distributed for the Court's November 13, 2026 conference and then rescheduled; the docket does not yet show any decision on the petition.

## What each outcome would mean

Hall, the student denied grant funding, is asking the Court to take up her case and overturn the lower court's ruling against her. If the Court grants review, it would hear full arguments on whether to overrule the 2004 precedent, deciding nothing yet about who wins. If the Court denies review, the Fourth Circuit's ruling against Hall stands, leaving Virginia's exclusion of ministry-degree students from grant funding in place, along with similar laws in other states that rely on the same precedent.
