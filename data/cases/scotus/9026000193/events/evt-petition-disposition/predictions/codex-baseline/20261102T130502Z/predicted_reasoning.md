# Forecast of the Court's action

The Court will most likely **deny the petition**, leaving the Fourth Circuit's judgment standing without deciding anew whether Virginia's funding restriction violates the Constitution. The probability of any grant is **40%**. Denial would not itself endorse the lower court's reasoning or generate a majority opinion.

The starting record contains **one distribution**, for the November 13, 2026 conference, followed by an October 28 reschedule. The petition will very likely be distributed again: **96% probability of at least one additional distribution**. Expect a replacement conference setting first, then disposition after that conference or one further true relist. A replacement distribution is not evidence that the Court previously considered and relisted the petition. The likeliest disposition window is late November 2026 through January 2027, depending on the replacement setting, rather than a guaranteed order after the withdrawn November 13 setting.

The Court is unlikely to request the Solicitor General's views: **4% probability of a new CVSG**. If it does request them, the invitation is most likely to follow an initial replacement conference and to postpone disposition for months. The parties have already framed the constitutional and historical issue without an obvious need for federal administrative expertise.

If review is granted, expect the Court to take the petition's single question substantially as written: **whether to overrule Locke v. Davey**. The record's admitted factual fit with Locke makes a narrow distinction an unlikely reason to take this vehicle. Plenary briefing and argument are much likelier than a summary order: the summary-disposition probability is **2.5%, conditional on a grant**. No identified intervening holding supplies an ordinary GVR route.

If the Court reaches the merits after granting, the likely dispute will concern whether an exception for religious vocational training survives the constitutional prohibition on excluding religious exercise from generally available benefits. A reversal would likely distinguish direct state support for clergy from neutral aid allocated through students' choices. That merits reasoning is conditional; it is not an unconditional prediction of an opinion accompanying denial.

If review is denied, expect no separate writing as the modal outcome, but assign **27%, conditional on denial**, to at least one dissent or statement respecting denial. Thomas is the most plausible author, with Alito and Gorsuch the next possibilities. Such a writing would likely argue that later religious-funding cases have undermined Locke and that this case offers a direct vehicle for reconsideration. No particular Justice's vote will necessarily be disclosed.
