# Forecast — Hall v. Fleming, No. 26-193

**Where the docket stands.** Distributed once (Oct 28, 2026, for the Nov 13, 2026 conference) and marked "Rescheduled" the same day, before any conference has acted. No relist after a conference, no CVSG. The BIO (Oct 14) and reply (Oct 26) are in. The docket shows 14 amicus filings supporting the petitioner, including West Virginia and 21 other States.

**Disposition.** More likely denied than granted, but this is a live grant candidate: about 27% for a grant.

**Further distributions.** I expect it to be distributed at least once more (~55%). The pre-conference reschedule already points that way, and if the Court is weighing a grant it will relist before granting. A one-conference denial is still a real possibility, since the Court declined to reach Locke in Carson and may prefer to wait again.

**CVSG.** Not expected (~2%). The respondent is a State, the issue is a state funding program, and the Court does not call for the Solicitor General's views in Religion Clause cases like this one.

**Question taken if granted.** The single QP as written: whether Locke v. Davey should be overruled. The Court might add or reframe a question on whether Virginia's VTAG religious-major exclusion violates the Free Exercise Clause under Trinity Lutheran, Espinoza and Carson.

**Route if granted.** Plenary review: briefing and argument. A summary disposition is very unlikely (~3%). There is no intervening decision to GVR on, and overruling a precedent is not done per curiam.

**If denied.** Roughly a one-in-three chance of a written dissent or statement. Thomas is the most likely writer (he has criticized Locke in Espinoza), then Gorsuch (Trinity Lutheran concurrence) and Alito. A statement from Kavanaugh or Barrett saying the Court would revisit Locke in a better case is possible but less likely.
