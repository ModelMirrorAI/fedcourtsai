The Supreme Court is unlikely to hear this appeal, with a one-in-four (25%) chance of granting review and a three-in-four (75%) chance of denying it. Although the case raises a high-profile religious freedom question—whether States may exclude college students majoring in theology or ministry from general tuition grants, asking the Court to overturn its 2004 decision in *Locke v. Davey*—lower federal courts are not divided on the issue. Moreover, the student bringing this case has already graduated and did not appeal the dismissal of her claims for past money damages, while a companion case brought by current students is scheduled for argument in the lower appellate court in January 2027, giving the Justices strong reason to wait for a cleaner case.
