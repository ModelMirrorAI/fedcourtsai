## III. Substantive Weighing: Petition vs. Brief in Opposition

### A. Factors Favoring Certiorari
1. **Doctrinal Tension Around *Locke v. Davey***:
   Virginia's Tuition Assistance Grant Program ("VTAG"), Va. Code Ann. § 23.1-628 *et seq.*, provides ~$5,000 annual grants to Virginia residents attending private Virginia colleges but excludes students whose primary purpose is "to provide religious training or theological education" (implemented by SCHEV via NCES CIP Code 39). Petitioner argues that *Trinity Lutheran* (2017), *Espinoza* (2020), and *Carson v. Makin* (2022) repudiated the premise of *Locke v. Davey*—especially after *Carson* rejected any distinction between religious status and religious use—and that Founding-era disestablishment history (including Virginia's 1786 Bill for Establishing Religious Freedom) barred only compulsory tax assessments for clergy/churches, not neutral student tuition grants used at private universities. Furthermore, in *Catholic Charities Bureau, Inc. v. Wisconsin Labor & Industry Review Commission*, 605 U.S. 238 (2025), the Court reaffirmed strict limits on denominational and theological line-drawing. Multiple Justices (Thomas, Alito, Gorsuch) and lower-court judges (e.g., Judge Readler concurring in *St. Dominic Academy v. Makin*, 142 F.4th 45, 57 (1st Cir. 2025)) have openly questioned *Locke*'s continuing vitality.
2. **Elite Counsel & Coordinated Amicus Support**:
   Petitioner is represented by Alliance Defending Freedom (which litigated *Trinity Lutheran*). Six cert-stage amicus briefs support certiorari: West Virginia and 21 other States; the ACLJ and Joshua Davey; the Council for Christian Colleges & Universities and the Association of Presbyterian Colleges & Universities; the Notre Dame Education Law Project, Religious Liberty Clinic, and Union of Orthodox Jewish Congregations of America; Samaritan's Purse and the Billy Graham Evangelistic Association; and the Association for Biblical Higher Education et al.
3. **Pre-Conference Reschedule**:
   The October 14, 2026 reschedule and joint redistribution with No. 26-240 for November 6, 2026 show that at least one chambers flagged the pair for coordinated consideration.

### B. Factors Weighing Against Certiorari (Decisive Downward Adjustments)
1. **No Circuit Split**:
   As the BIO demonstrates (BIO at 11–18), there is zero conflict among the circuits or state high courts. The Fourth Circuit below (175 F.4th at 516–19), the First Circuit in *St. Dominic Academy* (2025), and the Tenth Circuit in *Colorado Christian University v. Weaver*, 534 F.3d 1245 (10th Cir. 2008), all agree that *Locke* governs post-secondary scholarship exclusions of devotional theology/ministry programs and that under *Agostini v. Felton*, 521 U.S. 203, 237 (1997), lower courts must follow *Locke*. Moreover, in *Carson v. Makin* (2022)—decided just four years ago—Chief Justice Roberts's majority opinion (joined by Justices Kavanaugh and Barrett) explicitly preserved and distinguished *Locke* rather than overruling it.
2. **Severe Vehicle, Mootness, and Eleventh Amendment Obstacles in *Hall***:
   The BIO (at 18–22) identifies critical vehicle defects in *Hall*:
   - **Graduation & Mootness**: Hall graduated from Regent University in May 2025—more than a year before her cert petition was filed—and can never apply for VTAG again (VTAG is limited to 8 undergraduate semesters).
   - **Unappealed Eleventh Amendment Bar on Retrospective Relief**: The district court held that Eleventh Amendment sovereign immunity barred Hall's claims for retrospective compensatory and nominal damages against Respondent Fleming in his official capacity. Crucially, Hall **did not appeal** that sovereign-immunity dismissal to the Fourth Circuit (175 F.4th at 514 n.3).
   - **Remedial Catch-22**: Although the Fourth Circuit panel accepted Hall's representation at oral argument that VTAG could still disburse funds to her open Regent account if litigation resolved in her favor (175 F.4th at 514–15), Respondent points out (BIO at 20–21) that ordering a state official to pay state treasury funds for past academic years (2023–2025) after a student has graduated is classic retrospective monetary relief barred by *Edelman v. Jordan*, 415 U.S. 651 (1974), and the unappealed district court judgment. Because Article III mootness and Eleventh Amendment jurisdiction can be raised at any stage, granting *Hall* risks a dismissal as improvidently granted (DIG) or a jurisdictional remand without reaching *Locke*.
   - **Thin Rule 12(b)(6) Record**: Because the case was dismissed on the pleadings, factual questions about how Regent self-selected CIP Code 39.0201 rather than CIP Code 38 and how SCHEV administers post-graduation accounts were never developed.
3. **Posture of Companion Petition *Johnson v. Fleming* (No. 26-240)**:
   Through `uv run fedcourts query --court scotus --docket-number 26-240` and CourtListener MCP inspection of the Fourth Circuit docket in *Johnson v. Fleming* (`CA4 No. 26-1437`, docket ID `73188902`), we confirmed that *Johnson* is a petition for **certiorari before judgment** under Rule 11, filed by current undergraduate and National Guard students after the Eastern District of Virginia denied a preliminary injunction on March 31, 2026. More importantly, the Fourth Circuit docket in *Johnson* (Entry 73, filed Oct. 26, 2026) shows that the Fourth Circuit has **tentatively calendared *Johnson* for oral argument during the January 26–29, 2027 argument session**. The Supreme Court almost never grants certiorari before judgment to private plaintiffs when the court of appeals is actively proceeding to oral argument within weeks. Thus, neither *Hall* (tainted by post-graduation mootness and an unappealed Eleventh Amendment bar) nor *Johnson* (premature cert before judgment with Fourth Circuit oral argument imminent in January 2027) is an attractive vehicle to grant right now. The Court can deny both petitions now and wait a few months for the Fourth Circuit to enter final judgment in *Johnson*—where the plaintiffs are still enrolled in college and seek prospective injunctive relief.
