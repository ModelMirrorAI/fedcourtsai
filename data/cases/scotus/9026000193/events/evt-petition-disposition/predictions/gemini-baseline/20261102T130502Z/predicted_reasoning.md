## Disposition & Merits Route Forecast

- **Disposition (`disposition`: 0.25, `denied`)**: The Court will **deny** the petition for a writ of certiorari (75% probability of denial, 25% probability of grant). Although the petition squarely asks the Court to overrule *Locke v. Davey*, 540 U.S. 712 (2004), and has attracted six amicus briefs (including 22 States and Joshua Davey), there is no circuit split: every court of appeals to consider the issue agrees that *Locke* remains binding precedent unless overruled by this Court. Moreover, *Hall* suffers from severe vehicle defects: Petitioner graduated from Regent University in May 2025, did not appeal the district court's Eleventh Amendment dismissal of retrospective monetary and nominal damages against Respondent in his official capacity, and relies on an untested post-graduation disbursement theory to avoid mootness. Meanwhile, the companion case brought by current students (*Johnson v. Fleming*, No. 26-240) seeks extraordinary certiorari before judgment while its underlying appeal (CA4 No. 26-1437) is already tentatively calendared for oral argument in the Fourth Circuit in January 2027. Rather than take a jurisdictionally flawed vehicle in *Hall* or grant certiorari before judgment in *Johnson*, the Court will deny review and allow *Johnson* or another clean post-judgment vehicle to reach it in the ordinary course.
- **Question Presented if Granted**: If the Court were to grant certiorari, it would take the Petition's single Question Presented as written—whether *Locke v. Davey* should be overruled—or reformulate it slightly to include threshold consideration of whether the post-graduation claim for tuition grant disbursement remains a live Article III controversy capable of redress against a state officer under *Ex parte Young*.
- **Summary Disposition Route (`summary-disposition-route`: 0.05)**: Conditional on a grant, a summary disposition (GVR or summary reversal) is **very unlikely (5%)**. The Fourth Circuit faithfully applied *Locke v. Davey* and *Agostini v. Felton*, and the Supreme Court would not overrule a landmark precedent like *Locke* by summary order without plenary merits briefing and oral argument. The only conceivable GVR scenario (5%) would be a remand for further factual development on mootness or after an intervening ruling, which is unlikely given the posture.
- **Conditional Merits Ground**: If granted and set for plenary review, a majority of the Court would likely narrow or overrule *Locke v. Davey*, holding under *Trinity Lutheran*, *Espinoza*, and *Carson v. Makin* that excluding college students who major in devotional theology or biblical studies from a neutral, generally available tuition-grant program constitutes impermissible religious status and use discrimination under the Free Exercise Clause, and that historical Founding-era objections to compulsory tax assessments for clergy do not justify excluding voluntary student aid used at accredited private universities.
