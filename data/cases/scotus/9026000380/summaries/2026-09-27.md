## What happened

Badar Khan Suri, an Indian scholar at Georgetown University on a J-1 visa, was detained by immigration officers in March 2025 after the Secretary of State determined his presence posed foreign-policy concerns. Suri filed a habeas petition (a request that a court review the legality of his detention) in federal district court in Virginia, arguing his detention and planned removal were retaliation for his speech and associations. The district court ordered him released and blocked his removal while the case proceeded. The Fourth Circuit Court of Appeals affirmed, rejecting the government's argument that immigration law barred the district court from hearing the case, and acknowledged its ruling conflicts with decisions from other appeals courts. The government, represented by the Solicitor General, now asks the Supreme Court to take up the case.

## What the Court is being asked

Federal immigration law generally requires that legal challenges tied to a person's removal from the country go through a specific appeals-court process, not an ordinary lawsuit in district court. The government argues this rule also blocks a detained immigrant from using a habeas petition to challenge his detention when the reasons he gives for challenging detention are really the same reasons he'd use to challenge his removal. Suri's side maintains that a challenge to detention itself can still go forward in district court even if it overlaps with issues that could also affect removal. The Court is being asked whether the law's channeling rule extends that far.
