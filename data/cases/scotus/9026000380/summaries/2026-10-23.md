## In brief

The federal government asks the Court to decide whether a detained noncitizen can challenge his detention in a habeas lawsuit that also questions the legality of his removal.

## What happened

Badar Khan Suri, a researcher at Georgetown University, was detained by immigration authorities after the Secretary of State determined his presence posed foreign policy concerns tied to alleged Hamas associations. Suri filed a habeas petition claiming his detention was retaliation for protected speech, and a district court ordered his release and blocked his removal. The Fourth Circuit affirmed, rejecting the government's argument that immigration law stripped the district court of jurisdiction. The government says this conflicts with decisions from the Third Circuit and a Second Circuit panel, which held similar claims must go through a different review process in the courts of appeals.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked whether immigration law requires that challenges to a detained noncitizen's removal go exclusively through a petition for review in a court of appeals, even when the noncitizen frames the challenge as being about his detention rather than his removal, where the claims underlying both are the same.

## Where it stands

The petition was filed and the response deadline was extended to November 20, 2026. Amicus briefs supporting the petition have been filed, and the docket shows no action yet on whether the Court will hear the case.

## What each outcome would mean

The government, which lost below, is asking the Court to take up the case and reverse the Fourth Circuit's ruling. If the Court grants review, it will hear full arguments and decide the jurisdictional question; a grant alone would not decide who wins. If the Court denies review, the Fourth Circuit's decision stands, leaving in place the order requiring Suri's release and barring his removal while the case proceeds, and preserving his ability to pursue his detention challenge in district court.
