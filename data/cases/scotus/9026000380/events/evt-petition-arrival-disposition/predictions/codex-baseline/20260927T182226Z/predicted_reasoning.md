# Forecast of the Court's action

The Court will grant certiorari, most likely for plenary review of the immigration-jurisdiction question rather than for immediate summary reversal. The forecast is **82% for any grant**, including a GVR or other grant-family disposition. Ordinary plenary review is the modal specific disposition.

The provisioned docket has **zero distributions and no CVSG**. I expect briefing, an initial conference distribution, and approximately one further distribution before the Court acts; coordination with the anticipated Khalil petition could lengthen that sequence. The probability of at least one distribution from the present zero-distribution state is **98%**. This is the declared `relist-increment` claim at arrival, not a 98% prediction of an actual second conference after the initial distribution.

I expect **no CVSG**. The Solicitor General is already presenting the federal government's position as petitioner's counsel; an invitation to state that position separately is unlikely (**1%**).

If granted, the Court will address the question substantially as presented: whether 8 U.S.C. § 1252 bars detention habeas claims whose legal grounds also contest the validity of removal. The likely focus is the boundary between an independent challenge to detention and a collateral challenge to removal, not a general merits ruling on the respondent's speech or associations. The conflict described in the petition and the competing statutory readings in the appended opinion make plenary clarification more likely than treatment as straightforward error correction.

Conditional on a grant, I assign **30%** to disposition in the cert order itself and **70%** to review opening a merits proceeding. The principal summary-route scenario is a hold followed by a GVR after the Court resolves the jurisdictional question in a companion vehicle. A mootness-related vacatur is another possibility. Summary reversal without further clarification of the competing jurisdictional theories is less likely than those routes. The petition's request for review in both this case and Khalil makes companion management a concrete possibility, but it does not establish that a companion petition has already been filed or granted.

If the Court instead denies review, I expect no separate writing, while assigning **30% conditional probability** to at least one noted dissent or statement respecting denial. No individual cert vote or author is forecast.
