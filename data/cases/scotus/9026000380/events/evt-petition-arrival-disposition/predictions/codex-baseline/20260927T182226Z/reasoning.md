## Why 82%, rather than 71% or near certainty

**Reasons to raise the federal-class prior.** The petition presents a specific, recurring jurisdictional conflict, rather than only disagreement with an individual release order. Petition pages 20–22 identify the Third Circuit's Khalil decision as contrary authority and describe a related Second Circuit panel decision in Mahdawi. Petition pages 12–13 report the Fourth Circuit's express disagreement and a dissent. The government's request concerns the availability of district-court habeas review during removal proceedings and therefore reaches beyond this respondent. Petition pages 22–24 frame a nationwide administration problem and expressly seek review in both Suri and the anticipated Khalil petition. These features support intervention above the already substantial federal-petitioner baseline; federal representation itself is already in that baseline and is not counted twice.

**Reasons not to go higher.** The petition reports that the Second Circuit granted rehearing en banc on September 3, 2026 (page 12 n.2). I therefore do not count its panel result as an additional settled circuit position. The petition's own discussion of ongoing removal proceedings and possible mootness (pages 23–24) raises a genuine vehicle concern. A grant in another case could result in a hold and GVR here, but it could also leave this case denied or otherwise terminated; selection of this particular docket is less certain than eventual resolution of the legal issue.

The appended majority supplies a substantive qualification to the government's account. At appendix pages 50a–53a, it distinguishes detention-specific due-process claims from removal challenges and explains why release need not invalidate an eventual removal order. That makes the government's characterization of every detention claim as derivative contestable and creates scope for an independent-ground or vehicle objection. I do not infer that the government's allegations about the respondent's associations are adjudicated facts. Those allegations are unnecessary to the cert assessment.

For general legal context, I checked **Jennings v. Rodriguez, 583 U.S. 281, 294–295 (2018)** through CourtListener MCP, opinion 4248498. The retrieved passage declines a comprehensive interpretation of § 1252(b)(9) and finds no jurisdictional bar on the particular claims before it. That supports treating the scope question as one requiring clarification rather than assuming Jennings mechanically dictates summary reversal. I did not retrieve this petition's disposition, later history, or any other predictor's forecast. Attempts to obtain general Supreme Court rules and Jennings materials through the web tool returned no usable content; they supplied no evidence.

Putting the asserted inter-circuit conflict and recurring importance above the 70.79% prior, while retaining substantial uncertainty about the opposing arguments, independent detention grounds, companion choice, and mootness, gives **P(any grant) = 0.82**. This is a judgmental adjustment, not a fitted estimate.
