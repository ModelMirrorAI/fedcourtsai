# Forecast: what the Court will do with No. 26-380, Trump v. Suri

**Disposition.** The Court grants certiorari and sets the case for briefing and
argument (P(grant family) 0.80). The petition is the Solicitor General's, it
rests on an express and acknowledged circuit conflict (the Fourth Circuit
below against the Third Circuit in *Khalil* and a Second Circuit merits panel
in *Mahdawi*), the Second Circuit has already held its en banc rehearing in
abeyance pending this Court's possible grant, and the question — whether 8
U.S.C. 1252(b)(9) and (g) bar a pre-final-order habeas challenge to detention
whose grounds also attack removability — is one the Court left open in
*Jennings v. Rodriguez*. Petitions of this shape are granted far more often
than not.

**Distribution and relists.** The record shows zero distributions (the
petition was docketed September 21, 2026; the response is due October 21).
Expect a distribution for a conference in December 2026 or January 2027,
depending on whether respondent takes an extension, and then **one relist**
before the order: the Court routinely relists once before granting a case it
means to hear, and here it has a specific reason to hold the order for a
conference or two — aligning this petition with the *Khalil* petition the
Solicitor General says is due by October 19, 2026 — and to confirm the
respondent's ongoing removal proceedings have not mooted the case. I do not
expect a long relist string; this is not a petition the Court will hold in
suspense for months.

**CVSG.** None. The United States is the petitioner, so there is no Solicitor
General to invite.

**Which question.** The Court takes the QP essentially as the Solicitor
General wrote it — whether, notwithstanding Section 1252, a noncitizen
detained pending removal proceedings may challenge that detention in habeas on
claims that also go to the validity of removal — possibly rephrased to name
Sections 1252(b)(9) and (g) expressly. It does not reach the Fourth Circuit's
separate holding on where habeas jurisdiction lies after an interstate
transfer; the petition does not present it.

**Summary disposition.** Unlikely (P ≈ 0.12 conditional on a grant). There is
no intervening decision to GVR in light of; the only summary route is if the
Court grants *Khalil* alone as the vehicle, holds this petition, and later
GVRs it after deciding *Khalil*. The Solicitor General asked for a grant in
both cases precisely to insure against mootness, and the Fourth Circuit
opinion is the one that created the split, so plenary review here — likely
consolidated with *Khalil* if that petition is filed — is the far likelier
route. If granted by January 2027, argument in April 2027 and decision by the
end of June 2027 is realistic; a later grant pushes argument to October Term
2027.

**If denied.** A denial would most plausibly follow a mootness development
(the respondent departing, a final order of removal shifting his custody out
of Section 1226(a), or termination of his removal proceedings) or the Court
choosing *Khalil* as the sole vehicle. Conditional on a denial, I expect a
dissent or statement respecting denial with probability about 0.25 — likeliest
from Justice Alito or Justice Thomas, given the Wilkinson dissent below and
the importance the government attaches to the channeling scheme — but a
mootness-driven denial would most likely be silent.

**Conditional merits.** If granted, the likely outcome is reversal of the
Fourth Circuit, with the Court holding that at least Section 1252(b)(9)
channels detention claims that are derivative of a challenge to the decision
to seek removal, consistent with the *Jennings* plurality's description of the
provision's core.
