# Forecast: Americans for Citizen Voting - Michigan v. Michigan Board of State Canvassers (26A275)

Emergency application for an injunction pending appeal, submitted to Justice
Kavanaugh on August 31, 2026. The applicants ask the Court to order their
proof-of-citizenship / voter-ID constitutional amendment onto Michigan's
November 3, 2026 ballot after the Board of State Canvassers deadlocked 2-2 on
certification and the Michigan Supreme Court declined relief. They have asked
for action by September 3; Michigan's ballot must be settled by September 4.

**Disposition: denial, and quickly.** The Court will not enter the injunction.
An injunction pending appeal is the most demanding form of interim relief —
the applicant's entitlement must be indisputably clear — and this application
stacks three features the Court consistently refuses: it asks the Court to
override a state supreme court on what is at bottom a state-law
signature-verification dispute (the rejected affidavits and the 1,000-signature
sample), it asks for mandatory relief that would change the composition of a
ballot days before the state's printing deadline, and the federal hook (a
First Amendment / due process gloss on the initiative process, to which there
is no freestanding federal right) is thin. Expect a denial — most likely a
brief order, possibly by the full Court after referral, without noted
dissents; a single-Justice or unexplained-order denial by September 3–4 is the
modal path. P(unqualified grant) = 0.03.

**Escalation ladder, from the arrival state (no response requested, not
referred, zero amicus briefs):**

- **Response called for: ~35%.** The compressed clock cuts both ways. The
  application is credible enough (700k+ signatures, experienced Supreme Court
  counsel) that Justice Kavanaugh may call for a response on a 24–48 hour
  schedule, as is common in election emergencies; but an application this
  weak on the merits standard can equally be denied without one, and the
  September 3 ask leaves barely two working days.
- **Referral to the full Court: ~55%.** Modern practice on substantive,
  publicly salient applications is referral rather than in-chambers action,
  and an election measure affecting a statewide general election is the kind
  the full Court disposes of collectively. Still, a routine in-chambers denial
  remains a live path for an application resting on state-law grounds.
- **Further amicus filings: ~12%.** The disposition window is roughly three
  days. Election-integrity and voting-rights groups are mobilized on this
  measure, but few amicus efforts materialize inside 72 hours, and the docket
  shows none yet.

**Timing (unscored, but it disciplines the rest):** disposition on or before
September 4, 2026 — the relief is worthless after the ballot is settled, so
the Court will act, in either direction, within the week. If the Court instead
lets the date pass without acting, that is functionally a denial and I expect
a formal denial (or dismissal as moot) shortly after.

**Conditional merits shape:** if, against expectation, relief is granted, it
would most plausibly be a narrow injunction ordering the measure certified for
the ballot pending disposition of a forthcoming cert petition, likely with
noted dissents from three or more Justices. I do not expect a written opinion
in either direction beyond, at most, a short statement respecting denial.
