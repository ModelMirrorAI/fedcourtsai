# Forecast

The Court will deny the application, likely on or before the applicants' requested September 3 deadline or shortly after ballot finalization becomes unavoidable.

At prediction time the docket shows no requested response, no referral to the full Court, and no amicus briefs. The Court or Circuit Justice will probably call for an expedited response, and the application will probably be referred to the full Court. The very short schedule makes an additional amicus filing less likely than not.

The applicants have a substantial irreparable-harm argument because exclusion from the November ballot cannot be remedied after the election. But an unqualified injunction would require the Court to order placement of a statewide initiative on the ballot without a lower-court judgment or an adversarial factual record. The dispute turns on Michigan's signature-review process, the treatment and timing of nine affidavits, and a state-board deadlock. The Court is unlikely to resolve those contested matters through mandatory emergency relief while the state supreme court proceeding is still undeveloped and ballot preparation is imminent.

The most likely route is therefore a short denial order after expedited consideration, without merits reasoning. Referral is more likely than disposition by the Circuit Justice alone because the requested relief would affect a statewide election and a politically significant voter-identification amendment.
