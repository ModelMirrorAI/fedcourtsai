# Rationale

I assign a 0.13 probability to an unqualified grant and predict denial. The applicable prior-Term baseline is 31 grants among 296 resolved substantive applications in OT2024 and OT2025, or 10.47%. That pool is strictly before this OT2026 application and clears the 50-resolution floor. It is an imperfect anchor: parsing is uneven across those Terms, and the applications selected for prediction sit systematically higher on the escalation ladder than the pooled cohort.

The frozen arrival state shows no requested response, no referral to the full Court, and no amicus briefs. Those zeros are observable rather than missing. Across the prior-Term substantive cohort, the terminal/as-last-polled signals were 51 response requests, 161 referrals, and 48 applications with amici among 297 applications. Those figures describe the ladder's shape, not conditional rates from this application's arrival state.

The application raises the estimate modestly above the pooled baseline because the injury is concrete and exceptionally time-sensitive: it says the ballot must be settled around September 4 and that exclusion cannot be repaired after the election. The petition also alleges a three-signature shortfall after the Board rejected notarized affidavits, which gives the dispute a focused due-process and equal-protection theory rather than a generalized policy objection. The statewide voter-identification initiative and the large number of submitted signatures make the matter salient enough that accelerated consideration, a response request, and full-Court referral are more likely than in an ordinary application.

The same record makes ultimate relief unlikely. Applicants seek a mandatory order placing an initiative on the ballot before any lower court has issued a judgment. The Michigan Supreme Court had the matter for only several days, and the federal theories depend heavily on contested state-law procedures, signature comparisons, affidavit timing, and factual assertions presented from one side. The requested remedy would alter an election process on the eve of ballot finalization and give applicants essentially all the relief they seek before adversarial development. Those vehicle and institutional concerns outweigh the irreparable-harm showing.

I put the response-request increment at 0.66 and referral increment at 0.78 because a consequential election application can warrant full-Court attention even when relief is denied. I put the amicus increment at 0.32 because the issue is politically salient but the requested September 3 disposition leaves very little filing time.

No filed-document text was provisioned under `record/documents/`. I retrieved and read the 43-page emergency application through the Supreme Court PDF URL contained in the snapshot. That gave me only applicants' presentation; no response, lower-court opinion, or merits-tested factual record was available. CourtListener searches did not locate the exact state proceeding. This one-sided and compressed record is the main reason to discount the legal-specific adjustment.
