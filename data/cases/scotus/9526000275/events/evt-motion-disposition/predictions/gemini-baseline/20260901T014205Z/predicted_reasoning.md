The Court will deny the emergency application for an injunction pending appeal. The application seeks to place a state constitutional amendment regarding citizenship voting requirements on the Michigan ballot, following a deadlock by the Michigan Board of State Canvassers over a signature sampling dispute. Because this dispute centers overwhelmingly on the interpretation of state election law and signature verification procedures, the Court is highly likely to defer to state authorities. Furthermore, the applicants filed this federal emergency request based on a "constructive denial" while the matter was still technically pending before the Michigan Supreme Court, raising exhaustion and federalism concerns. Finally, granting an injunction to alter a state ballot just days before the printing deadline would violate the *Purcell* principle against late-stage federal judicial interference in state elections. Justice Kavanaugh may request a rapid response from the state given the election context, and he will likely refer the application to the full Court, which will summarily deny the request. Expect few, if any, amicus briefs due to the extremely compressed timeline.
