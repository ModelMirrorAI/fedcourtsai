# Forecast — 26A275, ACVM v. Michigan Board of State Canvassers (injunction pending appeal, response-requested moment)

**Disposition: denied, on or about September 3–4, 2026.** Justice Kavanaugh called
for a response within a day of filing, due by noon September 2 — the timetable of a
Justice who intends to act before the applicants' September 3 deadline and the
Board's September 4 ballot-finalization meeting, not one letting the application
sit. I expect the Court (or the Circuit Justice) to deny the application rather
than order Michigan officials to certify the initiative for the November 3, 2026
ballot. The likeliest shape is a denial without opinion, quite possibly framed as
without prejudice in light of the still-pending Michigan Supreme Court mandamus
case (No. 170595): the application asks the Court to leapfrog a state high court
that has had the case only since August 27, over a Labor Day weekend, on a
"constructive denial" theory of § 1257 jurisdiction with no final judgment below.
A mandatory injunction placing a measure *onto* a ballot in the first instance —
rather than staying a lower court's order — is relief the Court's emergency
docket essentially never gives, and the timing objection (ballots finalize within
days) cuts against, not for, federal intervention.

- **Referral to the full Court: more likely than not (~60%).** Contested
  applications in which a response has been requested are typically referred and
  disposed of by the Court rather than by the Circuit Justice alone (recent
  pattern: the solo denials are the ones where no response was ever called for).
  A high-profile election application fits that pattern, though the compressed
  clock leaves room for a single-Justice denial.
- **Further response request: already fired.** The record I was shown already
  carries the September 1 request from Justice Kavanaugh, so the
  response-requested increment is vacuous from this vantage; the low number I
  state is a formality, and the harness masks the claim.
- **Amicus filings: unlikely (~15%).** The window between the response deadline
  and disposition is a day or two. The initiative has organized opposition and
  national salience, so a fast-turnaround amicus is conceivable, but most
  applications on this fuse resolve with none.
- **Timing.** Disposition by September 3, or September 4 at the latest — after
  the Board meets and finalizes the ballot, the requested relief becomes much
  harder to give and the application is likely denied as effectively moot. A
  wildcard: if the Michigan Supreme Court rules first (it may, given
  Mich. Comp. Laws § 168.479(2)'s priority mandate), the application is likely
  denied as moot or withdrawn — I fold roughly 8% combined into
  withdrawn/dismissed routes, inside the 93% ungranted mass.
- **Separate writings.** If the full Court denies, a short statement or noted
  dissent from one or more of Justices Thomas, Alito, or Gorsuch is plausible
  given the election-integrity subject matter, but I expect a bare denial more
  often than not. (No claim resolves on this; context only.)

If, against my expectation, relief is granted, I would expect it to be narrow —
an injunction requiring the Board to certify pending the Michigan Supreme
Court's disposition, or an administrative freeze of the September 4
finalization — rather than an outright order placing the measure on the ballot.
