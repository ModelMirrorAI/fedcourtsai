## What happened

Americans for Citizen Voting – Michigan (ACVM) collected more than 700,000 signatures for a ballot initiative requiring proof of citizenship to vote, well over the roughly 446,000 needed. Michigan election officials sampled 1,000 signatures and found some invalid, leaving the petition several signatures short. ACVM gathered sworn affidavits from voters confirming their signatures, but the Michigan Board of State Canvassers rejected nine of them as untimely or mismatched, deadlocking 2-2 and keeping the measure off the ballot. ACVM and voter Fred Smith asked the Michigan Supreme Court to order the petition onto the ballot; that court did not act. With a state ballot-finalization deadline approaching, ACVM and Smith asked a single Justice of the U.S. Supreme Court to step in.

## What the Court is being asked

The applicants asked for emergency, temporary relief ordering election officials to put the petition on the November ballot while their appeal continues, without waiting for the Michigan Supreme Court to rule first. They argue the officials' delays and refusal to let them see comparison signatures, combined with rejecting sworn voter affidavits, violated constitutional guarantees of fair process and equal treatment of voters. The state officials, in their response, oppose that request.

## Where it stands

The application for emergency relief was submitted to Justice Kavanaugh, who requested a response from the state officials; after receiving that response and the applicants' reply, Justice Kavanaugh denied the application on September 3, 2026. An outside group, the Initiative and Referendum Institute, also filed a supporting brief.
