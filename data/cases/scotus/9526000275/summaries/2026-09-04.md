## In brief

A ballot petition group asked the Supreme Court to order Michigan officials to place its citizenship-voting initiative on the November ballot; a Justice denied the request.

## What happened

Americans for Citizen Voting – Michigan (ACVM) gathered over 700,000 signatures for a ballot initiative requiring proof of citizenship to vote. Michigan election officials found the petition three signatures short after rejecting several voter affidavits meant to fix disputed signatures, and the Board of State Canvassers deadlocked, keeping the measure off the ballot. ACVM and voter Fred Smith asked the Michigan Supreme Court for a court order (mandamus) forcing certification. That court did not act. With the state's ballot-finalization deadline approaching, ACVM and Smith asked the U.S. Supreme Court for emergency relief ordering Michigan officials to place the petition on the ballot while the appeal continues.

## What the Court is being asked

The applicants argue that election officials violated their rights by rejecting sworn voter affidavits without letting them see the comparison signatures used to challenge those affidavits, and by treating similarly situated voters and challenges inconsistently. They ask the Court to order the petition onto the ballot immediately, without waiting for the state courts to rule, given the pending deadline.

## Where it stands

Justice Kavanaugh, acting as Circuit Justice, requested a response from Michigan officials, received replies from both sides, and denied the application on September 3, 2026. An amicus brief was also filed the same day.

## What each outcome would mean

ACVM and Smith, as applicants, asked the Court to order the petition placed on the ballot while their appeal continued. Granting that request would have required Michigan officials to include the measure on the November 3 ballot on a temporary basis, without deciding the underlying legal claims. Denying it, which is what happened, leaves the Board of State Canvassers' decision keeping the petition off the ballot in effect for this election, while the underlying dispute over the rejected signatures and affidavits can still proceed in the Michigan courts.
