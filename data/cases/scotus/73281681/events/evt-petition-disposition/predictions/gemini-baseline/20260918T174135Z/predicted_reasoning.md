The petition in this case, brought by the Pacific Legal Foundation, presents a clear Takings Clause issue regarding the application of the *Nollan*/*Dolan* unconstitutional conditions doctrine to monetary exactions outside the context of real property dedications. This follows up on the Court's precedents in *Koontz* and *Sheetz*. The case has garnered significant attention, with five amicus briefs filed at the petition stage from prominent conservative and property-rights groups (Cato, Manhattan Institute, Advancing American Freedom). Furthermore, after the City of New York waived its right to respond, the Court affirmatively requested a response, signaling strong interest from at least one Justice.

The petition is currently distributed for the September 28, 2026, Long Conference. Given the complexity of the vehicle arguments raised in the brief in opposition (which argues the lower court's holding rests on alternative state-law grounds) and the typical practice at the Long Conference, it is highly likely that the petition will be relisted at least once if the Court intends to grant it, to allow for a closer vehicle check. 

A CVSG is unlikely, as the dispute involves a local zoning ordinance and does not directly implicate the federal government's interests. If granted, the Court is likely to take the single question presented for plenary review to clarify the scope of *Koontz*, making a summary disposition improbable. If the Court denies certiorari due to the alleged vehicle issues, a dissent from denial by Justice Thomas, Alito, or Gorsuch is highly probable given their strong interest in Takings Clause jurisprudence and the involvement of PLF.
