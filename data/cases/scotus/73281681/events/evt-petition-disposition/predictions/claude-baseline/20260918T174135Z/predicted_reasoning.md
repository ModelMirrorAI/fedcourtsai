# Forecast: what the Court will do with No. 25-1187

**State forecast from.** The docket shows **two distributions**: the first (May 26, for the June 11 conference) was superseded when the Court **called for a response** on June 2 after the City had waived; the second (Aug 19) is for the **September 28 Long Conference**, with the petition, five cert-stage amicus briefs, the brief in opposition, and the reply all in hand. No CVSG is on the docket. So the petition has not yet been considered at a conference with full briefing; the Long Conference is its first real look.

**Disposition.** More likely denied than granted, but this is a genuine candidate: P(grant) 0.24. The Court asked for the City's views on its own initiative, which it does for a small minority of waived paid petitions, and it did so in the same fortnight in which it denied the fact-bound Sheetz remand petition (No. 25-958, denied June 15, 2026) on the same Koontz question. That pairing reads as the Court treating this as the cleaner vehicle for the "does Koontz reach standalone monetary demands" question, not as disinterest in the question. Against that, the City's brief in opposition identifies a real vehicle problem — the Court of Appeals' threshold holding that JLWQA owners never held a property interest in unrestricted residential use, which the petition's question presented does not name — plus a thin two-state high-court split. I expect at least three Justices to want the question and the vehicle objection to be what decides whether a fourth joins.

**Relist.** P(at least one further distribution beyond the two shown) 0.35. If the Court grants, roughly even odds it does so straight out of the Long Conference (announced in the early-October orders) versus after one relist; if it denies, a relist for a separate writing or a closer look at the threshold-holding objection is plausible but the more common outcome is a clean denial in the October 5 order list. A grant after two or more relists is unlikely; a long relist string usually means summary work or a dissent, neither of which fits here.

**CVSG.** P 0.04. No federal party, no federal statute or program, and the Solicitor General has not been invited in the modern exactions cases at the cert stage (Koontz, Sheetz were taken without one). A CVSG would only come if the Court wanted the United States' view on the reach of Koontz to federal permitting, which nothing in this record prompts.

**Which question, if granted.** The Court would take the petition's single question essentially as written — whether Nollan/Dolan scrutiny extends to monetary permit demands not imposed in lieu of a real-property dedication — and would treat the "no property interest / new stick in the bundle" threshold holding as part of the predicate-taking inquiry that question already asks (the reply's Mendenhall/Rule 14 argument), rather than reformulating to add it. It would not take up whether the Arts Fund fee actually satisfies nexus and rough proportionality; that would go back to the New York courts.

**Summary disposition.** Unlikely: P(cert-order disposition | grant) 0.06. There is no intervening decision to GVR against (the closest candidate, Sheetz II, was denied), and a per curiam reversal is a poor fit for a divided state high court decision that rests on a contested reading of Koontz and an alternative state-law ground. If the Court acts, it sets the case for argument.

**If denied.** P(a noted dissent or statement respecting denial | denial) 0.22. Justices Thomas and Gorsuch have written separately on exactions and takings vehicles before, and Justice Gorsuch's Sheetz concurrence signals interest in the scope question; a short statement noting that the Koontz "in lieu" question remains open and awaits a better vehicle is the likeliest form. A silent denial remains the more probable outcome, as it was for Sheetz II.

**If granted, on the merits (conditional only).** The Court would most likely hold that a monetary demand tied to a specific parcel as a condition of a land-use permit is subject to Nollan/Dolan regardless of whether it substitutes for a dedication, reversing or vacating and remanding for application of nexus and proportionality; a majority built on the Koontz five's successors is the expected shape, with the threshold state-law property-interest point addressed briefly as not defeating the unconstitutional-conditions framing.
