## In brief

Owners of artist-restricted lofts in SoHo and NoHo ask the Court to revive a challenge to a City fee charged for converting units to unrestricted residential use.

## What happened

New York City long restricted certain SoHo and NoHo lofts to artists certified by the City. A 2021 rezoning let owners convert these units to unrestricted residential use, but only after paying a one-time fee to a City arts fund. A coalition of owners and residents sued, arguing the fee was an unconstitutional condition on a land-use permit. New York's trial court upheld the fee; an appeals court sided with the owners; the New York Court of Appeals then reversed, ruling for the City on two independent grounds: the owners had no property interest in unrestricted use to begin with, and the fee, being purely monetary and not offered in place of a land dedication, did not require the stricter scrutiny that applies to some permit conditions.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked whether the constitutional limits that apply when government demands land, or an interest tied to land, in exchange for a permit also apply to a standalone monetary fee that is not offered as a substitute for handing over real property.

## Where it stands

The petition has been distributed for conference more than once, most recently for the conference of September 28, 2026, and the docket shows no ruling yet on whether the Court will hear the case.

## What each outcome would mean

The Coalition for Fairness in SoHo and NoHo and other owners petitioned after losing before the New York Court of Appeals. If the Court grants review, it would hear full arguments and decide the legal question, without yet deciding who wins. If it denies review, the New York Court of Appeals ruling stands, leaving the fee in place and the owners' takings claim rejected. A denial would not mean the Court agrees the fee is constitutional, only that the lower ruling remains final.
