## What happened

Properties in New York's SoHo and NoHo neighborhoods have long been restricted to artist occupancy under a zoning designation called Joint Living-Work Quarters for Artists. In 2021 the City created an option letting owners convert these units to unrestricted residential use if they pay a one-time fee into an arts fund. A group of owners and residents, along with an advocacy organization, sued, arguing the fee was an unconstitutional condition on receiving a permit. A New York trial court rejected the challenge; an appeals court sided with the property owners; the New York Court of Appeals then reversed, ruling for the City on two independent grounds. The property owners and organization now ask the U.S. Supreme Court to review that ruling. The City opposes review.

## What the Court is being asked

The dispute is about when a government's request for money, as a condition for approving a land-use permit, must meet a constitutional fairness test that normally applies when a government demands land or an easement in exchange for a permit. New York's high court said that test does not apply here because the fee was not offered as an alternative to handing over real property, and separately because the property owners never had a legal right to unrestricted residential use in the first place. The property owners argue the fee should still be checked against that fairness test, and that other state courts disagree with New York's approach. The City argues there is no real disagreement among courts and that New York's decision was correct.

## Where it stands

The petition and responses have been filed, including the City's waiver and later a requested response, plus multiple amicus briefs supporting the property owners. The case was distributed for conference in June 2026 and, after a briefing extension and additional filings, was distributed again for a conference on September 28, 2026, where the Court will decide whether to grant certiorari, the review procedure that allows it to hear the case.
