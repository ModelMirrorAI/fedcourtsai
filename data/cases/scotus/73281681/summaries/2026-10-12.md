## In brief

Property owners in Manhattan's SoHo and NoHo ask the Court to revive their claim that a city conversion fee is an unconstitutional taking.

## What happened

Owners of units restricted to certified artists under a decades-old zoning rule challenged a 2021 city program letting them convert to unrestricted residential use for a fee of $100 per square foot, paid into an arts fund. They argued the fee must meet a constitutional test for permit conditions that demand property. New York's trial court rejected the claim; an intermediate appellate court sided with the owners; New York's highest court reversed, ruling for the city on two grounds: the owners had no property interest the fee could burden, and the fee was not demanded "in lieu of" a real-property transfer, so no heightened scrutiny applied.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked whether constitutional protections for permit applicants, which limit government demands for property in exchange for approvals, cover a straightforward monetary fee, or whether those protections apply only when money is demanded as a substitute for requiring a landowner to hand over an interest in real property.

## Where it stands

The petition has been distributed for conference more than once, most recently for the October 9, 2026 conference, after full briefing including the city's opposition and the owners' reply. The docket shows no decision yet on whether the Court will hear the case.

## What each outcome would mean

The Coalition for Fairness in SoHo and NoHo and the other owners asked the Court to take the case. If the Court grants review, it would hear full arguments and decide whether this constitutional test applies to standalone monetary fees, resolving nothing about who ultimately wins until it rules. If the Court denies review, the New York Court of Appeals ruling favoring the city stands, leaving the fee in effect for owners seeking to convert their units; a denial would not mean the Court agrees the fee is constitutional, only that the lower court's decision remains in place.
