## In brief

The Supreme Court declined to review whether a New York City conversion fee for artist housing must meet the constitutional test applied to other permit conditions.

## What happened

New York City restricts certain SoHo and NoHo loft buildings to occupancy by certified artists, but few qualifying artists remain. A 2021 zoning change let owners convert these units to unrestricted residential use by paying a fee of $100 per square foot into a city arts fund. A coalition of unit owners and residents sued, arguing the fee was an unconstitutional condition on their property. New York's trial court rejected the claim, an intermediate appeals court sided with the owners, and New York's highest court then reversed, ruling the fee was not subject to heightened scrutiny because owners had no existing right to unrestricted use and the fee was not demanded as a substitute for taking real property.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked whether constitutional protections for people seeking land-use permits cover a demand for money even when that money is not offered as a stand-in for taking an interest in real property, or whether those protections apply only when a fee substitutes for such a property demand.

## Where it stands

The petition was distributed for conference more than once, including after the Court asked for a response from the City, and was denied, leaving the New York Court of Appeals decision in place.

## What each outcome would mean

The Coalition for Fairness in SoHo and NoHo and other residents asked the Court to hear the case. Because the petition was denied, the New York Court of Appeals ruling favoring the City stands: the fee remains in effect, and owners seeking to convert their units must pay it without the heightened scrutiny the residents sought. The denial does not mean the Court agreed with the New York court's reasoning; it simply leaves that decision as the final word in this case.
