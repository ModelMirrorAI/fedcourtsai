## In brief

The Court declined to hear a property owners' group's challenge to a New York City fee charged to convert artist-only housing units to regular residential use.

## What happened

Owners of apartments in New York's SoHo and NoHo neighborhoods, long restricted to certified artists under a 1970s zoning rule, challenged a 2021 city law letting them convert their units to unrestricted residential use for a fee of about $100 per square foot, paid into an arts fund. A trial court rejected their claim that the fee was an unconstitutional taking. New York's intermediate appellate court reversed, siding with the owners. New York's highest court then reversed again, ruling for the city on two grounds: the owners had no property interest the fee could burden, and the fee wasn't the kind of monetary demand that triggers heightened scrutiny under takings law.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court was asked whether constitutional protections for people seeking government permits, which limit demands the government can attach in exchange for approval, cover money demands generally or only apply when money is sought as a substitute for requiring the owner to hand over an interest in real property.

## Where it stands

The petition was distributed for conference more than once, and after being rescheduled and distributed again, the Court denied it on October 13, 2026.

## What each outcome would mean

The Coalition for Fairness in SoHo and NoHo and individual residents asked the Court to take the case and overturn the ruling in the city's favor. Because the Court denied the petition, the New York Court of Appeals decision stands: the fee remains in effect, and owners seeking to convert their units still must pay it. A grant would have let the Court decide whether such fees must meet the nexus and proportionality tests the owners sought to apply, without determining who would ultimately win that analysis.
