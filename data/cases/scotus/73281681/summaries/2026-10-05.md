## In brief

Property owners in Manhattan's SoHo and NoHo neighborhoods ask the Court to revive their challenge to a city fee charged for converting artist-only housing to general residential use.

## What happened

Properties in SoHo and NoHo have long been restricted to artist occupancy under a zoning designation called Joint Living-Work Quarters for Artists. In 2021 New York City created an option letting owners convert these units to unrestricted residential use if they pay a one-time fee into a municipal arts fund. A coalition of owners and residents sued, arguing the fee is an unconstitutional condition on a land-use permit. A New York trial court rejected the claim, an intermediate appellate court sided with the owners, and the New York Court of Appeals reversed, ruling against the owners on two independent grounds.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked whether constitutional limits on government demands for property in exchange for land-use permits also cover monetary fees that are not offered as a substitute for requiring owners to hand over an interest in real property, or whether those limits apply only to fees tied to such a substitution.

## Where it stands

The petition has been distributed for conference several times, most recently for the October 9, 2026 conference, after full briefing including the city's opposition, the petitioners' reply, and several amicus briefs; the docket shows no decision yet on whether the Court will hear the case.

## What each outcome would mean

The Coalition for Fairness in SoHo and NoHo and other property owners asked the Court to take the case. If the Court grants review, it would hear full arguments and decide the legal question, deciding nothing yet about who wins. If it denies review, the New York Court of Appeals ruling stands, leaving the city's fee in place and the owners' challenge rejected. A denial would not mean the Court agrees the lower court was right, only that its decision remains final.
