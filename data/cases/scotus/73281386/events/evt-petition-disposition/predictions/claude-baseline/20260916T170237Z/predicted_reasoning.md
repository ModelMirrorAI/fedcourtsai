# Forecast: what the Court will do with No. 25-1103

**Docket state forecast from.** The petition was distributed once, on July 8, 2026, for the September 28, 2026 long conference. It has never been relisted. No CVSG is on the docket, and none can be: the Solicitor General is the petitioner.

**Disposition.** The Court will grant relief in some form (P = 0.60). It has already intervened in this exact dispute once: on June 6, 2025 it treated the government's stay application as a petition, granted certiorari, vacated the D.C. Circuit's denial of mandamus, told the court of appeals to narrow the April 15 discovery order, and stayed discovery "pending ... disposition of the petition for a writ of certiorari, if such writ is timely sought." Three Justices would have denied. A six-Justice majority that has once found the D.C. Circuit's handling of Cheney deficient, and that framed the stay as anticipating a second petition, is more likely than not to act again when the remand order disposed of the government's remaining objections in a single sentence. The most likely form is a second order in the same shape as the first: grant, vacate the denial of mandamus, and remand with instructions to conduct a request-by-request Cheney analysis of the surviving discovery (the Gleason deposition, the "directives" requests, the employee-roster and classified-data-access requests). Plenary review on the second question presented, whether discovery may ever be used to decide an EOP unit's FOIA "agency" status, is the next most likely grant form. A denial (roughly 0.40) is the live alternative, and if it comes it will be because enough of the June 2025 majority reads CREW's withdrawal of the recommendation-content requests as satisfying that order, and views a second round of supervising an unpublished, fact-bound mandamus denial over a year-old discovery dispute as unwarranted; the automatic termination of the stay would then send the case back to the district court.

**Relists.** Expect at least one further distribution (P = 0.55). A denial of an SG petition can issue straight off the long conference, but the grant forms most likely here, a per curiam or a reasoned GVR-style order, are drafted between conferences, and the June 2025 order itself was written rather than one-line. Roughly one to two relists if the Court is writing; zero if it denies without opinion.

**CVSG.** None (P = 0.01). The United States is the petitioner.

**Question presented, if taken.** If the Court sets the case for argument it will take QP 2 substantially as written, reframed around whether Kissinger's "advise and assist" carve-out may be tested through discovery into an EOP unit's actual authority. QP 1 is case-specific and is the ground for a summary order rather than plenary review.

**Summary disposition versus plenary review.** Conditional on any grant, a disposition in the cert order itself is somewhat more likely than briefing and argument (P = 0.55). The Court's prior order in this case cited In re United States, 583 U.S. 29 (2017), a per curiam summary disposition in the same posture, and the petition itself asks for "plenary review or summary reversal." The counter-case for plenary review is that QP 2 is a clean statutory question the Court flagged in June 2025 ("cannot turn on the entity's ability to persuade") and might prefer to settle with an opinion.

**Dissent from denial.** If the petition is denied, a noted dissent or statement is a coin-flip-minus (P = 0.40). The Justices most likely to write are Justice Alito or Justice Gorsuch, joined by Justice Thomas, on the Cheney separation-of-powers ground; a bare denial is otherwise the norm even for SG petitions.

**If granted for argument, conditional merits shape.** The Court would most likely hold that discovery into an EOP unit's FOIA status must be confined to the unit's formal grant of authority and cannot probe internal communications or the practical influence of its advice, reversing or vacating. That content is conditional on a plenary grant and forecasts nothing about a denial.
