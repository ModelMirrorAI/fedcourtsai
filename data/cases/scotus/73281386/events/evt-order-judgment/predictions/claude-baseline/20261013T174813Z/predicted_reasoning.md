# Forecast — U.S. DOGE Service v. U.S. District Court for D.C. (No. 25-1103), merits judgment

**Judgment class: reversed** (the D.C. Circuit's July 14, 2025 order denying mandamus), with
remand for the lower courts to confine or bar the remaining discovery. A *vacate-and-remand*
in the Cheney (2004) mould is the main alternative and would also disturb the judgment
below. Probability the judgment below is disturbed: **0.84**.

**Vote lineup: 6–3.** Majority: Roberts, Thomas, Alito, Gorsuch, Kavanaugh, Barrett.
Dissent: Sotomayor, Kagan, Jackson — the same alignment the Court showed in the June 2025
stay/GVR order this case already passed through. A separate concurrence (most likely
Thomas, Alito or Gorsuch) pressing the broader QP2 position — that FOIA "agency" status of a
presidential advisory unit may not be litigated through intrusive discovery at all — is
plausible; I do not commit to its author.

**Which question the Court reaches.** QP1 (departure from the remand order and from
Cheney's separation-of-powers principles). It can decide the case there without resolving
QP2 categorically. The mandamus posture pushes the same way: the Court needs only to find
the D.C. Circuit's refusal to apply Cheney to the residual requests a clear error, not to
announce a FOIA-wide rule.

**Ground and breadth.** The majority's ground: the D.C. Circuit read the 2025 order as
reaching only the withdrawn "recommendations" requests and so never applied Cheney's
necessity-and-tailoring inquiry to what remained (internal directives, data-access
"plans", employee records, a deposition of the Administrator); given the Court's earlier
statement that a unit's FOIA status cannot turn on its ability to persuade, that discovery
is not tailored to the substantial-independent-authority question. Breadth: narrow —
applied to this order, leaving the Soucie/Meyer test standing and declining a categorical
no-discovery rule.

**Procedural exits.** Mootness is live rather than merely conceivable: the DOGE temporary
organization's charter ran to July 2026 and the BIO itself frames the discovery as
backward-looking. But USDS as an EOP component and the FOIA suit both persist, and the
Court granted plenary review after the BIO made its equities point, so I put a DIG or
mootness dismissal at roughly 5–8%. A Munsingwear-style vacatur would still disturb the
judgment. An equally divided Court is not in play (no recusal is apparent).

**Timing.** Argument in the winter sitting (January–February 2027); decision by end of
June 2027, quite possibly earlier given the narrow question.
