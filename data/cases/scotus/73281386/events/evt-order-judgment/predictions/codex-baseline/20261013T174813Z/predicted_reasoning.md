# Forecast of the Court's judgment

The Court will **vacate the D.C. Circuit's judgment and remand** for further consideration of the remaining discovery under the separation-of-powers constraints applicable to presidential advisory bodies. This is a forecast of the judgment following the October 13, 2026 certiorari grant, not of the grant itself or the earlier June 2025 disposition.

I predict a **6–3 division**: Roberts, Thomas, Alito, Gorsuch, Kavanaugh, and Barrett in the majority; Sotomayor, Kagan, and Jackson dissenting. The vote forecast assumes those nine Justices participate. I do not forecast authorship or separate-writing roles.

## Majority ground

The majority will hold that the D.C. Circuit did not adequately apply the separation-of-powers inquiry required by *Cheney*. Removing the discovery requests directed expressly at recommendations did not exhaust the obligation to examine the necessity, burden, and tailoring of the remaining requests. In particular, internal directives, information about sensitive access, and examination of a senior advisory official require more than reliance on the availability of later privilege objections. The majority will distinguish evidence of independent governmental authority from evidence that presidential advice was persuasive.

The Court will resolve the first question presented against the judgment below and use the second question to explain the constraints on threshold agency-status discovery. It will not finally adjudicate whether USDS is a FOIA agency or which particular records CREW must ultimately receive.

## Breadth

The holding will require further narrowing of this discovery order, **not categorical immunity from every inquiry into an Executive Office entity's independent authority**. Targeted evidence about an actual delegation of authority will remain distinguishable from a broad examination of advisory communications and day-to-day operations. This is the specific breadth forecast stated in the structured claim.

The dissent will emphasize the distinction between recommendations and binding directives, the factual questions surrounding USDS's authority, and the demanding standard for mandamus. It will regard the remaining discovery and the lower courts' protective authority as sufficient.

A DIG is a small residual possibility, not the central prediction; I expect a substantive judgment. I do not expect recusal or an equally divided Court. The overall probability of disturbance is 84%; vacatur is the single most likely judgment class, not an 84% prediction of vacatur alone.
