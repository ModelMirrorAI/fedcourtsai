## What happened

Citizens for Responsibility and Ethics in Washington (CREW), a watchdog group, filed a Freedom of Information Act (FOIA) request with the U.S. DOGE Service (USDS), a body President Trump created within the Executive Office of the President. The government argued USDS is exempt from FOIA because it only advises the President. A district court found USDS likely wields independent authority and, after the government sought summary judgment, allowed limited discovery into USDS's structure and operations. The government sought mandamus (an order overturning the discovery ruling) in the D.C. Circuit, which was denied. The Supreme Court earlier vacated part of that ruling and sent the case back for narrower discovery. On remand, CREW dropped some requests, and the D.C. Circuit again denied the government's mandamus request for the rest.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked whether the appeals court properly followed the Supreme Court's earlier instructions and the separation-of-powers limits that apply when courts order fact-finding into how a presidential advisory body operates. The Court is also asked whether it is proper for a court to allow broad fact-finding aimed at deciding if an entity within the White House must comply with public-records law, when that fact-finding might hand over much of the same information the requester is separately trying to obtain under that law.

## Where it stands

The petition has been fully briefed, with CREW opposing review and the government replying, and the case was distributed for the Court's conference on September 28, 2026, where the Court will decide whether to take up the case.
