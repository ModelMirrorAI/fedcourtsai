## In brief

The government asks the Court to block discovery into USDS's structure and operations sought to determine whether the agency is subject to public-records law.

## What happened

Citizens for Responsibility and Ethics in Washington (CREW) filed a public-records request with the U.S. DOGE Service (USDS), a unit in the Executive Office of the President. USDS argued the records law does not apply to it because it only advises the President. A district court allowed limited discovery into USDS's structure and authority to test that claim. The government sought to block the discovery through a mandamus petition (an emergency order to a lower court), which the D.C. Circuit denied. The Supreme Court previously stepped in, narrowed part of that discovery, and sent the case back. On remand, CREW dropped some requests, and the D.C. Circuit again allowed most of the remaining discovery, prompting this petition.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked whether the appeals court properly followed the Supreme Court's earlier instructions and respected separation-of-powers limits in allowing discovery against a presidential advisory body, and whether courts may ever order broad discovery into such a body's operations just to decide whether the public-records law covers it in the first place.

## Where it stands

The Court granted the petition on October 13, 2026. The case has not yet been argued; most recently, the government asked for an extension of time connected to the merits briefing schedule.

## What each outcome would mean

USDS and other government petitioners are asking the Court to overturn the D.C. Circuit's decision allowing the discovery to proceed. If the Court affirms, CREW could pursue the discovery, including information about USDS's structure, directives, and a deposition of its acting administrator, as the case over whether USDS must comply with records requests continues. If the Court reverses, that discovery would be blocked or significantly narrowed. A vacate-and-remand would set aside the D.C. Circuit's ruling and send the case back without resolving the discovery dispute for either side.
