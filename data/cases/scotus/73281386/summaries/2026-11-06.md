## In brief

The government asks the Court to block discovery into whether USDS, an entity that advised the President, must comply with public records requests.

## What happened

Citizens for Responsibility and Ethics in Washington (CREW) filed a Freedom of Information Act request with the U.S. DOGE Service (USDS), which the government said was not covered by the law. A district court allowed limited discovery to determine whether USDS exercised independent authority rather than merely advising the President. The government sought mandamus (an order blocking the discovery) in the D.C. Circuit, which was denied. The Supreme Court earlier stepped in, narrowing part of that discovery and sending the case back. On remand, CREW withdrew some requests, and the D.C. Circuit again denied mandamus as to the rest. The government then asked the Supreme Court to take the case.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked whether the appeals court properly followed the Supreme Court's earlier instructions and respected separation-of-powers limits when it let most of the discovery go forward. The Court is also asked whether courts may ever order discovery into an executive office entity's operations to figure out if it counts as covered by the public records law, or whether that determination must rest solely on formal legal authority.

## Where it stands

The Supreme Court granted the petition on October 13, 2026. The parties are now preparing merits briefs, with deadlines extended into December 2026 and January 2027.

## What each outcome would mean

USDS and other government petitioners are asking the Court to overturn the lower court's decision allowing the discovery to proceed. If the Court sides with the government, it would block or sharply limit the discovery into USDS's structure and operations, including a deposition of its administrator, and would narrow when such discovery is allowed in future disputes over whether an entity counts as covered by the public records law. If the Court sides with CREW, the discovery could proceed, and the case would continue toward resolving whether USDS must respond to CREW's records request. A ruling sending the case back without deciding it would leave these questions for further proceedings below.
