## In brief

The federal government asks the Court to block discovery into USDS's operations, arguing lower courts ignored its prior order limiting such discovery against a presidential advisory body.

## What happened

Citizens for Responsibility and Ethics in Washington (CREW) filed a public-records request with the U.S. DOGE Service (USDS), seeking information about its structure and activities. USDS argued it is not subject to the public-records law because it only advises the President. A district court allowed limited discovery to test that claim, and the government sought emergency relief. The Supreme Court previously stepped in, narrowing part of that discovery order and sending the case back to the D.C. Circuit. On remand, CREW withdrew some requests, and the D.C. Circuit again allowed most of the remaining discovery to proceed, rejecting the government's renewed objections. The government then asked the Supreme Court to take up the case again.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked whether the lower court properly followed the Supreme Court's earlier instructions and respected separation-of-powers limits when it allowed discovery, including a deposition of a top USDS official, to go forward. The Court is also asked whether courts may ever order this kind of discovery into an advisory White House office to figure out whether disclosure law applies to it at all, when that discovery might hand over much of the same information being sought in the underlying records request.

## Where it stands

The petition was distributed for conference twice, and on October 13, 2026, the Court granted the petition, agreeing to hear the case.

## What each outcome would mean

USDS and other government petitioners asked the Court to take the case. Because the Court granted review, it will now hear full arguments and decide the questions itself rather than leaving the D.C. Circuit's order in place automatically. If the Court ultimately sides with the government, it would significantly limit or block the discovery CREW has been seeking into USDS's structure and operations. If it sides with CREW, the discovery allowed by the lower courts could proceed. The Court could also vacate the lower court's ruling and send the case back without deciding who is right.
