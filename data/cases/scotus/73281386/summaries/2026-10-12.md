## In brief

The federal government asks the Court to block discovery into a White House efficiency office, arguing a lower court defied its earlier order limiting such discovery.

## What happened

A watchdog group, CREW, filed a public-records request with the U.S. DOGE Service (USDS), housed in the White House, and sued when USDS refused to process it, arguing USDS is not covered by federal disclosure law. A district court allowed limited discovery into USDS's structure and authority. The government sought mandamus (an order overturning the discovery) in the D.C. Circuit, which refused. The Supreme Court previously stepped in, narrowing one part of that discovery and sending the case back. On remand, CREW dropped some requests, and the D.C. Circuit again denied the government's mandamus request as to the rest. The government again asks the Supreme Court to intervene.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked whether the appeals court properly followed the Supreme Court's earlier instructions and separation-of-powers principles limiting court-ordered fact-finding aimed at White House advisory bodies, and whether courts may order broad discovery into such a body to decide if disclosure law applies to it at all, even though that discovery might hand over much of the same information sought in the underlying records request.

## Where it stands

The petition has been distributed for conference twice, most recently October 9, 2026, after full briefing including a reply from the government; the docket does not show any decision on the petition since that conference.

## What each outcome would mean

The government, as petitioner, asks the Court to step in. If the Court grants the petition, it would hear the case and decide these discovery questions itself; a grant does not decide who is right. If the Court denies the petition, the D.C. Circuit's decision stands, leaving in place the order allowing CREW limited discovery into USDS, including depositions and documents about its structure and authority. A decision to summarily reverse, which the government also requested, would overturn the lower court's ruling without full briefing or argument.
