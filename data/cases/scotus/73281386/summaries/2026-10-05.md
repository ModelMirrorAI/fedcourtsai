## In brief

The federal government asks the Court to block discovery into a White House efficiency office's operations in a public records lawsuit.

## What happened

A watchdog group, Citizens for Responsibility and Ethics in Washington (CREW), filed a Freedom of Information Act request with the U.S. DOGE Service, seeking records about its structure and activities. The government argued DOGE Service is exempt because it only advises the President. A district court allowed limited discovery to test that claim, and the government sought to block it. The Court previously stepped in once, narrowing part of that discovery and sending the case back to the D.C. Circuit, which again allowed most of the discovery to proceed. The government now returns to the Court seeking further review.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked whether the appeals court properly followed the Court's earlier instructions and respected separation-of-powers limits when it allowed discovery into the operations of a presidential advisory body. The Court is also asked whether courts may ever order discovery into such a body to figure out whether public-records law applies to it in the first place, when that discovery might reveal much of the same information sought in the underlying records request.

## Where it stands

The petition has been distributed for conference twice, most recently for October 9, 2026, after full briefing including a response from CREW and a reply from the government. The docket does not yet show any action taken at either conference.

## What each outcome would mean

The government, as petitioner, is asking the Court to step in again. If the Court grants review, it would hear the case and decide whether the discovery order and the appeals court's approach comply with separation-of-powers principles and federal records law. If the Court denies review, the D.C. Circuit's decision stands, and the discovery into the DOGE Service's structure and operations—including depositions—can proceed in the district court, continuing CREW's underlying lawsuit.
