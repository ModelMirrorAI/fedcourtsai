# Forecast — Goldman v. Phillips, No. 26-315

The docket shows one distribution (for the Nov. 13, 2026 conference) and a response requested on Nov. 4, due Dec. 4, 2026.

- **Further distribution.** The petition will be distributed at least once more after the response arrives — most likely in late December 2026 for a January 2027 conference, later if respondents obtain an extension (likely, about a 30-day extension). After that redistribution, I expect at most one true relist; the more likely course is a decision at the first post-response conference.
- **CVSG.** Probably not (about 12%). If one issues it will come at that first post-response conference, because the United States Trustee Program's oversight of Chapter 7 trustees gives the government a stake in the liability standard.
- **Disposition.** Denial is the likeliest outcome (about 80%), most plausibly because the response persuades the Court that the conflict is shallower or staler than the petition says, or that the interlocutory posture and the opinion's immunity framing make this a poor vehicle. A grant has about a one-in-five chance.
- **Question presented, if granted.** The Court would take the question essentially as written but likely focused on its second half: what standard of fault (negligence, gross negligence, or willful and deliberate misconduct) governs a Chapter 7 trustee's personal liability for breaches of official duties. If granted, the likely ground of decision turns on the reading of *Mosser v. Darrow* and common-law trustee and immunity principles.
- **Summary disposition.** Unlikely; any grant would be plenary, with argument in spring 2027 or the following Term.
- **Separate writing on denial.** None expected.
