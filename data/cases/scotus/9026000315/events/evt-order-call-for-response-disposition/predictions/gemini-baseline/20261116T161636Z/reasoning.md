## 2. Case-Specific Adjustments (`5.2%` anchor / `~15–16%` amicus reference → `21%`)

### Upward Signals
- **Published, unanimous *en banc* Ninth Circuit decision explicitly deepening a multi-circuit split:** In *Phillips v. Goldman*, 176 F.4th 1152 (9th Cir. 2026) (en banc) (`App. 1a–24a`), the Ninth Circuit voted *sua sponte* after panel argument to hear the appeal *en banc* in the first instance (`App. 53a`) in order to revisit *In re Cochise College Park, Inc.*, 703 F.2d 1339 (9th Cir. 1983). Judge Nguyen's unanimous 11-judge opinion expressly recognized an entrenched circuit split (`App. 15a–16a`) over the standard for holding a bankruptcy trustee personally liable under *Mosser v. Darrow*, 341 U.S. 267 (1951): four circuits (4th, 6th, 7th, 10th) require *willful and deliberate* breaches of duty, whereas the Ninth Circuit joined the First and Fifth Circuits in adopting *gross negligence* (while noting unoverruled Second Circuit authority in *In re Gorski*, 766 F.2d 723 (2d Cir. 1985), applying ordinary negligence, alongside Third Circuit precedent in *Phoenician Mediterranean Villa, LLC v. Swope*, 872 F.3d 138 (3d Cir. 2017), applying *Harlow* qualified immunity).
- **Pre-conference Call for a Response and repeat Supreme Court advocacy:** Respondents (represented by solo appellate counsel) neither answered nor waived by the October 13, 2026 due date; after the October 28 distribution, at least one chambers requested a response on November 4, nine days before conference. Petitioner is represented by Daniel L. Geyser, an experienced Supreme Court bankruptcy litigator, supported by respected retired bankruptcy judges (`Hon. Leif Clark`, `Hon. Melanie Cyganowski`, `Hon. Bruce Markell`, `Hon. Eugene Wedoff`, and `Prof. Nancy Rapoport`). The Court regularly takes clean splits in federal bankruptcy administration.

### Downward Signals and Vehicle Headwinds
- **Interlocutory *Barton* leave-to-sue posture and appellate-finality complications:** No court has held petitioner Goldman liable or even adjudicated the merits of respondents' tort claims. The Bankruptcy Court denied respondents' *Barton* gatekeeping motion for leave to sue (`App. 42a–52a`), the District Court vacated in part and remanded (`App. 7a`), appellate jurisdiction under 28 U.S.C. § 158(d) required respondents to file a post-appeal waiver disclaiming remand issues after a Ninth Circuit show-cause order (`App. 7a–10a`), and the *en banc* Ninth Circuit itself remanded to the Bankruptcy Court (`App. 24a`) both to apply the five discretionary *In re Kashani* gatekeeping factors (`App. 12a n.3`) and to assess whether respondents' proposed complaint states a *prima facie* case. Goldman can still defeat leave to sue on remand without Supreme Court intervention, a classic posture in which the Court denies certiorari.
- **Doubt whether the split is outcome-determinative on intentional-tort allegations:** Unlike classic estate-surcharge cases involving negligent asset management, respondents seek leave to assert *abuse of process* and *intentional infliction of emotional distress* (`App. 6a, 23a`), alleging that Goldman deliberately prolonged administration and pursued retaliatory sanctions out of fee-related animus (`App. 5a, 23a`). Because both state-law causes of action are intentional torts requiring willful or outrageous conduct, a brief in opposition (or Solicitor General brief) can forcefully argue that respondents' allegations, if proven, would cross *even* the 4th/6th/7th/10th Circuits' "willful and deliberate" threshold—making this appeal a poor vehicle to test the space between gross negligence and willful misconduct.
- **Overreach and preservation defect on petitioner's lead statutory argument:** Petitioner devotes the lead portion of her merits pitch (`Pet. 17–24`) to arguing that under the 1978 Bankruptcy Code (`11 U.S.C. §§ 322, 323`), Chapter 7 trustees cannot be held personally liable *at all* beyond their official bond. Not a single circuit accepts that proposition, and the *en banc* Ninth Circuit explicitly observed that Goldman raised that contention only at oral argument (`App. 14a`), creating a serious forfeiture obstacle on the first half of the Question Presented.
- **Long-tolerated split with limited institutional urgency:** The divide between *Sherr*/*Yadkin*/*Chicago Pacific* and *Mailman Steam*/*Smyth* has stood for more than 25 years without drawing Supreme Court review (`same-question.json` found 0 related petitions across 15,905 indexed petitions, and CourtListener reflects no prior cert grants on the split cases). Notably, while the National Association of Bankruptcy Trustees filed an *en banc* amicus brief below (`CA9 Dkt. 87`), it did not file a cert-stage amicus brief here.

Balancing the genuine *en banc* 4–4 split, specialist counsel, and federal bankruptcy-administration stakes against the interlocutory *Barton* remand, intentional-tort vehicle mismatch, and unpreserved `§ 322` lead theory yields **`probability = 0.21`**—roughly four times the `5.2%` `baseline` anchor and modestly above the `16.3%` amicus-arm reference, while keeping denial (`79%`) clearly favored.
