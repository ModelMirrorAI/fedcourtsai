# Predicted Court Disposition and Procedural Trajectory

The Court will **deny** the petition for a writ of certiorari after receiving the requested brief in opposition.

## Procedural Path From the Current Docket State

- **Further distributions and relists:** The docket currently shows **one** distribution (`October 28, 2026`, for the November 13, 2026 Conference), which was superseded before conference by the Court's `November 4, 2026` call for a response (due December 4, 2026). Absent pre-submission settlement or Rule 46 dismissal, the petition will be **distributed at least once more** in late December 2026 or January 2027 once the brief in opposition is filed or the response period expires. At that post-response conference, the modal outcome is a denial without further relists, with a secondary path of **one true relist** (a third total distribution) while chambers scrutinize the vehicle and consider whether to invite the Solicitor General's views.
- **Call for the Views of the Solicitor General (CVSG):** No CVSG is currently on the docket, and the Court will most likely **resolve the petition without calling for the Solicitor General's views**. There is a meaningful minority chance (~12%) that the Court issues a CVSG in early 2027—given the Executive Office for United States Trustees' statutory supervision of Chapter 7 panel trustees under 28 U.S.C. § 586—before acting on the petition, in which event the Solicitor General would likely flag the interlocutory *Barton* remand and recommend denial.
- **Scope of the question presented (if granted):** If the Court were to grant certiorari, it would either take the petition's single two-part question as framed or **narrow review to the second clause**—the *mens rea* standard governing a Chapter 7 trustee's personal liability for official acts (`willful and deliberate` versus `gross negligence` under *Mosser v. Darrow*, 341 U.S. 267 (1951))—leaving aside or swiftly rejecting petitioner's unpreserved categorical contention that 11 U.S.C. § 322 forecloses personal liability altogether. Conditional on plenary review, the Court would likely clarify the post-*Mosser* immunity framework for discretionary estate administration rather than abolishing personal liability outright.
- **Summary disposition route:** Conditional on a grant, **plenary briefing and oral argument** is overwhelmingly likelier than summary disposition. No pending Supreme Court case presents an intervening holding for a grant, vacate, and remand (GVR), and the Court will not summarily reverse a unanimous 11-judge *en banc* opinion that aligned with two other circuits on a longstanding open question.
- **Separate writings on denial:** Conditional on denial, the Court will most likely issue an **unaccompanied one-line denial order** without noted dissent or statement. In the unlikely event (~4%) of a separate writing, the most plausible authors are Justice Thomas or Justice Gorsuch (dissenting from or noting the persistent multi-circuit split over *Mosser v. Darrow*), or Justice Sotomayor or Justice Kavanaugh (respecting denial on interlocutory vehicle grounds).
