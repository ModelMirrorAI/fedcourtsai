# Forecast — Goldman v. Phillips, No. 26-315

The docket shows one distribution (for the Nov. 13, 2026 conference) and a response requested on Nov. 4, due Dec. 4, 2026.

- **Further distribution:** yes. Respondents will file a brief in opposition (possibly after an extension), petitioner will reply, and the petition will be redistributed for a conference in January or February 2027. I expect at most one true relist after that redistribution; most likely the petition is decided at the first conference after briefing completes.
- **CVSG:** probably not (about one in eight). If one issues it will come at that first post-briefing conference, and the petition would then stay pending into late 2027.
- **Disposition:** denial is the most likely outcome (about 83%). The respondents will argue that the decision below is interlocutory, that the en banc court's holding concerns immunity doctrine rather than the standard of care, and that the trustee can still prevail on remand; that is enough to keep the Court away in most such cases despite the acknowledged disagreement among circuits.
- **If granted:** the Court would take the single question as written, perhaps narrowed to the standard of care for personal liability of a Chapter 7 trustee, and set the case for argument. A summary disposition is unlikely; no intervening decision bears on the question.
- **Separate writing:** if denied, no dissent or statement is expected.
