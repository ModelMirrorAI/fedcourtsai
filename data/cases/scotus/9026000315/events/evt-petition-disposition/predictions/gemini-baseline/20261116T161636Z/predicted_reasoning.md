**Call for the Views of the Solicitor General (CVSG).** Expect no CVSG, as vehicle defects are likely to resolve the petition at the post-opposition conference. **Question presented if granted.** If the Court grants review, it will take only the second half of the petition's question presented—what standard of fault governs a Chapter 7 trustee's personal liability for official acts under *Mosser v. Darrow*, 341 U.S. 267 (1951), and the Bankruptcy Code—or reformulate the question to focus on that three-way circuit split, leaving behind the unpreserved threshold contention that Chapter 7 trustees face no personal-capacity liability at all.

**Summary disposition vs. plenary review.** Conditional on a grant, the Court will set the case for plenary merits briefing and oral argument rather than disposing of it summarily in the cert order. No pending Supreme Court merits case addresses bankruptcy trustee liability or *Mosser*, foreclosing a hold-and-GVR path, and summary reversal of a unanimous 11-judge en banc opinion sitting on a four-circuit side of an entrenched 4-4-1 split is practically out of the question. If granted on the merits, the Court would likely confront sharp tension between common-law trust liability and 11 U.S.C. § 322's faithful-performance bond scheme on one side and *Mosser*'s "willful and deliberate" language on the other.

**Separate writings on denial.** Expect a clean denial order with no separate writing. In the unlikely event a Justice writes separately upon denial, Justice Thomas or Justice Gorsuch (or, less likely, Justice Kavanaugh) is the likeliest author of a brief statement respecting denial or dissent from denial noting the acknowledged circuit split over Chapter 7 trustee personal liability while pointing to the interlocutory remand posture as reason to await a final judgment.
