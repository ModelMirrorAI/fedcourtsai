### Downward adjustments (vehicle, posture, and merits headwinds)
1. **Interlocutory Rule 12(b)(6) remand with multiple untouched threshold defenses**: Reading `appendix.txt` closely reveals a major vehicle defect that respondents' forthcoming BIO will predictably center. The Ninth Circuit reversed a Rule 12(b)(6) dismissal of an adversary complaint and remanded while *expressly leaving open* both (a) **derived judicial immunity** based on bankruptcy-court approval/abandonment (App. 24a) and (b) Goldman's unaddressed Rule 12(b)(6) grounds—above all the **business judgment rule**, which the en banc court went out of its way to reaffirm protects trustees from liability for reasonable discretionary judgments (App. 5a–6a n.4, 21a–22a, 24a), alongside lack of duty to "babysit" encumbered collateral for a lienholder who slept on her lien for 14 years, speculative damages, and estoppel. Goldman may well win dismissal on remand without ever facing personal liability, mooting the standard-of-care dispute; and because the issue here is the substantive fault standard for breach of fiduciary duty rather than *Mitchell v. Forsyth* collateral-order immunity from trial (her functional quasi-judicial immunity claim was rejected on *Antoine*/*Castillo* grounds), the Court's strong presumption against interlocutory cert review applies with full force.
2. **Partial non-preservation and state-law/bond framing below**: The first clause of the petition's question presented—*"Whether Chapter 7 trustees may be personally liable for their official acts"* (developed in Pet. 19 n.7 as an implied-right-of-action critique)—was never raised or passed upon below. Worse, the district court noted below that respondents pleaded a *California state-law* fiduciary breach claim alongside a derivative claim on Goldman's faithful-performance bond under **11 U.S.C. § 322** and Fed. R. Bankr. P. 2010(b) (App. 5a n.3, 36a), with federal law invoked as an affirmative immunity defense. Even petitioner's amici concede personal liability exists for gross/willful misconduct rather than endorsing complete non-liability.
