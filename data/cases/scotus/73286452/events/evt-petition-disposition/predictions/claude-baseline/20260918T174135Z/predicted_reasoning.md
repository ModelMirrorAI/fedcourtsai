# Forecast: what the Court will do with No. 25-1240, Khatibi v. Yip

**Where the docket stands.** Two distributions so far: the June 25, 2026 conference
(after which the Court called for a response over the State's waiver) and the
September 28, 2026 long conference, to which the petition was redistributed once
the brief in opposition and reply were in. The second distribution is the
mechanical post-response redistribution, not a discretionary relist; in the
classic sense this petition has never been relisted. No CVSG. Six cert-stage
amicus briefs (Cato, AAPS, Center for Equal Opportunity and Manhattan Institute,
Judicial Watch, Buckeye Institute, and Montana leading a coalition of States).

**Disposition.** I forecast a denial as the modal outcome but with a substantial
minority chance of a grant, P(grant) = 0.40. If the Court grants, it will grant
plenary review on the question as petitioners framed it ("whether private
instruction in courses required for state licensure constitutes government
speech"), probably lightly reformulated to ask whether California's CME
implicit-bias curriculum requirement is government speech exempt from First
Amendment scrutiny, and set the case for argument in the January or February
2027 sittings. It will not take up the State's reframing (whether the Ninth
Circuit "correctly applied" Shurtleff), which is a merits-error framing.

**Relists.** The long conference clears most of the summer's paid petitions
either way, so the first order after September 28 is the likeliest disposal
point. I put P(at least one further distribution) at 0.45: grants from the long
conference are now frequently announced without a relist, and a plain denial
would need none. A further distribution most likely comes either because a
Justice is writing on a denial or because the Court wants one more conference
before a grant. If there is a relist, I expect one or two, not a long string.

**CVSG.** Very unlikely (0.03). The challenged law is a state licensing
curriculum requirement with no federal statute, federal program, or federal
party implicated, and the petition asks a pure constitutional question. The
Solicitor General could file an unsolicited amicus brief, but that is not a
CVSG and I do not expect the Court to invite one.

**Summary disposition versus plenary review.** Conditional on a grant, I put the
cert-order route (a GVR or a per curiam reversal) at 0.25. The candidate GVR
vehicle is Chiles v. Salazar (decided March 31, 2026, after the Ninth Circuit's
panel and en banc rulings), which treated a licensed professional's talk therapy
as private speech; the Court could send the case back for reconsideration in
that light. But Chiles did not address the government-speech threshold the
Ninth Circuit rested on, and the State's brief in opposition makes exactly that
point, so a GVR would be a loose fit. A summary reversal of a published,
unanimous, heavily reasoned opinion with a State as respondent is possible given
how far the panel stretched Walker and Shurtleff, but the Court usually prefers
argument for a doctrinal correction of this kind. Plenary review is the likelier
grant route by a wide margin.

**Dissent from denial.** Conditional on a denial, P(some Justice notes a dissent
or files a statement respecting denial) = 0.35. The profile fits: an
ideologically salient compelled-speech claim, a three-judge dissent from the
denial of rehearing en banc, a multistate amicus coalition, and a doctrine
(government speech) that Justice Thomas and Justice Alito have both written
about with concern. The likeliest author of a dissent would be Justice Thomas
or Justice Alito, possibly joined by Justice Gorsuch. The "from whom" half is
context only.

**If granted, on the merits (conditional).** The likely ground is that the
Ninth Circuit misapplied the holistic Shurtleff inquiry by treating pervasive
regulation of the medical profession and topic-level curricular requirements as
government authorship of privately created courses, contrary to Matal v. Tam's
insistence that regulation, review, and official approval do not make private
expression the government's. The Court would reverse and remand for the
compelled-speech claim to be analyzed as a regulation of private speech under
NIFLA and Chiles, without itself deciding whether the implicit-bias requirement
survives scrutiny. A lineup along the Court's usual First Amendment lines is
the expectation, with a meaningful chance of a broader majority given
Shurtleff's own unanimity.
