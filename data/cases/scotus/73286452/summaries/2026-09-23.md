## What happened

California requires physicians to complete continuing medical education (CME) to keep their medical licenses. Since 2022, the state has required CME courses to address implicit bias and its possible role in health care disparities. Dr. Azadeh Khatibi, who teaches CME courses, and Do No Harm, a physicians' organization, sued, arguing this requirement forces them to convey a message they disagree with, violating their free-speech rights. A federal district court dismissed the suit, and the Ninth Circuit Court of Appeals affirmed, ruling that because California so extensively shapes and controls CME content, the courses count as the government's own speech rather than private speech, so the free-speech objection did not apply. The full Ninth Circuit declined to rehear the case, over dissenting judges' objections.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked to decide whether instruction that private teachers create and deliver, in courses required for a professional license, should be treated as the government's own speech or as the instructor's private speech. If it counts as government speech, free-speech protections do not apply to content requirements; if it counts as private speech, the state's mandate would face stricter constitutional review. The petitioners also argue that lower courts are applying this distinction inconsistently, while California responds that its ruling was narrow and consistent with other courts.

## Where it stands

The petition for cert, which asks the Court to take up the case, has been fully briefed with multiple amicus briefs filed on both sides, and the case was distributed for the Court's conference on September 28, 2026, after an earlier conference in June; the Court has not yet acted on the petition.
