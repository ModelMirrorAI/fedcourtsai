## In brief

A California physician asks the Court to revive her challenge to a state rule requiring continuing-education courses for doctors to cover implicit bias.

## What happened

Azadeh Khatibi, an ophthalmologist, and Do No Harm, a nonprofit of healthcare professionals, sued officials of the Medical Board of California over a 2019 law requiring that continuing medical education (CME) courses include content on implicit bias to qualify for credit toward license renewal. They argued this compelled their speech in violation of the First Amendment. The district court dismissed the suit, holding that CME courses are government speech, not private speech protected from compelled-content rules. The Ninth Circuit affirmed, and the full court denied rehearing en banc over two dissents.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked whether instruction that private teachers give in courses required for a professional license counts as the government's own speech, which would place it outside ordinary free-speech protections, or whether it remains private speech that the state cannot force instructors to shape to its liking.

## Where it stands

The petition has been distributed for conference multiple times, most recently for October 16, 2026, after full briefing including an opposition brief and a reply; the docket does not show that the Court has yet acted on it.

## What each outcome would mean

Khatibi and Do No Harm, as petitioners, are asking the Court to overturn the Ninth Circuit's ruling that favored the Medical Board. If the Court grants review, it would hear full arguments and decide whether the CME requirement is government speech; a grant alone would not decide who wins. If the Court denies review, the Ninth Circuit's decision stands, leaving the implicit-bias requirement in effect and the Medical Board's position intact. Either way, denial would not mean the Court agreed the lower court was right.
