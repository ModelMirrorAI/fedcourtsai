## In brief

A Wisconsin homeowner asked the Court to hear his claim that a judge should have told him about his appeal rights before dismissing his lawsuit; the Court denied review.

## What happened

Dan Schmidt sued the City of Omro, a bank, and an insurer, Employers Mutual Casualty Company (EMC), claiming a neighboring bank's new parking lot flooded his property and damaged his home. A Wisconsin trial court dismissed his claims against EMC and its adjuster, finding he had no legal right to sue them. The written dismissal order did not tell Schmidt about his right to appeal. He later missed the deadline, and Wisconsin's Court of Appeals dismissed his appeal as untimely, reasoning that the phrase "with prejudice" was enough notice. The Wisconsin Supreme Court denied further review. Schmidt, representing himself, then asked the U.S. Supreme Court to take the case.

## What the Court is being asked

Schmidt asked the Court to decide whether the Constitution's guarantee of fair treatment by the states requires a trial judge to specifically tell a party about their right to appeal, in civil lawsuits over property damage and injury, not just in criminal cases. He also asked whether courts can excuse that requirement simply because the dismissal order used the words "with prejudice."

## Where it stands

The petition was distributed for the Court's September 28, 2026 conference, and on October 5, 2026 the Court denied it.

## What each outcome would mean

Schmidt, who brought the case to the Court, asked it to reverse the Wisconsin Supreme Court's decision and reinstate his appeal against EMC and its adjuster. By denying the petition, the Court leaves the Wisconsin courts' rulings in place: Schmidt's appeal remains dismissed as untimely, and EMC and its adjuster remain out of the lawsuit. The denial does not mean the Court agreed with the Wisconsin courts' reasoning, only that it will not review the case further.
