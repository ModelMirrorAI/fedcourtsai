## In brief

A Wisconsin homeowner who lost his lawsuit over property damage asks the Court to review whether he should have been told about his right to appeal.

## What happened

Dan Schmidt sued the City of Omro, a bank, and an insurance company, claiming that construction next to his property caused flooding that damaged his home and health. A trial court dismissed his claims against the insurance company, Employers Mutual Casualty Company (EMC) and its adjuster, with prejudice. Schmidt later tried to appeal, but the Wisconsin Court of Appeals dismissed his appeal as untimely, and the Wisconsin Supreme Court denied further review. Schmidt, representing himself, now asks the U.S. Supreme Court to take up his case.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked whether a state trial judge must tell a party, at a hearing and in a written order dismissing a civil case, that the party has a right to appeal, and whether that requirement can be satisfied simply by saying the dismissal is "with prejudice" without further explanation.

## Where it stands

The petition was distributed for the Court's conference on September 28, 2026. EMC and its adjuster have waived their right to file a response.

## What each outcome would mean

Schmidt, the petitioner, is asking the Court to take up his case and reverse the lower courts. If the Court grants review, it would hear the case and decide whether trial judges must give this kind of notice, without yet deciding who wins. If the Court denies review, the Wisconsin Supreme Court's decision stands, meaning the dismissal of Schmidt's claims against EMC and its adjuster remains final and his appeal stays dismissed as untimely.
