## What happened

Dan Schmidt, representing himself, sued the City of Omro, Wisconsin, several city officials, a bank, and his insurer, EMC Insurance, over water damage he says ruined his home's foundation and caused him serious illness. A Wisconsin trial court dismissed his claims against EMC and its adjuster with prejudice, meaning permanently, but did not tell him at the hearing or in the written order that he had a right to appeal. By the time Schmidt tried to appeal, the Wisconsin Court of Appeals ruled his notice of appeal was too late, reasoning that the words "with prejudice" put him on notice that the clock had started. The Wisconsin Supreme Court declined to review that decision and ordered him to pay costs.

## What the Court is being asked

Schmidt asks the Court to decide whether the Constitution's guarantee of fair treatment by the states requires a trial judge to specifically tell a person, at the hearing and in writing, that they have a right to appeal a dismissal in a civil injury or property case. He also asks whether it matters that the judge merely said the dismissal was "with prejudice," rather than plainly spelling out the appeal right. Courts already require this kind of warning in criminal cases; Schmidt argues the same protection should apply here.

## Where it stands
