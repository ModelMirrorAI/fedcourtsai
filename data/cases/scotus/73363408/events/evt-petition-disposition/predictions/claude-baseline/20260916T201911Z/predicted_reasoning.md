# Forecast: Schmidt v. City of Omro, Wisconsin, No. 25-1293 (evt-petition-disposition)

**The Court will deny the petition on the first Order List after the September 28, 2026 long conference, without comment.**

## Relists

The docket shows **one distribution** (July 1, 2026, for the conference of September 28, 2026) and no relists. Respondents waived their right to respond on May 27, 2026, so there is nothing left to arrive and nothing to wait for. I expect **no further distribution**: the petition is decided at the long conference and denied on the October 5, 2026 Order List. The residual chance of a further distribution is essentially the mechanical one, a reschedule or a redistribution caused by the long conference's volume, not a Justice's interest in the case.

## CVSG

**No call for the views of the Solicitor General.** This is a private civil tort dispute between a pro se homeowner and an insurer and its adjuster over a state court's dismissal of an untimely appeal. No federal party, statute, or program is implicated, and the Court does not invite the Solicitor General into a case of this shape.

## Which question, if any

If the Court took anything, it would not be either question as written. Question 1 asks the Court to hold that the Fourteenth Amendment obliges a state trial judge, in a civil case, to advise a litigant of the state-created right to appeal at the hearing and in the written order of dismissal, by analogy to the criminal-sentencing practice in Rodriguez and Peguero. Question 2 asks for an equitable exception where the judge said only that the dismissal was "with prejudice." Neither question describes a conflict among lower courts, and the petition's own account of the decisions below shows the appeal was dismissed on a state timeliness ground, so the federal question sits behind an adequate and independent state ground. There is no narrowed or reformulated version the Court would find worth taking.

## Summary disposition versus plenary review

Conditional on the very unlikely event of a grant, the route is more likely summary than plenary, since no Justice would set this record down for argument. But no intervening decision of this Court supplies a reason to grant, vacate, and remand: the petitioner's reliance on Hunter v. United States, No. 24-1063, concerns appeal waivers in criminal plea agreements, and whatever that case held it does not speak to a civil trial court's duty to announce appellate rights. So I forecast neither route in fact, just a denial.

## Dissent or statement respecting denial

**None.** A pro se civil petition raising a novel due process theory with no split and a procedural posture that independently defeats it does not draw a separate writing from any Justice.

## If granted (conditional only)

Were the Court somehow to reach the merits, it would reject the theory: the Court has never held that due process requires a civil trial court to advise a litigant of appellate rights, and it would decline to extend the criminal-sentencing admonishment cases to civil dismissals, leaving the timeliness of a state civil appeal to state law.
