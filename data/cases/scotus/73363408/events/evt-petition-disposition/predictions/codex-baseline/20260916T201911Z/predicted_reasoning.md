# Forecast

The Court will deny the petition for certiorari. The most likely disposition is an ordinary orders-list denial after the September 28, 2026 conference, in late September or early October 2026, without a separate writing. This is a timing forecast, not a separately scored claim.

The provisioned docket records one distribution, entered July 1 for that September conference, and no CVSG. I expect no further distribution: P(at least one additional distribution) is 0.04. In the less likely event of further consideration, one additional distribution is more likely than a prolonged series. I expect no request for the Solicitor General's views; P(a new CVSG) is 0.001.

The petition's request to extend criminal-appeal protections to civil tort litigants will not persuade the Court to take this particular vehicle. The dispute about the finality of dismissal orders and the timing of this appeal will dominate over the proposed broad constitutional rule. This predicts selection for review, not a merits holding validating the state courts' reasoning.

If the Court grants review, I expect it to focus on the first question, narrowed to whether due process permits dismissal of an otherwise available civil appeal when the litigant alleges inadequate or misleading notice that an order is final and appealable. The second question about an exception for dismissal with prejudice would be subsidiary to that notice inquiry. This question-framing forecast is context only.

Conditional on any grant, plenary review is more likely than disposition in the cert order: P(summary-disposition route | grant) is 0.20. The petition's analogy to Hunter's criminal appeal-waiver question, as described in the filed petition, does not itself identify a controlling decision requiring a GVR here. A summary route remains possible but is not the central forecast.

Conditional on denial, P(a noted dissent or statement respecting denial) is 0.005. I expect none and do not forecast an individual Justice's cert vote or an opinion author.
