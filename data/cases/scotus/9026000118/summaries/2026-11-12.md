## In brief

Alabama officials appeal a district court order blocking the state's 2023 congressional map, in a long-running dispute over Black voters' representation.

## What happened

Alabama's congressional map has been challenged since 2021 by several groups of voters, including state senator Bobby Singleton and others, who argued it diluted Black voting strength in violation of the Voting Rights Act and the Constitution. After earlier rounds reached the Supreme Court, a three-judge district court again blocked the state's map (the "2023 Plan") in May 2026, ruling that it likely violated federal law and intentionally discriminated against Black voters, and ordered the state to use a court-drawn map instead. Alabama appealed, and the Supreme Court temporarily paused that order while the appeal proceeds.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked to decide whether the lower court wrongly excused the challengers from showing an alternative map that met the state's stated map-drawing goals just as well as the state's own map, whether the lower court mistook ordinary political patterns for race-based voting, whether the overall evidence supported a finding of likely discrimination, and whether Alabama violated the Constitution by not drawing a map with an additional district favorable to Black voters.

## Where it stands

The case was distributed for conferences in October and November 2026, most recently November 13; the docket shows no action taken on the appeal as of the snapshot date.

## What each outcome would mean

Alabama officials, led by Secretary of State Wes Allen, are asking the Court to overturn the district court's order, which favored Singleton and the other voters who challenged the map. If the Court agrees with Alabama, the 2023 Plan could go back into effect, and the challengers' claims would not succeed as decided below. If the Court agrees with Singleton and the other challengers, the district court's order blocking the 2023 Plan and requiring a court-drawn substitute would stand. The Court could also send the case back for further proceedings without resolving the dispute for either side.
