## In brief

Alabama officials ask the Court to reverse a district court order blocking the state's 2023 congressional map as racially discriminatory.

## What happened

This appeal is the latest round in long-running litigation over Alabama's congressional districts. After earlier rulings found Alabama's prior map likely violated federal voting-rights law by diluting Black voters' strength, the state passed a new 2023 map. A three-judge district court again blocked that map, ruling it violated the Voting Rights Act and intentionally discriminated against Black voters under the Fourteenth Amendment, and ordered the state to keep using a court-drawn map instead. State officials, including the Secretary of State and legislative leaders, appealed to the Supreme Court, arguing the lower court misapplied the legal standards for proving vote dilution and intentional discrimination.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked whether the lower court was wrong to find a voting-rights violation without first requiring challengers to produce an alternative map that served the state's stated map-drawing goals just as well; whether the court wrongly found race-based patterns in voting without separating out the effect of party politics; whether the overall evidence supported finding a likely violation; and whether the state's decision not to draw a map with an additional majority-Black district amounted to intentional racial discrimination.

## Where it stands

The case was docketed in July 2026, and after a response, a reply, and other filings, it was distributed for conference three times, most recently November 13, 2026. No action from any of those conferences is yet recorded.

## What each outcome would mean

The state officials who brought this appeal are asking the Court to overturn the district court's order blocking their map. If the Court affirms, the lower court's ruling stands, and Alabama would remain barred from using its 2023 map, continuing under the court-ordered map instead. If the Court reverses, the state's 2023 map could go into effect, and the finding of intentional discrimination would not stand. If the Court vacates, the case would return to the lower court for further review without a final decision favoring either side.
