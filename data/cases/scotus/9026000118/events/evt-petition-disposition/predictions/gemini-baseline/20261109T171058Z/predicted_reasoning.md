## 3. Summary Disposition vs. Plenary Review (`summary-disposition-route`: 0.625; `predicted_disposition`: `gvr`)

Conditional on a grant-family disposition (`granted = 1`, `P = 0.80`), a **summary disposition in the order itself** (`P = 0.625` conditional on grant) is likelier than noting probable jurisdiction for plenary merits briefing and oral argument (`P = 0.375` conditional on grant):

- **Primary route — Vacatur and Remand (`gvr`, ~0.44 unconditional / ~0.55 conditional on grant):** The May 26, 2026 order under appeal (`App.1a–113a`) is an interlocutory preliminary injunction that by its express decretal terms (`App.111a–112a`) enjoined use of Alabama's 2023 Congressional Plan **solely for the 2026 general election** and denied preliminary relief for later elections. Because this Court stayed that preliminary injunction on June 2, 2026 (*Allen v. Milligan*, 146 S. Ct. 1377), Alabama conducted its August 11, 2026 special primary, September 8, 2026 runoff, and November 3, 2026 general election under the enacted 2023 Plan. Meanwhile, proceedings on permanent relief for the 2028 and 2030 elections remain pending—and stayed—before the three-judge district court (`Milligan Doc. 544`, July 20, 2026). Now that the 2026 general election has occurred, the interlocutory dispute over which map governs the 2026 election is moot. Following *United States v. Munsingwear, Inc.*, 340 U.S. 36 (1950), the Court is most likely to vacate the May 26, 2026 preliminary injunction order and remand for further proceedings on final relief under *Louisiana v. Callais* (matching the `gvr` disposition entered in `25-273` *Allen v. Singleton* and `25-274` *Allen v. Milligan* on May 11, 2026).
- **Secondary summary route — Per Curiam Summary Reversal (`summary-reversal`, ~0.06 unconditional / ~0.075 conditional on grant):** Alternatively, as Appellants urge (`JS at 12, 36`) and as Justice Thomas (joined by Justice Gorsuch) previewed in concurring in the June 2, 2026 stay (`146 S. Ct. 1377`), the Court could summarily reverse or vacate the preliminary injunction on the merits for failing to apply *Callais*'s requirement that plaintiffs disentangle race from partisan affiliation.
- **Plenary Review Route (`granted` — Probable Jurisdiction Noted, ~0.30 unconditional / ~0.375 conditional on grant):** If the Court invokes the "capable of repetition, yet evading review" exception to mootness because the 2023 Plan's validity for the 2028 and 2030 elections rests on the already-completed 2025 trial record and the district court stayed further proceedings awaiting this Court's guidance (`Milligan Doc. 544`), the Court will note probable jurisdiction in Nos. 26-117 and 26-118 (and grant certiorari before judgment in *Caster*, No. 26-116), consolidating the three cases for oral argument.

## 4. Questions Presented (If Plenary Review Is Granted)

If the Court notes probable jurisdiction instead of entering a vacatur-and-remand order, it is expected to take **Questions Presented 1, 2, and 3** together—focusing on what *Louisiana v.
