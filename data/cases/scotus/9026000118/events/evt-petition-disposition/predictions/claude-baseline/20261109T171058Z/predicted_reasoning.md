# Forecast — Allen v. Singleton, No. 26-118 (direct appeal, 28 U.S.C. §1253)

**State the docket stands in:** a jurisdictional statement, not a cert petition. Distributed three times (conferences of 10/16, 11/6 and now 11/13/2026), so it has already been relisted twice. No CVSG. Companion filings: Milligan (26-117) and Caster (26-116, cert before judgment), filed the same day on the same order.

**Disposition.** The Court is obliged to act on the merits of an appeal. I expect it to rule for Alabama in some form (about 82%), most likely by **summarily reversing** the May 26, 2026 preliminary injunction in a per curiam opinion. That would follow its June 2, 2026 stay opinion (Allen v. Milligan, 146 S. Ct. 1377), which already said the panel departed from Callais and failed to presume the Legislature's good faith, and its April 2026 summary reversal in Abbott v. LULAC. The next most likely grant-family route is **vacatur and remand**, either under Munsingwear because the injunction expired with the November 3, 2026 election (Alabama asked for this in the alternative) or for reconsideration in light of the stay opinion. Noting probable jurisdiction for plenary review is less likely, because the State itself says the record needs no more briefing. The main non-grant risk is a **dismissal as moot**, which the Singleton appellees requested on the authority of Christian Civic League (2006). A summary affirmance is very unlikely after the stay.

**Route.** If the Court rules for Alabama, I put about 78% on it doing so in the order itself (summary reversal, or vacatur and remand) rather than setting the case for argument.

**Further relists.** More likely than not (about 55%) the case is distributed again after the 11/13 conference. A per curiam with dissents takes time to circulate, and the three Alabama dockets will move together.

**CVSG.** No. The United States has participated as amicus, and the Court does not call for the Solicitor General's views on mandatory appeals in this posture.

**Separate writings.** A summary reversal would very likely draw a dissent from Justice Jackson and/or Justice Sotomayor, possibly joined or written by Justice Kagan. Justice Thomas might write separately about §2's scope. If the Court instead dismisses as moot, a statement or dissent from a Justice in the majority of the stay (Alito, Thomas or Gorsuch) arguing for vacatur or reversal is about as likely as not.

**If plenary review is noted,** the Court would take QP 1 (the alternative map must match the State's districting criteria) and QP 4 (whether intentional discrimination can be found in declining to draw a race-based plan) as written.
