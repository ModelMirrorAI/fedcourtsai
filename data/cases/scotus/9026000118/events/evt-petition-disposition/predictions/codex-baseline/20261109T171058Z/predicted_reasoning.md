# Forecast of the Court's disposition

The Court will most likely dispose of this appeal by **summary reversal**, addressing the district court's treatment of Alabama's nonracial districting objectives and its finding of intentional discrimination without a new round of plenary argument. A vacatur and remand is the principal alternative, including vacatur if the Court finds that election-related relief has become moot. Mere dismissal without vacating the challenged order is less likely.

The baseline already records **three distributions**, for October 16, November 6, and November 13, 2026. I expect **one or two additional redistributions** before a disposition, most likely during November or December 2026; the chance of at least one is 0.68. A resolution immediately after the November 13 conference remains a substantial alternative. The Court will not seek a new Solicitor General recommendation; the probability of a new CVSG is 0.01.

If the Court takes the substantive issues, it will concentrate on **Question 1**, concerning an alternative map that satisfies legitimate state criteria, and **Question 4**, concerning the intentional-discrimination theory. It will likely connect them through the Court's existing requirements for separating racial discrimination from lawful political choices. Question 2 may provide an additional ground. A freestanding new resolution of Question 3 is less likely than application of the already-announced framework. If mootness governs, no question presented needs a fresh merits answer.

Conditional on a grant-family outcome, the probability of a **disposition in the order itself is 0.95**. Summary reversal is more likely than a remand merely inviting another application of Callais, because the Court has already identified errors in this injunction. A mootness-based vacatur remains a substantial competing order-stage route. These are different predicted routes, even though both count within this event's grant family.

A summary reversal will likely draw a dissent, most plausibly authored by **Justice Sotomayor** and joined by Justices Kagan and Jackson. Their joining her opinion would not constitute separate authorship. If the Court instead denies review or uses an equivalent ungranted denial disposition, no separate writing is the more likely result: the conditional probability of a dissent or statement respecting denial is **0.18**, with Thomas the most plausible individual author on that branch. A jurisdictional or mootness resolution may produce a different alignment from a substantive reversal.
