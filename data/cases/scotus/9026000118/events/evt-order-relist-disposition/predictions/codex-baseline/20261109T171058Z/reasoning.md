My large upward adjustment is case-specific. ## Opposition, timing, and route

I retrieved the already-filed September 18 motion to dismiss and September 29 response from the exact official PDF links in the snapshot, without retrieving a current docket or subsequent disposition. These briefs were not provisioned as extracted text. The motion, questions presented and argument I, says the preliminary relief concerns the November 3, 2026 election and will be moot afterward; argument II contests the State's description of race-neutral alternative maps. This materially reduces the chance of plenary review and prevents treating the jurisdictional statement as a balanced record.

Alabama's response, argument I, disputes mootness: it identifies remaining canvassing and certification events, reads the injunction as persisting until new legislation, and invokes continuing consequences and recurring disputes. Importantly, footnote 2 expressly requests Munsingwear vacatur if mootness is found. November 3 is already past at this prediction, but the response itself explains why election day is not automatically the end of the appeal. I therefore assign substantial probability to both summary reversal and mootness vacatur, rather than assume dismissal follows mechanically from the calendar. My route decomposition is approximately 0.40 GVR, 0.30 summary reversal, 0.06 plenary review, and 0.24 ungranted, mainly dismissal. The conditional summary-route forecast of 0.92 is rounded judgment consistent with that allocation.

The matched-question file lists 26-116 and 26-117 as pending, with similarity 1.0. The jurisdictional statement independently explains the near-identical Milligan and Caster filings. I use that explanation for coordination risk, not the text similarity as proof of another docket's present status; I did not inspect either companion's docket or disposition.
