# Forecast of the Court's action

The Court will most likely vacate the preliminary-injunction judgment and remand, with election-related mootness providing the leading route. This is a GVR under the contract, not a plenary decision endorsing Alabama's entire position. Summary reversal based on the standards discussed in the earlier stay is the principal alternative. Any grant-family action has probability 0.76; conditional on such action, disposition in the order itself rather than new briefing and argument has probability 0.92.

Three distributions are already recorded: for October 16, November 6, and November 13, 2026. I expect one further distribution beyond those three before disposition; the probability of at least one additional distribution is 0.58. Two or more additional distributions are possible but are not the central forecast. The Court will not call for the Solicitor General's views; I assign a new invitation probability of 0.01.

The election's passage will make the continued legal effect of the preliminary injunction the immediate issue. If the Court accepts mootness, it will most likely address whether to erase the preliminary judgment rather than decide all four redistricting questions. If it reaches the merits summarily, the likely grounds are the treatment of nonracial districting criteria and the inference of unconstitutional intent. A plenary grant, if chosen instead, would most naturally concentrate on questions 1 and 4 together: what alternative-map showing is required, and whether the State's refusal to adopt another race-conscious plan supports intentional-discrimination liability. I do not forecast that all four questions receive independent plenary treatment.

If review is denied, the likeliest order is silent; a dissent or statement respecting denial has conditional probability 0.24. Thomas or Alito would be plausible writers if the Court declines to correct the lower court, but neither is a central prediction. A summary grant could instead draw a separate writing from Kagan, Sotomayor, or Jackson. No merits majority author is forecast.
