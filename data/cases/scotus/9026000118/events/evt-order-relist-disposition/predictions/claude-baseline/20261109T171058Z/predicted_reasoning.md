# Forecast: Allen v. Singleton, No. 26-118

**State of the docket.** The docket shows three distributions (conferences of 10/16, 11/6 and now 11/13), no CVSG and no disposition. The Nov 3, 2026 election that the preliminary injunction governed has now passed.

**Disposition.** The Court will most likely **vacate the district court's May 26, 2026 preliminary-injunction order and remand with instructions to dismiss as moot (United States v. Munsingwear)**, together with companion appeal No. 26-117 (Milligan) and probably a GVR-style vacatur of the cert-before-judgment petition in No. 26-116 (Caster). A plain dismissal of the appeal as moot without vacatur, which the appellees ask for, is the main alternative. Summary reversal and noting probable jurisdiction are live but less likely now that the injunction has expired by its own terms.

**Further relists.** This is close to even. The Court may dispose of the case from the 11/13 conference. A separate writing objecting to vacatur, or a decision to set the case for argument, could keep it on the list for one or two more conferences.

**CVSG.** No CVSG. The United States has participated as amicus throughout, and CVSGs on mandatory appeals are rare.

**Question taken, if plenary.** If the Court notes probable jurisdiction instead, it would most likely take QP 4 (intentional discrimination / the presumption of legislative good faith) together with QP 1 (the *Callais* alternative-map requirement). It would order briefing on mootness and the capable-of-repetition question.

**Summary route.** If the Court grants relief, it most likely does so in the order itself (vacatur), not through plenary review.

**Separate writings.** If the Court vacates, expect Justice Jackson, possibly joined by Justices Sotomayor and Kagan, to dissent or write respecting the vacatur, arguing that dismissal without vacatur is appropriate. Justice Thomas might write that the Court should reach and reverse. If the Court instead affirms or denies outright, a dissent from Justices Thomas, Alito or Gorsuch is about even odds.
