## In brief

A federal prisoner asks the Court to decide whether credits he earned for prison programs can shorten his supervised release term.

## What happened

Raul Rivera-Perez earned more than 700 days of credit under the First Step Act for completing prison programs. The Bureau of Prisons applied 365 days to move up his transfer to prerelease custody, but treated the rest as unusable. He filed a habeas petition arguing the remaining credits should shorten his term of supervised release, the period of monitored freedom that follows imprisonment. A district court agreed with him. A divided Second Circuit panel reversed, holding the statute lets credits only accelerate the start of prerelease custody or supervised release, not shorten the release term itself, and sent the case back to be dismissed as moot. The Sixth Circuit has reached the same conclusion; the Ninth Circuit has held the opposite.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked to resolve what a specific provision of the First Step Act means when it says earned time credits "shall be applied toward" time in prerelease custody or supervised release. One reading says the credits only move up the date release begins; the other says they can shorten how long the release period lasts.

## Where it stands

The petition was distributed for the Court's November 6, 2026 conference. The government waived its right to respond, but the Court then asked for a response, due December 14, 2026, after an extension. An amicus brief supporting the petition has been filed. No decision on whether to grant or deny review has been recorded.

## What each outcome would mean

Rivera-Perez is asking the Court to take up his case and reverse the Second Circuit. If the Court grants review, it will decide the question itself, which would not by itself decide who wins. If it denies review, the Second Circuit's ruling stands, meaning Rivera-Perez's unused credits cannot reduce his supervised release term, and the same rule would continue to apply in the Second and Sixth Circuits while the Ninth Circuit's contrary rule continues to apply there.
