Caryn Flowers, Warden*, No. 26-396, has been distributed once so far (on November 4, 2026, for the Conference of November 20, 2026) following the Solicitor General's October 21, 2026 waiver of the United States' response, with no Call for a Response (CFR) issued prior to distribution.

Because the Solicitor General waived a response and the Court would call for a response before considering a grant or relist sequence, a second distribution is unlikely.
- **Call for the Views of the Solicitor General (`cvsg-increment` = 0.001):** No CVSG will issue because the respondent is a federal prison warden represented by the Solicitor General, who is already a party to the case.
- **Question Presented (if granted):** If the Court were to call for a response and grant review, it would take the single question presented as written in the petition: whether time credits earned under the First Step Act of 2018, 18 U.S.C. § 3632(d)(4)(C), can be applied to reduce a term of supervised release. If granted on the merits, the Court would likely affirm the Second Circuit's judgment holding that § 3632(d)(4)(C), read together with 18 U.S.C. § 3624(g)(3) and *United States v. Johnson*, 529 U.S. 53 (2000), permits earned time credits to advance the transfer date into supervised release (by up to 12 months) rather than shortening the duration of supervised release itself.
- **Summary Disposition Route (`summary-disposition-route` = 0.15 conditional on a grant):** Conditional on a grant, plenary review is far likelier than a summary disposition (GVR or summary reversal). Every court of appeals to decide the issue (eight circuits) has agreed with the Second Circuit's judgment, making a summary reversal highly improbable, and *Maxwell v. Dinis*, No. 25-5930 (argued/pending in OT2026), addresses mandatory transfer into prerelease custody rather than reducing supervised release after release from imprisonment, making a hold-and-GVR secondary to plenary consideration.
- **Dissent from Denial (`dissent-from-denial` = 0.02):** No separate dissent or statement respecting the denial of certiorari is expected. When the Court denied certiorari on the identical question in *Hargrove v. Healy*, No. 25-818, on April 20, 2026, no Justice noted a dissent or issued a statement. In the unlikely event a separate writing appears, Justice Sotomayor, Justice Jackson, or Justice Gorsuch would be the most plausible authors given the panel dissent by Judge Calabresi emphasizing the statutory text of § 3632(d)(4)(C) and the rehabilitative incentives of the First Step Act.
