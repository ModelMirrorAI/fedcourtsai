# Forecast of the Court's action

The Court will most likely **deny certiorari**, without an explanatory opinion or separate writing, after obtaining the government's requested response. Any grant has a 42% probability, so the denial forecast is not a prediction that this is a routine or insubstantial petition.

The supplied docket contains **one distribution**, for the November 6, 2026 conference. The October 13 response request and the extension through December 14 make **at least one further distribution** the expected next conference step. I expect that redistribution after the response/reply cycle, most naturally in early 2027 if the current deadline holds, rather than an immediate disposition on the existing papers. In the modal denial path there will be one additional distribution and no further true relist. If the Court instead moves toward a grant, one or two additional consideration-related distributions are plausible. These are forecasts of future entries, not a claim that the November conference already produced a substantive relist.

The Court will **not call for the Solicitor General's views separately**. The government is already the respondent and must provide the requested response; that filing will not itself constitute a CVSG.

If review is granted, the likely question is the petition's single QP substantially as written: whether time credits earned under the First Step Act can be applied under **18 U.S.C. 3632(d)(4)(C) to reduce a term of supervised release**, rather than merely move its starting date forward. The Court would not need to take a broad question about all BOP credit calculations. Whether that issue keeps the habeas case live is its remedial consequence, not a separate prediction that the Court will adopt the petitioner's merits interpretation.

**Conditional on a grant, plenary briefing and argument is the likelier route (88%)**, rather than a summary disposition (12%). A statutory conflict with competing textual and contextual readings is not an obvious summary-reversal case. A later GVR could become appropriate if *Maxwell* materially changes the habeas framework, but the two cases do not present identical statutory questions and no such intervening ruling is currently established in this record.

**Conditional on denial, a dissent or statement respecting denial is unlikely (8%)**. The modal order will identify no individual Justice's cert vote. If there is a writing, Gorsuch, Sotomayor, or Jackson is a more plausible author than the other Justices, but no particular author is predicted as likely.
