# Forecast of the Court's action

The Court will most likely deny the petition after receiving the requested federal response. The probability of any grant is 36%; denial is the point prediction, not a prediction that the statutory issue lacks importance.

The baseline docket contains exactly one distribution, entered October 7, 2026, and no CVSG. The petition will be distributed again after the response is received or its deadline passes. The modal path is one further distribution followed by denial without a separate writing. If the Court instead grants plenary review, an additional conference distribution after that is plausible. The 99% increment forecast concerns the count rising above one, not a claim that an actual conference has already relisted this petition.

The Court will not separately request the Solicitor General's views. The government is already a respondent, and the requested response provides the natural vehicle for its position. The probability of a new CVSG is 0.2%.

If the Court grants review, it will most likely take the single question as presented: whether credits earned under the First Step Act can be applied under 18 U.S.C. 3632(d)(4)(C) to reduce a term of supervised release. The published disagreement between the Ninth Circuit and the Second and Sixth Circuits, rather than an individualized credit calculation, is the reason to hear this case. The mootness ruling supplies the procedural setting because it depends on whether unused credits can still provide relief.

Plenary briefing and argument are more likely than a summary disposition if review is granted: the conditional cert-order route probability is 18%. Maxwell v. Dinis provides a plausible reason to hold the petition while the Court clarifies the habeas route for credit claims, but does not itself answer this statutory question. If a later ruling in that case undermines the procedural foundation here, a grant, vacatur and remand becomes the principal summary alternative. A summary reversal deciding the contested credit question without argument is less likely.

If the Court denies review, it will most likely issue no explanation; the conditional probability of a dissent or statement is 8%. If a Justice does write, Gorsuch, Sotomayor or Jackson is more plausible than the other individual authors, but no specific author is the point prediction. No majority merits opinion is forecast on the denial path.
