## 2. Disposition and Route (`disposition: 0.42`, `summary-disposition-route: 0.05`)

- **Disposition Forecast (`denied`, `P(grant) = 0.42`):** The most likely outcome (58%) is that the Court denies certiorari after reviewing the Solicitor General's Brief in Opposition, though plenary review is a very live possibility (42%). While the petition squarely presents an acknowledged circuit split over 18 U.S.C. § 3632(d)(4)(C) and cures the threshold statutory-eligibility vehicle defect that led the Court to deny review in *Hargrove v. Healy* (No. 25-818), the Second Circuit's decision below aligns with the clear majority of circuits (2d, 6th, and unpublished 4th, 5th, and 11th Circuit decisions) and with the statutory structure tying § 3632(d)(4)(C)'s time-credit application to BOP transfers under 18 U.S.C. § 3624(g)(3). Because the decision below reached the result favored by the statutory text and structure (preventing uncabined, extra-judicial reductions of supervised release terms without § 3583(e) judicial review or § 3624(g) risk-level guardrails), the Court may decline to grant a prisoner's petition merely to affirm the Second Circuit and correct the Ninth Circuit's outlier ruling in *Gonzalez*—or may prefer to await a government petition from the Ninth Circuit or further percolation.
- **Question Presented If Granted:** If the Court grants certiorari, it will take the single Question Presented as written in the petition: whether time credits earned under the First Step Act of 2018 can be applied under 18 U.S.C. § 3632(d)(4)(C) to reduce a term of supervised release.

- **Summary Disposition Route Conditional on Grant (`summary-disposition-route: 0.05`):** Conditional on a grant, plenary briefing and oral argument is overwhelmingly likely (~95%). A summary reversal is virtually impossible given the strength of the Second Circuit majority's textual analysis, and a GVR is unlikely unless the Court's pending OT2026 decision in *Maxwell v.
