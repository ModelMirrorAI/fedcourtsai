## In brief

A former anesthesiologist asks the Court to revive his appeal after the Fourth Circuit dismissed it for lack of jurisdiction over his discrimination and retaliation claims.

## What happened

Timothy Hall sued his former employer, Eastern Panhandle Anesthesia Associates, and several other hospitals, agencies, and officials, alleging disability discrimination, retaliation for whistleblowing, and related claims under federal and state law. The district court dismissed most claims with prejudice, dismissed two defendants without prejudice for improper service, and ordered Hall's claims against his former employer sent to arbitration, staying that part of the case. Hall appealed, but the Fourth Circuit dismissed the appeal for lack of jurisdiction, reasoning that the order was not final because the arbitration stay left part of the case still pending. The court of appeals later denied rehearing.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked whether an appeals court may treat an order sending part of a case to arbitration as blocking an appeal, without first considering that the state court which originally ordered arbitration in an earlier, related lawsuit had later lifted that order and dismissed the case. The petition also asks whether a company can send a worker's discrimination and whistleblower claims to arbitration based on that earlier, since-dismissed state court arbitration order, when the worker argues the arbitration agreement does not clearly cover those kinds of legal claims.

## Where it stands

The petition was filed on October 19, 2026, and the respondents have filed waivers of their right to respond, meaning they do not plan to file opposing briefs unless the Court requests one. The case has not yet been scheduled for the justices to consider at a conference.

## What each outcome would mean

Hall, who lost in the lower courts, is asking the Court to take up his case. If the Court grants the petition, it would hear full arguments and decide whether the appeals court wrongly dismissed his appeal and whether his claims were properly sent to arbitration. If the Court denies the petition, the Fourth Circuit's dismissal stands, leaving Hall's claims against his former employer in arbitration and the rest of his case dismissed, with no appellate review of those rulings.
