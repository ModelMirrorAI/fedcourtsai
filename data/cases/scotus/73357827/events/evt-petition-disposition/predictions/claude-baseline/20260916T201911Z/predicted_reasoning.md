# Predicted reasoning — what the Court will do with No. 25-1288

**Bottom line: the petition will be denied, most likely in the order list following the September 28, 2026 long conference, without a call for a response, without a CVSG, and without any noted dissent or statement.**

## Relists

The docket shows **one distribution** (June 17, 2026, for the Conference of September 28, 2026) and no relist. Respondents waived their response on June 11, and the Court did not call for one before distributing. I expect the petition to be considered once and denied in the order list of October 5, 2026 (or the day after the conference, if the Court issues its miscellaneous order early). A further distribution is unlikely; if one occurs, the likelier cause is a routine reschedule or a call for a response after the long conference rather than a hold for a pending case, because no argued case I can identify presents the HAVA private-enforcement or organizational-standing question this Term.

## Call for the views of the Solicitor General

No CVSG. HAVA is a federal statute and the Department of Justice has its own enforcement authority under 52 U.S.C. § 21111 (and wrote to the Wisconsin Elections Commission in June 2025 about its complaint procedures), so the federal interest is real, but the Court does not invite the Solicitor General on a petition it is not otherwise inclined to take, and the standing ground below makes the federal-enforcement question unreachable in this vehicle.

## Which question the Court would take, if it took one

If the Court granted, it would not take QP 1 as written (whether HAVA "creates federal rights enforceable by private litigants through 42 U.S.C. § 1983, or otherwise"); the Seventh Circuit did not rest its judgment on that ground, and after *Medina v. Planned Parenthood South Atlantic* (2025) the § 1983 question is largely settled against petitioners. The only question the judgment below actually presents is the Article III one, and the Court would reformulate it as something like: *whether a complainant's total deprivation of a statutorily mandated state administrative adjudication is a concrete injury under TransUnion, and whether an advocacy organization whose "core activities" include filing such complaints has organizational standing under Alliance for Hippocratic Medicine*. Even so framed, the record is a summary-judgment standing record the panel found thin, which is why I expect no grant.

## Summary disposition versus plenary review

Conditional on a grant, plenary review is the likelier route: no intervening decision of this Court bears on the standing holding, so there is nothing to GVR in light of. A GVR would become plausible only if the Court were to decide an organizational-standing or intangible-injury case during OT2026 and hold this petition for it, which would show up first as repeated relists; the current docket shows none. The `summary-disposition-route` figure (0.25) reflects that the grant family as a whole is GVR-heavy while this case lacks an obvious GVR trigger.

## Dissent from or statement respecting denial

None expected. The standing questions (post-*TransUnion* intangible injury, post-*Hippocratic Medicine* organizational standing) are ones some Justices have signaled interest in, and Chief Judge Brennan's concurrence invites further guidance, but this is a poor vehicle: the panel was unanimous, the holding is fact-bound, the petitioners changed standing theories mid-litigation, and the Court denied certiorari without noted dissent in the *Public Interest Legal Foundation* election-records standing cases earlier in 2026. If any separate writing appears, the most plausible form is a short statement respecting denial noting that the scope of *Hippocratic Medicine*'s "core business activities" test remains open, and I put that at roughly one in twenty-five.

## If granted (conditional only)

If the Court granted on the reformulated standing question, the likelier outcome is affirmance: the Court's recent standing decisions (*TransUnion*, *Hippocratic Medicine*, and the *Public Interest Legal Foundation* cert denials) point toward treating a state agency's non-decision on an administrative complaint as a procedural violation without a concrete downstream harm on this record. A reversal would have to rest on a *Havens Realty*-style theory that the Commission's refusal directly interfered with the organization's actual complaint-filing operations, which the panel found unsupported at summary judgment.
