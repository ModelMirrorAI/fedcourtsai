## In brief

The Court declined to hear a voter-advocacy group's bid to revive its lawsuit over Wisconsin election officials' refusal to decide complaints filed against themselves.

## What happened

The Wisconsin Voter Alliance and two of its members filed complaints with the Wisconsin Elections Commission alleging the commission itself was violating a federal elections law, but the commission returned both complaints without deciding them. The petitioners sued, arguing this denied them a federally required administrative process. A federal district court dismissed the case, finding the petitioners lacked the kind of concrete injury needed to sue in federal court. The Seventh Circuit Court of Appeals affirmed that dismissal, agreeing the alleged harm was too abstract to support a lawsuit.

## What the Court is being asked

The case asks whether this federal elections law gives private citizens and organizations a right to sue when a state fails to provide the complaint procedures the law requires, and separately, whether being completely denied such a procedure counts as a real, concrete harm that courts can address, or only a technical violation that courts cannot remedy.

## Where it stands

The petition was distributed for conference, and the petitioners later filed a supplemental brief. On October 5, 2026, the Court denied the petition.

## What each outcome would mean

The Wisconsin Voter Alliance and its members asked the Court to take up the case. With the petition denied, the Seventh Circuit's ruling stands, meaning the lawsuit remains dismissed and the petitioners cannot pursue their claims against the Wisconsin Elections Commission in federal court over its handling of their complaints. The denial does not mean the Supreme Court agreed with the Seventh Circuit's reasoning; it simply leaves that lower court decision in place without the Supreme Court deciding the underlying legal questions itself.
