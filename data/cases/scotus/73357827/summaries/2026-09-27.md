## In brief

Wisconsin Voter Alliance asks the Court to revive its lawsuit over a state elections agency's refusal to decide its federal voting-law complaints.

## What happened

Wisconsin Voter Alliance and two of its members filed complaints with the Wisconsin Elections Commission, alleging the commission itself was violating the Help America Vote Act, a federal law setting election administration standards. The commission returned both complaints without deciding them, saying it could not rule on complaints against itself. The group sued commission officials, but the district court dismissed the case, finding no concrete injury under Article III, the constitutional requirement that a plaintiff show real harm to sue in federal court. The Seventh Circuit Court of Appeals affirmed, also adopting a narrower standard for when organizations may sue.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked to decide two things: first, whether this federal voting-law statute gives private citizens a right to sue over violations, rather than leaving enforcement solely to the federal government; and second, whether being completely denied a hearing and decision that the law requires counts as a real, concrete harm allowing a lawsuit, or whether it is only a technical violation that courts cannot review.

## Where it stands

The petition was distributed for the Court's conference on September 28, 2026, after the respondents waived their right to respond and an amicus brief and a supplemental brief were filed. The docket does not yet show any action taken at or after that conference.

## What each outcome would mean

Wisconsin Voter Alliance, the petitioner, is asking the Court to take up the case. If the Court grants review, it would hear the case fully and decide whether the statute allows private lawsuits and whether the alleged harm is concrete enough for federal court, without yet deciding who wins. If the Court denies review, the Seventh Circuit's ruling stands, leaving the case dismissed and the commission's officials, the respondents, prevailing.
