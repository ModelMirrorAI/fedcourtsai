P(grant) is estimated at 0.08. The prior-Term salience band ("baseline") for a distributed paid petition carries a low historical grant rate (around 4-6% based on the statpack risk set). I adjust this up slightly because the petition identifies a real circuit split regarding the interpretation of Fed. R. Civ. P. 54(d)(1) and whether a district court can consider the "chilling effect" on civil rights plaintiffs when taxing costs to a prevailing defendant. The Ninth Circuit explicitly permits this consideration, while the Eighth (in the decision below) and Sixth Circuits reject it. 

However, I discount the probability of a grant because the vehicle is messy. The underlying case involved over 700 experts, leading to an enormous $366,461.96 cost award against the plaintiffs (who are civilly committed sex offenders). The fact-bound nature of the case, combined with the deferential abuse of discretion standard typically applied to cost awards, makes it a less-than-ideal candidate for Supreme Court review. Furthermore, the second QP asks the Court to review alleged appellate fact-finding, which the Court rarely takes up.

The respondent waived their right to respond, indicating they do not view the petition as a serious threat, though the Court could always request a response if interested. Given these factors, I predict a denial, but assign a 15% probability of a dissent from denial due to the high stakes for the indigent plaintiffs.
