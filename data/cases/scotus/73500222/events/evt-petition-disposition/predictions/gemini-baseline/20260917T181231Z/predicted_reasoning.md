Distributed once so far; expect the petition to be denied without further relists. While the petition points to a genuine circuit split regarding whether a district court may consider the "chilling effect" on future civil rights litigation when awarding costs under Rule 54(d)(1) (with the Ninth Circuit permitting it and the Sixth and Eighth rejecting it), the underlying issue of cost allocation is typically reviewed for abuse of discretion. Furthermore, the staggering $366,461.96 cost award here was driven by the plaintiffs' use of over 700 experts in a massive institutional reform case, making it an idiosyncratic vehicle rather than a clean test of the legal rule.

No CVSG is expected, as the federal government's interests are not directly implicated by this dispute between state officials and civil rights plaintiffs. If granted, the Court would likely focus on the first QP regarding the proper interpretation of Rule 54(d)(1) and ignore the second, fact-specific QP about appellate fact-finding. A summary disposition is unlikely given the need to resolve the circuit split on the legal standard. Should the Court deny review, there is a reasonable chance of a dissent from denial by one of the liberal Justices, given the sheer size of the cost award against civilly committed plaintiffs and the resulting chilling effect on civil rights litigation.
