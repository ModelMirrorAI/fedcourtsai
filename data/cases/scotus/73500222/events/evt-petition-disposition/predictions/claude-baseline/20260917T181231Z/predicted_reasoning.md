# Forecast: what the Court will do with No. 25-1321, Karsjens v. Gandhi

**Disposition.** The petition will be denied, most likely in the order list
following the September 28, 2026 long conference or the one after it.

**Relists.** The docket shows exactly one distribution (July 1, 2026, for the
September 28 conference) and no relist. I expect no further distribution: the
petition will be considered once and denied. The main route to a second
distribution is a call for a response, since Minnesota waived its right to
respond and the Court does not grant without hearing from the respondent; a
CFR here is possible but unlikely, and a reschedule off the long conference is
the residual. Roughly a one-in-seven chance of at least one more distribution.

**CVSG.** No call for the views of the Solicitor General. The questions are
about Rule 54(d)(1) costs in a section 1983 suit against state officials; no
federal program, statute, or federal party's interest is implicated.

**Which question, if taken.** If the Court took anything it would be Question 1
as written, framed as whether a district court may weigh the chilling effect
on civil-rights plaintiffs when denying costs to a prevailing defendant, with
the Ninth Circuit's Stanley line on one side and the Sixth and Eighth Circuits
on the other. Question 2 (appellate factfinding on ability to pay) is
case-specific error correction and would not be granted on its own; the
Excessive Fines argument attached to it was not decided below and would be
left behind.

**Summary disposition.** No intervening decision bears on either question, so
a GVR is not in play. Conditional on any grant, I put a meaningful but minority
weight (about 0.3) on a summary route, because the more plausible grant story
is a short per curiam correcting the Eighth Circuit's own finding on ability to
pay rather than plenary review of a discretionary costs factor. Plenary review
remains the likelier form of any grant.

**Separate writing on denial.** I expect a bare denial with no statement or
dissent. The petitioners' circumstances (indigent, indefinitely civilly
committed, facing a joint-and-several $366,461.96 cost judgment) are the kind
that occasionally draw a statement respecting denial, but the underlying legal
question is discretionary cost allocation, and no Justice has staked out a
position on it.

**If granted (conditional).** The likely holding would be that Rule 54(d)(1)'s
presumption in favor of costs leaves district courts discretion to weigh a
litigant's indigence, but that the "chilling effect" on future civil-rights
plaintiffs is not by itself a legitimate ground to deny costs to a prevailing
defendant, affirming the Eighth Circuit's rule while possibly remanding on the
factfinding point.
