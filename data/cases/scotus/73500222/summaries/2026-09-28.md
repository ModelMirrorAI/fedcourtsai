## In brief

Civilly committed sex offenders ask the Court to review an appeals court order making them pay over $366,000 in litigation costs to state officials they sued and lost against.

## What happened

Kevin Scott Karsjens and other patients civilly committed at the Minnesota Sex Offender Program sued state officials in 2011 over the program's constitutionality and conditions. After years of litigation, the officials prevailed on all claims. The officials then sought to recover costs, including fees for court-appointed experts. The district court denied costs entirely, citing the patients' indigence, their good faith, the case's public importance, and the chilling effect a cost award could have on future civil rights litigants. The Eighth Circuit reversed, holding the district court failed to weigh a 2013 proposal by the patients' counsel to split expert costs, and it ordered costs of $366,461.96 awarded to the officials. The Eighth Circuit denied rehearing.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked whether a court awarding litigation costs may take into account the possibility that a large cost award would discourage future civil rights lawsuits, and whether an appeals court may resolve disputed factual questions itself to justify a costs order rather than sending the case back to the trial court to sort out those facts.

## Where it stands

The petition was distributed for the Court's conference on September 28, 2026, after the respondents waived their right to file a response. The docket does not yet show any action taken at that conference.

## What each outcome would mean

Karsjens and the other patients, as petitioners, are asking the Court to take up their case. If the Court grants the petition, it would hear full arguments and decide the costs questions itself, without yet deciding who wins. If the Court denies the petition, the Eighth Circuit's ruling stands, meaning the patients would remain liable for the $366,461.96 in costs awarded to the state officials.
