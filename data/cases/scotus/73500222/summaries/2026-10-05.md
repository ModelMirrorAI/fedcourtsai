## In brief

Civilly committed patients asked the Supreme Court to review a lower court's order requiring them to pay costs to the state officials they sued; the Court denied review.

## What happened

Kevin Scott Karsjens and other patients civilly committed at the Minnesota Sex Offender Program sued state officials in 2011 over conditions of their confinement. After more than a decade of litigation, the officials prevailed on all claims. The officials then sought to recover costs, including fees for court-appointed experts. The district court denied the officials any costs, citing the patients' inability to pay and the potential chilling effect on future civil rights litigants. The Eighth Circuit reversed, holding the district court had ignored the patients' earlier offer to split expert costs, and ordered the patients to pay $366,461.96. The Eighth Circuit denied rehearing.

## What the Court is being asked

The patients raised two questions: first, whether a court deciding how to divide litigation costs may reduce or excuse what a losing civil rights plaintiff owes because of the risk that charging costs would discourage future civil rights lawsuits. Second, whether an appeals court may resolve disputed factual questions itself to justify a costs order, rather than sending the case back to the trial court to sort out those facts.

## Where it stands

The officials waived their right to respond to the petition. The petition was distributed for the Court's September 28, 2026 conference, and the Court denied it on October 5, 2026.

## What each outcome would mean

The patients, as petitioners, asked the Court to take up their case. By denying the petition, the Court leaves the Eighth Circuit's ruling in place: the patients must pay $366,461.96 in costs to the officials, and the Eighth Circuit's approach to weighing cost awards against civil rights plaintiffs stands as the law in that circuit. The denial does not mean the Court agreed with the Eighth Circuit's reasoning; it simply leaves that decision undisturbed.
