## What happened

Kevin Scott Karsjens and other patients civilly committed under Minnesota's sex offender treatment program sued state officials in 2011 over conditions of their confinement. After more than a decade of litigation, the officials prevailed on all claims. The dispute here concerns who must pay for court-appointed experts used during the case. The district court, citing the patients' poverty and the risk of discouraging future civil rights lawsuits, denied the officials any award of costs. On appeal, the Eighth Circuit disagreed, concluding the patients had shown an ability to pay by once offering to split expert costs, and it sent the case back with instructions to order the patients to pay $366,461.96.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked whether a court deciding how to divide litigation costs may take into account the risk that charging a civil rights plaintiff could discourage future civil rights lawsuits, or whether such plaintiffs must be treated the same as any other litigant. The petition also asks whether an appeals court may resolve disputed factual questions itself to justify a costs order, rather than sending the case back to the trial court to sort out those facts, and whether doing so here unfairly prevented the patients from arguing that the costs charged were an unconstitutionally excessive fine.

## Where it stands

The petition for review was filed in May 2026. The state officials waived their right to respond, and the case was distributed for the Court's conference on September 28, 2026, where the Justices will decide whether to take up the case.
