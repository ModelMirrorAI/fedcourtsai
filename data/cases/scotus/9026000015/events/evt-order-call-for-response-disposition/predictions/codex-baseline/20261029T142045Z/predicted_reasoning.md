The central obstacle is vehicle quality: the petition combines a disputed application of a state religious-freedom statute with federal constitutional questions, and the judgment below also rests on an alternative strict-scrutiny holding.

No CVSG is recorded. The Court will probably decide without inviting the Solicitor General's views: P(a new CVSG) is 4%. The dispute concerns a city's park management and its obligations to particular religious practitioners, rather than the administration of a federal program. If an invitation issues, expect it after the response and a subsequent conference, not merely because the Court requested a response.

If review is granted, the likelier question is a narrowed version of QP 1: whether a court assessing a burden on religious exercise may treat worship elsewhere as an adequate substitute when the claimant sincerely regards this particular site and its natural features as indispensable. The Court would distinguish accepting the claimant's religious premises from deciding the legal consequences of those premises. QP 2, concerning consideration of less restrictive alternatives before litigation, is a less clean lead question, but the alternative strict-scrutiny holding means it cannot simply be ignored in selecting or framing review.

Conditional on a grant, plenary review is more likely than disposition in the cert order: P(summary-disposition route | grant) is 22%. The record supplies no identified intervening decision requiring a GVR, and the alternative holding and factual disputes complicate summary reversal. Conditional on a denial, expect no dissent or statement: P(at least one such notation | denial) is 18%. A separate writing, if there is one, is most plausibly concerned with judicial treatment of sincere religious beliefs and protection of indigenous sacred places. Most likely the Court will issue an unexplained denial rather than a doctrinal opinion.
