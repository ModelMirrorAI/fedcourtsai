# Forecast: Perez v. City of San Antonio, No. 26-15 (evt-petition-disposition)

**Bottom line.** The petition is more likely denied than granted, but this is
a genuine contender: P(any grant) = 0.30. If the Court denies, a noted
dissent or statement respecting denial is close to a coin flip.

## Relists

The docket shows **one distribution** (Jul 29, 2026, for the Sept. 28
conference), immediately overtaken by a **call for a response** on Jul 30
after the City had waived. The petition therefore never reached the Sept. 28
conference. Once the brief in opposition (due Oct. 30, 2026) and the reply
are in, the Clerk will redistribute it, so at least one further
distribution is near-certain (P ≈ 0.95), probably for a conference in
early-to-mid January 2027. Conditional on that first redistribution, I expect
the Court to relist it at least once more before acting: the case has thirteen
amici, a six-judge dissent from denial of rehearing en banc, and a question
at least two Justices have already said they want to answer, so it will not
be disposed of at a first look. Expected total distributions by disposition:
three, with a real tail to four or five if a Justice is writing a dissent
from denial.

## Call for the views of the Solicitor General

Unlikely (P ≈ 0.07). The respondent is a city and the questions are framed
under the Religion Clauses, not RFRA. The federal government's exposure is
indirect (a parallel substantial-burden standard under RFRA and RLUIPA and
its own sacred-site litigation), and the Court does not usually invite the
SG where no federal statute or federal party is in play. If one came, it
would be at the January conference, pushing disposition into OT2026's late
spring or beyond.

## Which question, if granted

Question 1 as written or lightly reformulated: whether a court assessing
substantial burden may reject the claimant's sincere account of what the
exercise requires. That is the question Judge Oldham's en banc dissent and
the Gorsuch dissent from the denial of certiorari in Apache Stronghold both
frame, and it is where the circuit disagreement is cleanest. Question 2 (the
least-restrictive-means burden where the government concedes it never studied
accommodation before suit) would most likely be left behind or folded into
Question 1, because the Fifth Circuit's narrow-tailoring holding is
record-bound and the City disputes the characterization of its admission.

## Summary disposition

Unlikely conditional on a grant (P ≈ 0.08). There is no intervening decision
of this Court to GVR in light of, and the Fifth Circuit's affirmance of a
preliminary-injunction denial on a developed record is not the shape the Court
summarily reverses. A grant here means plenary review.

## If denied: separate writings

P(some dissent from, or statement respecting, denial) ≈ 0.45. Justice Gorsuch,
joined by Justice Thomas, dissented from the denial of certiorari in Apache
Stronghold in May 2025 on an adjacent sacred-site substantial-burden question
and said the issue would recur; this petition is that recurrence, with a
municipal rather than federal defendant. A denial here would plausibly draw a
Gorsuch dissent or a statement that the vehicle, not the question, was the
problem. The alternative, a silent denial, is what the vehicle problems point
toward if the Justices who care about the question want a cleaner case.

## If granted: merits-shaped content, conditional only

If the Court took Question 1, the likely result would be reversal or vacatur
of the Fifth Circuit, holding that once sincerity is found the burden inquiry
takes the claimant's account of the practice as given and asks only whether
the government's action prevents or penalizes it, with Lyng's land-management
carve-out as the contested boundary.
