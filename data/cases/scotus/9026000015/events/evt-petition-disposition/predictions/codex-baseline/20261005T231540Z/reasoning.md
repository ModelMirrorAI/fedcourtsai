## Anchor and adjustment

I used the committed `metrics/statpack.md` sal-v4 table's **bracketed reached baseline** rates, not the terminal-baseline rates. Pooling every displayed strictly prior Term, 2017 through 2025, using the published rounded rates and weighted denominators gives approximately **5.01%**, with weighted n=12,720. The component reached rates and denominators are: 2025 3.9%/1,140; 2024 5.7%/1,271; 2023 5.9%/1,312; 2022 5.8%/1,192; 2021 5.6%/1,500; 2020 4.5%/1,739; 2019 4.6%/1,399; 2018 4.6%/1,524; 2017 4.7%/1,643. This is an approximate reconstruction from rounded published values, not an exact underlying-count estimate. The pack does not provide a build timestamp in the material inspected; I have not refreshed or queried the remote corpus.

The 24% grant estimate is a substantial, judgmental increase from that anchor, not a fitted conditional rate. Three case-specific signals justify it: an affirmative response request after a waiver; twelve amicus entries; and the petition's account of a panel dissent and six judges dissenting from denial of rehearing. QP1 identifies a potentially consequential boundary between legal assessment of burden and judicial substitution of theological judgments. QP2 articulates an asserted multi-circuit conflict about actual consideration of less restrictive alternatives. See the petition at pp. 15–22, 29–37. I treat these signals as overlapping rather than multiply independent likelihood ratios.

I keep denial well above 50% because the vehicle is complicated. This is an appeal from preliminary-injunction proceedings, with intertwined Texas statutory, Texas constitutional, and federal claims. The purported conflicts draw on different legal regimes and factual settings; their square presentation is unverified. Most importantly, the appended panel opinion at pp. 41a–42a says the City considered viable alternatives, relies on deferential review of the factual record, and holds alternatively that the challenged measures survive strict scrutiny. It expressly imports that reasoning into the federal Free Exercise claim while assuming strict scrutiny applies. Accordingly, correcting the substantial-burden analysis alone may not change the judgment. QP2 challenges that second route, but its framing risks turning a factual disagreement about alternatives into a categorical legal question. These obstacles explain why the substantive importance does not translate into a majority probability of review.

The statpack's paid-segment relist and CVSG cuts confirm that terminal trajectories differ sharply: the no-relist bucket has about 1.7% grants including GVRs, while two relists have about 40.9%; the CVSG bucket has about 34.9%. Those are terminal buckets across the pack, not forward hazards or leakage-safe substitutes for the prior-Term anchor. They are used only to understand shape. The **93%** increment instead follows this record's unusually concrete sequencing: distribution preceded the response request, and the extended deadline postdates the originally listed conference. It must not be interpreted as a 93% probability of a substantive relist after completed briefing.

The **4%** CVSG estimate reflects the absence of a federal party or a demonstrated need for the Solicitor General's views. The **25% conditional summary-route** estimate allows error correction but discounts it for the alternative holding and record complexity. The **22% conditional denial-writing** estimate reflects the issue's religious-liberty significance and reported lower-court dissents, without claiming a published baseline for that forecast. These are subjective probabilities; only the disposition has the pooled salience anchor used here.

The **0.66 stakes score** is separate from cert likelihood. The dispute nevertheless remains tied to a particular municipal project and preliminary record. I do not assume that every asserted constitutional issue will be reached.
