## In brief

Two religious practitioners ask the Court to revive their claim that San Antonio's park renovation plan unlawfully burdens their worship at a sacred riverbend.

## What happened

Gary Perez and Matilde Torres, ceremonial leaders of the Lipan Native American Church, worship at a specific bend of the San Antonio River in Brackenridge Park, which they consider sacred. The City of San Antonio plans to remove trees and deter nesting cormorants there as part of a park renovation project. Perez and Torres sued, arguing this would destroy elements necessary to their religious ceremonies. A federal district court granted them limited access to the site but declined to block the tree removal or bird deterrence. The Fifth Circuit, after a Texas Supreme Court ruling on a certified state-law question, affirmed, finding no substantial burden on their religious exercise because they retained access to other parts of the park. Judges dissented from the full appeals court's refusal to rehear the case.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked whether, in deciding if government action burdens someone's religious exercise, courts may second-guess that person's own sincere understanding of what their religious practice requires, rather than accepting their account of it. The Court is also asked whether the government can meet its burden of showing it used the least restrictive means available when it did not consider alternative approaches before the lawsuit began, only afterward.

## Where it stands

The petition was distributed for the Court's conference on September 28, 2026; the docket shows no decision from that conference. The City's deadline to respond has since been extended more than once, most recently to October 30, 2026.

## What each outcome would mean

Perez and Torres, as petitioners, are asking the Court to take up their case. If the Court grants review, it would hear the case in full and decide the legal questions about how courts assess religious burdens and government justifications, without yet deciding who wins. If the Court denies review, the Fifth Circuit's ruling against Perez and Torres stands, leaving the City free to proceed with its tree removal and bird deterrence plans. A denial would not mean the Court agrees with the Fifth Circuit's reasoning, only that the case will not be heard.
