## In brief

Two religious leaders ask the Court to revive their claim that San Antonio's park renovation plans unlawfully burden their Native American religious practices.

## What happened

Gary Perez and Matilde Torres, ceremonial leaders of the Lipan Native American Church, worship at a specific riverbend in Brackenridge Park that they believe is sacred, requiring the presence of certain trees and nesting cormorants. The City of San Antonio's renovation project would remove most trees in the area and deter the birds from nesting there. After a preliminary injunction hearing, a federal district court granted Perez and Torres limited access to the site but declined to block the tree removal or bird deterrence measures. The Fifth Circuit, after certifying a question to the Texas Supreme Court, affirmed, holding their religious exercise was not substantially burdened because they retained access to the rest of the park and the birds could nest elsewhere. The full Fifth Circuit denied rehearing en banc over several judges' dissents.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked two questions. First, whether courts may second-guess a religious believer's own sincere understanding of what their faith requires when deciding if government action burdens that practice, rather than accepting the believer's account. Second, whether the government can meet its burden of showing it used the least restrictive means available when it admits it never considered workable alternatives before the lawsuit began, relying instead on justifications developed during litigation.

## Where it stands

The petition was distributed for the Court's conference of September 28, 2026, after which the Court requested a response from the City, due October 30, 2026 following two extensions. The docket shows no action since that distribution.

## What each outcome would mean

Perez and Torres, as petitioners, are asking the Court to take up their case and reverse the Fifth Circuit's ruling. If the Court grants review, it will hear full arguments and decide the legal questions itself, deciding nothing yet about who wins. If the Court denies review, the Fifth Circuit's decision stands, leaving in place the rulings that the City's tree removal and bird deterrence plans may proceed without a finding that they substantially burden the petitioners' religious exercise.
