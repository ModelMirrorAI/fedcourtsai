# Forecast: what the Court will do with No. 25-1099, Reel v. North Carolina

**Bottom line.** The petition will be **denied**, most likely on the order list following the September 28, 2026 long conference or one relist after it. P(any grant, including GVR or summary reversal) = 0.13.

## Distributions and relists

The docket shows **two distributions**: April 15, 2026 for the May 1 conference, then, after the Court called for a response on April 27 and the State filed its brief in opposition, a redistribution on July 1, 2026 for the September 28, 2026 conference. The first distribution never produced a conference vote on the merits of the petition; the second is the first conference at which the fully briefed petition will actually be considered. So this is not a twice-relisted petition. It is a once-considered petition sitting at the long conference with a response the Court asked for.

I forecast a **38% chance of at least one further distribution** past the two shown. The Court asked for a response over a waiver, which means at least one chambers wanted a closer look, and that is the kind of petition that gets carried over one conference either for a closer look or so that a Justice can decide whether to write. If it is relisted, I expect one relist, possibly two, not a long string; the 55-second video and the short record do not need many conferences to evaluate. A denial with no relist at all is the single most likely path.

## CVSG

**No CVSG.** The federal government is not a party, and although the petition points to federal agents' use of knock-and-talks, the Court almost never invites the Solicitor General into a state criminal suppression case. If the Court wanted the government's view it would grant and let the SG file as amicus. P(CVSG called for after prediction time) = 0.03.

## Which question the Court would take, if any

If the Court granted, it would not take the petition's QP as written. The QP asks whether entering the curtilage "with the purpose of gathering incriminating evidence" is a search, which would collapse every knock-and-talk into a search and cut against Jardines' own footnote 4. A grant would come with a reformulation closer to: whether officers who follow an invited guest onto the porch without knocking, and hold the door as the guest is admitted, exceed the implied license recognized in Florida v. Jardines. That reformulation is exactly the argument petitioner did make below, so it would also cure the preservation problem the State presses.

## Summary disposition versus plenary review

Conditional on a grant, I put the **summary-disposition route at 0.15**. There is no intervening decision to GVR in light of, and Jardines and Collins predate every ruling below, so a GVR would be unusual. A per curiam reversal is conceivable if a majority sees the Court of Appeals as having applied a state "knock-and-talk exception" without doing the Jardines analysis, but the State disputes the factual premise (the officer testified he only meant to talk, the trial court found no pretext), and the Court does not summarily reverse where the record is contested. A grant, if it comes, would be for briefing and argument on a reformulated question.

## Dissent or statement on denial

Conditional on denial, **P(some Justice writes) = 0.25**. Justice Gorsuch's Bovat v. Vermont statement (joined by Justices Sotomayor and Kagan) expressly flagged lower-court drift from Jardines while explaining why that case was denied, and this petition was drafted to answer his "has Jardines eluded other courts?" question. The video of officers holding the storm door and then kicking the door in is the kind of fact pattern that prompts a short statement respecting denial. Against that, the preservation problem gives a Justice an easy reason to stay silent, and most Bovat-adjacent petitions the petition itself lists (Chute, Frederick, Christensen, Morgan, Brienza) were denied without writing.

## If granted, the likely ground (conditional only)

If the Court took the case, the likely holding would be that the officers exceeded the implied license by not approaching the front door to knock and wait themselves but instead standing behind an invited guest and stepping into the doorway as she was admitted, so the marijuana odor was obtained during a search and cannot supply exigency. That would be a narrow, fact-bound extension of Jardines rather than a purpose-based rule.
