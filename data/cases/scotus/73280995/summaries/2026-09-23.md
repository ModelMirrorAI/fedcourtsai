## What happened

Jordan Watkins, a federal inmate, had hernia repair surgery while held at a Chicago detention center. He says medical staff, including Dr. Brij Mohan, ignored his severe post-surgical pain and swelling, gave him ineffective medication, and denied follow-up care. He later needed additional surgery. Watkins sued Mohan and other officials for damages, along with a separate claim against the United States. A district court dismissed his claims, but the Seventh Circuit reversed, holding his case fit within a Supreme Court precedent (Carlson v. Green) allowing such prisoner suits. One judge dissented. The government, representing Mohan, then asked the Supreme Court to review the case.

## What the Court is being asked

The government argues that letting federal prisoners sue individual officials for money damages over inadequate medical care should not extend to cases like this one, because things have changed since the earlier ruling that allowed such suits: prisoners now have an internal grievance process, Congress has passed related legislation, and Watkins's situation—a detainee later convicted, with an ongoing rather than sudden medical problem—differs from the original case. The government suggests the Court might even decide that this kind of prisoner lawsuit is no longer viable at all. Watkins responds that his case is materially the same as the original precedent and that no real disagreement among lower courts justifies review.
