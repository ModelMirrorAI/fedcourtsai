## In brief

The Court heard a Catholic parish's challenge to Colorado's rule requiring preschools in its subsidy program to admit families regardless of sexual orientation or gender identity.

## What happened

Colorado's universal preschool program pays for four-year-olds to attend participating public or private preschools, including religious ones, but requires all participating preschools to give children an equal opportunity to enroll regardless of race, religious affiliation, sexual orientation, gender identity, housing status, income, or disability. St. Mary Catholic Parish and other Catholic schools, along with parents who wanted to use the program, said their faith required them to exclude families who do not support Catholic teaching on marriage and gender, so they did not join the program and received no funding. They sued, arguing the program's equal-opportunity rule violated their religious freedom. A federal district court and the Tenth Circuit Court of Appeals ruled for Colorado, holding the rule was a neutral, generally applicable policy that survived constitutional review.

## What the Court is being asked

The case asks whether a public benefits program's nondiscrimination rule must survive the toughest level of judicial review whenever it burdens religious conduct, even though the rule applies the same way to religious and nonreligious providers alike. It also asks how courts should decide whether such a rule truly applies evenhandedly, given that Colorado allows some exceptions, such as preferences for children with disabilities or from low-income families. A third question asks whether the Court should abandon the current legal standard, from a 1990 case, that generally lets neutral, generally applicable laws stand even if they incidentally burden religious practice.

## Where it stands

The Court granted review limited to two of the three questions presented, and the case was briefed, argued on November 3, 2026, and now awaits decision.

## What each outcome would mean

St. Mary Catholic Parish and the other schools and families, as petitioners, are asking the Court to overturn the Tenth Circuit's ruling in Colorado's favor. If the Court reverses, the program's equal-opportunity rule would likely need to survive stricter review, and the schools could gain a path to receive public preschool funding while maintaining their enrollment practices. If the Court affirms, Colorado's rule stays in place, and the schools would remain unable to join the program unless they comply with its equal-access terms.
