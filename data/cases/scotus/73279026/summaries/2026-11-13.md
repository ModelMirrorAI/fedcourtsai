## In brief

The Supreme Court heard a Catholic parish's challenge to Colorado's requirement that preschools receiving universal preschool funding admit families regardless of sexual orientation, gender identity, or religious affiliation.

## What happened

St. Mary Catholic Parish and other Catholic institutions sued Colorado officials, arguing that the state's universal preschool program unconstitutionally excluded them because their religious practice requires considering a family's views on marriage, sex, and gender before enrollment. Colorado's program funds preschool at providers of a family's choosing, including religious ones, but requires all participating preschools to offer equal enrollment opportunity regardless of several protected characteristics. After a bench trial, a federal district court ruled mostly for Colorado, and the Tenth Circuit Court of Appeals affirmed, holding that Colorado's rule was neutral and generally applicable and that precedent barring religious exclusion from public benefits did not apply because Colorado had not explicitly targeted religion.

## What the Court is being asked

The case asks the Court to decide what test determines whether a government rule that burdens religious practice is applied evenhandedly enough to avoid the toughest constitutional scrutiny, including whether a rule with some exceptions for other groups must also exempt comparable religious conduct. It also asks whether rules barring the government from denying public benefits based on a recipient's religious exercise apply only when a law explicitly singles out religion, or also when the exclusion arises from a seemingly neutral rule. A further question asks whether the Court should abandon its longstanding framework for evaluating such religious-liberty claims altogether.

## Where it stands

The Court granted review limited to two of the three questions raised, heard oral argument on November 3, 2026, and the case now awaits decision.

## What each outcome would mean

St. Mary Catholic Parish and the other petitioners are asking the Court to reverse the Tenth Circuit's ruling in Colorado's favor. If the Court sides with the petitioners, it would send the case back with instructions requiring Colorado to justify its exclusion of the Catholic preschools under a demanding legal standard, which could ultimately let them participate in the funding program while maintaining their enrollment practices. If the Court instead affirms, Colorado's rule would stand, and the preschools would remain unable to receive state preschool funding unless they comply with the equal-enrollment requirement.
