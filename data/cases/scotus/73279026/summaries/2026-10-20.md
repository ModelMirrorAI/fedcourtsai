## In brief

The Supreme Court heard a Catholic parish's challenge to Colorado's exclusion of its preschools from a state preschool program over religious objections to enrollment rules.

## What happened

St. Mary Catholic Parish and other Catholic institutions sued Colorado officials after being excluded from the state's Universal Preschool Program (UPK), which funds free preschool at participating public and private schools. UPK requires participating preschools to give families equal opportunity to enroll regardless of race, religious affiliation, sexual orientation, gender identity, and other protected characteristics. The parishes' preschools decline to enroll children of same-sex parents or transgender children, conflicting with this requirement. After a bench trial, the district court ruled against the parishes on most claims, and the Tenth Circuit affirmed, holding Colorado's rule was neutral and generally applicable under existing Supreme Court precedent.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked to decide what standard applies when a religiously motivated party claims a government rule unfairly burdens its practice while allowing comparable secular exceptions — specifically, whether courts may find a rule still evenhanded unless the challenger shows unlimited government discretion or an identical secular exception. The Court is also asked whether a separate rule against denying public benefits because of a recipient's religious conduct applies only when the government excludes religious applicants in explicitly religious terms, or more broadly whenever religious practice is the actual reason for exclusion.

## Where it stands

The petition was granted limited to two of the three questions presented. The case was fully briefed, argued on November 3, 2026, and now awaits the Court's decision.

## What each outcome would mean

St. Mary Catholic Parish and the other petitioners sought the Court's review after losing below. If the Court rules for the parishes, it could require Colorado to accommodate their religious enrollment practices or send the case back for further proceedings under a different legal standard, allowing them to access UPK funding. If the Court rules for Colorado, the Tenth Circuit's decision stands, and Colorado may continue excluding the parishes' preschools from UPK funding unless they comply with the equal-opportunity requirement. A ruling vacating and remanding would leave the dispute unresolved pending further lower-court proceedings.
