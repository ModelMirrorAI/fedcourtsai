## In brief

The Supreme Court heard a Catholic parish's challenge to Colorado rules excluding religious preschools from a state funding program over their enrollment policies.

## What happened

Colorado's universal preschool program ("UPK") pays for four-year-olds to attend participating preschools, public or private, including religious ones. Participating preschools must give families "equal opportunity" to enroll regardless of race, religious affiliation, sexual orientation, gender identity, income, or disability. St. Mary Catholic Parish, another parish, the Archdiocese of Denver, and two parents sued, arguing this requirement unconstitutionally excluded Catholic preschools that limit enrollment based on families' support for Catholic teaching on sex and marriage. After a bench trial, a federal district court ruled against them, and the Tenth Circuit Court of Appeals affirmed, holding Colorado's rule neutral and generally applicable under the Supreme Court's existing framework for religious-exercise claims.

## What the Court is being asked

The petitioners ask the Court to decide two main questions. First, whether showing that a government rule is not evenhanded toward religion requires proving the government has completely unrestricted discretion to grant exceptions, or categorical exceptions for exactly the same conduct, as opposed to a broader look at any favorable treatment of comparable non-religious conduct. Second, whether a separate rule against denying public benefits because of religious conduct applies here, or only when a government excludes religious groups in explicitly religious terms. A related question is whether the Court should reconsider the decades-old precedent governing how courts evaluate burdens on religious exercise.

## Where it stands

The Court granted review limited to the first two questions, heard oral argument on November 3, 2026, and the case was awaiting decision as of the snapshot date.

## What each outcome would mean

St. Mary Catholic Parish and the other petitioners sought Supreme Court review after losing in the lower courts. If the Court affirms, Colorado's exclusion of the Catholic preschools from UPK funding unless they change their enrollment policies would stand. If the Court reverses, it would hold that Colorado's rule violates the petitioners' rights, likely requiring Colorado to accommodate the preschools' religious practices or extend funding without the challenged conditions. If the Court vacates, it would send the case back to the lower courts without deciding which side is right.
