## In brief

Catholic preschools ask the Court to let them turn away children over gender identity or sexual orientation while still receiving Colorado's universal preschool funding.

## What happened

St. Mary Catholic Parish and other Catholic preschools joined the Archdiocese of Denver, along with parents wanting to enroll their children, in suing Colorado officials over the state's universal preschool program. The program funds preschool for all four-year-olds but requires participating providers to give children equal opportunity to enroll regardless of religion, sexual orientation, gender identity, and other traits. The parishes wanted an exemption allowing them to exclude children, or children of parents, whose gender identity or sexual orientation conflicts with Catholic teaching. A federal trial court ruled against them after a bench trial, and the Tenth Circuit Court of Appeals affirmed, holding Colorado's rule neutral and generally applicable.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked whether a rule that applies to everyone can still be challenged as unfairly burdening religion if the government has some discretion to grant exceptions, or only if it grants identical exceptions for secular reasons; whether a separate rule barring the exclusion of religious groups from public benefits applies only when the exclusion explicitly targets religion; and whether the Court should abandon a longstanding precedent that generally permits neutral, generally applicable laws to burden religious practice without extra justification.

## Where it stands

The Court granted review limited to the first two questions, denying review of the third (whether to overrule the older precedent). Briefing and numerous amicus filings followed, and the case has been set for oral argument on November 3, 2026.

## What each outcome would mean

The parishes and the Archdiocese, as petitioners, asked the Court to take up the case. If the Court rules for the parishes, it would find Colorado's program legally treats religious exercise unfavorably and send the case back for further proceedings under stricter legal standards, likely improving the parishes' chances of winning an exemption. If the Court rules for Colorado, the Tenth Circuit's decision upholding the nondiscrimination requirement as applied to the parishes would stand. The Court could also vacate and return the case without deciding the questions for either side.
