## In brief

St. Mary Catholic Parish and other Catholic schools challenged Colorado's exclusion of them from a state-funded preschool program; the Supreme Court heard the case.

## What happened

Colorado's Universal Preschool Program funds free preschool at participating public and private schools, provided they give children "equal opportunity to enroll" regardless of race, religious affiliation, sexual orientation, gender identity, income, disability, or other listed traits. St. Mary Catholic Parish and other Catholic schools, along with parents seeking to enroll children, argued their religious practice of declining families who reject Catholic teaching on sex and marriage meant they could not sign the program's required nondiscrimination agreement. Colorado denied them a religious exemption. After a bench trial, the district court and then the Tenth Circuit Court of Appeals ruled for Colorado, holding its program neutral and generally applicable under existing Supreme Court precedent and therefore not subject to the toughest judicial scrutiny.

## What the Court is being asked

The case asks the Court to decide what counts as proof that a government rule burdening religious practice is not evenhanded, such that courts must apply strict scrutiny: specifically, whether a challenger must show the government has unrestricted discretion to grant exceptions, or identical secular exceptions already granted, or whether a broader range of comparable exceptions suffices. It also asks when a rule allowing an exception for other favored activities but not religious practice must automatically be treated as targeting religion, and whether the Court's 1990 decision in Employment Division v. Smith, which set a lenient standard for such rules, should be overturned.

## Where it stands

The Court granted review limited to two of the three questions presented, and the case was briefed, argued on November 3, 2026, and awaits decision.

## What each outcome would mean

St. Mary Catholic Parish and the other church and parent petitioners asked the Court to take up the case after losing in the lower courts, which had ruled for Colorado. If the Court affirms, Colorado's exclusion of these Catholic schools from preschool funding stands, and they remain unable to participate without changing their enrollment practices. If the Court reverses, it would hold that Colorado's program cannot be enforced against the schools this way, likely requiring the lower courts to apply stricter review or grant an exemption. If the Court vacates, it would send the case back to the lower courts without deciding which side is right, leaving the dispute to continue there.
