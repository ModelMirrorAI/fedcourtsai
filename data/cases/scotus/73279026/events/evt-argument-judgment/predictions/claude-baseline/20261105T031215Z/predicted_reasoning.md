# Forecast — St. Mary Catholic Parish v. Roy (No. 25-581)

**Judgment: reversed** (P(disturbed) 0.85). **Vote: 6–3.** Roberts, Thomas, Alito, Gorsuch, Kavanaugh and
Barrett in the majority; Sotomayor, Kagan and Jackson in dissent.

**Question reached and ground.** The Court reaches Question 1 and decides on it. It holds that Colorado's
universal-preschool program is not generally applicable. The program lets participating schools shape
enrollment through a range of secular preferences and a catch-all for programs serving "specific
communities" or "interests," and it allows single-sex programs. Yet it treats the equal-opportunity mandate as
a hard limit against religious accommodation. Under *Fulton* and *Tandon*, comparability is measured against
the State's asserted interest. It does not require identical secular conduct or "unfettered" discretion, so
the Tenth Circuit's test is rejected. Strict scrutiny applies, and Colorado fails it: about 2,000 participating
providers, a thin record of excluded families, and a refusal to accommodate even co-religionist preference.
The majority does not adopt the broad rule that *Carson*/*Sherbert* triggers strict scrutiny for every facially
neutral funding condition. It may mention the *Carson* concern about indirect exclusion, but it does not rest
on it.

**Breadth.** Moderately narrow. It states a general rule for the general-applicability inquiry, then applies
it to this program's structure. It does not decide whether states can ever condition benefits on
nondiscrimination.

**Writers.** Most likely author: Roberts, then Barrett and Kavanaugh. Expected separate writings: a principal
dissent by Sotomayor; a separate dissent by Jackson; concurrences from Gorsuch and/or Alito (with Thomas)
arguing that petitioners should also win under *Carson*/*Sherbert*, or that *Smith* should go; and possibly a
Barrett concurrence on the doctrinal path. A DIG is not a live risk.
