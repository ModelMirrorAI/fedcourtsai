# Predicted Reasoning of the Court: *St. Mary Catholic Parish in Littleton, Colorado, et al. v. Lisa Roy, et al.* (No. 25-581)

## 1. Judgment Class and Vote Lineup

- **Judgment Class:** **Reversed** (`reversed`). The Court will reverse the judgment of the United States Court of Appeals for the Tenth Circuit and hold that Respondents violated the Free Exercise Clause by excluding Petitioners' Catholic parish preschools from Colorado's Universal Preschool Program (UPK) based on their faith-based admissions standards. The Court will not remand for a new application of strict scrutiny because the district court already conducted a bench trial and addressed strict scrutiny in the alternative, and the record establishes as a matter of law under *Fulton v. City of Philadelphia*, 593 U.S. 522 (2021), that Colorado's refusal to grant a religious accommodation fails strict scrutiny.
- **Procedural Exits:** Neither a dismissal as improvidently granted (`dismissed-as-improvidently-granted`) nor an affirmance by an equally divided Court (`affirmed-by-an-equally-divided-court`) is live. All nine Justices participated at oral argument, standing and injury-in-fact were established at trial (including for parent petitioners Daniel and Lisa Sheley), and the vehicle cleanly presents the preserved Free Exercise questions.
- **Vote Split and Alignment (6–3):**
  - **Majority (6):** Chief Justice Roberts, Justice Thomas, Justice Alito, Justice Gorsuch, Justice Kavanaugh, and Justice Barrett.
  - **Dissent (3):** Justice Sotomayor, Justice Kagan, and Justice Jackson.

---

## 2. Questions Presented Reached and Doctrinal Ground (`majority-ground`)

The Court will reach Question 1 as its primary dispositive holding and harmonize Question 2 (`Carson v. Makin`, 596 U.S. 767 (2022)) as reinforcing context for public educational benefit programs, without reaching the ungranted third question on overruling *Employment Division v. Smith*, 494 U.S. 872 (1990).

Specifically, the majority opinion will rest on the following doctrinal propositions:
1. **Burden on Religious Exercise:** Requiring Catholic parish preschools to abandon their requirement that enrolling families share or agree to respect Catholic teachings on marriage, sexuality, and biological sex as a condition of participating in a generally available state preschool funding program burdens sincere religious exercise—both the religious education mission of the parish preschools and the religious upbringing choices of Catholic parents (`Mahmoud v. Taylor`, 606 U.S. 522 (2025); `Carson v. Makin`, 596 U.S. at 778–780).
2. **Lack of General Applicability under *Fulton* and *Tandon*:** Colorado's UPK equal-opportunity mandate (Colo. Rev. Stat. § 26.5-4-205(2)(b)) and implementing regulations (8 CCR 1404-1 § 4.109) are not generally applicable in two independent respects:
   - First, under *Fulton*, § 4.109(A)(9) creates a system of individualized, discretionary "programmatic preferences" under which the Colorado Department of Early Childhood (CDEC) approves bespoke enrollment preferences—including preferences for Dual Language Immersion, Montessori, Waldorf, and STEM programs, as well as preferences for families receiving housing assistance—that allow secular providers to prioritize families who share their pedagogical or programmatic mission while CDEC refuses to grant an equivalent preference or exemption for religious schools seeking families who share their religious mission.
   - Second, under *Tandon v. Newsom*, 593 U.S. 61 (2021), the UPK regime permits secular categorical exceptions for protected characteristics listed in the very same statutory sentence—such as allowing providers to restrict or prioritize seats by disability (IEPs) or income level (Head Start)—that undermine Colorado's asserted interest in guaranteeing every four-year-old equal, unrestricted access to every participating provider on the same terms.
3. **Failure of Strict Scrutiny:** Under strict scrutiny, Colorado must show a compelling interest in *denying an exception to Petitioners specifically* (`Fulton`, 593 U.S. at 541). Colorado cannot make that showing: its asserted interest in equal access is undermined by the secular and programmatic preferences it permits, and excluding Catholic preschools from UPK does not create a single additional seat for families who do not share Catholic beliefs, but instead shrinks the overall pool of UPK providers and deprives Catholic families of state preschool funding.

---

## 3. Breadth of the Majority Ground (`ground-breadth`)

The majority will resolve the case **within existing Free Exercise doctrine** by applying *Fulton*, *Tandon*, and *Carson* to Colorado's UPK statutory and regulatory scheme, rather than announcing a sweeping categorical rule that *Employment Division v. Smith* never applies to conditions on public benefits or government contracts, or that every facially neutral nondiscrimination condition in any public funding program automatically triggers strict scrutiny regardless of exemptions.

While the majority will reject the Tenth Circuit's view that *Carson v. Makin* applies only to express statutory exclusions of "sectarian" schools—confirming that *Carson* and *Fulton* together forbid states from excluding religious schools from public tuition-assistance programs because they maintain faith-based community admissions while secular schools may select for secular pedagogical fit—it will leave broader questions about whether *Smith* should be formally overruled or categorically displaced across all civil public-benefit programs to separate concurrences.

## 4. Opinion Authorship and Separate Writings

- **Majority Opinion (`majority-author`):** **Chief Justice Roberts** is the most likely author of the Opinion of the Court (`0.36`), as the author of *Trinity Lutheran*, *Espinoza*, *Fulton*, and *Carson*, and the member of the majority most invested in crafting a cohesive six-Justice opinion anchored on *Fulton*'s general-applicability framework alongside *Carson*. **Justice Kavanaugh** (`0.21`) and **Justice Gorsuch** (`0.15`) are the next most likely assignees.
- **Separate Concurrences (`separate-writers`):**
  - **Justice Gorsuch** (`0.52`) and **Justice Thomas** (`0.45`) are likely to write or join a concurrence arguing that Question 2 independently requires reversal under *Carson*, *Sherbert v. Verner*, and *Mahmoud*, and criticizing lower courts' continued reliance on *Smith* to uphold conditions that exclude religious institutions from public benefits.
  - **Justice Kavanaugh** (`0.42`) is a strong candidate to write a concurrence emphasizing that under *Carson*, *Mahmoud*, and *Boy Scouts of America v. Dale*, religious schools across diverse faith traditions (Catholic, Orthodox Jewish, Muslim, Seventh-day Adventist) constitutionally exercise religion by maintaining faith-aligned school communities.
- **Dissent (`separate-writers`):**
  - **Justice Sotomayor** (`0.65`) is the most likely author of the principal dissent (joined by Justices Kagan and Jackson), arguing that Colorado's UPK equal-opportunity mandate is a neutral and generally applicable nondiscrimination rule governing a state-funded service contract, that algorithmic matching preferences for STEM or dual-language programs and statutory accommodations for disabled or low-income children do not authorize discrimination against protected classes, and that extending *Carson* to invalidate public-accommodations-style nondiscrimination rules in government programs destabilizes civil-rights protections.
  - **Justice Jackson** (`0.35`) may also write a separate dissent focusing on the distinction between government service contracts definirg the scope of a public program and unconditional vouchers.
