# Forecast of the Court's decision

## Judgment and lineup

The Court will **reverse** the Tenth Circuit and remand for proceedings consistent with its opinion. I expect a **6–3** decision: Roberts, Thomas, Alito, Gorsuch, Kavanaugh and Barrett form the majority; Sotomayor, Kagan and Jackson dissent. Reversal and remand is the predicted judgment label, not vacatur simply because further proceedings follow. A vacatur limited to correcting the scrutiny standard is the principal alternative remedy.

## Majority ground

**Conditioning generally available private-school tuition assistance on a religious school's abandonment of its sincere religious admissions practices triggers strict scrutiny under Sherbert and Carson even without a facial religious classification; Smith does not supply rational-basis review for that condition.**

The Court will principally resolve Question 2 against the Tenth Circuit's limitation of the educational-benefits cases to explicit religious exclusions. It will view the parental commitment to the school's religious community as religious exercise rather than treating permission to teach religion as a complete answer. Families' nominal option to forgo funding will not eliminate the burden.

I expect the Court to hold that Colorado has not justified denying the requested accommodation under claimant-specific strict scrutiny. It will distinguish a weighty general commitment to equal opportunity from the necessity of excluding these schools, emphasize the limited demonstrated access problem and available alternative providers, and treat accommodation as adding choices rather than compelling families to attend a religious school. It will not declare every nondiscrimination requirement constitutionally invalid. Colorado's arguments about location and practical accessibility will be addressed but will not carry its burden on this record.

## Breadth and questions left open

**The majority announces a rule governing religious-exercise conditions in generally available private-school aid programs, rather than confining its holding to Colorado's particular secular enrollment exceptions or discretionary matching arrangements.**

The announced principle will therefore extend beyond these named schools and beyond Colorado's current program design. The Court will nevertheless leave room for genuinely compelling and appropriately tailored conditions, including the distinct race-discrimination concerns raised at argument. It need not resolve every hypothetical concerning public services, tax benefits or regulatory licensing.

On Question 1, secular exceptions and administrative flexibility will reinforce the result, but I expect the Court not to make the precise scope of the catchall preference the indispensable basis of decision. A narrower Fulton/Tandon-only holding is the main competing doctrinal forecast, not the ground asserted by my semantic claims. Question 3 was not granted: Smith will not be formally overruled, and the majority will distinguish its criminal-prohibition setting rather than replace the entire framework.

## Opinions and procedural alternatives

Roberts is my most likely Court-opinion author, with Gorsuch a close alternative. Roberts's focus on preserving the public-benefits precedents while testing their boundaries makes him a plausible author of a six-Justice opinion. I expect Sotomayor to write the principal dissent, joined by Kagan and Jackson, arguing that the majority turns equal treatment into a preferred exemption and disrupts settled nondiscrimination doctrine. Jackson is a meaningful alternative dissent author or an additional writer.

A separate Alito or Gorsuch concurrence may argue that the majority still leaves Smith too much room or explain why the distinction between funding and prohibition is unstable. Barrett may separately explain the narrower doctrinal boundary she accepts. Those possible writings do not change the modal forecast that six join the controlling opinion. A DIG is a small residual risk, not an anticipated procedural resolution; no known recusal creates a meaningful equally divided-Court scenario.
