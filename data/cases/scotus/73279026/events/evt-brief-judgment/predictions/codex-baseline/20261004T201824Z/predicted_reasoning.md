# Forecast of the Court's judgment

The Court will **reverse** the Tenth Circuit's judgment and remand for proceedings consistent with its Free Exercise holding. The predicted division is **6–3**: Chief Justice Roberts and Justices Thomas, Alito, Gorsuch, Kavanaugh, and Barrett in the majority; Justices Sotomayor, Kagan, and Jackson dissenting. The probability that the judgment below is disturbed, including vacatur or partial reversal, is **0.83**. Reversal is the single most likely judgment class, not the entire disturbed probability.

The majority will hold that Colorado's allowance of comparable secular enrollment preferences defeats general applicability and that the State fails strict scrutiny as applied to these petitioners. It will reject the proposition that only an exemption for identical conduct or wholly unfettered administrative discretion can trigger heightened scrutiny. Comparability will turn on the asserted equal-access interest, not simply on whether the preferred and disfavored admissions criteria use the same protected classification.

The majority will treat the program's accommodation of particular secular communities as inconsistent with refusing comparable religious accommodation. It will find Colorado's justification for denying these petitioners an accommodation insufficiently tailored, rather than hold that nondiscrimination is never a compelling governmental interest. This predicts acceptance of the petitioners' characterization of the disputed enrollment preferences; it is not a finding that their factual characterization is already established.

The holding will be limited to unequal accommodation in this preschool funding scheme, not a categorical exemption from every neutral public-funding condition. The Court will address the first granted question through general applicability. On the second, it will reject an exclusively express-discrimination reading of the religious-benefits precedents without declaring all incidental burdens in public-benefits programs unconstitutional. It will leave Employment Division v. Smith formally intact: the April 20 grant expressly excluded the petition's third question asking that Smith be overruled.

The dissent will defend Colorado's distinction between providing specialized services to disadvantaged children and excluding children because of their families' protected characteristics. It will emphasize the trial findings, the participation of other religious providers, and the absence of authority to waive the equal-opportunity requirement. A separate concurrence urging a broader benefits rule is plausible, but the committed vote forecast is that all six majority Justices join the controlling ground; no author or writing role is specified.

The expected judgment follows the scheduled November 3, 2026 argument and arrives in spring or early summer 2027, most likely May or June. Vacatur for reconsideration under a corrected standard is the principal alternative to outright reversal. A DIG or equally divided affirmance is a small residual possibility, not the central forecast; the provisioned record supplies no specific recusal, mootness, or jurisdictional defect.
