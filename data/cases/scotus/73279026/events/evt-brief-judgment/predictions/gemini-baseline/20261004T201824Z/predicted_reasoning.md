**Judgment and Vote Lineup:**
I predict the Court will enter a judgment of reversed. The six conservative justices (Roberts, Thomas, Alito, Gorsuch, Kavanaugh, Barrett) will form the majority in favor of the religious petitioners. Justices Sotomayor, Kagan, and Jackson will dissent, arguing that the state's exemptions for low-income and disabled children are materially different from religious exemptions and do not undermine the law's general applicability.

**Majority Ground and Breadth:**
The majority opinion will hold that Colorado's non-discrimination mandate for the Universal Preschool program is not generally applicable under *Employment Division v. Smith*. The Court will point to the categorical and discretionary secular exemptions granted by the state—such as those for low-income families and children with disabilities—and conclude that the state cannot refuse to extend similar accommodations for religious exercise. By doing so, the Court will rule that the policy triggers strict scrutiny, which Colorado cannot satisfy.

The Court will articulate a broad rule that *any* categorical or discretionary secular exemption to a non-discrimination policy renders the law not generally applicable under *Smith*, requiring strict scrutiny for religious objectors without needing to show explicit governmental hostility. The Court is likely to rely heavily on *Fulton v. City of Philadelphia* and *Carson v. Makin*, cementing the rule that government benefit programs cannot condition participation on the relinquishment of religious practices, especially when secular exceptions are permitted.

Because the Court limited its grant of certiorari to Questions 1 and 2, the majority will not formally overrule *Employment Division v. Smith*, but will further cabin its reach in the context of non-discrimination mandates. 

**Procedural Exit:**
A procedural exit (such as a DIG) is highly unlikely. The case presents a clean vehicle to address an entrenched circuit split over how secular exemptions affect general applicability under *Smith*, a question the Court has been eager to resolve.
