# Predicted reasoning — St. Mary Catholic Parish v. Roy, No. 25-581 (`evt-brief-judgment`)

Forecast of what the Court will do with the Tenth Circuit's judgment, made from the record as of the respondents' merits brief (filed August 17, 2026; argument set for November 3, 2026).

## Judgment class

**Reversed.** The Court will reverse the Tenth Circuit's judgment upholding Colorado's exclusion of the parish preschools from the Universal Preschool (UPK) program and remand for entry of relief consistent with strict scrutiny having been failed. I put a plain reversal well ahead of a vacatur-and-remand for reapplication of the correct test: the Court has the parties' stipulated and tried facts, both sides have briefed strict scrutiny in full, and in the comparable funding-condition cases (Trinity Lutheran, Espinoza, Carson, Fulton, Mahmoud) the Court decided the scrutiny question itself rather than sending it back open. A DIG is not live: the grant was already narrowed to Questions 1 and 2, the respondents raise mootness only as to one closed parish school in a footnote and do not ask for dismissal, and the live parish and the parent plaintiff keep the controversy alive. No recusal is signalled, so an equally divided affirmance is not in play.

## Vote lineup

**6–3** on the familiar lines: Roberts, Thomas, Alito, Gorsuch, Kavanaugh and Barrett in the majority; Sotomayor, Kagan and Jackson in dissent. The Chief Justice is the most likely author, having kept Trinity Lutheran, Espinoza, Carson and Fulton for himself; Sotomayor the most likely principal dissenter. A broader majority (Kagan concurring in the judgment on the discretionary-exemption point alone, as she joined the Fulton majority) is the main alternative, and I rate it well below the 6–3 split because the condition here is a sexual-orientation and gender-identity nondiscrimination rule, where the three Justices who dissented in Mahmoud and 303 Creative have consistently written separately. Expect at least one concurrence from Thomas, Alito or Gorsuch pressing for more than the majority gives, most plausibly on the status of Smith itself or on Question 2.

## Which question the Court reaches, and the ground

The Court decides the case on **Question 1** and resolves the circuit split against the Tenth Circuit's reading of Smith. The majority will hold that general applicability under Smith, as construed in Fulton and Tandon, does not require a showing of unfettered discretion or of a categorical exemption for *identical* secular conduct. It is enough that the government permits secular conduct that undermines its asserted interest in a *comparable* way, and comparability is judged against the interest the government actually asserts. On this record the majority will point to two features of UPK: the categorical enrollment preferences for income-based and disability-focused providers, and the discretionary "programmatic preference" catchall under which Colorado's own program director testified a preschool could favor "children of color" or "the LGBTQ community". The majority will treat the existence of a mechanism for individualized consideration as triggering Fulton whether or not Colorado has ever used it to waive the equal-opportunity rule, and will reject the Tenth Circuit's reliance on the regulation's text over the director's testimony as a question of what the regime permits rather than what the agency has so far done.

Strict scrutiny then applies and fails. The majority will say that Colorado's interest in equal access, stated at the level of the program as a whole, cannot be compelling when the State itself tolerates a system of secular preferences that cut against it (Fulton's "system of exceptions" point), and that the State has made no showing that less restrictive means, such as the alternative placement and disclosure measures petitioners proposed, would not serve its interest. The Court will not hold that nondiscrimination interests can never be compelling; it will hold that this program's interest, as implemented, is not.

**Question 2** (whether Carson's rule applies only to explicit religious exclusions) is the question most likely to be left undecided or addressed in a paragraph. Having found the rule not generally applicable, the majority does not need Carson, and a holding that any benefit denial tied to religious *exercise* triggers strict scrutiny regardless of facial neutrality would be a larger doctrinal step than the Court needs. I expect the opinion to say the Carson line confirms the result without resting on it, and to decline the Solicitor General's broader "public benefits" theory as outside the questions presented. The Court will not revisit Smith; it declined the question when it narrowed the grant.

## Breadth

The ground will be stated as a general rule for free-exercise challenges, not as a holding about Colorado preschools: a law is not generally applicable where it permits any secular exception, categorical or discretionary, that undermines the asserted interest to a comparable degree, and the identical-conduct and unfettered-discretion thresholds the Tenth Circuit (and the minority side of the split) applied are wrong. The remedy, by contrast, will be case-specific: the exclusion of these parishes from UPK violates the Free Exercise Clause as applied, and the opinion will not purport to invalidate the equal-opportunity statute on its face.

## Separate writings and timing

Expect a Sotomayor dissent, joined by Kagan and Jackson, arguing that the Court has converted every benefit program with any eligibility preference into a non-generally-applicable law and that nondiscrimination protections for LGBTQ families are a compelling interest on this record, with emphasis on the trial court's factual findings that the agency had no power to waive the rule. A separate concurrence from Thomas or Gorsuch on Smith, and possibly an Alito concurrence on Question 2, are likely. With a November argument, the decision should issue between March and the end of June 2027, most likely in the last weeks of the Term.
