# Predicted reasoning — St. Mary Catholic Parish v. Roy, No. 25-581 (merits, moment: grant)

## Judgment class

The Court will **reverse** the Tenth Circuit's judgment. The judgment below
will be disturbed; the modal label is `reversed` (the Court resolving the
free-exercise claim itself, as in Fulton, rather than remanding for a redo of
the analysis). The nearest alternative is `vacated` — announcing the correct
general-applicability standard and remanding for its application — but this
Court has applied strict scrutiny itself in every recent case of this shape
(Fulton, Tandon, Kennedy), and the record on Colorado's exemptions is fully
developed. A DIG is unlikely: the grant was limited to Questions 1 and 2, so
the Court has already trimmed the vehicle to the questions it wants, and
nothing on the docket since suggests a mootness or vehicle problem. An
affirmance would require holding that a program with categorical and
discretionary secular carve-outs is nonetheless generally applicable — the
position of the side of the circuit split the Court granted to review.

## Vote lineup

**6–3.** Majority: Roberts, Thomas, Alito, Gorsuch, Kavanaugh, Barrett.
Dissent: Sotomayor, Kagan, Jackson. This is the lineup of Carson v. Makin and
Mahmoud v. Taylor, the two closest recent analogues (religious families vs. a
state education program, with LGBTQ-related nondiscrimination interests on the
other side). There is real upside to a broader majority — Fulton (the closest
doctrinal analogue) was 9–0 in the judgment, and Catholic Charities v.
Wisconsin (2025) was unanimous — so a 7–2 or 9–0 disposition on a narrow
exemptions-comparability ground would not surprise; Kagan is the likeliest
crossover, as in Fulton. But Kagan dissented in both Espinoza and Carson, the
funding-program cases, and the QP-1 standard the petitioners seek is broader
than Fulton's individualized-exemptions holding, so 6–3 is the modal call.

## Which question the Court reaches

The grant was limited to QP1 (what defeats general applicability under Smith)
and QP2 (whether Carson's rule requires an explicit religious exclusion); QP3
(overruling Smith) was denied. The Court will decide the case on **QP1**: it
will reject the Tenth Circuit's requirement of "unfettered" discretion or
categorical exemptions for "identical" secular conduct, and hold that
Colorado's carve-outs — providers limited to low-income families, children
with disabilities, or specified identity groups, plus the department's
discretionary variance authority — defeat general applicability under
Fulton/Tandon comparability. Having resolved QP1, the majority will likely
leave QP2 undecided or address it only briefly (as it left the
church-autonomy alternative unaddressed in Fulton); if it does reach QP2, it
will say Carson does not require an explicit religious classification, but
that will not carry the judgment.

## Ground and breadth; separate writings

Strict scrutiny will apply and Colorado will fail it, largely on Fulton's
template: the State cannot show a compelling interest in denying an exemption
to these petitioners while tolerating comparable secular exceptions. The rule
announced will be program-general (any exemption-bearing benefit scheme), but
the Court will not revisit Smith — the limited grant signals exactly that.
Expect a concurrence from Thomas, Alito, or Gorsuch (as in Fulton) renewing
the call to reconsider Smith and faulting the Court for resolving the case
without it; Barrett and/or Kavanaugh may write separately, as in Fulton, on
what should eventually replace Smith. Sotomayor writes the principal dissent,
joined by Kagan and Jackson, on the ground that a religious provider may not
claim public preschool funds while turning away families based on sexual
orientation or gender identity, and that Colorado's categorical
equal-opportunity requirement contains no comparable secular exemptions.

## Timing (unscored, stated for discipline)

Argument is set for November 3, 2026. A decision is likely in the back half of
the Term — expect the opinion in **May or June 2027**, given the case's
salience and the near-certainty of separate writings.
