I adjusted sharply upward from that anchor because the granted questions align with a durable six-Justice majority's recent Free Exercise decisions, the United States supports petitioners, and the grant excluded the request to overrule Smith while preserving two paths to reversal under existing precedent.

The provisioned questions presented, petition, and brief in opposition supplied the competing theories. Petitioners characterize the UPK income and disability preferences and the catchall preference as secular exemptions or individualized discretion that undermine equal access in the same way as their requested accommodation. Colorado answers that none is legally an exception, that concurrent lower-court findings foreclose the factual premise, and that Carson reaches explicit exclusions based on religious status or use rather than a neutral nondiscrimination condition. The Court granted Questions 1 and 2 despite those vehicle objections. That choice makes an affirmance based on the same objections possible but unlikely.

This was a forward-mode forecast. I used the latest provisioned docket snapshot, dated August 16, 2026, together with the provisioned cert-stage texts. The snapshot postdates the April 20 grant and shows petitioners' merits brief, extensive petitioner-side amicus activity, and argument set for November 3; it does not provide the contents of those filings. I retrieved the petitioners' merits brief and the United States' merits amicus brief from the official Supreme Court URLs recorded in the snapshot. Both ask for reversal, put Carson's public-benefits rule first or alongside general applicability, and contend that Colorado cannot satisfy strict scrutiny. The respondent's merits brief was due August 17 and was not on the snapshot, so the principal defense remains the provisioned brief in opposition. The provisioned petition and opposition texts are marked truncated, although their principal arguments and lower-court materials were available.

The 6-3 lineup follows the Court's most recent closely analogous education-and-religious-exercise case, Mahmoud v. Taylor: the six conservative Justices formed the majority and Justices Sotomayor, Kagan, and Jackson dissented. CourtListener confirmed that lineup. Fulton creates some possibility of a broader or differently reasoned coalition, but the intersection of religious education, public funding, and sexual-orientation and gender-identity rules makes the Mahmoud alignment the better forecast. I omitted writing-role predictions because authorship and separate writings are materially less certain than the votes.

The main uncertainty is judgment form rather than the disturbed binary. A majority might vacate and remand because the Tenth Circuit did not decide strict scrutiny, or it might reverse after finding Colorado's own preferences defeat a compelling uniformity interest. There is also record-sensitive risk in the lower courts' findings that the preferences do not permit violations of the equal-opportunity requirement. These considerations keep the probability below near-certainty. I did not use the context's high salience band or three distributions: they describe the completed cert stage and have no bearing on the merits judgment.
