# Forecast

The Court will reverse the Tenth Circuit, most likely by a 6-3 vote. Chief Justice Roberts and Justices Thomas, Alito, Gorsuch, Kavanaugh, and Barrett will vote to disturb the judgment; Justices Sotomayor, Kagan, and Jackson will dissent. The mandate will be returned to the lower courts under strict scrutiny, with the majority likely concluding that Colorado cannot defend the exclusion while allowing enrollment preferences and retaining less restrictive ways to help families identify suitable preschools.

The majority will reach both granted questions while treating Carson as the principal ground. It will hold that a state cannot condition a generally available educational benefit on abandonment of otherwise lawful religious exercise merely by expressing the condition in facially secular terms. Carson's protection for exclusions based on religious exercise will therefore not be confined to statutes that explicitly classify by religion.

The Court will also reject the Tenth Circuit's narrow understanding of general applicability. Under Fulton and Tandon, comparability turns on whether secular conduct undermines the government's asserted interest in a similar way, not whether it is identical to the requested religious conduct. Colorado's income- and disability-based enrollment preferences undermine its asserted interest in uniform equal access, and the catchall preference mechanism invites individualized assessment even if no official has unfettered authority acting alone.

The holding will be categorical within generally available educational-benefit programs but will not overrule Employment Division v. Smith. The Court granted review only on Questions 1 and 2, leaving the petition's third question about overruling Smith outside the case. Separate writings may press a broader reconsideration of Smith, but the controlling opinion will resolve the case through Carson and existing general-applicability doctrine.

A dismissal as improvidently granted or an equally divided affirmance is not a substantial possibility. The live uncertainty concerns judgment form: the Court could describe its action as vacatur and remand for strict-scrutiny analysis, but the more likely formal judgment is reversal and remand because both petitioners and the United States seek reversal and argue that the present record cannot satisfy strict scrutiny.
