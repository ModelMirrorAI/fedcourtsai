# Forecast: what the Court will do in DHS v. D.V.D. (No. 26-426)

**Judgment class.** The Court will **reverse** the First Circuit's judgment
(P(disturbed) = 0.87). Reversal is the modal label; a *vacate and remand*
(if the Court stops at a jurisdictional or 1252(f)(1) holding and sends the
case back with instructions) is the next most likely disturbed form, and an
*affirmed in part, reversed in part* (some notice requirement survives while
the classwide remedies fall) is a live minority path. A full affirmance
requires respondents to win jurisdiction, remedy, and merits together before
a bench six of whose members have twice found the Government likely to
succeed in this very case; I put it near 0.12. A DIG is remote: the Court
itself granted, framed the questions, and expedited argument. No recusal is
apparent, so an equally divided affirmance is negligible.

**Vote lineup.** 6–3. Roberts, Thomas, Alito, Gorsuch, Kavanaugh, and Barrett
in the majority; Sotomayor, Kagan, and Jackson in dissent, the same three who
noted they would deny the stay on September 29, 2026 and who dissented from
the June 2025 stay of the preliminary injunction. The most plausible
departure is a narrower concurrence from Barrett or Roberts on the
1252(f)(1) declaratory-relief question, where respondents' textual argument
(the caption reads "Limit on injunctive relief"; 1252(e)(1)(A) bars
declaratory relief expressly and 1252(f)(1) does not) is strongest. A 5–4
against the Government is not my forecast.

**Which question the Court reaches and the ground it rests on.** The grant
order directs three questions plus an open-ended fourth. I expect the
majority to reach the third, the lawfulness of the Guidance, and to rest the
judgment there: neither 8 U.S.C. 1231(b)(3) nor FARRA/CAT and their
regulations impose pre-removal notice-and-hearing requirements on
third-country removals beyond their text; 1231(h) forbids construing 1231 to
create enforceable procedural rights; constitutional avoidance is no license
to graft procedures onto the statute (Jennings v. Rodriguez); and the
Guidance's procedures, including reliance on a receiving country's credible
diplomatic assurances (Munaf v. Geren), satisfy any due process owed to
aliens already under final removal orders who had a hearing. I expect the
Court also to hold that 1252(f)(1)'s "restrain" (Garland v. Aleman Gonzalez)
bars the classwide declaratory judgments and the universal APA vacatur,
answering the question reserved in Aleman Gonzalez n.2 and Biden v. Texas
n.4. On the first question I expect the Court to reject the broad 1252(g)
reading (Reno v. AADC and DHS v. Regents confine it to the three discrete
discretionary actions) and to find 1252(b)(9) and 1252(a)(4)/FARRA 2242(d)
do not channel a collateral APA challenge to a post-order policy, though a
1252(b)(9) channeling holding is a real minority route.

**Breadth.** Categorical rather than as-applied: a rule governing every
third-country removal under the Guidance, not confined to the named
plaintiffs, the class as certified, or the 24-hour timing, and a
1252(f)(1) rule stated for all lower courts. The dissent will press the
Solicitor General's own concession at the Riley v. Bondi argument that
notice and an opportunity to raise a fear are required before third-country
removal, and the record of chain refoulement and detention abroad.

**Separate writings.** Alito is my (low-confidence) guess for the majority
author, with Sotomayor writing the principal dissent. A Kagan separate
writing on the Riley concession and a Gorsuch or Barrett concurrence on the
APA-vacatur question are plausible; none of this is scored.

**Timing.** Argued in the December 2026 session per the grant order. Given
the expedition and the stay's terms, a decision between March and June 2027,
with late June the single most likely window. Not scored.
