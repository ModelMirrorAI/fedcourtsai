# Forecast of the Court's judgment

The Court will **reverse** the First Circuit's judgment and remand. The modal lineup is **6–3**: Roberts, Thomas, Alito, Gorsuch, Kavanaugh, and Barrett in the majority; Sotomayor, Kagan, and Jackson dissenting. The probability that the judgment below is disturbed, including vacatur or a mixed judgment, is **0.86**. This forecasts the merits judgment, not the already-granted stay or certiorari petition.

The majority will resolve the dispute principally through the limit on lower-court remedies in 8 U.S.C. 1252(f)(1). It will treat the classwide declarations and APA vacatur as restraints on operation of the removal provisions, notwithstanding their different labels. It will extend the functional reading of that limitation in *Garland v. Aleman Gonzalez*, rather than permit substantially equivalent constraints through a declaration followed by individual enforcement proceedings. This is a prediction of an extension, not a statement that the precedent already decides the question.

The resulting rule will apply beyond these named plaintiffs to classwide lower-court remedies restraining the covered INA provisions. It will not establish that every immigration-related declaratory judgment is forbidden, or that APA vacatur is unavailable throughout administrative law. Most importantly, the controlling opinion will leave unresolved the constitutional minimum of notice and opportunity to present a country-specific protection claim in an individual removal case. A government victory on the remedy is not a holding that removal to persecution or torture is lawful.

The Court will reach the second question specified in its grant order. I expect its treatment of jurisdiction to preserve a distinction between access to adjudication and authority to award the challenged classwide remedy, rather than endorse every jurisdiction-stripping theory the government advances. It will not need to decide all the substantive alternatives grouped in the third question, including the sufficiency of blanket diplomatic assurances. I do not forecast an additional holding under the open-ended fourth question.

The dissent will distinguish declaratory relief and agency-action vacatur from injunctions and emphasize that review confined to an old removal order may not meaningfully address a newly selected destination. A separate writing within the prevailing coalition may stress the continuing possibility of individual protection claims without changing the judgment. I make no author prediction.

Given the record's December 2026 argument direction, I expect a reasoned judgment during the first half of 2027, most likely by the end of June. Vacatur and remand on a narrower jurisdictional or remedial ground is the main alternative judgment class. A DIG is a small residual possibility, not the expected route; the record supplies no affirmative reason to forecast recusal or an equally divided Court.
