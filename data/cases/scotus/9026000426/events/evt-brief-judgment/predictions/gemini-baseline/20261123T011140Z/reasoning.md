Every comparable OT2024–OT2025 Trump Administration immigration/remedies case examined in retrieval (`Trump v. CASA, Inc.`, 606 U.S. 831 (2025); `Blanche v. Lau`, 609 U.S. 1 (2026); `Mullin v. Al Otro Lado`, 609 U.S. 258 (2026); `Mullin v. Doe`, 609 U.S. 324 (2026)) resolved `6–3` in the Government's favor.
2. **Asymmetric Conjunctive Structure Across Four Questions Presented**: To win an undisturbed affirmance, Respondents must prevail on **all** of: (a) jurisdiction against `8 U.S.C. §§ 1252(g)`, `(b)(9)`/`(a)(5)`, and `(a)(4)` / FARRA `§ 2242(d)` (QP2); (b) remedial authority for both classwide declaratory relief and universal APA `§ 706` vacatur notwithstanding `8 U.S.C. § 1252(f)(1)` (QP1); **and** (c) either statutory/regulatory invalidity despite `8 U.S.C. § 1231(h)`'s express bar on construing `§ 1231` to create enforceable procedural rights (QP3) or **facial** unconstitutionality under `United States v. Salerno`, 481 U.S. 739 (1987), despite DHS's diplomatic-assurance and translated-notice/screening provisions (QP4). DHS disturbs the judgment if five Justices agree with it on **any one** of QP2, QP1, or QP3+QP4.
3. **Acute Vulnerability of the Remedy Below Under `§ 1252(f)(1)`**: After this Court stayed the classwide preliminary injunction (`D.V.D. I`), the District Court dissolved the injunction and replaced it with parallel classwide declarations and universal APA vacatur of DHS's internal Guidance (`D.V.D. Even conservatives who defend APA `§ 706` vacatur in ordinary administrative litigation (Roberts, Kavanaugh) have a clean INA-specific textual path — flagged explicitly by the Solicitor General in Brief n.5 (asking the Court *not* to decide general APA universal vacatur here, pointing instead to *HHS v. Grace Brethren Church`, 457 U.S. 393 (1982), that `§ 1252(f)(1)` forbids lower courts from `"restrain[ing]"` the operation of `§ 1231` via coercive classwide declarations or vacatur. And insofar as individual named-plaintiff claims remain within `§ 1252(f)(1)`'s `"individual alien"` carveout (`Biden v. Texas`, 597 U.S. 785, 798–801 (2022)), `§ 1231(h)`, `Vermont Yankee`, `Munaf v. Geren`, 553 U.S. 674 (2008), and `Salerno` strongly favor DHS against a facial challenge.
