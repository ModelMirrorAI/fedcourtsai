# Predicted Court Resolution (`scotus/9026000426` — `evt-brief-judgment`)

## Judgment and Vote Lineup

- **Judgment**: **Reversed** (reversing the judgment of the First Circuit and remanding for further proceedings). A vacatur and remand is the primary alternative if the Court stops strictly at the remedial bar of `8 U.S.C. § 1252(f)(1)` without adjudicating the named plaintiffs' underlying claims.
- **Vote Split**: **6–3**. Chief Justice Roberts and Justices Thomas, Alito, Gorsuch, Kavanaugh, and Barrett join the judgment reversing the First Circuit; Justices Sotomayor, Kagan, and Jackson dissent, tracking the `6–3` division on the Court's June 23, 2025 and September 29, 2026 stay orders in this litigation.
- **Procedural Exit**: Neither a dismissal as improvidently granted nor an equally divided Court is live. The Court framed all four Questions Presented itself when converting the stay application in No. 26A406 into certiorari, all nine Justices participated in that order without recusal, and the final declaratory and vacatur judgment below keeps the controversy live under `Mullin v. Al Otro Lado`, 609 U.S. 258 (2026).

## Questions Reached and Doctrinal Ground

1. **Remedial Bar Under `8 U.S.C. § 1252(f)(1)` (Question 1 — Reached and Dispositive of Classwide & Vacatur Relief)**:
   The majority holds under the plain text of `8 U.S.C. § 1252(f)(1)`, extending `Garland v. Aleman Gonzalez`, 596 U.S. 543 (2022), and applying `California v. Grace Brethren Church`, 457 U.S. 393 (1982), that lower federal courts lack authority to `"enjoin or restrain the operation"` of `8 U.S.C. § 1231` through classwide declaratory judgments or APA `§ 706` vacatur orders that practically check or dictate executive implementation of third-country removals. Because the District Court substituted classwide declarations and universal vacatur for its stayed preliminary injunction specifically to govern DHS's execution of `§ 1231(b)` across an entire class, both remedies impermissibly `"restrain"` the statute's operation as to non-individual class members.
2. **Jurisdiction-Channeling Bars (Question 2 — Partially Reached or Bypassed in Favor of Narrower Remedial/Merits Grounds)**:
   To hold all six conservative Justices together — given Justice Gorsuch's concern in `Riley v. Bondi`, 606 U.S. 259 (2025), against reading `§ 1252`'s petition-for-review provisions to trap noncitizens with no forum for post-order protection claims, and the Court's approach in `Biden v. Texas`, 597 U.S. 785, 798–801 (2022) — the majority treats `§ 1252(f)(1)` as stripping the District Court of authority to award any non-individualized relief without needing a sweeping holding that `§ 1252(g)` or `§ 1252(b)(9)` forecloses even individual habeas review of an unconstitutional removal.
3. **Statutory and Facial Due Process Claims (Questions 3 & 4 — Reached as to Any Surviving Individual Claims and Rejected on Facial Grounds)**:
   To the extent the four named class representatives fall within `§ 1252(f)(1)`'s carveout for `"an individual alien against whom proceedings under such part have been initiated"` (or to resolve the questions framed in the grant order, as in `Biden v. Texas`), the majority rejects Respondents' facial statutory and constitutional challenges to the March and July 2025 Guidance. On Question 3, `8 U.S.C. § 1231(b)(3)` specifies no third-country notice-and-screening procedures (`Vermont Yankee`), `8 U.S.C. § 1231(h)` expressly bars construing `§ 1231` to create any enforceable procedural right, and State Department-evaluated diplomatic assurances satisfy executive CAT regulations (`8 C.F.R. § 208.18(c)`; `Munaf v. Geren`, 553 U.S. 674 (2008)). On Question 4, Respondents' facial Fifth Amendment challenge fails under `United States v. Salerno`, 481 U.S. 739 (1987), because aliens already subject to valid final removal orders cannot show that DHS's dual-track framework (credible diplomatic assurances or translated notice plus reasonable-fear screening) is unconstitutional across all applications.

## Breadth of the Holding

The majority confines its holding to statutory remedial and procedural challenges to classwide or universal relief under `8 U.S.C. §§ 1231` and `1252(f)(1)`, declining to abolish APA `§ 706` vacatur across administrative law generally (a broader question pending separately and disclaimed by the Solicitor General for this vehicle) and leaving open individualized as-applied habeas or injunctive challenges by a specific noncitizen facing imminent persecution or torture without notice (reconciling the outcome with `Trump v. J.G.G.`, 604 U.S. 670 (2025), and `A.A.R.P. v. Trump`, 605 U.S. 91 (2025)).

## Expected Authorship and Separate Writings

- **Majority Opinion**: **Justice Alito** (the author of `Aleman Gonzalez`, `Jennings`, `Thuraissigiam`, `Riley`, `Mullin v. Doe`, and `Mullin v. Al Otro Lado`) or **Chief Justice Roberts** (the author of `Biden v. Texas` and `Regents`), with **Justice Barrett** (`Trump v. - **Separate Concurrences**:
  - **Justice Thomas** is likely to concur to argue that `§ 1252(g)` and `§ 1252(b)(9)` independently strip district-court jurisdiction in full, that the APA does not authorize universal vacatur at all, and that noncitizens with final removal orders lack Fifth Amendment procedural due process rights regarding country selection.
  - **Justice Gorsuch** is a strong candidate to concur (potentially joined in part by Thomas or Barrett) addressing universal APA `§ 706` vacatur while distinguishing individual PFR/habeas access.
  - **Justice Kavanaugh** has a meaningful chance to concur to emphasize that ordinary APA vacatur outside `§ 1252(f)(1)` is undisturbed and that individual noncitizens retain as-applied avenues to contest a specific third-country transfer lacking notice or bona fide assurances.
- **Dissents**: **Justice Sotomayor** (who authored the June 2025 stay dissent in this case) or **Justice Jackson** (First Circuit Justice) is most likely to author the principal dissent joined by **Justice Kagan**, arguing that neither `§ 1252(f)(1)` nor `§ 1252`'s channeling clauses insulate systemic refoulement policies from declaratory and APA review and that deporting noncitizens to unfamiliar third countries on hours' notice or unreviewable assurances violates the INA, FARRA/CAT, and Fifth Amendment Due Process; a second separate dissent by Jackson or Sotomayor highlighting the record's chain-refoulement examples is also plausible.
