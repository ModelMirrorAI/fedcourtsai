# Forecast: DHS v. D.V.D., No. 26-426 (briefed moment)

**Judgment class.** The Court reverses the First Circuit's judgment (which affirmed the classwide declarations on notice and opportunity-to-be-heard and the APA vacatur of the third-country removal Guidance). A "vacated and remanded with instructions to dismiss" formulation is the main alternative label; a split judgment (jurisdiction upheld, classwide relief or part of the merits reversed) is the next. An outright affirmance is unlikely, and a DIG is not live: the Court itself wrote the questions and expedited the case.

**Vote lineup.** 6-3: Roberts, Thomas, Alito, Gorsuch, Kavanaugh and Barrett in the majority; Sotomayor, Kagan and Jackson in dissent. The likeliest departure from that lineup is Kagan (or less likely Barrett or Gorsuch) joining only part of the result if the majority reaches the due process merits.

**Ground.** The majority rests on jurisdiction: 8 U.S.C. 1252(g) bars this suit because the claims arise from the decision or action to execute removal orders, and the discretion/authority distinction the lower courts drew is rejected. Section 1252(b)(9) and the FARRA/1252(a)(4) bar may be invoked as reinforcement. The Court does not issue a merits ruling that the Guidance satisfies due process, and it treats the 1252(f)(1) question as unnecessary or addresses it only briefly. The second most likely ground is 1252(f)(1) (no classwide declaratory relief or vacatur) paired with a merits ruling for the government on the named plaintiffs' claims.

**Breadth.** A categorical rule about programmatic district-court challenges to how removal orders are executed, with an explicit reservation of what individual review (habeas, petition for review, motion to reopen) remains for a noncitizen who fears torture in a newly designated country.

**Who writes.** Alito is the single likeliest author (he wrote Aleman Gonzalez and Jennings), then the Chief Justice, Kavanaugh and Barrett. Sotomayor writes the principal dissent, as she did at the stay stage in 2025; Jackson has an even chance of a separate dissent. A concurrence from Thomas or Gorsuch (on APA vacatur or remedial questions) or from Kavanaugh or Barrett (on what review remains) is plausible but not the base case.

**Timing.** Argued December 9, 2026 on an expedited schedule; decision between February and June 2027, more likely in the back half of that window.
