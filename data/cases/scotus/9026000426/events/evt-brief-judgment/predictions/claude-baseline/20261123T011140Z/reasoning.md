**Adjustments up.**
- The Court granted the government's stay of the district court's final judgment in the same order that granted certiorari, over three noted dissents (Sotomayor, Kagan, Jackson). A stay requires a majority to see a fair prospect of reversal. The docket and the First Circuit opinion also show this is the same litigation in which the Court stayed earlier relief in 2025.
- The Court treated the stay application as a petition, wrote the questions itself, and expedited to the December sitting. That is the posture of a Court inclined to correct the judgment, not to leave it.
- The government has three independent routes (jurisdiction, 1252(f)(1), merits); respondents must win all three to preserve the judgment intact. Even a partial government win (for example classwide relief barred) is "disturbed".
- The First Circuit itself vacated part of the district court's judgment for lack of standing, so the judgment is not a clean one.

**Adjustments down.** The respondents' brief leans on the Court's 2025 statements in A.A.R.P. and J.G.G. that roughly 24 hours' notice does not satisfy due process, which several majority Justices joined; a majority might find the no-notice diplomatic-assurance track troubling. But that pushes toward a jurisdictional or remedial ground rather than an affirmance. I leave about 10% for affirmance, an equally divided Court or a DIG, almost all of it affirmance.
