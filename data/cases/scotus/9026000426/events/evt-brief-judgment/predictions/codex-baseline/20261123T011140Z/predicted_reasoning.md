The Court will reverse the First Circuit, 6–3. I expect Justice Alito to write for Chief Justice Roberts and Justices Thomas, Gorsuch, Kavanaugh and Barrett. Justice Sotomayor will dissent, joined by Justices Kagan and Jackson.

The majority will decide the first question in the grant order. A final removal order authorizes DHS to carry it out by selecting a destination permitted by statute, including one first identified after the immigration hearing. A challenge to the procedures DHS must follow before that transfer arises from execution of the order, one of the acts Congress expressly placed beyond district-court review in 8 U.S.C. § 1252(g). Bringing the challenge for a class under the Administrative Procedure Act will make no difference. The Court will send the case back for dismissal.

That rule will apply across third-country destinations, both where DHS relies on diplomatic assurances and where it offers a fear screening after giving notice. The Court will leave open what process is due to a person facing persecution or torture in a particular country. The jurisdictional holding will also dispose of the classwide declarations and the order setting aside the guidance without deciding the independent reach of § 1252(f)(1). DHS proposed no additional question under the grant order’s fourth provision.

Alito’s opinions on the immigration review provisions in *Jennings* and the limits on lower-court relief in *Aleman Gonzalez* make him the likeliest author. I expect a single principal dissent from Sotomayor, who opposed the stay. She will reason that a person who accepts the removal order can still contest a policy that denies a meaningful opportunity to identify danger in a country first named after proceedings end. She will point to the experience of O.C.G. to explain why review of the original order cannot cure that injury.
