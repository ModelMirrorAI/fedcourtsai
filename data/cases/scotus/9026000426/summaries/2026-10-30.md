The Supreme Court agreed to decide whether a federal policy for deporting people to countries other than those in their removal orders is lawful.

## What happened

Four immigrants with final removal orders, identified by initials, sued the Department of Homeland Security over its policy for removing people to "third countries" not listed in their original orders. A federal district court in Massachusetts ruled for the immigrants, declaring the policy unlawful and setting it aside; the First Circuit mostly affirmed. After the First Circuit lifted its own pause on the ruling, the government again asked the Supreme Court to step in.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked to decide three questions: whether courts even have authority to hear this kind of case given limits Congress placed on reviewing immigration removal decisions; whether a court can issue nationwide-style declarations and set aside the policy for an entire class of people, given a law restricting broad orders in immigration cases; and whether the policy itself violates immigration statutes, due process protections, or treaty-based rules against sending people to countries where they may face torture or persecution.

## What each outcome would mean
