The Supreme Court is likely to decline to hear Martin Mizrahi's appeal, with
about a one-in-five chance that it agrees to review the case. He argues that
a jury, rather than a judge, should decide the facts supporting the criminal
forfeiture of his property. The Court's request for a government response and
support from outside legal groups make the petition worth watching. But lower
courts agree on the existing rule, and changing the Supreme Court's older
precedent remains a substantial hurdle.
