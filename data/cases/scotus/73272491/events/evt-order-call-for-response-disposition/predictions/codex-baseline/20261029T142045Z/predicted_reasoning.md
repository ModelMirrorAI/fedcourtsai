# Forecast of the Court's action

The Court will most likely **deny the petition**, after receiving the response
it requested from the United States. The request merits closer attention than
the government's waiver, but is not itself a commitment to review the case.
The absence of conflicting appellate rules and the continued force of Libretti
will leave this petition short of the votes needed for plenary review.

The record already has **one distribution**, for the June 18, 2026 conference,
and a June 15 response request. Expect at least one more distribution after
the response arrives or its deadline passes; P(further distribution) is 0.99.
That routine redistribution is not necessarily a true relist after conference
consideration. The likeliest path is denial at the first conference that can
consider the completed response-stage papers, rather than a long series of
substantive relists. From the June response-request vantage, a decision in
autumn 2026 is the central timing forecast, with extensions or further
consideration potentially moving it into early 2027.

Expect **no separate CVSG**: P(a new invitation for the Solicitor General's
views) is 0.005. The United States is already the respondent and will supply
its position through the requested response.

If review is granted, expect full briefing and argument, not an immediate
summary reversal or GVR. P(summary disposition | grant) is 0.07. The question
most likely taken is whether the Fifth and Sixth Amendments require a jury,
using proof beyond a reasonable doubt, to find facts establishing criminal
forfeiture, and whether Libretti must be overruled to that extent. That is a
more focused formulation of the petition's request to overrule Libretti, not
a vehicle for revisiting the conviction, all asset-forfeiture practices, or
the separate excessive-fines challenge. Any merits reasoning would likely
turn on the relationship between the jury-factfinding cases and Libretti;
there is no unconditional forecast of a merits opinion on the denial path.

On denial, expect **no separate writing** as the most likely outcome, but
assign a 0.22 conditional chance of a dissent or statement respecting denial.
Gorsuch is the likeliest author if a writing appears, given the related
jury-factfinding concerns recounted in the petition. An order-writing forecast
is not a forecast of individual votes to grant or deny review.
