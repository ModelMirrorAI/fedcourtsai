# Mizrahi v. United States, No. 25-1238: what the Court will do

**Disposition: the Court denies certiorari.** I put a grant at about 7%.

**Where the docket stands.** The petition was distributed once, for the June 18, 2026 conference. The United States waived its response on May 21. The Court then called for a response on June 15, after two amicus briefs supporting the petitioner had been filed. The record I was provisioned stops at that call. Live docket, read in forward mode: the government's time to respond was extended three times. It filed its brief in opposition on October 13, the petitioner replied on October 19, and on October 28 the petition was distributed for the November 13, 2026 conference.

**Further distributions.** The petition will be distributed again for November 13, past the single distribution my record shows. The harness masks that increment on a response-requested cell. I expect the Court to decide it at the first conference after the response, or after one or two relists if a Justice is writing separately. I would not read one or two relists as a sign of a grant here, since they are what a separate writing usually costs.

**No CVSG.** The United States is the respondent and has already filed its views.

**If granted.** The Court would take the question as written: whether *Libretti v. United States* should be overruled insofar as it held that the Sixth Amendment does not reach the facts behind criminal forfeiture. Read as a whole, that is whether *Apprendi* and *Southern Union* require a jury to find, beyond a reasonable doubt, the facts that set the amount of forfeiture. Plenary review would be the route. A GVR or summary disposition is very unlikely, because no intervening decision of the Court bears directly on the issue and the petitioner's request is an overruling. It would follow briefing and argument.

**Should it deny.** I give a separate writing roughly a one-in-four chance. The most likely is a dissent from denial or a statement respecting it by Justice Gorsuch, who has written on the jury right for restitution (*Hester*, *Rimlawi*) and has pressed the historical-practice argument the petition makes here. Justice Thomas, Justice Jackson or Justice Sotomayor could join or write. Someone at the Court asked for this response after the government waived, and that is consistent with one Justice wanting a full record before writing. With a dozen prior denials, no circuit split, and every circuit following *Libretti*, the most likely single outcome is still a plain denial.
