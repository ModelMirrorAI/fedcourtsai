## Disposition and Route
- **Predicted Disposition (`denied`, P(grant) = 0.045):** The Court will deny the petition for a writ of certiorari after receiving the Solicitor General's brief in opposition. Every federal court of appeals applies *Libretti v. United States*, 516 U.S. 29 (1995), as binding precedent, so there is no circuit conflict, and the Court has repeatedly denied certiorari on petitions asking to overrule *Libretti* under *Apprendi v. New Jersey*, 530 U.S. 466 (2000), and *Southern Union Co. v. United States*, 567 U.S. 343 (2012). In addition, the decision below is a brief, non-precedential Second Circuit summary order, and the factual record is complicated by multi-object money-laundering counts involving both wire/bank fraud and narcotics proceeds.
- **Question Presented if Granted:** If the Court were to grant certiorari, it would take the single question presented as written—whether *Libretti v. United States* should be overruled insofar as it holds that the Sixth Amendment jury-trial right does not apply to criminal forfeiture determinations.
- **Summary Disposition vs. Plenary Review (`summary-disposition-route = 0.15`):** Conditional on a grant, plenary review (85%) is far more likely than a summary disposition in the cert order (15%). The Court does not overrule its own precedent via summary reversal without merits briefing and oral argument, and there is no pending merits case on criminal forfeiture for which this petition would be held and GVR'd; a GVR would occur only in the unlikely event that the Solicitor General confesses a case-specific forfeiture calculation error in the brief in opposition.
- **Conditional Merits Ground if Granted:** If granted for plenary review, the Court would address whether *Libretti*'s characterization of criminal forfeiture as an indeterminate sentencing element rather than a determinate punishment survives *Southern Union*, *Alleyne v. United States*, 570 U.S. 99 (2013), and *Erlinger v. United States*, 602 U.S. 821 (2024).
