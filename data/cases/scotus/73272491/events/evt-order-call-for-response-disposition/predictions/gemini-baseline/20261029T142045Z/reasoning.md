# Certiorari Prediction: *Martin Mizrahi v. United States* (No. 25-1238)

## I. Overview and Procedural Posture

Petitioner Martin Mizrahi seeks a writ of certiorari to the United States Court of Appeals for the Second Circuit, which affirmed his convictions and sentence—including a $4,545,704 criminal forfeiture money judgment under 18 U.S.C. § 982(a)(1)—by unpublished summary order on November 7, 2025, and denied rehearing on January 30, 2026. The single question presented is whether the Court should overrule *Libretti v. United States*, 516 U.S. 29 (1995), insofar as *Libretti* stated that "the right to a jury verdict on forfeitability does not fall within the Sixth Amendment's constitutional protection" (*id.* at 49), and hold instead that criminal forfeiture is subject to the Fifth and Sixth Amendment jury-finding rule of *Apprendi v. New Jersey*, 530 U.S. 466 (2000), and *Southern Union Co. v. United States*, 567 U.S. 343 (2012).

This forecast is taken at the `response-requested` moment (`evt-order-call-for-response-disposition`, cutoff `2026-06-16`). After the petition was docketed on April 30, 2026, the Solicitor General waived the United States' right to respond on May 21, 2026, and the petition was distributed on May 27, 2026 for the June 11, 2026 conference (`distribution_count = 1`). On June 15, 2026, the Court called for a response (CFR), due July 15, 2026.

## III. Case-Specific Analysis

### A. Certworthiness of the Question Presented
- **Complete Absence of a Circuit Split:** Petitioner candidly concedes that "there is no split among the lower courts" (Pet. 26). Every federal circuit to address the question holds that *Libretti* remains binding Supreme Court precedent under *Rodriguez de Quijas v. Shearson/American Express, Inc.*, 490 U.S. 477, 484 (1989), and that *Southern Union* did not implicitly overrule *Libretti*. While a circuit split is technically impossible when lower courts are bound by directly on-point Supreme Court precedent, the Court rarely grants certiorari to overrule its own precedent without either strong percolation of competing rationales or a clear four-Justice appetite to overrule.
- **Doctrinal Tension vs. Stare Decisis:** Petitioner makes a coherent doctrinal and historical argument (represented by experienced Supreme Court counsel at Winston & Strawn) that *Libretti*'s Sixth Amendment language rested on pre-*Apprendi* cases (*McMillan*, *Cabana*, *Spaziano*) that have since been overruled, that criminal forfeiture is a punitive fine under *Bajakajian* and *Ellingburg*, and that founding-era English and American practice required juries to specify forfeited property. That tension is almost certainly why a chambers (most likely Justice Gorsuch's) called for a response after the SG waived.
- **Practical and Institutional Headwinds:** Overruling *Libretti* would upend criminal forfeiture practice nationwide across thousands of federal prosecutions annually and conflict with Federal Rule of Criminal Procedure 32.2(b)(1)(A) & (b)(5), which governs judicial nexus determinations and limits the jury's role to specific property nexus upon party request rather than money judgment amounts. Moreover, several Circuits (e.g., *United States v. Phillips*, 704 F.3d 754, 770 (9th Cir. 2012); *United States v. Fruchter*, 411 F.3d 377, 382–83 (2d Cir. 2005)) have defended *Libretti* on the independent post-*Apprendi* ground that criminal forfeiture statutes authorize forfeiture of all property "involved in" the offense and therefore impose no determinate statutory maximum that judicial factfinding exceeds. As repeated denials in *Hester*, *Rimlawi*, and *Esformes* show, there have not been four votes to extend *Apprendi* to restitution or criminal forfeiture.

### B. Vehicle Assessment
Even if four Justices were inclined to revisit *Libretti* in an appropriate case, *Mizrahi* presents significant vehicle complications that the Solicitor General's brief in opposition will highlight:
1. **Unpublished Summary Order Below:** The Second Circuit disposed of the appeal in a short, non-precedential summary order that devoted a single paragraph (App. 5a) to the *Libretti* issue, simply noting that "[a]s Mizrahi concedes, the law in this Circuit is clear."
2. **Fact-Bound Dispute Over Multi-Object Money Laundering Counts:** Much of the petition (Pet. 6–15) is devoted to arguing that Mizrahi is an autistic, overly trusting businessman who was duped by co-conspirators Zubaid and Goran and did not knowingly launder Mexican drug-cartel proceeds, and that Counts 4 and 5 were duplicitous because they alleged three specified unlawful activities (wire fraud, bank fraud, and narcotics trafficking). The Second Circuit held that the trial record amply supported the finding that Mizrahi knowingly laundered $3,855,704 in narcotics proceeds and $690,000 in fraud proceeds.
3. **Harmlessness / Partial overlap with Jury Verdict:** The jury convicted Mizrahi on Counts 1–3 (wire and bank fraud conspiracy and substantive counts) as well as Counts 4–5 (money laundering) and Count 7 (conspiracy to operate an unlicensed money transmitting business). At least as to the $690,000 in LV.Net bank-fraud/wire-fraud funds, the jury's guilty verdicts on the fraud counts directly tied those funds to the offenses of conviction, and the SG will argue that any *Apprendi* error as to forfeitability or nexus was harmless on the trial record under *Washington v. Recuenco*, 548 U.S. 212 (2006), and *Erlinger*, 602 U.S. at 850 (Roberts, C.J., concurring).

## IV. Decomposition and Claim Probabilities

1. **`disposition` (`probability = 0.045`, `predicted_disposition = "denied"`, `granted = false`):**
   - Starting from the `baseline` band's ~0.4%–0.7% base rate, the fact that this is a paid petition with experienced counsel and that the Court called for a response raises the probability substantially above the raw first-distribution baseline. However, given the unbroken line of denials on the exact question (*Esformes* et al.), the lack of a four-Justice coalition in the closely related restitution cases (*Hester*, *Rimlawi*), and the fact-bound vehicle complications, a grant remains unlikely. I estimate a **4.5% (0.045)** probability of a grant-family disposition (`granted` or `gvr`), and **95.5%** probability of denial.

2. **`relist-increment` (`probability = 0.99`):**
   - Because the Court called for a response on June 15, 2026 (with the response due July 15, 2026), the petition was taken off the June 11 conference list and will be redistributed for a future conference (likely the OT2026 Long Conference or an autumn conference after any extensions) once the SG's brief in opposition is filed. Thus, barring a rare voluntary dismissal before redistribution, `distribution_count` is virtually certain to rise above its frozen value of 1 (note also that `relist-increment` is moment-vacuous at `response-requested`).

3. **`cvsg-increment` (`probability = 0.0`):**
   - The United States is the respondent, so the Court will not issue a Call for the Views of the Solicitor General (CVSG).

4. **`summary-disposition-route` (`probability = 0.15`):**
   - Conditional on the petition being granted (`granted` or `gvr`), the question presented asks the Court to overrule its own precedent (*Libretti v. United States*). The Court almost never overrules one of its own decisions by summary reversal without merits briefing and oral argument, and there is no pending merits case on criminal forfeiture for which *Mizrahi* would be held and GVR'd. The only plausible non-plenary grant route would be a GVR if the Solicitor General confessed some narrow sentencing/forfeiture error in the BIO, which is unlikely given the defense of the judgment in the Second Circuit. Accordingly, conditional on a grant-family outcome, I assign **0.15** to a cert-order summary disposition (`gvr` or `summary-reversal`) and **0.85** to plenary review. (This puts roughly 3.8% unconditional probability on plenary grant and 0.7% on GVR.)

5. **`dissent-from-denial` (`probability = 0.07`):**
   - Conditional on certiorari being denied, there is a meaningful chance—well above the ~1% unconditional order-list rate—that Justice Gorsuch (potentially joined by Justice Sotomayor or Justice Thomas) authors a dissent from the denial of certiorari or a statement respecting denial, just as he did in *Hester v. United States*, 586 U.S. 1104 (2019), *Rimlawi v. United States*, 145 S. Ct. 518 (2025), and *Burnett v. United States*, 146 S. Ct. 875 (2026). Indeed, the call for a response after the SG's waiver was very likely triggered by Justice Gorsuch's chambers examining the *Libretti* question. Still, most CFRs do not ultimately produce a published dissent from denial once the BIO is circulated (for example, *Esformes* was denied without noted dissent), so I place the conditional probability of a noted dissent or statement respecting denial at **7.0% (0.07)**.

6. **`order-writers` (distribution claim):**
   - Reflecting the per-Justice likelihood of authoring a separate writing on the cert order (primarily a dissent from or statement respecting denial), Justice Gorsuch is the clear lead candidate (`0.065`), followed by Justice Sotomayor (`0.020`, who authored *Southern Union* and joined Justice Gorsuch in *Hester*), Justice Thomas (`0.015`, longtime proponent of an expansive originalist reading of the Sixth Amendment in *Apprendi* and *Erlinger*), and Justice Jackson (`0.008`), with minimal probabilities (`0.001`–`0.002`) for the remaining Justices.
