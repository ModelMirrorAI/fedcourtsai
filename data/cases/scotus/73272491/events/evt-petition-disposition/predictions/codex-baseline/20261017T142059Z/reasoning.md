## Anchor and adjustment

The committed `metrics/statpack.md` and `metrics/statpack.json` publish matching sal-v4 bands. Their last commit is dated October 13, 2026 (cf9ae960e); that is artifact vintage, not a verified corpus-wide pull watermark. I made no live corpus query and do not claim a current corpus census.

For the anchor I pooled every displayed strictly prior docket-number Term, 2017 through 2024, excluding 2025 and 2026. Summing `prefix_est_grant_rate * prefix_weighted_resolved` for baseline and dividing by the summed denominator gives 593 / 11,580 = 5.1209%. This uses bracketed reached rates, not the much lower terminal-baseline rates. The whole modern-cert disposition section, which mixes paid and IFP petitions, is background rather than this selected cell's anchor.

I increase that anchor to **20%**. The most important affirmative signal is the Court's June 15 request for a response after the government waived. That is a concrete act of attention not captured by the baseline label. Two supporting amicus filings, from former federal judges and a coalition of legal organizations, reinforce the petition's institutional importance, although their substantive arguments were not supplied and I do not attribute unreviewed arguments to them.

The substantive question is serious: the petition asks the Court to reconsider Libretti's treatment of jury findings in criminal forfeiture in light of Apprendi and Southern Union (QP; petition pp. 15–24). Erlinger v. United States, 602 U.S. 821 (2024), independently checked through CourtListener opinion 11066653, requires jury resolution of the ACCA separate-occasions inquiry beyond a reasonable doubt. It supplies a relevant analogy for continued enforcement of jury-finding protections, not a holding about forfeiture. The petition's argument that only the Supreme Court can remove an existing Supreme Court obstacle makes the absence of a split less damaging than in an ordinary statutory disagreement.

The vehicle has some affirmative strengths. The petition identifies a sentencing objection preserving the constitutional issue, a roughly $4.55 million forfeiture based on judicial findings, and a general verdict allegedly insufficient to establish the entire forfeitable amount (petition pp. 9–13). The appended Second Circuit decision actually addresses the jury-finding argument and relies on Libretti and circuit precedent (App. 5a). This is not merely a constitutional question introduced for the first time in the cert petition.

Nevertheless, denial remains the modal outcome. The petition expressly acknowledges no circuit split (pp. 26–27); the unpublished decision below applies existing authority rather than announces a conflicting rule. The statutory-maximum distinction addressed by the petition remains a substantive obstacle, not something the forfeiture-as-punishment analogy automatically resolves. The appendix also describes substantial evidence supporting the forfeiture and rejects the factual challenge (App. 6a); I do not adopt the petition's account of evidentiary weakness as an established fact. An eventual harmless-error or remedy objection is possible, but without the BIO I cannot assert that the government has made one or that it would succeed. These considerations keep the estimate far below an even chance despite the response request.

## Other probabilities and stakes

The statpack's paid-segment relist and CVSG cuts show that terminal multi-distribution and CVSG populations have much higher grant rates, but they are not forward transition probabilities. I do not convert them into a measured hazard for this cell. Here, 96% for another distribution reflects the unfinished requested-briefing cycle; it is compatible with a 20% grant chance because redistribution need not signal sustained merits interest. The 0.3% CVSG estimate reflects the United States' existing respondent role. The 4% summary-route estimate is conditional on a grant and reflects the need to revisit controlling precedent. The 18% separate-writing estimate is conditional on denial and allows for the jury-rights concerns illustrated by the petition's discussion of Hester and Rimlawi (pp. 19, 22), while still predicting an unexplained denial.

The 0.62 stakes score concerns the potential national effect on criminal-forfeiture procedure, not the likelihood of review. I omit individual votes because this record does not support a useful cert-vote lineup.
