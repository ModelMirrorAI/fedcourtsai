# Predicted Court Reasoning and Procedural Trajectory

## Procedural Trajectory: Relists and CVSG

The docket currently records **one distribution** (distributed on October 21, 2026, for the Conference of November 6, 2026) and **no CVSG**.

The Court will likely **deny certiorari without further relisting** at the November 6, 2026 conference (`P(relist-increment) = 0.12`). While both respondents (the Tibbs plaintiffs and the court-appointed Receiver, Peter D. Protopapas) waived their right to respond on October 15, 2026, and no request for a response has issued as of the snapshot date, there is a modest chance of a single administrative or synchronizing relist alongside the vide companion petition (*Altrad Investment Authority S.A.S., et al. v. Cape Intermediate Holdings Limited, et al.*, No. 26-350, also distributed for November 6, 2026) or to check the status of the pending Third Circuit petitions in *Protopapas v. Whittaker Clark & Daniels, Inc.* (No. 26-159) and *Official Committee of Talc Claimants v. Whittaker, Clark & Daniels Inc.* (No. 26-263). However, because the South Carolina Supreme Court already denied certiorari in the closely analogous predecessor dispute earlier this year (*Atlas Turner, Inc. v. Welch*, No. 25-213, cert. denied Jan. 12, 2026) and expressly narrowed the pre-judgment receivership here so that it does not govern corporate internal affairs, the Court is unlikely to call for a response or engage in extended multi-conference relisting.

The Court will **not call for the views of the Solicitor General** (`P(cvsg-increment) = 0.005`). Although petitioners invoke foreign-affairs preemption and an anti-suit injunction entered by the High Court of Justice of England and Wales, the dispute arises out of state-court tort and alter-ego litigation between private parties, and the foreign-affairs argument was held unpreserved below.

## Questions Presented and Conditional Merits Posture

If the Court were to grant review, it would take the single Question Presented in the petition—whether territorial limits under the Due Process Clause, the Commerce Clause, and principles of federalism prohibit a state-court receiver from controlling the litigation decisions and internal affairs of a foreign corporation when the receiver has not been recognized in the corporation's place of incorporation—likely consolidated with the companion petition in No. 26-350.

Were the Court to reach the merits, a majority would first have to confront severe threshold obstacles: appellate standing to assert the rights of a non-appealing foreign corporation (Cape Intermediate Holdings Limited), finality under 28 U.S.C. § 1257(a) given that the trial court has not yet adjudicated personal jurisdiction or the merits of the third-party alter-ego claims, and preservation of the federal constitutional claims. On the substantive question, if reached, the Court would likely address whether authorizing a pre-judgment receiver to pursue insurance assets and third-party alter-ego claims in a pending state tort action constitutes impermissible extraterritorial regulation of corporate internal affairs or remains within traditional state equity power to prevent fraudulent dissipation or concealment of assets responsive to an in-state tort claim.

## Summary Disposition vs. Plenary Review

Conditional on a grant, **plenary review is substantially more likely than a summary disposition** (`P(summary-disposition-route) = 0.15`). No intervening Supreme Court decision warrants a GVR, and the threshold jurisdictional and preservation disputes make summary reversal on the papers improbable. The small conditional probability of a summary route reflects only the remote possibility of a hold followed by a GVR if the Court were to grant and resolve the pending *Whittaker, Clark & Daniels* petitions (Nos. 26-159 and 26-263) on broad constitutional grounds.

## Separate Writings Respecting Denial

Upon denial of certiorari, **no dissent from denial or statement respecting denial is expected** (`P(dissent-from-denial) = 0.02`). The Court denied certiorari in *Atlas Turner, Inc. v. Welch* (No. 25-213) without any recorded dissent earlier in 2026, and the interlocutory, vehicle-encumbered posture of this appeal makes a separate writing unlikely.
