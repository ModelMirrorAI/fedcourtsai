# Predicted course: Charter Consolidated v. Cape Intermediate Holdings (No. 26-328)

**Where the docket stands.** One distribution so far, for the November 6, 2026 conference. Both respondent groups (the receiver acting for Cape Intermediate Holdings, and the Tibbs plaintiffs) waived their right to respond. Three amicus briefs support the petition: Washington Legal Foundation, Atlantic Legal Foundation, and National Union Fire Insurance. Each was filed jointly with the companion petition from the same judgment, No. 26-350 (Altrad), which is also distributed for November 6. There is no CVSG.

**Further distributions: likely (about 85%).** I do not expect the petition to be decided on November 6. A paid petition from a state court of last resort, filed by experienced Supreme Court counsel, backed by three amici, alleging a direct split, and met with a waiver is exactly the profile where the Court calls for a response. Once a response is called for, the case is redistributed after the brief in opposition comes in. Separately, the circuit decision the petition says it conflicts with, *In re Whittaker Clark & Daniels* (3d Cir. 2026), is itself before the Court on two pending petitions (Nos. 26-159 and 26-263). The receiver here is the petitioner in 26-159, and neither of those petitions had been distributed at the last corpus poll. So the Court has a strong reason to keep this case alive until it can consider the whole set together, either by calling for a response and rescheduling or, later, by holding it. I expect at least one more distribution and plausibly several. Even if review is denied, I expect that denial in winter 2026–27, not on November 6.

**CVSG: unlikely (about 8%).** No federal party is involved and the federal interest is indirect. The best argument for a CVSG is the foreign-relations angle: a U.K. court has enjoined the receiver. Still, the Court usually decides state-court-of-last-resort cases of this kind without asking the Solicitor General.

**Disposition: denial is the single most likely outcome, but grant routes are real (P(any grant) ≈ 0.18).** There are two grant paths:
1. **Plenary review**, of this case or of 26-328 and 26-350 together with the Third Circuit vehicle. The Court would take the question presented essentially as written: whether due process, Commerce Clause, and federalism limits bar a state-court receiver from controlling an out-of-state corporation's litigation when the place of incorporation has not recognized the receiver. It would most likely set aside the separate foreign-commerce and preemption strands.
2. **Hold, then GVR.** If the Court grants the Third Circuit vehicle instead and holds this case, it would GVR this case if it rules against the receiver's authority and deny it if it rules for the receiver.

**Summary route if granted: about 40%.** Most of the grant probability through the hold path ends in a GVR. Through the plenary path it does not. A summary reversal on this record is unlikely.

**Vehicle risks pointing toward denial.** First, finality under 28 U.S.C. § 1257: trial on the merits remains, and the petition relies on the *Cox Broadcasting* / *Langdeau* "anterior and separate" exception. Second, the Court may prefer the federal-court Whittaker vehicle. Third, the waivers signal that respondents do not see the case as cert-worthy, but a response request would neutralize that.

**Dissent from denial: unlikely (about 5%).** A denial here would most likely be a vehicle denial, possibly one that coincides with action on the Whittaker petitions, and I would not expect any separate writing.
