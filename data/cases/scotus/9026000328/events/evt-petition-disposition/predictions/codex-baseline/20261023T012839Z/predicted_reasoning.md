# Forecast

The Court will most likely deny certiorari without a separate writing. The record already shows one distribution, on October 21, 2026, for the November 6 conference, and no CVSG. I expect no further distribution; if the petition is distributed again, one additional conference is more likely than an extended sequence of relists. I do not expect a new CVSG.

The asserted conflict over a receiver's control of a foreign corporation will not, most likely, overcome the vehicle's complications. The Court will leave the South Carolina proceedings undisturbed at this stage rather than decide whether the limited prejudgment receivership is equivalent to the control over a corporate bankruptcy filing addressed in the Third Circuit litigation.

If review is granted, the natural question is a narrower version of the petition's single question: whether a state court may authorize a receiver to accept service and prosecute claims for a corporation incorporated elsewhere, without recognition in the incorporation jurisdiction. The Court is less likely to undertake an unrestricted review of every aspect of foreign receiverships or decide all three constitutional theories independently.

Conditional on any grant, plenary review is more likely than a disposition in the certiorari order: I assign 30% to the latter route. A hold followed by a GVR tied to another receivership case is a plausible alternative, but the provisioned materials establish no intervening Supreme Court decision requiring that result. An immediate summary reversal is less likely than either plenary review or such a GVR.

Conditional on denial, I assign 8% to a noted dissent or statement respecting denial. The modal outcome is an unsigned denial with no Justice separately identified; I do not forecast a particular author or cert vote.
