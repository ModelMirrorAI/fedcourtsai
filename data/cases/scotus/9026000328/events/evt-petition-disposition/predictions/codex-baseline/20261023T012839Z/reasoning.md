# Why 18% rather than 5% or 50%

The strongest upward adjustment is a concrete, identified disagreement between a state supreme court and a federal court of appeals about the same receiver's authority over out-of-state corporations. The petition's discussion of Whittaker, read against the appendix at 29a–30a, establishes a serious asserted conflict rather than just a general claim that the judgment is wrong. An English corporation, an English injunction described in the petition, and litigation decisions made without the corporation's own board sharpen the territorial and international-comity stakes. Three amici, including an insurer, also suggest effects beyond the immediate tort claim. These features justify a material departure above the ordinary private-petitioner floor.

The conflict is nonetheless contestable. The state court expressly narrowed the receiver's authority to insurance recovery and claims connected to the pending lawsuit, while describing Whittaker as a postjudgment receivership involving a New Jersey corporation's bankruptcy filing. Its account treats parts of the broader constitutional discussion as unnecessary to that decision. I cannot equate the two holdings merely because the petition calls the split direct. The internal-affairs characterization is itself disputed: the lower court treats the remaining claims as external litigation, whereas petitioners treat control of litigation as a board function. This distinction supplies a plausible reason to deny without resolving the petition's broad constitutional formulation.

There is also a substantial vehicle discount. The underlying third-party claims remain pending. The petition invokes finality exceptions under section 1257, including Mercantile National Bank and Cox Broadcasting, but the asserted exception is an argument, not a verified jurisdictional entitlement. A Court interested in territorial receivership limits could prefer another case or a later final judgment. Multiple related petitions may facilitate eventual coordinated review, but their mere existence does not establish a grant, an intervening rule, or a particular lead vehicle. I did not seek their later outcomes.

Finally, one distribution following waivers is not an affirmative signal comparable to a requested response, CVSG, or repeated consideration. The current posture leaves room for a response request before serious review, but does not establish that one will occur. On balance, I assign **18% to any grant**, including a GVR or summary reversal, and predict denial. Roughly, my internal allocation is 82% no grant, 12.6% plenary grant, and 5.4% grant resolved in the cert order. The latter two sum to 18%; their ratio produces the separately elicited 30% conditional summary-route claim.
