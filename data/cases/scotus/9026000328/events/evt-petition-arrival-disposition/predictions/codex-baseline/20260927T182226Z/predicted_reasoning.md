# Forecast of the Court's action

The Court will most likely **deny certiorari**, leaving the South Carolina decision in place without deciding the federal constitutional issue. I assign 22% to any grant, including summary relief. The likely obstacle is the combination of an incompletely resolved state proceeding and distinctions between a litigation-specific prejudgment receivership and the bankruptcy-board control considered in Whittaker, despite the substantial interstate and international concerns alleged in the petition.

The baseline shows **zero distributions** and **no CVSG**. I expect an initial distribution after the response process, followed most likely by denial without a further conventional relist. Thus the modal forecast is one additional distribution relative to this record, not one additional conference after an already completed conference. I assign 98% to at least one distribution beyond the frozen zero; a hold for related litigation or one or more subsequent distributions is a plausible alternative, not the central forecast.

I expect **no call for the Solicitor General's views** (12% probability of a new CVSG). If the Court does request views, I expect it during initial consideration after the response process, because of the asserted international conflict and federal interest in foreign commerce, rather than because a CVSG is already signaled by the record.

If review is granted, I expect the Court to take the single question substantially as presented but focus it on whether a state-appointed receiver can displace an unrecognized foreign corporation's board in accepting service and prosecuting litigation-related claims. I do not expect a grant to invite resolution of every possible extraterritorial asset-administration power. On the grant branch, the likely substantive direction is a limitation on unilateral displacement of the foreign board's litigation authority, with the scope of ordinary local asset protection left distinct.

**Conditional on any grant, plenary review is more likely than a cert-order disposition:** 65% versus 35%. A hold followed by a GVR in light of a later ruling in a related receivership case is the strongest summary-route alternative. I do not forecast an unconditional GVR or assume any related case has been granted.

If certiorari is denied, I expect **no noted dissent or statement respecting denial**; I assign 8% to the existence of such a writing conditional on denial. No particular Justice is forecast to write.
