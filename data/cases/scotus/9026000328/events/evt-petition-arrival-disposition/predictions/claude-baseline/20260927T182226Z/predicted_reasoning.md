# Forecast: what the Court will do with No. 26-328

**Disposition.** The petition will most likely be **denied** (P(any grant) = 0.20).
The Court will not act on it in isolation: it arrives as one of four petitions
about the same South Carolina receiver, Peter Protopapas, and the Court will
dispose of them as a set. Nos. 26-159 (the receiver's own petition from the
Third Circuit's *Whittaker* decision, fully briefed as of September 4, 2026) and
26-263 (the talc claimants' petition from the same decision, response now due
November 6, 2026) attack the Third Circuit from the receiver's side; this
petition and its companion No. 26-350 (the Altrad petitioners, Davis Polk,
filed the same day and cross-referenced "Vide" on both dockets) attack the
South Carolina Supreme Court from the corporations' side. Expect the Court to
hold whichever of these is ready first until all four have been briefed, and
then to resolve them at one conference in early 2027.

**Distributions and relists.** The docket shows **zero distributions** — the
petition was docketed September 10, 2026 and the response is due October 13.
I forecast the response will be extended once (to mid-November), a reply will
follow, and the petition will first be distributed for a conference in
**late December 2026 or January 2027**. It will be considered together with
26-350 and, very likely, with 26-159 and 26-263. I expect it to be **relisted
at least once** — either because the set is held for the last of the four to
become ready, or because the Court is weighing which vehicle to take — with a
final order by roughly **February–March 2027**. If instead the Court grants
26-159 to review *Whittaker* on the merits, this petition will be held for the
decision in that case and disposed of at the end of OT2026 (June 2027).

**CVSG.** No call for the views of the Solicitor General (P = 0.07). The
petition's foreign-affairs framing (an English company, a High Court injunction
the receiver has defied) is the one hook a CVSG could hang on, but the Court did
not seek the government's views on the same receiver in *Atlas Turner v. Welch*
(No. 25-213) last Term, no federal statute or program is implicated, and the
United States is not a party in any of the four dockets.

**Which question, if taken.** If the Court grants, it would take the question
presented as written or lightly reformulated toward the Due Process Clause:
whether a state court may vest a receiver with authority over the litigation
decisions of a corporation organized elsewhere, absent recognition in the place
of incorporation. It would not reach the dormant Commerce Clause or
foreign-affairs strands independently. The likeliest grant shape is a
**consolidated plenary grant with No. 26-350**, with the Whittaker petitions
held. A less likely grant shape (roughly a third of the grant mass) is a
**hold followed by a GVR**: the Court grants 26-159, decides *Whittaker*, and
then vacates the South Carolina judgment for reconsideration — the
`summary-disposition-route` claim (P = 0.35 conditional on a grant) prices
that route. A summary reversal is unlikely: the state court's opinion is long,
the record is contested, and the finality question would make a per curiam
awkward.

**Why denial is still the base case.** The Court denied *Atlas Turner*
(No. 25-213), a paid petition against the same receiver and the same trial
judge, on January 12, 2026 with no noted dissent (Justice Alito took no part),
after calling for a response and with three business amici on file. The
petition here is stronger — an express refusal by the state court to follow a
federal circuit, a receiver controlling litigation decisions rather than only
insurance assets, and an outstanding foreign injunction — but it is also
interlocutory: trial on the receiver's third-party claims against petitioners
remains to be completed on remand, and the respondent will lead with 28 U.S.C.
§ 1257 finality under *Cox Broadcasting*. The Court more often declines such
petitions and waits for a final judgment.

**Dissent from denial.** If denied, I expect **no separate writing**
(P(some dissent or statement respecting denial) = 0.15). The *Atlas Turner*
denial drew none. The strongest chance of a statement is a short one — from
Justice Kavanaugh or Justice Gorsuch — flagging the receivership practice while
agreeing the interlocutory posture is a poor vehicle; that is a minority
scenario.

**Conditional merits note.** If the Court grants and reaches the merits, the
likely holding is a narrow due-process / federalism rule that a state court's
receivership order cannot, of its own force, displace an out-of-state
corporation's board over litigation decisions absent recognition in the state
or country of incorporation — reversing or vacating the South Carolina judgment.
Justice Alito's recusal in *Atlas Turner* may recur here (ESAB Corporation is a
public petitioner), which would leave eight Justices.
