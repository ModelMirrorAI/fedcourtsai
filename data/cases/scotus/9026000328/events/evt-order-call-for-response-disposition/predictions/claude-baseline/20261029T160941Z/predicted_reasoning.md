# Forecast: Charter Consolidated v. Cape Intermediate Holdings (No. 26-328)

**State of the docket.** The petition was distributed once, for the November 6, 2026
conference, and on October 27 the Court called for a response, due November 27. Both
respondents had waived. That takes the petition off the November 6 conference. No CVSG
is on the docket. Three amicus briefs support review: Washington Legal Foundation,
Atlantic Legal Foundation and National Union Fire Insurance. Each was filed in both this
case and the Vide companion, No. 26-350 (Altrad Investment Authority v. Cape). The
companion's docket matches this one step for step, including the October 27 call for a
response.

**Relists.** The petition will be distributed again once the response is filed,
probably for a conference in mid-to-late December 2026 or in January 2027. I expect
it to be considered together with No. 26-350. I also expect it to be considered
alongside the two Third Circuit petitions on the other side of the split: No. 26-159
(Protopapas, the receiver, v. Whittaker Clark & Daniels) and No. 26-263 (Official
Committee of Talc Claimants v. Whittaker). The Court may reschedule it so that all four
petitions reach the same conference. More than one further distribution is likely
(reschedules or relists) before a final order.

**Most likely outcome: denial (about 73%).** If the Court takes up this question at
all, the Third Circuit bankruptcy case is a competing vehicle. This petition comes from
an interlocutory state-court posture, with a trial on the merits still pending. That
raises a 28 U.S.C. § 1257 finality question under Cox Broadcasting, which the
respondents can be expected to press. That question makes this petition a weaker lead
vehicle than its stakes suggest.

**Grant paths (about 27% combined):**
- **Plenary grant of this petition, possibly consolidated with 26-350.** This is the
  likelier grant path if the Court wants the question decided under the Constitution in
  a state-court case rather than inside a bankruptcy.
- **Hold for a granted Whittaker petition, then GVR.** This path counts as a grant if
  the Court decides Whittaker on territorial or internal-affairs grounds that undercut
  the South Carolina decision. A hold followed by denial is equally possible.

Given a grant, I put roughly even odds on disposal in the cert order itself (a GVR after
a hold) versus briefing and argument.

**CVSG.** A CVSG is unlikely but not negligible (about 12%). Foreign-commerce and
foreign-relations concerns, including the U.K. injunction against the receiver, give
the United States an interest. Still, the Court can resolve the question without the
Solicitor General's views, and any CVSG would more likely come in the Third Circuit
cases.

**Question the Court would take.** If granted, the Court would take the question as
written, likely narrowed to the Due Process and internal-affairs ground (Edgar v. MITE,
Fuld). It would probably not reach the dormant foreign Commerce Clause theory.

**Separate writing on a denial.** None expected (about 6%). If anyone writes, the most
likely writers are Justices Gorsuch or Thomas on territorial limits on state courts.
