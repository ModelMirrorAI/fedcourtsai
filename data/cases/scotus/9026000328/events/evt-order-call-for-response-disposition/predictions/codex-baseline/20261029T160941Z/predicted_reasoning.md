# Forecast of the Court's action

The Court will most likely deny certiorari after receiving the requested response. The forecast is P(any grant) = 0.18; denial is the modal disposition. The Court is unlikely to publish reasons for declining review.

The provisioned docket shows **one distribution**, on October 21, 2026, for the November 6 conference, followed by a call for a response on October 27, due November 27. There is no CVSG or filed opposition in this record. The response request should interrupt consideration on the original conference schedule. I expect at least one further distribution after the response and reply opportunity, with denial most likely in January–March 2027 absent extensions or a hold. P(at least one additional distribution) is 0.99. This is primarily redistribution after a response request, not a prediction that the Court will discuss the petition and then repeatedly relist it; I do not expect multiple substantive relists. The redistribution claim is masked at this moment by the harness.

I expect **no invitation to the Solicitor General**; P(a new CVSG) = 0.10. The English-company and foreign-commerce features make federal input conceivable, but the operative dispute concerns the authority of a litigation-specific receiver, not administration of a federal program. The Court can decline review on vehicle grounds without obtaining the Executive's views.

The strongest route to review is the alleged conflict between South Carolina's receivership ruling and the Third Circuit's Whittaker decision concerning corporate litigation authority. If review is granted, I expect the Court to narrow the petition's single question to whether a state court may authorize a receiver to make litigation decisions for a foreign corporation without recognition in its place of incorporation. I do not expect a holding invalidating all out-of-state receiverships or all local proceedings involving foreign corporations.

Conditional on a grant, **plenary review is more likely than disposition in the cert order**: P(summary-disposition route | grant) = 0.15. The restricted receivership, differences from the bankruptcy decision, and disputed federal preservation call for briefing rather than a summary reversal. No provisioned intervening Supreme Court decision supplies an identified GVR trigger. The other petitions mentioned in the petition create a possible hold-and-remand route, but I do not predict that any of those petitions will be granted.

Conditional on denial, I expect no noted dissent or statement; P(any such dissent or statement | denial) = 0.08. If a Justice writes, Gorsuch, Thomas, and Alito are my most plausible individual writers on the territorial-power issue, though each has a very small unconditional probability. The independent order-writer probabilities in prediction.json are unconditional on disposition and do not constitute a predicted vote lineup.

If granted and decided on the merits, the likely dispute would be the boundary between legitimate local claims administration and impermissible displacement of foreign corporate governance. That is conditional context, not a forecast that the Court will reach the merits or issue a constitutional opinion.
