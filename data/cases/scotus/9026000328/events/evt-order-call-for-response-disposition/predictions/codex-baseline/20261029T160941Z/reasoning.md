## Why move above the prior

The petition's single question challenges territorial limits on state judicial power under due process, commerce, and federalism, rather than asking only for correction of an asbestos damages award. Petition pp. 14–22 describes an express disagreement with a published Third Circuit ruling about whether a South Carolina receiver can displace a foreign corporation's litigation decisions. The English injunction and the competing claims to control the company make the dispute more consequential than a routine local receivership. The requested response on both this docket and 26-350 is a modest affirmative attention signal after waivers, and three amici reinforce the possibility that the issue has importance beyond these parties. Those considerations justify a substantial upward move from 5.1%.

## Why stop at 18%, rather than predict review

1. **The operative order is narrower than the broadest petition framing.** Appendix 27a–30a limits the receiver to insurance assets and claims capable of producing assets responsive to this particular litigation, and distinguishes bankruptcy control from these local claims. The Third Circuit conflict is therefore a serious contention, not an established identical-question split. I checked the operative appellate passages rather than treating the original appointment's broad language as the final scope.

2. **Federal preservation and whose rights are asserted are contested in the actual orders.** The August 25 stay/recall order, appendix 48a–49a, expressly questions preservation of the commerce and due-process theories and petitioners' assertion of another corporation's due-process rights. It specifically states that the exclusive-federal-foreign-relations theory was not previously presented. This does not establish that every federal theory is barred—the petition describes earlier constitutional objections, pp. 10–11—but makes a clean merits vehicle less certain. The missing portions of the briefs below prevent a definitive resolution here.

3. **Finality is a live threshold, not a conclusively absent jurisdictional basis.** The third-party claims remain for trial. Petition pp. 1–2 and 35 invokes Mercantile National Bank v. Langdeau and Cox Broadcasting for immediate review of a separable, finally settled threshold issue. That is a reasoned response to the problem, not a guarantee that the Court will accept it. I discount, rather than categorically exclude, review on this basis.

4. **There may be cleaner related vehicles.** The linked 26-350 petition concerns the same judgment, and the petition identifies separately pending challenges to the Third Circuit's Whittaker ruling. The Court could address the general issue elsewhere or simply see meaningful factual and procedural distinctions. I have not assumed any related case's later disposition.

The result is **P(any grant) = 0.18**, with denial the most likely individual outcome. This number reflects a selected, supported response-requested petition with real certworthiness, discounted for finality, preservation, third-party-rights, and scope problems. It is not a mechanically estimated response-request uplift, and the missing opposition is a material uncertainty.
