# Predicted Court Reasoning and Procedural Trajectory: *Charter Consolidated, Ltd., et al. v. Cape Intermediate Holdings Limited, et al.* (No. 26-328)

## 1. Procedural Trajectory: Redistribution and CVSG

- **Further distributions (`relist-increment`):** The docket currently records a single distribution (`distribution_count = 1`, distributed on October 21, 2026 for the Conference of November 6, 2026), followed by the Court's October 27, 2026 call for a response (due November 27, 2026). Once respondents file their briefs in opposition (likely after one thirty-day extension into late December 2026 or January 2027) and Petitioners file their reply brief, the petition will be distributed a second time for conference (`relist-increment` = 0.98). At that post-response conference, the Court will likely deny certiorari without relisting the petition further, or at most relist once alongside the companion petition in *Altrad Investment Authority S.A.S. v. Cape Intermediate Holdings Limited* (No. 26-350) and the pending *Whittaker* petitions (Nos. 26-159 and 26-263) before denying review.
- **Solicitor General invitation (`cvsg-increment`):** The Court will **not** call for the views of the Solicitor General (`cvsg-increment` = 0.02). No federal statute or treaty is at issue, the Court declined to invite the Solicitor General in *Atlas Turner, Inc. v. Welch* (No. 25-213) earlier in 2026, and the South Carolina Supreme Court expressly noted that Petitioners never raised any foreign-relations preemption claim before its merits decision.

## 2. Disposition and Separate Writings

- **Disposition (`disposition`):** The Court will **deny** the petition for a writ of certiorari (`disposition` = 0.06). Once the briefs in opposition are filed, the threshold obstacles—lack of a final judgment under 28 U.S.C. § 1257(a) given the remand for trial on personal jurisdiction, choice of law, and the merits of the third-party claims; Petitioners' lack of third-party standing to assert the corporate-governance rights of an absent English corporation (CIHL) with which they disclaim affiliation; preservation defects; and the South Carolina Supreme Court's narrowing of the pre-judgment receivership to in-state collection of insurance assets and litigation claims responsive to the underlying tort action—will lead the Court to deny review, just as it denied certiorari in *Atlas Turner, Inc. v. Welch* (No. 25-213).
- **Separate writings on denial (`dissent-from-denial` and `order-writers`):** Expect **no separate writing** accompanying the denial (`dissent-from-denial` = 0.04). In the unlikely event a separate writing is filed, it would most likely be a brief dissent from or statement respecting the denial of certiorari by Justice Thomas, Justice Alito, or Justice Gorsuch flagging concerns over South Carolina's extraterritorial use of pre-judgment receiverships while noting the interlocutory posture.

## 3. Conditional Merits and Summary-Disposition Forecast (If Granted)

- **Summary disposition vs. plenary review (`summary-disposition-route`):** Conditional on a grant, the Court would set the case for plenary briefing and oral argument rather than disposing of it summarily (`summary-disposition-route` = 0.10), probably consolidating No. 26-328 with companion petition No. 26-350 (*Altrad Investment Authority*). A summary GVR is unlikely unless the Court first grants review in *Protopapas v. Whittaker, Clark & Daniels, Inc.* (No. 26-159) or *Official Committee of Talc Claimants v. Whittaker, Clark & Daniels, Inc.* (No. 26-263) and holds this petition for that decision.
- **Question Presented and conditional ground if granted:** If granted, the Court would take the single Question Presented as framed (or narrowed specifically to Fourteenth Amendment Due Process territorial limits on state-court pre-judgment receiverships over out-of-state corporations) and would leave unpreserved foreign-affairs preemption arguments behind. If the Court reached the merits, it would likely hold that while a state court may appoint a receiver over an out-of-state corporation's in-state property or insurance proceeds, the Due Process Clause prohibits a state-court receiver unrecognized in the corporation's place of incorporation from exercising the corporation's internal governance authority to waive personal jurisdiction or prosecute alter-ego claims against third parties on the corporation's behalf.
