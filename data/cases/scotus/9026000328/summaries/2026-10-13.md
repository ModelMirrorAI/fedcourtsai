## In brief

Companies tied to an English asbestos defendant ask the Court to hear their challenge to a South Carolina receiver's power over the foreign company's lawsuits.

## What happened

John and Margaret Tibbs sued many companies in South Carolina, alleging asbestos exposure caused Mr. Tibbs's lung cancer. One defendant, Cape Intermediate Holdings Limited (CIHL), an English company, did not appear in the case. A South Carolina trial court had earlier appointed a receiver to act for CIHL in a related asbestos case, and the receiver, acting for CIHL, sued Charter Consolidated Ltd., ESAB Corporation, Central Mining & Investment Corporation Ltd., and others, claiming they are responsible for CIHL's liabilities. Charter Consolidated and the others asked the South Carolina Supreme Court to limit or dissolve the receiver's authority; that court largely upheld the receivership, though it narrowed its scope and rejected the solvency finding behind part of it.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked whether constitutional limits on state power prevent a state-appointed receiver from making litigation and other internal decisions for a corporation formed and based outside the state when that receiver's authority has not been recognized where the corporation is incorporated.

## Where it stands

The petition for review was filed September 8, 2026, with a response due October 13, 2026, the snapshot date; the docket does not yet show further action.

## What each outcome would mean

Charter Consolidated and its co-petitioners are asking the Court to take up the case. If the Court grants review, it would hear full arguments and decide whether the receivership arrangement is constitutional, with no party yet determined to be right. If it denies review, the South Carolina Supreme Court's decision upholding the modified receivership stands, leaving the receiver able to pursue claims against Charter Consolidated and the others on CIHL's behalf within the limits that court set.
