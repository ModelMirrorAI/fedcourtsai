## In brief

Corporate defendants in a South Carolina asbestos case ask the Court to review whether a state-court receiver can control an overseas company's litigation decisions.

## What happened

John and Margaret Tibbs sued numerous companies in South Carolina state court, alleging asbestos exposure caused Mr. Tibbs's lung cancer. One defendant, Cape Intermediate Holdings Limited (CIHL), an English company that did not appear in the case, had previously been placed under a court-appointed receiver in a related case. The receiver accepted service for CIHL and filed claims against Charter Consolidated and other companies, alleging they were responsible for CIHL's liabilities. Those companies argued the receiver lacked authority over a foreign corporation's internal affairs. The South Carolina Supreme Court upheld the receivership, though it narrowed the receiver's powers, and denied rehearing and a stay.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked whether constitutional limits on state courts' power prevent a state-appointed receiver from making litigation and internal-governance decisions for a corporation based outside the state, or outside the country, when that corporation's home jurisdiction has not recognized the receiver's authority.

## Where it stands

The petition was filed and the other side waived its right to respond. Several amicus briefs supporting the petitioners have been submitted. The docket shows no further action since these filings.

## What each outcome would mean

Charter Consolidated and the other companies named as petitioners are asking the Court to take up the case. If the Court grants review, it would hear full arguments and decide whether the receivership arrangement is constitutional, resolving nothing until then. If the Court denies review, the South Carolina Supreme Court's decision upholding the receiver's modified authority over CIHL stands, and the receiver's claims against the petitioners may proceed in the South Carolina courts.
