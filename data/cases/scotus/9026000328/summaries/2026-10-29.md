## In brief

Companies sued as alter egos of an asbestos defendant ask the Court to review whether a South Carolina receiver may control a foreign corporation's litigation decisions.

## What happened

John and Margaret Tibbs sued numerous companies in South Carolina, alleging asbestos exposure caused Mr. Tibbs' lung cancer, naming Cape Intermediate Holdings Limited (CIHL), an English company, as a defendant. A court-appointed receiver, acting on CIHL's behalf, brought claims against Charter Consolidated, ESAB Corporation, and Central Mining & Investment Corporation, arguing they were CIHL's alter egos. These companies challenged the receiver's authority, but the South Carolina Supreme Court upheld the receivership, though it narrowed the receiver's powers. A British court had separately enjoined the receiver from acting for CIHL.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked whether constitutional limits on state courts' power prevent a state-appointed receiver from making litigation and other internal decisions for a corporation based in another country, when that country's courts have not recognized the receiver's authority.

## Where it stands

The petition was distributed for a November 6, 2026 conference. After that distribution, the Court asked the respondents to file a response by November 27, 2026, and one respondent has asked for more time to do so.

## What each outcome would mean

Charter Consolidated and the other petitioners are asking the Court to overturn the South Carolina Supreme Court's decision upholding the receivership. If the Court grants the petition, it would hear full arguments and decide whether the receiver's authority over CIHL is constitutional, deciding nothing yet about who prevails. If the Court denies the petition, the South Carolina Supreme Court's ruling stands, leaving the receiver's narrowed authority over CIHL intact and the underlying asbestos case to continue.
