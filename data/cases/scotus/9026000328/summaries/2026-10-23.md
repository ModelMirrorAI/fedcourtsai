## In brief

Companies tied to an English asbestos defendant ask the Court to decide whether a South Carolina receiver may control that foreign company's lawsuits and affairs.

## What happened

John and Margaret Tibbs sued many companies in South Carolina state court over Mr. Tibbs' lung cancer, alleging asbestos exposure. One defendant, later clarified to be Cape Intermediate Holdings Limited (CIHL), an English company, never appeared. A South Carolina court had appointed a receiver to act for CIHL in a related case, and that receiver answered the Tibbs complaint and sued Charter Consolidated, ESAB Corporation, Central Mining & Investment Corporation, and others, claiming they are CIHL's alter egos. The South Carolina Supreme Court upheld the receiver's authority, though it narrowed his powers. A British court had separately enjoined the receiver from acting for CIHL.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked whether constitutional limits on state courts' power prevent a state-appointed receiver from making litigation decisions and controlling other internal affairs of a company incorporated elsewhere, when that company's home jurisdiction has not recognized the receiver's authority.

## Where it stands

The petition was filed, the respondents waived their right to respond, several amicus briefs were filed, and the case was distributed for the Court's conference of November 6, 2026. The docket does not yet show any action taken at that conference.

## What each outcome would mean

Charter Consolidated and the other named companies, as petitioners, are asking the Court to overturn the South Carolina Supreme Court's ruling that upheld the receivership. If the Court grants review and ultimately reverses, it would limit or bar the receiver's authority over CIHL's litigation and affairs, benefiting the petitioners. If it affirms, the receiver's authority as defined by the South Carolina court would stand, benefiting the receiver and the Tibbses. If the Court denies review, the South Carolina Supreme Court's decision remains in effect, leaving the receiver's narrowed authority intact.
