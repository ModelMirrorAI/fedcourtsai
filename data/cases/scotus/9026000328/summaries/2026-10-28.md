## In brief

Companies fighting a South Carolina receiver's control over asbestos litigation ask the Court to review whether that arrangement violates constitutional limits on state power.

## What happened

John and Margaret Tibbs sued numerous companies in South Carolina, alleging asbestos exposure caused Mr. Tibbs's lung cancer. One defendant, Cape Intermediate Holdings Limited (CIHL), an English company, did not appear; a court-appointed receiver accepted service on its behalf and sued Charter Consolidated, ESAB Corporation, Central Mining & Investment, and others as third parties, claiming they were CIHL's alter egos. Charter and the others challenged the receiver's authority, including personal jurisdiction, in the South Carolina courts. The South Carolina Supreme Court upheld the receivership, though it narrowed the receiver's powers, and denied rehearing and a stay. A British court had separately enjoined the receiver from acting for CIHL, a ruling the South Carolina court declined to treat as controlling.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked whether constitutional limits on state courts' power prevent a state-appointed receiver from making litigation decisions and controlling other internal matters of a company incorporated elsewhere, when that company's home jurisdiction has not recognized the receiver's authority.

## Where it stands

The petition was distributed for the Court's November 6, 2026 conference. After that conference, the Court requested a response from the respondents, due November 27, 2026.

## What each outcome would mean

Charter Consolidated and the other petitioners are asking the Court to overturn the South Carolina Supreme Court's decision upholding the receivership. If the Court takes the case, it would decide the constitutional question itself; a grant would not by itself favor either side. If the Court denies review, the South Carolina ruling stands, leaving the receiver authorized to pursue insurance assets and related claims against Charter and the other companies on CIHL's behalf.
