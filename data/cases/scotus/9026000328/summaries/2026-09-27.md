## In brief

Companies sued in South Carolina asbestos litigation ask the Court to review whether a state-appointed receiver may control an out-of-state corporation's lawsuits without recognition in that corporation's home jurisdiction.

## What happened

A South Carolina trial court appointed a local attorney as receiver over Cape Intermediate Holdings Limited, an English company, in asbestos litigation brought by John and Margaret Tibbs. The receiver, acting for Cape without its board's approval, sued Charter Consolidated, ESAB Corporation, and Central Mining & Investment as third parties, claiming they were responsible for Cape's liabilities. Charter and the others argued the receivership violated constitutional limits on state power, especially since a UK court had separately barred the receiver from acting for Cape. The South Carolina Supreme Court upheld the receiver's authority, though it narrowed his powers, and denied rehearing.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked whether constitutional limits on a state's authority beyond its own borders stop a state court's receiver from making litigation decisions and other internal decisions for a corporation based elsewhere, when no court in that company's home jurisdiction has recognized the receiver's authority.

## Where it stands

The petition for review was filed on September 8, 2026, with a response due October 13, 2026. The Court has not yet acted on it.

## What each outcome would mean

Charter Consolidated and the other companies named as third-party defendants are asking the Court to take up the case. If the Court grants review, it will hear full arguments and decide whether the receivership arrangement is constitutional, without yet deciding who wins. If it denies review, the South Carolina Supreme Court's decision upholding the receiver's authority stands, and the receiver's third-party claims against Charter and the others may proceed in the South Carolina litigation.
