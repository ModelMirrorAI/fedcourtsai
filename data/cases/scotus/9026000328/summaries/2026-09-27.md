## What happened

John and Margaret Tibbs sued numerous companies in South Carolina, alleging exposure to asbestos. Among the defendants was Cape Intermediate Holdings Limited (CIHL), an English corporation. A South Carolina trial court had appointed a local attorney as receiver over CIHL, giving him power to accept service, hire counsel, and make other decisions for the company. Acting under that authority, without approval from CIHL's own board, the receiver filed claims against Charter Consolidated Ltd., ESAB Corporation, and Central Mining & Investment Corporation Ltd. (the petitioners), alleging they were responsible for CIHL's liabilities. A court in England separately ordered the receiver not to act on CIHL's behalf anywhere in the world. The South Carolina Supreme Court nonetheless upheld the receivership, though it narrowed the receiver's powers, and denied rehearing. The petitioners then asked the U.S. Supreme Court to review that decision.

## What the Court is being asked

The petitioners ask the Court to decide whether a state court can let a receiver it appoints make litigation and internal decisions for a corporation based in another jurisdiction, when that jurisdiction has not recognized the receiver's authority. They argue that limits on state power under the Constitution prevent this kind of cross-border control, and point to a conflicting federal appeals court decision that reached the opposite conclusion in a similar case involving the same receiver and the same South Carolina court, over a different out-of-state company.

## Where it stands
