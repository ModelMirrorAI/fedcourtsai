## In brief

Companies fighting a court-appointed receiver's authority over a foreign asbestos defendant ask the Court to review whether that authority violates constitutional limits on state power.

## What happened

John and Margaret Tibbs sued numerous companies in South Carolina state court over Mr. Tibbs's lung cancer, which they attributed to asbestos exposure. One defendant, Cape Intermediate Holdings Limited (CIHL), an English company, did not appear, and a court-appointed receiver answered and pursued claims on CIHL's behalf against Charter Consolidated and other companies, alleging they were CIHL's alter egos. Charter Consolidated and the others challenged the receiver's authority and personal jurisdiction over them. The South Carolina Supreme Court upheld the receivership, as modified, and rejected the challenges. A British court had separately enjoined the receiver from acting for CIHL.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked whether constitutional limits on state power prevent a state court's receiver from making litigation and other internal decisions for a corporation based outside the state when the place where that corporation is organized has not recognized the receiver's authority.

## Where it stands

The petition was distributed for a November 6, 2026 conference, after which the Court asked the respondents for a response; the respondents' deadline to file that response has since been extended to December 28, 2026, and the case has not yet been scheduled for another conference.

## What each outcome would mean

Charter Consolidated and the other companies are asking the Court to take up the case and overturn the South Carolina ruling. If the Court grants review, it will decide the constitutional question in a full case; a grant decides nothing about who ultimately wins. If the Court denies review, the South Carolina Supreme Court's decision upholding the receiver's authority over CIHL stands, and the receiver's claims against Charter Consolidated and the others may continue in the South Carolina courts.
