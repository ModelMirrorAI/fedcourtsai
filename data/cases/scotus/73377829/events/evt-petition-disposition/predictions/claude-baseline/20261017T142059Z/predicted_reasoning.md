# Predicted course — Strickland v. Moritz, No. 25-1303

**State I am forecasting from.** The docket shows one distribution (for the
June 25, 2026 conference), entered after the federal respondents waived a
response. Six days later the Court **requested a response**, which pulled the
petition off that conference; the Solicitor General then took three extensions,
the last to October 13, 2026. The snapshot (October 10) predates that due date,
so no brief in opposition is on my record. One amicus brief (Georgia
Association for Women Lawyers and the Legal Accountability Project) supports
the petitioner. No CVSG.

**Relists.** Once the federal respondents' brief in opposition is filed (or a
further extension runs out), the petition will be distributed again — expect a
distribution for a November 2026 conference, roughly three weeks after the
reply window. That is the further distribution I am confident of. Beyond it, I
expect **no further relist**: the petition is most likely denied at its first
post-response conference.

**CVSG.** None. The Solicitor General already represents the respondents, so
there is no separate federal interest to invite.

**Disposition.** Denied. The questions — whether the Fifth Amendment permits
the judiciary to route its employees' harassment and discrimination claims
through its internal EDR system, and whether a circuit EDR plan that departs
from Title VII principles violates the Congressional Accountability Act and
Judicial Conference policy — arrive with no circuit split (the Second Circuit's
*Dotson v. Griesa* points the same way as the Fourth Circuit), on a post-trial
record that is fact-heavy, and against a backdrop in which the Judicial
Conference (chaired by the Chief Justice) has been revising EDR policy
administratively. The Court is unlikely to take up a constitutional review of
its own branch's internal personnel machinery in this vehicle.

**If granted.** The Court would take QP 1, likely narrowed to the
due-process question (whether the EDR process supplied constitutionally
adequate procedure for the protected interest the Fourth Circuit's earlier
2022 decision recognized), leaving QP 2's CAA/policy theory aside as lacking
an enforceable right of action.

**Summary disposition.** Unlikely; no intervening decision bears on the
questions, so a GVR or summary reversal is not a live route.

**Separate writing on denial.** Most likely none. A short statement respecting
denial — e.g. from Justice Jackson or Justice Sotomayor noting the judiciary's
workplace-conduct concerns — is possible but improbable; recusal notes by
individual Justices are also possible given the Judicial Conference's role, but
those are not dissents.
