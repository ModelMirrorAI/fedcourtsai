Expect at least two further distributions (relists) before a decision on the petition once the Solicitor General files the response, given the gravity of the constitutional challenge to the judiciary's own administrative processes and the significant amicus support. No CVSG is expected, because the Solicitor General is already the respondent in the case representing the Judicial Conference and other officials. 

If granted, the Court would likely take up the first QP regarding whether the EDR plan affords adequate Due Process and Equal Protection, potentially leaving the statutory question under the CAA behind. A summary disposition route (like a GVR) is highly unlikely as there is no intervening decision and the case presents a major constitutional question. 

Should the Court deny review, expect a dissent from denial or a statement respecting the denial from one of the liberal Justices (such as Justice Sotomayor or Justice Jackson) given the serious allegations of sexual harassment, the recognized flaws in the handling of the petitioner's complaint, and the lack of standard Title VII protections for judiciary employees.
