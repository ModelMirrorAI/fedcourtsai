# Forecast

The Court will deny certiorari in Strickland v. Moritz. I assign a 7% probability to any grant, including a GVR or summary reversal. The likely order will not explain the denial; denial would not endorse the employment practices challenged here.

## Further proceedings

The provisioned record contains one distribution, entered June 9, 2026, for the June 25 conference. The Court then requested a response on June 15 after the federal respondents waived their response. The last recorded extension sets October 13, 2026, as the response deadline. No response filing or subsequent distribution appears in this October 10 snapshot.

I expect one additional distribution after the requested response and any reply, followed by denial, rather than a prolonged sequence of substantive relists. P(at least one additional distribution) is 95%. This forecast is unusually high for a baseline-band petition because the claim counts another distribution entry, including redistribution after a requested response, not only renewed consideration after a completed conference. The original June listing does not establish that the Court actually considered the petition then. Conditional on the response arriving around the recorded deadline, the most likely disposition window is late October through December 2026; another extension would shift that window.

No CVSG is expected: P(a new CVSG) is 0.5%. The Solicitor General already represents the federal respondents, and the requested response supplies the government's litigation position without a separate invitation for views. The existing call for a response is not a CVSG.

## If review is granted

The likeliest question would be a narrowed version of the first question presented: whether the EDR procedures and available remedies satisfy Fifth Amendment due process for an employee alleging discrimination, including the significance of abandoning the final hearing process. I do not expect the Court to take the broad proposition that all Title VII procedures and remedies must constitutionally be replicated, or to reach the second question's full statutory-and-policy formulation.

Plenary briefing and argument would be more likely than disposal in the grant order. P(summary disposition | any grant) is 10%. The supplied materials identify no intervening decision that clearly supplies a GVR route, and the disputed procedural and factual issues do not suggest a straightforward summary reversal.

If the Court denies, I expect no separate writing. P(a noted dissent or statement respecting denial | denial) is 8%. The institutional stakes make a statement conceivable, but the vehicle problems make an unexplained denial the more likely course. I do not forecast an individual Justice's vote or authorship.
