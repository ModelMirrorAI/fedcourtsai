# Forecast of the Court's action

The Court will most likely deny the petition after receiving or awaiting the requested response. It will leave the lower-court judgment standing without deciding whether the judiciary's employment-dispute system must supply Title VII-equivalent procedures or remedies. P(any grant) is **8%**.

The baseline records **one distribution**, for the June 25, 2026 conference, and no CVSG. I expect **one further distribution** after the response process, with **98% probability of at least one additional distribution** overall. Multiple additional substantive relists are not the central forecast. The redistribution associated with obtaining a response will not itself signal agreement to review the case.

I forecast **no separate call for the Solicitor General's views**: P(a new CVSG) is **0.5%**. The Solicitor General's office already represents the respondents and can present its position in the requested response.

**If review is granted**, the likeliest question is a narrower version of Question 1: whether the procedures and available remedies afforded this employee under the governing EDR plan satisfied Fifth Amendment due process, including the asserted lack of an impartial decisionmaker. I expect the Court to avoid a wholesale constitutional extension of Title VII to the judiciary and to be less likely to take Question 2's broad claim that the Congressional Accountability Act and judiciary policy independently require the full statutory protections. Conditional on review, argument and a merits opinion are substantially likelier than a summary order: **P(summary disposition | any grant) = 10%**. No particular intervening decision is identified as a basis for a GVR.

**If review is denied**, I expect an unexplained order with no separate writing: **P(any noted dissent or statement | denial) = 12%**. Jackson or Sotomayor is the most plausible author in the smaller separate-writing scenario; no particular author is the modal forecast. A denial will not resolve the validity of the EDR system on its merits.
