## What happened

Caryn Strickland, a former federal public defender in North Carolina, sued judiciary officials after alleging her supervisor sexually harassed her and that the judiciary's own internal complaint system, the Employment Dispute Resolution (EDR) Plan, failed to address it. Federal judiciary employees are not covered by Title VII, the law banning workplace discrimination for most employers, and instead must use this internal process. After a six-day trial, the district court found many flaws in how the EDR process was handled but ruled against Strickland. The Fourth Circuit Court of Appeals, sitting with judges brought in from other circuits because all Fourth Circuit judges recused themselves, affirmed that decision. The court then denied rehearing.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked whether the Constitution's guarantees of fair process and equal treatment allow the judiciary to make its own employees resolve sexual harassment and discrimination claims through an internal system that lacks an independent decisionmaker, adequate safeguards, and meaningful remedies, instead of giving them the kind of protections other workers get under employment discrimination law. A related question is whether a court circuit's internal complaint system that falls short of those broader legal standards violates the law and policy meant to extend similar protections to judiciary employees.
