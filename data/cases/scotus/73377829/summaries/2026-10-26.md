## In brief

A former federal public defender asks the Court to revive her claim that the judiciary's internal harassment-complaint process violated her constitutional rights.

## What happened

Caryn Strickland worked for the Federal Public Defender's Office in the Western District of North Carolina. She reported that her supervisor sexually harassed her and pursued relief through the judiciary's internal Employment Dispute Resolution Plan before leaving the office for another job. She later sued judiciary officials, claiming the EDR process denied her due process and equal protection. After a bench trial, the district court ruled against her, and the Fourth Circuit affirmed, finding she had forfeited some arguments and that her claims failed on the facts. Rehearing was denied.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked whether requiring judiciary employees to resolve harassment and discrimination claims through the judiciary's own internal process, rather than through the protections Title VII gives most other workers, violates the Constitution's guarantees of fair process and equal treatment. The Court is also asked whether a 1995 law requiring a report on applying Title VII to judiciary employees means the judiciary's internal plan must match Title VII's standards.

## Where it stands

The petition was distributed for conference, and the Court then asked the federal government to respond. The government filed a brief opposing review, arguing the questions were not properly raised below and lack merit; the case has not yet been scheduled for another conference in the record shown.

## What each outcome would mean

Strickland is asking the Court to take up her case and reverse the Fourth Circuit's ruling, which favored the federal officials she sued. If the Court grants review, it would hear full briefing and argument and decide the legal questions; a grant alone would not determine who wins. If the Court denies review, the Fourth Circuit's decision rejecting Strickland's claims stands, and the government officials' win remains in place.
