## In brief

A former federal public defender asks the Court to review her claim that the judiciary's internal process for handling her harassment complaint violated her constitutional rights.

## What happened

Caryn Strickland worked for the Federal Public Defender's Office in the Western District of North Carolina. She reported that her supervisor sexually harassed her and pursued relief through the judiciary's internal Employment Dispute Resolution Plan before withdrawing and accepting a different job. She later sued, claiming the process violated her due process and equal protection rights. After a bench trial, the district court ruled for the government defendants, and the Fourth Circuit affirmed, finding her procedural arguments forfeited or unsupported by the trial record. Rehearing was denied.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked whether the Constitution's fairness guarantees allow the judiciary to resolve its own employees' harassment and discrimination claims through its internal dispute process, rather than requiring the protections that apply to most other employers under federal civil rights law. The Court is also asked whether that internal process conflicts with a separate law directing the judiciary to study how such protections should apply to its own employees.

## Where it stands

The federal government, as respondent, filed a brief opposing review, arguing the questions were not properly raised in the lower courts and lack merit. The petition has not yet been scheduled for a conference as reflected in this docket.

## What each outcome would mean

Strickland, as petitioner, asks the Court to take up her case. If the Court grants review, it would hear full arguments and decide the constitutional and statutory questions; a grant would not itself decide who is right. If the Court denies review, the Fourth Circuit's ruling against Strickland and in favor of the judiciary officials she sued stands, leaving the lower court's judgment in place without the Supreme Court ruling on the merits.
