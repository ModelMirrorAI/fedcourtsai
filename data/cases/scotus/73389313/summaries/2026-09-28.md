## What happened

Joan Farr, representing herself, sued Alexandra Grant and grantLOVE LLC, attorney Scott Sims and his firm Ziffren Brittenham LLP, WME IMG, and several federal agencies, alleging a decades-long conspiracy that violated her constitutional rights, blocked her from holding public office, and harmed her personally. The Court of Appeals for the Eighth Circuit affirmed that dismissal and later denied her request for the full court to rehear the case. Farr then asked the Supreme Court to take up her case.

## What the Court is being asked

Farr's filing raises numerous issues: whether she was denied various constitutional protections and legal representation, whether the appeals court wrongly upheld dismissal of her conspiracy claims, whether venue in Missouri was proper, whether the judge who ruled against her should have been disqualified because of his age, and whether the case should be sent back for a full trial rather than dismissed at an early stage.

## Where it stands

The federal government and the other named respondents have all waived their right to respond to the petition. The case was distributed for the Court's conference on September 28, 2026, and the docket shows no action taken since that conference.

## What each outcome would mean

Farr, the petitioner, is asking the Court to take up her case. If the Court grants her petition, it would agree to hear the case in full and decide the questions she raises, without yet deciding who is right. If the Court denies the petition, the Eighth Circuit's decision affirming dismissal of Farr's lawsuit stands, and the case ends there. A denial would not mean the Supreme Court agrees the lower courts ruled correctly; it would simply leave that ruling in place.
