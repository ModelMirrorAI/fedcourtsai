# Forecast: Farr v. Grant, No. 25-1306 (evt-petition-disposition)

The Court will **deny** the petition on the order list following the September 28, 2026 long conference, most likely the October 5, 2026 order list.

**Relists.** The docket shows one distribution (July 8, 2026, for the Conference of 9/28/2026) and no relist. I expect no further distribution: the petition raises no question the Court would hold for another case, no respondent has filed anything to consider, and the Court disposes of this kind of pro se petition in one pass. The only realistic path to a second distribution entry is a routine reschedule off the long conference, which is uncommon.

**CVSG.** No. The Solicitor General is already counsel for the federal respondents and has waived a response; the Court does not invite the views of a party. There is no federal interest the SG has not already spoken to by waiving.

**Which question.** None. The eight questions presented are a compound recitation of constitutional grievances, an evidentiary-sufficiency complaint, a claim to a right to hold the Presidency under 42 U.S.C. 1985, a venue complaint, an en banc denial complaint, an attack on the presiding district judge's age, and a request for a merits remand. None states a legal rule that divides the circuits or conflicts with this Court's precedent. If the Court were somehow to act, it would not take any of them as written.

**Summary disposition.** Not applicable to what I expect. Conditionally, if the Court did act on this petition, the action would be a GVR-style order rather than plenary review, since nothing here could be set for argument; hence the conditional route number in `prediction.json` leans toward the cert-order route.

**Dissent or statement respecting denial.** None. No Justice has a reason to write on a pro se conspiracy petition from a serial filer whose prior petitions were denied without comment, and there is no doctrinal hook a statement could attach to.
