I reduce the grant forecast to **0.1%** for case-specific reasons:

- **The petition principally seeks correction of its own dismissal.** Questions 1–3, 5, and 7–8 ask the Court to accept alleged rights violations, reassess the sufficiency of circumstantial evidence, reconsider the denial of rehearing, or provide a trial/remedy. The reasons section repeatedly incorporates filings from this and earlier litigation rather than presenting a focused legal disagreement. It does not juxtapose conflicting appellate holdings on the same legal question. The references in the rehearing argument likewise do not identify such a conflict. (Petition, printed pp. 13–15.) I have not independently surveyed all circuits and do not claim that no relevant conflict exists anywhere.
- **The presentation creates substantial vehicle uncertainty.** The petition disputes pleading sufficiency, jurisdiction, immunity, rehearing, and the district judge's age, with much of the asserted evidence outside the supplied documents. A favorable answer on one issue might not remove the other asserted grounds for dismissal. Without the appendix, I cannot independently determine the precise combination of dispositive grounds. (Petition, printed pp. 10–17.)
- **The visible docket supplies no countervailing attention signal.** Multiple response waivers and a single distribution do not prove denial, but the record provides no relist, requested response, or invitation for federal views that would offset the weak cert presentation. This is modest supporting evidence, not a categorical rule.

For the legal selection standard, I checked the Court's own Rule 10, effective March 16, 2026, printed pp. 5–6. Applying that standard to the supplied advocacy is my predictive inference, not an adjudication of the allegations. The source and retrieval limitations are recorded in `retrieval.md`.
