# Predicted Reasoning: *Juan Baltazar, Warden, et al. v. Eva Daley* (No. 26-146)

## Procedural Trajectory and Hold Posture

This petition for a writ of certiorari presents a single statutory question: whether a habeas corpus proceeding brought by a noncitizen detainee under 28 U.S.C. § 2241 is a "civil action" eligible for attorney's fee shifting under the Equal Access to Justice Act (EAJA), 28 U.S.C. § 2412(d)(1)(A). Below, a divided panel of the Tenth Circuit (157 F.4th 1207) held that § 2241 immigration habeas proceedings qualify as "civil actions" under the EAJA, deepening an entrenched circuit split against the Third, Fourth, and Fifth Circuits.

On June 29, 2026, the Supreme Court granted certiorari on this exact question in *Montoya Palacios v. Liggins*, No. 25-1223 (arising out of the Fourth Circuit, which ruled in the government's favor). The Solicitor General subsequently filed two protective follow-on petitions on July 31, 2026, challenging adverse circuit decisions on the EAJA question: *Warden, Moshannon Valley Correctional Center v. Michelin*, No. 26-145 (from the Third Circuit's *Party-Appointed Amicus* fee ruling), and this petition, *Baltazar v. Daley*, No. 26-146 (from the Tenth Circuit). In both petitions, the Solicitor General asked the Court to hold the petition pending the merits decision in *Montoya Palacios v. Liggins* and then dispose of it accordingly.

Although Respondent Eva Daley (represented by Andrew Timothy Tutt, who also filed an amicus brief on behalf of Daley and Michelin in *Montoya Palacios*) initially waived the right to file a response on September 1, 2026, and the petition was distributed for the September 28, 2026 Long Conference, the Court called for a response on September 22, 2026 (due October 22, 2026). Calling for a response in a follow-on hold candidate after an initial waiver is standard practice to ensure that the respondent has an opportunity to identify any vehicle distinctions, waiver arguments, or alternative grounds before the Court places the petition on hold or issues a summary disposition.

## Expected Procedural Actions

1. **Redistribution and Hold (`relist-increment`)**: Once the brief in opposition and the government's reply are filed, the petition will be redistributed for a conference (`distribution_count` will increase beyond its frozen snapshot count of 1) and placed on hold pending the merits resolution of *Montoya Palacios v. Liggins* (No. 25-1223).
2. **No CVSG (`cvsg-increment`)**: Because the Solicitor General is the counsel of record for the federal petitioners (Warden Baltazar and federal immigration officials), the Court will not invite the Solicitor General to file an amicus brief.
3. **Summary Disposition Following *Montoya Palacios* (`disposition` and `summary-disposition-route`)**:
   - **If the Government prevails in *Montoya Palacios*** (i.e., the Court affirms the Fourth Circuit and holds that habeas corpus proceedings are hybrid proceedings outside the EAJA's sovereign-immunity waiver for "any civil action"): The Court will **grant** the petition in *Baltazar v. Daley*, **vacate** the Tenth Circuit's judgment, and **remand** for further proceedings in light of *Montoya Palacios* (**GVR**).
   - **If the Petitioner prevails in *Montoya Palacios*** (i.e., the Court reverses the Fourth Circuit and holds that immigration habeas proceedings under § 2241 are "civil actions" under the EAJA): The Tenth Circuit's judgment in favor of Daley will be consistent with the Court's merits holding, and the Court will **deny** the government's petition in *Baltazar v. Daley* (unless the Court's reasoning alters the standard for evaluating whether the government's position was "substantially justified" in a way that warrants a GVR).
   - Conditional on a grant in this docket, the overwhelmingly likely route is a post-*Montoya Palacios* **GVR** rather than a standalone plenary merits grant, because *Montoya Palacios* is already briefed and argued as the plenary vehicle for the Term.
4. **Separate Writings (`dissent-from-denial` and `order-writers`)**: Whether the petition ends in a routine mop-up GVR or a routine post-merits denial after *Montoya Palacios* resolves the underlying legal question, separate opinions at the order stage are unlikely, as the Justices will already have expressed their merits views in *Montoya Palacios*.
