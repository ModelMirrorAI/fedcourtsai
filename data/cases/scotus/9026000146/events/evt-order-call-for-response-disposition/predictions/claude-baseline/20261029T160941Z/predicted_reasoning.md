# Forecast — Baltazar v. Daley, No. 26-146 (response-requested moment)

**Disposition: granted (plenary review).** The Solicitor General's petition asks
whether the Equal Access to Justice Act's fee provision — which reaches a "civil
action (other than cases sounding in tort)" against the United States — covers a
habeas petition challenging immigration detention. The Tenth Circuit (Daley v.
Ceja, Nov. 3, 2025) held that it does and affirmed a fee award. Other circuits
have read EAJA as not reaching habeas (the Fifth Circuit for immigration
habeas, and older Second and Ninth Circuit authority on habeas generally), so
the government presents an acknowledged circuit split on a recurring question of
federal fiscal exposure in a sovereign-immunity setting, with the volume of
detention habeas litigation now very high. I expect the Court to grant after the
response is in, most likely at a conference in December 2026 or January 2027.

**Relists / distributions.** The docket shows one distribution (for the
October 9, 2026 conference), overtaken by the September 22 call for a response.
The petition will be distributed again once the brief in opposition (due date
since extended into late November) is filed; I expect one further distribution,
possibly one relist before the grant.

**CVSG.** None — the United States is the petitioner.

**Question presented.** If granted, the Court takes the QP as written: whether
an immigration-detention habeas proceeding is a "civil action" within EAJA's
waiver of sovereign immunity.

**Summary disposition.** Unlikely. No intervening decision supports a GVR, and a
statutory split of this kind is decided after argument, not summarily.

**If denied.** A denial would most plausibly rest on vehicle grounds (e.g. a
fee award already paid or a respondent argument that the issue is fact-bound).
In that event a dissent from denial is possible — Justice Alito, Thomas or
Gorsuch noting the split and the sovereign-immunity canon — but more likely the
Court simply denies without writing.
