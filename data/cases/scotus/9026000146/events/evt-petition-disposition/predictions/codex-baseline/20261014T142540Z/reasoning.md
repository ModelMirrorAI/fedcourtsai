## Why 0.80 rather than the anchor

The main evidence is the published decision below: Daley v. Choate, No. 24-1191 (10th Cir. Nov. 3, 2025), CourtListener opinion 11196210. Pages 3–5 identify a single legal issue about EAJA fee availability in immigration habeas proceedings, affirm a fee award, and explain that neither the underlying habeas relief nor substantial justification was contested on appeal. Pages 26–30 expressly disagree with the Fourth and Fifth Circuits' hybrid-proceeding analysis and conclude that immigration habeas falls unambiguously within the statute. This is evidence of a focused statutory issue and a relatively clean vehicle, not merely a petitioner's unverified assertion of conflict.

I cross-checked the Fifth Circuit side directly in Gomez Barco v. Witte, 65 F.4th 782 (5th Cir. 2023), CourtListener opinion 9388593, pages 4–6. It rejects EAJA fees in immigration habeas on the ground that the waiver does not cover the hybrid proceedings. Its footnote also rejects the Second and Ninth Circuits' contrary reasoning. I rely on Daley's own discussion for the Fourth Circuit comparison, not on an independently read Fourth Circuit opinion. The conflict therefore involves more than an isolated disagreement over the particular fee calculation.

Federal sponsorship, an express disagreement on a recurring federal statute, and the September 22 request for a response together warrant a substantial increase over 0.235. The federal comparison rate around 0.70 provides scale for that increase; the focused conflict and apparent absence of alternate appellate issues move my judgment to 0.80. This is a judgmental probability, not a fitted likelihood calculation, and the correlated signals should not be multiplied as if independent. A response request means the Court wants an adversarial presentation, not that four votes to grant are already established.

I retain 0.20 for no grant chiefly because the petition and BIO are unavailable: I cannot verify the exact question presented, any jurisdictional objection, a vehicle defect raised in opposition, or the government's response to such an objection. The reviewed record makes plenary review more plausible than error correction, but it cannot rule out denial on vehicle or priority grounds. I did not infer that the October 9 conference actually considered the petition; the response request and later deadline make that inference unsafe.

## Other probabilities and stakes

- Relist increment, 0.96: another distribution after the requested response is highly likely under this claim's mechanical definition. It can happen even if the Court ultimately denies. The terminal relist table does not itself estimate this hazard.
- CVSG increment, 0.005: no CVSG is recorded, and the Solicitor General already represents petitioners. A separate invitation would ordinarily add little information here.
- Summary route given grant, 0.08: the competing statutory interpretations favor full briefing and argument. No intervening controlling case requiring remand was identified in the sources consulted; this is not a claim to have exhaustively excluded all related litigation.
- Separate writing given denial, 0.18: the explicit split could motivate a statement, but a silent denial remains substantially more likely. No Justice-specific inference is made.
- Stakes, 0.55: the fee-eligibility rule matters across immigration habeas litigation, including incentives to obtain representation and the government's exposure to fees. The case does not itself decide removal authority or the underlying legality of detention. The modest individual award described below does not erase the rule's broader effects, but neither does the immigration setting make this a maximal-stakes case.
