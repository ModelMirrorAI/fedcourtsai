## In brief

A fired Chicago firefighter asks the Court to review whether his off-duty Facebook posts were wrongly found unprotected speech.

## What happened

Sam Inendino, a longtime Chicago firefighter, was fired after the city's Inspector General flagged Facebook posts it called racially offensive. He sued the city and two officials, claiming the firing violated his First Amendment rights. A federal district court ruled against him, finding his posts did not address matters of public concern. The Seventh Circuit disagreed on that point but still ruled for the city, applying a Supreme Court balancing test from a case called Pickering and concluding that concerns about public trust in the fire department outweighed Inendino's speech interests, even though it found no evidence his posts actually disrupted the workplace.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked whether a government employer can restrict an employee's off-duty speech on public issues by pointing only to worries about public trust, without any evidence that the speech actually disrupted, or was reasonably likely to disrupt, the workplace. The petition says federal appeals courts are split on how much proof of disruption is required before such concerns can outweigh an employee's speech rights.

## Where it stands

The petition was filed and distributed for the Court's November 13, 2026 conference; the docket shows no action from that conference yet. The city later asked for, and received, an extension until January 4, 2027 to file its response.

## What each outcome would mean

Inendino, who lost in the lower court, is asking the Court to take up his case and reverse that ruling. If the Court grants review, it would hear full arguments and decide the legal question itself, deciding nothing yet about who wins. If it denies review, the Seventh Circuit's decision upholding his firing stays in place, and the city's win stands. A grant would not by itself mean Inendino's firing was wrongful; it would only mean the Court will examine the question.
