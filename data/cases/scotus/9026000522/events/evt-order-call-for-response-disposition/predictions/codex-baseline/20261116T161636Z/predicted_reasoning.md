# Forecast of the Court's action

The Court will most likely deny the petition after obtaining the requested response. The record already shows **one distribution**, no CVSG, and no filed amicus briefs. The December 2 response deadline falls after the originally scheduled November 13 conference, so I expect one new distribution after the response process, ordinarily for a winter conference. That procedural redistribution is the main reason P(a distribution increment) is 0.97; it does not itself predict heightened deliberation. The modal path has no additional substantive relist after that redistribution, although another conference or two remains plausible.

The Court is unlikely to invite the Solicitor General's views: P(a new CVSG) is 0.015. This is a constitutional employment-speech dispute against local officials, rather than a case whose resolution requires the federal government's administrative expertise.

If the Court grants review, it will most likely take the petition's single question substantially as presented, focused on the evidentiary basis necessary to rely on predicted harm to public trust under Pickering. It may frame that inquiry around public-facing safety employees rather than establish one rule for every public job. It will not need to decide whether all offensive off-duty speech is protected. A grant is more likely to lead to plenary briefing and argument than an immediate judgment: conditional probability of summary disposition is 0.12. No identified intervening decision supplies a specific grant-vacate-remand rationale.

If review is denied, an unexplained order is most likely. I assign 0.13, conditional on denial, to a noted dissent or a statement respecting denial. If someone writes, Thomas is the most plausible author because the petition presents the evidentiary issue it says his MacRae statement reserved for an appropriate case; Alito is a secondary possibility. This does not predict a majority opinion accompanying denial.
