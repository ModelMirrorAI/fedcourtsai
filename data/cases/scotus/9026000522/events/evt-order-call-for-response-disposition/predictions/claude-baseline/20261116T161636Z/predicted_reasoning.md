# Forecast — Inendino v. Nance-Holt, No. 26-522

The docket shows one distribution (for the Nov 13, 2026 conference), overtaken by the Court's Nov 2 request for a response due Dec 2.

- **Disposition.** The Court will deny certiorari. The City will file a brief in opposition (possibly after one extension), the petitioner will reply, and the petition will be redistributed for a conference in January or February 2027, with the denial on the following order list.
- **Further distributions.** One more distribution is near certain (after the response). A relist beyond that is possible but not the expected path: perhaps a one-in-five chance of a true relist after the post-response conference, most plausibly because a Justice is writing.
- **CVSG.** None. No federal interest is at stake.
- **Amici.** Several petition-stage amicus briefs supporting the petitioner will be filed by the Rule 37.2 deadline (free-speech and police-union organisations).
- **Question presented, if granted.** The Court would take the petition's single question as written: whether asserted public-trust interests can outweigh employee speech under Pickering absent evidence of actual or reasonably threatened disruption. A grant would proceed to plenary briefing and argument rather than a summary disposition; no intervening decision invites a GVR. If granted, the likely ground would be a requirement that predicted harm be reasonably supported by the record, with vacatur and remand for rebalancing.
- **Separate writing.** Should the Court deny, most likely no writing accompanies the order. If anyone writes, it is Justice Thomas, in a statement or dissent tied to his MacRae statement on speculative disruption, possibly joined by Justice Alito or Justice Gorsuch.
