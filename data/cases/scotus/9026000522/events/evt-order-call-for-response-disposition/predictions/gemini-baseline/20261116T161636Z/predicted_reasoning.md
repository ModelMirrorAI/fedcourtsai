## Scope of Review and Route Conditional on a Grant

- **Question presented if granted:** If the Court were to grant review (~6% probability), it would take the petition's single question presented substantially as framed—addressing whether a public employer's unsubstantiated predictions of eroded public trust can outweigh a public employee's private speech on matters of public concern under *Pickering* without concrete evidence of actual or reasonably threatened operational disruption—rather than revisiting *Pickering-Garcetti* from first principles. Conditional on a grant, a majority reversing or vacating would likely hold that generalized "public confidence" concerns cannot substitute for record evidence of reasonably foreseeable operational interference, while remanding threshold *Connick* public-concern questions and individual-capacity qualified-immunity defenses to the Seventh Circuit.
- **Summary disposition vs. plenary review (`summary-disposition-route` = 0.12):** Conditional on a grant, **plenary briefing and oral argument** (~88%) is far likelier than a summary disposition in the cert order (~12%). Because the circuits apply diverging evidentiary formulations under *Pickering*, the Seventh Circuit assumed *Connick* public concern only *arguendo*, and *Monell* waiver plus unaddressed qualified immunity complicate the judgment below, a summary per curiam reversal is exceedingly unlikely; a summary order would occur almost exclusively via a GVR if another public-employee speech case were unexpectedly granted ahead of this one.

## Expected Disposition and Separate Writings on Denial

- **Denial of certiorari:** Once Respondents file their Brief in Opposition, the Court will deny certiorari. Although the Seventh Circuit candidly acknowledged the absence of meaningful record evidence of internal workplace disruption before ruling for the City on public-trust and manner grounds, the opposition will surface decisive vehicle obstacles: (1) Petitioner waived his *Monell* claim against the City of Chicago on appeal, leaving judgment for the City independently intact; (2) the remaining respondents (Fire Commissioner Nance-Holt and Assistant Commissioner Casey) face damages claims backed by an unaddressed, formidable qualified-immunity defense under settled Seventh Circuit precedent; (3) the Seventh Circuit assumed only *arguendo* that Petitioner's thirteen posts addressed matters of public concern; and (4) Petitioner prominently displayed his Chicago Fire Department uniform and title on his public Facebook profile alongside racially derogatory slurs and captions celebrating violence against protesters while serving as an EMT in a predominantly Black neighborhood. With the First Circuit currently reconsidering public-safety social-media *Pickering* standards en banc in *Hussey v. City of Cambridge*, the Court has every incentive to await a cleaner vehicle.
- **Separate writings (`dissent-from-denial` = 0.14):** Most likely (~86% conditional on denial), the denial order issues without noted dissent or statement, just as *Hedgepeth v. Britton* (No. 25-819) was denied without recorded comment in May 2026. However, there is an elevated ~14% chance conditional on denial that **Justice Thomas** (potentially joined by or alongside **Justice Alito** or **Justice Gorsuch**) authors a brief statement respecting or dissenting from the denial, building on his solo statement in *MacRae v. Mattos*, 606 U.S. 937 (2025), because the Seventh Circuit expressly found no meaningful evidence of workplace disruption yet sustained termination on predicted erosion of community trust.
