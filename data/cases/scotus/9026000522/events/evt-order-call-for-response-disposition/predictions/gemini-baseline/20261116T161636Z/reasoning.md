## Case-Specific Adjustments

### Upward Factors (Why `P(grant)` Moves Above Raw Arrival/Waiver Baseline)
- **Direct response to Justice Thomas's *MacRae* invitation on an explicit "no workplace disruption" finding:** In *MacRae v. Mattos*, 606 U.S. 937 (June 30, 2025) (read in full via CourtListener, `opinion_id=11086517`), Justice Thomas wrote a solo 7-page statement respecting denial of certiorari criticizing appellate courts for discounting offensive/mocking social-media memes and crediting speculative disruption, concluding that "[i]n an appropriate case, I would make clear that public employers cannot use *Pickering-Garcetti* balancing generally or unsupported claims of disruption in particular to target employees who express disfavored political views." Here, the Seventh Circuit explicitly held on the first *Pickering* factor that "the record lacks any meaningful evidence to substantiate the City's concerns about workplace disruption" (Pet.App. 26a; two years had elapsed without workplace friction, and 31 coworkers supported Inendino), yet affirmed summary judgment for the City based on predicted erosion of community trust, public-safety solicitude, and derogatory tone.
- **Genuine circuit tension and organized First Amendment / union amicus support:** Represented by Florida State University College of Law's First Amendment Clinic, the petition documents real doctrinal divergence between circuits demanding concrete evidence of operational disruption even for public-safety employees (e.g., the Sixth Circuit in *Noble*, the Ninth Circuit in *Hernandez* and *Moser*, and the Third Circuit in *Fenico*) and circuits permitting generalized public-trust predictions for police and firefighters (the Second Circuit in *Locurto*, Fourth Circuit in *Grutzmacher*, Seventh Circuit here, and Eighth Circuit in *Henry*). Combined with the November 2 CFR and cert-stage amicus support, a clean vehicle presenting this split would plausibly sit around 12–15%.

### Downward Factors (Why the Petition Settles Back to `0.06`)
1. **Ambiguous signal strength of this specific CFR:** Respondents filed a waiver on October 20, 2026—just one day after docketing—triggering an automatic October 28 distribution for the November 13 conference, nearly a week *before* the November 18 Rule 37.2 amicus deadline. On October 29, Petitioner's counsel filed a letter (`party-letter.txt`) asking the Court to hold distribution so FIRE, FOP, and ACLJ could timely file amicus briefs. The November 2 Call for Response followed two business days later. While a chamber (very plausibly Justice Thomas's) plainly wanted fuller briefing and time for the incoming amici, the procedural context makes this CFR somewhat less discriminating than an unprompted post-conference call.
2. **Strong negative corpus prior — *Hedgepeth v. Britton*, No. 25-819:** Corpus lookup (`fedcourts query --court scotus --docket-number 25-819`) shows that on **May 18, 2026**—just six months ago—the Court **denied certiorari without a single recorded dissent** (`distribution_count: 2`, `has_opinion: false`) in *Hedgepeth v. Britton*, another Seventh Circuit teacher social-media *Pickering* petition litigated by Paul D. Clement and backed by **seven** amicus briefs (including FIRE, ACLJ, Cato, PLF, and Manhattan Institute). In *MacRae* itself, no other Justice joined Justice Thomas's statement. Assembling four votes for certiorari in controversial employee social-media cases has proven consistently elusive.
3. **Severe, potentially outcome-blocking vehicle defects that Respondents' BIO will spotlight:**
   - **Waived *Monell* claim against the City of Chicago:** Footnote 2 of the Seventh Circuit's opinion holds unequivocally that "Inendino has waived his argument that the district court erred in granting summary judgment in the City's favor as to his *Monell* claim" by raising it for the first time in his reply brief (Pet.App. 6a n.2). Judgment for the City of Chicago therefore rests on an independent procedural waiver!
   - **Unaddressed qualified immunity for individual-capacity defendants:** With the City insulated by *Monell* waiver, the remaining respondents named in the petition (Pet. iii) are Fire Commissioner Nance-Holt and Assistant Commissioner Casey—both asserted **qualified immunity** at summary judgment, which Judge Kennelly expressly left unreached (Pet.App. 45a; see also Judge Durkin's earlier Rule 12(b)(6) opinion, N.D. Ill. Dkt. 32, deferring qualified immunity to summary judgment). Given settled Seventh Circuit precedent in 2021 (*Lalowski*, *Craig*, *Kokkinis*) and the five-way circuit split Petitioner himself emphasizes, Nance-Holt and Casey have an overwhelming argument that any constitutional violation was not "clearly established" in June 2021—making reversal on *Pickering* unlikely to alter the ultimate damages outcome on remand (though a narrow official-capacity claim against Casey appeared in the First Amended Complaint caption on the N.D. Ill. docket, Pet. iii lists both human respondents solely in their individual capacities).
   - **Assumed-*arguendo* *Connick* predicate and disputed "zero evidence" premise:** The Seventh Circuit declined to hold that all thirteen posts addressed matters of public concern, instead expressing doubt and assuming *Connick* step one only *arguendo* (Pet.App. 17a–18a), while the District Court found *actual* disruption from two citizen complaints to the Office of Inspector General (Pet.App. 44a).
   - **Deliberate self-identification in CFD uniform and deeply unsympathetic facts:** Unlike *MacRae* (pre-employment posts with no workplace link), Inendino explicitly identified himself as a Chicago Fire Department firefighter on his public Facebook page, used a profile photo in CFD firefighter gear on a fire truck next to every comment (invoking *Pickering* factor 7, Pet.App. 27a), invoked his job in arguments with commenters ("welfare fucks," "take your ass back over the border"), celebrated violence against protesters ("should aim for the torso!!!", "All Lives Splatter"), and referred to Black people as "animals" and "dirty hoodrat" while working as a first-responder EMT in Englewood, a predominantly Black neighborhood.
   - **Active en banc percolation in the First Circuit:** As the petition notes (Pet. 13) and CourtListener confirms (`24-1279`), the First Circuit granted rehearing en banc in *Hussey v. City of Cambridge* (a police social-media *Pickering* termination) and has not yet issued its en banc decision, giving the Court an immediate reason to let the circuits percolate further.

Weighing the CFR, circuit tension, amicus mobilization, and *MacRae* hook upward from the 5.17% `baseline` reached anchor, and then discounting sharply for *Hedgepeth*'s recent denial and the cascading vehicle problems (*Monell* waiver, individual-capacity qualified immunity, assumed-*arguendo* *Connick* posture, uniformed self-identification, extreme facts, and pending CA1 en banc percolation), I set **`probability = 0.06`**.
