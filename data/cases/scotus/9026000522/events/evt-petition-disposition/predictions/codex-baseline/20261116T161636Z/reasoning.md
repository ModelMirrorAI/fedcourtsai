The principal anchor is the frozen **5.172958%** reached-baseline rate in `context.cert_anchor`, drawn from prior Terms under sal-v8. It is not the much lower terminal-baseline rate. The displayed sal-v8 segment table in `metrics/statpack.md` covers Terms 2017–2026; the prior-Term rows are 2017–2025. The frozen anchor is preferable to recomputing from rounded table entries. As a broad cross-check, the modern discretionary-cert table reports 642 grants and 640 GVRs among 45,129 resolved petitions, about 2.84% combined, but that broader population is not this paid, selected petition's controlling baseline.

There is **one docketed amicus brief**, from the Illinois Council of Police. The context's band-conditioned amicus reference is **16.3283%**, based on 2,315 resolved petitions in the `some` arm. The paid-segment statpack's one-brief bucket has n=1,163 resolved petitions, with 10.1% grants and 4.7% GVRs. These are useful comparators, not independent likelihood multipliers: the buckets measure eventual amicus support and correlate with the Court's other signals. The October 26 party letter says FIRE, the Fraternal Order of Police, and ACLJ intended to file before November 18. I credit the expressed interest modestly but do not count promised briefs as filed, assume their contents, or infer institutional support beyond the letter. The paid-segment terminal relist-0 bucket has n=10,284 resolved petitions and 1.8% combined grants/GVRs; relist-1 has n=2,539 and 13.5%; relist-2 has n=498 and 41.4%; relist-3+ has n=483 and 37.3%. The required broad corpus query returned recent grants, including both cert petitions and a substantive application. ## Why move above the anchor?

The petition presents an identifiable legal question rather than only disputing a factual result: whether projected loss of public trust can outweigh private-citizen speech on public concerns without evidence of actual or reasonably threatened operational disruption. Its strongest comparator is **Melton**, another firefighter's offensive Facebook speech. The Eighth Circuit rejected unsupported predictions and required further proceedings rather than defer automatically to the employer. I checked the opinion independently through CourtListener, especially the discussion surrounding its record/prediction mismatch. That makes the alleged disagreement more concrete than the petition's broader five-category taxonomy alone. [Sources: `questions-presented.txt`; petition pp. 8–13; Melton, CourtListener opinion 11119639, discussion around character 16967.]

**Noble** supplies additional support, involving even the same meme. Its majority specifically rejected treating the employee's public-facing position as sufficient where no patron was shown to have seen or objected to the post. But its limited audience and short posting duration also distinguish it from this petition. The majority/dissent disagreement illustrates both the issue's importance and its susceptibility to fact-specific balancing. [Sources: petition pp. 12–14; Noble, 112 F.4th 373, CourtListener opinion 10502456, majority footnote 5 and ensuing dissent.]

The petition quotes Justice Thomas's MacRae statement inviting an appropriate case about unsupported disruption claims. I treat that as a concrete interest signal from one Justice, **not four votes**, and rely on the quotation as presented in petition pp. 37–41 rather than claiming an independently retrieved MacRae text. The November 2 response request adds evidence of interest, although the record cannot establish whether it arose from the merits, impending amici, or both. Taken together with actual amicus participation, these considerations support moving above 5.17% and moderately above the 16.33% amicus reference.

## Why denial still leads

The decision below is narrower and more fact-sensitive than the petition's shorthand suggests. It rejected unsupported predictions of **coworker discord**, but separately linked community confidence to this firefighter/EMT's patient-facing duties, public identification with CFD, repeated posts, and disparagement of residents he served. It also describes actual public complaints prompting the investigation. Thus, absence of workplace disruption is not absence of complaints, public exposure, or contextual evidence. Respondents can argue that maintaining trust necessary to emergency services is itself an operational interest, and that other circuits would recognize it on these facts. [Sources: provisioned decision below, opinion pp. 19–25; petition Appendix A, particularly pp. 23a–30a.]

The lower-court summary identifies a July 20 CourtListener filing date after the July 16 judgment. I checked the opinion against the petition appendix: both identify the same parties, No. 24-2908, July 16 decision, panel, author, and basic holding. I treat it as the matched opinion, not an independent later judicial endorsement. The provisioned text repeats the opinion; repetition adds no evidentiary weight. The docket summary expressly lacks rehearing entries, so I infer neither denial of en banc rehearing nor absence of a rehearing dissent.

No opposition has yet tested preservation, remedies, or the claimed split. The unanimous panel opinion identifies several considerations supporting the employer, and the precedent comparisons remain context-dependent. Those limits keep my estimate at 20%, rather than the much higher rate appropriate to a genuinely multiply relisted or CVSG petition. The 0.62 stakes score concerns the potential national reach of the speech rule, not the chance of review.

## Other probabilities and limitations

I assign 98% to another distribution because a requested response remains outstanding; 2% to a new CVSG because the dispute does not present an evident specialized federal-government interest; 10% to a summary route **conditional on a grant**, since no intervening controlling decision is identified and resolving the alleged disagreement more naturally calls for argument; and 20% to a dissent or statement **conditional on denial**, chiefly reflecting the Thomas invitation quoted in the petition. The independent, unconditional order-writer probabilities are deliberately small and do not predict cert votes.

External retrieval was confined to historical legal context and corpus priors. I neither sought this petition's disposition nor consulted another forecast of it. Several web attempts supplied no usable source text; CourtListener's lower-court opinions supplied the independent comparison instead. The initial CLI invocation encountered a read-only default uv cache; setting `UV_CACHE_DIR=/tmp/uv-cache` allowed the existing environment to run without installing anything. The main substantive uncertainty is the unfiled opposition, not a missing snapshot.
