# Forecast — Inendino v. Nance-Holt, No. 26-522

The docket shows **one distribution** so far (for the conference of November 13, 2026), overtaken by the Court's November 2 request for a response.

- **Disposition.** The Court will deny the petition. Probability of any grant: 7%.
- **Further distribution.** The petition will be distributed once more after the City files its brief in opposition (due December 2, likely extended into January 2027) and the petitioner replies — most likely for a conference in January or February 2027. It will be denied at that conference or after at most one relist; I do not expect a string of relists. A relist after the post-response conference would signal either a Justice writing respecting denial or genuine grant interest.
- **CVSG.** None. No federal interest is implicated.
- **Question presented, if granted.** The Court would take the QP substantially as written — whether an asserted interest in public trust can outweigh employee speech under Pickering absent evidence of actual or reasonably threatened disruption — possibly trimmed to drop its argumentative preamble. The likely ground would be a requirement that predicted harm be supported by record evidence, not a wholesale revision of Pickering.
- **Summary disposition.** Unlikely. No intervening decision supports a GVR, and a per curiam reversal of a fact-bound balancing judgment on these facts is improbable. If the Court grants, it sets the case for argument.
- **Separate writing on denial.** Most likely none. If anyone writes, it is Justice Thomas, repeating the concern he voiced in *MacRae v. Mattos* about speculative disruption — about a one-in-eight chance given a denial.
