# Reasoning

## Case Context
This is a high-profile capital case (the 9/11 military commissions involving Khalid Shaikh Mohammad). The government (represented by Solicitor General D. John Sauer) successfully obtained a writ of mandamus from the D.C. Circuit to review an interlocutory procedural order. Petitioners (KSM et al.) seek certiorari to challenge the D.C. Circuit's jurisdiction under the All Writs Act.

## Grant Probability (15%)
While the case is of immense national importance (Big Case Score: 0.85), interlocutory criminal appeals by defendants are overwhelmingly denied certiorari. The Court typically prefers to await final judgment, even in capital cases, unless the procedural issue represents a severe structural violation that evades review. Given the posture, a denial is the most likely outcome, though the substantive stakes elevate the grant probability above baseline.

## Claims
- **disposition**: 0.15. Denial is highly likely due to the interlocutory posture.
- **relist-increment**: 0.80. As a capital case of massive historical and political consequence, it is highly likely to be relisted at least once so that a Justice (potentially dissenting from denial) can review the record or draft a statement.
- **cvsg-increment**: 0.0. The United States is already a party, represented by the Solicitor General, making a CVSG impossible.
- **dissent-from-denial**: 0.35. Given the high-profile nature and capital implications, there is a moderate chance a Justice issues a statement or dissent from the denial of certiorari.
- **summary-disposition-route**: 0.05. Highly unlikely to be summarily resolved in this interlocutory posture.
