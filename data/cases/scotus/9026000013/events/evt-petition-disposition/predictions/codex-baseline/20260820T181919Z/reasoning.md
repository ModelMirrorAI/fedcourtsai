# Rationale

I assign a 5% probability to any grant-family disposition and predict denial. The only case-specific provisioned input was `event.yaml`, which identifies an unresolved cert-stage petition at the distribution moment and supplies the caption and opening date. The expected `record/` directory was absent, so I had no snapshot, docket proceedings, questions presented, petition, brief in opposition, document manifest, or `context.json`. In particular, I could not observe the distribution count, CVSG state, fee class, originating court, Term, salience band, or salience version. I did not retrieve the live docket because the prompt forbids doing so when a forward cell lacks its provisioned snapshot.

For calibration I used the committed `metrics/statpack.md`. Because no frozen band was available, the prompt directs a cert cell to the weakest salience band's bracketed `reached` rate. Pooling the displayed OT2017–OT2025 baseline risk-set rows using their rounded rates and denominators gives approximately 6.5% for any grant. I moved modestly below that anchor because the missing filings provide no affirmative evidence of a split, clean vehicle, or other certworthy feature. The caption identifies litigation of exceptional public consequence, which supports the 0.94 big-case score, but importance alone does not establish a recurring legal conflict or vehicle quality.

The 25% relist-increment probability is a population-level estimate rather than a state-conditioned forecast: the statpack shows that most paid-segment petitions end with no relist, but a meaningful minority reach at least one. The record gap means I cannot tell whether this petition is at its first distribution or already beyond it, so this number should be discounted. The 0.5% CVSG-increment probability is low because nothing in the available record supports expecting the uncommon CVSG path. Conditional on a grant, I put 10% on a cert-order summary route; without an intervening authority or an identifiable correctable error in the provisioned material, plenary review would be more plausible than a GVR or summary reversal. Conditional on denial, I put 5% on any separate dissent or statement, reflecting the case's salience but the rarity of such writings.

The principal uncertainty is total absence of the provisioned record. These probabilities are conservative prior-based estimates, not a merits assessment of the petition or opposition.
