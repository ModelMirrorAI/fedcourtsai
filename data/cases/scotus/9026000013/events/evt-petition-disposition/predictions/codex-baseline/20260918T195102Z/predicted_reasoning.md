# Forecast

The Court will deny certiorari. The modal path is an unsigned denial following the September 28, 2026 conference, during early October, without another distribution or a separate writing. If the petition receives additional consideration, I expect one or two further distributions and disposition during October or November rather than an extended hold. These dates are forecasts, not a verified future orders calendar.

The provisioned record shows **one petition distribution**, on September 9 for the September 28 conference. The June distribution and June 29 grant concern the separate sealing motion, not review of the petition. From this one-distribution state, I assign **40%** to at least one further distribution. The unusually consequential prosecution and disputed use of mandamus make additional consideration plausible, but no further distribution remains the most likely individual path.

No CVSG appears in the record. I expect none, assigning **0.5%** to a new CVSG: the Solicitor General has already supplied the United States' position in the brief opposing this very petition.

I assign **16%** to any grant-family disposition. Conditional on a grant, plenary review is substantially more likely than disposition in the cert order: **12%** for the summary route, **88%** for briefing and argument. No identified intervening decision supplies a natural GVR ground. The competing readings of mandamus jurisdiction and of performance under the plea agreements favor fuller consideration if the Court intervenes.

If review is granted, I expect a narrowed version of question one: whether the D.C. Circuit's prospective military-commission appellate jurisdiction permits prosecution mandamus to prevent acceptance of these pleas despite the absence of an available interlocutory appeal. The related clear-and-indisputable requirement in question two would frame the application, rather than a general survey of every circuit's verbal formulation. Any merits holding would concern the availability or limits of extraordinary relief, not the defendants' guilt. These are conditional forecasts only.

Conditional on denial, I assign **30%** to at least one noted dissent or statement respecting denial; no separate writing is more likely. I do not predict a particular Justice's writing or infer petition votes from participation in the sealing motion. The anticipated denial leaves the appellate order in place without announcing the Court's agreement with its reasoning.
