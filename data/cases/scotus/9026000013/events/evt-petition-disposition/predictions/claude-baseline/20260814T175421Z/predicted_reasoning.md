# Predicted reasoning — Mohammad, et al. v. United States, No. 26-13 (evt-petition-disposition)

Forecast of what the Court will do with this petition, from the docket as of the
2026-08-14 snapshot.

## Procedural path

The docket shows one distribution so far — and it was of the sealing motion
(25M91), distributed June 10 for the June 25, 2026 conference and granted
June 29. The petition itself has never been distributed: the brief in
opposition is due August 26, 2026 (extended once, at the government's request).
So the state I am forecasting from is effectively pre-distribution on the
merits of cert.

- **Distribution and relists.** The petition will be distributed after the BIO
  arrives — most likely for the long conference (late September 2026) or the
  first October conference. I expect at least one further distribution with
  near-certainty (~0.97; only a withdrawal or dismissal before conference
  avoids it), and I expect **one to three relists beyond the first petition
  distribution**: this is exactly the profile — high-profile, amicus-supported,
  divided published panel below — that the Court holds for a close look or a
  written dissent from denial rather than resolving on first pass.
- **No CVSG.** The United States is the respondent, so the Court will not call
  for the Solicitor General's views (~0.01, essentially the mechanical
  impossibility discount).
- **Timing.** A disposition of the petition by the end of the 2026 calendar
  year — October–December 2026 — with January 2027 possible if it relists
  repeatedly.
- **Recusal.** Justice Kavanaugh took no part in the sealing motion; I expect
  him to take no part in the petition's disposition either, leaving an
  eight-Justice conference on this case.

## Disposition

**Deny (~87%).** The Court has turned away every Guantánamo military-commission
petition for more than a decade (al-Nashiri, al Bahlul, Baluchi), the Solicitor
General will oppose, the government won below, and the practical consequence of
a grant — reviving plea agreements that would spare the 9/11 defendants a
capital trial, over the objection of two administrations — is one several
Justices will be reluctant to invite. The posture is also functionally
interlocutory: denial leaves the commission trial to proceed, with the legal
questions preservable for post-conviction review.

If it denies, I expect a **meaningful chance (~40%) of a separate writing** — a
dissent from denial or statement respecting denial, most likely from Justice
Sotomayor or Justice Jackson (on the finality/plea-agreement side), with
Justice Gorsuch a plausible third given his past willingness to write on
Guantánamo-related denials.

**If granted (~13%),** the Court takes **QP 1 as written** — the All Writs Act
/ Will v. United States question of appellate mandamus jurisdiction where
Congress gave the government no right of appeal — and most likely QP 2 with it,
since the "clear and indisputable" standard is the merits half of the same
mandamus challenge. A grant limited to QP 1 alone is the likeliest narrowing. A
**summary disposition is unlikely**: there is no intervening decision to GVR
against, and a per curiam summary reversal of a divided published D.C. Circuit
opinion in a case of this magnitude is not this Court's practice — plenary
review with argument in the spring of 2027 would follow.
