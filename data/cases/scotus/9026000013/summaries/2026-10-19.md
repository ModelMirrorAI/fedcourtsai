## In brief

The Court declined to hear September 11 defendants' bid to undo an appeals court order that let the government withdraw from plea deals sparing them the death penalty.

## What happened

Khalid Shaikh Mohammad and a co-defendant, accused of planning the September 11 attacks, signed pretrial agreements in 2024 to plead guilty in exchange for the government not seeking the death penalty. Two days later, the Secretary of Defense withdrew from those agreements. A military judge and a military appeals court ruled the withdrawal came too late, since the defendants had already begun performing their side of the deal. On the government's request, the D.C. Circuit issued writs of mandamus and prohibition overriding those rulings and letting the withdrawal stand, finding the government had a clear right to relief. The defendants asked the Supreme Court to review that decision.

## What the Court is being asked

The case raised two questions: whether the appeals court had power to issue this kind of emergency order when Congress had not given the government a right to appeal this sort of ruling in these proceedings, and whether the appeals court was right that the government's entitlement to that order was clear and beyond dispute, given that a lower military court had ruled the other way.

## Where it stands

The petition was distributed for several conferences, including ones in September and October 2026, before the Court denied it on October 19, 2026.

## What each outcome would mean

Mohammad and his co-defendant, as petitioners, asked the Court to take up their case. The denial means the Court will not review the matter, so the D.C. Circuit's order stands: the Secretary's withdrawal from the plea agreements remains in effect, the military judge cannot accept the guilty pleas under those agreements, and the case returns to the military commission process, where the government may continue to pursue the death penalty.
