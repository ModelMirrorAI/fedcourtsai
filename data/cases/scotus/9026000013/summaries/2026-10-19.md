## In brief

The Court declined to review accused September 11 defendants' bid to undo an appeals court order that blocked their guilty pleas under withdrawn plea agreements.

## What happened

Khalid Shaikh Mohammad and a co-defendant, facing capital charges before a military commission for the September 11 attacks, had signed plea agreements in which they would plead guilty in exchange for the government not seeking the death penalty. Two days later, the Secretary of Defense withdrew from those agreements. The military judge and a military appellate court ruled the withdrawal came too late, but the United States Court of Appeals for the District of Columbia Circuit, acting on the government's request, issued orders blocking the guilty pleas and giving effect to the Secretary's withdrawal. The defendants asked the Supreme Court to review that ruling.

## What the Court is being asked

The case asks whether the appeals court had authority to intervene this way when Congress gave the government no right to an ordinary appeal at this stage of a military commission case, and whether the appeals court was wrong to find the government's position so clearly correct that this unusual intervention was justified, when the military judge had followed existing precedent.

## Where it stands

The petition was distributed for several conferences, rescheduled at points, and on October 19, 2026, the Court denied the petition.

## What each outcome would mean

Mohammad and his co-defendant, who asked the Court to take the case, sought to overturn the appeals court's decision so their guilty pleas under the original plea agreements could proceed without the death penalty. By denying the petition, the Court leaves the appeals court's decision in place: the Secretary's withdrawal from the plea agreements stands, the guilty pleas under those agreements cannot go forward, and the military commission case continues without that question being decided by the Supreme Court.
