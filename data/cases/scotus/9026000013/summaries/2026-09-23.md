## What happened

Khalid Shaikh Mohammad and a co-defendant, accused of planning the September 11, 2001 attacks, are being tried before a military commission at Guantanamo Bay. In 2024, a military official authorized to negotiate on the government's behalf signed plea agreements sparing them the death penalty in exchange for guilty pleas. Two days later, the Secretary of Defense tried to withdraw from those agreements. The military judge ruled the withdrawal came too late because the defendants had already begun acting on the agreements. On the government's request, the D.C. Circuit Court of Appeals overturned that ruling and blocked the guilty pleas from being entered, using its power to issue emergency orders (mandamus and prohibition) rather than through a normal appeal. The defendants asked the Supreme Court to review that decision.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked whether an appeals court may use its mandamus power to overturn a trial-level ruling in a criminal case when Congress has not given the government any ordinary right to appeal that kind of ruling, and when the order does not end the prosecution. It is also asked whether that emergency power was used properly here, given that the trial judge had followed existing legal precedent rather than committing a clear, obvious legal error.
