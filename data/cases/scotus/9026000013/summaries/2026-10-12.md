## In brief

September 11 defendants ask the Court to undo an appeals court order that blocked their guilty pleas under plea agreements the Pentagon tried to cancel.

## What happened

Khalid Shaikh Mohammad and a co-defendant, accused in the 2001 September 11 attacks, signed pretrial agreements to plead guilty in exchange for the government not seeking the death penalty. Two days later, the Secretary of Defense tried to withdraw from those agreements. A military judge and the Court of Military Commission Review both ruled the withdrawal came too late because the defendants had already begun performing their side of the deal. On the government's request, the D.C. Circuit issued writs of mandamus and prohibition overriding those rulings, holding the government had a clear right to withdraw and blocking the guilty pleas from going forward. Rehearing en banc was denied.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked two things: first, whether an appeals court may use its power to issue emergency orders to review a trial judge's procedural ruling in a criminal case when Congress gave the government no ordinary right to appeal that ruling and the ruling did not end the case; second, whether that emergency power may be used to override a trial court's ruling when the trial court was following an established, undisputed legal rule rather than committing a clear and obvious error.

## Where it stands

The petition has been fully briefed, including an opposition from the government and a reply from the defendants, and was distributed for conferences in September and October 2026. The docket shows the case was rescheduled from both conferences, with no decision yet recorded on whether the Court will hear it.

## What each outcome would mean

Mohammad and his co-defendant, as petitioners, are asking the Court to take up the case. If the Court grants review, it will decide the legal questions itself, after full briefing and argument, without yet deciding who is right. If the Court denies review, the D.C. Circuit's decision stands, meaning the Secretary of Defense's withdrawal from the plea agreements remains in effect and the guilty pleas the defendants sought to enter stay blocked, leaving the underlying military commission case to continue without the plea agreements.
