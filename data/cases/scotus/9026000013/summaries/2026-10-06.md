## In brief

Khalid Shaikh Mohammad and a co-defendant ask the Court to undo an appeals court order that let the government withdraw from plea agreements sparing them the death penalty.

## What happened

Petitioners are charged before a military commission at Guantanamo Bay over the September 11 attacks. In 2024 a military official approved plea agreements under which petitioners would plead guilty in exchange for the government not seeking the death penalty. The Secretary of Defense then tried to withdraw from those agreements. The military judge and a military appellate court refused to honor the withdrawal, finding petitioners had already begun performing their side of the deal. On the government's request, the D.C. Circuit issued writs of mandamus and prohibition giving effect to the Secretary's withdrawal and barring further proceedings under the agreements. Petitioners' request for rehearing by the full D.C. Circuit was denied.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked whether the appeals court had authority to issue this kind of emergency order when Congress had not given the government a right to appeal the military judge's ruling, and whether the appeals court was wrong to find the government's case so clear-cut that this extraordinary intervention was justified, given that the military judge had followed existing precedent.

## Where it stands

The petition has been distributed for more than one conference, most recently in October 2026, after full briefing including an opposition from the government and a reply; the docket shows no decision yet on whether the Court will hear the case.

## What each outcome would mean

Mohammad and his co-petitioner are asking the Court to take up the case. If the Court grants review, it will decide the legal questions after further briefing and argument, deciding nothing yet about who wins. If it denies review, the D.C. Circuit's decision stands, meaning the Secretary's withdrawal from the plea agreements remains in effect and the agreements do not bind the government, leaving petitioners' underlying military commission prosecution to continue without the plea deal.
