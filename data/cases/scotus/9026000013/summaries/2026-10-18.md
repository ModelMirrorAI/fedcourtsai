## In brief

Accused September 11 plotters ask the Court to review a ruling letting the Defense Secretary withdraw from plea agreements that would have avoided the death penalty.

## What happened

Khalid Shaikh Mohammad and a co-defendant face capital charges before a military commission for the September 11 attacks. In 2024, a military official approved plea agreements sparing them the death penalty; two days later the Secretary of Defense withdrew from those agreements. A military judge ruled the withdrawal came too late because the defendants had already begun performing their promises, and a military appeals court agreed. On the government's request, the D.C. Circuit issued writs of mandamus and prohibition overturning those rulings and clearing the way for the Secretary's withdrawal to take effect, over a partial dissent. The full D.C. Circuit declined to rehear the case.

## What the Court is being asked

The petitioners raise two questions. First, whether a federal appeals court may use its power to issue emergency orders to review a trial-level ruling in a criminal case when the government has no ordinary right to appeal that ruling and no dismissal resulted. Second, whether a trial court's ruling that followed settled, undisputed precedent can still be overturned through that kind of emergency order, which is normally reserved for clear and obvious errors.

## Where it stands

The petition has been fully briefed, including the government's opposition and the petitioners' reply, and amicus briefs were filed. It has been distributed for several conferences, most recently one scheduled after the snapshot date, and the docket shows no decision yet on whether the Court will hear the case.

## What each outcome would mean

Mohammad and his co-defendant, as petitioners, are asking the Court to take up their case. If the Court grants review, it will hear full arguments on whether the appeals court properly used mandamus to revive the Secretary's withdrawal, deciding nothing about who is right simply by taking the case. If the Court denies review, the D.C. Circuit's ruling stands, leaving the Secretary's withdrawal in effect and the plea agreements void, with the capital prosecution continuing.
