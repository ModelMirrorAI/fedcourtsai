Distributed once so far; expect the petition to be denied without further relists. The petition is a pro se challenge to state court civil protective orders, raising Second Amendment, Due Process, and Supremacy Clause arguments against both state laws (RCW 7.105) and federal statutes (VAWA / 18 U.S.C. § 922(g)(8)). Such fact-bound, pro se family law disputes are overwhelmingly denied by the Court, as they generally lack a clean circuit split or broader national significance beyond the petitioner's own grievances. No CVSG is expected, as the case does not cleanly implicate federal interests in a way that would require the Solicitor General's views, and the Solicitor General is rarely invited in pro se domestic relations matters. If granted, the Court would likely narrow the questions to the constitutionality of the federal enforcement of state civil protection orders (e.g., 18 U.S.C. § 922(g)(8)), which it recently addressed in *Rahimi*, rather than reviewing the state procedures themselves. A summary disposition route (like a GVR) is unlikely, as no immediately intervening decision alters the landscape since *Rahimi*. Should it deny, expect no separate writing or dissent from denial.
