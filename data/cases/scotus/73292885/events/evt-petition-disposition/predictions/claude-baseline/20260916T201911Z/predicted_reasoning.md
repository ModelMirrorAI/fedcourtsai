# Forecast: what the Court will do with No. 25-1249, Pestarino v. Pestarino

**Disposition.** The petition will be denied at or shortly after the September 28, 2026 conference (the "long conference"), without comment, in the order list of early October 2026.

**Relists.** The docket shows exactly one distribution (June 17, 2026, for the conference of September 28, 2026); the petition has never been relisted. I expect no further distribution: a pro se paid petition from a state family-law protective-order proceeding, raising no circuit split and resting on an unpublished intermediate appellate opinion, is the paradigm of a first-conference denial. The residual chance of one more distribution is mostly administrative (a reschedule or a routine relist of a long-conference petition) rather than a signal of interest, and I do not expect a hold for any pending Second Amendment or due process case, since the firearm question the petition raises is the one *United States v. Rahimi* (2024) has already answered on the federal side.

**CVSG.** None. The United States is not a party, the respondent is a private individual, and the Court does not invite the Solicitor General's views on pro se petitions attacking federal statutes collaterally in a state domestic-relations appeal.

**Which question, if any.** If the Court took anything, it would not be the omnibus question as written, which bundles due process, the Second Amendment, the Supremacy Clause, bills of attainder, and immigration law into a single sentence. A grant is only conceivable as a hold-and-GVR in light of some intervening decision on protective-order firearm prohibitions, not as plenary review of any part of the question presented.

**Summary route versus plenary review.** Conditional on any grant, the summary route (a GVR) is the likelier form, because no version of this record is a vehicle the Court would set for argument.

**Dissent or statement respecting denial.** None. No Justice is likely to write separately on a pro se state protective-order petition; Justice Thomas's occasional Second Amendment statements attach to counseled vehicles with developed lower-court opinions, which this is not.
