## In brief

The federal government asks the Court to review a ruling that struck down a presidential proclamation barring asylum for migrants crossing the southern border illegally.

## What happened

In January 2025, the President issued a proclamation suspending entry at the southern border and barring covered migrants from applying for asylum, withholding of removal, or similar protections. Immigrant advocacy groups and thirteen individuals sued, arguing the proclamation exceeded the government's authority under immigration law. A district court ruled for the challengers, certified a nationwide class, and ordered officials to stop enforcing the policy's restrictions on asylum and related protections. The U.S. Court of Appeals for the District of Columbia Circuit largely affirmed, though it narrowed the class definition and a different panel had earlier allowed the asylum restriction to stay in effect temporarily while the case proceeded.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked three questions: whether the proclamation and the agency guidance carrying it out are lawful under immigration statutes; whether a federal law limiting courts' power to block removal-related enforcement actions on a classwide basis prevented the lower court from issuing the order it did; and whether the group of people covered by the lawsuit was properly defined under class-action rules and constitutional limits on who may sue.

## Where it stands

The petition has been fully briefed, including an opposition brief, several amicus briefs, and the government's reply. The case was distributed for the Court's conference on November 6, 2026, and the docket shows no action since.

## What each outcome would mean

The government, as petitioner, is asking the Court to reverse the D.C. Circuit's decision. If the Court grants review, it will hear full arguments and decide the legal questions itself, deciding nothing at this stage about who is right. If it denies review, the D.C. Circuit's ruling stands, leaving in place the block on the proclamation's asylum restrictions and the class action brought by the immigrant advocacy groups and individual challengers.
