## In brief

The government asks the Court to revive a proclamation barring asylum for migrants crossing the southern border without authorization, after lower courts blocked it.

## What happened

In January 2025, the President issued a proclamation suspending entry across the southern border and directing that covered migrants be barred from applying for asylum, withholding of removal, or protection against torture, implemented through agency guidance creating new removal procedures. Several immigrant-assistance organizations and individuals sued, arguing this violated the asylum statute and other immigration laws. A federal district court ruled for the challengers, certified a class, and blocked the policy's asylum restrictions and new removal procedures. The U.S. Court of Appeals for the D.C. Circuit largely affirmed, though it narrowed the certified class and a stay panel had earlier allowed part of the policy to continue while the case proceeded.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked three things: whether the President had authority to bar migrants from applying for asylum and other protections against removal; whether a law limiting courts' power to block removal procedures prevented the lower court from issuing the broad order it did; and whether the group of people covered by that order was properly defined under class-action rules and constitutional limits on who can sue.

## Where it stands

The petition was distributed for conferences on November 6 and November 13, 2026; the docket does not yet show a decision on whether the Court will hear the case.

## What each outcome would mean

The federal government, as petitioner, is asking the Court to reverse the D.C. Circuit's ruling, which favored the challengers led by the Refugee and Immigrant Center for Education and Legal Services. If the Court grants review, it will decide the legal questions after full briefing and argument, deciding nothing yet about who prevails. If it denies review, the D.C. Circuit's decision stands, leaving in place the block on the asylum ban and the broad class the lower courts certified, pending any further proceedings in that case.
