## 3. Case-Specific Adjustments and Probability Decomposition (`P(disturbed) = 0.84`)

Starting from the **`0.7054`** disturbed base rate, I adjust upward by **`+0.135`** to **`P(disturbed) = 0.84`** (`judgment: "reversed"`). That adjustment reflects two cumulative paths by which Petitioners win reversal or vacatur of the D.C. Circuit's judgment, tempered by genuine textualist counter-arguments that keep the undisturbed tail at **`0.16`**:

1. **Cumulative Structure of the Limited Grant (Questions 1 and 2 Granted; Question 3 Denied):**
   The D.C. Circuit affirmed in full the district court's summary judgment and classwide injunction barring DHS from implementing Proclamation 10888 to preclude asylum or conduct non-statutory removals. By granting **both** Question 1 (substantive lawfulness) and Question 2 (§ 1252(f)(1)) while leaving behind Question 3 (Rule 23 commonality / Article III standing of future class members), the Court positioned itself to disturb the judgment below if the Government prevails on *either* Question 1 *or* Question 2.
2. **Strength of Question 1's Discretionary-Asylum Ground (Pet. 17–20):**
   In the Supreme Court, the Solicitor General did **not** defend precluding mandatory statutory withholding of removal (§ 1231(b)(3)(A)) or regulatory CAT protections. Instead, the SG centered Question 1 on **asylum**, arguing that because granting asylum under 8 U.S.C. § 1158(b)(1)(A) is discretionary ("*may* grant"), a § 1182(f) proclamation categorically exercising executive discretion to deny asylum makes accepting and adjudicating applications under § 1158(a)(1) futile (*Huisha-Huisha v. Mayorkas*, 27 F.4th 718, 730–31 (D.C. Cir. 2022)). Crucially, that exact distinction persuaded not only Judges Katsas and Walker below (and Judge Ho in *Texas v. Biden*, 161 F.4th 881 (5th Cir. 2025)), **but also Judge Patricia Millett** in voting to stay the district court's asylum injunction pending appeal (Pet. App. 166a–167a). Combined with *Trump v. Hawaii*, 585 U.S. at 684, 688–95 (rejecting intra-INA implied limits on § 1182(f)), and *Mullin v. Al Otro Lado* (June 25, 2026) (reversing 6–3 an expansive reading of § 1158(a)(1)), the six conservative Justices are strongly predisposed (~`56%`–`62%` on Question 1A) to uphold Proclamation 10888's preclusion of discretionary asylum—without needing to endorse the SG's far weaker second sub-argument (Pet. 20–23) that §§ 1182(f) and 1185(a)(1) authorize extra-statutory expulsions bypassing §§ 1225(b) and 1229a, a theory rejected 6–0 across the D.C. Circuit stay and merits panels.
3. **Independent Backstop of Question 2 (`8 U.S.C. § 1252(f)(1)`):**
   Even if two conservative Justices (such as Chief Justice Roberts, Justice Gorsuch, or Justice Barrett) sided with Respondents on Question 1's text—as occurred when majorities struck down IEEPA tariffs in *Learning Resources* (Feb. 2026) and the Birthright Citizenship EO in *Trump v. Barbara* (June 2026)—the Government has an exceptionally strong independent path on Question 2. In *Aleman Gonzalez*, 596 U.S. at 550, all six conservative Justices held that § 1252(f)(1) bars classwide injunctions ordering officials "to take or to refrain from taking actions to enforce, implement, or otherwise carry out" Part IV (§§ 1221–1232). Because Proclamation 10888 is a Presidential action not subject to APA vacatur (*Franklin v. Massachusetts*, 505 U.S. 788 (1992)), plaintiffs rely directly on the district court's **classwide injunction**, which forbids removing absent class members outside Part IV (§§ 1225(b), 1229a) or without asylum screening (conducted under § 1225(b)(1)(B)). As Judges Katsas and Walker explained, commanding DHS to channel removals exclusively through Part IV and its credible-fear procedures directly orders officials how to operate Part IV.
4. **Judgment-Class Decomposition (`0.84` Disturbed vs. `0.16` Undisturbed):**
   - `reversed`: **`0.55`** — Modal outcome. In *Hawaii*, *Aleman Gonzalez*, *Biden v. Texas*, and *Al Otro Lado*, the Court entered `"reversed and remanded"` when overturning the circuit's merits/remedial rulings, even where remand issues remained.
   - `vacated`: **`0.22`** — Substantial secondary probability if the Court resolves § 1252(f)(1) in tandem with *DHS v. D.V.D.* (No. 26-426, argued Dec. 9, 2026) or vacates for the D.C. Circuit to untangle the unchallenged withholding/CAT Guidance vacatur from the reversed asylum and injunction holdings.
   - `affirmed-in-part-reversed-in-part`: **`0.07`** — Elevated above the `2.1%` statpack base rate because the Court could formally affirm on non-INA removal procedures (or Question 1) while reversing the classwide injunction under Question 2, though kept modest because the Court entered zero `in_part` judgments across `232` parsed cases in OT2022–OT2025.
   - `affirmed`: **`0.13`** — Main downside risk to my call. *Aleman Gonzalez*, 596 U.S. at 553 n.4, expressly left open whether an injunction directed at non-Part IV authority with "collateral effects" on Part IV survives § 1252(f)(1) (as the Fifth Circuit held in *Texas v. DHS*, 123 F.4th 186, 210 (5th Cir. 2024)), and the SG did not separately challenge APA vacatur of the Guidance in its petition (BIO 31). Following *Barbara* and *Learning Resources*, a 5–4 coalition of Justices Sotomayor, Kagan, and Jackson plus two of `{Roberts, Barrett, Gorsuch}` affirming on both § 1158(a)(1)/(b)(2)(C) text and *Aleman Gonzalez* footnote 4 is a real ~`13%` possibility.
