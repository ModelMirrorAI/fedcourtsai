# Predicted Court Reasoning — *Mullin v. Refugee and Immigrant Center for Education and Legal Services* (No. 26-239)

## Judgment and Vote Lineup

The Supreme Court will **reverse** the judgment of the D.C. Circuit and remand for further proceedings (`reversed`), dividing **6–3** along the same lineup as *Mullin v. Al Otro Lado* (2026) and *Garland v. Aleman Gonzalez*, 596 U.S. 543 (2022): **Chief Justice Roberts** and **Justices Thomas, Alito, Gorsuch, Kavanaugh, and Barrett** in the majority, with **Justices Sotomayor, Kagan, and Jackson** in dissent.

## Questions Reached

In granting certiorari on November 13, 2026, the Court limited review to Questions 1 and 2 of the petition and declined to take up Question 3 (the Article III and Rule 23 class-definition challenge). Following *Biden v. Texas*, 597 U.S. 785, 798–801 (2022), the Court will treat 8 U.S.C. § 1252(f)(1) as a limitation on lower courts' remedial power rather than a bar to subject-matter jurisdiction, enabling the majority to resolve **both** Question 2's remedial bar and Question 1's substantive asylum question rather than remanding on § 1252(f)(1) alone.

## Doctrinal Basis of the Majority Opinion (`majority-ground`)

Extending *Trump v. Hawaii*, 585 U.S. 667 (2018), and *Garland v. Aleman Gonzalez*, 596 U.S. 543 (2022), the majority will rest its reversal on two reinforcing statutory holdings:

1. **Discretionary Preclusion of Asylum Under §§ 1182(f) and 1158(b)(1)(A) (Question 1):** While 8 U.S.C. § 1158(a)(1) permits an alien physically present in the United States to apply for asylum regardless of status, 8 U.S.C. § 1158(b)(1)(A) makes the ultimate grant of asylum strictly discretionary ("*may* grant asylum"), in sharp contrast to mandatory withholding of removal under 8 U.S.C. § 1231(b)(3)(A) ("*may not* remove"). Drawing on *Hawaii*'s recognition that § 1182(f) vests broad authority in the President to suspend entry and impose accompanying restrictions, alongside *INS v. Cardoza-Fonseca*, 480 U.S. 421 (1987), and *Lopez v. Davis*, 531 U.S. 230 (2001), the majority will hold that when a Presidential proclamation under § 1182(f) categorically exercises executive discretion against granting asylum to aliens who enter in violation of an entry suspension, neither § 1158(a)(1) nor the agency-rulemaking provision in § 1158(b)(2)(C) compels DHS to accept and adjudicate tens of thousands of futile asylum applications or release entrants while doing so. As in *Hawaii*'s rejection of an implied § 1152(a)(1)(A) constraint on § 1182(f), § 1158(b)(2)(C) governs eligibility regulations promulgated by the Attorney General or Secretary of Homeland Security, not a Presidential proclamation foreclosing discretionary relief at the threshold.
2. **Section 1252(f)(1)'s Bar on Classwide Injunctive Relief (Question 2):** Applying *Aleman Gonzalez*, 596 U.S. at 550, the majority will hold that 8 U.S.C. § 1252(f)(1) independently barred the district court's classwide injunction. Prohibiting federal officials as to absent class members from using non-statutory repatriation proceedings and from removing class members without complying with the asylum statute directly orders officials to enforce, implement, and carry out Part IV removal procedures—specifically expedited removal and credible-fear screening under 8 U.S.C. § 1225(b)(1) and ordinary removal proceedings under 8 U.S.C. § 1229a. The majority will reject the D.C. Circuit's "collateral effect" carveout insofar as plaintiffs frame an injunction around Part I (§ 1158) or Part II (§§ 1182(f), 1185(a)(1)) to dictate how Part IV removals of absent class members must proceed, and will coordinate any discussion of non-injunctive remedies on remand with *DHS v. D.V.D.*, No. 26-426.

## Breadth of the Holding (`ground-breadth`)

The majority will resolve the merits on statutory harmonization of §§ 1182(f) and 1158(b)(1)(A) and § 1252(f)(1)'s remedial bar across the certified class, while stopping short of holding either that Article II confers inherent extra-statutory expulsion power or that § 1182(f) authorizes summary removals inside the United States that bypass Part IV's statutory procedures or mandatory withholding of removal under § 1231(b)(3) and Convention Against Torture (CAT) protections. Because the Government did not seek Supreme Court review of the lower courts' rulings preserving mandatory statutory withholding of removal and regulatory CAT screening—and because expedited removal under § 1225(b)(1) already allows prompt removal once futile asylum claims are foreclosed—the majority has no need to endorse a standalone non-INA deportation regime on U.S. soil, leaving the interaction between any surviving APA vacatur of the Implementing Guidance on non-asylum grounds and *DHS v. D.V.D.* to be addressed on remand.

## Opinion Assignment and Separate Writings

- **Majority Opinion:** **Chief Justice Roberts** is the single likeliest author (having written *Trump v. Hawaii* on § 1182(f) and *Biden v. Texas* on § 1252(f)(1)), closely followed by **Justice Alito** (author of *Aleman Gonzalez* and *Mullin v. Al Otro Lado*), **Justice Kavanaugh**, and **Justice Barrett**.
- **Separate Concurrences:** **Justice Thomas** is likely to concur separately to argue that §§ 1182(f) and 1185(a)(1)—reinforced by inherent executive foreign-affairs power—also authorize direct repulsion and repatriation of unlawful border crossers, and to reiterate skepticism of universal APA vacatur. **Justice Gorsuch** (and potentially **Justice Kavanaugh** or **Justice Barrett**) is a live candidate to write separately to underscore the narrow boundary of the Court's Question 1 holding: executive discretion under § 1158(b)(1)(A) permits categorical pre-denial of discretionary asylum during a § 1182(f) suspension, but § 1182(f)'s power to suspend *entry* does not rewrite Part IV's statutory removal procedures or mandatory *non-refoulement* protections under § 1231(b)(3) and FARRA.
- **Dissents:** **Justice Sotomayor** is most likely to author the principal dissent for the three-Justice minority (joined by Justices Kagan and Jackson), arguing that § 1158(a)(1) unambiguously guarantees every noncitizen physically present on U.S. soil the right to apply for asylum "irrespective of such alien's status," that Congress channeled any categorical eligibility bars exclusively through regulations "consistent with this section" under § 1158(b)(2)(C), and that § 1252(f)(1) protects Congress's Part IV scheme rather than insulating ultra vires proclamations issued under Part II. **Justice Jackson** also carries a substantial likelihood of adding a separate dissent focused on statutory structure and remedial accountability, as she did in *Trump v. CASA, Inc.* (2025) and *Mullin v. Al Otro Lado* (2026).

## Procedural Exits

Neither a dismissal as improvidently granted (`dismissed-as-improvidently-granted`) nor an affirmance by an equally divided Court (`affirmed-by-an-equally-divided-court`) is a likely exit. Certiorari was granted on the Solicitor General's petition with Question 3 deliberately pared off, Proclamation 10888 remains continuously in force by its terms until a Presidential finding ceases it, and no sitting Justice participated in the D.C. Circuit proceedings below (which began in February 2025).
