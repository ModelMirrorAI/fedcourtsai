# Forecast — Mullin v. Refugee and Immigrant Center for Education and Legal Services (No. 26-239)

**Judgment.** The Court will reverse the D.C. Circuit's judgment and remand. It will not affirm in full. A dismissal as improvidently granted or an equally divided Court is not a live prospect: nine Justices are sitting and the case is a clean final judgment.

**Lineup.** 6–3. Roberts, Thomas, Alito, Gorsuch, Kavanaugh and Barrett in the majority; Sotomayor, Kagan and Jackson in dissent.

**Question 1 (legality of Proclamation 10,888 and its guidance).** The Court will reach it and rule for the government on asylum. The majority will reason from the statute, not from inherent Article II power: 8 U.S.C. 1158(b)(1)(A) makes the grant of asylum discretionary, nothing in §1158 requires that discretion to be exercised one application at a time, and so the Executive may categorically deny asylum to aliens who enter in violation of a §1182(f) suspension and need not process applications whose denial is foreordained. The Court will reject the D.C. Circuit's view that this is an eligibility bar that had to be adopted by regulation under §1158(b)(2)(C). *Trump v. Hawaii* will be cited for the breadth of §1182(f), but the holding will rest on §1158.

**Breadth.** The holding will be confined to asylum. The Court will not hold that §1182(f) or §1185(a) lets the President set aside the INA's removal procedures generally, and it will leave in place the rulings below that covered aliens keep access to withholding of removal and Convention Against Torture protection, which the government no longer contests.

**Question 2 (§1252(f)(1)).** The Court will address it and hold that the classwide injunction is barred at least insofar as it dictates the procedures by which class members are removed, applying *Garland v. Aleman Gonzalez*. It will leave the vacatur of the guidance, which the government did not challenge, for the remand.

**Question 3 (class definition).** Not before the Court; the grant excluded it. Expect at most a passing remark in a concurrence.

**Who writes.** The Chief Justice is the most likely author of the opinion of the Court, with Alito, Kavanaugh and Barrett the next most likely. Sotomayor will write the principal dissent; Jackson will probably write separately as well. Thomas is the most likely concurrence, on the President's inherent authority over the exclusion of aliens. Gorsuch or Kavanaugh may add a short concurrence on the scope of §1252(f)(1) or class relief.

**Timing.** Argument in the February or March 2027 sitting; decision in the last two weeks of June 2027.
