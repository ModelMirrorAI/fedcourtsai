## Opposition, vehicle, and route

The opposition supplies substantial reasons not to treat review as certain. Its introduction and argument I distinguish Congress's express right to apply for asylum under Section 1158(a)(1) from discretion ultimately to grant it, and challenge the government's categorical-discretion theory as a post hoc rationale under Chenery. Its arguments II–III assert that the government has not challenged independent vacatur of the guidance, that Section 1252(f)(1) does not reach the asylum provision, and that the clarified class covers people only when injury becomes imminent. The panel opinion's conclusion and opening of Walker's dissent confirm both the genuine disagreement over asylum and classwide relief and agreement that mandatory protections against removal to persecution cannot simply be discarded. Those limitations make the government's request broader than the strongest reviewable question.

The reply disputes the asserted distinction between eligibility and discretion, defends its original rationale, and contends that an injunction effectively compelling use of statutory removal procedures is covered by Section 1252(f)(1). The staged filings discuss Garland v. Aleman Gonzalez, 596 U.S. 543 (2022), as imposing an existing restriction on lower-court classwide injunctions; that precedent does not automatically answer this petition's different challenge to an asserted extra-statutory regime or the availability of APA vacatur. CourtListener verified the precedent's identity and supplied an official PDF address, but I received no readable web-tool payload and did not independently read its opinion. The opposition's narrower statutory and remedial arguments could persuade the Court to deny, limit the grant, or defer action. They are not reasons to equate a contested merits position with a poor cert petition automatically.

The reply expressly proposes a hold for DHS v. D.V.D., No. 26-426. The separate corpus row verifies its grant. The reply, page 12, identifies a Section 1252(f)(1) question involving a broad class and reports argument scheduled for December 9, 2026. My attempted official-docket web read returned no readable tool payload, so the exact granted questions and argument schedule were not independently verified from that docket. I rely on the staged reply for those limited descriptions, not an assumed full companion record or a supposed resolution of its merits. This supports a hold-and-GVR alternative, but its force is uncertain without the companion's filings. It is not a `Vide` or shared-amicus relationship in the provisioned record, and I do not label it as one. Nothing in the available descriptions establishes that D.V.D. subsumes this petition's central categorical-asylum question, which is why plenary review remains the modal route.

An illustrative disposition allocation is 63% plenary grant, 25% GVR, 2% summary reversal, 9% denial, and 1% dismissal or withdrawal. Thus any grant is 90%, while the conditional summary-disposition route is 27/90 = **30%**. A hold alone does not resolve the event. The timing and route uncertainty are larger than uncertainty about eventual intervention.
