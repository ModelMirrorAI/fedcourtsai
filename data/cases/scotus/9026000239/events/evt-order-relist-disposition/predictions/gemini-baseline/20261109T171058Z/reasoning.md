## 2. Analysis of the Provisioned Record (`petition.txt`, `brief-in-opposition.txt`, `cert-reply.txt`, `decision-below.txt`, `lower-court-docket.txt`)

All core cert-stage filings were provisioned with full text (`empty_text: false`, `ocr_derived: false`, `truncated: false`):
- **`questions-presented.txt` & `petition.txt` (122 pages):** The Solicitor General seeks review of the D.C. Circuit's judgment affirming in large part a universal/classwide permanent injunction and APA vacatur against Presidential Proclamation No. 10,888 (90 Fed. Reg. 8333) and its DHS implementing guidance. The petition presents three questions: (1) whether the Proclamation and guidance—which suspended entry across the southern border under 8 U.S.C. §§ 1182(f) and 1185(a)(1) and precluded covered noncitizens from accessing asylum under 8 U.S.C. § 1158—are lawful; (2) whether 8 U.S.C. § 1252(f)(1) bars the district court's classwide injunctive relief; and (3) whether certifying a Rule 23(b)(2) class of all current *and future* noncitizens who become subject to the Proclamation in the United States comports with Article III and Rule 23.
- **`decision-below.txt` (131 pages) & `lower-court-docket.txt`:** Checked against the petition, `decision-below.txt` is the exact D.C. Circuit opinion under review (No. 25-5243, decided April 24, 2026). Judge Childs (joined by Judge Pillard) authored the majority opinion; Judge Walker authored a comprehensive partial concurrence and partial dissent (~90 pages). While the panel unanimously rejected extra-statutory repatriation and the abrogation of mandatory withholding (§ 1231(b)(3)) and CAT protections, and unanimously held that § 1252(f)(1) does not bar APA vacatur, Judge Walker dissented on all three questions presented in the SG's petition:
  1. On **QP 1 (Asylum)**, Judge Walker (like Judges Millett and Katsas on the earlier stay panel) concluded that because asylum under § 1158(b)(1)(A) is discretionary, the President may exercise that discretion categorically up front to deny asylum to aliens who enter in violation of an § 1182(f) suspension, making asylum applications futile under *Huisha-Huisha v. Mayorkas*, 27 F.4th 718 (D.C. Cir. 2022).
  2. On **QP 2 (§ 1252(f)(1))**, Judge Walker (like Judge Katsas at the stay stage) concluded that enjoining the government from removing class members outside Part IV procedures indirectly compels the operation of Part IV (§§ 1225, 1229a) on a classwide basis, contravening *Garland v. Aleman Gonzalez*, 596 U.S. 543 (2022).
  3. On **QP 3 (Article III / Rule 23)**, Judge Walker argued that under *TransUnion LLC v. Ramirez*, 594 U.S. 413 (2021), and *Trump v. CASA, Inc.*, 606 U.S. 831 (2025), a district court cannot certify an open-ended Rule 23(b)(2) class encompassing future border-crossers who have not yet entered the country or suffered an injury-in-fact, nor treat heterogeneous claims as indivisible under *Wal-Mart Stores, Inc. v. Dukes*, 564 U.S. 338 (2011).
  Per `lower-court-docket.txt` and the petition, the D.C. Circuit denied rehearing en banc on July 23, 2026 (no dissents noted from the denial of en banc), **withheld issuance of the mandate pending the disposition of this cert petition**, and left the partial stay in place as to asylum.
- **`brief-in-opposition.txt` (59 pages):** Respondents emphasize major vehicle problems: (a) the government no longer defends the extra-statutory repatriation procedures or the restrictions on withholding of removal and CAT protection, which formed the core of the guidance; (b) the government did not seek cert on the holding that § 1252(f)(1) does not bar APA vacatur, meaning the vacatur of the implementing guidance stands regardless of QPs 2 and 3; (c) on QP 1, the Proclamation and guidance invoked §§ 1182(f) and 1185(a) rather than backend discretionary denial under § 1158(b)(1)(A), raising a *SEC v. Chenery Corp.* problem; and (d) the government could achieve broad asylum restrictions through joint DHS/DOJ rulemaking under § 1158(b)(2)(C) (which it has proposed) rather than by proclamation.
- **`cert-reply.txt` (17 pages):** The Solicitor General responds that: (a) the Court **already granted certiorari on September 29, 2026 in *Department of Homeland Security v. D.V.D.*, No. 26-426** (confirmed via `fedcourts query`), which presents QP 2 (§ 1252(f)(1)'s application to injunctions framed around non-Part IV authority) and where the government also challenges inclusion of future class members; (b) while the Court could hold this petition for *D.V.D.* if it did not wish to grant plenary review on QPs 1 and 3, the Court should grant plenary review here because *D.V.D.* does not address QP 1 (the legality of Proclamation 10,888's asylum suspension) and because QP 3 squarely presents the post-*CASA* Rule 23 future-class-member issue as a standalone question; and (c) the unappealed vacatur of the *guidance* does not moot QPs 2 and 3 because the Proclamation itself is self-executing and outranks agency guidance, so only the classwide injunction blocks direct enforcement of the Proclamation's asylum bar once the mandate issues.

## 3. Justification for Probability Adjustments (`probability: 0.85`)

Starting from the `federal` band base rate of **0.7311**, I adjust upward to **0.85** (+0.12):
- **Strong upward drivers:**
  1. **SG Petition Defending a Flagship Presidential Proclamation:** The Court almost invariably grants review when a court of appeals invalidates a major presidential immigration proclamation or nationwide border policy and the Solicitor General petitions for certiorari.
  2. **Immediate Practical Impact of Mandate Stay:** Because the D.C. Circuit stayed its mandate pending the disposition of this petition, a denial of certiorari would immediately dissolve the stay and halt enforcement of Proclamation 10,888's asylum bar nationwide.
  3. **Deep Lower-Court Division:** Four D.C. Circuit judges split across the stay and merits stages (Judges Walker, Millett, and Katsas each siding with the government on key issues), producing a thorough 90-page dissent by Judge Walker that frames all three questions for Supreme Court review.
  4. **Interlock with *DHS v. D.V.D.* (No. 26-426):** The grant in *D.V.D.* confirms the Court's active interest in both § 1252(f)(1) evasion and prospective immigration class actions this Term, while leaving Question 1 (Proclamation 10,888) unaddressed unless the Court grants here.
