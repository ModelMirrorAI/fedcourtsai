## 3. Disposition and Scope of Review (`disposition`)

I forecast an **85% probability (`0.85`)** that the Court grants review (`predicted_disposition: "granted"`), most likely via a plenary grant of certiorari on the November 9, 2026 or subsequent November order list.

If granted for plenary review, the Court is likely to grant the petition on **Question 1** (whether Presidential Proclamation No. 10,888 and its implementing guidance lawfully preclude access to asylum under 8 U.S.C. §§ 1158, 1182(f), and 1185(a)) and **Question 3** (whether certifying a Rule 23(b)(2) class that includes future unlawful entrants comports with Article III and Rule 23), either alongside Question 2 (8 U.S.C. § 1252(f)(1)) or while leaving Question 2 to be resolved by or coordinated with *D.V.D.*

## 4. Summary Disposition Route (`summary-disposition-route`)

Conditional on a grant, I forecast a **12% probability (`0.12`)** that the Court disposes of the petition in the cert order itself rather than setting it for plenary briefing and argument. That conditional probability reflects the possibility that the Court holds the petition pending *Department of Homeland Security v. D.V.D.*, No. 26-426, and subsequently grants, vacates, and remands (GVR) in light of *D.V.D.* on the 8 U.S.C. § 1252(f)(1) and class-action questions. However, because *D.V.D.* does not resolve Question 1's substantive challenge to Proclamation 10,888's asylum suspension—and because the Solicitor General specifically urges plenary review of Question 1 and Question 3 now—full merits review is far likelier than a hold-and-GVR.
