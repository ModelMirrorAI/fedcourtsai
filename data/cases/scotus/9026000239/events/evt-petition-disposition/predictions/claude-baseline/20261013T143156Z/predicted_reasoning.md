# Forecast — Mullin v. RAICES, No. 26-239 (cert petition)

**Disposition: certiorari granted** (P ≈ 0.86), most likely after the November 6, 2026
conference or one relist after it, and set down for plenary briefing and argument this Term.

- **Where things stand.** The docket shows one distribution (for the 11/6/2026 conference).
  The petition has never been relisted and no CVSG is on the docket.
- **Relists.** I expect at least one more distribution (P ≈ 0.6). The Court often relists a
  petition once before granting it. A denial in a case this prominent would also very likely
  sit through relists while a Justice writes. A grant straight from the first conference is
  also quite possible, since the petitioner is the SG and the case is plainly important.
  If a relist happens, expect one or two, not a long run.
- **CVSG.** None. The Solicitor General is the petitioner, so there is nothing to invite.
- **Which question.** If the Court grants, it most likely takes QP 1 (whether Proclamation
  10,888 and its guidance lawfully cut off access to asylum and related statutory
  protections) as written, or narrowed to the asylum question the government is still
  defending. The respondents say that is the only merits issue left. I expect the Court to
  take QP 2 (whether 8 U.S.C. 1252(f)(1) bars classwide relief) alongside it, and QP 3
  (the class definition under Article III and Rule 23) perhaps as well. The Court has
  recently been receptive to remedial questions in government cases, so granting all three
  as written is plausible. Leaving QP 3 out is the most likely narrowing.
- **Route.** Plenary review, not a summary disposition. There is no intervening decision
  that would support a GVR, and a summary reversal of a 127-page D.C. Circuit opinion on a
  headline immigration policy is very unlikely (P ≈ 0.03 conditional on a grant).
- **If it is denied.** A denial would very likely draw a dissent from denial, most plausibly
  from one or more of Justices Thomas, Alito and Gorsuch, given how the government frames
  the issue's importance (P ≈ 0.65 conditional on a denial).
- **If granted, the likely ground.** The case would turn on whether 1182(f)/1185(a) let the
  President override the right to apply for asylum in 1158(a)(1), "irrespective of"
  manner of entry. The remedial questions decide how far any loss for the government
  actually reaches.
