**Adjustments up from ~70%.**
- The SG is the petitioner, the government lost below, and the case is about a
  presidential proclamation central to the administration's border policy. Those features
  put it at the high end of the federal class. Many federal-band petitions are routine
  acquiescences or protective filings, not cases the SG is pushing this hard.
- The Court took Trump v. Hawaii (1182(f)) without a circuit split. The petition argues
  credibly that no split can ever develop, because the universal class swallows every
  potential plaintiff. The Court has also repeatedly reviewed this administration's
  immigration and remedy cases.
- The three QPs combine a major statutory merits question with remedial questions
  (1252(f)(1) and the class scope after CASA) that this Court has shown interest in.
- 22 States and several other amici support the petition.

  any urgency, though it does not make review less worthwhile.

  asylum elimination alone, that the statutory text ("irrespective of … status," "whether
  or not at a designated port of arrival") is clear, that QP 2 is irrelevant because the
  government did not appeal the unanimous vacatur holding, and that there is no split on
  QPs 2–3. The vacatur point is a real vehicle objection to QP 2, but it does not touch
  QP 1. A cert reply (15 pp.) was provisioned, but I did not read it.
