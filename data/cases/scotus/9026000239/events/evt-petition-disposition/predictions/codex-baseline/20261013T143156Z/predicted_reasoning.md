# Forecast of the Court's action

The Court will grant certiorari for plenary consideration, most likely centered on whether the Executive may use the proclamation and implementing guidance to categorically foreclose asylum applications by covered entrants. P(any grant), including a partial grant, GVR, or summary reversal, is **86%**. The modal disposition is an ordinary grant, not a summary merits ruling.

The record shows **one distribution and no completed relist**, with the first conference scheduled for November 6, 2026. I expect one further distribution before a grant; two further distributions are a plausible variant. The probability of at least one additional distribution is **60%**. The central timing forecast is a November–December 2026 grant. A hold for the companion remedial litigation identified in the reply could instead delay action into 2027; such a delay would not itself forecast denial.

The Court will not call for the Solicitor General's views: the government is already the petitioner and has supplied its position in both petition and reply. P(a new CVSG) is **0.5%**.

If review is granted, the first question is the strongest candidate, likely understood or narrowed to the asylum-related conflict between the asserted entry-suspension/discretionary-denial powers and the statutory right to apply. I do not expect the Court to take up every abandoned withholding-of-removal or CAT defense simply because the question is phrased broadly. The Section 1252(f)(1) and class-definition questions may also be taken, but a narrowing that leaves those matters to the companion case is plausible.

Conditional on a grant, plenary briefing and argument are more likely than a cert-order disposition: **18%** for the summary route and **82%** for plenary review. The principal summary alternative is a hold followed by a GVR informed by the companion's remedial ruling. Immediate summary reversal is less likely because the asylum issue presents contested statutory and administrative-law grounds beyond that remedial overlap. If a plenary case follows, the core dispute will concern whether discretionary authority to deny asylum supports refusing applications categorically; no unconditional merits judgment is forecast here.

If the petition is denied, I narrowly expect no separate writing. The conditional probability of at least one noted dissent or statement respecting denial is **45%**. I do not identify a particular Justice or predict unrecorded cert votes.
