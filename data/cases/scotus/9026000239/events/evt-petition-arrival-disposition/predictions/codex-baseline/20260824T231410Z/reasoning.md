# Rationale

I assign a 0.92 probability to a grant-family disposition and predict a plenary grant as the single most likely label.

The arrival-time conditioning is unusually strong. The frozen context places this paid petition in the `federal` band under `sal-v3`, with zero distributions and no CVSG as of docketing. Pooling the statpack's federal-band rows for Terms 2017 through 2025 (all Terms strictly before OT2026) gives a grant-family rate of about 71% over 201 resolved weighted cases. That is the appropriate arrival anchor; I did not use the terminal relist-zero rate.

I adjust substantially upward from that anchor. The Solicitor General seeks review of a divided D.C. Circuit decision invalidating an operative, high-priority federal immigration policy; the court of appeals stayed its mandate pending Supreme Court proceedings. The petition presents the legality of the proclamation plus independently important remedial and class-certification questions under 8 U.S.C. 1252(f)(1), Article III, and Rule 23. Its cited comparator, *Mullin v. Al Otro Lado*, was granted and decided for the government 6-3 on a related asylum-at-the-border question in June 2026. The CourtListener opinion confirms both the recent six-Justice alignment and the majority's text-focused, government-favorable approach, though its holding concerned aliens who had not entered the country and therefore does not decide this petition.

The main downward adjustments are the absence of a circuit conflict and a meaningful merits weakness. Section 1158(a)(1) expressly says an alien physically present in the United States may apply for asylum irrespective of status, while the challenged policy categorically denies access through a proclamation and informal guidance rather than the statute's regulatory route. The D.C. Circuit majority also treated the INA's removal procedures as exclusive. Those points could make the Court prefer further percolation, deny, or dispose of the case on the narrower injunction or class grounds.

The 0.22 summary-route probability is conditional on a grant. A GVR or summary action drawing on *Al Otro Lado*, *Aleman Gonzalez*, or *Trump v. CASA* is plausible, but the distinct statutory question and the breadth and importance of the policy make plenary review more likely. The 0.99 distribution-increment probability reflects the arrival vantage: the record shows zero distributions, and a properly docketed paid petition of this kind is overwhelmingly likely to be distributed at least once. A CVSG is effectively unavailable because the United States is already the petitioner represented by the Solicitor General. If the Court unexpectedly denies review, I put a 0.20 conditional probability on a noted dissent or statement.

The provisioned petition supplied all three questions presented and the complete 33-page petition argument. `documents.json` marks the 408-page filing as truncated, and the text ends during the appended lower-court opinion; no brief in opposition was provisioned at this arrival moment. That limits my ability to test the government's account against the respondents' eventual opposition, although the petition itself includes substantial portions of the majority's competing reasoning.
