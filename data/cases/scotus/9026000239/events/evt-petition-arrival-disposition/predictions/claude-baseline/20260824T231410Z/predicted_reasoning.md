# Forecast — Mullin v. RAICES, No. 26-239 (petition at arrival)

The Court will grant certiorari and set the case for plenary briefing and
argument, most likely granting in late 2026 or very early 2027 and hearing
argument this Term (OT2026), with a decision by June 2027. This is the
Solicitor General petitioning from a final D.C. Circuit judgment that held
unlawful the entry-suspension and asylum-preclusion provisions of Proclamation
10,888 — the administration's central border policy — and the court of appeals
has stayed its own mandate, which is the posture of a court expecting this
Court to take the case.

**Distribution and relists.** The docket shows zero distributions — the
petition was docketed today, August 24, 2026, with the brief in opposition due
September 23, 2026. Respondents (institutional immigrant-rights organizations
with sophisticated counsel) will oppose rather than waive, and may take a
modest extension. Expect first distribution in October or November 2026. From
this state I expect the petition to be distributed at least once with near
certainty, and one or two relists at most: the Court decides high-profile
government petitions promptly, and relists here would reflect opinion-writing
around the grant order or calendar management, not doubt. A grant within one
to three conferences of first distribution is the modal path.

**CVSG.** None will issue. The petitioner *is* the United States, represented
by the Solicitor General; a call for the views of the Solicitor General is
conceptually inapplicable.

**Which question presented.** If the Court grants, it takes QP 1 as written —
the lawfulness of the Proclamation and its implementing guidance, including
the asylum preclusion — because that is the question on which the whole policy
turns. QP 2 (whether 8 U.S.C. 1252(f)(1) bars the classwide relief) very
likely rides along: the Court has shown sustained interest in 1252(f)(1)
(Garland v. Aleman Gonzalez) and in the scope of universal remedies generally.
QP 3 (Rule 23 / Article III class definition) is the most likely to be pruned
or subsumed; the Court could grant limited to QPs 1 and 2. (Registered
context-only; nothing scores this.)

**Summary disposition.** Unlikely. There is no intervening decision of this
Court to GVR in light of, and the government itself asks for plenary review of
a 100-plus-page court of appeals opinion. A summary reversal of a ruling of
this magnitude, without argument, is not this Court's practice. Conditional on
a grant, I put the cert-order-disposition route at about 4%.

**If denied.** A denial is the ~8% branch, and its likeliest form is not a
quiet rejection on the merits of certworthiness but an overtaken petition — a
rescinded or superseded proclamation, or a legislative change mooting the
dispute — in which case a dismissal or an unexplained denial follows. In a
true contested denial I would expect a dissent from denial (Justice Thomas
and/or Justice Alito most plausibly), but across all denial branches, many of
which are quiet mootness denials, I put a noted dissent or statement
respecting denial at about 30%.

**Merits, conditionally.** If granted, the government's odds are good: the
Court granted and largely sided with the Executive in Trump v. Hawaii on the
closely related 1182(f) suspension power, and its recent remedies
jurisprudence (universal relief, 1252(f)(1)) runs the government's way. A
reversal in whole or in substantial part is more likely than affirmance. That
is context for the grant call, not a claim this cert-stage cell resolves.
