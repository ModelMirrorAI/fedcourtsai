## In brief

A mother whose son's death photo was taken and shared by a police officer asks the Court to revive her civil rights lawsuit against him.

## What happened

Marcus Boone died after jumping from a Philadelphia overpass in 2022. A police officer at the scene, Christopher Culver, photographed him on the ground and texted the photo to other officers; it later spread online and reached Boone's mother, Karen Brookins. Brookins sued Culver and the city, claiming the photo-taking violated her constitutional right to privacy and caused her emotional distress. The district court found Culver's conduct "reprehensible" and conscience-shocking but granted him qualified immunity, a legal protection shielding officials from liability unless they violated clearly established law. The Third Circuit affirmed, over one judge's partial dissent.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked to decide what a plaintiff must show to win this kind of constitutional claim against a government official. Brookins argues that proving the official's conduct was shockingly wrong should be enough by itself. The lower court instead required her to also show that the specific right at stake—control over a deceased relative's death images—was a separately, clearly recognized right with deep historical roots. Brookins says other appeals courts only require the first showing, not both.

## Where it stands

The petition was filed in September 2026. The city and officer respondents waived their right to respond, and amici curiae (outside parties, here law professors) filed a brief supporting Brookins. The case was distributed for the Court's November 13, 2026 conference, where the justices will decide whether to take it.

## What each outcome would mean

Brookins, the mother, is asking the Court to take up her case and reverse the Third Circuit's ruling against her. If the Court grants review, it will hear full arguments and decide the legal question itself; a grant alone would not decide who wins. If the Court denies review, the Third Circuit's decision stands, meaning Culver keeps qualified immunity and Brookins's lawsuit over the photograph remains rejected.
