# Predicted course of the Court — Brookins v. City of Philadelphia, No. 26-433

**Disposition:** denied, most likely without any separate writing.

**Where the docket stands now:** the petition has been distributed once, for the
November 13, 2026 conference. It has never been relisted. All respondents waived
a response (Oct 16 and Oct 20), and one amicus brief, from Constitutional Law
Scholars, was filed on Oct 27.

**Further distributions (relist-increment):** this is the claim I am least sure
of. A paid petition that drew a waiver and then an amicus brief, filed by
experienced Supreme Court counsel, is the usual profile for a **call for a
response**. I put a call for response before or at the November 13 conference
at roughly 40%. If one comes, the petition gets distributed again after the
brief in opposition, probably for a January–February 2027 conference, and that
second distribution satisfies the increment. Without a call, the most likely
outcome is a straight denial on the November 13 or November 16 order list,
with no relist.

**CVSG:** no. No federal party is involved and the question is a §1983
constitutional standard that the Court does not usually route through the
Solicitor General.

**Question the Court would take if it grants:** the QP as written, whether an
executive-action substantive-due-process claim needs a deeply rooted
fundamental right in addition to conscience-shocking conduct. The Court might
reformulate it to put the qualified-immunity "clearly established" step in
front.

**Summary disposition:** unlikely, even if the Court grants. No intervening
decision supports a GVR. A per curiam reversal in the petitioner's favour
would be unusual in a qualified-immunity posture where the panel relied on
the absence of clearly established law. Conditional on a grant, I put the
summary route at about 10%.

**Separate writing on a denial:** unlikely (about 5%). If anyone writes, the
likeliest are Justice Sotomayor or Justice Jackson, objecting that egregious
conduct went without a remedy, as Sotomayor has done in qualified-immunity
denials. Less likely is Justice Thomas or Justice Gorsuch, questioning
whether substantive due process for executive action should exist at all.
