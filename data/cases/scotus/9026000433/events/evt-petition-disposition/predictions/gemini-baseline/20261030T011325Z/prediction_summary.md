The Supreme Court is very likely to deny review in this case (less than a one-in-one-hundred chance of a grant, at 0.8%). Although a Philadelphia police officer's decision to photograph and share an image of a dying man was widely condemned by the judges below, the lower court ruled that the officer was protected by qualified immunity—a legal rule that shields officials from lawsuits unless prior court decisions clearly established that their conduct violated the Constitution. Because the mother's new legal argument about how the Constitution's Due Process Clause applies was never raised in the lower courts, and the respondents waived their right to file a response, the Court is expected to turn the appeal away without hearing it.
