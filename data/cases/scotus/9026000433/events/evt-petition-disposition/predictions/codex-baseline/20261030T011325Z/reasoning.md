# Rationale for the probabilities

## Record and information set

This is a forward cert-stage distribution cell. I used `record/snapshots/2026-10-30.json`, the distribution event definition, `record/context.json`, the extracted questions presented, the petition, the document manifest, and the relevant lower-court decisions in the appendix. The frozen conditioning is sal-v4 `baseline`, Term 2026, one distribution, no CVSG, with observable proceedings and no retrieval cutoff. The docketed petition is No. 26-433. The snapshot's last listed proceeding is the October 28 distribution for November 13, 2026; its single amicus entry is the October 27 Constitutional Law Scholars brief. I counted that entry directly, rather than treating the context's null amicus count as zero.

The petition is available without truncation or OCR. The appendix is marked truncated; I examined the appellate majority and the opening of Judge Roth's dissent, along with the petition's discussion of the district court. I do not treat omitted appendix material as absent evidence. No brief in opposition is provisioned, consistent with both respondent groups' filed waivers, so there is no opposition argument to weigh as though it had been read. The scholars' amicus text is not provisioned; I use its existence, not any assumed contents. No outcome or another cell's forecast was consulted, and I do not independently know this petition's disposition.

## Anchor and numerical adjustment

The committed statpack's sal-v4 table matches this cell's salience version. Pooling the bracketed `reached` baseline figures over every rendered Term strictly before 2026, namely 2017–2025, gives 660 grant-family outcomes over 12,913 weighted resolved petitions, or 5.1111%. I computed this from the corresponding exact `prefix_est_grant_rate` and `prefix_weighted_resolved` fields in `metrics/statpack.json`, not by averaging rounded percentages. I exclude the own-Term 2026 row and do not use the much lower rate among petitions that ended in baseline.

Population-shape cross-checks in `metrics/statpack.md` are consistent with a low initial probability: the paid scored segment with zero relists has 10,203 resolved petitions and approximately 1.8% grants plus GVRs; the one-relist bucket has 2,538 resolved and approximately 13.7% grants plus GVRs. These are terminal buckets, not forward transition probabilities. The no-CVSG bucket has 13,556 resolved and approximately 6.4% grants plus GVRs, versus 164 resolved and approximately 34.8% with a CVSG. The one-amicus bucket has 1,162 resolved and approximately 15.1% grants plus GVRs; its total population is 1,207. That marginal, terminal-count comparison is not an independent multiplier on the band rate. The Third Circuit aggregate is approximately 2.5% grant-family over 1,909 resolved, but mixes fee classes and is not this selected population's anchor.

Freshness limitation: these figures describe the committed statpack available to this cell, not a newly refreshed estimate. It provides no corpus-wide pull/snapshot vintage in the material consulted. `fedcourts corpus-info` failed because the cell's service backend has no client-side connection. I therefore cannot attest to the underlying blob's newest pull or snapshot stamp. The case-specific input is explicitly the October 30 snapshot; no per-case `last_pulled` value was supplied for this docket. The broad corpus query was contextual only and does not establish population rates or corpus-wide freshness.

I move above the 5.11% anchor to **12% P(any grant)** because the petition identifies a consequential legal-framework conflict, supports it with specific appellate authorities, presents deliberate conduct whose conscience-shocking character was undisputed below, and comes with a panel dissent and one scholarly amicus brief. Those are stronger reasons for review than a disagreement about application of settled law to ordinary facts.

The adjustment is restrained for three reasons. First, the Third Circuit decision is expressly nonprecedential. Second, the judgment actually rests on qualified immunity and whether a family member's asserted privacy interest was clearly established, not simply an express merits holding rejecting all conscience-shocking claims absent historical pedigree. The majority assumes some propositions in the petitioner's favor while rejecting controlling authority, a robust consensus, and obvious constitutional unlawfulness. A new answer about the constitutional test might not by itself settle the damages defense. This is a vehicle risk, not a claim that the panel identified a wholly separate alternative ground. Third, the split concerns different interests and factual settings, and some circuits' formulations are internally mixed according to the petition itself. That weakens the inference that the Court faces a clean, outcome-determinative conflict on this record.

The external source check reinforced that distinction. In *DeMarcus v. University of South Alabama*, 133 F.4th 1305 (11th Cir. 2025), opinion pp. 17–20, the court describes executive-action due process using the conscience-shocking standard, but separately requires overcoming qualified immunity and resolves the claims on the absence of clearly established law without deciding their constitutional merits. This supports taking the petition's doctrinal argument seriously while declining to infer that a favorable test automatically defeats immunity. It is a legal analogue, not a matched statistical prior.

## Other elicited beliefs

The 32% redistribution probability includes the possibility of a call for a response followed by a new distribution. One existing distribution is not itself a relist, and the relist-count tables do not measure this future hazard. The alleged split makes further scrutiny plausible, but the unpublished qualified-immunity vehicle and response waivers leave immediate denial the modal path. No-CVSG at present is not proof of no future invitation; I nevertheless put that increment at just 1% because the private/local-official dispute has no identified special federal-government interest requiring the Solicitor General's advice.

The summary-route probability is **12% conditional on a grant**, not 12% unconditionally. No intervening controlling ruling has been identified as a GVR trigger. Resolving the conflicting constitutional formulations would ordinarily benefit from plenary review; an obvious-violation summary reversal is possible but not the principal grant scenario. The denial-writing probability is **9% conditional on denial**. The per-Justice order-writing numbers are small, independent marginals, not probabilities summing to one; they allow more than one author and writings on a grant as well as a denial. Their modest elevation for Sotomayor and Jackson reflects the subject matter, not knowledge of any actual votes.

The stakes score is **0.56**, independently of the grant probability. The family-photograph facts are narrow, but a decision defining substantive due process for executive abuse could have nationwide consequences for constitutional damages claims. This is meaningful doctrinal significance without the immediate institutional reach of a major nationwide injunction or election dispute.
