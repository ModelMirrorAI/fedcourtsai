# Forecast

The Court will most likely deny the petition without a separate writing. The October 30, 2026 snapshot shows one distribution, for the November 13 conference, no subsequent distribution, and no invitation to the Solicitor General. Both respondent groups have waived a response. The modal path is denial after that first conference, without another distribution. I assign a 32% chance of at least one additional distribution; if the Court pursues the petition, a requested response followed by redistribution is a more plausible first step than immediate review on the present one-sided briefing. In that branch, expect roughly one or two additional distributions before disposition, not a prolonged series of relists.

No call for the Solicitor General's views is expected. This is a private damages action against local officials, and the petition does not present the kind of federal administrative or statutory interest that would make an invitation the natural next step. The probability of a new invitation is 1%.

If review is granted, the likely question is substantially the petition's stated question: whether conscience-shocking executive conduct suffices for a substantive-due-process claim, or whether the plaintiff must additionally identify a fundamental right deeply rooted in history and tradition. The Court could focus that question on a damages action involving an official's deliberate dissemination of a family member's death-scene image, with the clearly-established-law issue remaining part of the case's posture. It is unlikely to take the separate state-law emotional-distress dispute.

Conditional on any grant, plenary briefing and argument are more likely than disposition in the grant order: I assign only 12% to the summary route. The petition presents a disputed constitutional framework rather than an identified intervening decision that supplies a straightforward grant-vacate-remand instruction. Summary reversal over the treatment of obviously wrongful conduct remains a secondary possibility.

Conditional on denial, I assign 9% to a noted dissent or statement respecting denial. No such writing is the clear favorite. If one appears, Sotomayor or Jackson is the more plausible author of a writing concerned with the remedy for egregious police misconduct; Thomas is another, less likely possibility focused on qualified immunity. These are author forecasts, not predictions of publicly observable individual cert votes.
