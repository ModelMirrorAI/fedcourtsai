# Forecast of the Court's action

The Court will most likely **summarily reverse** the May 26 injunction rather than summarily affirm it or set this appeal for a full merits calendar. A vacatur and remand for application of the corrected legal standards is the next most likely corrective route. Conditional on grant-side action, summary resolution is about 82% likely.

The docket already contains **three distributions**, most recently for the November 13, 2026 conference. The appeal will probably receive **one to three additional distributions** beyond that baseline before disposition, with action most likely later in November or December 2026. The probability of at least one further distribution is 75%; the forecast does not count the existing relists as future ones.

The Court will not request the Solicitor General's views. A new invitation is only 3% likely. The dispute principally concerns implementing the Court's recent redistricting standards, and the Court will resolve it without another round of executive-branch advice.

If it takes the case for plenary consideration instead, the Court will focus on **question 1**, concerning alternative maps satisfying legitimate state districting objectives, together with **question 4**, concerning the independent intentional-discrimination ground. Question 2's distinction between racial and partisan polarization will supply a related issue. The Court is less likely to take the broad, record-intensive totality-of-circumstances question 3 as the principal vehicle.

If corrective relief issues, its likely rationale will be that the injunction failed to give sufficient effect to Callais's comparator-map requirements and the presumption of legislative good faith, including in assessing the separate constitutional claim. The Court need not categorically eliminate intentional vote-dilution claims to reach that result. A summary reversal decides the legal error itself; a GVR would instead direct reconsideration, and these remain distinct forecast routes.

Corrective summary action is likely to draw a liberal separate writing, with Kagan the leading individual candidate and Sotomayor and Jackson other candidates. If the Court instead denies review or summarily affirms, no separate statement is slightly more likely than one; the probability of a dissent or statement conditional on denial is 45%, with Thomas or Alito the leading candidates on that branch. Joining another Justice's writing is not predicted authorship.
