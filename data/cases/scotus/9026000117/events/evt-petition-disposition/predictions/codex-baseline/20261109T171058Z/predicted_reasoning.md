# Forecast of the Court's action

The Court will most likely dispose of Alabama's appeal by **summary reversal**, rather than schedule full merits briefing and argument. The formal label is important: this predicts a merits correction in the disposition order, not merely a grant and remand for reconsideration. I put 86% on any grant-side outcome, including the alternative routes of vacatur-remand and plenary review.

## Procedure from this snapshot

Three conference distributions, including two apparent relists, are already on the November 9 record. The next scheduled conference is November 13. I expect a disposition in the next several weeks, with **one further distribution** the most plausible extension if the Court does not act at that conference. The probability of at least one additional distribution beyond the three observed is **60%**; a long sequence extending into the next calendar year is less likely. This timing forecast is context, not a scored horizon claim.

The Court is unlikely to seek the Solicitor General's views now: **3%** probability of a new CVSG, and **97%** of none. If one issues, it would most likely precede substantive disposition during the coming conference cycle, with resolution then delayed. There is no existing CVSG to re-forecast.

## Conditional explanation and question selection

If the Court takes the predicted summary-reversal route, it will likely explain that the district court did not adequately implement Callais's treatment of legitimate state districting objectives and improperly converted disagreement over legal obligations into evidence of discriminatory purpose. It would address the independent constitutional basis as well as the statutory basis rather than leave an alternative ground sustaining the injunction untouched.

If instead it sets the appeal for argument, the most plausible focus is the first and fourth questions together: the treatment of alternative maps and legitimate districting objectives, and the independent intentional-discrimination ground. The Court is less likely to resolve every statistical and totality-of-circumstances contention separately. This is conditional question-selection context, not an assertion that review has already been granted.

Conditional on a grant-side outcome, I put **86% on disposition in the order itself** and 14% on a plenary proceeding. Summary reversal is the leading order route; a narrower vacatur-remand, including one for mootness, is the principal alternative. Dismissal without grant-side relief remains possible if the Court finds no live appellate controversy. The parties' competing election-expiration arguments make that a genuine issue, not the modal forecast.

## Separate writings

On the predicted summary reversal, expect a separate writing defending the factual discrimination findings or objecting to deciding the appeal summarily, most plausibly authored by Jackson or Sotomayor. Kagan is more likely to join than to write independently. This forecast does not posit a majority-opinion author on a denial.

Conditional on a denial-side resolution, the probability of some dissent or statement respecting that disposition is **40%**, so no separate writing remains the more likely denial branch. Thomas or Alito would be plausible authors on that different branch. All nine independent author probabilities in `prediction.json` cover the final order across outcome branches; they are not probabilities of merely joining a writing or certiorari votes.
