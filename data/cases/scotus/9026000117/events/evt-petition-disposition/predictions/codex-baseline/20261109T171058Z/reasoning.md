## Case-specific update

The dominant upward signal is the earlier Supreme Court stay described in the jurisdictional statement, pp. 1–2 and 7–9. Alabama represents that the June 2, 2026 order identified failures to follow Callais and to presume legislative good faith. This is a prior interim ruling in the litigation, **not the disposition being predicted**. Its articulated merits concerns are much stronger evidence than the existence of a stay alone. They also support a summary correction rather than a fresh argument about an unsettled nationwide rule. I discount the state's characterization because a stay is provisional and the opening filing is advocacy.

The district court's own embedded opinion, especially appendix pp. 3a–5a and 27a–45a, supplies a real competing account: it treats intentional racial discrimination as independently established through historical sequence, legislative departures, foreseeability, and contemporary statements. It distinguishes geographic facts from Callais and rejects the idea that its constitutional finding merely duplicates statutory liability. Thus, rejecting its §2 reasoning alone would not necessarily dispose of the injunction; the constitutional ground must also be addressed.

I retrieved the missing September 18 motion to dismiss or affirm and September 29 state response from the snapshot's exact filing links, without opening the live target docket or searching for its disposition. Appellees argue that the election-specific preliminary injunction has become moot and that any merits review should respect factual findings or receive full briefing. Alabama disputes expiration, invokes remaining certification events and continuing restraints, and alternatively requests Munsingwear vacatur. These opposing filings materially reduce confidence in straightforward reversal and add a grant-side vacatur route. Neither side's factual or legal assertions are treated as judicial findings. See the official filings identified in `retrieval.md`, respondent pp. 11–14 and state pp. 1–4.

November 9 is after the November 3 voting date discussed in the state's filing, but I do not equate voting with completion of every election-related event. I have not independently checked current certification activity. Nor does pending litigation automatically preserve appellate jurisdiction over an expired preliminary injunction. That distinction is the principal downside risk.

The same-question file lists 26-116 and 26-118 at similarity 1.0, both pending. These are text matches, not official Vide links; no provisioned `related/` relationship is asserted. Corpus lookups confirm Caster and Singleton, respectively, both unresolved and at three distributions for November 13. ## Calibration and secondary claims

The first three sum to 0.86. Their summary share is 0.74/0.86, rounded to the declared conditional route probability of 0.86. These are judgmental allocations, not frequencies extracted from a matching direct-appeal cohort.

The 0.60 further-distribution probability reflects possible circulation of a summary order and separate writings after two relists; it is not a claim that the two observed relists will occur again. The 0.03 CVSG probability reflects the advanced posture and an existing merits framework despite the federal statute involved. Conditional on denial, I assign 0.40 to a dissent or statement. That conditional event differs from the likely liberal separate writing on a summary reversal. Jackson and Sotomayor have the largest unconditional order-writing probabilities because of that latter route; a Justice joining someone else's writing is not counted as an author.
