## In brief

Alabama officials ask the Court to overturn a lower court order blocking the state's 2023 congressional map in an ongoing Voting Rights Act dispute.

## What happened

Alabama's congressional map has been fought over since 2021, when a lower court found the state's districts likely diluted Black voters' strength under the Voting Rights Act, a ruling the Supreme Court affirmed in 2023. The state then passed a replacement map in 2023 that still contained only one district where Black voters could elect their preferred candidate. The lower court blocked that map too, ruled it intentionally discriminated against Black voters, and later reaffirmed that conclusion after the Supreme Court decided another voting-rights case. Alabama's Secretary of State and legislative leaders now appeal that ruling, arguing the lower court misapplied the law and wrongly rejected the state's explanations for its map.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked whether the lower court used the wrong legal test for deciding if an alternative map shows the state's map treats voters unequally, whether it wrongly found racially split voting patterns without separating out ordinary political party preferences, whether it wrongly concluded discrimination was likely under all the circumstances, and whether the state violated equal-protection guarantees by declining to draw a district organized around race.

## Where it stands

The case was fully briefed, including the state's opening filing, the other side's response, and the state's reply, and was distributed for three consecutive conferences, most recently November 13, 2026. The docket shows no decision yet on whether the Court will take up the case.

## What each outcome would mean

Alabama's officials are asking the Court to overturn the lower court's order, which blocked the 2023 map and sided with the voters and organizations who challenged it. If the Court takes the case and later reverses, Alabama's map would no longer be blocked on the grounds the lower court gave, and the case would return for further proceedings. If the Court affirms, the lower court's block on the 2023 map stands and the challengers' win continues. If the Court vacates, the lower court's order would be set aside and sent back without a final decision favoring either side. If the Court declines to hear the case, the lower court's order blocking the map remains in place.
