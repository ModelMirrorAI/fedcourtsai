## In brief

Alabama officials appeal a district court order blocking the state's 2023 congressional map over claims it dilutes Black voters' influence and discriminates intentionally.

## What happened

Alabama's congressional map has been litigated for years. Courts found Alabama's 2021 map likely violated the Voting Rights Act by limiting Black Alabamians to one opportunity district out of seven; the Supreme Court affirmed that finding in 2023. The legislature then passed a replacement, the 2023 Plan, which again included only one majority-Black district. A three-judge district court enjoined the 2023 Plan, finding it both violated the Voting Rights Act and intentionally discriminated against Black voters under the Constitution. After the Supreme Court's decision in Louisiana v. Callais revised the legal test for these claims, the district court reconsidered and again blocked the map for the 2026 elections, ordering use of a court-drawn substitute. The state appealed to the Supreme Court. Appellees argue the appeal is moot because the 2026 elections already proceeded under the state's map after the Supreme Court stayed the injunction.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked whether the district court was wrong to excuse the challengers from drawing alternative maps that matched the state's stated map-drawing priorities, such as keeping certain regions together and protecting incumbents; whether the lower court improperly found race-based voting patterns without adequately separating race from political party; whether the evidence supported finding a likelihood of discrimination overall; and whether the state's decision not to draw an additional majority-Black district itself amounted to unconstitutional discrimination.

## Where it stands

The case has been distributed for three consecutive conferences, most recently November 13, 2026, with no action on the petition recorded since. Briefing is complete, including the state's jurisdictional statement, the challengers' motion to dismiss or affirm, and the state's reply.

## What each outcome would mean

Alabama officials, who lost below, are asking the Court to overturn the district court's injunction against the 2023 Plan. If the Court notes probable jurisdiction and later reverses, the 2023 Plan could go into effect, and the challengers' claims of vote dilution and intentional discrimination would not stand. If the Court summarily affirms, the lower court's injunction against the 2023 Plan remains in place, and the court-ordered map continues to govern elections. If the Court dismisses the appeal as moot, as Milligan and the other challengers urge, the district court's ruling would stand without Supreme Court review of the merits.
