# Forecast: what the Court will do with No. 25-1176, Lindsey v. South Carolina

**Disposition.** The petition will be denied. P(any grant) = 0.08.

**Relists.** The docket shows a single distribution, for the Conference of
September 28, 2026 (the long conference). I expect this petition to draw at
least one further distribution with probability 0.38: a capital petition with
elite counsel, a 3-2 split below with a lengthy dissent, and a vivid
"signed-without-reading" narrative is the kind the long conference tends to
carry over once while a Justice decides whether to write. I do not expect a
second relist beyond that; the most likely path is one relist, then denial in
October or early November 2026.

**CVSG.** No. The respondent is a State in a state post-conviction matter and
no federal interest is implicated; a call for the Solicitor General's views is
essentially off the table (0.02).

**Which question, if taken.** If the Court acts, it would act on Question 1
(cumulative Strickland prejudice), most likely reformulated to ask whether a
court that assumes multiple deficiencies must assess their combined prejudice,
and it would leave Question 2 (verbatim adoption of the State's proposed order)
behind as factbound after the state supreme court's own remand. A plenary grant
on Question 2 alone is very unlikely.

**Summary disposition.** Conditional on any grant, a summary route is nearly
as likely as plenary review (0.45): the Court's closest analogue, Jefferson v.
Upton (2010), was a per curiam vacatur, and the Court's recent interventions
for capital petitioners have tended to be per curiam reversals or GVRs rather
than argued cases. Unconditionally, a summary disposition is still improbable
(about 0.04).

**Dissent from denial.** Conditional on denial, I expect a written dissent or
statement respecting denial with probability 0.25, most plausibly from Justice
Sotomayor (joined by Justice Jackson), keyed to the post-conviction court twice
signing the State's proposed order and to the state dissent's tabulated
comparison of the trial and post-conviction mitigation records. The base case
remains a silent denial.

**If granted (conditional only).** The Court would hold that Strickland's
prejudice inquiry, when a court assumes several deficiencies, must weigh their
aggregate effect against the aggravation, and would vacate and remand for the
South Carolina Supreme Court to conduct that weighing rather than decide
prejudice itself.
