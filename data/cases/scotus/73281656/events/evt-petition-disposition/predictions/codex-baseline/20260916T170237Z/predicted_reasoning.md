The summer interval before the scheduled conference is not itself a relist signal.

If the Court grants plenary review, **Question 1 is the likelier focus**, narrowed to whether a court that assumes or finds multiple deficiencies in capital sentencing representation must evaluate their combined prejudicial effect before rejecting relief. I predict that the Court would leave the more fact-dependent proposed-order question behind rather than take both questions together. This is a forecast of a possible grant's scope, not a prediction that an opinion will necessarily issue.

Conditional on any grant, I assign **30% to disposition in the cert order itself**, and 70% to a proceeding for further briefing and argument. The competing characterizations of the prejudice analysis and order-review process make immediate summary correction less likely than plenary treatment if the Court intervenes. No intervening decision requiring a GVR has been identified in the materials available to me.

Conditional on denial, I assign **25% to a noted dissent or statement respecting denial**, and 75% to neither. The capital mitigation and independent-review concerns provide the plausible subject of a separate writing. I do not forecast individual Justices' unobservable cert votes or a majority opinion accompanying denial.
