## What happened

Marion Lindsey was convicted and sentenced to death in South Carolina for killing his estranged wife. After the state courts denied his request for post-conviction relief, he appealed to the South Carolina Supreme Court, arguing his trial lawyer failed to adequately investigate and present mitigating evidence, and that the judge who denied him relief had improperly signed the state's proposed order without meaningfully reviewing it, errors and all. The South Carolina Supreme Court affirmed the denial of relief, with two justices dissenting. Lindsey then asked the U.S. Supreme Court to review that decision.

## What the Court is being asked

Lindsey raised two issues. First, whether courts must weigh the combined effect of a lawyer's alleged mistakes together when deciding if those mistakes changed the outcome, rather than considering each mistake separately. Second, whether a judge in a death penalty case violates constitutional fair-process protections by adopting, essentially unchanged, an order written by the opposing side without showing independent judicial consideration.
