## In brief

The Supreme Court declined to hear a death-sentenced South Carolina inmate's challenge to how courts assess his ineffective-assistance claim and a judge's adoption of the state's proposed order.

## What happened

Marion Lindsey was convicted of murder and sentenced to death in South Carolina. He sought state post-conviction relief, arguing his trial lawyer failed to adequately investigate and present mitigating evidence at sentencing. The post-conviction court denied relief by signing an order drafted by the state that contained numerous errors; after the state supreme court sent the order back for correction, the judge re-adopted the same findings with only cosmetic changes. The South Carolina Supreme Court affirmed the denial of relief, over two dissenting justices. Lindsey then asked the U.S. Supreme Court to review that decision.

## What the Court is being asked

The case raised two questions. First, when courts decide whether a defendant was harmed by his lawyer's mistakes, must they weigh all the mistakes together, or can they reject the claim by considering each mistake separately? Second, does it violate a defendant's constitutional rights for a judge in a death penalty case to adopt, essentially unchanged, an order written by the state without showing independent judicial consideration?

## Where it stands

The petition was distributed for the Court's conference on September 28, 2026, and on October 5, 2026, the Court denied it.

## What each outcome would mean

Lindsey, as the petitioner, asked the Court to overturn the South Carolina Supreme Court's decision upholding the denial of relief. By denying the petition, the Court leaves that state court decision in place, meaning Lindsey's conviction and death sentence stand and he does not receive a new hearing on his ineffective-assistance or proposed-order claims through this petition. The denial does not mean the Court agreed the lower court was correct; it simply leaves that ruling undisturbed. The docket notes related federal habeas proceedings are pending separately.
