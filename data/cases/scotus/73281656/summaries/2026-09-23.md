## What happened

Marion Lindsey was convicted of murdering his estranged wife and sentenced to death in South Carolina. He sought state post-conviction relief, arguing his trial lawyer failed to adequately investigate and present evidence that might have persuaded a jury to spare his life. The trial court denied relief by signing, largely unchanged, a lengthy order drafted by the state, which contained numerous drafting errors. South Carolina's supreme court sent the order back for revision; the trial court then reissued essentially the same order with only cosmetic corrections. South Carolina's supreme court affirmed the denial of relief, over a two-justice dissent. Lindsey now asks the U.S. Supreme Court to review that decision.

## What the Court is being asked

The case raises two questions. First, when a court decides that a defendant's trial lawyer made mistakes but must determine whether those mistakes actually affected the outcome, must the court weigh all the mistakes together, or can it evaluate each one separately and reject the claim if no single mistake was enough on its own? Second, does it violate a defendant's rights for a judge deciding a death-penalty appeal to adopt, essentially word-for-word, an order written by the state, without independently explaining his reasoning or making substantive changes?

## Where it stands

The petition has been fully briefed: South Carolina filed a brief opposing review, and Lindsey filed a reply. The case was distributed for the Court's conference on September 28, 2026, where the justices will decide whether to grant cert, the Court's permission to hear the appeal.
