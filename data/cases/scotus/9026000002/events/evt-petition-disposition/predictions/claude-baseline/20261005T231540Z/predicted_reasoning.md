# Forecast: what the Court will do with No. 26-2 (evt-petition-disposition)

**Bottom line.** The petition will most likely be **denied** (P(any grant) = 0.19), and
its fate is not its own: it rides on the lead petition on the identical question,
No. 25-1383, *Indian Harbor Ins. Co. v. Town of Vinton*. The petition here asks
for exactly that treatment ("hold this petition ... pending the decision in
Indian Harbor"), so the Court's disposition of 26-2 will be entered on the same
day as, or after, its disposition of 25-1383.

## Distributions (the state I am forecasting from)

The docket shows **one** distribution (July 22, 2026, for the September 28 long
conference). The Court then requested a response (July 28), which pulled the
petition from that conference; the response deadline was extended to September 28
and no brief in opposition is on the docket yet. **Expect at least one further
distribution** (P = 0.93): once the BIO is docketed the Clerk will redistribute,
almost certainly so that 26-2, 25-1383 (fully briefed since September 9) and 26-53
(*Indian Harbor v. One Lakeside Plaza*, "Rescheduled" on August 27 for exactly this
reason) are considered at one conference, probably in late October or November 2026.
After that first redistribution I expect one to three more relists of the cluster if
the lead case is being seriously considered, and a long hold (no further orders for
months) if the lead is granted. The residual on the relist claim is a Rule 46
withdrawal: the district court had set a bench trial for August 10, 2026, so a
settlement that moots this petition before redistribution is possible.

## CVSG

A CVSG is more likely on the **lead** petition than on this one. The question is
one of treaty interpretation (which law supplies the equitable-estoppel rule under
the New York Convention), the Solicitor General participated in *GE Energy*, and the
Seventh Circuit's August 2026 decision in *Kim v. Jump Trading* has turned a 4-1
claimed split into an acknowledged 4-2 split. I put a CVSG on 25-1383 at roughly
0.3. If that happens, 26-2 will most likely simply be held without its own
invitation, though the Court sometimes invites the SG in a companion when both are
fully briefed. **P(CVSG on this docket after prediction time) = 0.10.** If one
issues it will come from the cluster's first post-briefing conference (November
2026 to January 2027), and the SG's brief would then push any disposition to
spring or summer 2027.

## Which question, if the Court takes one

The Court would take the lead petition's question presented essentially as written
(federal common law versus state law for nonsignatory equitable estoppel under the
Convention), the question *GE Energy* expressly reserved. It would not take 26-2's
summary-affirmance record as the vehicle. If 26-2 joins the grant at all it would be
by consolidation with 25-1383, which I put at only about 15% of the grant branch:
the petition is nine pages long, the court of appeals entered a one-line summary
affirmance, and petitioners themselves asked for a hold.

## Summary disposition versus plenary review

Conditional on 26-2 ending up in the grant family, the route is very likely a
**cert-order disposition**, not argument: the Court grants 25-1383, decides it in
OT2026, and then GVRs 26-2 in light of that decision (P(summary route | grant) =
0.78). A GVR is the disposition if the Court reverses or vacates in the lead case,
which I expect it would do in roughly two-thirds of grants given that the Fifth
Circuit (now with the Seventh) holds the minority position. If the Court affirms in
the lead, 26-2 is denied.

## Timing

- Lead case denied at the cluster's conference: 26-2 denied on the same order list,
  most likely between late October 2026 and January 2027.
- Lead case CVSG'd: 26-2 sits without orders until the SG files; disposition
  mid-2027.
- Lead case granted: 26-2 held through argument and decision (likely spring 2027),
  then GVR'd or denied in the final order lists of OT2026 or early OT2027.

## Dissent from denial

If the cluster is denied I expect **no separate writing** on 26-2 (P = 0.05). Any
dissent from or statement respecting denial would attach to the lead petition, and
even there a writing is unlikely: the question is technical and the BIO's vehicle
objections (no live foreign party, public-entity respondents, separate contracts)
give the Court a quiet reason to wait for a cleaner case such as a petition from
*Kim v. Jump Trading*.

## Conditional merits note

If the Court grants and reaches the question, the likelier ground is that Chapter 2
and the Convention's uniformity purpose call for a federal rule of decision for
nonsignatory enforcement, distinguishing *Arthur Andersen* as resting on Chapter 1's
"save upon such grounds as exist at law or in equity" text, with the real contest
over whether 9 U.S.C. § 208 imports Chapter 1's state-law approach. That is
conditional content only; a denial produces no opinion.
