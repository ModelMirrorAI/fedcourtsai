## In brief

Insurers asked the Court to review an arbitration dispute with a Louisiana hospital district, then withdrew the case before the Court acted.

## What happened

A Louisiana hospital district and related entity sued their insurers, including Independent Specialty Insurance Company, over hurricane damage claims. The insurers wanted the dispute sent to arbitration under an international insurance treaty, but a Louisiana law bars arbitration clauses in insurance policies issued there. The district court and the Fifth Circuit Court of Appeals sided with the hospital district, ruling that state law, not federal law, controlled whether the insurers—who had not signed the specific arbitration agreement at issue—could still force arbitration. The insurers then asked the Supreme Court to take up the case.

## What the Court is being asked

The petition asked the Court to decide whether federal law or state law determines when a company that did not sign an arbitration agreement can still rely on it to require arbitration, in cases governed by an international arbitration treaty. The petition noted that appeals courts disagree on this question, and it asked the Court to hold the case until a similar petition in another case was resolved.

## Where it stands

The hospital district waived its right to respond, but the Court later requested a response anyway, and the response deadline was extended to September 28, 2026. Before the Court took any action on the petition, the insurers filed a Rule 46 dismissal, withdrawing the case on October 23, 2026.

## What each outcome would mean

Independent Specialty Insurance Company and the other insurer petitioners asked the Court to take the case and reverse the Fifth Circuit's ruling, which had favored the hospital district. By dismissing the petition themselves, the insurers ended their bid for Supreme Court review. The Fifth Circuit's decision applying Louisiana law, which favored the hospital district, remains in place for this case, and the underlying lawsuit continues in the lower courts without Supreme Court involvement.
