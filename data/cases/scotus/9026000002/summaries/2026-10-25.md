## In brief

Insurers asked the Supreme Court to review whether federal or state law governs arbitration disputes under an international insurance treaty, but withdrew the case.

## What happened

A Louisiana hospital district and hospital operator sued their insurers over hurricane damage claims, suing only the domestic insurers and not a foreign insurer also on the policy. The insurers sought to force arbitration under an international arbitration treaty, arguing they could invoke arbitration terms tied to the foreign insurer through a legal doctrine called equitable estoppel. The district court and the Fifth Circuit both ruled against the insurers, applying Louisiana law, which bars arbitration clauses in insurance policies and does not allow equitable estoppel to get around that bar. The insurers then asked the Supreme Court to take the case.

## What the Court is being asked

The petition raised the question of whether federal rules or state law should control when a company that did not sign an arbitration agreement tries to use equitable estoppel to force arbitration under the international arbitration treaty. The petition said courts of appeals are divided on this question, with most applying federal rules and the Fifth Circuit applying state law instead.

## Where it stands

The petition was distributed for conference, and the Court later asked the opposing party to respond after an initial waiver of response. Before the Court acted on the petition, the insurers filed a Rule 46 dismissal, ending their request.

## What each outcome would mean

The insurers, Independent Specialty Insurance Company and others, asked the Court to take up their case and reverse the Fifth Circuit's ruling favoring the hospital district and hospital operator. Because the insurers dismissed the petition under Rule 46, the Court will not rule on the question presented. The Fifth Circuit's decision, which applied Louisiana law and rejected the insurers' arbitration argument, remains in place, and the case continues in the lower courts without Supreme Court review of the circuit split described in the petition.
