## In brief

Insurance companies ask the Court to decide whether federal or state law governs when a nonsignatory can use arbitration agreements in international insurance disputes.

## What happened

A Louisiana hospital district bought storm-damage insurance from several companies, including Independent Specialty Insurance and Interstate Fire & Casualty, after Hurricane Laura. The hospital sued the domestic insurers in Louisiana court without suing a foreign insurer also on the policy. The insurers tried to move the case into arbitration, arguing they could enforce the foreign insurer's arbitration clause under a legal principle called equitable estoppel, which lets a nonsignatory to a contract sometimes enforce or be bound by it. The district court and the Fifth Circuit, relying on a related Fifth Circuit case and a Louisiana Supreme Court ruling, held that Louisiana law bars this, rejecting the insurers' approach.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked whether, under the treaty governing international arbitration agreements, a national common-law rule or instead the law of the relevant state controls whether a company that did not sign an arbitration agreement can still rely on it to require arbitration.

## Where it stands

The petition was distributed for a September 28, 2026 conference, after which the Court asked the hospital district to respond; the response deadline was later extended, with no new conference date reflected in the docket. The petition itself also asks the Court to instead hold the case until it resolves a related petition involving similar parties and the same legal question.

## What each outcome would mean

Independent Specialty Insurance and the other insurers, as petitioners, are asking the Court to take up the case. If the Court grants the petition, it would hear full arguments and decide which body of law governs; granting decides nothing by itself about who wins. If the Court denies the petition, the Fifth Circuit's decision favoring the hospital district stands, leaving state law controlling in that circuit. The Court could also hold the petition pending a related case, as the insurers request, delaying any decision.
