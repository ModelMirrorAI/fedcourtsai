## In brief

Insurers asked the Court to decide which law governs arbitration disputes over an international insurance policy, then jointly agreed to dismiss the case.

## What happened

A Louisiana hospital district and related entities sued their insurers, including Independent Specialty Insurance Company, over unpaid hurricane damage claims. The insurers wanted the dispute sent to arbitration under international rules, arguing that even though the policy had been split into separate contracts with each insurer, they could still invoke an arbitration clause signed by a foreign insurer in the group. The district court and the Fifth Circuit Court of Appeals both disagreed, ruling that state law controlled and blocked that argument, so the case stayed in court rather than going to arbitration.

## What the Court is being asked

The petition asked the Court to decide whether federal rules or state rules should determine when a company that did not sign an arbitration agreement can still rely on it to force arbitration, in cases involving an international treaty on enforcing arbitration agreements. The petition noted that courts of appeals disagreed on this question and asked the Court to hold the case until it resolved a similar petition elsewhere.

## Where it stands

The petition was distributed for conference, and the Court later asked the hospital district to respond after initially receiving a waiver. Before the Court acted on the petition, the parties jointly asked to dismiss it, and the Court dismissed the petition on October 26, 2026.

## What each outcome would mean

Independent Specialty Insurance Company and the other insurers, as petitioners, had asked the Court to take up the case and reverse the Fifth Circuit's ruling that state law governed. Because the parties jointly stipulated to dismiss the petition, the Court did not decide the legal question. The Fifth Circuit's decision favoring the hospital district and against arbitration remains in place, and the underlying lawsuit continues in the lower courts without Supreme Court review of the arbitration issue.
