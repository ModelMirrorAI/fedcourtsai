## What happened

In 2022, an armed fugitive fleeing U.S. Marshals forced his way into Carlos Pena's print shop in North Hollywood, barricaded himself inside, and held off police for about thirteen hours. LAPD SWAT eventually saturated the shop with tear gas; the fugitive had already escaped. Pena says the damage exceeded $60,000 and was not covered by insurance. He sued the City of Los Angeles, arguing the destruction of his property was a taking requiring compensation. The district court ruled against him, finding the police had validly exercised their power to protect public safety. The Ninth Circuit affirmed, holding that property destroyed by officers acting reasonably to protect public safety falls outside the Takings Clause; a concurring judge reasoned separately that Pena never had a property right to exclude police making a lawful arrest.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked whether the government must pay compensation when police intentionally destroy an innocent bystander's property while trying to catch a dangerous suspect, and whether an emergency or "necessity" can excuse the government from that payment obligation. Pena argues that courts of appeals disagree on this, and that the founding-era historical record required compensation even for necessary destruction of property. The city responds that no circuit or claims court has ever ruled otherwise on similar facts, that any disagreement among courts is not a genuine split affecting outcomes, and that changing the rule would be a job for legislatures, not courts.

## Where it stands

The case has not yet been scheduled for oral argument. After full briefing, including several amicus briefs and a reply from Pena, the petition was distributed for the Court's conference on September 28, 2026, where the Justices will decide whether to grant cert, the permission needed to hear the appeal.
