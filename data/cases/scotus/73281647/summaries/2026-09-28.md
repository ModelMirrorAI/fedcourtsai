## What happened

An armed fugitive fleeing law enforcement forced his way into Carlos Pena's print shop and barricaded himself inside. After a lengthy standoff, an LAPD SWAT team deployed tear gas into the shop and neighboring businesses, badly damaging Pena's equipment and inventory. Pena sued the City of Los Angeles, arguing the destruction of his property was a taking requiring compensation under the Constitution. The district court ruled against him, finding the police had validly used their law-enforcement power and no taking occurred. The Ninth Circuit affirmed, holding that no taking occurs when officers destroy property while acting reasonably to protect public safety. The full appeals court declined to rehear the case.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked whether the government must pay compensation when police intentionally destroy an innocent person's property while trying to catch a fugitive, and whether a "public necessity" defense can excuse the government from paying compensation it would otherwise owe for property it destroys.
