## In brief

A print shop owner asks the Court to decide whether police must pay for property they destroyed while catching an armed fugitive.

## What happened

In 2022, an armed fugitive fled into Carlos Pena's print shop in Los Angeles, forced him out, and barricaded himself inside. After a standoff, a police SWAT team used tear gas that destroyed Pena's equipment and inventory, causing damage he estimates at $60,000. The fugitive had already escaped. The city refused to pay. Pena sued, arguing the destruction was a taking of property requiring compensation. The district court ruled against him, and the Ninth Circuit affirmed, holding that police do not have to pay for property they destroy while reasonably protecting public safety from a dangerous suspect.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked whether the government must pay property owners when police intentionally destroy their property while trying to catch a fugitive, and whether an emergency or "necessity" justification can excuse the government from that payment requirement.

## Where it stands

The petition has been fully briefed, including multiple amicus briefs and the city's opposition. It was distributed for conference twice, most recently for the October 9, 2026 conference, and the docket does not yet show a ruling on whether the Court will hear the case.

## What each outcome would mean

Pena, who lost in the lower court, is asking the Court to take up the case. If the Court grants review, it would hear full arguments and decide whether the Takings Clause requires compensation in these circumstances, without yet deciding who wins. If the Court denies review, the Ninth Circuit's ruling against Pena stands, and the city would not have to pay him for the damage, though that denial would not mean the Court agrees the lower court was right.
