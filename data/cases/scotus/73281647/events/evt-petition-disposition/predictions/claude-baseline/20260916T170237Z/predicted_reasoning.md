# Forecast: what the Court will do with No. 25-1163, Pena v. City of Los Angeles

**State forecast from.** The docket shows a single distribution, for the Long
Conference of September 28, 2026. No relist yet, no CVSG. Five amicus briefs
were filed in support at the cert stage. The companion petition, Hadley v. City
of South Bend (No. 25-1158, Seventh Circuit), is distributed for the same
conference.

**Relists.** More likely than not (about 55%) the petition is distributed at
least once more. The Court routinely carries Long Conference petitions with
this profile (paired petitions, five amici, a published decision with a
separate concurrence) over to the early-October conferences, whether it is
moving toward a grant or a Justice is writing separately on a denial. If the
Court is seriously considering a grant, expect two or three relists, with a
decision on the petition in October or early November 2026. If it denies
without a writing, expect denial on the first Order List of October Term 2026
with no relist at all.

**CVSG.** Unlikely (about 10%). The respondent is a city and the operation was
led by a U.S. Marshals task force, so a federal interest exists (the Federal
Circuit's AmeriSource rule and federal-agent property damage), but the Court
called for no SG views in Lech, Baker, or Slaybaugh on the same fact pattern
and the question is framed as one of constitutional history the Court can
resolve without the Solicitor General.

**Disposition.** Denial is the more likely outcome, but this is a genuinely
live petition: I put a grant at 28%. Two Justices (Sotomayor and Gorsuch)
said in Baker that the question is important and divides the courts of
appeals but should percolate; the Ninth Circuit has since published a
lengthy history-based opinion with a concurrence in the judgment on a
different theory, the Seventh Circuit reaffirmed its police-power rule in
Hadley, and the two petitions arrive together. That is the percolation Baker
asked for. Against it, every circuit to face the SWAT fact pattern has denied
compensation, so the split is over reasoning rather than outcome, and the
Court has denied three prior petitions on these facts, the last only
seventeen months ago.

**Which question, if granted.** The Court would grant both Pena and Hadley
(or grant one and hold the other) and take Question 2 substantially as
written, reformulated to cover both theories the circuits use: whether the
Takings Clause categorically exempts property destroyed by law enforcement
acting reasonably under the police power or public necessity. Question 1's
"innocent person" framing would be folded into that question rather than
taken separately. If granted, the likely ground is that necessity and the
search-and-arrest privilege are personal tort defenses that do not displace
the compensation duty, with the inevitable-destruction line (Caltex) left as
the recognized limit; a narrower alternative is a remand to apply a
history-based test without deciding the compensation question outright.

**Summary disposition.** Very unlikely (about 4% conditional on a grant). No
intervening decision of this Court bears on the question, so there is nothing
to GVR in light of, and the Court would want argument on a question it has
twice called open.

**Dissent or statement on denial.** Roughly even odds, slightly below (45%).
Sotomayor and Gorsuch wrote in Baker that the question deserved percolation;
a denial after that percolation invites either a Gorsuch or Thomas dissent
from denial or a renewed Sotomayor statement explaining why the Court is still
waiting for an outcome split. A silent denial, as in Slaybaugh, is the other
real possibility.
