## In brief

A woman held in civil contempt for not paying a divorce-related money judgment asks the Court to review whether jailing her without a finding she could pay was constitutional.

## What happened

Christy Ann Martin and John Fredrick Martin divorced, and a Mississippi chancery court awarded Martin's ex-husband $21,040.72 for property damage, incorporating that award into the divorce judgment. When Christy Ann Martin did not pay, the chancery court held her in contempt, ordering six months' incarceration suspended so long as she made payments. The Mississippi Court of Appeals affirmed, finding the debt enforceable through contempt and the evidence supported that she could pay but chose not to. The Mississippi Supreme Court denied further review by a divided vote. Christy Ann Martin now asks the Supreme Court to take the case; John Fredrick Martin opposes and asks for fees, calling the petition frivolous.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked whether the Constitution's promises of fair treatment and equal footing under the law bar a state from jailing someone for not paying a private money judgment unless a court first asks about, and expressly finds, that the person actually has the present ability to pay. The petition also asks whether treating a civil damages award as "contempt" gets around the constitutional ban on debtors' prisons, and whether putting the burden on the debtor to prove she cannot pay, with a process letting opposing counsel trigger incarceration after a missed payment without a further hearing, meets constitutional minimums.

Christy Ann Martin is asking the Court to take the case. If the Court grants review, it would hear full arguments and decide the constitutional questions, deciding nothing yet about who wins.
