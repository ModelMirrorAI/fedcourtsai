## In brief

The Supreme Court declined to hear a Mississippi woman's challenge to a contempt order that threatened her with jail over an unpaid divorce-related debt.

## What happened

Christy Ann Martin was ordered in her divorce case to pay her ex-husband, John Fredrick Martin, over $21,000 for damaged property. When she did not pay, a chancery court held her in contempt and sentenced her to six months in jail, suspended as long as she made payments. She argued she could not afford to pay and that the court never properly found she had the ability to pay. The Mississippi Court of Appeals upheld the contempt order, and the Mississippi Supreme Court denied further review, with two justices voting to hear it. John Fredrick Martin's brief disputes Martin's account, saying the record shows the court did find she could pay but chose not to.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked whether the Constitution's guarantees of fair treatment bar a state from jailing someone for not paying a private civil debt unless a court first holds a hearing and expressly finds that person can actually afford to pay. A related question is whether courts may treat a money judgment as contempt of court, exposing someone to jail, when ordinary debt-collection tools remain available instead.

## Where it stands

The petition was distributed for conference, considered, and denied. The Mississippi Court of Appeals' decision upholding the contempt order remains in place.

Because the Court denied review, the Mississippi Court of Appeals' decision upholding the contempt finding and suspended incarceration order stands. This means John Fredrick Martin's win below remains in effect, and the Court did not decide whether the constitutional arguments Martin raised were correct.
