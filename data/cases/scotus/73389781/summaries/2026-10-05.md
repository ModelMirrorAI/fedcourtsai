## In brief

The Supreme Court declined to review a Mississippi woman's challenge to a contempt order jailing her for not paying a divorce-related money judgment.

## What happened

Christy Ann Martin was ordered in her divorce case to pay John Fredrick Martin $21,040.72 for property damage. When she did not pay, a Mississippi chancery court held her in civil contempt and sentenced her to six months in jail, suspended if she followed a payment plan. Martin argued the court never expressly found she could pay, and that incarcerating her for an unpaid civil debt was unconstitutional. The Mississippi Court of Appeals affirmed, finding the chancery court's decision supported by the evidence and incarceration a permissible contempt sanction. The Mississippi Supreme Court denied further review by a divided vote.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked whether the Constitution's guarantees of fair treatment require a court to specifically find, on the record, that a person can actually pay before jailing them for failing to pay a private money judgment, and whether using contempt power this way amounts to unconstitutional imprisonment for debt when other collection methods remain available. The petition also asks whether placing the burden on the debtor to prove she cannot pay, rather than requiring the other side to prove she can, meets constitutional standards.

## Where it stands

The petition was distributed for the Court's conference on September 28, 2026, and the Court denied it on October 5, 2026.

## What each outcome would mean

Martin, as petitioner, asked the Court to take up her case and overturn the Mississippi courts' rulings. The Court's denial leaves the Mississippi Court of Appeals' decision in place, meaning the contempt finding and suspended incarceration order against Martin stand, and John Fredrick Martin's win in the lower courts is unaffected. The denial does not mean the Court agreed with the lower court's reasoning; it simply leaves that decision as the final word in this case.
