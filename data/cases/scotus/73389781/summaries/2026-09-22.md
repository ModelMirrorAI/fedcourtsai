## What happened

Christy Ann Martin and John Fredrick Martin divorced, and a Mississippi chancery court awarded John $21,040.72 for property Christy damaged when she moved out. When Christy did not pay, the court held her in contempt and ordered six months of incarceration, suspended so long as she followed a payment plan. Christy testified at a hearing about her finances; the chancellor found she could work but chose not to, and was not truthful about her inability to pay. The Mississippi Court of Appeals affirmed, and the Mississippi Supreme Court denied further review by a 5-2 vote. Christy was never actually jailed because she appealed right after the order was entered. John, in his brief, argues Christy's petition misstates the record and asks the Court to award him fees for a frivolous filing.

## What the Court is being asked

Christy argues that jailing someone for failing to pay a private debt violates constitutional guarantees unless a court expressly finds, on the record, that the person actually has the present ability to pay, and that courts should not be able to use the label "contempt" to get around long-standing limits on imprisoning people for unpaid debts. She also argues the burden should not fall on her to prove she cannot pay, and that a process letting the other side automatically trigger her arrest for a missed payment does not provide enough of a hearing. John responds that the case turns only on the trial court's factual, credibility-based findings, that she did receive a hearing and an explicit ability-to-pay finding against her, that she was never jailed and can still raise the defense later, and that she did not properly raise these federal constitutional arguments in the state courts.
