# Forecast: Martin v. Martin, No. 25-1307 (evt-petition-disposition)

**Disposition.** The Court will deny certiorari at or shortly after the September 28, 2026 conference. P(any grant) = 0.015.

**Relists.** The docket shows one distribution (July 8, 2026, for the conference of September 28, 2026); the petition has never been relisted. I expect no further distribution: the order list following the long conference will carry a denial. A single relist is the main alternative (about 12%), most plausibly because a Justice is considering a short statement about the debtors'-prison theme rather than because the Court is moving toward a grant.

**CVSG.** None. No federal party, statute, or program is implicated; this is a private divorce-decree contempt dispute under Mississippi law. P(CVSG) is negligible.

**Which question, if any.** If the Court took anything, it would be a reformulated version of Questions 1 and 3 together: whether Turner v. Rogers' minimum safeguards, in particular an express finding of present ability to pay, govern a state court's coercive incarceration to enforce a money award incorporated into a divorce decree. Question 2 (that "relabeling" a civil judgment as contempt is itself a Fourteenth Amendment violation) would be left behind; it rests mainly on state anti-imprisonment-for-debt clauses and has no developed federal doctrine behind it.

**Summary disposition versus plenary review.** Conditional on a grant, a summary route is about as likely as plenary review (0.45). There is no intervening decision to GVR against, but the fact pattern the petition describes (a suspended six-month sentence, counsel authorized to "automatically" submit an incarceration order) is the kind the Court has occasionally corrected per curiam under Turner rather than set for argument. On the other side, the Court would want full briefing on the private-judgment question if it thought it worth reaching at all. Either way the grant itself is the improbable step.

**Dissent or statement respecting denial.** Unlikely (0.03). The debtors'-prison theme has drawn separate writings before, but here the chancellor made an on-the-record willfulness finding, the petitioner was represented by counsel and testified, she was never jailed, and the federal claim was not squarely presented below. Those features leave little for a Justice to write about; expect a bare denial. The respondent's Rule 42.2 request for damages and costs will be ignored, as such requests almost always are.

**If granted (conditional only).** The Court would most likely hold that Turner's procedural floor applies to coercive incarceration for nonpayment of any court-ordered money obligation, including one incorporated into a divorce judgment, and would vacate and remand for the state court to apply it, without reaching whether the Fourteenth Amendment forbids contempt enforcement of private money judgments altogether.
