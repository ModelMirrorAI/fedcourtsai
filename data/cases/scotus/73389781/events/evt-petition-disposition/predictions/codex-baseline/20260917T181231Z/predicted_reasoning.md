# Forecast

The Court will most likely deny certiorari without explanation following the September 28, 2026 conference, probably in the late-September or early-October orders. This is a timing forecast, not a confirmed orders schedule. The provisioned docket shows one distribution, entered July 8, and no CVSG. I expect zero additional distributions; the probability of at least one more is 9%. If further consideration occurs, one additional distribution is more likely than a prolonged sequence.

The Court will not call for the Solicitor General's views: probability of a new CVSG is 0.3%. The dispute concerns constitutional limits on a private judgment creditor's use of state contempt procedures, rather than a federal program whose administration calls for the government's specialized input.

Any grant is unlikely (1.8%). Conditional on a grant, plenary review is the more likely route (70%), rather than disposition in the cert order itself (30%). A summary route would more plausibly address the adequacy of the ability-to-pay safeguards than respond to an identified intervening decision; no such intervening decision is identified in the materials consulted.

If review is granted, expect a narrowed question combining QP 1 and QP 3: whether the findings and procedures supporting threatened confinement establish present ability to satisfy the purge conditions and preserve a meaningful judicial hearing before incarceration. The Court would be less likely to take QP 2's broad challenge to contempt enforcement of private money judgments. If it reaches the merits, the likely focus is the distinction between earning capacity and present ability to comply, not a categorical invalidation of all monetary divorce-decree contempt sanctions.

Conditional on denial, I assign 3.5% to any noted dissent or statement respecting denial and expect none. I do not predict individual cert votes or identify a likely author. A denial would leave the state judgment in place without endorsing its constitutional reasoning.
