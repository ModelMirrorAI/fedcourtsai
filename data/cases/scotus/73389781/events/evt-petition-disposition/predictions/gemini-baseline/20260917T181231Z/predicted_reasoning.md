The case presents a weak vehicle for the constitutional questions raised regarding debtors' prisons, as the state court's decision rests heavily on factual findings that the petitioner voluntarily chose unemployment and possessed the capacity to pay, rather than a denial of a hearing on indigency. 

No CVSG is expected, as the case is a private family law dispute from state court that does not implicate the interests of the federal government. If the Court were to grant the petition, it would likely take the first Question Presented regarding the necessity of an express on-the-record finding of present ability to pay before incarcerating a judgment debtor. A summary disposition route (GVR) is highly unlikely since there is no intervening decision bearing on the issue and the respondent vehemently contests error. Should the Court deny the petition, expect no separate writing, as the fact-bound credibility determinations of the state court make this a poor candidate for a dissent from denial.
