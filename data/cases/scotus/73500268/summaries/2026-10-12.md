## In brief

Roofing Designs asks the Court to send its lawsuit back to the appeals court for another look, based on a recent ruling about when bankruptcy filing mistakes block later claims.

## What happened

Roofing Designs sued Royal American and Hartford over a construction dispute, after disclosing its own claims against them only vaguely and at a $0 value during its bankruptcy case. The district court ruled that Roofing Designs could not pursue those claims because it had taken inconsistent positions in the two proceedings, a doctrine called judicial estoppel. The Fifth Circuit affirmed, applying its rule that any potential motive to conceal a claim defeats a finding that the omission was an honest mistake. Roofing Designs asked the Supreme Court to hold its petition until deciding a similar case, Keathley, then send its case back for reconsideration.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked whether a lower court may rule that a debtor's inconsistent bankruptcy disclosures were not an honest mistake just by pointing to a possible motive to hide a claim's value, without proof the debtor actually acted in bad faith, or whether courts must instead weigh all the circumstances together.

## Where it stands

After the Supreme Court decided Keathley, the parties filed opposing briefs on what that decision means for this case, and the petition was distributed for a conference on October 9, 2026. The docket does not yet show any action taken at that conference.

## What each outcome would mean

Roofing Designs, as petitioner, asks the Court to send its case back to the Fifth Circuit for reconsideration. If the Court grants that request, the Fifth Circuit's ruling against Roofing Designs would be set aside and the case sent back for another look under the Keathley decision, without the Supreme Court deciding who should ultimately win. If the Court denies review, the Fifth Circuit's decision barring Roofing Designs' claims against Royal American and Hartford stays in place.
