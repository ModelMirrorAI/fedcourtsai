## In brief

The Court vacated a company's lost lawsuit against a contractor and insurer and sent it back for review under a newly decided ruling on bankruptcy disclosures.

## What happened

Roofing Designs sued Royal American Construction and, in turn, Hartford Fire Insurance, over unpaid work on a Houston apartment project. While that suit was pending, Roofing Designs filed for bankruptcy and listed its claim against Royal American as worth nothing, and initially left out its claim against Hartford entirely. A federal district court ruled that these inconsistent positions barred Roofing Designs from pursuing its claims at all, a doctrine called judicial estoppel, and the Fifth Circuit Court of Appeals affirmed. Roofing Designs then asked the Supreme Court to review that decision.

## What the Court is being asked

Roofing Designs asked the Court to hold its case until deciding a separate case, Keathley, that raised the same issue: whether courts may find a company failed to properly disclose a lawsuit as a bankruptcy asset just because it had some possible reason to hide it, without proof it actually acted in bad faith. Royal American and Hartford argued the Keathley decision did not adopt that rule and that the lower courts' findings against Roofing Designs would stand anyway under the approach Keathley did adopt.

## Where it stands

The Supreme Court granted the petition, vacated the Fifth Circuit's judgment, and sent the case back for further consideration in light of its decision in Keathley v. Buddy Ayers Construction, Inc.

## What each outcome would mean

Roofing Designs, which lost in the lower courts, asked the Court to act. The Court's action sends the case back to the Fifth Circuit to reconsider its ruling using the standard set out in Keathley, rather than the approach it used before. This does not decide who wins: the Fifth Circuit could still rule against Roofing Designs, as Royal American and Hartford argued the facts support, or it could rule in Roofing Designs' favor and let its claims proceed.
