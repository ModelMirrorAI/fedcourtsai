## In brief

The Court sent a contractor's lawsuit back to a lower court to be reconsidered under a new Supreme Court ruling on when bankruptcy disclosure mistakes bar later claims.

## What happened

Roofing Designs sued, and was sued by, Royal American Construction over a construction project, also bringing in Hartford Fire Insurance as a third party. While that suit was pending, Roofing Designs filed for bankruptcy and listed its claim against Royal American as worth nothing, without mentioning its claim against Hartford at all. After the bankruptcy plan was approved, a federal district court ruled that Roofing Designs could not pursue its claims because its bankruptcy filings contradicted its litigation position, a result called judicial estoppel. The Fifth Circuit Court of Appeals affirmed, and rehearing was denied.

## What the Court is being asked

Roofing Designs asked the Court to hold its case until it decided a separate case, Keathley, about the standard for finding that a bankruptcy disclosure mistake was not innocent, and then to send this case back for another look under whatever rule Keathley set. Royal American and Hartford answered that Keathley did not adopt the rule Roofing Designs wanted and that the lower courts' findings already support the result under Keathley's standard.

## Where it stands

The Supreme Court granted the petition, set aside the Fifth Circuit's judgment, and sent the case back to that court to reconsider in light of its recent decision in Keathley v. Buddy Ayers Construction, Inc.

## What each outcome would mean

Roofing Designs, which lost in the lower courts, asked the Court to act. The Court's order does not decide who is right. It wipes out the Fifth Circuit's ruling against Roofing Designs and requires that court to re-examine the case using the standard set out in Keathley. Royal American and Hartford, who had the earlier victory, argued the same result should follow anyway; whether it does will now be decided again by the Fifth Circuit.
