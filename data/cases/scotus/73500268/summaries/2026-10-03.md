## In brief

A roofing contractor asks the Court to send its lawsuit back for another look after the Court ruled in a different bankruptcy-disclosure case.

## What happened

Roofing Designs sued, and was sued by, Royal American Construction over a Houston construction project, also bringing in Hartford Fire Insurance as a third party. While that suit was pending, Roofing Designs filed for bankruptcy and listed its claims against Royal American as worth nothing and left out its claim against Hartford entirely. A federal district court ruled that this inconsistency barred Roofing Designs from pursuing its claims at all, a doctrine called judicial estoppel. The Fifth Circuit affirmed, applying its rule that a company's mere potential motive to hide a claim defeats any excuse that the omission was accidental.

## What the Court is being asked

Roofing Designs asked the Court to hold its case until it decided a similar case, Keathley, involving the same legal rule, and then to send the case back to the Fifth Circuit to apply whatever new standard Keathley set. Royal American and Hartford respond that Keathley has since been decided, did not adopt the rule Roofing Designs wanted, and left the lower court's ruling against Roofing Designs fully supported under the new standard, so no further review or do-over is needed.

## Where it stands

The petition has been distributed for a conference after full briefing, including a response and reply; the docket does not yet show any action taken on it.

## What each outcome would mean

Roofing Designs is asking the Court to act. If the Court grants the petition and sends the case back (a GVR), the Fifth Circuit would reconsider its ruling in light of the Keathley decision, without the Supreme Court deciding who should ultimately win. If the Court denies the petition, the Fifth Circuit's ruling stands, leaving Roofing Designs barred from pursuing its claims against Royal American and Hartford. A denial would not mean the Court agrees the lower ruling was correct, only that it leaves it in place.
