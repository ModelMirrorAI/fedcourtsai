## In brief

Floyd Johnson asks the Supreme Court to decide whether veterans may bring constitutional challenges to federal benefits statutes directly in federal district court.

## What happened

Floyd Johnson, an Army veteran, had his disability benefits reduced under a federal law that limits payments to incarcerated felons. Rather than pursue review through the Department of Veterans Affairs and its specialized appeals courts, he sued in federal district court, arguing the law was unconstitutional. The district court dismissed his claim. The Eleventh Circuit agreed the suit could not proceed there, holding that a 1988 law created a system that sends such claims exclusively through the VA's own appeals board, a specialized veterans court, and the Federal Circuit, bypassing ordinary district courts. Johnson says other circuits have allowed similar constitutional claims to be filed directly in district court, creating a split among the courts of appeals.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked whether the specialized review system Congress created for veterans' benefits disputes also covers claims that a benefits statute itself is unconstitutional, or whether district courts keep their ordinary power to hear such constitutional claims directly.

## Where it stands

The Court granted review in April 2026. The case was argued on October 5, 2026, and now awaits decision.

## What each outcome would mean

Johnson, who asked the Court to hear the case, argues district courts should keep jurisdiction over such constitutional claims; the United States Congress, represented by the Solicitor General, argues the specialized veterans' review system is the only path. If the Court affirms the lower court's ruling, Johnson's suit would remain barred from district court, and veterans with similar constitutional claims would have to pursue them through the VA's internal appeals board and specialized courts. If the Court reverses, Johnson's case could proceed in district court, and veterans generally would be able to raise such challenges there directly. If the Court vacates, the lower court's decision would be set aside and the case sent back without resolving the question for either side.
