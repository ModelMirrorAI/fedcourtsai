Floyd Johnson asks the Supreme Court to decide whether veterans may bring constitutional challenges to federal benefits statutes directly in federal district court.

## What happened

Johnson, an Army veteran convicted of felonies and imprisoned, had his disability benefits reduced under a law limiting payments to incarcerated veterans. Representing himself, he sued the United States Congress in federal district court, arguing the law was unconstitutional. The district court dismissed his case. On appeal, the Eleventh Circuit ruled that Congress could not be sued and that amending the complaint to name a proper defendant would be pointless, because it held that a separate law, the Veterans' Judicial Review Act, requires such constitutional claims to go through a specialized administrative and appeals process rather than ordinary federal court, and that this process does not include review by district courts.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked whether a law setting up a special review system for veterans' benefits decisions also requires veterans to route constitutional challenges to the benefits statutes themselves through that system, or whether such challenges may instead be filed directly in federal district court, as this Court held was permitted under an earlier version of the law.

## Where it stands

The Court granted review, and the case was argued on October 5, 2026. It now awaits decision.

## What each outcome would mean

Johnson, who brought this case, asks the Court to reverse the lower court's ruling. If the Court rules for Johnson, veterans could bring constitutional challenges to benefits statutes directly in district court, and his case would continue there. If the Court rules for the government, which defends the appeals court's decision, Johnson's case would remain dismissed for lack of jurisdiction, and veterans with similar claims would have to pursue them through the specialized veterans' benefits review process instead of district court. The Court could also send the case back without deciding the question either way.
