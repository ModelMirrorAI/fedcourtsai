## In brief

A veteran asks the Court to decide whether federal district courts, rather than only the specialized veterans' benefits review system, can hear his constitutional challenge to a benefits-reduction law.

## What happened

Floyd Johnson, an Army veteran, received disability benefits after being diagnosed with PTSD, but a federal law cut his benefits because he was incarcerated for a felony. Representing himself, Johnson sued Congress in federal district court, arguing the law was an unconstitutional bill of attainder and violated equal protection. The district court dismissed the case. On appeal, the Eleventh Circuit held that Congress could not be sued and that, under the Veterans' Judicial Review Act, only the specialized veterans' courts—not federal district courts—could hear this kind of constitutional challenge. The appeals court vacated the dismissal and ordered the case thrown out for lack of jurisdiction, departing from several other circuits that allow such claims in district court.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked whether a law setting up a special review process for veterans' benefits decisions also takes away federal district courts' power to hear claims that a benefits statute itself is unconstitutional, or whether, as the Court held in an earlier case about a similar law, district courts keep that authority alongside the specialized system.

## Where it stands

The Court granted review of the case, and the parties and supporting groups filed their written arguments. The case was argued before the Court on October 5, 2026, and a decision has not yet been recorded on the docket.

## What each outcome would mean

Johnson, the veteran, asked the Court to take up the case after the Eleventh Circuit ruled against him. If the Court sides with Johnson, veterans could bring standalone constitutional challenges to benefits statutes directly in federal district court, as they did for decades under the Court's earlier ruling, rather than going through the lengthy veterans' administrative appeals process first. If the Court sides with Congress, the government defending the statute, such challenges would have to proceed through the specialized Board of Veterans' Appeals, the Court of Appeals for Veterans Claims, and the Federal Circuit instead of starting in district court.
