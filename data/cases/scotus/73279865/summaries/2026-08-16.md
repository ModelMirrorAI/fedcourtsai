## What happened

Floyd Johnson, an Army veteran, had his disability benefits reduced under a law that limits benefits for veterans incarcerated for more than 60 days on a felony conviction. Rather than going through the Department of Veterans Affairs' internal appeals process, Johnson filed a pro se lawsuit in federal district court naming the United States Congress as defendant, arguing the law is unconstitutional. The district court dismissed his claim on the merits. On appeal, the Eleventh Circuit ruled instead that the district court had no jurisdiction at all: it held that a 1988 law creating a specialized review system for veterans' benefits claims requires all constitutional challenges, including broad challenges to a law itself, to go through that system rather than ordinary federal court.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked whether that 1988 review law took away federal district courts' power to hear constitutional challenges to laws affecting veterans' benefits, or whether such challenges can still be filed directly in district court as this Court had previously allowed under an older version of the law. Johnson argues district courts retain that power; the government argues the review law now channels all such claims exclusively through the veterans' benefits appeals system and ultimately a specialized appeals court.

## Where it stands

The Court granted cert and the case has been fully briefed, with amicus briefs filed on both sides. It was argued on October 5, 2026, and now awaits decision.
