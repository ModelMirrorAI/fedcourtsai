## In brief

A veteran whose disability benefits were cut asks the Court to decide whether his constitutional challenge to that cut belongs in federal district court.

## What happened

Floyd Johnson, an Army veteran, received VA disability benefits that were reduced under a law limiting benefits for veterans incarcerated after a felony conviction. Representing himself, Johnson sued in federal district court, naming the United States Congress as defendant and arguing the law was unconstitutional. A magistrate judge recommended dismissal on the merits, and the district court agreed. On appeal, the Eleventh Circuit appointed Johnson a lawyer, then ruled that district courts lack jurisdiction over such claims at all, because a specialized review system for veterans' claims must handle them instead, and ordered the case dismissed on that basis.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked whether a law setting up a special administrative and court system for veterans' benefits disputes also closes the door to ordinary federal district courts for a veteran's claim that the benefits-cutting statute itself is unconstitutional, or whether such constitutional challenges can still be filed directly in district court as this Court allowed under an earlier version of the law.

## Where it stands

## What each outcome would mean

Johnson, who lost in the lower court, asked the Court to take up the case.
