P(disturbed) 0.70. I predict a reversal of the Eleventh Circuit's dismissal.

The Eleventh Circuit, joined only by the Eighth Circuit, held that the VJRA stripped district courts of jurisdiction over constitutional challenges to veterans' benefits statutes, creating a 6-2 circuit split. The base rate for reversing a lower court judgment at the merits stage is approximately 65-70%. I anchor near the base rate of ~70% disturbed, making a small upward adjustment because the Supreme Court granted certiorari despite the Solicitor General's opposition and victory below, which typically signals an intent to reverse the outlier and resolve the split in favor of the majority rule. 

Furthermore, recent Supreme Court jurisprudence (e.g., *Axon Enter. v. FTC*) demonstrates a strong current majority preference for preserving district court jurisdiction over constitutional claims unless Congress has provided a clear statement to the contrary. The petitioner's argument that *Johnson v. Robison*'s interpretation of "decision under a law" survives the VJRA amendments is a strong textual hook that will likely appeal to the textualist wing, while the preservation of veterans' access to courts for constitutional claims will appeal to the liberal wing.

My primary uncertainty lies in the strength of *Elgin v. Department of the Treasury* and *Thunder Basin* as precedents for the Solicitor General's argument. In those cases, the Court held that comprehensive administrative schemes with eventual Article III review (like the CSRA and the Federal Circuit) preclude district court jurisdiction. The VJRA channels claims similarly to the CAVC and the Federal Circuit. If the Court strictly follows the *Elgin* framework rather than *Axon* or *Robison*, it would affirm. However, the explicit language of Section 511(a) and the clear statement rule lead me to predict a reversal.
