# Forecast

The Court will affirm the Eleventh Circuit and hold that Floyd Johnson's constitutional challenge to 38 U.S.C. § 5313 must proceed through the review system created by the Veterans' Judicial Review Act rather than in district court. The likely lineup is 6–3, with Chief Justice Roberts and Justices Thomas, Sotomayor, Kavanaugh, Barrett, and Jackson in the majority, and Justices Alito, Kagan, and Gorsuch dissenting.

The majority will begin with current 38 U.S.C. § 511(a). Its first sentence directs the Secretary to decide all questions of law and fact necessary to a benefits decision, and its second sentence bars other-court review of the Secretary's decision on any such question except through the statutory route. Whether § 5313 is constitutional determines Johnson's benefit amount and therefore falls within that language. Sections 7261 and 7292 reinforce this reading by authorizing the Veterans Court and Federal Circuit to decide constitutional questions and challenges to statutory validity. The Court will reject a facial/as-applied distinction because both forms of challenge can supply the legal premise for changing an individual benefits award.

The opinion will distinguish Johnson v. Robison rather than overrule it. Robison construed the differently structured pre-VJRA provision at a time when channeling the claim would have eliminated judicial review. The VJRA both rewrote the operative sentence and supplied review in the Veterans Court and an Article III Federal Circuit. Elgin's treatment of constitutional claims within a specialized review scheme will control, while Axon will be distinguished because Johnson seeks to undo a particular benefits reduction rather than challenge the VA's constitutional authority to conduct proceedings at all.

The holding will be narrow to constitutional questions necessary to individual veterans-benefits decisions. It will leave open whether a wholly collateral structural challenge could begin in district court and will not decide the merits of Johnson's bill-of-attainder or equal-protection claims. A dismissal as improvidently granted is unlikely: the Eleventh Circuit squarely made VJRA preclusion dispositive, and substituting a proper VA defendant would matter only if Johnson prevails on that question.
