# Prediction rationale

I assign a 0.40 probability that the Court disturbs the judgment below and select an affirmance as the modal judgment. The committed merits statpack provides a 69.7% disturbed-rate anchor (359 disturbed judgments among 515 parsed judgments in the available OT2017–OT2024 rows of the ten-Term window preceding the case's OT2025 grant Term). That pool clears the registered sample floor and publishes the pool-guard exclusions, although it covers the parsed slice and the most recent Terms remain affected by pendency. I adjust substantially downward from that generic merits anchor because the respondent has a strong case-specific textual argument under the amended § 511(a), an express alternative judicial route exists, and Elgin closely supports channeling a nonstructural constitutional claim whose success would directly change an individual benefit award.

The principal reason not to go lower is Robison. It unanimously treated a constitutional attack on a benefits statute as a challenge to Congress's decision rather than the Administrator's decision under benefits law. The VJRA retained some of the language central to that construction, and the Court often demands clarity before reading a statute to displace ordinary § 1331 jurisdiction. Johnson also argues persuasively that adding review for previously unreviewable benefits decisions need not silently withdraw preexisting district-court review. The grant after a relist, despite the government's opposition and an acknowledged circuit split in which the Eleventh Circuit took the minority position, modestly strengthens the reversal side. Those considerations keep disturbance at 0.40 rather than treating the government's statutory reading as decisive.

The predicted lineup draws most heavily on the division in Elgin and on more recent skepticism of jurisdiction stripping. Thomas, Roberts, and Sotomayor joined the Elgin majority; Alito and Kagan took the anti-preclusion side there; and Gorsuch's Axon concurrence strongly favored ordinary federal-question jurisdiction absent unmistakable displacement. Kavanaugh, Barrett, and Jackson are less certain in this precise setting, but the current statute's affirmative direction to decide necessary legal questions makes an affirming coalition more likely than not. No writing roles are forecast because the available record does not support a reliable authorship prediction.
