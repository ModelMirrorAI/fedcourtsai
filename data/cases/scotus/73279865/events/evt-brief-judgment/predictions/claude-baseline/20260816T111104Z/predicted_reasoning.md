# Forecast: Johnson v. United States Congress, No. 25-735 (evt-brief-judgment)

## Judgment class

The Court will **reverse** the Eleventh Circuit's judgment and remand. The
Eleventh Circuit vacated the district court's merits dismissal and ordered
dismissal for lack of jurisdiction; the Court will hold that the Veterans'
Judicial Review Act did not strip district courts of the jurisdiction
recognized in *Johnson v. Robison*, 415 U.S. 361 (1974), over facial
constitutional challenges to acts of Congress affecting veterans' benefits.
P(judgment below disturbed) = 0.78. The likeliest label is `reversed`;
`vacated` (with a remand for the court of appeals to address the proper-party
question in the first instance) is the main alternative within the disturbed
set. A DIG is unlikely: the Solicitor General's brief in opposition itself
called the case a suitable vehicle and supported the grant, and the Eleventh
Circuit treated the wrong-defendant defect as non-dispositive, resting on
futility because of its preclusion holding.

## Vote lineup

Modal lineup: **unanimous (9–0) for reversal** — Roberts, Thomas, Alito,
Sotomayor, Kagan, Gorsuch, Kavanaugh, Barrett, Jackson all in the majority.
*Axon Enterprise v. FTC* (2023) was unanimous in favor of district-court
jurisdiction over structural constitutional claims on this Court, and the
textual argument here is stronger for the challenger than Axon's was: there is
an on-point unanimous precedent (*Robison*) construing materially similar
language. If the Court divides, the likeliest shape is 7–2 or 8–1 with a
dissent adopting the government's *Elgin*-style channeling reading — Thomas
(the author of *Elgin*) is the likeliest dissenter, though his *Axon*
concurrence expressing doubts about *Thunder Basin* channeling cuts the other
way. Expect one or two separate concurrences (Gorsuch and/or Thomas) using the
case to renew criticism of implied-channeling doctrine. Authorship is not
scored and is a guess: a junior-to-middle Justice writing for a lopsided
Court — Barrett or Jackson is the modal author.

## Ground and breadth

The majority will decide on **the text of 38 U.S.C. § 511(a), reaffirming
*Robison***: the preclusion clause reaches only "decision[s] of the Secretary"
made "under" a benefits law, and a facial constitutional challenge to an act of
Congress challenges a decision of Congress, not of the Secretary. Congress
preserved the operative language *Robison* construed when it enacted the VJRA
in 1988, so the prior construction carried forward. The Court will treat the
government's *Elgin*/*Thunder Basin* framework as beside the point where the
express text does not reach the claim, distinguishing rather than disturbing
those cases.

Breadth: **categorical as to the class, and no broader** — facial
constitutional challenges to veterans'-benefits statutes may proceed in
district court under § 1331, while individual benefits determinations and
as-applied challenges remain exclusively channeled through the Board, the
Veterans Court, and the Federal Circuit. The opinion will not announce a
general rule about channeling schemes outside Title 38.

## Which question, and what the Court leaves open

The Court will take the QP as written — it is single and clean — and will not
reach the underlying bill-of-attainder and equal-protection merits of 38
U.S.C. § 5313, which go back down on remand. The sovereign-immunity /
proper-defendant question (petitioner named Congress rather than the
Secretary) will be left for amendment on remand, not resolved as an
alternative ground of affirmance.
