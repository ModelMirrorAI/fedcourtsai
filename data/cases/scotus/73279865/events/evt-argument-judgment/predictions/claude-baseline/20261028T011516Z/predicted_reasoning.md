# Johnson v. United States Congress (No. 25-735) — forecast of the judgment

**Judgment class: reversed** (P(disturbed) = 0.64). The Court holds that 38 U.S.C. 511(a) does
not strip district courts of jurisdiction over a challenge to the constitutional validity of a
veterans'-benefits statute (here, the 38 U.S.C. 5313 benefit reduction for incarcerated
veterans), and reverses the Eleventh Circuit's jurisdictional ruling, remanding for further
proceedings. Because the Eleventh Circuit's own judgment was a vacatur with instructions to
dismiss, the mandate may read "vacated and remanded" rather than "reversed"; either disturbs the
judgment below. A DIG is unlikely: the government confessed the circuit split and the question
is cleanly presented. The sovereign-immunity point (Congress as named defendant) is left for
remand, since the Eleventh Circuit treated amendment as futile only because of the VJRA.

**Vote lineup: 7–2 for petitioner.** Majority: Roberts, Thomas, Alito, Sotomayor, Gorsuch,
Kavanaugh, Jackson. Dissent: Kagan, Barrett. Plausible alternatives are 6–3 (Thomas or Alito
joining the dissent) or a government win with Kagan, Alito, Barrett, Kavanaugh, Roberts.

**Ground.** The majority reads "a decision by the Secretary under a law" as a decision applying
the benefits statute as written, so the statute's own validity is not a question "necessary to"
that decision. It treats the VJRA as re-enacting Robison's construction of that phrase against a
settled 1988 understanding that administrative tribunals do not decide the constitutionality of
Acts of Congress, reinforced by decades of BVA practice disclaiming that authority and by the
government's own recent contrary position. It answers the 7292(c) argument by reading that
provision as defining the Federal Circuit's scope of review within the VJRA track, not as a
channeling command.

**Breadth.** Narrow: limited to challenges to the statute itself. Claims attacking VA action,
constitutional ones included, stay channeled. The Court does not reach the Article II /
Appointments Clause question about BVA authority, though it may mention it as a reason to avoid
the government's reading.

**Who writes.** Gorsuch is the likeliest author: he pressed the original-public-meaning and
settled-practice theory hardest. Kavanaugh and Alito are next. Alito dissented in Elgin in favor
of district-court review of facial challenges, and asked how to write a narrow opinion. Expect a
Kagan dissent on the text of 511(a) (she said the statute "as it exists" favors the government,
and cited the House report), possibly joined by Barrett. A separate concurrence from Thomas,
Alito, or Gorsuch (if not author) on the Article II concern or on legislative history is
plausible.
