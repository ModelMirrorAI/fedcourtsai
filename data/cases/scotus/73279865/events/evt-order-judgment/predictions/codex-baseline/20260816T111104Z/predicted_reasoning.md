# Forecast of the Court's disposition and reasoning

The Court will reverse the Eleventh Circuit and remand, likely by a 7–2 vote. Chief Justice Roberts and Justices Alito, Kagan, Gorsuch, Kavanaugh, Barrett, and Jackson will vote to disturb the judgment; Justices Thomas and Sotomayor will dissent. Neither recusal nor an equally divided Court is expected.

The majority will answer the question presented in Johnson's favor without reaching the constitutionality of the incarcerated-veteran benefit reduction. It will reason that 38 U.S.C. § 511(a) makes final a decision of the Secretary on questions necessary to a benefits decision, whereas a genuine facial challenge to an Act of Congress contests Congress's enactment rather than the Secretary's application of that enactment. Because the VJRA retained the statutory language central to *Johnson v. Robison*, its creation of review in the Veterans Court and Federal Circuit did not silently abrogate *Robison* or displace the district courts' 28 U.S.C. § 1331 jurisdiction.

The holding will be bounded. Facial constitutional challenges to veterans-benefits statutes may begin in district court, but ordinary benefits disputes and as-applied claims requiring review of an individual VA determination will remain within the VJRA channel. The majority will distinguish *Elgin* as construction of a different review scheme without *Robison*'s controlling interpretation of retained statutory text. It may invoke the presumption of judicial review and the *Thunder Basin* factors as confirmation, but not adopt a general rule allowing every constitutional objection connected to veterans' benefits to bypass the specialized process.

The dissent will treat the constitutional issue as a question necessary to the Secretary's reduction of Johnson's benefits. It will emphasize that the VJRA broadened the predecessor statute, expressly empowered the Veterans Court and Federal Circuit to decide constitutional issues, and supplies meaningful Article III review under *Elgin*. On that view, distinguishing facial from as-applied challenges has no footing in the enacted channel and invites duplicative litigation.

The Court will remand for proceedings concerning a proper defendant and the district court's consideration of Johnson's constitutional claims. A decision is most likely during the 2026 Term after plenary argument, rather than through a procedural dismissal.
