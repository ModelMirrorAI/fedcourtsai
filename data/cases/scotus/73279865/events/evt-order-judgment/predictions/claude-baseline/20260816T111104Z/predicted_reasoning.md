# Forecast — Johnson v. United States Congress, No. 25-735 (merits, moment: grant)

**Judgment class: `reversed`.** The Court will hold that the Veterans' Judicial
Review Act did not strip district courts of the jurisdiction recognized in
*Johnson v. Robison*, 415 U.S. 361 (1974), to hear facial constitutional
challenges to acts of Congress affecting veterans' benefits, and will reverse
the Eleventh Circuit's contrary judgment and remand. A vacatur label is the
main alternative (the Court may prefer "vacated and remanded" so the Eleventh
Circuit can address sovereign-immunity/amendment questions first), but the
squarely rejected holding below makes outright reversal the modal form.

**Vote lineup: unanimous or near-unanimous for petitioner — I predict 9–0.**
Every recent jurisdiction-channeling case cutting this direction (*Axon v.
FTC*, 9–0) was lopsided; *Robison* itself was unanimous; the House Report
called *Robison* "clearly correct" while Congress preserved the operative
statutory language; and the merits amicus lineup spans the ideological
spectrum (NCLA, IJ, PLF, WLF alongside NVLSP, Military-Veterans Advocacy, the
veterans clinics, NTEU, and the Federal Circuit Bar Association — none
supporting respondent). If there is a defection it is most likely Justice
Thomas or Justice Kavanaugh crediting the government's *Elgin* channeling
theory, but I put each Justice's probability of joining the majority well
above one-half. Expect one or two separate concurrences (Gorsuch or Thomas
writing separately on implied-preclusion doctrine, as in *Axon*), with the
majority plausibly assigned to Kagan or Barrett. Authorship and writings are
unscored context.

**Which question the Court takes:** the QP as written — it is single, clean,
and was granted without reformulation. The Court will answer the
jurisdictional question only, leaving the Bill of Attainder and equal
protection merits, and any defendant-substitution/sovereign-immunity issues,
for remand.

**Ground and breadth** (the two structured semantic claims restate this): the
holding will rest on §511(a)'s text as read in *Robison* — a facial challenge
to a statute contests a decision of Congress, not a "decision of the
Secretary" — reinforced by *Thunder Basin*/*Axon* factors (no meaningful
VJRA path for facial challenges; constitutional questions outside VA
expertise). The rule will be categorical for facial challenges to
veterans'-benefits statutes, while leaving as-applied challenges to
individual benefits decisions channeled through the VJRA.

**Procedural exits are unlikely.** A DIG is improbable (~3–4%): the vehicle
concern (petitioner sued Congress pro se; sovereign immunity) was fully aired
in the BIO and the Court granted anyway, and the Eleventh Circuit's
alternative jurisdictional holding — that amendment to name the Secretary
would be futile — keeps the QP outcome-determinative. No recusal is apparent,
so an equally divided affirmance is a remote tail.

**Timing:** argued October 5, 2026 (the first Monday of OT2026). Given the
likely lopsided vote and a single statutory question, expect a decision in
the winter — most likely December 2026 through March 2027, well before the
end-of-Term crush.
