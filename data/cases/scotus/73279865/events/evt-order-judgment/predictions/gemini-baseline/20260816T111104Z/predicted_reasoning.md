The Court will affirm the judgment of the Eleventh Circuit, resolving the circuit split in favor of the minority position held by the Eighth and Eleventh Circuits. The majority will hold that the Veterans' Judicial Review Act (VJRA) implicitly stripped district courts of jurisdiction over facial constitutional challenges to acts of Congress affecting veterans' benefits.

Applying the frameworks from *Thunder Basin Coal Co. v. Reich* and *Elgin v. Department of Treasury*, the Court will conclude that Congress established a comprehensive statutory scheme of administrative review (via the VA) followed by judicial review in the Court of Appeals for Veterans Claims (CAVC) and exclusively in the Federal Circuit (under 38 U.S.C. §§ 511, 7292). Because this scheme provides a meaningful avenue for constitutional challenges, it precludes original jurisdiction in federal district courts.

The Court will take the question presented directly, distinguishing or limiting its 1974 decision in *Johnson v. Robison*, which predated the VJRA and the creation of the CAVC. The majority's holding will announce a categorical rule that these facial constitutional challenges must proceed through the agency and the Federal Circuit, rather than resting on narrow, case-specific facts. I expect a solid majority for this outcome (e.g., 8-1), as the channeling jurisprudence is generally strongly supported across the Court when a specialized Article I/Article III path exists. A summary disposition route is unlikely, given that merits briefing has already occurred and the case is set for oral argument.
