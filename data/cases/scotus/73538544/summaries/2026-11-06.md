## In brief

A mother whose daughter died in a truck crash asks the Court to revisit a ruling that federal trucking law barred her negligence claims against the farm and store involved.

## What happened

Christina Casarez's daughter died when a tractor-trailer, driven by an exhausted independent contractor hauling produce from Irigoyen Farms to a Walmart distribution center, ran a stop sign. Casarez sued Irigoyen Farms and Walmart for negligence, arguing each company's own conduct—Irigoyen Farms releasing a visibly fatigued driver, Walmart's delivery pressures—contributed to the crash. California trial and appellate courts ruled her claims were barred by a federal trucking law that preempts state rules affecting motor carrier prices, routes, or services, finding neither a "safety" exception nor a limit to carriers, brokers, and freight forwarders helped her. California's supreme court denied review. Casarez then asked the U.S. Supreme Court to grant review, pointing to a later Supreme Court decision, Montgomery, that she says undercuts the reasoning below.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked whether the California courts' reasoning about the preemption law's safety exception survives a more recent Supreme Court decision interpreting that exception, whether the preemption law can be invoked by companies like farms and retailers that are not carriers, brokers, or freight forwarders, and whether the case should be sent back for another look, summarily reversed, or left alone.

## Where it stands

The petition has been distributed for conference twice, most recently for November 13, 2026, after full briefing including responses from both Irigoyen Farms and Walmart and replies from Casarez; the docket shows no decision yet on whether the Court will act on the petition.

## What each outcome would mean

Casarez, who lost in the lower courts, is asking the Court to act. If the Court denies the petition, the California ruling that her claims are barred stands, ending her lawsuit. If the Court grants, vacates, and remands—an outcome Walmart and Casarez both support but Irigoyen Farms opposes—the California Court of Appeal would reconsider the case in light of the intervening Montgomery decision without the Supreme Court deciding who is right. If the Court summarily reverses, as Casarez urges, her claims would proceed despite the preemption defense. If the Court instead grants full review, it would hear arguments and decide the legal questions itself.
