## In brief

A mother whose daughter died in a truck crash asks the Court to send her lawsuit against the farm and retailer involved in shipping the cargo back for another look.

## What happened

Christina Casarez's daughter died when a tractor-trailer hauling produce ran a stop sign; the driver's fatigue was found to be a factor. Casarez sued Irigoyen Farms, the grower that loaded the truck, and Walmart, the delivery recipient, for negligence. California courts ruled her claims were preempted by a federal trucking law, finding neither defendant needed to be a named carrier or broker to claim preemption, and that a safety exception to that law did not apply because she had not alleged the truck itself was unsafe. California's Supreme Court denied review. Afterward, the U.S. Supreme Court decided a different case, Montgomery, addressing that same safety exception for freight brokers.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked three things: whether the lower court's ruling should be set aside in light of the Court's intervening Montgomery decision about when state injury claims survive federal trucking-law preemption; whether that federal law's preemption protection can be claimed by businesses, like shippers and receivers, that the law does not specifically name; and whether the right fix is to summarily reverse the lower court or simply vacate and send the case back without a full ruling.

## Where it stands

The petition has been fully briefed, including responses from both Irigoyen Farms and Walmart and replies from Casarez. It was distributed for a September 2026 conference; the Court then asked for a response, which Walmart filed, and the case was distributed again for a November 2026 conference. No decision on the petition has yet been recorded.

## What each outcome would mean

Casarez, who lost in the California courts, is asking the Court to act. If the Court denies the petition, the California ruling against her claims stands. If it grants the petition, the Court would hear the case and decide the preemption questions itself. The Court could also grant, vacate, and remand, sending the case back to California courts to reconsider in light of its Montgomery decision without deciding who is right; Walmart and, in part, Irigoyen Farms support this outcome. Summary reversal, which Casarez urges, would rule in her favor without full briefing or argument.
