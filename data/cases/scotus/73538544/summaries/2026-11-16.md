## In brief

The Court vacated a California ruling that a truck crash lawsuit against a shipper and a retailer was barred by federal trucking law, and sent the case back for reconsideration.

## What happened

Christina Casarez sued Irigoyen Farms, which shipped produce, and Walmart, the intended recipient, after a fatigued driver hauling their shipment ran a stop sign and killed her daughter. California courts ruled her negligence claims were preempted by a federal law governing motor carrier prices, routes, and services, because the claims did not allege the truck itself was unsafe. The California Court of Appeal affirmed, and the California Supreme Court denied review. While Casarez's petition to the U.S. Supreme Court was pending, the Court decided another case, Montgomery, addressing a related preemption question involving freight brokers.

## What the Court is being asked

The case asks whether the California courts' reasoning survives Montgomery, which held that lawsuits can proceed despite the federal preemption law if they concern motor vehicle safety, even without alleging a vehicle defect. Casarez argues Montgomery requires reversing the ruling against her. Irigoyen Farms and Walmart offer different views on how Montgomery applies to a shipper and a receiver, as opposed to a broker, and on whether non-carrier businesses like theirs can claim preemption's protection at all.

## Where it stands

The Court granted the petition, vacated the lower court's judgment, and sent the case back to the California Court of Appeal for reconsideration in light of Montgomery. The case had been distributed for conference twice, with the Court requesting a response from Walmart after the first distribution.

## What each outcome would mean

Casarez, who lost in the California courts, asked the Supreme Court to overturn that result. By vacating the judgment and remanding, the Court set aside the Court of Appeal's ruling without deciding who should ultimately win. The California Court of Appeal will now reconsider the preemption and safety-exception issues in light of Montgomery, potentially reaching the same result or a different one for Casarez, Irigoyen Farms, and Walmart.
