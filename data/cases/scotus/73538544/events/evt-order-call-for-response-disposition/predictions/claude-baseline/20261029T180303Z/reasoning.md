# Reasoning — P(grant, incl. GVR) = 0.55

**Inputs read.** Snapshot `record/snapshots/2026-09-11.json` (forward mode,
date cut at 2026-09-11, band `baseline` under sal-v4, distribution_count 1);
`questions-presented.txt`, `petition.txt` (skimmed), Irigoyen Farms' brief
(Aug 26, 2026, the first section of `brief-in-opposition.txt`) and the
petitioner's reply (Aug 31). No web or CourtListener retrieval.

**Anchor.** In the statpack's sal-v4 band table the `baseline` band's
bracketed `reached` rate, pooled over the Terms strictly before 2025
(2017–2024), is about 5% (≈4.5–5.9% per Term, n≈1,200–1,700 per Term). This
is the score yardstick, and it includes GVRs in the grant family.

**Why I move far off it.**
- The question is not a new cert-worthy split. It is a request to GVR in light
  of *Montgomery v. Caribe Transport II* (2026), decided after the judgment
  below. The decision below (Cal. Ct. App., Sept. 2025) refused the safety
  exception because the claims did not allege a defective truck, and it
  relied on *Aspen*. On the petitioner's account, which Irigoyen's brief does
  not really contradict on this point, *Montgomery* rejected both the
  "direct connection" gloss and *Aspen*. The intervening-decision GVR is the
  Court's lowest-cost grant route, and the band's base rate does not see it.
- The Court called for a response from Walmart after Irigoyen had already
  filed. That is the Court's own sign that a chambers is looking, and with a
  GVR request on the table the most natural reading is that it wants every
  respondent heard before vacating. The prompt notes that most petitions with
  a response called for are still denied. But that population is mostly
  plenary-review asks, not intervening-decision GVR asks.
- Irigoyen *supports* cert on QP 2, which is unusual. Even so, I think the
  Court is more likely to GVR than to set QP 2 for argument.

**Why not higher.** Irigoyen's distinctions have some force: it is a shipper
that did not select the carrier, *Montgomery* was a negligent-selection claim
against a broker, and the judgment might stand on (c)(1) preemption plus
state-law evidentiary issues. The Court sometimes denies GVR requests where the
intervening decision does not clearly touch the dispositive ground. California
state-court judgments get no special treatment. I also cannot see what Walmart
will say.

**Disclosure.** `brief-in-opposition.txt` combines Irigoyen's brief with
Walmart's response, docketed Oct 13, 2026, which is after my cutoff. I did
not read Walmart's response and it does not inform this forecast (see
`flags.json`).

**Other claims.** relist-increment 0.95: masked on this moment, and
redistribution after the response is near certain. cvsg-increment 0.03: purely
private parties. summary-disposition-route 0.85: given a grant, a GVR
dominates. dissent-from-denial 0.07: a low-profile private tort denial rarely
draws a writing. order-writers: small values, weighted toward the Justices who

**Where to discount me.** My view of *Montgomery*'s holding comes largely from
the parties' own descriptions in these filings. The 0.55 is a judgement call:
no committed table conditions on a called-for response or on a GVR posture.
