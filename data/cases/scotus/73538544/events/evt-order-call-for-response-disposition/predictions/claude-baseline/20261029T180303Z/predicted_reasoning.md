# Forecast — Casarez v. Irigoyen Farms, Inc., No. 25-1421

**State forecast from.** The petition has been distributed once (for the
conference of September 28, 2026) and has never been relisted. On September 10
the Court called for a response. Respondent Irigoyen Farms had already filed a
brief, so the call is aimed at Walmart Inc., which had waived (August 27). No
CVSG is on the docket.

**Disposition: a GVR in light of *Montgomery v. Caribe Transport II* is the
likeliest outcome (about 55% for any grant; most of that is GVR).** The
California Court of Appeal (September 2025, before *Montgomery*) held the
FAAAA safety exception inapplicable because the claims did not allege the
tractor-trailer itself was defective, and it rested on the Eleventh Circuit's
*Aspen* "direct connection" reasoning. *Montgomery* has since read "with
respect to motor vehicles" broadly and rejected *Aspen*. That is the textbook
GVR posture: an intervening decision of this Court that undercuts the ground
below, with a reasonable probability the state court would decide
differently. Calling for a response from the respondent that waived is
consistent with the Court weighing a summary disposition and wanting every
party heard first.

**Relisting.** The petition will be distributed again once Walmart's response
is in (near certain), and I expect it to be decided at the first conference
after that, or one more relist at most if a Justice is writing.

**CVSG.** No. This is a private tort suit; a CVSG is very unlikely.

**If granted, which question.** QP 3 (GVR / summary disposition), and through
it QP 1. Plenary review of QP 2 (whether non-enumerated shippers may invoke
§ 14501(c)(1)), which Irigoyen supports, is much less likely. The Court would
rather let the California court apply *Montgomery* first, and the shipper
question is better raised from a decision that has done so.

**Summary route.** Conditional on any grant, about 85% it is a GVR (summary
reversal is possible but unlikely). Plenary review of QP 2 accounts for the
rest.

**If denied.** That would most likely reflect a view that the judgment rests
on (c)(1) "related to" preemption, independently of the safety exception, or
on state-law vehicle problems (the evidentiary objections Irigoyen raises). I
expect no separate writing on a denial. A GVR could draw a short dissent
(Thomas, Alito or Gorsuch have objected to GVRs where they see no real change
in law), but that is not a writing on a denial.
