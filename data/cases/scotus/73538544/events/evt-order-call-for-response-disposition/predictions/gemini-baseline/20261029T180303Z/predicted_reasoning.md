## Disposition and Route if Granted

- **Primary forecast — Denial of certiorari (`disposition`: 0.32):** Although the September 10, 2026 call for a response reflects chambers interest in testing whether the California Court of Appeal's decision can stand after *Montgomery*, the Court will most likely **deny** the petition once Walmart's response is filed. In *Montgomery*, the Court held that a negligent-selection claim against a freight broker falls within the Federal Aviation Administration Authorization Act (FAAAA) safety exception, 49 U.S.C. § 14501(c)(2)(A), because selecting a motor carrier to operate a truck on the highway "concerns motor vehicles." Here, by contrast, respondents are a produce grower-shipper (Irigoyen Farms) and a retail purchaser-consignee (Walmart) separated from the driver by a multi-tier brokerage and carrier chain (Irigoyen Farms contracted with broker Royal Violet Logistics, which subcontracted with broker SIO Logistics, which hired motor carrier Gold Coast Logistics Group, which engaged driver Andre Hill). The Court will likely conclude that challenging a retail purchaser's delivery-window contracts and a shipper's loading-dock release of an independent sub-brokered carrier presents distinct threshold preemption and state-law duty questions that do not warrant disturbing the judgment below.
- **Conditional route if granted (`summary-disposition-route`: 0.90):** **If** the Court grants the petition, it is overwhelmingly likely (`0.90` conditional probability) to dispose of the case in the cert order itself via a **summary GVR in light of *Montgomery v. Caribe Transport II, LLC*** rather than setting the case for plenary merits briefing and oral argument or issuing a summary reversal. The California Court of Appeal decided this case on September 10, 2025 and the California Supreme Court denied review on January 14, 2026—four months before *Montgomery* was decided on May 14, 2026. In rejecting the § 14501(c)(2)(A) safety exception, the California Court of Appeal expressly rejected *Miller v. C.H. Robinson Worldwide, Inc.*, 976 F.3d 1016 (9th Cir. 2020), and *Cox v. Total Quality Logistics, Inc.*, 142 F.4th 847 (6th Cir. 2025), adopted the Eleventh Circuit's "direct connection" standard from *Aspen American Insurance Co. v. Landstar Ranger*, 65 F.4th 1261 (11th Cir. 2023), held that the safety exception did not apply because petitioner's claims "do not purport the tractor trailer itself was defective or otherwise unsafe," and declined to reach any alternative grounds for affirmance (App. 15–19 & n.7). Because *Montgomery* unanimously repudiated *Aspen*'s narrow approach and affirmed the *Miller*/*Cox* side of the split, any grant here would almost certainly take the form of a GVR on **Question Presented 1 and Question Presented 3** so the California Court of Appeal can reconsider the safety exception and any unaddressed state-law or preemption arguments in the first instance.
- **Questions Presented if granted for plenary review:** In the unlikely event (`0.10` conditional share of grants) that the Court sets the case for plenary review rather than issuing a GVR, it would reformulate or limit the grant to **Question Presented 2**—whether non-enumerated shippers and consignees may invoke 49 U.S.C. § 14501(c)(1) express preemption at all—leaving the fact-bound application of *Montgomery* behind.
