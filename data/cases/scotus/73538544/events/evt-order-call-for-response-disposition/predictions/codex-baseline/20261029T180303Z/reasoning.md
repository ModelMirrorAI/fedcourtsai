## Anchor and adjustment

The committed statpack's `sal-v4` table matches the frozen salience version. I pooled the bracketed `reached` baseline figures for **all eight displayed Terms strictly before the case's Term 2025**, namely 2017–2024, excluding 2025 and 2026. Calculating from `metrics/statpack.json` gives 607 grant-family outcomes over 11,599 weighted resolved risk-set observations, or **5.2332%**. This is the private petitioner's arrival-floor risk set, not the much smaller terminal-baseline grant rate. These are figures from the committed pack read on October 29, 2026, not a newly refreshed corpus-wide measurement; the pack does not expose the underlying pull/snapshot vintage in the consulted fields, so I make no claim that its corpus state is current.

The paid-segment terminal cuts provide context, not a forward hazard: zero relists has 1.8% grant-family outcomes among 10,203 resolved observations; one relist 13.7% among 2,538; two 41.2% among 495; and three-plus 37.4% among 484, using the rounded displayed shares. No CVSG has 6.4% grant-family outcomes among 13,556, against 34.8% among 164 with a CVSG. The baseline docket shows no amicus entries; the terminal zero-amicus bucket has 3.8% grant-family outcomes among 11,109, but a pending count can rise. None of those terminal cuts estimates the chance of a further distribution after a response request. There is no committed response-request-conditioned anchor.

The move from roughly 5.2% to **88% P(any grant)** is large and judgmental. It is not justified by the response request alone, and it is not fitted from a measured comparable-case cohort. It rests on these unusually specific filings:

1. **A concrete reconsideration ground.** The petition, particularly its first question and argument I, identifies the lower court's restrictive understanding of the safety exception and seeks remand in light of Montgomery. The lower-court opinion in the petition's appendix relies on the narrower line of circuit authority. The requested relief is procedural reconsideration, not an assumption that every element of negligence is established.
2. **The responding receiver supports exactly that remedy.** Walmart's October 13 brief, introduction and argument I, expressly requests GVR in light of Montgomery. It acknowledges that the intervening decision calls the lower court's reasoning into question while reserving its own defenses. A respondent willing to relinquish the existing judgment for reconsideration is substantially stronger evidence than a bare response request.
3. **The other respondent's position is not blanket opposition.** Irigoyen's August 26 brief, argument I, affirmatively requests plenary review limited to question 2 while defending the preemption ruling. Its argument II opposes the Montgomery-based summary remedies. Thus there is no unanimous respondent concession to GVR, but both respondents recognize a real federal issue or a reason to disturb the present procedural disposition.
4. **The strongest objections favor remand over outright reversal.** Irigoyen did not select the carrier or driver; Walmart's alleged scheduling pressure is more attenuated than a broker's negligent selection. State-law duty, causation, and evidentiary issues remain contested. Neither respondent identifies those defenses as an independent state-law holding actually reached by the appellate court. Those unresolved matters make it inappropriate to forecast a final liability victory, but leave room for reconsideration of the federal basis the court did reach.
5. **Plenary review is less compelling.** Walmart's argument II notes the lack of an identified split on the shipper/receiver question and the possibility that the safety exception or state-law defenses could make it unnecessary. A GVR addresses the intervening precedent without deciding that broader question. This supports the 0.97 conditional summary-route probability, not a prediction of a full argued case.

I retain 12% for nongrant outcomes, chiefly denial. Party support for a remedy is not binding, Montgomery involved a different supply-chain role, and the Court could conclude that reconsideration would not affect the judgment. Those are the best reasons not to assign near certainty. An independent, provisioned-input-only critique likewise favored GVR but emphasized those distinctions; the final 0.88 is my assessment rather than an empirical response-request success rate.
