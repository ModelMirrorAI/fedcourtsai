## Anchor and adjustment

The committed statpack's matching `sal-v4` elevated-band risk-set figures provide the required anchor. Pooling every displayed Term strictly before 2025, namely 2017–2024, gives 493 estimated grants over 2,819 weighted resolved petitions, or 17.49%. I computed this from the exact `prefix_est_grant_rate` and `prefix_weighted_resolved` fields in `metrics/statpack.json`, rather than averaging rounded percentages. I excluded the 2025 and 2026 rows. The much lower whole-docket rate is not the selected cohort's anchor.

For shape checks, the paid-segment terminal one-relist bucket has 2,538 resolved petitions and 13.7% granted-or-GVR; the no-CVSG bucket has 13,556 resolved and 6.4% granted-or-GVR; the zero-amicus bucket has 11,109 resolved and 3.8% granted-or-GVR. These are overlapping terminal populations, not independent likelihood ratios or estimates of the forward increment hazard. They do not outweigh direct evidence of an intervening-precedent remand.

The large move to P(any grant) = 0.88 is case-specific, principally the October Walmart response. Its introduction and argument expressly agree that a Montgomery GVR is warranted (combined opposition, lines 1086 and 1307 onward). Walmart explains that the California reasoning rested on precedents Montgomery called into question, while reserving the possibility that it still wins on remand. That concession is much stronger evidence than an uncontested assertion in a petition seeking error correction.

The appended appellate opinion independently supports the connection: its App-17 through App-19 discussion adopts a direct-connection restriction, and its conclusion excludes the safety exception because the claims do not allege that the truck itself was defective or unsafe. Its footnote 7 leaves other arguments undecided. Those are not adjudicated independent state grounds insulating the federal judgment from reconsideration.

Irigoyen does not join Walmart's requested disposition. It seeks plenary review of Question 2 while resisting Questions 1 and 3, argues that a shipper differs from the carrier-selecting broker in Montgomery, and preserves state-law and evidentiary defenses. I verified its conclusion and the argument at combined-opposition lines 834–881. Its fallback reference to remand is not an unqualified GVR concession. These disagreements account for much of the remaining 12% non-grant probability and strongly favor remand over immediate merits reversal. Walmart also identifies no pertinent authority split on Question 2, and that issue could become unnecessary if the safety exception applies.
