## 2. Summary Disposition Route vs. Plenary Review (`summary-disposition-route`: 0.99)

- **GVR in Light of *Montgomery v. Caribe Transport II, LLC*:** Conditional on granting the petition, the Court will almost certainly dispose of the case in the certiorari order itself via a **grant, vacate, and remand (GVR)** order in light of *Montgomery v. Caribe Transport II, LLC*, 608 U.S. 219 (2026), rather than setting the case for plenary merits briefing and oral argument (`P(summary-disposition-route | grant) = 0.99`).
- **Why the Court Will Enter a GVR Order:**
  1. The California Court of Appeal affirmed summary judgment for both Walmart and Irigoyen Farms on October 7, 2025, by expressly adopting the Seventh Circuit's holdings in *Ye v. GlobalTranz Enterprises, Inc.*, 74 F.4th 453 (7th Cir. 2023), and *Montgomery v. Caribe Transport II, LLC*, 124 F.4th 1053 (7th Cir. 2025), that the FAAAA's safety exception, 49 U.S.C. § 14501(c)(2)(A), applies only where a claim involves the direct ownership or operation of a motor vehicle.
  2. The California Supreme Court denied review on January 14, 2026—four months before this Court unanimously reversed the Seventh Circuit in *Montgomery* and abrogated *Ye* on May 14, 2026.
  3. Under *Lawrence v. Chater*, 516 U.S. 163, 167 (1996) (per curiam), vacatur and remand is the standard disposition so the California Court of Appeal can reconsider its unified preemption ruling—and address any preserved state-law pleading or alternative arguments—under *Montgomery* in the first instance.
- **Why Summary Reversal Is Rejected:** Summary reversal on the merits (Question 3) is unwarranted because the California courts decided this case before *Montgomery* was handed down and left unaddressed alternative state-law and record-specific arguments raised by both respondents.

## 3. Question Presented If Plenary Review Were Granted (Context-Only)

- In the very unlikely event (~1% unconditional probability) that the Court granted plenary review instead of issuing a GVR, it would grant **Question 2** ("Whether the FAAAA, 49 U.S.C. § 14501(c)(1), preempts a state common-law negligence claim against a shipper who is neither a motor carrier nor a broker") and decline Questions 1 and 3.
- Conditionally, if the Court were to decide Question 2 on the merits, it would likely hold that § 14501(c)(2)(A)'s safety exception as construed in *Montgomery* and *City of Columbus v. Ours Garage & Wrecker Service, Inc.*, 536 U.S. 424 (2002), saves state common-law negligence claims genuinely responsive to highway motor-vehicle safety (including negligent carrier selection and negligent cargo loading onto a highway semi-trailer), or vacate for consideration of the safety exception before reaching the threshold scope of § 14501(c)(1).
