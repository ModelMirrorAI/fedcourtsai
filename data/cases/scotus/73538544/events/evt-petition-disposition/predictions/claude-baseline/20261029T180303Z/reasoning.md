# Why P(grant, incl. GVR) = 0.80

**Inputs read:** snapshot `2026-10-29.json`; `context.json` (forward, band `elevated`
under sal-v4, distribution_count 2, no CVSG); `questions-presented.txt`, `petition.txt`
(skimmed), `brief-in-opposition.txt` (both Irigoyen's BIO and Walmart's 10/13 response,
combined), `cert-reply.txt`.

**Anchor.** Statpack "Segment base rate by salience band (sal-v4)", `elevated` column,
bracketed `reached` rate over Terms before OT2026 (the docket is OT2025): roughly
13–21% per Term, ~16% pooled. The relist-count cut's 2-distribution bucket (n=495
resolved) reads granted 28.3% + gvr 12.9% — the grant family is ~41% — but that counts a
terminal state, so I use it only as a cross-check.

**Adjustments, all upward, large:**
- The petition is at its heart a GVR request in light of an intervening decision of
  this Court (*Montgomery*, May 2026) that resolved the circuit split on the exact
  provision the decision below construed, and rejected the *Aspen*/*Ye* line the Court of
  Appeal and trial court relied on. Per Walmart's brief (respondent's own description),
  *Montgomery* cited *Aspen* and *Ye* as the losing side of the split.
- **A respondent agrees.** Walmart (Latham, Roman Martinez) argues a GVR is warranted
  under *Lawrence v. Chater*. A respondent conceding GVR is close to decisive in practice.
- **The Court asked for that response** after the 9/28 conference despite Walmart's
  waiver. A called-for response on a GVR-shaped petition is a strong signal that at
  least one Justice is looking at the GVR route.
- Irigoyen's opposition (shipper ≠ broker; no "direct nexus") argues a fact-application
  question that the GVR standard (reasonable probability the lower court would rethink)
  leaves to the state court. Irigoyen even supports cert on QP2.

**Why not higher.** (1) A GVR as to Walmart alone with denial as to Irigoyen, or some
mixed order, is possible; I count any GVR as a grant. (2) Independent state-law
no-duty grounds were argued below; the Court sometimes denies where an alternative
ground could sustain the judgment — though the Court of Appeal did not reach them,
which usually favours GVR. (3) The Court might see the shipper/receiver posture as far
enough from *Montgomery* to deny. (4) Lower state court (intermediate appellate,
review denied) — no bar to GVR. I put residual denial around 20%.

**Route given grant (0.90):** GVR overwhelmingly; plenary on QP2 is the remaining ~10%
(Irigoyen's support plus a clean statutory question, but no split and possibly academic).

**Relist-increment (0.20):** Decision expected at 11/13; a hold-over relist is the main
risk. **CVSG (0.02).** **Dissent from denial | denial (0.08):** a deny here would be
unusual and could draw a statement, but most denials draw none.

**Order-writers:** GVR orders rarely carry writings; small residual mass on Justices who
occasionally note dissents from GVRs or write on denials (Thomas, Alito, Gorsuch,
Sotomayor, Jackson).

**Limits.** I did not independently read *Montgomery*; its holding is taken from the
parties' briefs, which agree on its core (claim "concerns" motor vehicles → safety
exception; *Ye*/*Aspen* rejected). One corpus query (GVR priors) was run for shape; no
web/MCP retrieval.
