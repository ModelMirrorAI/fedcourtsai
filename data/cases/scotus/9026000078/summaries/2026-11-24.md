## In brief

A death-row inmate asks the Court to review whether Florida's non-unanimous jury sentencing rule can be applied to a crime committed before the rule existed.

## What happened

Michael H. Hunt was convicted of first-degree murder and other crimes in Florida for a 2019 home invasion that killed one victim and wounded three others. At sentencing, a jury voted 10-2 to recommend death under a 2023 state law allowing a death recommendation with only eight of twelve jurors agreeing, rather than the unanimous vote required by the law in effect when the crime occurred. The trial court imposed death, and the Florida Supreme Court affirmed, rejecting Hunt's arguments that the sentencing procedure violated the Sixth and Eighth Amendments and that applying the newer, non-unanimous rule retroactively violated the constitutional ban on retroactive criminal punishment changes.

## What the Court is being asked

The case raises two questions: first, whether the Constitution requires a unanimous jury vote before a death sentence can be recommended, rather than the eight-of-twelve vote Florida's statute allows; and second, whether applying that less-demanding voting rule to a crime committed years before the rule took effect amounts to an impermissible retroactive increase in punishment.

## Where it stands

The petition has been distributed twice, for conferences on December 4 and December 11, 2026, after full briefing including a brief in opposition and a reply; the docket does not yet show a ruling on the petition.

## What each outcome would mean

Hunt, who lost below, is asking the Court to reverse the Florida Supreme Court's decision upholding his death sentence. If the Court grants review, it would hear full arguments on whether the sentencing procedure and its retroactive application are constitutional, without yet deciding who wins. If it denies review, the Florida Supreme Court's decision affirming Hunt's conviction and death sentence stands, leaving the sentencing procedure and its application to his case in place.
