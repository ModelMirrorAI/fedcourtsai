## Case-Specific Adjustments from the `0.2481` Anchor (and `0.3966` Amicus Line)

Starting from `cert_anchor.rate = 0.2481` (and weighing `cert_amicus_line.rate = 0.3966`), I adjust downward to **`0.16`** for four primary structural and doctrinal reasons, partially offset by strong elite-counsel and tandem-coordination signals:

### 1. Why `0.2481` / `0.3966` Overstate Grant Odds Here (Downward Adjustments)

- **`distribution_count = 2` Is a Mechanical Pre-Conference Reschedule, Not a Conference Relist:** Inspection of `record/snapshots/2026-11-24.json` and `record/related/26-76.json` shows that *Hunt* has **never been to a Conference**. On November 17, 2026, both *Jackson* (No. 26-76) and *Hunt* (No. 26-78) were distributed for the December 4, 2026 Conference; that same day, Hunt filed his cert reply and both dockets were marked `Rescheduled`, then redistributed on November 24 for the **December 11, 2026 Conference**. Most petitions driving the `elevated` (`24.8%`) and `elevated + some-amicus` (`39.7%`) rates survived an actual Conference and earned a true post-conference relist from four or more Justices (or a chambers grant check). *Hunt* has not yet cleared first-conference screening. Also, its lone amicus brief is a VIDED historical brief on QP 1 shared with *Jackson* (which drew three amici), not a standalone amicus coalition on QP 2.
- **Controlling Precedent and No Active Split on QP 1 (Sixth & Eighth Amendment Selection Unanimity):** Just six years ago in *McKinney v. Arizona*, 589 U.S. 139, 144–45 (2020) (5–4, Kavanaugh, J., joined by Roberts, C.J., and Thomas, Alito, and Gorsuch, JJ.), the Court squarely held that while *Ring* and *Hurst* require a jury to find the eligibility-stage aggravating circumstance, *"in a capital sentencing proceeding just as in an ordinary sentencing proceeding, a jury (as opposed to a judge) is not constitutionally required to weigh the aggravating and mitigating circumstances or to make the ultimate sentencing decision within the relevant sentencing range."* Here, a 12-person jury unanimously convicted Hunt and **unanimously found five statutory aggravating factors beyond a reasonable doubt** (Fla. Stat. § 921.141(2)(b)), authorizing a statutory maximum of death before reaching the 8-4 selection recommendation (§ 921.141(2)(c)). Even Justice Gorsuch — petitioner's essential bridge vote on originalist Sixth Amendment claims (*Ramos*, *Erlinger*, and *Kian v. Florida*, No. 25-6623, on 6-person guilt juries) — joined *McKinney* because *Apprendi*/*Erlinger* attach to facts increasing the statutory maximum, not discretionary mercy selection within an authorized range. Moreover, there is **no live lower-court split**: *Hurst v. State* (Fla. 2016) was overruled by *State v. Poole*, 297 So. 3d 487 (Fla. 2020), and *Rauf v. State* (Del. 2016) pre-dated *McKinney* in a state that has since abolished capital punishment, while Alabama, Florida, the Eleventh Circuit, and judicial-selection states (Montana, Nebraska) all reject constitutional selection-unanimity claims. Finally, on QP 1 specifically, Hunt's jury voted **10-2** (whereas companion *Jackson*, No. 26-76, actually had an **8-4** vote).
- **Substantial *Dobbert* / *Collins* Hurdles and Narrow Split Framing on QP 2 (Ex Post Facto Clause):** QP 2 is *Hunt*'s distinct and stronger vehicle feature: Hunt committed the murder on April 4, 2019, during Florida's 2017–2023 statutory unanimity regime under which a 10-2 vote mandated life without parole, yet was sentenced to death under the April 2023 statute's 8-4 threshold. Even so, gathering four votes faces major obstacles:
  1. In *Dobbert v. Florida*, 432 U.S. 282, 292–97 (1977), the Court rejected an Ex Post Facto challenge to a retroactive change in Florida's capital jury sentencing statute **even though Dobbert's jury voted 10-2 for life** — a vote that legally mandated life imprisonment under the statute in force when Dobbert murdered his children, but was overridden for death under the new statute. And *Collins v. Youngblood*, 497 U.S. 37, 51–52 (1990), expressly overruled *Thompson v. Utah*, 170 U.S. 343 (1898), eliminating the foundation of petitioner's 1899 Louisiana citation (*State v. Ardoin*).
  2. Every post-*Collins* decision addressing retroactive changes to capital jury sentencing rules (*State v. Cohen*, 604 A.2d 846 (Del. 1992); *United States v. Joyner*, 201 F.3d 61 (2d Cir. 2000); and the Florida courts) has found no Ex Post Facto violation. Petitioner's only post-*Collins* authorities (*Barton v. SCDC*, 2013; *Clay v. Massachusetts Parole Bd.*, 2016) are decade-old **parole-board voting** cases under *Garner*/*Morales*, not jury sentencing cases.
  3. Chief Justice Roberts and Justices Thomas and Alito **dissented in *Peugh v. United States*, 569 U.S. 530, 551 (2013)**, arguing on originalist grounds that *Calder* Category 3 covers only retroactive increases in the statutory maximum penalty (which remained death in Florida throughout 2017–2023), while *Calder* Category 4 (*Carmell v. Texas*) textually governs evidentiary/sufficiency rules required *"in order to convict the offender."*
  4. Practically, QP 2 governs a closed, shrinking cohort of pre-April 2023 Florida murders tried after April 2023 that produce 8-4 to 11-1 votes in a single state, and the underlying offense facts (trafficking and sexually battering a 16-year-old witness, then orchestrating a disguised home-invasion murder Plot — *"no witness, no case"* — that killed an innocent young woman and shot three others) make *Hunt* an exceptionally unsympathetic vehicle for conservative Justices.
