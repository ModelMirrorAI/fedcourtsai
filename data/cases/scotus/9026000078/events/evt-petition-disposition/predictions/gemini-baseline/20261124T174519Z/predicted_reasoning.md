# Predicted Court Action: *Michael H. Hunt v. Florida* (No. 26-78)

## Procedural Trajectory from Current Docket State

As of the November 24, 2026 snapshot, the petition has been distributed **twice** (`distribution_count = 2`), reflecting an initial November 17, 2026 distribution for the December 4, 2026 Conference that was rescheduled the same day (when petitioner's reply brief was filed) and a second distribution on November 24, 2026 for the **December 11, 2026 Conference** alongside companion petition *Michael James Jackson v. Florida*, No. 26-76. Neither petition has yet been considered at a Conference.

- **Further Relists (`relist-increment` = 0.52):** Expect a narrow majority chance that the petition is distributed **at least once more** (`distribution_count >= 3`) after the December 11, 2026 Conference — most likely **one to three additional distributions** into January 2027. Because December 11 is the pair's first actual Conference, any plenary grant would require at least one post-conference vehicle-check relist, and even on the likelier denial path, the coordinated pairing of Nos. 26-76 and 26-78 and the strong prospect of a separate dissent or statement respecting denial (particularly from Justice Sotomayor or Justice Jackson) will frequently hold both dockets across multiple conferences while separate writings circulate.
- **Solicitor General Invitation (`cvsg-increment` = 0.005):** Expect **no CVSG**. This is a direct state capital appeal presenting pure Sixth Amendment, Eighth Amendment, and Ex Post Facto Clause questions with no federal statutory, regulatory, or programmatic interest, and the United States already addressed Founding-era Sixth Amendment jury doctrine earlier this Term in *Kian v. Florida*, No. 25-6623.

## Disposition and Scope of Review

- **Primary Disposition (`denied`, P(any grant) = 0.16):** Certiorari will most likely be **denied** in tandem with (or alongside) *Jackson v. Florida*, No. 26-76.
- **Question Presented if Granted:** If the Court grants plenary review in *Hunt*, the likeliest scope is **Question Presented 2 alone** (limiting the grant to the Ex Post Facto Clause challenge to retroactive application of Florida's 2023 nonunanimous sentencing statute to crimes committed during Florida's 2017–2023 statutory unanimity window, where Hunt's 10-2 jury vote turned a mandatory life sentence into death), or **both Questions Presented 1 and 2 in tandem** with *Jackson v. Florida*, No. 26-76 (where the jury voted 8-4). If granted on QP 2, the merits inquiry would center on whether repealing a binding jury-unanimity sentencing rule alters the sufficiency/quantum of persuasion under *Carmell v. Texas*, 529 U.S. 513 (2000), or creates a "significant risk" of increased punishment under *Peugh v. United States*, 569 U.S. 530 (2013), versus constituting a procedural change governed by *Dobbert v. Florida*, 432 U.S. 282 (1977), and *Collins v. Youngblood*, 497 U.S. 37 (1990). If QP 1 were reached, a majority would likely adhere to *Spaziano v. Florida*, 468 U.S. 447 (1984), and *McKinney v. Arizona*, 589 U.S. 139 (2020), because Hunt's twelve-person jury unanimously found five statutory aggravating factors beyond a reasonable doubt.
- **Summary Disposition vs. Plenary Review (`summary-disposition-route` = 0.09 conditional on grant):** Conditional on a grant, **plenary briefing and oral argument** is overwhelmingly likelier than summary disposition in the cert order. A summary per curiam reversal invalidating a state capital sentencing statute (or its retroactivity under *Dobbert*) without argument is virtually out of the question; the small (~9% conditional) summary-disposition slice comes almost entirely from the possibility that the Court grants *Jackson* (No. 26-76) alone on QP 1, holds *Hunt*, and later GVRs *Hunt* if *Jackson* prevails.

## Separate Writings at the Certiorari Stage

- **Dissent or Statement Respecting Denial (`dissent-from-denial` = 0.30 conditional on denial):** Conditional on denial, there is roughly a three-in-ten chance that at least one Justice notes a dissent or authors a dissent/statement respecting the denial of certiorari (potentially captioned across both Nos. 26-76 and 26-78, or directed specifically to *Hunt*'s Ex Post Facto posture). **Justice Sotomayor** (who authored both *Hurst v. Florida*, 577 U.S. 92 (2016), and *Peugh v. United States*, 569 U.S. 530 (2013), and has repeatedly written on Florida and Alabama nonunanimous capital sentencing) is by far the likeliest author, followed by **Justice Jackson** (either writing separately or joining Justice Sotomayor, alongside Justice Kagan), with a much smaller chance of a separate writing from **Justice Gorsuch** on common-law jury history or a brief concurrence in the denial by **Justice Thomas** or **Justice Alito** if a dissent circulates.
