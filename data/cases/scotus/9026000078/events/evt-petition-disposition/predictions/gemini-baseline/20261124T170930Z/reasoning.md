## 2. Case-Specific Adjustments

### Upward Factors (+5 to +6 percentage points from structural ~8.5%)
- **Elite repeat-player Supreme Court representation and coordinated vehicle design:** Counsel of record Jeffrey L. Fisher, with Easha Anand and Brian H. Fletcher (Stanford Law School Supreme Court Litigation Clinic), argued *Hurst v. Florida*, 577 U.S. 92 (2016), and *Ramos v. Louisiana*, 590 U.S. 83 (2020). Stanford deliberately paired *Jackson* (No. 26-76, whose 2005 murders predated Florida's 2017 unanimity statute, making *Jackson* the lead brief on Question Presented 1) and *Hunt* (No. 26-78, whose April 2019 murder occurred squarely during Florida's 2017–2023 statutory-unanimity window, making *Hunt* the clean lead brief on Question Presented 2).
- **Clean preservation and stark outcome-determinative Ex Post Facto posture on QP 2:** At the time of Hunt's April 2019 offense, Fla. Stat. § 921.141(2)(c) (2017) mandated life without parole unless all 12 jurors voted for death. Tried seven months after Florida enacted SB 450 (`ch. 2023-23`, effective April 20, 2023), Hunt received a 10–2 death recommendation and was sentenced to death. The federal Ex Post Facto claim was preserved before trial and squarely decided on federal grounds by the Florida Supreme Court (`App. 22a–27a`), and it governs roughly a dozen existing death sentences plus dozens of pending Florida prosecutions from the 2017–2023 window.
- **Plausible path to four votes if one conservative Justice joins the three liberals:** Justices Sotomayor, Kagan, and Jackson are sympathetic on both Eighth/Sixth Amendment capital safeguards and *Peugh* Ex Post Facto scrutiny; on June 15, 2026, the Court also granted certiorari in *Kian v. Florida*, No. 25-6623 (revisiting six-person noncapital felony juries under *Williams v. Florida*). Only one conservative vote (most plausibly Justice Gorsuch on *Calder* Category 4 / *Carmell* or historical jury-trial grounds) would be needed to grant.

### Downward Factors (-4 to -5 percentage points, landing at `0.10`)
- **Recent 6–3 precedent squarely forecloses QP 1, and *Hunt* is secondary to *Jackson* on QP 1:** In *McKinney v. Arizona*, 589 U.S. 139, 144–45 (2020) (Kavanaugh, J., joined by Roberts, C.J., and Thomas, Alito, and Gorsuch, JJ.) and *Kansas v. Carr*, 577 U.S. 108, 119 (2016), the Court held that while *Ring* and *Hurst* require a jury to find an aggravating circumstance making a defendant death-eligible—which Hunt's jury found unanimously (12–0) on four aggravators—the weighing and ultimate death-recommendation phase is "mostly a question of mercy" that need not be made by a jury at all (*Spaziano v. Florida*, 468 U.S. 447 (1984)). No active death-penalty state court holds otherwise. Moreover, *Hunt* devotes less than two pages to QP 1 (`Pet. 7–8`), incorporating *Jackson* by reference.
- ***Dobbert* and *Collins* insulate the judgment below on QP 2, and *Peugh* lacks conservative appetite to expand:** In *Collins v. Youngblood*, 497 U.S. 37, 51–52 (1990), the Court overruled *Thompson v. Utah*, 170 U.S. 343 (1898), holding that retroactively reducing a capital jury from 12 to 8 jurors does not implicate "the definition of crimes, defenses, or punishments, which is the concern of the Ex Post Facto Clause." And in *Dobbert v. Florida*, 432 U.S. 282, 293–94 & n.6 (1977), the Court treated retroactive changes to Florida's capital jury/judge sentencing allocation as procedural. Meanwhile, *Peugh v. United States*, 569 U.S. 530 (2013), was a 5–4 Guidelines decision in which Chief Justice Roberts and Justices Thomas and Alito dissented, and petitioner's *Carmell*/*Calder* Category 4 authorities either predate *Collins* (`Bowles`, `McIntosh`, `Holloway`) or involved stripping jury sentencing altogether (`State v. Barton`, 745 S.E.2d 110 (S.C. 2013)). Thus, there is no clean post-*Collins* split on capital-jury vote thresholds, and the retroactive issue is confined to a closed six-year statutory window in a single state.
- **Modest amicus support and aggravated facts:** Despite Stanford Clinic's involvement, the companion petitions drew only a single joint amicus brief from two Florida defense organizations (unlike *Kian v. Florida*, No. 25-6623, which drew broad national amicus support). The underlying offense—a planned home-invasion witness-elimination shooting using a pizza-delivery ruse in which an innocent 19-year-old was shot in the face and her stepfather wounded—further discourages discretionary error-correction or doctrinal expansion.

## 3. Auxiliary Claims, Authorship Distribution, and Stakes
- **`relist-increment` (`0.38`):** Because December 11, 2026 is *Hunt*'s first actual conference, virtually all grant paths (~0.095 unconditional), nearly all published dissent-from-denial paths (~0.18 unconditional), and roughly 10–11% of quiet conference-scrutiny / winter-recess relist paths that still end in an unannotated denial require at least one further distribution (`distribution_count >= 3`). Summing those disjoint paths yields `0.38`.
- **`cvsg-increment` (`0.005`):** Pure state capital constitutional appeal with zero federal statutory or executive-branch nexus (`statpack.md` overall paid CVSG rate is `1.46%`, `109 / 7,482`, concentrated almost entirely in federal-interest civil/statutory disputes).
- **`summary-disposition-route` (`0.12` conditional on grant):** *Hunt* is the lead vehicle on QP 2, so ~88% of its `0.10` grant probability is plenary review (either standalone on QP 2 or consolidated with *Jackson*). The remaining ~12% conditional share (`~0.012` unconditional) covers a hold-and-GVR behind *Jackson* (No. 26-76) if *Jackson* were granted alone on QP 1 and reversed, a remote GVR in light of *Kian* (No. 25-6623), or a summary reversal.
- **`dissent-from-denial` (`0.22` conditional on denial) & `order-writers`:** As the first two direct-appeal challenges to reach the Court under Florida's 2023 8–4 nonunanimous death-penalty law—coupled with the Florida Supreme Court's cabining of Justice Sotomayor's opinion in *Peugh* to affirm a death sentence that would have been life without parole under the statute in force at the time of the 2019 crime—there is a 22% chance conditional on denial (`~19.8%` unconditional) of a noted dissent or separate statement. **Justice Sotomayor** (`0.13`) is by far the likeliest author, followed by **Justice Jackson** (`0.045`), **Justice Gorsuch** (`0.012`), and small tail probabilities for **Thomas** (`0.008`), **Alito** (`0.006`), **Kagan** (`0.005`), **Kavanaugh** (`0.003`), **Barrett** (`0.002`), and **Roberts** (`0.001`).
- **`big_case_score` (`0.68`):** Decoupled from grant odds, a merits decision would determine the fate of dozens of Florida capital prosecutions from the 2017–2023 window, define the Ex Post Facto reach of *Peugh* versus *Collins*/*Dobbert*, and—if QP 1 were included—potentially require unanimous capital sentencing juries in Florida and Alabama.
