# Forecast: Hunt v. Florida, No. 26-78 (capital case)

**State forecast from.** The docket shows two distribution entries: distributed for the 12/4/2026 conference, rescheduled the same day before any conference sat, then distributed for 12/11/2026. No conference has yet considered the petition. No CVSG. The companion petition, Jackson v. Florida (No. 26-76), has been rescheduled twice and travels with this one.

**Disposition.** The Court will most likely deny certiorari (about 83%). Hunt's fate is tied to Jackson: the Court will act on the two together. A grant here happens essentially only if the Court takes the Sixth/Eighth Amendment unanimity question in Jackson; in that world Hunt is either granted alongside it (to add the ex post facto question) or held and later disposed of in light of Jackson.

**Further distributions.** More likely than not (about 55%) the petition is distributed at least once more after the 12/11 conference. The repeated rescheduling suggests at least one chambers is doing something with the pair, and the Court's current practice is to relist before any grant; a Justice writing respecting denial would also produce relists. I expect one to three further distributions if it is not denied outright on the December 14 order list.

**CVSG.** None. This is a state capital prosecution with no federal interest.

**Question presented, if granted.** The Court's interest would be in QP 1 (unanimity under the Sixth/Eighth Amendments), presented principally through Jackson. If Hunt itself is granted for plenary review it would be for QP 2 (Ex Post Facto), probably limited to that question and consolidated or argued in tandem with Jackson. A grant of Hunt's ex post facto question alone, without Jackson, is very unlikely.

**Summary route.** Conditional on a grant, plenary review is the likelier route (about 75%); the cert-order route (about 25%) is a hold for Jackson followed by a GVR. A summary reversal is not realistic.

**Separate writing.** If the Court denies, there is about a one-in-four chance that a Justice writes or notes a dissent on this order; Justice Sotomayor is the likeliest author (possibly joined by Justice Jackson), with Justice Gorsuch an outside possibility given his jury-right writings. Any such writing is somewhat more likely to be attached to Jackson than to Hunt, though a single writing could cover both.
