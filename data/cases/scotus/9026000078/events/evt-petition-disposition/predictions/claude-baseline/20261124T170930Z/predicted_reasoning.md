# Forecast — Hunt v. Florida, No. 26-78

**The Court will deny certiorari.** Probability of any grant: 0.18.

**Distributions.** The docket shows two distributions (12/4, rescheduled; then 12/11) and no conference has yet considered the petition. The petition will be distributed at least once more (0.58): the Court will not dispose of it on the first order list after the December 11 conference. It will travel with Jackson v. Florida, No. 26-76, and the two will be resolved on the same order list. Expect one to three further distributions, with final action in January–February 2027, unless the Court holds both for Kian v. Florida, No. 25-6623, in which case action comes after Kian is decided, by the end of June 2027.

**CVSG.** None. The case is a state capital prosecution with no federal interest.

**Question presented, if the Court takes one.** A grant here would be limited to Question 2, the Ex Post Facto question, as written, with the Sixth and Eighth Amendment unanimity question taken, if at all, in Jackson. If granted, the likely ground for petitioner is that lowering the vote needed for a death determination from 12-0 to 8-4 creates a significant risk of increased punishment under Peugh and Carmell, and that Collins and Dobbert do not exempt jury-procedure changes from that test.

**Summary disposition.** Unlikely. If the Court grants, it will set the case for briefing and argument (0.88 conditional on a grant). The only cert-order route is a GVR in light of Kian, and Kian's question about jury size does not control either question here.

**Separate writing.** On a denial, there is a 0.40 chance that a Justice notes a dissent or files a statement respecting the denial. The most likely author is Justice Sotomayor, with Justice Jackson next; Justice Gorsuch is the most likely writer from the rest of the bench. Any writing may be captioned in Jackson and cover both cases.
