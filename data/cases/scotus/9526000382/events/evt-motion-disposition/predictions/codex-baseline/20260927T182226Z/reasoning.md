## Reasons favoring relief

The application's strongest distinction is between adjudicating civil property rights and compelling changes to religious activity. According to its account of the April 24 order, the injunction prohibits further proceedings in another tribunal and requires steps to secure withdrawal of a rabbinical censure. Applicants expressly do not ask to halt the civil ownership case, compel Bain to arbitrate, or give a religious award civil force. That makes the proposed stay narrower than a demand to displace the secular court entirely. See application printed pages 6–9, 18–24, and 26.

The compelled-retraction component is especially substantial: if the order operates as described, it requires affirmative efforts directed at a religious judgment with which the applicant agrees. The application's free-exercise, compelled-speech, and religious-autonomy arguments therefore warrant more weight than an ordinary private arbitration dispute. These are advocacy-based assessments; I do not simply adopt the application's characterization of each point as undisputed or its categorical assertion that every individualized order escapes neutral-law treatment. See printed pages 19–23.

The application also gives a concrete account of attempted state relief: trial-court refusal, individual appellate Justice's denial, a fully briefed appellate stay motion left undecided for months, and a letter requesting action. Those allegations make the claim that continued delay causes ongoing injury more credible and reduce, without eliminating, the procedural objection. See printed pages 2–5 and 9–11.

## Reasons denial remains more likely

Interim relief is not awarded merely because a constitutional claim is substantial. Hollingsworth v. Perry, 558 U.S. 183, 190 (2010), retrieved through CourtListener opinion 9413203, states the stay-pending-cert framework in terms of likely certiorari consideration, a fair prospect of reversal, irreparable harm, and balancing the parties' harms in close cases. It is general context, not a holding about this application's separate All Writs Act theory.

Here the underlying litigation and the appellate stay motion remain pending on the applicants' own presentation. The jurisdictional argument depends on treating inaction as sufficiently final or intervening in aid of future jurisdiction. The application addresses that difficulty rather than eliminating it. It invokes Skokie and distinguishes Yeshiva University on printed pages 4 and 11; I treat those as arguments made in the provisioned filing, not as independently verified analogues. The requested intervention would reach an ongoing state commercial case without an appellate panel's resolution of the immediate dispute.

The elapsed months also cut both ways. Delay may prolong a constitutional injury, but the filing identifies no fixed imminent execution, auction, contempt hearing, or comparable irreversible deadline. The threatened permanent injunction and future sanctions are described as risks, not completed acts. That leaves room for a short denial or denial without prejudice that permits further state action. See application printed pages 11–12 and 24–26.

The absent respondent's account matters. Bain was not a party to the original religious arbitration according to the application, and he asserts interests in the same property. A competing account could frame the censure and second religious proceeding as pressure on a nonconsenting litigant or interference with an existing injunction, rather than solely independent religious expression. The application itself acknowledges claimed reputational and communal harm at printed page 25. I neither assume those interests defeat the First Amendment arguments nor accept the claim that they impose no countervailing harm.

Finally, the two challenged provisions are separable. The Court could be more concerned about compelled withdrawal of the censure than about restrictions on parallel adjudication. Because an expressly mixed grant-and-denial order resolves as denied under this event's contract, meaningful partial protection can coexist with failure of the binary grant claim. My 0.38 prices an unqualified disposing grant, not any interim accommodation, temporary administrative stay, or eventual constitutional victory.

The 0.82 response-request probability reflects a serious, specifically supported constitutional complaint for which the other party's account would be useful before intervention. The 0.72 referral probability reflects the institutional significance of overriding a state injunction and the possibility that several Justices would want to consider its religious-liberty implications. The 0.65 amicus-increment probability reflects the cross-community implications of the questions and capacity for organized religious-liberty advocacy; it is not an assertion that the snapshot already records any brief. These are subjective forward probabilities, not terminal-signal frequencies taken from the pack.

The **0.56 stakes score** measures potential significance rather than success odds. Limits on compelled religious speech and civil interference with religious tribunals could extend beyond the named parties and faith community, but this application seeks limited interim protection within a private commercial dispute, not a nationwide change in enforcement or public policy. I omit individual votes and a merits judgment because neither is necessary to make the interim forecast honest and complete.
