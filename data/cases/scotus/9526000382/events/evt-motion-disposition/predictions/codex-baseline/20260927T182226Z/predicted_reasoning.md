# Forecast of the Court's action

The Court will most likely deny the stay application, potentially without prejudice to a renewed request following further state appellate action. The probability of an unqualified grant of the requested interim relief is **0.38**. This predicts the disposition of the application, not the constitutional merits or eventual ownership of the nursing home. An order expressly granting in part and denying in part belongs on the ungranted side of this forecast under the interim resolver's convention.

The frozen arrival record contains the September 17, 2026 application submission to Justice Sotomayor, no response request, no full-Court referral, and zero amicus entries. From that state:

- A response will probably be requested: **0.82** probability of a new request.
- The application will probably be referred to the full Court: **0.72** probability of a new referral. Referral is distinct from its initial submission to the Circuit Justice.
- At least one qualifying amicus entry or distinct submitted amicus brief will probably arrive: **0.65** probability of an increase above zero. The central count forecast is one to three additional briefs or qualifying entries, counting a submitted-and-accepted brief only once.
- A short order, rather than plenary briefing and argument, will dispose of the application. The central timing forecast is approximately two to four weeks after the September 17 submission, around October 1–15, 2026. This is an unscored timing estimate, not a claim that any intervening docket activity has been observed.

The likely obstacle to immediate relief is the still-pending state appellate process, not an endorsement of compelled religious speech. The Court will probably allow that process another opportunity to address the injunction rather than use this emergency application to settle the constitutional limits of religious arbitration. The application nevertheless presents sufficiently concrete religious-liberty concerns to draw a response and collective consideration.

If relief is granted, its likely rationale is preservation of voluntary religious participation and protection against compelled efforts to reverse a religious tribunal's censure, while leaving the civil ownership litigation free to proceed. A narrower order protecting only the censure-related provision is a meaningful alternative; an expressly mixed grant-and-denial order would not satisfy the unqualified-grant claim. No merits judgment, majority author, or individual Justice's vote is forecast.
