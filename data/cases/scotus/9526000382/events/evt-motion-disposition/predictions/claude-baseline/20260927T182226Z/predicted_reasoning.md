# Forecast: what the Court will do with application 26A382

**Disposition.** The application for a stay will be **denied** (P(unqualified grant) = 0.17). The most likely form is a one-line order from the full Court denying the application, with a fair chance of a separate statement or dissent from Justice Alito, joined by Justices Thomas and Gorsuch, on the compelled-withdrawal provision of the order below. The second most likely non-grant shape is a partial order staying only the provision that compels Strulovitch to seek withdrawal of the seruv while leaving the bar on furthering the beis din proceeding in place; under the resolver's denial-first rule that resolves as ungranted, and it is a real share of my complement. A grant of the whole stay is possible but requires five Justices to reach past the finality and exhaustion obstacles that stopped the Court in Yeshiva University v. YU Pride Alliance (2022).

**Response requested.** From the state my snapshot froze (no response called for), a response **will be** requested. In forward mode I can see that this has already happened: Justice Sotomayor called for a response on September 23, due September 28. I state the increment as near-certain rather than pretending not to know.

**Referral.** The application will most likely be **referred to the full Court** (P = 0.75) after the response arrives. A Circuit Justice who has called for a response on a contested First Amendment application from experienced Supreme Court counsel, with a Becket amicus on file, rarely disposes of it alone; denying in chambers would only invite a renewed application to another Justice. The chance it is not referred rests on Justice Sotomayor denying in chambers, or the application being withdrawn or dismissed as moot if the Appellate Division finally rules on the pending state stay motion.

**Amicus briefs.** The count will rise past the zero my record shows. In forward mode this is already visible: the Becket Fund for Religious Liberty submitted an amicus brief on September 25. I expect one or two more from religious-liberty organizations or Orthodox communal bodies before disposition, but the resolvable claim is only that the count rises, which it has.

**Timing and form.** The response is due September 28. Expect a reply within days, then disposition by the full Court in roughly the first two weeks of October 2026, most likely on or before the start of the October sitting's order lists. A possible alternative path is that the Court holds the application while the Appellate Division, prodded by the response request, rules on the long-pending state stay motion, after which the application is denied as moot or withdrawn.

**Conditional merits view.** If the Court does grant relief, the likelier ground is the compelled-speech and church-autonomy problem with the order's affirmative command to lobby the beis din to retract its seruv, not the bar on litigating before the tribunal; a grant would most plausibly be framed as a stay pending the state appeal and any timely petition, with dissents from the Justices who joined the Yeshiva University majority.
