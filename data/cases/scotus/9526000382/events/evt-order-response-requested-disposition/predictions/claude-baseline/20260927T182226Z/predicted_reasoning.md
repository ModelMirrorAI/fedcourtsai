# Forecast: what the Court will do with application 26A382

**Disposition.** The application for a stay will most likely be **denied**
(P(unqualified grant) = 0.22). The likeliest form is a denial by the full Court
after referral, worded without prejudice to renewal once the Appellate Division
acts, in the shape of the Court's 2022 Yeshiva University order, and carrying a
dissent or statement from Justice Alito joined by some of Justices Thomas,
Gorsuch, and Barrett. A partial stay confined to the order's second directive
(the command that Strulovitch procure withdrawal of the seruv) is the most
plausible grant-shaped outcome, and it resolves as ungranted under the interim
vocabulary's denial-first rule. An unqualified stay of both provisions pending
the state appeal and a timely certiorari petition, on the Malliotakis model, is
the minority path.

**Response requested.** Already on the record: Justice Sotomayor called for a
response on September 23, due September 28. No further call is expected
(P = 0.03 for another request, for instance for supplemental briefing).

**Referral to the Court.** Very likely (P = 0.80). Once a response is in, the
Circuit Justice will refer the application rather than act alone: the applicant
has elite counsel, at least one institutional amicus, and a claim four Justices
have historically found compelling, so a solo denial would simply be renewed to
another Justice. The main route to no referral is withdrawal or mootness if the
Second Department decides the pending stay motion first.

**Amicus briefs.** The record shows none; the count will rise (P = 0.96).
Religious-liberty organizations file quickly on applications of this kind, and
the five-day response window is enough time for them.

**Timing.** Response due September 28; expect a reply within a day or two,
referral at once, and a disposition in the first half of October, before or
around the Court's first sittings of the Term. If the Court denies on
exhaustion grounds, the order will be short and will point applicants back to
the Appellate Division.

**If the Court grants (conditional).** The likely ground is that the order
compels Strulovitch to advocate before his rabbis against a religious ruling he
agrees with, a compelled-speech and church-autonomy problem no neutral-principles
rationale reaches, with jurisdiction rested on the All Writs Act and the state
courts' failure to act on a fully briefed stay motion for four months. The stay
would run pending disposition of the appeal in the New York courts and a timely
petition for certiorari, on the Malliotakis template.
