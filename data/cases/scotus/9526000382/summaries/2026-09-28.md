## What happened

Samuel Strulovitch and Moshe Bain, both Orthodox Jewish businessmen, disagree over ownership of a nursing home. Strulovitch brought the dispute to a beis din, a rabbinical court, which he believes his faith requires. Bain instead sued in New York state court to challenge that process. A trial court barred Strulovitch from pursuing any religious proceeding and ordered him to get the beis din to withdraw a "seruv," a religious censure it had issued against Bain. Strulovitch asked the trial court and then New York's Appellate Division for a pause while he appealed; both declined or did not act, and the appellate stay motion has sat undecided for months. Strulovitch, joined by two of his companies, then asked the Supreme Court directly for emergency relief.

## What the Court is being asked

The application asks a single justice, acting as circuit justice for this region, to pause the state court's order while the appeal moves forward. Underlying that request are two questions the applicants want ultimately addressed: whether a civil court can stop someone from taking a private dispute to a religious tribunal of his own faith, and whether a civil court can force him to ask his own religious leaders to reverse a religious ruling he agrees with. The applicants argue this violates religious-liberty and speech protections; Bain has not yet filed a response addressing these arguments.

## Where it stands

Justice Sotomayor, who handles emergency matters from this circuit, asked Bain to respond by September 28, 2026. An outside religious-liberty organization has since filed a brief supporting Strulovitch. The docket shows no ruling yet on the stay request.
