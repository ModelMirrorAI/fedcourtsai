# Forecast

The Court will reverse the Ninth Circuit and hold, by a 6-3 vote, that Watanabe may not pursue a Bivens damages action. Chief Justice Roberts and Justices Thomas, Alito, Gorsuch, Kavanaugh, and Barrett will join the judgment-disturbing majority; Justices Sotomayor, Kagan, and Jackson will dissent. A vacatur for further analysis is possible, but reversal is more likely because the petition reports that a new-context holding is case-dispositive under the positions taken below.

The majority will answer the granted question directly. It will treat the Bureau of Prisons' Administrative Remedy Program as an alternative remedial structure that Carlson did not consider and conclude that, under Ziglar and Egbert, this difference supplies both a new context and a reason for the judiciary not to create a damages remedy. The Court will reinforce that conclusion with the differences between Carlson's acute, fatal asthma episode and Watanabe's nonfatal injury and delayed treatment, but the alternative remedial structure will be the principal doctrinal ground.

The ruling will be broad within federal-prison medical-care litigation. It will make the availability of the Administrative Remedy Program generally sufficient to foreclose a Bivens action, while declining formally to overrule Carlson and confining that precedent to materially identical facts. The majority will therefore reject respondent's position that the Westfall Act and the Prison Litigation Reform Act preserve Carlson claims throughout the deliberate-indifference category.

The dissent will argue that this is Carlson's recognized context: the same constitutional right, kind of federal prisoner, class of federal medical official, and deliberate indifference to serious medical needs. It will also rely on statutory stare decisis and Congress's express preservation of constitutional claims in the Westfall Act. At least one separate majority-side writing is plausible on whether Carlson should be overruled outright, but the controlling judgment will stop short of doing so. No recusal or equally divided disposition is expected, and dismissal as improvidently granted is unlikely.
