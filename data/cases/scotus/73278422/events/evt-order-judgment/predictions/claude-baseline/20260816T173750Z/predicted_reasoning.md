# Predicted reasoning — Nielsen v. Watanabe, No. 25-417 (merits, moment: grant)

**Judgment class: `reversed`.** The Court will reverse the Ninth Circuit's
judgment recognizing a Bivens cause of action for Watanabe's Eighth Amendment
medical-indifference claim. Outright reversal rather than vacatur-and-remand:
Watanabe conceded below that his claim cannot survive Bivens step two if it
presents a new context (Pet. 32, citing App. 96a n.1), so a holding that the
context is new is case-dispositive and leaves nothing to remand on the Bivens
claim. A DIG is unlikely — the case was resolved on the pleadings, both
questions were preserved and passed on below, and the en banc dissents aired
both sides; nothing in the record suggests a vehicle defect that would emerge
in merits briefing. No apparent recusal, so an equally divided affirmance is
a remote possibility only.

**Vote lineup: 6–3.** Roberts, Thomas, Alito, Gorsuch, Kavanaugh, and Barrett
in the majority; Sotomayor, Kagan, and Jackson in dissent. This mirrors Egbert
v. Boule's alignment on essentially the same doctrinal question. A broader
majority (8–1 or 9–0) is possible if the opinion is written narrowly — Goldey
v. Fields (2025) drew no recorded dissent — but the grant of plenary review
rather than another summary reversal suggests the Court intends to say
something the dissenters will not join, most likely a categorical rule about
alternative remedies at step one.

**Ground.** The majority will hold that Watanabe's claim arises in a new
Bivens context because it differs meaningfully from Carlson v. Green: the BOP
Administrative Remedy Program and the PLRA are alternative remedial
structures the Carlson Court did not consider, and Ziglar v. Abbasi lists
exactly that — "potential special factors that previous Bivens cases did not
consider" — as what makes a context new. Given the new context, the same
alternative remedies give Congress-is-better-suited reasons that foreclose
the action under Egbert's "any rational reason" formulation. The majority
will reject the Ninth Circuit's rule (reiterated in Schwartz v. Miller) that
alternative remedies matter only at step two, resolving the acknowledged
split with the First Circuit's Arias v. Herzon on one side and the Third,
Fifth, Seventh, Tenth, and Eleventh Circuits on the other. The severity
question (whether a non-lethal, non-immediate injury alone makes the context
new) will likely be subsumed rather than independently resolved — the
alternative-remedies holding is cleaner and disposes of the case. I do not
expect the Court to overrule Bivens, Davis, or Carlson outright; the majority
will say, as Egbert and Goldey did, that those cases stand in their own
contexts while confining Carlson to claims materially indistinguishable from
its facts.

**Breadth.** Categorical, not fact-bound. Because the ARP and PLRA apply to
every federal inmate, a holding keyed to them effectively ends
federal-prisoner medical-indifference Bivens suits generally rather than
turning on Watanabe's particular injury. The opinion will read as the
practical end of new Carlson claims while formally preserving Carlson itself.

**Separate writings.** Gorsuch will write a concurrence (in full or in the
judgment) repeating his Egbert position that the Court should stop
"preserving what remains" and overrule Bivens; Thomas may join it or write
similarly. The principal dissent — most likely Sotomayor, possibly Jackson —
will argue that Watanabe's claim sits squarely within Carlson's context on
every Ziglar dimension, that Carlson itself considered alternative remedies
(the FTCA), and that the Westfall Act's constitutional-claim carve-out
reflects congressional ratification of the Carlson remedy.

**Timing** (context only; no claim resolves on it): argument is set for
November 9, 2026. A decision most likely lands between late February and June
2027, with June more likely if the Court writes broadly or draws multiple
separate writings.

**Question taken.** The petition presents a single question — whether the
Ninth Circuit erred in recognizing a Bivens cause of action — and the Court
granted without limitation; it will decide that question as written, with the
alternative-remedies issue as the operative sub-question.
