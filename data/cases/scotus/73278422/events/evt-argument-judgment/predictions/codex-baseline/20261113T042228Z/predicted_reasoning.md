# Forecast of the Court's decision

## Judgment and lineup

The Court will **reverse** the Ninth Circuit's judgment and remand. The predicted division is **7–2**: Roberts, Thomas, Alito, Kagan, Gorsuch, Kavanaugh, and Barrett will support reversal; Sotomayor and Jackson will dissent. The Court will reject recognition of this individual damages action, rather than decide whether Nielsen's treatment actually satisfied the Eighth Amendment.

## Ground and breadth

The majority will hold that this claim presents a new Bivens context. It will focus on meaningful differences between the alleged treatment here and Carlson's rapidly fatal medical emergency, including the official's knowledge and affirmative contribution to the harm. A common constitutional provision and a common deliberate-indifference label will not by themselves establish the same context. Once the context is new, special factors will preclude extending the judicially created damages action; Congress is better placed to authorize such liability.

The majority's ground will be narrower than the government's preferred categorical elimination of Carlson. It will preserve Carlson within its core context while distinguishing this nonfatal, nonemergency treatment claim. It will not overrule Bivens altogether or declare that all federal-prison medical-care damages claims are unavailable. Nor will it announce that death is an element of an Eighth Amendment violation. Administrative remedies, prospective relief, and the FTCA will reinforce institutional caution without a holding that those mechanisms necessarily compensated this respondent adequately.

The Court will answer the presented cause-of-action question through the existing new-context framework and leave the proposed wholesale overruling of Bivens unresolved. It will not dispose of the case through a DIG or an equally divided affirmance.

## Opinions

Alito will deliver the opinion of the Court, explaining why the narrower distinctions are meaningful without erasing Carlson. Thomas will write separately to favor a broader repudiation of judicially created constitutional damages actions; Gorsuch will be a likely joiner of that concurrence. Sotomayor will write the principal dissent, joined by Jackson, maintaining that this is an application of Carlson, that factual variations concern the substantive violation rather than the existence of a remedy, and that Congress's legislation does not justify extinguishing the existing action.

Authorship and the seven-to-two coalition are more uncertain than the reversal call. The structured author distribution expresses that uncertainty; its highest-probability individual author is Alito, not a majority-probability assignment.
