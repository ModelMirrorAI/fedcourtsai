# Forecast: Nielsen v. Watanabe, No. 25-417 (argued Nov. 9, 2026)

**Judgment.** The Court reverses the Ninth Circuit and remands; Watanabe's Bivens/Carlson damages claim against the prison nurse cannot proceed. A plain "reversed" is the expected label; "vacated" is the minor alternative. A DIG or an affirmance is unlikely.

**Lineup.** 7-2: Roberts, Thomas, Alito, Gorsuch, Kavanaugh, Barrett and Kagan for reversal; Sotomayor and Jackson in dissent. Kagan is the least certain vote: she joins (or concurs only in the judgment) if the opinion distinguishes Carlson on its facts, and dissents if the opinion declares Carlson claims a null set. A 6-3 result with Kagan in dissent is the second most likely split.

**Ground.** The Court does not overrule Bivens and does not formally overrule Carlson. It applies the Abbasi/Egbert two-step: the claim is a new context because it differs meaningfully from Carlson — a non-fatal, non-emergency injury, where the prisoner could and did pursue administrative and injunctive relief — and special factors (the Administrative Remedy Program, the PLRA and Congress's continued legislating on prisoner suits without creating a damages action) bar extending the remedy. The opinion relies heavily on Goldey v. Fields and Abbasi.

**Breadth.** Intermediate. Carlson is confined close to its facts; the opinion stops short of saying no Carlson claim survives and reserves the Fourth Amendment Bivens core. In practice most federal-prisoner medical-care damages suits end, with the FTCA named as the remaining damages route.

**Who writes.** The opinion of the Court most likely comes from the Chief Justice or Justice Kavanaugh (the two who pressed for an incremental, no-overruling approach), with Alito and Barrett next. Gorsuch and/or Thomas write separately to say Bivens and Carlson should be overruled outright. Jackson or Sotomayor writes the principal dissent, arguing that the majority overrules Carlson in all but name without a stare decisis analysis; both may write. Kagan may write a short concurrence limiting the holding.

**Timing.** Decision between March and June 2027.
