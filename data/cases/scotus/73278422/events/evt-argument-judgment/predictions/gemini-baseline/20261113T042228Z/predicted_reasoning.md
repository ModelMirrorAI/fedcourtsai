# Predicted Court Reasoning: *Francis Nielsen v. Kekai Watanabe* (No. 25-417)

## Judgment and Vote Lineup

The Supreme Court will **reverse** the judgment of the Ninth Circuit (`judgment: "reversed"`), reinstating the district court's dismissal of Kekai Watanabe's *Bivens* damages claim against Bureau of Prisons (BOP) nurse Francis Nielsen. Because Step Two special factors under *Egbert v. Boule*, 596 U.S. 482 (2022), and *Goldey v. Fields*, 606 U.S. 942 (2025), foreclose a judicially created damages action as a pure matter of law once a new context is identified, the Court will reverse outright (or "reverse and remand for further proceedings consistent with this opinion," which resolves to `reversed`) rather than vacating for a second-step remand.

The decision will divide **6–3**: Chief Justice Roberts and Justices Thomas, Alito, Gorsuch, Kavanaugh, and Barrett will form the six-Justice majority reversing the Ninth Circuit, while Justices Sotomayor, Kagan, and Jackson will dissent. (A live secondary alignment is **7–2**, in which Justice Kagan either joins a tightly cabined opinion or concurs in the judgment solely on case-specific Step One factual distinctions between acute, fatal neglect in *Carlson v. Green*, 446 U.S. 14 (1980), and non-fatal chronic pain management.) Neither a dismissal of the writ as improvidently granted nor an equally divided affirmance is a live outcome: all nine Justices participated at oral argument with no recusal, and even though the conservative Justices divide over whether to formally overrule *Bivens* and *Carlson*, all six agree that the Ninth Circuit erred under existing doctrine.

## Scope of Review: Question Reached and Question Left Unresolved

The Court will resolve only the Question Presented in the certiorari petition—whether the Ninth Circuit erred in recognizing a *Bivens* cause of action here—and will **decline** Petitioner Nielsen's merits-stage invitation to formally overrule *Bivens v. Six Unknown Named Agents of Federal Bureau of Narcotics*, 403 U.S. 388 (1971), *Davis v. Passman*, 442 U.S. 228 (1979), and *Carlson v. Green* across the board. Modeling its disposition on Footnote 3 of *Cisco Systems, Inc. v. Doe*, 609 U.S. ___ (2026) ("this far and no further"), the majority will observe that overruling Fourth Amendment *Bivens* claims was neither presented in the petition nor urged by the United States as *amicus curiae* (which limited its abrogation argument to *Carlson* alone because Fourth Amendment search-and-seizure torts raise distinct Federal Tort Claims Act (FTCA) intentional-tort and discretionary-function questions), and is unnecessary because Watanabe's claim fails under settled precedent.

## Doctrinal Ground and Breadth of the Majority Holding

**Doctrinal basis (`majority-ground`):** Applying the two-step separation-of-powers framework of *Ziglar v. Abbasi*, 582 U.S. 120 (2017), *Egbert*, and *Goldey* rather than overruling *Bivens* or *Carlson* under *stare decisis*, the majority will hold that Watanabe's Eighth Amendment medical-care claim arises in a "new *Bivens* context" and is foreclosed by "special factors." First, at Step One, Watanabe's allegations differ meaningfully from *Carlson* both factually and legally: whereas *Carlson* involved affirmative, acute mistreatment during a medical crisis that caused the prisoner's death—leaving his estate with "damages or nothing" where Indiana survivorship law threatened FTCA recovery—Watanabe alleges ongoing, non-fatal failure to provide stronger pain medication and faster specialist care for a chronic bone fracture, a setting in which prospective administrative grievances, federal injunctive relief, and an FTCA medical-malpractice suit against the United States were accessible. Moreover, under *Abbasi*, 582 U.S. at 140, 148–149, Watanabe's suit implicates potential special factors *Carlson* never considered: Congress's post-1980 enactment of the Prison Litigation Reform Act of 1995 (PLRA), 42 U.S.C. § 1997e(a), without a standalone federal prisoner damages cause of action, and the expanded four-tier BOP Administrative Remedy Program (ARP), 28 C.F.R. pt. 542. Second, at Step Two (which *Egbert* merges into the single question whether there is *any* rational reason to think Congress is better suited than the Judiciary to weigh a damages remedy), *Goldey*, 606 U.S. at 944–945, controls: the PLRA, the alternative remedial mechanisms of the BOP ARP, injunctive actions, and the FTCA, and the systemic burden of turning federal courts into medical supervisors for routine prison treatment complaints independently bar extending *Bivens*. The majority will reject Respondent's contention that the 1988 Westfall Act carveout, 28 U.S.C. § 2679(b)(2)(A), or the PLRA's exhaustion mandate ratified or froze broad *Carlson* liability, reading both provisions (as in *Hernandez v. Mesa*, 589 U.S. 93, 111 n.9 (2020), and *Abbasi*) as neutral preservation and gatekeeping clauses rather than congressional commands to extend judge-made causes of action.

**Breadth of the holding (`ground-breadth`):** The majority will stop short of formally overruling *Bivens*, *Davis*, or *Carlson* ("this far and no further"), leaving Fourth and Fifth Amendment *Bivens* precedents untouched, while stating a rule categorical enough across post-PLRA, non-fatal federal prison medical disputes—resting on both the non-lethal nature of the alleged neglect (where ARP, injunctive, and FTCA remedies are available) and post-*Carlson* statutory and administrative developments—to foreclose Eighth Amendment *Bivens* medical-indifference suits in federal prisons beyond *Carlson*'s own acute, fatal facts, thereby resolving the circuit conflict between the Ninth Circuit and the Third, Fourth, Seventh, Tenth, and Eleventh Circuits.

## Opinion Authorship and Separate Writings

- **Opinion of the Court:** **Justice Kavanaugh** is the likeliest author of the Court's opinion (followed closely by Justice Alito, Chief Justice Roberts, and Justice Barrett). At oral argument, Kavanaugh framed the exact *Cisco Systems* Footnote 3 institutional compromise—declining to reach out to abolish Fourth Amendment *Bivens* claims while emphasizing FTCA availability and the operational gulf between fatal emergencies and routine prison medical care—and has not yet authored a lead *Bivens* opinion for the Court.
- **Concurrences:** **Justice Thomas** and **Justice Gorsuch** are each likely to write (or co-sign) a separate concurrence (or, for Gorsuch, an opinion concurring in the judgment on the *Egbert* pattern) arguing that the Court's own logic leaves zero viable *Carlson* claims and that the Court should forthrightly overrule *Bivens*, *Davis*, and *Carlson* rather than prolonging confusion for *pro se* prisoners who would be better served filing FTCA claims.
- **Dissents:** **Justice Sotomayor** will likely author the principal dissent (joined by Justices Kagan and Jackson), contending that Watanabe's deliberate-indifference claim against a BOP nurse falls squarely inside *Carlson*'s core Eighth Amendment context, that distinguishing *Carlson* based on whether a prisoner survives or on the PLRA/ARP stealth-overrules *Carlson* without satisfying *stare decisis*, and that Congress legislated in reliance on *Carlson* in both the Westfall Act and the PLRA. **Justice Jackson** also carries a substantial likelihood of authoring either a separate dissent emphasizing the practical inadequacy of administrative, injunctive, and FTCA remedies for incarcerated *pro se* litigants or the lead dissent if assigned by Justice Sotomayor.
