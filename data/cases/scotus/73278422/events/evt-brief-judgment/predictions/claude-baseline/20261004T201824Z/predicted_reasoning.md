# Forecast: what the Court will do in Nielsen v. Watanabe (No. 25-417)

Moment: `briefed` — both merits briefs are on the record; argument is set for November 9, 2026.

## Judgment class

**Reversed.** The Court will hold that Watanabe's Eighth Amendment deliberate-indifference claim arises in a new Bivens context, that special factors foreclose extending Carlson v. Green to it, and that no Bivens remedy is available. Because Watanabe conceded below (and petitioner's brief holds him to it) that his claim fails at step two if the context is new, the Court can reverse outright rather than vacate and remand for a step-two analysis. The judgment of the Ninth Circuit will be reversed and the case remanded for entry of a dismissal of the Bivens claim; the injunctive claim the Ninth Circuit allowed is not before the Court and will be untouched.

Less likely routes, in order: affirmance on the ground that the claim sits in Carlson's heartland (the respondent's "this far and no further" framing borrowed from Cisco Systems v. Doe); vacatur with a remand to apply a reformulated new-context test; a dismissal as improvidently granted (nothing in the briefing suggests a vehicle defect, and the Court held the petition for months before granting, so it chose this vehicle deliberately).

## Vote lineup

**6–3.** Chief Justice Roberts and Justices Thomas, Alito, Gorsuch, Kavanaugh, and Barrett in the majority; Justices Sotomayor, Kagan, and Jackson in dissent. The same six formed the Egbert v. Boule majority, and all nine joined the per curiam in Goldey v. Fields refusing a prison excessive-force extension — but Goldey was an extension case, while this one asks the Court to treat a medical-care claim against a federal jailer as outside Carlson, which is the one thing the three dissenters will not do.

Separate writings: expect a concurrence from Justice Gorsuch (joined or paralleled by Justice Thomas) arguing that Bivens, Davis, and Carlson should be overruled outright, as he did in Egbert. Expect the principal dissent from Justice Sotomayor, pressing that the ARP existed in 1974, before Carlson, and that the Westfall Act and the PLRA legislated in reliance on Carlson. Authorship of the majority is unforecastable from the record; the opinion is most likely assigned to one of Justices Kavanaugh, Barrett, or Thomas.

## The ground the majority rests on

The majority will not overrule Bivens or Carlson. It will apply the existing two-step framework and hold that (1) the Bureau of Prisons' Administrative Remedy Program and Congress's post-Carlson prison legislation (the PLRA's exhaustion mandate and its decision not to enact a damages remedy) are "potential special factors" Carlson did not consider, which under Ziglar v. Abbasi makes the context new even where the right, the mechanism of injury, and the class of defendant match; and (2) under Egbert, the existence of that alternative remedial structure alone forecloses the remedy. It will describe Carlson as remaining good law on its own facts, in the Abbasi/Egbert formula, while reaching a result that leaves very little room for a Carlson claim to proceed.

The majority will treat the severity and "damages or nothing" differences petitioner presses as supporting, not necessary, grounds — probably noting them and declining to rest the holding on a life-versus-injury line that would invite line-drawing. It will reject the ratification argument: the Westfall Act's carve-out for constitutional torts and the PLRA's exhaustion requirement will be read as Congress declining to displace an existing judge-made remedy, not as creating or ratifying one, and the Court will say that congressional silence on a damages remedy cuts against the plaintiff (Egbert, Abbasi). It will not decide whether Bivens should be overruled; the Cisco Systems footnote reaffirming the three historical ATS torts is the template for how this Court handles a residual judge-made remedy it no longer believes in.

## Breadth

The ground will be categorical rather than fact-bound. Because the ARP and the PLRA apply to every federal prisoner, the rule announced will make federal-prisoner deliberate-indifference medical claims new-context claims as a class, confining Carlson to its own facts (the acute, fatal emergency of 1980 with no administrative avenue available). The opinion will not announce a narrow holding tied to the chronic or non-fatal character of Watanabe's injury.

## Which question is reached

The single question presented as written. The Court will decide the new-context question and will treat step two as settled by Watanabe's concession and Goldey. Petitioner's second argument (overrule Bivens) will be noted and left for the concurrence.

## Timing

Argued November 9, 2026. A decision is likely between February and April 2027; a 6–3 opinion with a concurrence and a dissent on a well-trodden doctrine does not require a June release, but a June release is possible if the dissent is long.
