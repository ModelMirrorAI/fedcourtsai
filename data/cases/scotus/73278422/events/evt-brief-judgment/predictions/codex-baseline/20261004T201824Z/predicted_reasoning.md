# Forecast of the Court's judgment

The Court will **reverse** the Ninth Circuit and remand for proceedings consistent with rejecting Watanabe's Bivens cause of action against Nielsen. The disposition will be **6–3**: Roberts, Thomas, Alito, Kavanaugh, and Barrett in the majority; Gorsuch concurring in the judgment; Sotomayor, Kagan, and Jackson dissenting. Reversal and remand is the predicted judgment class, not merely vacatur for reconsideration.

The majority will reach the question whether this deliberate-indifference claim falls within Carlson's existing context. It will hold that an ongoing, nonfatal medical-care dispute in which administrative or prospective relief was available differs meaningfully from Carlson's acute, fatal emergency. Under the restrictive framework of Egbert and Abbasi, those remedial differences will matter at the new-context inquiry, not only after that inquiry has been completed. Once the context is new, the alternative remedial structure and Congress's role in designing prisoner remedies will foreclose judicial extension of the damages action. The holding will concern the availability of a cause of action, not a finding that the alleged mistreatment was constitutionally permissible.

The rule will extend beyond this particular injury to non-emergency medical-care claims with administrative or prospective remedial avenues. The majority will nevertheless leave Carlson formally intact, rather than expressly overrule all three recognized Bivens contexts. It will not make death an element of every Eighth Amendment violation. Gorsuch's separate rationale will favor a more categorical rejection of judicially created damages remedies; the dissent will argue that differences in the seriousness or duration of an injury do not transform an established medical-indifference context into a new one, and that narrowing Carlson this far evades stare decisis.

The Court will not decide whether Nielsen ultimately acted with deliberate indifference, resolve disputed medical facts, or award damages. It will reject the respondent's argument that the Westfall Act and PLRA require preserving this claim within Carlson's existing sphere. A DIG or an equally divided affirmance is not the expected route; the supplied record identifies no recusal or concrete jurisdictional obstacle.

The snapshot schedules argument for November 9, 2026. Expect a signed merits decision during the ensuing Term, most likely by June 2027. This timing forecast is contextual, not a declared scored claim. The binary forecast is P(judgment disturbed) = **0.84**, including reversal, vacatur, and a mixed judgment; it is not a forecast about whether certiorari will be granted.
