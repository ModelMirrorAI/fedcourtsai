## In brief

A federal prison nurse asks the Court to overturn a ruling letting a former inmate sue him for damages over his medical care.

## What happened

Kekai Watanabe, injured in a prison fight at a federal facility in Honolulu, alleges that nurse Francis Nielsen refused his requests for hospital care and that he went undiagnosed for months before doctors found a fractured tailbone. Watanabe sued Nielsen for damages, invoking a line of cases, including Carlson v. Green, in which the Supreme Court has allowed such suits against federal officials despite the absence of a statute authorizing them. A federal district court dismissed the claim, reasoning that Watanabe's case differed too much from Carlson to qualify. The U.S. Court of Appeals for the Ninth Circuit reversed, holding the claim was not meaningfully different from Carlson. The full Ninth Circuit declined to rehear the case over several judges' dissents.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked whether prisoners in Watanabe's situation may sue individual federal officers for money damages over inadequate medical care, or whether that kind of claim is different enough from the Court's past cases that courts may not recognize it without Congress's say-so. The parties also dispute whether the Court should go further and eliminate this category of suit entirely, or even abandon the broader line of cases allowing damages suits against federal officials.

## Where it stands

The Court granted review, and the case has been argued and now awaits decision. The government participated in the oral argument as well, supporting Nielsen's position.

## What each outcome would mean

Nielsen, the prison nurse, is asking the Court to reverse the Ninth Circuit's decision, which allowed Watanabe's case to go forward. If the Court sides with Nielsen, Watanabe's suit would be dismissed, cutting off this avenue of compensation, and courts generally would be barred from hearing similar damages claims by federal prisoners over medical care. If the Court sides with Watanabe, the Ninth Circuit's decision allowing the case to proceed would stand, and Watanabe could continue pursuing his claim in the lower courts. A ruling could also address, more broadly, whether this entire category of suits against federal officials should continue to exist.
