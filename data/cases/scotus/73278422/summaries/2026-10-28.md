## In brief

The Court heard a federal prisoner's case asking whether his lawsuit against a prison nurse for inadequate medical care could proceed under a 1980 precedent.

## What happened

Kekai Watanabe, injured in a prison fight, alleges that nurse Francis Nielsen refused to send him to a hospital despite his severe pain; Watanabe was later found to have a fractured tailbone. He sued Nielsen for damages, arguing prison staff violated his Eighth Amendment rights through deliberate indifference to his medical needs, relying on Carlson v. Green, a 1980 Supreme Court case allowing such suits against federal officials. A federal district court dismissed the claim, finding it differed too much from Carlson to proceed. The Ninth Circuit reversed, reinstating the claim, over a partial dissent. The full Ninth Circuit later declined to rehear the case, with eleven judges dissenting from that denial.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked whether Watanabe's lawsuit fits within the narrow category of cases where the Court has allowed prisoners to sue federal officials personally for money damages over constitutional violations, or whether it differs enough — in the severity of the harm, or because other remedies like prison grievance procedures exist — that courts should not recognize such a lawsuit without Congress's authorization. The Court is also asked more broadly whether it should eliminate or narrow this category of lawsuits altogether.

## Where it stands

The Court granted review, received full briefing from both sides and the federal government (supporting Nielsen), heard oral argument in November 2026, and the case now awaits decision.

## What each outcome would mean

Nielsen, the prison nurse, asked the Court to take the case and is seeking to overturn the Ninth Circuit's decision reinstating Watanabe's suit; the federal government has filed in support of Nielsen's position. If the Court affirms, Watanabe's case would go forward in the lower courts, and prisoners with similar claims in the Ninth Circuit's region could continue pursuing damages suits against federal officials. If the Court reverses, Watanabe's suit would be dismissed, and such claims would become harder or impossible to bring nationwide. If the Court vacates, it would send the case back without deciding the outcome for either side.
