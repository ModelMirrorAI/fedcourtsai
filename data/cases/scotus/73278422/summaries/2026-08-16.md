## In brief

The Supreme Court agreed to hear a prison nurse's challenge to a federal inmate's lawsuit over delayed medical treatment for a fractured bone.

## What happened

Kekai Watanabe, a federal inmate, alleged that after a prison assault, staff nurse Francis Nielsen and other officials denied him adequate care for months despite a fractured coccyx, treating him only with over-the-counter medication. Watanabe sued for damages and an injunction. The district court dismissed his claim, but the Ninth Circuit reversed, holding the claim was not meaningfully different from a 1980 Supreme Court case allowing similar damages suits by federal prisoners over medical neglect. The full Ninth Circuit declined to rehear the case, over the dissent of eleven judges.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked whether the appeals court was wrong to let this kind of damages lawsuit against a federal official go forward. More specifically, the case turns on whether a prison grievance system that did not exist when the Court first allowed such suits, and whether the lesser severity of Watanabe's injuries compared to the earlier case, should have blocked his claim from proceeding under that older precedent.

## Where it stands

The Court granted the petition on June 22, 2026. Briefing is underway, and the case has been set for oral argument on November 9, 2026.

## What each outcome would mean

Nielsen, the prison nurse, asked the Court to take up the case after losing before the Ninth Circuit. If the Court affirms, Watanabe's lawsuit continues, preserving his ability to sue prison officials for damages over inadequate medical care. If the Court reverses, Watanabe's claim would be barred, and prisoners with similar complaints in that circuit would lose that damages option. If the Court vacates, it would send the case back without resolving who is right, leaving the dispute for further proceedings.
