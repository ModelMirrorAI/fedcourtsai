# Forecast: PhRMA v. O'Day (No. 25-1018), disposition after the CVSG

The docket as of the 2026-08-14 snapshot shows two distributions (conferences
of 4/17/2026 and 6/18/2026) and the CVSG invitation issued June 22, 2026, out
of the second conference. Everything below forecasts from that state.

## Relists and timing

- **The petition will be distributed again — near-certainty (~0.97).** A
  CVSG'd petition is held until the Solicitor General files and is then
  redistributed; the only paths that avoid a third distribution are a
  withdrawal or a dismissal, neither of which anything on this docket
  suggests.
- **The SG's brief lands between roughly December 2026 and May 2027.** An
  invitation issued at the end of a Term is typically answered either by
  late fall/winter (in time for a same-Term disposition) or in the May
  filing wave. Given the case's regulatory subject matter and the number of
  federal disclosure regimes the brief must speak to, I expect the later end:
  most likely window is **spring 2027**.
- **Disposition by the end of October Term 2026 (June 2027) with probability
  ~0.75**; otherwise it slips to a redistribution early in OT2027. After the
  SG files, expect one to three further distributions before the order
  issues.

## The disposition itself

- **P(any grant — plenary, granted-in-part, or GVR) = 0.42.** The modal
  single outcome is still denial, but this petition sits far above the
  docket's base rate: the Court has already requested a response the State
  waived, distributed twice, and called for the SG's views after only two
  conferences — fast, deliberate engagement.
- **The pivot is the SG's recommendation.** The Court follows the SG's cert
  recommendation in the large majority of CVSG cases. I put **P(the SG
  recommends grant, in whole or limited to QP1) ≈ 0.45**. The BIO hands the
  SG two classic deny hooks — the facial posture (the Moody/NetChoice
  concern) and the ripeness question on the takings claim — and the United
  States has its own disclosure regimes (SEC, FDA, CMS reporting) that make
  the federal government wary of a broad rule that reporting mandates draw
  strict scrutiny. A "deny, but the Ninth Circuit's reasoning is wrong in
  part" brief is a live and perhaps modal shape.
- **If granted, the grant is limited to Question 1** (the First Amendment
  question) with probability ~0.6 conditional on a grant; both questions
  ~0.3; QP2 alone ~0.1. QP1 would likely be taken essentially as written or
  lightly reformulated to ask what scrutiny applies to compelled
  product-specific disclosure regimes. The takings question is the more
  vehicle-impaired of the two (facial posture, ripeness), and the Court can
  reach the compelled-speech framework without it.
- **No summary disposition.** No intervening decision of the Court bears on
  either question, so a GVR is improbable (~0.02 of the total probability
  mass); a summary reversal of a published, reasoned, divided panel on a
  doctrinal framework question is not this Court's practice.
- **If denied, expect a meaningful chance of separate writing (~0.25):** a
  dissent from denial or statement respecting denial, most plausibly from
  Justice Thomas (a long-standing skeptic of reduced scrutiny for commercial
  speech) and/or Justice Kavanaugh, whose American Meat Institute concurrence
  the petition builds on. A denial after a CVSG and this much engagement
  often draws one.
