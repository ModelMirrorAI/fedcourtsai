# Predicted reasoning — what the Court will do with No. 25-1018 after the CVSG

This forecast is made from the CVSG moment: the invitation to the Solicitor
General issued June 22, 2026, after two distributions (conferences of 4/17/2026
and 6/18/2026), a Court-requested response following the State's initial
waiver, and one cert-stage amicus brief (X.AI, LLC).

## Path from here

- **The Solicitor General will file.** An invitation issued at the very end of
  OT2025 is typically answered between December of the following Term and the
  May filing window; expect the SG's brief between roughly December 2026 and
  May 2027, with disposition of the petition by the end of OT2026 (June 2027).
- **Further relists: near-certain.** The docket shows two distributions;
  a CVSG'd petition is redistributed once the SG's brief arrives, so at least
  one further distribution is close to mechanical (fails only if the case
  settles, is withdrawn, or becomes moot first). Expect one to three further
  distributions: redistribution on the SG brief, plus at most a relist or two
  while the Court composes any grant or separate writing.
- **No second CVSG.** One invitation is already on the docket; the Court does
  not re-invite in the same case.

## Disposition

**P(grant family) = 0.44** — grant, grant-in-part, or (unlikely here) GVR.
The single most likely outcome remains denial, but this petition sits well
above even the CVSG'd population's average: a divided Ninth Circuit panel
(Judge Bea dissenting), two independently developed circuit-conflict claims
(compelled commercial-disclosure scrutiny against *Amestoy* (CA2) and *NAM*
(CADC); the "highly regulated industry" takings carve-out against *Philip
Morris v. Reilly* (CA1)), elite Supreme Court counsel, and a nationally
recurring subject — many states have enacted similar drug-price transparency
statutes. The main hazard is the SG recommending denial: the federal
government administers scores of its own mandatory reporting regimes (SEC,
FDA) and has an institutional interest in resisting heightened scrutiny for
reporting requirements, and the facial posture of both claims gives the SG a
clean vehicle objection after *Moody v. NetChoice*.

- **Which question, if granted:** QP1 (the First Amendment question) as
  written or lightly reformulated is the likeliest grant; a grant on both
  questions is plausible because the takings question is cleanly presented and
  carries its own claimed split. A grant limited to QP2 alone is unlikely.
- **Summary disposition: unlikely (0.06 conditional on grant).** No
  intervening decision of this Court bears on either question, so there is no
  GVR predicate; the point of the CVSG is plenary consideration.
- **If denied: some chance of a separate writing (0.18 conditional on
  denial).** A denial here most likely follows an SG recommendation to deny; in
  a CVSG'd, high-salience compelled-speech case a statement respecting denial
  or a dissent from denial is a live possibility — Justice Thomas has
  repeatedly written on the scope of reduced scrutiny for compelled commercial
  disclosures, and the takings question could draw Justice Gorsuch — but most
  denials, even of CVSG'd petitions, issue without writing.
