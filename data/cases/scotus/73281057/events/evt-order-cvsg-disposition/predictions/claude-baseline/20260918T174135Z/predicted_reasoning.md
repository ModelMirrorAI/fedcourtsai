# Forecast: what the Court will do with No. 25-1018 (PhRMA v. O'Day)

**State forecast from.** The docket shows two distributions (Conference of April 17, 2026, then Conference of June 18, 2026), a Response Requested order after the State's initial waiver, one cert-stage amicus brief (X.AI, LLC), and the June 22, 2026 order inviting the Solicitor General to file a brief. No SG brief is on the docket yet.

**Further distributions.** Near-certain. The Court cannot act until the United States files, and once it does the petition will be redistributed for a conference. Expect the SG's brief in late November or December 2026 and a single redistribution for a January or February 2027 conference, with a decision on the petition on the order list after that conference or, if the Court is inclined to grant, one further relist first. So: at least one more distribution (very likely), plausibly two.

**A further CVSG.** None. The invitation is already on the docket; the Court will not issue a second.

**The Solicitor General's recommendation, and its effect.** This is the pivot. The United States has a heavy institutional interest in mandatory reporting regimes surviving under less than strict scrutiny (SEC, FDA, EPA, CMS reporting all sit behind Oregon's law), and the current administration has pursued drug-price transparency and price-lowering measures of its own. I expect the SG to defend the constitutionality of pricing-disclosure reporting requirements and, more likely than not, to recommend denial, characterizing the Ninth Circuit's holding as a fact-bound Bolger application and the takings question as unripe on a facial challenge. A grant recommendation limited to the First Amendment question is the live alternative. The Court follows the SG's recommendation in the clear majority of CVSG'd cases.

**Disposition.** Denial is more likely than not, but a grant is a substantial possibility. If the Court grants, it will grant plenary review on Question 1 only, most likely reformulated to ask whether a government reporting requirement compelling product-specific commercial information is subject to intermediate scrutiny under Central Hudson or to strict scrutiny, and whether an interest in correcting information asymmetries suffices under intermediate scrutiny. Question 2 (the "highly regulated industry" takings holding) is the weaker vehicle: the Ninth Circuit's takings ruling was unanimous, it rests on Ruckelshaus v. Monsanto, and the State has flagged a ripeness problem with a pre-enforcement facial challenge to a permissive disclosure provision. The Court would leave Question 2 behind or deny it while granting Question 1.

**Summary disposition.** Unlikely. No intervening decision of this Court bears on the compelled-reporting question, and a case the Court has already carried through Response Requested and a CVSG is set for briefing and argument if taken, not disposed of in the cert order. A GVR would require the SG to point to an intervening decision that the Court agrees changes the analysis, which I do not foresee.

**If denied: separate writing.** A dissent from denial or a statement respecting denial is plausible but not the expected outcome. Justice Thomas has repeatedly questioned the reduced protection afforded to compelled commercial disclosures (his separate writings in Moody v. NetChoice and Matal v. Tam, both cited by the petition), and Justice Kavanaugh's en banc concurrence in American Meat Institute rejected the very "information asymmetry" rationale the Ninth Circuit accepted. Either could write. Most CVSG'd denials nonetheless issue silently, and a denial that tracks the SG's recommendation is especially likely to be silent.

**If granted: merits shape (conditional only).** A majority would reverse or vacate on Question 1, holding that the Ninth Circuit's "product-specific government reporting requirement" category has no footing in Bolger or NIFLA, and remanding for application of the correct framework rather than deciding whether HB 4005 survives it. The Court would not reach the takings question.
