# Prediction rationale

I assign a 0.43 probability to any grant-family disposition and predict denial as the modal label. The committed statpack provides two relevant anchors. Pooling the `high` band's bracketed risk-set figures over the eight rendered Terms strictly before OT2025 gives 439 estimated grant-family outcomes among 1,074 weighted resolved petitions, or 40.9%. The paid scored-segment CVSG cut records 48 plenary grants and 9 GVRs among 163 resolved CVSG petitions, or 35.0% for the grant family. The context uses the matching `sal-v3` version, so the band anchor is applicable.

I adjusted above the CVSG cut and slightly above the prior-Term high-band anchor. The Court requested a response after Oregon initially waived one, considered the completed briefing at a second conference, and then called for the Solicitor General's views. Those affirmative acts show materially more interest than ordinary distribution. The petition also presents a published Ninth Circuit decision, a dissent on the First Amendment question, and asserted conflicts with other circuits on both compelled disclosure and trade-secret takings. The questions have consequences beyond pharmaceutical regulation, supporting the 0.74 significance score.

I did not adjust farther upward because Oregon's brief identifies meaningful obstacles. It characterizes the commercial-speech issue as a fact-bound application of existing doctrine, disputes that either asserted conflict is real, and emphasizes that even the district court classified the reports as commercial speech. On the takings issue, the Ninth Circuit was unanimous, the public-interest exception had not been enforced, and the facial posture permits denial without resolving whether a future disclosure would be a taking. The Solicitor General could regard those vehicle problems as sufficient despite the underlying importance. Only one amicus filing appears in the frozen snapshot, which is less evidence of broad coalition interest than is common in the strongest business petitions.

The 0.96 relist-increment probability reflects procedure rather than an independent merits signal: the record already shows two distributions, and a CVSG petition is ordinarily redistributed after the Solicitor General files before the Court disposes of it. The `cvsg-increment` probability is 0 because the June 22 invitation is already in the frozen state and that claim is vacuous at the CVSG moment. Conditional on a grant, I assign 0.12 to a summary route; the statpack's CVSG bucket contains some GVRs, but these constitutional questions do not present an evident intervening authority or simple error suitable for summary treatment. Conditional on denial, I assign 0.14 to some separate statement or dissent because of the constitutional stakes and Judge Bea's dissent, while recognizing that separate writings from denial remain unusual.

The provisioned petition, questions presented, brief in opposition, complete document manifest, June 23 snapshot, and frozen context were available and readable. I used no live CourtListener, web, or corpus-query retrieval. The chief uncertainty is the content and recommendation of the Solicitor General's eventual brief, which does not exist in the frozen record and is likely to dominate the post-CVSG decision.
