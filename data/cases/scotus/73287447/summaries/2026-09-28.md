## In brief

Nexstar asks the Court to hear its bid to shut down DirecTV's antitrust lawsuit over blacked-out television stations, after the lower court let the case proceed.

## What happened

DirecTV sued Nexstar, Mission Broadcasting, and White Knight, claiming they conspired to fix retransmission fees, causing DirecTV to end negotiations and lose subscribers when the broadcasters' stations went dark. A district court dismissed the suit, ruling DirecTV lacked "antitrust standing" because it never purchased at the disputed price. The Second Circuit reversed, holding DirecTV's losses counted as a recognized antitrust injury and that DirecTV was a proper plaintiff given its long history dealing with the broadcasters and its status as the conspiracy's sole target. A judge dissented. Rehearing en banc was denied, and the case returned to the district court, where discovery is underway.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked whether a company that refused to pay allegedly inflated prices, and so never completed the purchase, can still sue under antitrust law for business losses that followed from walking away, or whether such downstream losses are too indirect and uncertain to support a lawsuit.

## Where it stands

The petition was distributed for conference twice, and the Court asked for a response after the first distribution; DirecTV opposed the petition and Nexstar filed a reply. The case was distributed again for the September 28, 2026 conference, and the docket shows no action since.

## What each outcome would mean

Nexstar and its co-defendants are asking the Court to take up the case. If the Court grants review, it will hear full arguments and decide whether DirecTV's claims can proceed, without yet deciding who wins. If it denies review, the Second Circuit's ruling stands, and DirecTV's lawsuit continues in the district court, where discovery is already underway.
