## In brief

The Supreme Court declined to hear broadcasters' challenge to a ruling letting DirecTV sue them over an alleged price-fixing scheme.

## What happened

DirecTV sued Nexstar and two affiliated broadcasters, Mission and White Knight, claiming they conspired to demand inflated fees to carry their television stations. When DirecTV refused to pay and let its contracts with Mission and White Knight lapse, their stations went dark for DirecTV subscribers, some of whom canceled service. A federal district court dismissed the case, ruling DirecTV could not sue because it never actually paid the inflated prices. The Second Circuit Court of Appeals reversed, over one judge's dissent, holding DirecTV's lost subscribers and profits were a direct, non-speculative result of the alleged scheme given the companies' long history of dealing. The broadcasters then asked the Supreme Court to step in.

## What the Court is being asked

The broadcasters ask whether a company that refused to buy a price-fixed product, and never paid the inflated price, can still sue for antitrust damages based on business it lost as a result. They argue other appeals courts have barred such suits as too speculative, while DirecTV and the appeals court that ruled for it say the rule depends on case-specific facts, including whether the companies had dealt with each other before.

## Where it stands

The petition was distributed for conference twice, after full briefing including a reply from the broadcasters. On October 5, 2026, the Supreme Court denied the petition.

## What each outcome would mean

Nexstar, Mission, and White Knight, as petitioners, asked the Court to take up the case. With the petition denied, the Court will not review the question, and the Second Circuit's ruling allowing DirecTV's lawsuit to proceed stands. DirecTV's antitrust claims against the broadcasters continue in the lower courts, where discovery was already underway. Had the Court instead granted review, it would have heard full arguments on the standing question before deciding it.
