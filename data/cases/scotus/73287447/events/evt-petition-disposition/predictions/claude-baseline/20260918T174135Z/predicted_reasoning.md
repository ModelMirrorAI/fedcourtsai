# Forecast: Nexstar Media Group, Inc. v. DirecTV, LLC (No. 25-1243)

**Disposition: denied, P(any grant) = 0.13.** The petition will most likely be denied on the order list following the September 28, 2026 long conference.

## Relists

The docket shows two distributions: June 9 (for the June 25 conference, superseded when the Court requested a response on June 23) and September 9 (for the September 28 long conference). The petition has never been relisted in the classic sense; the September 28 conference is its first consideration on full briefing. Forecasting from that state: the modal path is disposal without any further distribution — denial on the first October order list. I put the chance of at least one further distribution at about 0.30, made up of three routes: a grant (which now almost always follows one relist), a CVSG (which produces a redistribution once the SG's brief arrives), and a relist for a Justice to consider writing separately, which then ends in denial. Conditional on relisting, expect one or two relists, not a long string.

## CVSG

No CVSG is on the docket. The Court calls for the SG's views in a meaningful share of private antitrust petitions, and the United States has already spoken to this question as amicus in the Second Circuit (July 2024, in support of neither party but urging that non-purchaser status is not a categorical bar). That brief was filed under the prior administration, which gives the Court a reason to ask whether the current Solicitor General holds the same view; but the low-percolation, interlocutory posture cuts against investing a CVSG here. I put P(CVSG after prediction time) at 0.12. If it happens, it would come from the September 28 conference or one relist later, with the SG's brief arriving around May 2027 and disposition at the end of OT2026.

## Which question, if granted

If the Court grants, it would take the question presented essentially as written — whether a priced-out non-purchaser has antitrust standing to recover downstream losses — possibly reframed around whether a prior course of dealing suffices to make such a plaintiff an efficient enforcer under Associated General Contractors. Plenary review would proceed on the Second Circuit's published 2-1 opinion with Judge Sullivan's dissent as the counterweight. If granted, I would expect the Court to side with the petitioners more often than not, given its historical preference for administrable limits on private treble-damages actions, but that is conditional and unscored.

## Summary disposition

Very unlikely: P(cert-order disposition | grant) = 0.04. No intervening decision of this Court bears on non-purchaser standing, so there is no GVR hook, and a summary reversal of a reasoned, published, multi-factor efficient-enforcer analysis at the pleading stage is not the Court's practice.

## Dissent from denial

Unlikely: P(some noted dissent or statement | denial) = 0.06. Business antitrust denials rarely draw separate writings, and the fact-bound, interlocutory posture gives a would-be dissenter little to write about. The response request shows at least one chambers' interest, which is why the number is not lower.
