## In brief

A former Shreveport Fire Department employee asks the Court to revive her job discrimination lawsuit after lower courts ruled against her.

## What happened

Felicia Scroggins sued the City of Shreveport, alleging she was denied promotions within the Shreveport Fire Department under circumstances suggesting unlawful discrimination. The district court granted summary judgment for the city, finding no genuine factual dispute for a jury to resolve. Scroggins appealed, and the Fifth Circuit affirmed that ruling, though one judge dissented, concluding the record showed real factual disputes that should have gone to a jury. The full Fifth Circuit later denied rehearing. Scroggins, representing herself, now asks the Supreme Court to take up her case.

## What the Court is being asked

Scroggins asks the Court to decide three things: whether the lower courts wrongly threw out her case despite factual disputes that should have gone to a jury; whether the appeals court applied the wrong standard for weighing an employer's stated reasons against claims that those reasons were a pretext for discrimination; and whether the appeals court was too strict in treating some of her arguments as forfeited because she was representing herself without a lawyer.

## Where it stands

The City of Shreveport has waived its right to respond to the petition, meaning it will not file a brief opposing review unless the Court asks for one. The petition is awaiting the Court's consideration at a conference; no action on it has yet been recorded.

## What each outcome would mean

Scroggins, the petitioner, is asking the Court to hear her case. If the Court grants the petition, it would take up the case and decide the questions about summary judgment, the discrimination-claim framework, and the treatment of self-represented litigants, without yet deciding who wins. If the Court denies the petition, the Fifth Circuit's decision affirming judgment for the City of Shreveport stands, and Scroggins's lawsuit remains dismissed.
