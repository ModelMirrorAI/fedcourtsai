## In brief

The Supreme Court declined to hear Felicia Scroggins's bid to revive her employment discrimination lawsuit against the City of Shreveport.

## What happened

Felicia Scroggins, a former employee, sued the City of Shreveport claiming she was denied promotions within the Shreveport Fire Department due to unlawful discrimination. The district court granted summary judgment to the city, finding no genuine factual dispute requiring a trial. Scroggins, representing herself, appealed to the Fifth Circuit, which affirmed the district court's ruling, though one judge dissented, concluding the record showed real factual disputes. The Fifth Circuit denied rehearing, and Scroggins then asked the Supreme Court to take up her case.

## What the Court is being asked

Scroggins's petition raises three issues: whether the lower courts wrongly decided the case without a trial despite disputed facts that should go to a jury; whether the appeals court applied the wrong standard for weighing an employer's stated reasons against claims that those reasons were a cover for discrimination; and whether the appeals court was too strict in treating some of her arguments as waived, given that she was representing herself without a lawyer.

## Where it stands

The city waived its right to respond to the petition. The Court considered the petition at its September 28, 2026 conference and denied it on October 5, 2026.

## What each outcome would mean

Scroggins asked the Court to take up her case. By denying the petition, the Court leaves the Fifth Circuit's decision in place, meaning the city's win on summary judgment stands and Scroggins's lawsuit remains dismissed. The denial does not mean the Court agreed the lower court was correct; it simply leaves that ruling as the final word in her case.
