## In brief

A former Shreveport Fire Department employee asks the Court to revive her job discrimination lawsuit after lower courts ruled against her.

## What happened

Felicia Scroggins sued the City of Shreveport, Louisiana, claiming she was denied promotions within the Shreveport Fire Department because of unlawful discrimination. The district court granted summary judgment to the City, finding no genuine factual dispute requiring a trial. Scroggins, representing herself, appealed, and the Fifth Circuit affirmed the district court's ruling, with one judge dissenting on the ground that the record showed real factual disputes. The Fifth Circuit later denied rehearing. Scroggins then asked the Supreme Court to review the case.

## What the Court is being asked

Scroggins asks the Court to decide three things: whether the lower courts wrongly ended her case early despite factual disputes that she says should go to a jury, whether the appeals court wrongly made her disprove the City's stated reasons for its promotion decisions instead of letting a jury weigh credibility, and whether the appeals court was too strict in treating some of her arguments as abandoned because she was representing herself without a lawyer.

## Where it stands

The City waived its right to respond to the petition. The case was distributed for the Court's September 28, 2026 conference, and the docket shows no action taken on it since.

## What each outcome would mean

Scroggins, who lost in the lower courts, is asking the Court to take up her case. If the Court grants the petition, it would hear full arguments and decide the legal questions itself, though granting review alone would not decide who wins. If the Court denies the petition, the Fifth Circuit's decision affirming judgment for the City of Shreveport would remain in place, ending Scroggins's case, though a denial would not mean the Court agreed the lower court was correct.
