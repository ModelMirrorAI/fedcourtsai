## In brief

The Court declined to hear a former city employee's bid to revive her job discrimination lawsuit after lower courts rejected it.

## What happened

Felicia Scroggins sued the City of Shreveport, Louisiana, alleging she was denied promotions within the Shreveport Fire Department under circumstances suggesting unlawful discrimination. The district court granted summary judgment for the city, finding no genuine factual dispute requiring a trial. Scroggins, representing herself, appealed to the Fifth Circuit, which affirmed the district court's ruling, though one judge dissented, concluding the record showed real factual disputes. The Fifth Circuit denied rehearing, and Scroggins then asked the Supreme Court to take up her case.

## What the Court is being asked

Scroggins raises three issues: whether the lower courts wrongly decided the case without a trial despite factual disputes that she says should have gone to a jury; whether the courts applied the standard framework for proving workplace discrimination claims in a way that unfairly required her to disprove the city's explanations rather than letting a jury weigh them; and whether the appeals court was too strict in treating some of her arguments as forfeited, given that she was representing herself without a lawyer.

## Where it stands

The city waived its right to respond to the petition. The case was distributed for the Court's September 28, 2026 conference, and on October 5, 2026, the Court denied the petition.

## What each outcome would mean

Scroggins, as petitioner, asked the Court to take up her case. By denying the petition, the Court leaves the Fifth Circuit's ruling in place, meaning the summary judgment against Scroggins stands and her discrimination claims against the City of Shreveport remain dismissed. The denial does not mean the Court agreed the lower courts were right; it simply leaves that decision as the final word in her case.
