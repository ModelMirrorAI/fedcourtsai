# Rationale

I assign a 1.8% probability to any grant-family disposition and predict denial. The sal-v3 arrival anchor for this paid, baseline-band OT2026 petition is 6.56%: across the displayed strictly prior Terms 2017–2025, the weakest band's bracketed risk set contains 863 grant-family outcomes among 13,163 resolved weighted petitions. That is the appropriate unconditional arrival-population rate; the terminal zero-relist rate would improperly assume that this newly docketed petition never progresses.

I adjust sharply downward because the petition presents three case-specific allegations of error in applying settled summary-judgment, *McDonnell Douglas*, and pro-se-forfeiture rules. It identifies no circuit split or recurring legal disagreement and largely asks the Court to reassess whether this record contains factual disputes. The nine-page pro-se petition states the asserted conflicts at a high level without developing a clean rule question, making both certworthiness and vehicle quality weak. The respondent's waiver of a response is consistent with that assessment.

The meaningful counterweight is that the decision below is published and, according to the petition, drew a dissent disputing summary judgment. The CourtListener opinion search independently confirmed the published Fifth Circuit decision dated October 17, 2025. A follow-up request for the opinion metadata/text was throttled, so I could not independently assess the dissent or the lower-court record and did not infer more than the provisioned petition states. No brief in opposition was provisioned.

The 98.5% distribution-increment probability reflects the arrival vantage: the frozen context shows zero distributions, so the claim asks whether the petition will ever receive its first distribution, not whether it will be relisted after a conference. The filed waiver makes ordinary distribution especially likely; dismissal or another pre-distribution termination is the small complement. The requested relief implicates no federal program or federal party, so a CVSG is exceptionally unlikely (0.1%). Conditional on the rare event of a grant, I put 78% on a cert-order disposition because this petition's plausible grant theory is correction of fact-bound summary-judgment error under *Tolan*, which is much more naturally handled summarily than through plenary review. Conditional on denial, the ordinary employment dispute and absence of a developed split make a separate statement or dissent very unlikely (0.3%).

The main uncertainty is that the appendix and full Fifth Circuit opinions were not provisioned, and the live opinion text could not be retrieved after the service throttled the second request. A genuine, clean disagreement in the panel opinions could raise the estimate, but the petition's presentation does not demonstrate one.
