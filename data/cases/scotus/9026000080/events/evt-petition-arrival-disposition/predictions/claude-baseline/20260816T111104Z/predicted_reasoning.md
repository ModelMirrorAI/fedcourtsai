# Predicted reasoning — Scroggins v. City of Shreveport, No. 26-80 (arrival)

Forecast of what the Court will do with this petition, made at docketing with
zero distributions on the record (the arrival moment's defining state).

**Disposition: denied, almost certainly without comment.** The petition asks
for error correction — that the Fifth Circuit misread a summary-judgment
record, misapplied McDonnell Douglas, and enforced forfeiture too strictly
against a pro se litigant. It alleges conflict with this Court's precedent but
no circuit split, and the Court does not sit to re-weigh summary-judgment
records. The respondent city has already waived its response (Aug 6, 2026),
the normal posture of a petition headed for a quiet denial.

**Distribution and relists.** The record shows zero distributions. With the
response waived, the petition will be distributed in ordinary course — expect
it on the late-September 2026 long conference or one shortly after. I expect
exactly one distribution and no relist: nothing about the petition invites a
closer look, and the waiver clears it for immediate conferencing. A call for
response is possible but unlikely (the Court CFRs where a petition has a real
chance; this one does not read that way).

**No CVSG.** No federal statute's meaning is contested in a way that implicates
the United States' interests beyond routine Title VII/§ 1983 application, and
no federal party is involved. A CVSG would be a major surprise.

**Which question, if any.** If the Court acted at all, it would not take any QP
as written — all three are case-specific. The only realistic vehicle for action
is QP 1 recast as a Tolan v. Cotton-style failure to view the evidence in the
light most favorable to the nonmovant.

**Summary route dominates, conditional on action.** If this petition lands in
the grant family at all — which I put at well under one percent — the far more
likely route is a summary disposition (a Tolan-style per curiam reversal or a
GVR) rather than plenary review; the Court does not set fact-bound
summary-judgment applications for argument. Hence the high conditional
summary-disposition-route number beside the very low grant number.

**On denial: no separate writing.** A dissent from denial or statement is
unlikely (~1%). The scenario that would produce one — a Justice writing on
summary-judgment standards or forfeiture against pro se litigants, most
plausibly Justice Sotomayor or Justice Jackson — is disfavored here because the
petition's record presentation is thin and the claimed factual disputes are
described only at a high level of generality.
