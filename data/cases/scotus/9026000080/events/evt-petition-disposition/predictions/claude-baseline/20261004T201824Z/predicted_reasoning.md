# Forecast: Scroggins v. City of Shreveport, No. 26-80 (petition disposition)

**Disposition.** The Court will deny certiorari, almost certainly on the order list following the September 28, 2026 conference (expected October 5, 2026), without opinion. The petition is a pro se, paid petition asking for fact-bound review of a Fifth Circuit summary-judgment affirmance in a Title VII promotion case; it alleges no circuit split, and the panel majority rested on forfeiture, an independent ground the petition does not seriously engage.

**Relists.** The docket shows exactly one distribution, for the September 28, 2026 long conference. I do not expect a further distribution. The only mechanism that would produce one is a Justice holding the petition to write about the dissent below, and the vehicle problems described below make that unlikely. Probability of at least one more distribution: about 7 percent.

**CVSG.** No call for the views of the Solicitor General. The case is private-party Title VII litigation against a municipality with no federal interest, and the Court does not invite the SG on fact-bound summary-judgment disputes. Probability: well under 1 percent.

**Which question presented, if any.** If the Court acted at all it would act on Questions 1 and 2 together, read as a single claim that the Fifth Circuit (through the district court's reasoning it affirmed) demanded that the plaintiff prove a policy violation rather than merely raise a genuine dispute about pretext. Question 3, the pro se liberal-construction claim, has no independent traction because the Court has never held that appellate forfeiture rules bend for pro se civil litigants beyond pleading-stage construction.

**Summary disposition versus plenary review.** Conditional on any grant, the route would be summary: a Tolan v. Cotton-style per curiam vacatur for failing to view the evidence in the light most favorable to the nonmovant, or a GVR. A plenary grant on these questions is essentially impossible. Conditional probability of the summary route given a grant: about 70 percent.

**Dissent from or statement respecting denial.** I expect none. The Fifth Circuit dissent (Judge Dennis) gives a Justice sympathetic to summary-judgment overreach a ready-made narrative, but the panel majority's finding that four of the ten authorities in the petitioner's appellate brief appear to be fabricated, together with the forfeiture ground, makes this an unattractive vehicle for a separate writing. Probability that any Justice notes a dissent or writes respecting the denial: about 3 percent.

**Timing.** Denial on the first order list of October Term 2026, October 5, 2026.
