## Why 43%, substantially above the anchor

The largest upward adjustment is substantive, not the response request alone. The petition presents the same central question the Court previously agreed to hear in Laboratory Corporation: whether a damages class can be certified with uninjured members. The provisioned petition describes that proceeding's dismissal without deciding the issue and Kavanaugh's separate discussion of Rule 23. Those facts make this materially different from a generic petition claiming error. I use that prior case only as described in the supplied filing; attempted independent web confirmation returned no usable material and CourtListener was throttled.

The appellate opinion itself supplies a genuine legal conflict candidate: Appendix 3a states that named-plaintiff standing suffices at certification and addresses absent members later. Thus the petition is not merely attacking an unexplained exercise of discretion. The petition also identifies contrary circuit approaches, although I have not independently checked every cited circuit decision. The Court's affirmative request for an opposition adds some evidence of interest. The Rule 23(f) posture and the petition's account of a district-court stay reduce the particular moving-class-definition concern it attributes to Laboratory Corporation. These considerations justify moving far above 5.1%; the magnitude of the move is a judgment, not an estimated response-requested coefficient from the statpack, which publishes none.

The strongest downward adjustment comes from reading the appendix rather than accepting the petition's clean-vehicle characterization. The panel does not find that 1,275 members conclusively lacked Article III injury. It assumes the possibility of adequately compensating refunds while separately affirming that sorting no-refund, deposit-refund and full-purchase-refund recipients is not especially involved (Appendix 4a-5a). The district-court reasoning treats the challenged charge as a common point-of-sale diminished-value injury and rejects the premise that later refunds necessarily remove that injury (43a-44a). The appellate opinion also describes a common ascertainable loss. Whether that reasoning is ultimately right is different from whether it complicates review of the petition's categorical question. The Court could regard the presence of actually uninjured members, and the practical complexity of identifying them, as insufficiently settled here.

The decision is unpublished and rests partly on fact-specific predominance review. Unpublished status is not disqualifying, especially where an opinion applies settled circuit law, but it is another reason not to equate an important question with a clean grant. No opposition is available to test preservation, alternative grounds or the petition's account of the refund record. I therefore stop below an even chance rather than treating earlier interest in the abstract issue as four committed votes for this vehicle. My point call is denial, P(any grant)=0.43; the remaining 0.57 is mostly denial with a small allowance for other nongrant exits.

## Other commitments and uncertainty

The 98% additional-distribution probability is measured against the frozen count of one, not a forecast of a genuine postconference relist. The response-requested moment mechanically masks that claim in scoring, but the number still reflects my belief. A CVSG is possible but unlikely at 3%. The 8% summary-route probability is conditional on a grant; it leaves about 39.6 percentage points of unconditional probability on plenary grants and 3.4 points on grant-side summary dispositions. A disputed, expressly reserved certification question is better suited to argument than summary reversal on this record.

The 14% writing-on-denial claim is conditional on denial. Kavanaugh's supplied prior writing makes him the strongest candidate, but his wish to decide an issue need not produce a statement every time a petition is denied. The order-writer distribution is unconditional, allows other kinds of order writings, and is not an asserted vote count. I omit optional cert votes because a silent denial would not reveal them.

The stakes score is 0.65: a general rule about class membership could affect consumer, employment and other damages classes nationwide. That importance does not imply the Court will choose this particular ten-cent bottle-deposit dispute.
