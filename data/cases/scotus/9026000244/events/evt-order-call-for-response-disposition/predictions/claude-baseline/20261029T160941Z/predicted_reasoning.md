# Predicted course: Kroger Co. (Fred Meyer) v. Solano, No. 26-244

**State forecast from.** The snapshot (cut 2026-09-04) shows one distribution (for the 9/28/2026 conference) and the Court's request for a response on 2026-09-03, made after the respondents waived. There is no CVSG on the record.

**Disposition.** I expect a denial, at about 17% for a grant. Once the brief in opposition is in, the petition will be distributed again. The live docket shows the response deadline extended twice, to December 4, 2026, so that distribution should land at a December or January conference. A decision at that first post-response conference is the likeliest course. If the Court is weighing a grant, I expect one or two relists before it acts.

**CVSG.** I don't expect one. This is a private Rule 23 dispute, and the United States is not a party.

**Question the Court would take.** If granted, the Court would take the question as written: the one it granted and then dismissed in *Labcorp v. Davis*, about certifying a Rule 23(b)(3) class that includes members without Article III injury. The Rule 23 predominance framing Justice Kavanaugh adopted in his *Labcorp* dissent is a likely way to narrow it.

**Summary route.** Plenary review is far likelier than a summary disposition if the Court grants. The one realistic summary path runs through a different vehicle: the Court grants another petition on the same question, holds this one, and later GVRs it (sends it back for reconsideration). I put that route at about 12% conditional on a grant.

**Dissent from denial.** I think a separate writing on a denial is unlikely (about 11%). If one appears, the likeliest author is Justice Kavanaugh, who dissented alone in *Labcorp* and called the Ninth Circuit's rule wrong. Justices Thomas and Alito are next.
