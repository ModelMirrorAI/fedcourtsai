# Forecast — Kroger Co. (Fred Meyer) v. Solano, No. 26-244

**State of the docket (snapshot 2026-10-25):** one distribution, for the
9/28/2026 conference. The respondents waived, and the Court then **requested a
response** on Sep 3. The response is now due **Dec 4, 2026** after two
extensions. Two amicus briefs support the petitioner: the Retail Litigation
Center, and the Chamber of Commerce with ATRA and the Business Roundtable. The
docket shows no CVSG.

**Relisting.** The petition will be **distributed again** once the brief in
opposition and any reply are in. That puts it at a late-December or
January 2027 conference. Expect one more distribution and then a decision.
A second relist, at most, is likely only if the Court is waiting on the
companion-question petition in *Bayer HealthCare v. Drake* (No. 26-376).
Holding this case for *Bayer* is the more probable form of extended pendency.

**CVSG.** Unlikely. These are private parties in a consumer class action under
state law, and no federal program is implicated. The Court did not ask for the
SG's views on this question in *LabCorp*.

**Disposition.** The most likely outcome is **denial**. The question is the
one the Court granted in *LabCorp v. Davis* and before that in *Tyson Foods*.
Both times it failed to reach the answer, and Justice Kavanaugh's *LabCorp*
dissent shows at least one Justice wants to reach it. Even so, the Court
denied *Takeda v. Painters* (25-625) on the same question in March 2026. This
vehicle is weaker than that one in several ways:

- The Ninth Circuit ruled in an unpublished memorandum that applied *Healy* and *Olean*.
- The stakes are a 10-cent deposit, multiplied by Oregon statutory damages.
- The uninjured members are those who received refunds, a subset that records may identify, so redefining the class could moot the issue.
- The Ninth Circuit also rejected a separate self-inflicted-injury argument, which adds a fact-bound wrinkle.

If the Court wants the issue this Term, the published, divided *Bayer* opinion
is the more natural candidate. In that scenario this petition is held, and it
is then GVR'd or denied in light of *Bayer*.

**If granted:** most likely as a hold followed by a GVR after the Court decides
a lead case. A plenary grant here is less likely. If the Court did take the
case outright, it would take the QP as written: whether a Rule 23(b)(3) class
may be certified when some members lack Article III injury. The Court would
most likely resolve it through Rule 23(b)(3) predominance rather than a
categorical Article III rule.

**Separate writing on denial:** probably none. A statement or dissent from
Justice Kavanaugh, perhaps joined by Justice Alito or Justice Thomas, is
possible but unlikely, since the Court has a pending alternative vehicle.
