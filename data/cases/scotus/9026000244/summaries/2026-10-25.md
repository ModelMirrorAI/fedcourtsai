## In brief

Kroger asks the Court to decide whether a class action can be certified when some class members were never actually injured.

## What happened

Customers sued Kroger's Fred Meyer stores, claiming the company wrongly charged 10-cent bottle deposits on orange juice cartons not covered by Oregon's bottle deposit law. A federal magistrate judge recommended certifying a class of affected customers, and the district court agreed over Kroger's objection that over a thousand class members had already received refunds and so had no remaining injury. The Ninth Circuit affirmed, holding that a class can include uninjured members as long as the named plaintiffs have standing and common issues still predominate.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked whether a class action for money damages can go forward when some people in the class never suffered the harm being sued over. Kroger argues that courts cannot aggregate claims this way, and that sorting out who was actually injured would require so many individual inquiries that it should block class treatment altogether. The customers oppose this view.

## Where it stands

The petition was distributed for the Court's September 28, 2026 conference. The Court then asked the customers to respond, and that response deadline has since been extended twice, most recently to December 4, 2026. No decision on whether to hear the case has been recorded.

## What each outcome would mean

Kroger, which lost in the lower courts, is asking the Court to take up the case and reverse. If the Court grants review, it would hear full arguments and decide the legal question itself, without yet determining who wins. If the Court denies review, the Ninth Circuit's decision stands, leaving the certified class of customers intact and allowing their lawsuit against Kroger to continue in the lower courts.
