## In brief

Kroger asks the Court to decide whether a class action can include members who suffered no injury, after the Ninth Circuit approved certifying such a class.

## What happened

Customers Elisha Solano, Kathleen Zach, and Denise Conroy sued Kroger's Fred Meyer stores, claiming Fred Meyer wrongly charged 10-cent bottle deposits on orange juice containers that were not eligible for deposits under Oregon's bottle law. A federal district court certified a class of affected customers, and the Ninth Circuit affirmed. Fred Meyer argued that because it had already refunded deposits to more than 1,275 class members, those customers had no injury and could not properly be included, but the Ninth Circuit disagreed, following its own precedent.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked whether a class action for money damages may go forward when some people in the class have not been harmed at all, or whether a lawsuit covering a group that includes unharmed members cannot proceed as a class action under federal court rules.

## Where it stands

Kroger's petition has been distributed for conference, and the Court asked the customers to respond; that response deadline has since been extended twice, most recently to December 4, 2026. The petition has not yet been granted or denied.

## What each outcome would mean

Kroger, which lost in the lower courts, is asking the Court to take up the case and reverse. If the Court grants the petition, it would hear full arguments and decide whether classes with uninjured members are allowed, deciding nothing yet about who wins. If the Court denies the petition, the Ninth Circuit's ruling stands, and the certified class, including members who received refunds, continues toward trial under Solano's win on this issue.
