## What happened

In May 2020, during civil unrest in Phoenix, police officer Eric Gomez fired a less-lethal foam projectile that struck David Saccoccio in the arm, breaking a bone, as Saccoccio ran with a group toward officers conducting a traffic stop, ignored commands to stop, and climbed a fence into a residential yard after curfew. Saccoccio sued Gomez for excessive force. A district court granted Gomez summary judgment, ruling the officer had qualified immunity, a legal protection shielding officials from suit unless they violated clearly established rights. The Ninth Circuit reversed, holding that two prior appeals court decisions, read together, clearly established that Gomez's conduct was unlawful even though neither case closely matched the facts here. The Ninth Circuit denied rehearing, and Gomez asked the Supreme Court to take the case.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked whether it is proper for a lower court to combine general principles from two earlier cases with different facts to conclude that an officer's conduct was clearly against the law, or whether courts must instead point to a case closely resembling the specific situation the officer faced before stripping away that legal protection.

## Where it stands

The petition was distributed for conference, and the Court later asked the respondent to file a response, which was submitted along with the petitioner's reply. The petition awaits the Court's decision on whether to grant review.
