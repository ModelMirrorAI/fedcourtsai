## In brief

A police officer asks the Court to review a ruling that let a man's excessive-force lawsuit over a less-lethal round proceed against him.

## What happened

During 2020 protests in Phoenix, Officer Eric Gomez fired a foam-and-chemical-irritant round that struck David Saccoccio's arm as Saccoccio, defying a curfew and police commands, tried to climb a fence into a yard. Saccoccio sued Gomez for excessive force. A federal district court ruled for Gomez, finding he had qualified immunity, a legal protection shielding officials from suit unless they violated clearly established law. The Ninth Circuit reversed, holding that two prior court decisions, each addressing different facts, could be combined to show Gomez should have known his conduct was unlawful. The Ninth Circuit denied further review.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked whether it is proper for a lower court to combine general lessons from two earlier cases, each involving different facts, to conclude that an officer's specific conduct was already clearly forbidden, or whether courts must instead point to a prior case closely resembling the actual situation the officer faced.
