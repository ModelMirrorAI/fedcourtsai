## In brief

The Supreme Court declined to hear a police officer's bid to revive his qualified immunity defense against a man he shot with a less-lethal round during 2020 protests.

## What happened

During curfew-related unrest in Phoenix in May 2020, Officer Eric Gomez shot David Saccoccio with a less-lethal round as Saccoccio tried to climb a fence away from police, breaking his arm. Saccoccio sued, claiming excessive force. The district court granted Gomez qualified immunity, a legal protection that shields officials from lawsuits unless they violated clearly established rights. The Ninth Circuit reversed, ruling that two of its prior decisions, read together, clearly established that Saccoccio's rights were violated. Gomez then asked the Supreme Court to review that ruling.

## What the Court is being asked

The case asked whether appeals courts may combine general principles from two different prior cases, each involving different facts, to conclude that a right was "clearly established" for qualified immunity purposes, or whether they must instead point to a single closely similar prior case before holding an officer should have known his conduct was unlawful.

## Where it stands

The Supreme Court denied the petition on October 19, 2026, after considering it at conferences in September and October.

## What each outcome would mean

Gomez, the officer, asked the Court to take up the case and overturn the Ninth Circuit's ruling against him. By denying review, the Court leaves the Ninth Circuit's decision in place, meaning Saccoccio's excessive-force lawsuit against Gomez can continue in the lower courts. The denial does not mean the Supreme Court agreed with the Ninth Circuit's reasoning; it simply leaves that ruling undisturbed and decides nothing about who should ultimately win the underlying case.
