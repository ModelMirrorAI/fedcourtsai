## In brief

The Supreme Court declined to hear a police officer's bid to revive his qualified immunity defense in an excessive force lawsuit over a protest-related shooting.

## What happened

David Saccoccio was shot in the arm with a less-lethal foam round by Phoenix police officer Eric Gomez during a 2020 curfew-related protest, fracturing a bone that required surgery. Saccoccio sued Gomez for excessive force. A federal district court ruled for Gomez, finding he was entitled to qualified immunity, a legal protection that shields officials from lawsuits unless they violated clearly established law. The Ninth Circuit reversed, holding that two of its prior rulings, read together, had already made clear that shooting someone in Saccoccio's situation was unlawful, so Gomez was not immune from suit.

## What the Court is being asked

The question is whether it was proper for the appeals court to combine two prior cases, each only partly similar to this one, to establish that the officer's conduct was clearly illegal, or whether courts must instead point to a single closely matching precedent before stripping an officer of immunity.

## Where it stands

The petition was distributed for conference twice, after briefing that included a request for the Court's views and full opposition and reply briefs. On October 19, 2026, the Court denied the petition.

## What each outcome would mean

Gomez, the officer, asked the Court to take up the case. The denial leaves the Ninth Circuit's ruling in place, meaning Gomez does not get immunity at this stage and Saccoccio's excessive force lawsuit against him can continue in the lower courts. A grant would have meant the Court would hear full arguments and decide the legal question itself, without determining in advance which side would win.
