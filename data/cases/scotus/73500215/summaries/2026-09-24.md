## What happened

In May 2020, during civil unrest in Phoenix, police officer Eric Gomez shot David Saccoccio in the arm with a less-lethal foam projectile as Saccoccio, defying an emergency curfew and police commands to stop, tried to climb a fence into a residential yard. Saccoccio sued Gomez for excessive force. A federal district court granted Gomez summary judgment, ruling that he was protected by qualified immunity, a legal shield that protects officials from lawsuits unless they violated rights that were "clearly established" at the time. The Ninth Circuit Court of Appeals reversed, holding that two prior appeals court rulings, each addressing different facts, together put Gomez on notice that his conduct was unlawful. The full appeals court declined to rehear the case.

## What the Court is being asked

Gomez is asking the Court to decide whether appeals courts may combine general lessons from two earlier cases, each involving different facts, to conclude that an officer's specific conduct was already clearly forbidden. Gomez argues the rule requires courts to compare an officer's conduct to closely similar past cases, not blend broader principles from separate, factually different ones.

## Where it stands

The petition has been distributed for conference, and the Court later asked for a response from Saccoccio, which was filed along with Gomez's reply. The docket shows no action yet on whether the Court will hear the case.
