## In brief

A police officer asks the Court to review a ruling that let a man's excessive-force lawsuit over a less-lethal round proceed.

## What happened

During 2020 curfew unrest in Phoenix, Officer Eric Gomez fired a less-lethal foam round that struck David Saccoccio's arm as Saccoccio, defying curfew and police commands, climbed a fence into a yard while officers nearby detained others. Saccoccio sued Gomez for excessive force. A federal district court granted Gomez summary judgment, ruling Gomez had qualified immunity, a legal protection that shields officials from suit unless they violated a right that was "clearly established" at the time. The Ninth Circuit reversed, holding that two prior appeals court rulings, read together, clearly established that Gomez's conduct was unlawful even though the court acknowledged neither prior case closely matched these facts.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked whether it is proper for a lower court to combine general principles from two prior cases that each differ substantially from the facts at hand to conclude that an officer's conduct was clearly unlawful, or whether courts must instead point to a closely similar past case to strip an officer of immunity.

## Where it stands

The petition was distributed for conference, the Court asked for a response from Saccoccio, briefing concluded with Gomez's reply, and the case was distributed for a second conference. No order on the petition has been recorded since.

## What each outcome would mean

Gomez, the officer, asked the Court to take the case. If the Court grants review, it will hear full arguments and decide the legal question itself, deciding nothing yet about who wins. If it denies review, the Ninth Circuit's ruling stands, meaning Saccoccio's lawsuit against Gomez continues in the lower courts and Gomez does not get immunity at this stage. A grant does not mean Gomez will ultimately prevail, and a denial does not mean the Ninth Circuit was correct, only that its decision remains in place.
