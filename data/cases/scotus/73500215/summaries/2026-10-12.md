## In brief

A police officer asks the Court to decide whether lower courts may combine two different prior cases to establish that his use of force was clearly unlawful.

## What happened

During protests and a curfew in Phoenix in May 2020, Officer Eric Gomez shot David Saccoccio with a less-lethal round as Saccoccio, part of a group police were pursuing, climbed a fence into a residential yard. The round broke a bone in Saccoccio's arm. Saccoccio sued Gomez for excessive force. A district court granted Gomez summary judgment, ruling he had qualified immunity, a legal protection that shields officials from suits unless they violated clearly established rights. The Ninth Circuit reversed, holding that two of its prior rulings, read together, clearly established that Gomez's conduct was unlawful. The full Ninth Circuit declined to rehear the case.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked whether it is proper for a court to combine general principles drawn from two cases with different facts to conclude that a right was clearly established, or whether courts must instead point to a single closely similar prior case before holding that an officer should have known his specific conduct was unlawful.

## Where it stands

The petition has been fully briefed, including a brief opposing review and a reply, and was distributed for the Court's conference on October 16, 2026. The docket does not yet show any action taken at that conference.

## What each outcome would mean

Gomez, the officer, asked the Court to take the case. If the Court grants review, it will hear full arguments on whether the Ninth Circuit used the right method to find clearly established law, without yet deciding who wins. If it denies review, the Ninth Circuit's ruling against Gomez stands, and Saccoccio's lawsuit continues in the lower courts. A grant does not mean the Court agrees with Gomez, and a denial does not mean the Ninth Circuit's reasoning was correct.
