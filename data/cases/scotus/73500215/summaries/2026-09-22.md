## What happened

In May 2020, during a night of curfew-related unrest in Phoenix, police officer Eric Gomez fired a less-lethal foam projectile that struck David Saccoccio's arm as Saccoccio, part of a group running toward officers, tried to climb a fence into a residential yard after ignoring commands to stop. Saccoccio sued Gomez for excessive force. A federal district court granted Gomez summary judgment, ruling he was protected by qualified immunity, a legal shield that protects officials from liability unless they violated clearly established rights. The Ninth Circuit reversed, holding that two prior appeals court decisions, read together, clearly established that Gomez's conduct was unlawful, even though Gomez argues neither prior case closely resembled his situation. The Ninth Circuit denied further rehearing, and Gomez asked the Supreme Court to take the case.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked whether it is proper for a lower court to combine general lessons from two earlier cases, each involving quite different facts, to conclude that an officer's specific conduct was already clearly forbidden. Gomez argues that officials should only be held liable when past cases closely resembling their situation gave clear warning that the conduct was unlawful, and that stitching together unrelated precedents does not provide that kind of fair notice.

## Where it stands

The respondent waived his right to respond, but the Court later requested a response, which was filed after an extension; the case was distributed for the Court's conference on September 28, 2026, where the justices will decide whether to take up the case.
