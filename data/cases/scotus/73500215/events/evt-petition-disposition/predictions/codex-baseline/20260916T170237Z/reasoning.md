## Why 16%, rather than the 5.12% anchor

The strongest upward adjustment is the Court's affirmative response request following a waiver. It demonstrates attention beyond passive distribution, although it neither implies four votes nor supplies a published conditional grant rate. The other upward adjustment is a plausible summary-correction theory on the clearly-established-law prong of qualified immunity. The petition argues that combining Nelson and Deorle at an abstract level fails to give notice about a specific use of an impact round during an attempted detention. That is a more concrete request for enforcement of existing precedent than an invitation to reconsider qualified immunity generally. See petition pp. 8–16 and question presented.

Zorn, whose March 23, 2026 per curiam opinion I checked through MCP, confirms a live summary-reversal route in a protest-related qualified-immunity case. Its majority insists on precedent sufficiently specific to the officer's conduct; its actual force involved a wristlock after repeated warnings, not an impact projectile. It supports the availability of intervention, not a finding that Gomez necessarily wins. Zorn postdates the Ninth Circuit's January decision and February rehearing denial but predates this petition. It could support reconsideration, although it principally reiterates an existing standard and does not mechanically dictate a GVR here.

Substantial counterweights keep denial the clear modal result. The memorandum is unpublished and nonprecedential, the panel was unanimous, and no judge requested an en banc vote (petition App. 1a–6a). The petition identifies no developed inter-circuit conflict over reading precedents together. The opposition points out that the petition itself allows multi-case reasoning in some circumstances and argues that Nelson independently supplies notice, making the abstract question an imperfect vehicle. See opposition pp. 6–8.

Most importantly, the parties disagree about the facts that must control summary judgment. The appended panel memorandum describes evidence that Gomez no longer perceived a threat to officers, had not seen Saccoccio engage in violence, and knew the munition could cause serious injury. It treats suspected crimes as nonviolent and the evasion as brief. The opposition develops those points and disputes the petition's descriptions of continued noncompliance and fence climbing; it also distinguishes intended aim from the projectile's actual strike and injury risk. See petition App. 2a–5a and opposition pp. 8–13. I do not adopt either party's contested narrative as an adjudicated fact. This dispute makes a clean abstract ruling less attractive and gives the Court a reason not to use summary reversal despite the qualified-immunity theme.

The move from 5.12% to 16% is a judgmental adjustment for the response call and correction potential, tempered by those vehicle problems; it is not a fitted estimate or a frequency measured from analogous petitions. My chief uncertainty is whether the Court treats the panel's use of Nelson and Deorle as an obvious specificity error even after accepting the plaintiff-favorable factual view. The forecast is materially less confident about that legal assessment than about denial being the most likely disposition.
