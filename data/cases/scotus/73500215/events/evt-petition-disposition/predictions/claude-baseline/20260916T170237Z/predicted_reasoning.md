# Forecast: what the Court will do with No. 25-1314, Gomez v. Saccoccio

**Bottom line.** The petition is most likely denied without opinion (P(any grant) = 0.13). If the Court acts, it acts summarily: a per curiam reversal of the Ninth Circuit on the clearly-established prong in the mold of *Kisela v. Hughes*, *Rivas-Villegas v. Cortesluna* and this Term's *Zorn v. Linton*, or a GVR in light of *Zorn*. Plenary review is very unlikely.

## Distributions and relists

The snapshot shows **one** distribution (June 24, 2026, for the September 28, 2026 conference), entered after the respondent waived. The Court then **requested a response** on July 14, the respondent obtained an extension, and the brief in opposition was filed September 14. That sequence means the petition will not be decided off the September 28 list: once the reply period runs, the Clerk redistributes it for a later conference. So I expect **at least one further distribution** (roughly 0.97), most likely for a conference in mid- to late October 2026. Beyond that first redistribution, one further relist is plausible (perhaps 35–40%) because a summary reversal or a dissent takes a chamber a conference or two to write; two or more further relists past the redistribution would signal that a per curiam is being drafted.

## CVSG

No call for the views of the Solicitor General. This is a Section 1983 suit between a municipal police officer and a private protester; no federal statute, program, or party is implicated, and the Court does not invite the SG on garden-variety qualified-immunity petitions. P ≈ 0.01.

## Which question the Court would take

If the Court acts, it does not take the question presented as written and set it for argument. It would treat the case as error correction: whether *Nelson v. City of Davis* and *Deorle v. Rutherford*, read together, placed the unlawfulness of firing a single 40-mm OC impact round at a fleeing curfew violator "beyond debate." A per curiam would decide only the clearly-established prong, which is the only holding the petition challenges, and would leave the panel's prong-one holding (a jury question on the constitutional violation) undisturbed.

## Summary disposition versus plenary review

Conditional on any grant, the summary route is far the likelier: P(disposed of in the cert order | grant) ≈ 0.85. The petition alleges no circuit split and asks the Court to reinstate the district court's judgment, which is the summary-reversal pitch. A GVR "in light of *Zorn v. Linton*" is a live, cheap alternative: *Zorn* (decided March 23, 2026) postdates both the Ninth Circuit's January 15, 2026 memorandum and its February 23, 2026 denial of rehearing, and it restates the specificity requirement in a protest-arrest setting. I put roughly 0.10 on a per curiam reversal, 0.02 on a GVR, and 0.01 on a plenary grant.

## Dissent from denial

If the Court denies, I expect it to deny silently; P(any dissent or statement respecting denial | denial) ≈ 0.08. The Court's practice in officer-side qualified-immunity petitions is binary: summary reversal or a bare denial. A short statement from Justice Thomas or Justice Alito noting the Ninth Circuit's continued reliance on *Deorle* is the most plausible exception. A dissent from a summary reversal, should one issue, would come from Justice Sotomayor or Justice Jackson, but that is not a denial-side event.

## If granted (conditional)

If the Court summarily reverses, the ground would be that *Nelson* (pepperballs fired into a non-dispersing party crowd) and *Deorle* (a beanbag round fired at an unarmed, emotionally disturbed man standing still) are too factually distinct from a curfew violator scaling a residential fence during a night of unrest, and that stitching general propositions from the two together is the "high level of generality" *Kisela* and *Sheehan* already condemned in this circuit's use of *Deorle*. The Court would emphasize the officer's seconds-long decision window and the ongoing detention of the truck occupants, and would say nothing about whether the force was constitutional.
