## Adjustments up (from ~5% to ~13%)

1. **The Court called for a response after a waiver.** On June 8 the respondent waived; on July 14 the Court entered "Response Requested." A call for a response is an affirmative act by at least one chamber and is the single strongest signal on this docket. The salience band does not encode it (`sal-v4` keys on relists, CVSG, and caption class), so the anchor understates this petition's position. From general knowledge, and not from any committed statpack cut, paid petitions that draw a call for response are granted at a rate several times the paid baseline, though still a minority of them. This is the bulk of my upward move.
2. **The genre.** A Phoenix officer asks the Court to reverse the Ninth Circuit on the clearly-established prong, arguing the panel combined general principles from two dissimilar cases (*Nelson*, *Deorle*). That is precisely the pattern the Court has summarily corrected in this circuit before (*Sheehan* 2015 and *Kisela* 2018 both rebuked the Ninth Circuit's use of *Deorle*; *Emmons* 2019; *Rivas-Villegas* 2021). And the appetite is current: *Zorn v. Linton*, No. 25-297 (per curiam, March 23, 2026), summarily reversed the Second Circuit's denial of qualified immunity to an officer who removed a passively resisting protester, applying exactly the "could have read the precedent beforehand and known it proscribed their specific conduct" formulation the petition leans on. *Zorn* also postdates the Ninth Circuit's judgment here, which makes a GVR a cheap route the Court did not have in, say, OT2023.
3. **Posture is clean.** Appeal from a final judgment, both prongs decided, one narrow QP, no jurisdictional wrinkle. The petition asks for reinstatement of the district court's judgment, which is what a per curiam would do.

## Adjustments down (why not 0.20 or higher)

1. **The facts are less officer-friendly than the Court's summary-reversal cases.** In *Kisela*, *Rivas-Villegas*, *Mullenix*, *White v. Pauly* and *Zorn*, the officer faced a knife, a firearm, a fleeing armed suspect, or gave repeated warnings. Here, on the summary-judgment record read for the plaintiff: the officer was about 100 feet away, gave no warning himself, admitted he did not fear for officer safety and did not suspect rioting, the target was moving away, and the round fractured the plaintiff's forearm and required surgery with a plate. The BIO presses these admissions and the record dispute over whether the plaintiff was even on the fence when shot (citing *Tolan v. Cotton*). The Court dislikes correcting a panel where the "context" the petitioner supplies is itself disputed.
2. **The precedent is closer than the petition allows.** *Nelson* involved OC projectiles fired at non-threatening people suspected at most of misdemeanors in a disorderly setting, and it held the potential for injury is judged by the round's capacity for harm, not where it was aimed. The BIO plausibly argues *Nelson* alone suffices, so the "two cases read together" framing that the QP is built on may not be load-bearing.
3. **Weak external markers.** Unpublished, unanimous memorandum; rehearing en banc denied with no judge calling for a vote; no dissent anywhere below; no circuit split alleged; no amici; petitioner's counsel are Arizona defense counsel and the City Attorney, not Supreme Court specialists; the petition runs about 17 pages of argument. Respondent is represented by Public Citizen Litigation Group, whose BIO is tight and framed around fact disputes.
4. **Selection.** Most petitions that draw a call for response are still denied, and officer-side QI petitions from the Ninth Circuit are a crowded field. The Court has issued roughly one or two QI summary reversals a Term in recent years against dozens of such petitions.

Net: I place the grant family at 0.13, composed of about 0.10 per curiam reversal, 0.02 GVR in light of *Zorn*, and 0.01 plenary grant. Hence `predicted_disposition: denied` and `granted: 0`.

- **summary-disposition-route 0.85 (conditional on grant).** No split, error-correction framing, an intervening per curiam (*Zorn*) available for a GVR. The prior-Terms cert-order share of grants in the statpack runs 30–59% where the label is populated; this petition's shape puts it well above that population share.
- **dissent-from-denial 0.08 (conditional on denial).** Officer-side QI denials are almost always silent. I give a small weight to a Thomas or Alito statement about the Ninth Circuit's *Deorle* line.
- **big_case_score 0.25.** Protest-policing and less-lethal munitions have some public salience, and a summary reversal would draw coverage, but the case is unpublished and fact-bound and would make no new law.
