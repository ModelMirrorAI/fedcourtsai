# Forecast: Radiall USA, Inc. v. Ramadei, No. 26-436 (cert petition)

**Bottom line: the petition will be denied, most likely at or shortly after the November 6, 2026 conference.** P(any grant) = 0.06.

## Relists

The docket shows **one distribution** (October 7, 2026, for the Conference of November 6, 2026). Respondent has waived his response, so the petition goes to that conference on the petition alone.

I put the chance that the docket records **at least one further distribution** at **0.40**. Almost all of that is the chance the Court calls for a response (roughly 0.35): the petition is professionally written by Steptoe, presents an acknowledged circuit split, and the waiver leaves the Court without any adversarial account of the vehicle. If a response is called for, the petition comes off the November 6 calendar and is re-distributed after the brief in opposition and reply arrive, probably for a January or February 2027 conference. Absent a call for a response, a straight relist off a single conference is unlikely (perhaps 0.05); this is not a petition the Court would hold for a pending argued case, because nothing on the merits docket turns on FMLA causation. If it is relisted at all, expect one or two distributions, not a long string.

## Call for the views of the Solicitor General

**Unlikely: 0.05.** The federal interest is real but indirect. The question is whether courts should keep deferring to the Department of Labor's "negative factor" regulation, 29 C.F.R. 825.220(c), and the Department filed as amicus on the plaintiff's side in the Second Circuit's recent Haran case, so the government has a view. But the United States is not a party, the Court would reach a CVSG only after a called-for response showed sustained interest, and the petition's vehicle problems (below) make the Court more likely to simply deny than to invest a CVSG in it.

## Which question, if any

If the Court granted, it would take **Question 1 as written**, the FMLA retaliation causation standard, and treat Question 2 (whether the Second Circuit could refuse to reach the issue on waiver and forfeiture grounds) as a threshold objection to be disposed of in the opinion rather than as a stand-alone question. A grant limited to Question 2 alone is the least likely grant shape: the Court leaves preservation rulings to the courts of appeals' discretion and would not take a civil preservation dispute from an unpublished summary order on its own.

## Summary disposition versus plenary review

**Conditional on a grant, P(disposed of in the cert order) = 0.12.** There is no intervening decision to GVR in light of; Loper Bright predates the Second Circuit's March 2026 order, and the panel already considered it. The only summary route is a per curiam reversal on Question 2 of the Davis v. United States kind, holding that a party need not have asked the district court to disobey binding circuit precedent in order to preserve a challenge to that precedent. That is conceivable but rare in a civil case, and the panel's alternative invited-error ground (petitioner proposed the motivating-factor instruction itself) gives the Court a reason not to intervene summarily. A grant here would far more likely be plenary, with the causation question argued.

## If denied: separate writings

**P(a dissent from or statement respecting denial | denial) = 0.04.** The FMLA causation split is the kind of post-Loper Bright question Justice Gorsuch or Justice Thomas might flag in a statement, but neither has written at the cert stage on FMLA causation, the Court denied the same split in Lapham v. Walgreen in October 2024 without any noted writing, and a denial here will read as a vehicle denial rather than a merits judgment. Expect a bare denial.

## Conditional merits note

If the Court reached Question 1, the likely outcome is reversal: the Nassar, Gross, and Comcast line treats but-for causation as the default absent contrary statutory text, Loper Bright removes the only basis the Second and Third Circuits gave for the motivating-factor standard, and the FMLA's retaliation provisions use "for" and "because" rather than "motivating factor." That is a forecast about an opinion a denial will never produce, and it is stated here only because it bears on whether the Court sees the split as worth resolving in this vehicle.
