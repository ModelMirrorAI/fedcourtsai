## In brief

Radiall asks the Court to decide what employees must prove to win retaliation claims under a federal family and medical leave law, after lower courts split on the standard.

## What happened

Gary Ramadei sued his former employer, Radiall USA, after it eliminated his position following medical leave. A jury found Radiall liable under the Family and Medical Leave Act and awarded over $770,000 in damages, using a standard requiring only that his leave was a motivating factor in his firing. Radiall appealed, arguing that a more demanding standard should apply after the Supreme Court eliminated a legal doctrine requiring courts to defer to certain agency interpretations. The Second Circuit affirmed, holding Radiall had waived or forfeited that argument and otherwise found no clear error.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked whether employees bringing this kind of retaliation claim must show their protected leave was the decisive reason for their firing, or may instead rely on a lower standard, requiring only that leave played some role, which rests on deference to a labor agency's regulation. The Court is also asked whether an appeals court may decline to rule on a purely legal argument about which standard applies when the party raised it at its first real opportunity, after a change in the law made the argument available only once the case was on appeal.

## Where it stands

The petition was distributed for the Court's November 6, 2026 conference after the respondent waived his right to respond. An amicus brief supporting Radiall was filed shortly before that conference. The docket shows no decision yet on whether the Court will hear the case.

## What each outcome would mean

Radiall, which lost below, is asking the Court to take up the case and reverse. If the Court grants review, it would hear full arguments and decide the legal questions itself, deciding nothing yet about who wins. If it denies review, the Second Circuit's ruling stands, leaving Ramadei's jury verdict and the lower causation standard in place in that circuit. Either way, the decision would not by itself resolve whether Radiall or Ramadei ultimately prevails unless the Court goes on to rule on the merits.
