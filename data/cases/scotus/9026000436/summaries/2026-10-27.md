## In brief

Radiall asks the Court to decide what employees must prove to win FMLA retaliation claims, after the lower court declined to revisit its causation standard.

## What happened

Gary Ramadei sued his former employer, Radiall USA, claiming it fired him in retaliation for taking medical leave, among other claims. A jury found for Ramadei on the FMLA claim, applying a standard requiring only that his leave was a "motivating factor" in his firing, and awarded him damages later increased to over $770,000 with fees. After trial, the Supreme Court overruled a legal doctrine called Chevron that required courts to defer to agency interpretations of ambiguous statutes. Radiall argued on appeal that this change meant a stricter "but-for causation" standard should apply instead. The Second Circuit affirmed the judgment without deciding that question, holding Radiall had waived or forfeited the argument.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked to decide what an employee must show to prove retaliation under the federal family and medical leave law: whether it is enough that taking leave was one factor among others in an employer's adverse decision, or whether the employee must show the leave was the actual deciding cause. The Court is also asked whether an appeals court may refuse to decide such a purely legal question on the ground that a party failed to raise it earlier, when doing so would have been pointless because existing circuit rulings foreclosed the argument until the law changed.

## Where it stands

Radiall's petition was filed in September 2026 after an extension of time. Ramadei waived his right to respond, and the case was distributed for the Court's November 6, 2026 conference. The docket does not show any action since then.

## What each outcome would mean

Radiall, which lost below, is asking the Court to take up the case and reverse. If the Court grants review, it would hear full arguments on the causation standard and the preservation question, deciding neither until it rules. If the Court denies review, the Second Circuit's decision affirming the judgment for Ramadei, including the damages award, stands, and the case ends in Ramadei's favor.
