## In brief

Radiall asks the Court to decide what employees must prove to win family-leave retaliation claims, after the Second Circuit refused to revisit its causation standard.

## What happened

Gary Ramadei sued his former employer, Radiall USA, claiming it fired him in retaliation for taking medical leave under the Family and Medical Leave Act. A jury agreed, awarding damages that grew to over $770,000 with fees. The district court and jury had applied a standard requiring only that leave be a "motivating factor" in the firing, following existing circuit precedent. After trial, the Supreme Court eliminated the legal doctrine underlying that precedent. On appeal, Radiall argued the court should now require proof that leave was the actual deciding cause, but the Second Circuit declined to address the argument, affirming the judgment.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked two questions. First, whether the family-leave law requires employees to prove their protected leave was the actual deciding cause of their firing, rather than merely one factor among others, and whether courts may instead follow a more lenient standard set by federal labor regulations. Second, whether an appeals court may refuse to decide a purely legal question about outdated precedent on the grounds that a party failed to raise it earlier, when raising it earlier would have been pointless because existing law already foreclosed the argument.

## Where it stands

The petition was filed in September 2026, the respondent waived his right to respond, and the case was distributed for the Court's November 6, 2026 conference. No decision on whether to hear the case has yet been recorded.

## What each outcome would mean

Radiall, which lost in the lower courts, is asking the Court to take up the case. If the Court grants the petition, it would hear full arguments and decide the causation standard and the preservation question itself, without yet deciding who wins. If it denies the petition, the Second Circuit's ruling stands, leaving Ramadei's judgment against Radiall in place and leaving the lenient causation standard intact in that circuit.
