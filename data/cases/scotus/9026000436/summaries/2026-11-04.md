## In brief

Radiall asks the Court to decide what employees must prove to win family-leave retaliation claims, after the appeals court declined to revisit its standard.

## What happened

Gary Ramadei sued his former employer, Radiall USA, for firing him, he said, in retaliation for taking medical leave under the Family and Medical Leave Act. A jury agreed and awarded him damages, later totaling over $770,000. The trial court had told the jury that Ramadei only needed to show his leave was "a motivating factor" in his firing, following existing appeals-court precedent. After trial, the Supreme Court eliminated a legal doctrine that precedent had relied on. On appeal, Radiall argued the standard should be revisited, but the Second Circuit affirmed, saying Radiall had not properly preserved that argument.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked to decide what an employee must show to prove that taking family or medical leave caused their firing or other punishment: whether it must have been the decisive reason, or merely one factor among others. The Court is also asked whether an appeals court may refuse to rule on a purely legal argument because a party did not raise it earlier, when raising it earlier would have been pointless under the law as it stood at the time.

## Where it stands

The petition was distributed for the Court's November 6, 2026 conference, after the respondent waived his right to respond and amicus briefs were filed supporting Radiall. The docket does not yet show any decision on the petition.

## What each outcome would mean

Radiall, which lost below, is asking the Court to take up the case and reverse. If the Court grants the petition, it would hear the case fully and decide the causation standard and preservation question itself, without deciding in advance who wins. If it denies the petition, the Second Circuit's ruling stands, leaving Ramadei's judgment against Radiall in place and leaving the lower-causation standard in effect in that circuit.
