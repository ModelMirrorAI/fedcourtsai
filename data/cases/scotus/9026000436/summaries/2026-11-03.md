## In brief

A company asks the Court to decide what employees must prove to win a retaliation claim under the federal medical-leave law.

## What happened

Gary Ramadei sued his former employer, Radiall USA, claiming Radiall fired him in retaliation for taking medical leave under the Family and Medical Leave Act. A jury agreed and awarded him damages, which the district court increased to more than $770,000. On appeal, Radiall argued that a recent Supreme Court decision undercut the lower causation standard the trial court had used, and asked the Second Circuit to adopt a stricter standard. The Second Circuit affirmed the judgment, holding Radiall had waived or forfeited that argument and finding no plain error.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked two things. First, whether employees bringing these retaliation claims must show their leave was the determining reason for their firing, or whether a lesser standard -- that leave merely played a negative or contributing role -- is enough. Second, whether an appeals court may decline to rule on a purely legal argument about outdated precedent, on the ground that a party should have raised it earlier, when a Supreme Court decision changed the legal landscape only after the trial had already concluded.

## Where it stands

Radiall filed its petition on September 28, 2026. Ramadei waived his right to respond, and the petition was distributed for the Court's November 6, 2026 conference. Two amicus briefs supporting Radiall have been filed. The docket shows no action yet on the petition since that conference.

## What each outcome would mean

Radiall, which lost in the lower courts, is asking the Court to take up the case. If the Court grants review, it will hear full arguments and decide the causation question and the preservation question itself, without yet deciding who wins. If the Court denies review, the Second Circuit's ruling stands, leaving Ramadei's judgment against Radiall in place and leaving the lower causation standard applicable in the Second Circuit.
