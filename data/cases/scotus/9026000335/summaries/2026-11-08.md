## In brief

Texas asks the Court to decide whether a federal law letting a private horseracing authority write industry rules improperly hands lawmaking power to a private entity.

## What happened

Congress created the Horseracing Integrity and Safety Authority, a private nonprofit, to write and help enforce nationwide rules for Thoroughbred racing, subject to Federal Trade Commission oversight. Texas and others sued, arguing this setup unconstitutionally delegates government power to a private group. The Fifth Circuit held that the Authority's enforcement powers were unconstitutional but that its rulemaking powers, after a congressional amendment letting the FTC override Authority rules, were constitutional. Texas, along with other parties, petitioned for review of the rulemaking question, while the Authority and the federal government separately petitioned on the enforcement question.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked whether Congress gave too much lawmaking authority to a private organization by letting it write industry-wide rules that a federal agency must approve unless they conflict with the law, with the agency's only check being its own, separate rulemaking power to later change those rules.

## Where it stands

The petition has been fully briefed, including a response from the federal government and from the Authority, both opposing review, and a reply from Texas. The case was distributed for the Court's December 4, 2026 conference, and the docket shows no action since.

## What each outcome would mean

Texas, joined by other challengers, is asking the Court to overturn the part of the Fifth Circuit's ruling that upheld the Authority's rulemaking powers as constitutional. If the Court grants review, it would hear the case and decide whether that rulemaking structure is valid, deciding nothing in advance. If the Court denies review, the Fifth Circuit's decision upholding the rulemaking provisions stays in place, leaving the current system, including the FTC's override power, intact for this case. The federal government and the Authority, as respondents, both urge denial and instead ask the Court to grant separate petitions on the enforcement-powers question.
