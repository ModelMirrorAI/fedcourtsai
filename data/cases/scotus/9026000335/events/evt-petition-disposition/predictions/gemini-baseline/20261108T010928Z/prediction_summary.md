We predict that the Supreme Court is very likely to deny Texas's petition for review (86% chance of denial, or about a one-in-seven chance of being granted). In this case, Texas argues that the Horseracing Integrity and Safety Act unconstitutionally gives lawmaking power over the horseracing industry to a private nonprofit corporation rather than a government agency. While the Supreme Court is expected to hear a related appeal over the corporation's power to enforce rules and issue fines—an issue on which federal appeals courts disagree—every appeals court has agreed that Congress fixed the rulemaking issue in 2022 by giving the Federal Trade Commission full power to rewrite or cancel any rule. Because there is no disagreement among lower courts on the rulemaking question and both the federal government and the horseracing authority oppose adding it, the Court will likely turn down Texas's separate appeal.
