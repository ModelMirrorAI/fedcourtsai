# Rationale

## Call and information set

I assign **18% to any grant**, including a grant-vacate-remand or summary reversal, and predict **denial**. This is the cert-stage distribution moment, not a prediction of the separate enforcement petitions or of a merits judgment.

The baseline is `record/snapshots/2026-11-08.json`, read at the case-level path. It records one distribution, entered November 4 for the December 4, 2026 conference, no prior relist, no CVSG, and one Repole Stable amicus brief. The government's opposition is a party filing, not an invited amicus brief. `record/context.json` freezes forward mode, Term 2026, distribution count 1, band `state`, salience version `sal-v8`, and no cutoff. Although its amicus field is null, the brief is directly observable in the snapshot; I do not treat the null as zero.

I read `questions-presented.txt`, substantive portions of `petition.txt`, both filings combined in `brief-in-opposition.txt`, and `cert-reply.txt`. The question is whether HISA unconstitutionally delegates legislative authority to a private entity. Texas's petition does not present the Appointments Clause question independently advanced by Gulf Coast. `documents.json` reports readable opposition and reply text, and a truncated petition. Pipeline notes locate substantial omissions in the petition's appendix; the petition's argument and requested relief are available. No independent `decision-below.txt` or `lower-court-docket.txt` was provisioned. My assessment of the lower court's reasoning relies on the parties' accounts, rather than an independently retrieved lower-court opinion.

## Calibration

The starting point is the **frozen 0.23333333333333334 cert anchor** in context, not the whole-docket rate or the state's terminal-band rate. The committed `metrics/statpack.json` reproduces that anchor: the `state` reached-risk sets in the available prior-Term window, OT2017–OT2025, contain 98 grant-family outcomes over 420 denial-reweighted resolved petitions. The context specifies a ten-Term lookback; these are the prior Terms the pack supplies within it. OT2026 is excluded. The Markdown table and context both use `sal-v8`, so no vocabulary mismatch or caption-floor substitution is needed.

I adjust downward because the disagreement Texas identifies is substantially less concrete than the enforcement split. Its petition, pp. 27–29, and reply, pp. 8–10, contrast the tests and oversight arrangements in earlier Third, Fourth, Ninth, and D.C. Circuit cases. The federal opposition, pp. 2–3, answers that those cases concern different statutes, mostly upheld the challenged schemes, and include a vacated D.C. Circuit judgment. Both oppositions report agreement on amended HISA's rulemaking provisions. That is a serious obstacle to treating Texas's asserted methodological conflict as an outcome-determinative circuit split.

The federal respondents expressly recommend denial, rather than merely asking the Court to defer review. That recommendation is in their party opposition, not a CVSG response. The Authority's opposition, pp. 33–35, likewise asks the Court to grant review of enforcement in Nos. 26-199 and 26-201 while denying these cross-petitions. Texas's reply, pp. 1–8, makes a substantial contrary argument: mandatory consistency review plus the FTC's optional notice-and-comment rulemaking allegedly leaves actual policy control with the private Authority. The reply's answer to the facial-challenge objection and its distinctions from FINRA prevent me from discounting the petition to a routine low-single-digit chance. But a substantive constitutional argument is not itself evidence that four Justices will expand review beyond the enforcement issue.

I retain appreciable grant probability because these are connected challenges to one regulatory structure. The petition, pp. 2–3 and 26–32, argues that the power to make rules is logically antecedent to enforcing them. The reply, p. 11, renews that request. A lead enforcement decision could alter the oversight framework enough to produce reconsideration here, even without separate argument on Texas's question. This possibility offsets part, but not all, of the downward adjustment. The resulting 18% is a judgmental net adjustment of approximately 5.3 percentage points from the frozen anchor, not an estimated regression coefficient.

## Procedural claims and related litigation

The paid-segment terminal relist table in `metrics/statpack.md` reports grant-family shares of 1.8% for zero relists (resolved n=10,204), 13.5% for one (n=2,532), 41.2% for two (n=495), and 37.1% for three or more (n=482). These describe where petitions ended, not the chance of moving from today's one distribution to another. I therefore do not substitute the zero-relist row for the state anchor. The terminal CVSG cut gives 34.8% grant-family outcomes with a CVSG (n=164), versus 6.4% without (n=13,549). The one-amicus bucket has a 14.8% grant-family share (n=1,158). These overlapping, pooled cuts are context, not independent likelihood multipliers or strictly-prior anchors.

I assign **67% to at least one additional distribution**. Coordinating the cross-petitions with enforcement review or holding them for a lead ruling is more plausible than for an isolated splitless petition. The staged `related/26-332.json` is a `vided-amicus` relation with only one shared brief, not a Court-ordered linkage. Its identical lower-court number, parties' joint opposition, and matching December 4 distribution independently establish the substantive connection. It shows no grant or hold. Corpus lookups of Nos. 26-199 and 26-201 returned open rows, no grant or denial, and distribution counts of zero. Those rows establish no completed conference action and may lag; I do not invent a lead-case grant or hold.

I put **1% on a new CVSG**, because the federal respondents have already supplied their position. Conditional on a grant here, **55% is summary disposition**, chiefly a possible remand after a lead enforcement ruling, versus 45% for plenary review. No already-issued new intervening decision is identified that requires another immediate GVR; the prior Consumers' Research remand is historical posture, not an unresolved new event. This route estimate is deliberately uncertain: the pack warns that historical `granted`/`gvr` labels are not comparable across Terms.

Conditional on denial, I assign **8% to a noted dissent or statement**. I expect an unexplained denial. The petition and reply identify prior nondelegation interest among Alito, Thomas, and Gorsuch; they receive the largest, still small, unconditional order-writing probabilities. Those per-Justice probabilities are not votes or a categorical author allocation, and include possible writings on a grant as well as on denial.

## Limitations and significance

The broad denial-filtered corpus sample is not a matched constitutional-petition study, and I use it only as a check that state status and multiple distributions do not guarantee review. The web search and attempted official-opinion opening returned no usable content. CourtListener verified Consumers' Research's citation and June 27, 2025 date, but a document-text lookup was throttled; I rely on the provisioned briefs for its doctrinal application. Retrieval details, including these unsuccessful attempts, appear in `retrieval.md`. I did not retrieve this petition's live disposition, subsequent history, or another prediction, and I have no independently known outcome for this petition.

The **0.68 significance score** reflects national horseracing standards, displacement of state regulation, and possible implications for other supervised private rulemakers. It does not represent grant likelihood. The major uncertainties are whether the Court treats rulemaking as necessary to enforcement review, whether a lead opinion changes the applicable supervision test, and whether it disposes of the cross-petitions promptly or holds them into 2027.
