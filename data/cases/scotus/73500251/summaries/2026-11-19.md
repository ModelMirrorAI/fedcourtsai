## In brief

Roxane Marschner asked the Supreme Court to decide whether federal law bars state courts from enforcing a veteran's voluntary agreement to make up his ex-spouse's lost share of retirement pay.

## What happened

Roxane and Richard Marschner, a North Dakota National Guard veteran, divorced in 2016 under a settlement giving Roxane a share of Richard's military retirement pay, including amounts he might later waive to receive disability benefits. After Richard was medically retired in 2021 and began receiving disability-related pay instead of standard retirement pay, Roxane stopped receiving her share. A trial court ordered Richard to pay her equivalent spousal support, but the North Dakota Supreme Court reversed, holding that federal law bars enforcement of such indemnification arrangements even when both spouses agreed to them.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked whether a federal law governing how military retirement pay is treated in divorce prevents state courts from enforcing a provision that a veteran voluntarily agreed to in a divorce settlement, under which he promised to compensate his ex-spouse if his disability-related pay reduced her share of his retirement benefits.

## Where it stands

The Court granted certiorari on October 1, 2026. The case has been fully briefed and was set for oral argument on January 19, 2027; the petitioner's merits brief was filed November 13, 2026, and the case now awaits argument and decision.

## What each outcome would mean

Roxane Marschner, the petitioner, is asking the Court to reverse the North Dakota Supreme Court's ruling, which favored Richard Marschner. If the Court affirms that ruling, indemnification agreements like the one here would remain unenforceable, and Roxane would not recover the share of Richard's retirement-related pay she says she bargained for. If the Court reverses, such agreements could be enforced, potentially entitling Roxane to the payments and arrears a trial court had previously awarded her. If the Court vacates the decision, the case would return to the North Dakota courts for further proceedings without a final ruling on the question for either side.
