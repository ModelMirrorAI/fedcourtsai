## In brief

A divorced veteran's ex-wife asks the Court to decide whether her divorce settlement's agreement that he indemnify her for lost retirement pay can be enforced.

## What happened

Roxane and Richard Marschner divorced in 2016, agreeing Roxane would get a share of Richard's military retirement pay, including amounts later identified in follow-up orders. In 2021, Richard was medically retired and began receiving disability-based pay instead of longevity retirement pay, so Roxane received nothing. A North Dakota trial court ordered Richard to pay Roxane the equivalent amount as spousal support. The North Dakota Supreme Court reversed, holding that federal law bars state courts from enforcing such indemnification, even when the veteran agreed to it in the settlement.

## What the Court is being asked

A federal law governs how military retirement benefits are divided in divorce and excludes certain disability-linked pay from division. The Court is asked whether that law still blocks a state court from enforcing a divorce settlement in which a veteran voluntarily agreed to make up the difference to a former spouse if his disability-related pay reduced her share, or whether the law only blocks a court from imposing such a requirement on its own.

## Where it stands

The Court granted the petition on October 1, 2026, after the case was distributed for conference more than once. It has since set the case for argument on January 19, 2027, and granted a motion to dispense with printing the joint appendix; no decision has been issued yet.

## What each outcome would mean

Roxane, who lost below, asks the Court to reverse the North Dakota Supreme Court's decision. If the Court reverses, it would hold that the indemnification agreement can be enforced, favoring Roxane. If it affirms, Richard's win below stands, and the agreement remains unenforceable. If the Court vacates, it would send the case back to the North Dakota courts without deciding who should win.
