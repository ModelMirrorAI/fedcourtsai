## In brief

The Supreme Court agreed to decide whether federal law bars enforcing a divorced veteran's promise to share disability-linked retirement pay with his ex-spouse.

## What happened

Roxane and Richard Marschner divorced in 2016, agreeing Roxane would receive a share of Richard's military retirement, defined to include pay he might later waive for disability benefits. After Richard medically retired in 2021 and began receiving disability-related pay instead of standard retirement pay, Roxane stopped receiving her share. A North Dakota trial court ordered Richard to pay her equivalent spousal support, but the North Dakota Supreme Court reversed, holding that federal law bars enforcing such indemnification arrangements regardless of whether the veteran agreed to them voluntarily. Roxane asked the U.S. Supreme Court to review that ruling.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked whether a federal law governing military retirement benefits in divorce prevents state courts from enforcing a divorced couple's own agreement that a veteran will compensate a former spouse if his disability-related pay later reduces her expected share, even though the couple voluntarily agreed to that arrangement in their settlement.

## Where it stands

The petition was distributed for conference twice, and the respondent filed a brief opposing review after initially waiving a response, then the petitioner replied. The Court granted the petition on October 1, 2026; the case has not yet been briefed or argued on the merits.

## What each outcome would mean

Roxane asked the Court to take the case after losing below. Because the Court granted review, it will now decide the legal question itself after full briefing and argument. If the Court ultimately rules for Roxane, it would mean courts can enforce such voluntary indemnification agreements, potentially restoring her payments. If it rules for Richard, the North Dakota Supreme Court's decision barring enforcement would stand. The Court could also send the case back without resolving the question for either side.
