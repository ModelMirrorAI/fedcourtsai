## In brief

Roxane Marschner's challenge to a ruling against her ex-husband Richard over military divorce benefits will be heard by the Supreme Court.

## What happened

Roxane and Richard Marschner divorced in 2016, agreeing Roxane would receive a share of Richard's military retirement pay. When Richard later medically retired and began receiving disability-related pay instead of standard retirement pay, Roxane stopped receiving her share. A North Dakota trial court ordered Richard to pay her an equivalent amount as spousal support. The North Dakota Supreme Court reversed, holding that federal law bars state courts from requiring veterans to make up for lost retirement pay once it has been converted to disability-related pay, even where the divorce agreement calls for it.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked whether a federal law governing military retirement benefits in divorce prevents state courts from enforcing a provision in a couple's own settlement agreement that requires a veteran to compensate a former spouse when the veteran's divorisible retirement pay is reduced because he later receives disability-related pay.

## Where it stands

The Court granted the petition on October 1, 2026, and the case has been set for argument on January 19, 2027.

## What each outcome would mean

Roxane asked the Court to take the case, and she is now asking it to reverse the North Dakota Supreme Court's decision, which favored Richard. If the Court affirms, the North Dakota ruling stands, and Roxane would not receive the disputed share of Richard's benefits under the agreement. If the Court reverses, Richard's indemnification obligation under the settlement could be enforced, meaning he would owe Roxane payments. If the Court vacates, it would send the case back to the North Dakota courts without deciding who is entitled to prevail.
