## In brief

The Court agreed to hear Roxane Marschner's bid to enforce a divorce settlement requiring her ex-husband to make up lost military retirement pay.

## What happened

Roxane and Richard Marschner divorced in North Dakota in 2016, agreeing Roxane would get a share of Richard's military retirement pay, including amounts he might later waive to receive disability benefits. In 2021, Richard was medically retired and began receiving disability-related pay instead, and Roxane stopped receiving her share. A trial court ordered Richard to pay Roxane the equivalent amount as spousal support. The North Dakota Supreme Court reversed, holding that federal law bars enforcing such an arrangement even when the veteran agreed to it in a settlement, because it amounts to dividing disability pay that federal law protects.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked whether a federal law protecting veterans' disability-related pay from division in divorce also prevents courts from enforcing an agreement, voluntarily made by the veteran as part of a divorce settlement, to pay the former spouse an equivalent amount if that disability-related pay later reduces what the spouse receives.

## Where it stands

The Court granted the petition for certiorari on October 1, 2026. The case has not yet been argued; a motion to dispense with filing a joint appendix was submitted on October 19, 2026.

## What each outcome would mean

Roxane Marschner is asking the Court to reverse the North Dakota Supreme Court's decision, which favored Richard Marschner. If the Court affirms, the North Dakota ruling stands, and Roxane would not be able to enforce the agreement requiring Richard to make up her reduced share. If the Court reverses, the agreement could be enforced, potentially requiring Richard to pay Roxane the amounts specified in their settlement. If the Court vacates, the case would return to the North Dakota courts for further proceedings without a final decision on who prevails.
