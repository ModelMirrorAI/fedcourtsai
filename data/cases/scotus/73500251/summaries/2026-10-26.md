## In brief

A divorced veteran's ex-wife asks the Court to decide whether federal law let her enforce a state court's order requiring him to share his disability-related military pay with her.

## What happened

Richard and Roxane Marschner divorced in 2016 under a settlement giving Roxane a share of Richard's future military retirement pay, including amounts he might later waive to receive veterans' disability benefits. After Richard medically retired in 2021 and began receiving disability-related pay instead of ordinary retirement pay, Roxane stopped receiving her share. A North Dakota trial court ordered Richard to make up the difference through spousal support. The North Dakota Supreme Court reversed, holding that federal law bars state courts from requiring veterans to pay former spouses amounts tied to waived or disability-related retirement pay, even when the veteran agreed to it in a settlement.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked whether a federal law governing military retirement pay in divorces blocks state courts from enforcing a divorced couple's own agreement that the veteran would make up any loss to his ex-spouse's share if his retirement pay was later reduced because he began receiving disability-related benefits instead.

## Where it stands

The Court granted the petition on October 1, 2026, after the case was distributed for conference more than once and the parties filed full briefing. The case has not yet been argued or decided.

## What each outcome would mean

Roxane, the veteran's ex-wife, is asking the Court to reverse the North Dakota Supreme Court's decision, which had ruled for Richard. If the Court affirms, Richard's win below stands, and such agreements to compensate an ex-spouse for lost retirement pay would remain unenforceable in North Dakota. If the Court reverses, Roxane's claim to payments under the couple's settlement would be revived. If the Court vacates, it would set aside the North Dakota ruling and send the case back without deciding who should ultimately prevail.
