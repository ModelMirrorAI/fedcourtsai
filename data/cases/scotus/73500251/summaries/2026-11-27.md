## In brief

The Supreme Court heard a divorced couple's dispute over whether a veteran's ex-wife can enforce a divorce settlement requiring him to make up her lost share of his retirement pay.

## What happened

Roxane and Richard Marschner divorced in 2016 under a settlement giving Roxane a share of Richard's military retirement pay, including amounts he might later waive to receive disability benefits. After Richard was medically retired in 2021 and began receiving disability-related pay instead of standard retirement pay, Roxane stopped receiving her share. A North Dakota trial court ordered Richard to pay her anyway, but the North Dakota Supreme Court reversed, holding that federal law bars enforcing such indemnification provisions even when the veteran agreed to them. Roxane asked the U.S. Supreme Court to review that ruling.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked whether a federal law governing military retirement pay in divorce cases forbids state courts from enforcing a provision in a divorce settlement, voluntarily agreed to by both spouses, requiring a veteran to make up a shortfall when he later waives retirement pay in favor of disability benefits.

## Where it stands

The Court granted review, and the case was argued; briefing included merits briefs from Roxane and amicus briefs supporting her position, and the case awaits decision.

## What each outcome would mean

Roxane, who lost below, is asking the Court to reverse the North Dakota Supreme Court's ruling. If the Court reverses, the settlement's indemnification provision would be enforceable, and Richard would owe Roxane payments matching her lost share. If the Court affirms, the North Dakota ruling stands, and Richard owes Roxane nothing further under the provision. If the Court vacates, it would send the case back to the North Dakota courts without deciding who should ultimately win.
