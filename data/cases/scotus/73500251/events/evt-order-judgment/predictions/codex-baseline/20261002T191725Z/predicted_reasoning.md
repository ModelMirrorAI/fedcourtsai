# Forecast of the Court's judgment

The Court will vacate the North Dakota Supreme Court's judgment and remand. My vote forecast is 7–2: Chief Justice Roberts and Justices Sotomayor, Kagan, Gorsuch, Kavanaugh, Barrett, and Jackson in the majority; Justices Thomas and Alito dissenting. I forecast no recusal and do not assign opinion authorship or writing roles.

The majority will reject the proposition that the USFSPA automatically invalidates every voluntarily assumed obligation to indemnify a former spouse. It will distinguish a personal payment obligation undertaken in a negotiated settlement from a state court's unilateral division of federally protected military benefits. Howell will remain controlling for compulsory reimbursement imposed without such an undertaking; the majority will not overrule Howell or Mansell. This forecasts the contract-versus-compulsory-division distinction presented by the petition, pp. 24–28, rather than merely predicting that the Court will interpret the statute.

The holding will be limited. It will not establish that all disability benefits are freely assignable, authorize their direct division by a divorce court, or affirm the trial court's particular support and arrearage award. Instead, the state courts will have to decide whether the decree contains an enforceable personal undertaking, what payments it actually promises, and what remedy respects the applicable federal restrictions. The majority will leave those applications for remand. That is why the forecast is vacatur, not outright reinstatement of the trial court's award. The specific uncertainties appear in the opposition, pp. 11–14 and 19–22, and in the petition's reproduced state opinion, App. 6a–8a.

The dissent will treat the decree and its enforcement as an impermissible effort to accomplish indirectly the protected-benefit division forbidden by Mansell and Howell, notwithstanding the parties' assent. It will emphasize that describing an obligation as contractual does not necessarily change the source or substance of the awarded property.

I expect a decision after briefing and argument, most likely by June 2027. Dismissal as improvidently granted is a genuine alternative because the challenged support order may not actually enforce the sort of voluntary indemnification promise the question presented assumes. An equally divided affirmance is only a remote alternative absent a recusal. These alternatives are included in the probability allocation in reasoning.md; they are not the point forecast.
