# Forecast: Marschner v. Marschner, No. 25-1349 (merits, grant moment)

**Judgment class.** The Court will **reverse** the North Dakota Supreme Court and remand. P(judgment below disturbed) = 0.70. Within that: reversal is the modal label (~0.40), with a vacate-and-remand that announces the federal rule and sends the state-law questions back a close second (~0.28). The undisturbed arm (~0.30) is mostly an outright affirmance on Mansell/Howell grounds (~0.24), with a dismissal as improvidently granted on the respondent's vehicle objections a live but small possibility (~0.05) and an affirmance in part negligible.

**Vote lineup.** 7-2 for petitioner: Roberts, Sotomayor, Kagan, Gorsuch, Kavanaugh, Barrett, and Jackson in the majority; Thomas and Alito in dissent, reading Mansell's settlement facts and the "regardless of their form" sentence of Howell as controlling, and reading 10 U.S.C. 1408(a)(2)'s inclusion of "a court ordered, ratified, or approved property settlement" within "court order" as Congress's own refusal to distinguish contracted-for from court-imposed terms. A unanimous reversal is the next-likeliest shape; a 5-4 either way is less likely than either, because the grant pattern (below) suggests the center of the Court is already uncomfortable with the no-enforcement rule.

**Question reached.** The Court will take the question presented as written and answer it in the negative: the USFSPA does not require state courts to refuse enforcement of indemnification provisions in divorce settlement agreements. It will not reach the respondent's alternative textual theory under 38 U.S.C. 5301(a)(3)(A) as a ground of decision, treating an indemnity obligation payable from any source as not an agreement by which the former spouse "acquires ... the right to receive such benefit." It will also decline to decide whether this decree actually embodies such an agreement, whether the trial court's Rule 60(b) and spousal-support framing was contract enforcement, and what the remedy on remand should be; those are state-law matters it will leave to North Dakota.

**Ground of the majority.** The majority will reason that 1408(c)(1) is a grant of power to state courts to "treat" disposable retired pay as divisible property, and that Mansell and Howell police that judicial power: a court may not divide, or restore dollar-for-dollar what it had divided, out of waived or Chapter 61 pay. A veteran's own promise is a different object. Statutory protections are presumptively waivable by the party they protect (United States v. Mezzanatto), nothing in 1408 indicates Congress meant the exclusion to be unwaivable, and Rose v. Rose already recognizes that a veteran's personal obligations can be enforced against him without regard to the federal character of the funds he uses to pay them. Howell itself reserved the point ("we need not and do not decide these matters"), and petitioner's counsel in Howell conceded at argument that a litigation waiver of USFSPA rights would be enforceable. The opinion will stress that holding a veteran to a bargain for which he received $140,500 of present consideration is not an obstacle to any federal purpose, and will note that Howell's own suggested cure (valuing the contingency up front) is precisely what these parties did.

**Breadth.** Categorical as to the federal question: negotiated indemnification and waiver provisions are not preempted, whatever the form of disability-based pay at issue and whether the agreement predates Howell. Narrow as to application: the Court will not decide whether this decree is such an agreement.

**Separate writings.** Expect a Thomas dissent (joined by Alito) rather than any concurrence splitting the rationale from the result; a short Thomas separate writing disclaiming purposes-and-objectives language is possible even if he joins, as in Howell. No equally-divided outcome: no recusal is indicated (Gorsuch sat out Howell only because he arrived mid-Term).

**Procedural exits.** A DIG is live but unlikely: the respondent's vehicle points (the trial court granted Rule 60(b) relief and styled the award spousal support; the operative terms sit in a post-judgment order rather than the settlement itself; the decree never uses the word "indemnify") were squarely before the Court in the brief in opposition, after the Court itself called for a response, and the Court granted anyway. The North Dakota opinion also states the federal holding in the alternative ("[e]ven if the parties' agreement obligated Richard to indemnify Roxane ... federal law renders the obligation unenforceable"), which is enough to reach the question.

**Timing.** Argument in the January or February 2027 sitting; decision by late June 2027.
