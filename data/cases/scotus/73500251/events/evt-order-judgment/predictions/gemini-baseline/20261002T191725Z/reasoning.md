Mansell* (1989) and *Howell v. Howell* (2017). 

In *Mansell*, the Court held that the USFSPA does not grant state courts the power to treat as property divisible upon divorce military retirement pay that has been waived to receive veterans' disability benefits, even where the parties had agreed to it in a property settlement. In *Howell*, the Court rejected state court workarounds that ordered indemnification for waived retirement pay, calling the difference between dividing property and ordering indemnification "semantic and nothing more."

The North Dakota Supreme Court relied directly on these cases to hold that a contractual indemnification provision in a divorce settlement agreement is preempted by federal law when it functionally divides non-divisible disability pay. The petitioner argues for a distinction between "contracted-for" and "court-imposed" indemnification, but given that *Mansell* itself involved a property settlement and *Howell* explicitly rejected indemnification as a workaround, the Supreme Court is highly likely to affirm the North Dakota Supreme Court's straightforward application of precedent.

I predict an affirmance (undisturbed judgment), largely tracking the lower court's reasoning. My main uncertainty is whether the Court might find the specific contractual nature of the agreement sufficient to invoke state contract law without triggering USFSPA preemption, perhaps championed by Justices favoring strong states' rights in contract and family law (like Justice Thomas, who concurred in judgment in *Howell* on preemption grounds), but this is unlikely to command a majority against the clear statutory exclusions for disability pay.
