I expect the Court to affirm the judgment of the North Dakota Supreme Court.

The Court will likely hold that the Uniformed Services Former Spouses’ Protection Act (USFSPA), as interpreted in *Mansell v. Mansell* and *Howell v. Howell*, preempts state courts from enforcing indemnification provisions in divorce settlement agreements that require a veteran to reimburse an ex-spouse for military retirement pay waived to receive disability benefits. The majority opinion will likely reject the distinction between "court-imposed" and "contracted-for" indemnification, reasoning that enforcing such a contractual provision functionally circumvents Congress's explicit exclusion of disability pay from divisible marital property.

The Court will likely take the question as presented and establish a categorical rule that state contract and family law cannot be used to indirectly divide veteran disability benefits that federal law shields. I expect a majority opinion authored by a core conservative or moderate Justice, with a possible dissent or concurrence in judgment from Justice Thomas, who has previously expressed reservations about the scope of implied preemption in family law, as seen in his separate writing in *Howell*. A procedural exit like a DIG is unlikely given the clean presentation of the legal question and the alleged split among state high courts.
