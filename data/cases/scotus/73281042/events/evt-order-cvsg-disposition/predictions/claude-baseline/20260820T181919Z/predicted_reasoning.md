# Forecast: Saadeh v. New Jersey State Bar Association, No. 25-1002 (CVSG moment)

Forecast as of the 2026-06-23 snapshot, the day after the Court invited the
Solicitor General's views.

## Relists

The docket shows **two distributions** (Conferences of 4/2/2026 and
6/18/2026); the CVSG issued four days after the second. The Court will now
hold the petition for the SG's brief. A CVSG brief carries no deadline and in
a case invited in late June typically arrives between December and the
following May; once it is filed the petition will be redistributed. I expect
**at least one and more likely two further distributions** before disposition
— a further relist past the two shown is near-certain (the alternative
requires a dismissal or withdrawal before the SG files, which nothing on this
docket suggests).

## CVSG

Already on the docket (June 22, 2026). No second invitation will issue; the
cvsg-increment claim is vacuous on this record.

## Disposition

**More likely denied than granted, but this is a genuinely close call:
P(any grant) = 0.40.** The Court has taken two affirmative steps — it
requested a response after the respondent waived (Mar 23, 2026), then called
for the SG's views — and the question presented (whether the First Amendment
shields race-based leadership set-asides from state antidiscrimination law
when framed as expressing "diversity") sits squarely in the current
majority's post-*SFFA*, post-*303 Creative* line of interest. The current
Solicitor General is very likely to tell the Court the decision below is
wrong on the merits.

What cuts the other way is the vehicle, and the SG's recommendation will
likely turn on it: (1) a *Carney v. Adams* standing problem — petitioner
never applied, or said he would apply, for a leadership position; (2) the
Association changed the challenged selection process in November 2025,
raising mootness/changed-circumstances objections; (3) the decision below is
unpublished and nonprecedential, from a state intermediate court after the
New Jersey Supreme Court denied certification; and (4) the state-law
discrimination claim was never decided below, inviting finality and
advisory-opinion objections to reaching the First Amendment defense first.
If the SG recommends denial on vehicle grounds — a live possibility even
from an administration sympathetic to the merits — the Court most likely
denies and waits for a cleaner case (several parallel challenges to
DEI-based selection are percolating in the lower courts).

## Route, if granted

**Plenary review, not a summary disposition** (P(cert-order disposition |
grant) ≈ 0.10). There is no intervening decision of this Court to GVR
against — no recent SCOTUS merits decision addresses expressive association
versus antidiscrimination law — and a summary reversal of an unpublished
state decision with an undecided state-law claim underneath is a poor fit.
If granted, I expect the Court to take the QP essentially as written, perhaps
reformulated to ask whether *Boy Scouts v. Dale*'s expressive-association
doctrine shields an organization's race-conscious selection of its leaders
from a state antidiscrimination law, leaving the "equity"/"inclusion"
framing behind.

## If denied

A **dissent from, or statement respecting, denial is a real possibility
(≈ 0.35 conditional on denial)** — most plausibly from Justice Thomas or
Justice Alito, who have repeatedly written on race-conscious selection and
on *Dale*'s scope. A denial after a CVSG in a case with an 18-state amicus
bloc is exactly the posture that draws a statement explaining that the
vehicle, not the question, failed.

## Timing

No disposition before early 2027. Expect the SG's brief in winter–spring
2027, redistribution, and a decision on the petition in the spring of 2027,
late in OT2026.
