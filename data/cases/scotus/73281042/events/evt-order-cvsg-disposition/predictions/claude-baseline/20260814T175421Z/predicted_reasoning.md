# Forecast: Saadeh v. New Jersey State Bar Association (No. 25-1002), after the CVSG

State of the docket I am forecasting from: the petition has been **distributed
twice** (Conferences of 4/2/2026 and 6/18/2026), the Court **requested a
response** after the respondent initially waived, four cert-stage amicus briefs
support the petitioner (West Virginia + 17 states, SFFA et al., America First
Legal, Manhattan Institute), and on **June 22, 2026** the Court invited the
Solicitor General to file a brief expressing the views of the United States. No
SG brief is on the docket yet.

## The path from here

- **The Solicitor General will file in roughly six to eleven months** — most
  likely between December 2026 and May 2027. CVSG briefs carry no deadline and
  the SG's office routinely takes two to three Terms' worth of conference
  cycles on them; a filing in time for an OT2026 spring conference is the modal
  outcome.
- **The SG will recommend granting certiorari, more likely than not (~0.55),
  with a real minority chance (~0.45) of a "deny — bad vehicle" or
  "hold/deny pending percolation" recommendation.** The administration's
  litigation posture on DEI (the executive orders the petition itself cites,
  the government's position in NADOHE v. Trump) makes it very likely the
  United States endorses the petitioner's merits position — that the First
  Amendment does not protect the act of status-based discrimination. The open
  question is whether the SG treats the Article III standing gap, the November
  2025 program change, and the unresolved state-law ground as disqualifying.
  The SG's office takes vehicle defects seriously even when it likes the
  question, so a "the issue is important but this is a poor vehicle" brief is a
  live outcome, not a tail case.
- **The petition will be redistributed after the SG files** — at least one
  further distribution is close to certain (I put 0.97 on at least one more,
  the residual being withdrawal, settlement, or dismissal before the SG
  responds), and two to four total further distributions are likely before
  disposition. Expect no disposition before spring 2027.
- **Disposition: denial is the single most likely outcome (~0.58), but the
  grant-family probability is high for a cert docket (~0.40).** If the Court
  grants, it will be a **plenary grant on the question presented essentially
  as written** — the QP is already framed narrowly around the status/message
  distinction, and there is no second QP to shear off. A **summary disposition
  is unlikely**: no intervening decision of this Court bears on the question,
  so a GVR has nothing to ride on (~0.03), and the Court does not summarily
  reverse state courts on contested First Amendment ground it has never
  addressed (~0.02).
- **If the Court denies, expect a separate writing (~0.45 conditional on
  denial)** — a dissent from denial or statement respecting denial from
  Justice Thomas and/or Justice Alito is the likely form. Thomas has written
  before about unpublished decisions being used to shield suspect reasoning
  (Plumley v. Austin), which this petition expressly invokes, and Alito
  handled the extension applications. A denial after a grant-recommending SG
  brief would make a separate writing more likely still.
- **Conditional merits note** (no unconditional claim intended): if granted,
  the likely ground of reversal is the status/message line of 303 Creative
  n.3 and Fearless Fund — that reserving seats by demographic status is
  conduct, not protected expression — with the standing question either
  resolved against dismissal under Northeastern Florida AGCA or ordered for
  briefing alongside the merits.
