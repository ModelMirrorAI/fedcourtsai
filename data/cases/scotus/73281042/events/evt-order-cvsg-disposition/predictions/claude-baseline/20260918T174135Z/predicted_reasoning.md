# Forecast: what the Court will do with No. 25-1002 after the CVSG

**State forecast from.** The snapshot (cut at 2026-06-23) shows two distributions: the first for the 4/2/2026 conference, after which the Court requested a response the respondent had waived; the second for the 6/18/2026 conference after the brief in opposition and reply. On 2026-06-22 the Court invited the Solicitor General to file a brief. No CVSG brief, no further distribution, and no disposition appear on the record.

**Disposition.** Deny is the modal outcome, but only narrowly: I put the grant family (plenary grant, grant-in-part, or a summary disposition) at 42%. The Court has now taken two affirmative steps on this petition (calling for a response over a waiver, then a CVSG), the question is one the current majority has shown appetite for, and the Solicitor General under this administration will almost certainly tell the Court the Appellate Division was wrong on the merits. Against that, the vehicle is weak enough that a denial is still the likelier single outcome: an unpublished, nonprecedential intermediate state-court opinion; an Article III standing problem the state courts never had to address; a leadership-selection policy that changed in November 2025; and an undecided state-law claim beneath the federal holding.

**The Solicitor General.** Expect the United States to file in late November to mid-December 2026, urging that the decision below misreads Dale, Roberts, Runyon and Hishon. The split I forecast is on the recommendation, not the merits view: roughly even odds that the SG recommends granting (possibly with summary reversal) versus recommending denial on vehicle grounds while agreeing the reasoning below is wrong. If the SG recommends a grant, expect the Court to follow; if the SG recommends denial, expect a denial with at least one separate writing.

**Relists.** The petition will be redistributed after the SG brief arrives, most likely for a January 2027 conference. Expect one to two further distributions (the post-CVSG redistribution plus at most one relist) before disposition in January or February 2027. Redistribution is nearly certain; a long relist chain is not.

**Which question.** If the Court grants, it takes the question as written or reformulates it slightly toward the doctrine: whether the First Amendment right of expressive association (Dale) shields a private organization's race- and sex-conscious selection of its own leaders from a state antidiscrimination law. It would not add a standing question; it would address standing in the opinion or dismiss as improvidently granted if the problem proves real.

**Summary disposition.** Unlikely: conditional on a grant, about a 10% chance the disposition rides in the cert order (a per curiam reversal of the state court). No intervening decision supports a GVR, and the expressive-association question is contested enough that the Court would want argument.

**If denied.** About a one-in-three chance of a separate writing. The most likely shape is a statement respecting the denial (Justice Alito or Justice Thomas) saying the decision below is wrong but the vehicle is poor, in the style of Alito's Huber statement, or a dissent from denial by Justice Thomas alone.

**If argued (conditional).** The likely ground is that Dale's deference to an association's view of its own message does not extend to facially race- and sex-based allocation of offices, which Roberts, Runyon and Hishon treat as regulable conduct; a reversal or vacatur and remand for the state courts to decide the state-law claim is more likely than an affirmance.
