## Why I move above the anchor, but not above one-half

**The federal question has substantial reach.** The petition frames a conflict between antidiscrimination requirements and expressive-association protection for demographic leadership preferences. Its comparison to discrimination in access to other private opportunities is consequential, not merely a dispute about this one board. Its principal merits distinction is between regulating status-based exclusion and compelling an organization's message (petition pp. 3–20). The appendix confirms that the appellate court affirmatively resolved the federal constitutional issue while leaving the state statutory question undecided (App. 30a–33a). This is not a case in which the First Amendment question appears only in an advocate's characterization.

**The case has demonstrated attention and national support.** The baseline shows a requested response, four accepted amicus-brief entries including eighteen states, and then a CVSG after the opposition was filed. The petition itself explains the United States' interest in analogous federal antidiscrimination statutes and First Amendment defenses (pp. 22–23). These features support a meaningful prospect that the Court wants to resolve the issue rather than simply correct local error. The CVSG is already reflected in the statistical anchor; I do not add a second independent CVSG premium. Nor do I assume that an invitation means the SG will recommend a grant.

**Standing is the strongest reason not to make grant the modal outcome.** The opposition says Saadeh offered no evidence below that he would actually seek a challenged position, did not apply while the program was suspended, and affirmatively disclaimed a need to show interest (BIO pp. 9–13). The petition counters with his prior board service, asserted readiness to apply, and a damages claim for past exclusion (pp. 20–21). His prior service makes the dispute less abstract, but an assertion in a cert petition is not necessarily a developed evidentiary record. I treat the opposition's account as a contested advocacy claim, not an adjudicated lack of jurisdiction. Still, the distinction between a nominal-damages remedy and proof of a concrete injury is a material vehicle risk. The missing reply is particularly important on this issue.

**The claimed conflict is imperfect.** The petition relies on *Fearless Fund* and the Fourth Circuit litigation over federal DEI-related funding conditions. The BIO distinguishes a commercial grant competition and government funding restrictions from a voluntary expressive association's selection of its leaders (pp. 13–16). That is a plausible distinction. The briefs establish opposing arguments about the reach of precedent, not a demonstrated square conflict over the same leadership-selection policy. The opinion's unpublished status is a practical reason to await a cleaner vehicle, not a jurisdictional bar or proof of an effort to evade review.

**Changed policy and unresolved state law reduce the payoff of review.** The BIO describes a November 2025 revision opening routes to the at-large seats through designated affinity-bar memberships (pp. 17–19). I have not independently verified the revision or concluded that it moots a damages claim. It nevertheless weakens the prospective fit between the petition's broad question and the policy now defended. The appendix also confirms that the state-law legality of the program was left open. That is not an existing adequate-and-independent state ground supporting this judgment: the judgment actually rests on the First Amendment. It is instead a possible alternative route on remand and a reason why deciding the federal issue may not settle the dispute.

Taken together, importance and demonstrated attention justify a moderate upward adjustment from approximately 35%, while standing, policy change, and the imperfect conflict keep my final probability at **46%**. This is a judgmental adjustment, not the output of an estimated regression. The SG's assessment of jurisdiction and vehicle suitability is the largest unresolved input. My stakes score of **0.74** reflects possible doctrinal reach and national interest, independently of the grant probability; it does not assume that every diversity program would fall within a ruling about voluntary association leadership.
