## In brief

A bar association member asks the Court to revive his discrimination suit after a state appellate court held the First Amendment protected the association's diversity-based leadership selection process.

## What happened

Rajeh Saadeh, a longtime member of the New Jersey State Bar Association, sued it in state court, claiming its practice of reserving some board and committee seats for members of specific underrepresented groups violated New Jersey's antidiscrimination law. A trial court agreed and ruled for Saadeh. New Jersey's intermediate appellate court reversed, holding that the association's selection process was protected First Amendment expression under the Supreme Court's Boy Scouts v. Dale decision, without deciding whether the process violated state law. The state supreme court declined further review. Saadeh now asks the U.S. Supreme Court to take the case.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked whether the First Amendment's protection for a group's expressive choices shields an organization from antidiscrimination liability when the challenged practice is meant to advance the organization's stated commitment to diversity, equity, or inclusion.

## Where it stands

The petition has been fully briefed, including a brief in opposition, a reply, and several amicus briefs, and was distributed for conference. The Court then asked the Solicitor General to file a brief giving the federal government's views, and that brief has since been submitted; the docket shows no further action since.

## What each outcome would mean

Saadeh, who lost in the lower court, is asking the Court to take up and reverse that ruling. If the Court grants review, it would hear full arguments and decide whether the association's process is constitutionally protected; a grant alone would not decide who wins. If the Court denies review, the appellate court's decision favoring the association stands, and Saadeh's suit does not proceed on the terms he sought. The government's invited brief may address whether review is warranted but does not itself change the case's posture.
