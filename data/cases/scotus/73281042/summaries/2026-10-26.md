## In brief

A bar association member asks the Court to review a ruling that the First Amendment shields the association's diversity-based leadership selection process from his discrimination lawsuit.

## What happened

Rajeh Saadeh, a longtime New Jersey State Bar Association member who once served on its Board, sued the Association in state court, arguing that its practice of reserving certain board and committee seats for members of specific demographic groups violated New Jersey's antidiscrimination law. A trial court ruled for Saadeh, but New Jersey's intermediate appellate court reversed in an unpublished opinion, holding that the Association's First Amendment right to expressive association shielded its leadership-selection process, without deciding whether that process actually violated state law. The New Jersey Supreme Court declined further review.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked whether an organization's free-speech and association rights can excuse it from following an antidiscrimination law when the organization says its selection practices express its views on diversity, equity, or inclusion.

## Where it stands

The petition was distributed for conference, the respondent filed a brief in opposition, and the petitioner replied. The docket's most recent entry shows the Court has invited the Solicitor General to file a brief giving the federal government's views on the case.

## What each outcome would mean

Saadeh, who sued the Association and lost in the appellate court, is asking the Court to take up and reverse that decision. If the Court grants review and later rules for Saadeh, the appellate court's decision would be reversed or vacated, reviving his discrimination claim. If it rules for the Association, the appellate court's decision favoring the Association would stand. If the Court denies review, the appellate court's decision favoring the Association remains in place, and Saadeh's lawsuit does not proceed. The Solicitor General's views, once filed, may inform whether the Court takes the case, but do not themselves decide it.
