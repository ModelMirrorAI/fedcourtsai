## In brief

A bar association member asks the Court to review a ruling that the First Amendment shields the association's leadership-selection process from his discrimination claim.

## What happened

Rajeh Saadeh, a longtime member of the New Jersey State Bar Association, sued the association, arguing that its practice of reserving certain board and committee seats for members of specific demographic groups violated New Jersey's antidiscrimination law. A trial court agreed and ruled for Saadeh. On appeal, New Jersey's intermediate appellate court reversed, in an unpublished opinion, without deciding the state-law question. It held instead that the First Amendment's protection of expressive association shields the association's selection process, relying on a Supreme Court precedent about a private group's right to choose its own members and leaders. New Jersey's Supreme Court declined further review.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked whether the First Amendment protects an organization's discriminatory practices from an antidiscrimination law when the organization says the practices express its commitment to diversity, equity, or inclusion. The association responds that Saadeh lacks the standing needed to sue in federal court, that there is no disagreement among lower courts requiring the Court's attention, that the case is a poor vehicle for deciding the issue because the challenged policy has since changed and the state-law question remains undecided, and that the lower court's ruling was correct.

## Where it stands

The petition has been distributed for conference more than once, and the respondent filed a brief opposing review along with several amicus briefs on both sides. Most recently, the Court invited the Solicitor General to file a brief expressing the federal government's views on the case.

## What each outcome would mean

Saadeh, who sued the association, is asking the Court to take up and reverse the lower court's decision. If the Court grants review and ultimately rules for Saadeh, the First Amendment ruling below would be overturned, and the case would likely return to lower courts to resolve the unanswered state-law discrimination claim. If the Court denies review, the New Jersey appellate court's decision favoring the association stands, leaving its selection process shielded from Saadeh's challenge. The Solicitor General's forthcoming views do not themselves decide the case.
