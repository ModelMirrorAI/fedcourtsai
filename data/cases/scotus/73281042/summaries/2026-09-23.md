## What happened

Rajeh Saadeh, a longtime member of the New Jersey State Bar Association, sued the Association in state court, arguing that its practice of reserving certain leadership seats for members of underrepresented groups violates New Jersey's antidiscrimination law. A trial court agreed with Saadeh and ordered the seats opened to all members. New Jersey's intermediate appellate court reversed, holding that the Association's First Amendment right to expressive association shields its leadership-selection process, without deciding whether that process actually violates state law. The New Jersey Supreme Court declined further review. Saadeh now asks the U.S. Supreme Court to take up the case. The Association responds that Saadeh never applied for the seats he challenges and cannot show he was harmed, and that the leadership process has since changed.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked whether a private association's free-speech rights can override antidiscrimination law when the association's leadership-selection practices are meant to express its views on diversity, equity, or inclusion. Saadeh argues the lower court wrongly let free-speech protections excuse what would otherwise be illegal discrimination. The Association counters that Saadeh cannot even bring the claim in federal court because he never showed he wanted or tried to obtain one of the contested seats, that no other courts have reached conflicting results on this question, and that the case is a poor vehicle for deciding it because the challenged program no longer exists in its original form.

## Where it stands

The petition has been distributed for conference more than once, and the Association was asked to respond and filed a brief opposing review; both sides have since filed additional briefs, including a reply from Saadeh. Most recently, the Court invited the Solicitor General to submit a brief giving the federal government's views before deciding whether to hear the case.
