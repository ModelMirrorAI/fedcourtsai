## In brief

The federal government asked the Supreme Court to review a ruling that unadmitted immigrants arrested inside the country may seek bond, rather than face mandatory detention.

## What happened

Ramon Rodriguez Vazquez, who entered the United States without authorization in 2009, was arrested in 2025 and denied a bond hearing under a government policy applying a mandatory-detention statute to people already living inside the country, not just those arriving at the border. He brought a class action and habeas case, and the district court ruled for the class, holding the mandatory-detention provision does not apply to them. The Ninth Circuit affirmed, agreeing that the provision covers only people seeking entry at the border, while the government argued it covers anyone present without having been formally admitted. Other circuits are divided on the question.

## What the Court is being asked

The question is whether a federal immigration statute requires the government to detain, without any possibility of bond, people who are in the United States without having been formally admitted, or whether that mandatory detention rule applies only to people seeking to enter at the border, leaving others eligible for a bond hearing under a different, more flexible statute.

## Where it stands

The petition has been filed and the respondent has waived his right to file a response. The government's petition itself asks the Court to hold this case and decide it later in light of its ruling in a separate case, Rhoney v. Barbosa da Cunha, where the Court has already granted certiorari on the same question.

## What each outcome would mean

The federal government, as petitioner, is asking the Court to reverse the Ninth Circuit's decision, which favored Rodriguez Vazquez and the class. If the Court grants the petition and later rules for the government, people in the class's position could face mandatory detention without bond hearings. If it rules for Rodriguez Vazquez, the lower court's decision allowing bond hearings would stand. The government itself has asked the Court to hold the case pending its decision in the related Barbosa da Cunha case rather than decide it separately now.
