## In brief

The federal government asks the Court to hold a case on mandatory immigration detention while it decides the same question in another pending case.

## What happened

Ramon Rodriguez Vazquez, who entered the United States illegally in 2009, was detained after being arrested on an immigration warrant and was denied a bond hearing because an immigration judge found his detention mandatory under a federal statute. He filed a class-action habeas case, and a district court ruled for his class, finding the law does not require mandatory detention without bond for people like him. The Ninth Circuit affirmed, agreeing the statute applies only to people seeking entry at the border, not those already living in the country. The government now asks the Supreme Court to review that decision.

## What the Court is being asked

The question is whether a federal immigration law requires the government to detain, without any chance of bond, people who are living in the United States without having been formally admitted, while their removal cases proceed. The government argues the law requires this. Rodriguez Vazquez's side argues the law only requires mandatory detention for people stopped at the border, and that people already inside the country may instead seek release on bond.

## Where it stands

The petition was filed on October 28, 2026, with the response due November 30, 2026. The government's own filing asks the Court to hold this petition until it decides the same legal question in another case, Rhoney v. Barbosa da Cunha, which it has already agreed to hear.

## What each outcome would mean

The federal government, as petitioner, is asking the Court to take up and ultimately reverse the Ninth Circuit's ruling, which favored Rodriguez Vazquez's class. If the Court grants review and later rules for the government, people in the class could be detained without bond hearings. If it rules for Rodriguez Vazquez's class, or denies review, the Ninth Circuit's decision allowing bond hearings would stand. The government has asked the Court to instead hold this case until it decides the related Barbosa da Cunha case first.
