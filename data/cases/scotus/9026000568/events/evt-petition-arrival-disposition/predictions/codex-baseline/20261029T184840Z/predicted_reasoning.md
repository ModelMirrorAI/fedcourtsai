# Forecast of the Court's action

The Court will hold this petition for its decision in Rhoney v. Barbosa da
Cunha, No. 26-104, and then grant the petition, vacate the Ninth Circuit's
judgment, and remand for reconsideration in light of that decision. This GVR
is the predicted disposition, not a prediction that the Court will separately
hear argument in this case. The hold itself is an intermediate step, not the
event's final disposition.

The baseline shows zero distributions and no CVSG. I expect at least one
distribution after briefing, probably followed by a hold rather than a long
sequence of ordinary relists. The 0.98 distribution-increment probability
includes the first distribution from this arrival-time state; it does not
mean a 98% chance of a true relist after an initial conference. One or two
distribution entries before or around the hold are more likely than repeated
active conference consideration. A return to conference after the lead
decision is also plausible. I expect no request for the Solicitor General's
views: the Solicitor General already represents the petitioners and has
expressly requested the hold.

The likely timing is a hold through the lead case's merits disposition,
followed by action shortly afterward, plausibly in late spring or summer
2027. That is a timing forecast, not a known calendar for the lead case.

If the Court grants relief here, I assign 0.97 probability to disposition in
the cert order rather than a separate merits proceeding. The expected route
is reconsideration on remand, not a summary reversal deciding the statutory
issue independently. If separate plenary review nevertheless occurs, the
question would be the petition's single statutory question: whether
Section 1225(b)(2)(A) requires detention without a bond hearing for people
present without admission and arrested inside the country. The facts would
permit narrowing to people not apprehended upon arrival and not covered by
the other mandatory-detention provisions. Conditional on a grant, the
lead decision's treatment of the relationship between Sections 1225 and 1226
would supply the governing ground.

Denial after the lead decision is the main alternative, especially if that
decision leaves the Ninth Circuit's statutory rule intact. Conditional on
denial, I put the probability of any noted dissent or statement at 0.08.
The expected disposing order is unsigned and without separate writing;
no particular Justice is predicted to write.
