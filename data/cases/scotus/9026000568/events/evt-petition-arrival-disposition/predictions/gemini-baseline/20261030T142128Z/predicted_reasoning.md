1. **Conference Distributions and Relists (`relist-increment`)**: The docket currently records zero distributions (`distribution_count: 0`), as the petition was just docketed on October 29, 2026. Once the respondent files a response or waives the right to respond, the petition will be distributed for its initial conference (likely in December 2026 or January 2027), where the Court will place it on hold pending the merits decision in *Rhoney v. Barbosa da Cunha*, No. 26-104 (cert. granted Oct. 1, 2026). After *Barbosa da Cunha* is decided on the merits in spring or early summer 2027, the petition is likely to be distributed a second time for the post-decision conference at which the Court disposes of held companion petitions, resulting in two total conference distributions.
2. **Solicitor General Invitation (`cvsg-increment`)**: The Court will not issue a call for the views of the Solicitor General (CVSG), because the Solicitor General is counsel of record for the federal petitioners.
3. **Question Presented Scope (If Plenary Review Were Granted)**: Plenary review is very unlikely because the Court has already granted *Rhoney v. Barbosa da Cunha*, No. 26-104, to resolve the identical question. If the Court were to grant plenary review in this case (for example, if a vehicle defect emerged in *Barbosa da Cunha*), it would take the single Question Presented as written—whether 8 U.S.C. § 1225(b)(2)(A) mandates the detention pending removal proceedings of aliens who are present in the United States without having been admitted—without adding any secondary question on classwide declaratory relief under 8 U.S.C. § 1252(f)(1).
4. **Summary Disposition vs. Plenary Review (`summary-disposition-route`)**: Conditional on a grant-family disposition, a summary grant, vacatur, and remand (GVR) in the certiorari order itself is overwhelmingly more likely than plenary briefing and oral argument. The Solicitor General explicitly asks the Court only to hold this petition pending *Barbosa da Cunha* and then dispose of it as appropriate in light of that decision. Because the Ninth Circuit below affirmed a judgment holding that 8 U.S.C. § 1226(a) rather than § 1225(b)(2)(A) governs interior-apprehended noncitizens present without admission, a decision reversing or vacating the Second Circuit in *Barbosa da Cunha* will produce a routine GVR order here:
   > *"The petition for a writ of certiorari is granted. The judgment is vacated, and the case is remanded to the United States Court of Appeals for the Ninth Circuit for further consideration in light of Rhoney v. Barbosa da Cunha, 609 U.S. ___ (2027)."*
5. **Dissent from Denial (`dissent-from-denial`) and Separate Writings (`order-writers`)**: Should the Court instead affirm the Second Circuit in *Barbosa da Cunha* and therefore deny this held petition in the subsequent cleanup order list, no separate writing or recorded dissent from denial is expected. Any dissenting Justices (most likely Justices Thomas and Alito) will already have stated their views in their merits dissent in *Barbosa da Cunha*, and cleanup orders disposing of held petitions are almost invariably unsigned routine entries.

### Conditional Merits Grounds (If Reached or Applied via *Barbosa da Cunha*)

If the Court disturbs the judgment below (via a GVR following reversal or vacatur in *Barbosa da Cunha*, or in the unlikely event of plenary review), the likely ground is that 8 U.S.C. § 1225(a)(1)'s express command that "[a]n alien present in the United States who has not been admitted ... shall be deemed for purposes of this chapter an applicant for admission" carries through to 8 U.S.C. § 1225(b)(2)(A), rendering unadmitted noncitizens apprehended in the interior subject to mandatory detention during removal proceedings under 8 U.S.C. § 1229a unless paroled under 8 U.S.C. § 1182(d)(5)(A), notwithstanding prior Executive Branch practice under 8 U.S.C. § 1226(a) or partial overlap with the criminal-alien detention provisions of 8 U.S.C. § 1226(c).
