# Forecast — No. 26-568, Quinones v. Rodriguez Vazquez (arrival)

**Posture.** Docketed October 2026; no distributions, no CVSG, response due November 30, 2026. The Solicitor General does not ask for plenary review: the petition asks the Court to **hold** it pending *Rhoney v. Barbosa da Cunha*, No. 26-104 (cert. granted Oct. 1, 2026), which presents the identical question — whether 8 U.S.C. 1225(b)(2)(A) mandates detention without bond of aliens present in the United States without admission.

**Disposition.** The petition will be held, not decided on its own. Its fate tracks *Barbosa da Cunha*, expected by the end of June 2027:
- If the Court adopts the government's reading in *Barbosa da Cunha* (I put this somewhat above even), the Ninth Circuit's judgment — affirming a class-wide declaration that the Bond Denial Class is detained under § 1226(a) — is directly contrary, and the Court will **grant, vacate, and remand** in light of it. A summary reversal is possible but less likely than a GVR, given the class-action and mootness wrinkles (the named plaintiff departed) that the Ninth Circuit should address first.
- If the Court rejects the government's reading, the petition will be **denied**.
- A plenary grant of this case alongside or instead of the lead case is unlikely; the lead case is already set for briefing.

**Relists / distributions.** Expect the petition to be distributed after the response (or waiver) comes in, around December 2026–January 2027, and then held without action through repeated conferences, with a final distribution for the conference after *Barbosa da Cunha* issues. At least one distribution is near-certain.

**CVSG.** None — the United States is the petitioner.

**Which question.** If anything is granted, it is the single QP as written, disposed of by GVR rather than reformulated.

**Separate writing.** Unlikely either way. A held-petition GVR or denial usually goes without comment; the most plausible writing would be a short statement by a Justice who dissented in the lead case.
