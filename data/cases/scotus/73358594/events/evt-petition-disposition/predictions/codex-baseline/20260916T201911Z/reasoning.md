## Why adjust downward

The central weakness is vehicle and certworthiness, not an assertion that whistleblower protection lacks importance. The Federal Circuit provided no precedential rule rejecting the Board's obligation to consider material claims. The petition itself distinguishes a failure to analyze from an erroneous statutory interpretation (p. 20). That makes the requested intervention look more like correction of an alleged omission in a lengthy individual administrative record than resolution of a clean, square doctrinal conflict.

The Fourth Circuit analogy is real and prevents treating the petition as legally empty. I checked *Flynn v. United States Securities & Exchange Commission*, 877 F.3d 200 (4th Cir. 2017), through CourtListener, opinion 4226778, in the passage discussing 5 C.F.R. § 1201.111(b). The court found an omitted regulatory claim insufficiently analyzed and selected remand for record development. This supports the petitioner's proposed remedy. It does not, by itself, establish a conflicting legal holding in a Federal Circuit Rule 36 affirmance. The petition also invokes *Delgado* as evidence of other circuits' scrutiny, but I did not independently retrieve that decision and do not count it as a verified square split.

There are further record-dependent obstacles. The petition at pp. 15–16 acknowledges that the MSPB also addressed whether USDA would have demoted the employee anyway, while arguing that analysis was tainted by its protected-disclosure error. I cannot assume that the alleged omission is both preserved and outcome-determinative, or independently resolve the substantial performance and credibility disputes. The government's waiver, followed by distribution without a recorded response request, supplies an additional modest negative attention signal. A waiver does not establish that the government's merits position is correct, and the Court could still request a response.

Against these negatives, the statutory protection extends across the federal workforce, the question is intelligible, the petition identifies a specific unaddressed category, and *Flynn* offers a plausible procedural remedy. Those positives justify nonzero probability, but not retaining the full 5.12% heterogeneous private-petitioner anchor. My resulting 1.5% is a judgmental adjustment, not a fitted empirical model or a claimed measured probability for waiver cases.
