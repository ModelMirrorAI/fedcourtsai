# Forecast: what the Court will do with No. 25-1289

**Disposition.** The petition will be denied on the order list following the September 28, 2026 long conference (most likely the October 5, 2026 order list), without comment.

**Relists.** The docket shows exactly one distribution, for the September 28 conference; the petition has never been relisted. I expect no further distribution: the Solicitor General waived a response and the Court did not call for one, which is the usual path to a first-conference denial. The residual chance of one more distribution comes from routine long-conference carry-over or a reschedule rather than from any Justice's interest.

**CVSG.** None. The United States (the Department of Agriculture, represented by the Solicitor General) is the respondent, so there is no non-party Solicitor General whose views could be invited. The docket shows no CVSG and none will issue.

**Which question presented, if any.** The petition asks whether a federal employee who premises a protected disclosure on both categories of 5 U.S.C. § 2302(b)(8)(A) is entitled to a Merit Systems Protection Board ruling on both. If the Court took anything it would be a narrowed version of that question, framed as whether the Board must make findings on each pleaded category. It will not take the petition's broader framing about whistleblowers' "fair process" before the Board and the Federal Circuit, which is a complaint about the outcome rather than a legal question.

**Summary disposition versus plenary review.** Conditional on any grant, I expect a summary route (a GVR directing the Federal Circuit or Board to address the gross-mismanagement theory) to be somewhat likelier than plenary review, because the alleged error is a failure to decide a pleaded claim rather than a contested legal rule. No intervening decision of this Court bears on the question, so an actual GVR is improbable; this claim is conditional on a grant that I put at about one in a hundred.

**Dissent from denial.** I expect none. The whistleblower-protection subject occasionally draws a statement, and the Federal Circuit's Rule 36 practice has drawn criticism, but no Justice has written on either in a comparable single-employee petition, and the record here is fact-bound and was reviewed under a deferential standard.
