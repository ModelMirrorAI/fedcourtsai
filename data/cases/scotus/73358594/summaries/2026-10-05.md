## In brief

The Court declined to hear a former USDA employee's bid to revive her whistleblower retaliation claim after agencies only partly reviewed it.

## What happened

Tomasa Gabriella Bolanos-Reynoso, a former Department of Agriculture employee, reported what she believed was financial mismanagement in her office. She says she was later demoted and poorly rated in retaliation. She pursued a whistleblower retaliation claim through the Merit Systems Protection Board, which ruled against her, finding she lacked a reasonable belief that she had reported a violation of law. She argues the Board never separately considered her claim that she had reported gross mismanagement, a distinct basis for protection under federal whistleblower law. The United States Court of Appeals for the Federal Circuit affirmed the Board's decision without issuing a written opinion, and later denied rehearing.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked whether a federal employee who reports wrongdoing on two separate grounds recognized by whistleblower protection law is entitled to have a review board actually decide both grounds, rather than rule against the employee based on only one of them.

## Where it stands

The petition was distributed for the Court's September 28, 2026 conference, and the Court denied it on October 5, 2026.

## What each outcome would mean

Bolanos-Reynoso asked the Court to take up her case. With the petition denied, the Federal Circuit's decision affirming the Board's ruling against her stands, and her retaliation claim remains rejected. Had the Court instead granted the petition, it would have agreed to hear full arguments and decide the legal question itself, without that step deciding who would ultimately win.
