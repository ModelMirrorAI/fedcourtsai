## In brief

The Court declined to hear a former federal employee's bid to revive her whistleblower retaliation claim against the Department of Agriculture.

## What happened

Bolanos-Reynoso, a longtime federal employee, raised concerns about financial mismanagement at the Department of Agriculture's civil rights office. She says she was then demoted and given a poor performance rating in retaliation. She pursued relief through the Merit Systems Protection Board, which denied her claim, finding she lacked a reasonable belief she had disclosed a violation of law, rule, or regulation. She argues the Board never separately analyzed whether her disclosures also showed gross mismanagement, a second, distinct category the whistleblower law protects. The Federal Circuit affirmed without issuing a written opinion and later denied rehearing.

## What the Court is being asked

The question is whether a federal employee who reports wrongdoing that could fall into two separate protected categories under the whistleblower law is entitled to have the review board actually decide both, rather than rule only on one and treat that as resolving the whole claim.

## Where it stands

The Department of Agriculture waived its right to respond to the petition. The case was distributed for the Court's September 28, 2026 conference, and on October 5, 2026, the Court denied the petition.

## What each outcome would mean

Bolanos-Reynoso asked the Court to take up her case. With the petition denied, the Federal Circuit's decision affirming the Board's ruling against her stands, and her whistleblower retaliation claim does not proceed further in the courts. The denial does not mean the Court agreed with the lower court's reasoning; it simply leaves that outcome in place without the Court deciding the underlying question about whether both disclosure categories must be addressed.
