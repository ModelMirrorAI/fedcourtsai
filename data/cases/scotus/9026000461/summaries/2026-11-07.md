## In brief

A journalist asks the Court to recognize stronger legal protection letting her refuse to name confidential sources in a privacy lawsuit against the government.

## What happened

Catherine Herridge, a journalist, reported on Yanping Chen, a naturalized citizen who had founded a university receiving Defense Department funding. Chen sued federal agencies under the Privacy Act, claiming officials illegally leaked her private records to Herridge. After exhausting other ways to identify the leak's source, Chen subpoenaed Herridge, who refused to name her source, citing a reporter's privilege under the First Amendment and federal evidence rules. The district court ordered disclosure and held Herridge in contempt. The U.S. Court of Appeals for the D.C. Circuit affirmed, applying a two-part test asking whether the source's identity was central to Chen's case and whether Chen had exhausted other options.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked whether, and under what circumstances, a civil plaintiff can force a journalist to reveal a confidential source, and whether courts must weigh broader public and press interests before ordering disclosure, rather than relying only on the two-part test the D.C. Circuit used.

## Where it stands

The petition was filed and distributed for a conference in September 2026. The government, representing the agencies involved, waived its right to respond, and Chen separately filed a brief opposing review. Several outside groups have since filed briefs supporting Herridge's position. The docket shows no decision yet on whether the Court will hear the case.

## What each outcome would mean

Herridge, the journalist, is asking the Court to take up the case and overturn the lower court's ruling. If the Court grants review, it would hear full arguments on how much protection reporters get against revealing sources in civil cases, without yet deciding who wins. If it denies review, the D.C. Circuit's decision stands, meaning Herridge would remain under the order to disclose her source's identity to Chen under the existing contempt ruling.
