## In brief

A journalist asks the Court to review a ruling that she must reveal the confidential sources behind her reporting in a privacy lawsuit against the government.

## What happened

Catherine Herridge, a journalist, reported in 2017 on Yanping Chen, the founder of a school that received Defense Department tuition funds, citing records Chen says came from a sealed FBI investigation into her. Chen sued federal agencies under the Privacy Act, which restricts government disclosure of personal records, and sought Herridge's confidential sources through discovery. Herridge refused, citing a reporter's privilege. The district court ordered her to answer and later held her in contempt; the Court of Appeals for the District of Columbia Circuit affirmed, applying a two-factor test asking whether the information was central to Chen's case and whether she had exhausted other sources.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked to decide when, if ever, people suing in civil cases can force journalists to name confidential sources, and whether courts should weigh a reporter's and the public's interest in confidentiality against a plaintiff's need for the information, rather than applying a narrower two-part test.
