## In brief

A journalist asks the Court to limit when civil plaintiffs can force reporters to reveal confidential sources.

## What happened

Yanping Chen sued federal agencies, alleging officials unlawfully leaked her private records to Fox News reporter Catherine Herridge, who then published stories questioning Chen's ties to the Chinese military. After years of discovery failed to identify the leaker, Chen subpoenaed Herridge to learn her source's identity. Herridge refused, citing a reporter's privilege under the First Amendment. The district court ruled Chen had overcome that privilege and held Herridge in contempt when she still refused to answer. The Court of Appeals for the District of Columbia Circuit affirmed, applying its existing two-factor test for overcoming the privilege and declining to adopt a broader privilege rooted in federal common law.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked to decide when, if ever, someone suing the government can force a journalist to name a confidential source, and whether courts should weigh a broader set of interests—such as the importance of the story or the harm from disclosure—rather than apply a narrower test focused only on whether the information is essential and other options have been tried.

## Where it stands

The petition was distributed for the Court's September 28, 2026 conference, and the docket shows no action recorded since. The government has waived its right to respond, Chen has filed a brief opposing review, and several amici have filed briefs on both sides.

## What each outcome would mean

Herridge, the journalist, is asking the Court to take up the case and overturn the lower court's ruling against her. If the Court grants review and ultimately sides with Herridge, it would make it harder for civil litigants to compel reporters to name confidential sources, and Herridge would not have to identify hers. If the Court instead affirms, Chen's position would prevail, and Herridge would remain subject to the contempt order requiring disclosure. If the Court denies review, the appeals court's decision stands, leaving Herridge obligated to disclose her source under the existing rule. A grant does not by itself decide who wins.
