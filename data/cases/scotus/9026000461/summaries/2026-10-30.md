## In brief

A journalist asks the Court to review an order compelling her to reveal a confidential source in a civil lawsuit against the government.

## What happened

Catherine Herridge, a journalist, reported on Yanping Chen, a university founder whom Chen's reports linked to the Chinese military. Chen later sued federal agencies, alleging they unlawfully disclosed her private records to Herridge in violation of the Privacy Act. In discovery, Chen sought to learn Herridge's confidential source. Herridge invoked a reporter's privilege and refused to answer; the district court held her in contempt. The D.C. Circuit affirmed, applying a two-factor test asking whether the source was central to Chen's claim and whether other avenues were exhausted, and declined to recognize a broader privilege. Rehearing was denied.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked whether, and under what circumstances, a civil plaintiff can force a journalist to disclose a confidential source, and whether courts should weigh broader public and press interests against the plaintiff's need for the information rather than applying a narrower test focused only on whether the source is central to the case and alternatives have been exhausted.

## Where it stands

The petition was distributed for a conference that has already occurred, and the docket shows no action on it since. Chen has since waived her right to respond.

## What each outcome would mean

Herridge, the journalist, is asking the Court to overturn the lower court's order. If the Court takes the case, it would decide the scope of the reporter's privilege without yet deciding who wins. If it declines, the D.C. Circuit's decision stands, and Herridge would remain subject to the contempt order requiring her to disclose her source or face fines. A decision on the merits could affirm the lower court's approach, reverse it in Herridge's favor, or send the case back without resolving the privilege question for either side.
