# Forecast: Herridge v. Chen, No. 26-461 (cert, moment: distribution)

**Bottom line.** The Court denies certiorari. P(any grant) = 0.10.

## Distributions and relists

The docket shows one distribution entry, dated September 2, 2026, for the Conference of September 28, 2026. That entry belongs to motion 26M17 (leave to file the petition under seal with redacted public copies), which the Court granted on October 5. The petition itself was docketed October 7 with a response due November 6, 2026, and has not yet been distributed for conference. So the state I forecast from is "one distribution recorded, zero conferences on the petition's merits."

- The petition will be distributed again, almost certainly: after the brief in opposition (expect at least one extension, so a January 2027 conference), the Clerk distributes it as a matter of course. P(at least one further distribution) = 0.95; the residual is withdrawal or a settlement of the underlying Privacy Act case before the petition reaches a conference.
- Once it reaches a conference, I expect one or two relists before disposition. Paul Clement is counsel of record, the question is a recurring First Amendment one, and at least one Justice (Kavanaugh, who noted at the stay stage that he would have granted a stay) is already engaged. A high-profile petition with an engaged Justice typically draws a relist while a dissent from denial is considered. Expected total distributions at resolution: three, counting the motion's.

## Call for the views of the Solicitor General

No CVSG. The federal agencies are the defendants in the underlying Privacy Act suit and the Court can hear from the government without an invitation if it wants to; the respondent of record is a private party; and the Court has never called for the SG's views on a reporter's-privilege petition in the modern era (none in Risen, Miller/Cooper, or Thomas v. Lee). P(CVSG after prediction time) = 0.06.

## Which question the Court would take, if any

The petition (public copy sealed in part; its framing is known from Chen's stay opposition) asks the Court to replace the D.C. Circuit's centrality-and-exhaustion test with a broader interest-balancing standard for the qualified First Amendment reporter's privilege in civil discovery, and alternatively to recognize a federal common-law newsgathering privilege under Rule 501. If the Court took anything, it would take the First Amendment question in a narrowed form: whether a court must weigh the public interest in newsgathering against the civil litigant's need once centrality and exhaustion are shown. The common-law question would be left behind, as the Court has not recognized a new Rule 501 privilege since Jaffee and the state-law landscape the D.C. Circuit described is too varied to support one.

## Summary disposition

No. There is no intervening decision of this Court bearing on the privilege, so a GVR has nothing to attach to, and the Court does not summarily reverse a unanimous panel applying forty-year-old circuit precedent. P(cert-order disposition | grant) = 0.03.

## Dissent from denial

A written dissent from, or statement respecting, the denial is a live possibility but not the likely outcome. Justice Kavanaugh is the natural author, having noted his vote on the stay application; Justice Gorsuch is the next most likely joiner. The Court denied Risen (2014), Miller and Cooper (2005), and Thomas v. Lee (2006) without any noted dissent, so the modal outcome is a bare denial. P(some dissent or statement | denial) = 0.18.

## If granted

If the Court granted, the likely ground would be that Branzburg's Powell concurrence requires case-by-case balancing that the D.C. Circuit's two-factor test forecloses, and the judgment would be vacated and remanded for that balancing rather than reversed outright, since the district court already made alternative findings under a balancing approach.
