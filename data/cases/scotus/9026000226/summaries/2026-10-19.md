## In brief

The Supreme Court declined to hear the Masts' challenge to a court order barring them from disclosing information identifying the family who sued them.

## What happened

Joshua and Stephanie Mast adopted an Afghan child rescued by U.S. forces; John and Jane Doe, who had also cared for the child, sued the Masts and others in federal court. The district court issued an order barring the Masts from disclosing information identifying the Does, citing safety risks. The Masts appealed, arguing this order improperly restricted their speech. The Fourth Circuit agreed the order restricted speech but upheld it anyway, reasoning that the government has a compelling interest in protecting people perceived as American collaborators abroad, a rationale the petition says no party, including the government, ever raised. The Fourth Circuit denied rehearing.

## What the Court is being asked

The Masts ask the Court to decide whether an appeals court may uphold a speech-restricting order using a justification that neither the parties nor the lower court ever raised or examined, rather than sending the case back for that justification to be tested first. They also ask, as an alternative, that the Court send the case back for reconsideration in light of a different recent decision addressing similar concerns about courts creating their own rationales.

## Where it stands

## What each outcome would mean

The Masts, who lost in the Fourth Circuit, asked the Court to take up their case. Because the petition was denied, the Fourth Circuit's decision stands: the order barring the Masts from disclosing information identifying the Does remains in effect, upheld on the national-security rationale the appeals court adopted. The denial does not mean the Supreme Court agreed with that reasoning; it simply leaves the lower court's ruling in place without the Supreme Court deciding the question itself.
