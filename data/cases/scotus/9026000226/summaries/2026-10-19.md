## In brief

The Supreme Court declined to review the Masts' challenge to a court order barring them from disclosing information identifying the family that sued them.

## What happened

Joshua and Stephanie Mast adopted a young Afghan girl they say was rescued as an injured orphan during a U.S. military operation; Richard Mast, Joshua's brother, served as their attorney. John and Jane Doe, who had also cared for the child and helped evacuate her from Afghanistan, sued the Masts in federal court. Early in that case, the district court issued an order, without hearing from the Masts first, barring them from disclosing information identifying the Does, citing safety concerns. The Masts challenged the order as an unconstitutional restriction on speech. The U.S. Court of Appeals for the Fourth Circuit agreed the order restricted speech based on its content, but upheld it anyway, on a rationale about protecting the government's ability to recruit foreign intelligence sources that no party had raised.

## What the Court is being asked

The Masts asked the Court to decide whether an appeals court may uphold a lower court's order by relying on its own justification, one that neither side nor the lower court ever raised or argued about, rather than reviewing the reasoning the lower court actually gave or sending the case back for further proceedings on the new rationale.

## Where it stands

The petition was distributed for the Court's October 16, 2026 conference, and on October 19, 2026, the Court denied it.

## What each outcome would mean

The Masts asked the Court to take up their case. By denying the petition, the Court leaves the Fourth Circuit's decision in place, meaning the order barring the Masts from disclosing information identifying the Does remains in effect. The denial does not mean the Court agreed with the Fourth Circuit's reasoning; it simply leaves that ruling undisturbed. The Does, who defended the order below, keep the benefit of that ruling without further proceedings in this case.
