## In brief

The Masts ask the Court to review a speech-restricting order upheld on grounds no party ever raised against them in their dispute with the Does.

## What happened

Joshua and Stephanie Mast adopted a young girl rescued as an infant from an Afghanistan battlefield; John and Jane Doe, her relatives, helped evacuate her and later sued the Masts over custody and related issues. Early in that suit, a federal district court barred the Masts from disclosing anything that might identify the Does, citing safety risks. The Masts challenged that order as an unconstitutional restriction on speech. The Fourth Circuit agreed strict legal scrutiny applied but upheld the order anyway, relying on a rationale about protecting the government's ability to recruit foreign intelligence sources—a rationale the petition says no party, including the government, had ever raised. The Fourth Circuit denied rehearing.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked whether an appeals court may uphold a lower court's speech restriction by inventing its own justification for why the restriction is constitutional, rather than reviewing the reasons the lower court actually gave or sending the case back for the parties to address the new rationale first. The petition also asks, as an alternative, whether the Court should send the case back for reconsideration in light of a recent decision on a related procedural question.

## Where it stands

The petition was filed in August 2026. The Does waived their right to respond, and the case was distributed for the Court's October 16, 2026 conference; the docket shows no action since then.

## What each outcome would mean

The Masts are asking the Court to take up the case. If the Court grants review, it will decide whether the appeals court could uphold the speech restriction on a rationale no party raised, with full briefing and argument; a grant does not decide who wins. If the Court denies review, the Fourth Circuit's decision upholding the restriction on the Does stays in place. The Court could also grant, vacate, and send the case back in light of a separate recent ruling, without deciding the question itself.
