# Forecast: Mast v. Doe, No. 26-226 (evt-petition-disposition)

**Bottom line.** The Court denies the petition, most likely at or shortly after the
October 16, 2026 conference. P(any grant, including a GVR) = 0.11.

## Distributions and relists

The docket shows **one distribution** (September 30, 2026, for the conference of
October 16, 2026). Respondents waived their right to respond on September 4, so the
petition reaches the conference unopposed and without amici.

I expect **no further distribution** as the modal path (P(at least one more) = 0.35).
The two ways the count rises are a call for a response and a relist. A call for a
response is the likelier of the two: the Court will not grant or GVR a counseled,
published-opinion First Amendment petition on a waiver, so any interest at all shows
up first as a response request, which pushes the petition to a later conference and
adds a distribution. A relist without a response request is less likely; if the
petition is relisted it will be once, not repeatedly, and a relist here is more
consistent with a Justice drafting a statement or dissent than with a grant.

## CVSG

No CVSG is on the docket and I expect none (P = 0.05). The United States is only a
nominal defendant below and never asserted the national-security interest the Fourth
Circuit relied on. That gap is the petition's argument, and it is the one feature
that gives a CVSG any plausibility, since the Court could ask the Solicitor General
whether the government actually holds the interest the panel attributed to it. But
this is a private tort suit over a protective order, and the Court rarely invites
the SG into a private discovery-order dispute.

## Which question, if any

If the Court acts, it acts on the party-presentation question as the petition frames
it (whether a court of appeals may affirm a prior restraint under strict scrutiny on a
compelling interest no party advanced), not on the merits of the prior restraint.
The Court would not take the underlying First Amendment question: it is fact-bound,
the panel applied strict scrutiny as the petitioners asked, and the record carries
undisputed findings of physical danger to the respondents' family in Afghanistan.

## Summary disposition versus plenary review

Conditional on any grant, a summary route is likelier than argument (P = 0.6). The
petition's own fallback asks for a GVR in light of Margolin v. National Association
of Immigration Judges (2026), which post-dates the panel decision and rehearing denial
and reversed the Fourth Circuit on party presentation. The Court's two recent
corrections of the Fourth Circuit on this principle (Clark v. Sweeney in 2025 and
Margolin in 2026) were short dispositions, and this interlocutory, record-heavy
posture is a poor candidate for plenary briefing. So the realistic grant shape is a
GVR for reconsideration in light of Margolin or a short per curiam, not an argued
case.

## Dissent from denial

On denial, I expect a plain denial with no separate writing (P(statement or dissent
respecting denial) = 0.08). A prior restraint sustained on appeal is the kind of
First Amendment posture that occasionally draws a statement from Justice Thomas,
Justice Alito, or Justice Gorsuch, but the restraint here protects identified
individuals from a documented safety risk, the contempt finding involved sharing a
photograph of the child, and the jurisdictional dissent below gives the denial a
clean procedural explanation. Those facts make a separate writing unattractive.

## Timing

Denial on the October 19 or October 20, 2026 order list in the modal case. If a
response is requested, disposition slips to December 2026 or January 2027.
