## Why 14%, rather than the 5% anchor

The strongest upward adjustment is a concrete route to summary relief, not a prediction that four Justices will want a broad First Amendment merits case. The petition expressly seeks a GVR in light of Margolin, decided after the April 22 appellate decision and May 20 rehearing denial. Margolin's per curiam opinion, pp. 3-5, summarily reversed the Fourth Circuit for substituting an unpresented theory without an opportunity for the parties to address it. It also describes the analogous intervention in Clark v. Sweeney. That verified precedent makes the petition's procedural complaint materially more substantial than a generic assertion of judicial error.

The provisioned appellate opinion independently confirms the important factual premise: App. 26 n.15 distinguishes the district court's personal-safety rationale from the panel's national-security rationale. The panel applies strict scrutiny and relies on confidentiality of perceived collaborators to protect intelligence recruitment. A prior restraint affecting information known before discovery, supported by a rationale the petition says nobody presented, creates a plausible supervisory concern. See petition pp. 11-24 and appendix pp. 17-28.

Several features keep denial substantially more likely. First, Margolin involved transformation of the question being litigated; the panel here could be understood as applying a different legal rationale to an already contested speech restriction and existing factual findings. I do not assume the two are legally identical. Second, the petition's circuit-conflict discussion, pp. 14-16, assembles general party-presentation decisions and criticisms rather than demonstrating squarely incompatible rules in comparable protective-order cases. Third, the actual opinion discusses concrete risks to relatives, permits general discussion of the litigation, and allows identifying disclosures under nondisclosure agreements. Those passages provide more resistance to summary correction than the petition's description alone suggests. See appendix pp. 24-28.

Finally, the appeal is interlocutory and challenges refusal to modify an earlier order. Judge King's dissent concerns appellate jurisdiction, not disagreement with the majority's First Amendment analysis; he explicitly withholds a conclusive merits view. That creates a vehicle complication rather than evidence of a merits split. The response waiver and absence of a response request are modest negative attention signals, not proof that the Court has rejected the argument. I therefore assign **P(any grant) = 0.14**, including GVR and summary reversal, with denial as the categorical call.

## Remaining probabilities and stakes

- **Another distribution: 0.28.** The focused summary-relief request makes further attention plausible, but the most likely trajectory remains denial without another distribution. This is a subjective hazard from the recorded count of one, not the statpack's terminal bucket rate.
- **New CVSG: 0.04.** The asserted national-security interest offers a reason to consult the United States, but the narrow procedural defect could be addressed without an Executive Branch position. A request for the respondents' own response would not be a CVSG.
- **Summary route conditional on any grant: 0.72.** A Margolin-based GVR is the leading grant-side route; summary reversal is another possibility. Plenary review is less likely conditional on intervention. This implies about 10.08% unconditional summary relief and 3.92% plenary grant, not 72% unconditional relief.
- **Separate writing conditional on denial: 0.10.** The speech issue could attract a statement, but I expect no recorded dissent or statement and make no per-Justice vote claim.

The stakes score of **0.57** reflects the speech, appellate-procedure, and national-security implications if decided. It is independent of grant likelihood and does not assume that this petition would decide the child's custody or validate either side's public narrative. The main uncertainty is the distinction between an impermissibly new issue and an alternative ground for affirmance, assessed without a BIO or the complete lower-court briefing.
