I assign a 0.004 probability to any grant and predict denial. The leakage-safe arrival anchor is the sal-v3 `baseline` band's bracketed `reached` rate pooled across OT2017-OT2025: 863 grant-family dispositions among 13,163 resolved paid petitions, or 6.556%. The exact California Second Appellate District bucket provides only a secondary check: 8 grant-family dispositions among 153 resolved petitions (5.23%), from a small heterogeneous, all-trajectory slice.

I adjust sharply below those anchors. The complete, non-truncated nine-page pro se petition supplies one question presented but no developed circuit split, conflicting authority, or concrete account of the facts said to create bias. It relies on *Murchison*, *Caperton*, and *Williams*, which establish a substantive due-process floor in exceptional bias circumstances, but it does not show that those decisions require a reasoned ruling on every adequately alleged disqualification motion. The underlying civil-enforcement action appears ongoing, and review follows summary denials of extraordinary relief, creating serious finality, record, and vehicle concerns. No brief in opposition was provisioned, so I could not test the petition's preservation and jurisdiction assertions against a respondent's account.

From zero distributions, I put 0.96 on at least one later distribution because the petition was docketed with a response date and remains actively pursued; this claim is the initial distribution increment at the arrival moment, not a forecast of a true relist after conference. I put only 0.001 on a later CVSG because the dispute is between private and state/local parties and implicates no apparent federal operational interest. No published prior-Term baseline exists for the conditional summary-route claim. I assign 0.25 conditional on a grant: an extraordinary-bias case can sometimes invite summary correction, but this petition identifies neither an intervening decision for a GVR nor a sufficient factual record for summary reversal. I assign 0.003 to a separate writing conditional on denial because the petition is fact-specific, vehicle-poor, and unlikely to attract a Justice's public statement.
