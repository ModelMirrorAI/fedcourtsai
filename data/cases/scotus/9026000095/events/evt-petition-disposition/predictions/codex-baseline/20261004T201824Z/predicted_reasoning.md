# Forecast

The Court will deny the petition for certiorari, most likely in an ordinary order without an explanatory opinion or separate writing. I assign a 0.6% probability to any grant, including a summary grant. The likely disposition window is the next several weeks after October 4, 2026, rather than extended consideration into the merits calendar.

The snapshot records one conference distribution, on September 9 for the September 28 conference. The September 12 reply and supplemental-brief entries marked “Distributed” do not establish another conference distribution. From that one-distribution state, I expect zero additional distributions; the probability of at least one more is 7%. If the petition is distributed again, one additional distribution is more likely than a prolonged series. The fact that the scheduled conference has passed is not itself evidence of a relist.

No CVSG appears in the record. I forecast no call for the Solicitor General's views, with a 0.1% probability of a new CVSG. This individual state-court disqualification dispute presents no identified federal institutional interest that would make such a request the expected next step.

If review is granted, the Court would most plausibly take a narrowed version of the sole question presented: whether federal due process requires a reasoned resolution of a properly preserved judicial-disqualification claim alleging a constitutionally intolerable risk of bias. The conditional merits inquiry would distinguish an actual constitutional bias problem from dissatisfaction with rulings or the form of state appellate review. Preservation, the content of the trial court's order, and reviewability of the interlocutory posture would have to be addressed before that question could be reached.

Conditional on a grant, I assign 25% to disposition in the cert order itself and 75% to review proceeding beyond that order. No intervening decision warranting a GVR is identified in the supplied materials. A summary correction remains possible if the underlying record establishes a clear constitutional defect, but that is not the expected route. These are conditional route probabilities, not additional unconditional probabilities of relief.

Conditional on denial, I assign 0.8% to any noted dissent or statement respecting denial. I expect neither and do not forecast an individual Justice's vote. Denial would leave the challenged order in place without endorsing the respondent's factual or legal account.
