## Why I reduce the anchor to 0.6%

The petition's sole QP asks whether due process permits summary rejection of a disqualification motion without reasoned analysis when the alleged facts would establish intolerable bias. That is a potentially meaningful constitutional question. But the supplied petition, especially pages 1–4, does not describe the operative bias facts with enough specificity to establish the exceptional premise, and it identifies no concrete conflict between appellate courts. Its assertion of nationally varying procedures is not accompanied by examples of conflicting holdings.

The respondent's BIO supplies several distinct vehicle objections, which I treat as adversarial assertions rather than independently verified findings:

- **Preservation:** BIO pages 2–5 and 7–9 contend that the verified statement and Court of Appeal writ petition raised only California statutory issues, with the federal theory first appearing in the petition for discretionary state-high-court review. The petition expressly says the federal issue was preserved. The supplied files do not include the underlying pleadings needed to resolve that disagreement independently. The BIO's specific references nonetheless make preservation a substantial obstacle rather than a hypothetical one.
- **Mismatch between question and record:** BIO pages 3–4 and 13–14 describe a six-page trial-court order addressing timeliness, sufficiency, tentative rulings, and the state impartiality standard, followed by a short appellate order citing legal authorities. That would materially weaken the premise of unexplained rejection. A state-law explanation does not necessarily answer the petition's narrower complaint about absent federal analysis, so I do not treat the two accounts as logically irreconcilable in every respect.
- **Finality and state-law grounds:** BIO pages 6–10 dispute section 1257 finality because the nuisance case remains pending and identify untimeliness and factual insufficiency as state-law grounds. Even the BIO recognizes a nonfrivolous counterargument concerning exclusive writ review. I price these as additional vehicle risks, not a conclusively established jurisdictional bar, and do not assume every allegation was rejected solely as untimely.
- **Substantive fit:** BIO pages 11–13 characterize the bias allegations as adverse judicial rulings and a tentative ruling, not financial interest, extraordinary campaign influence, or a conflicting prior prosecutorial role. The petition gives no concrete contrary account of an extraordinary interest. A narrow CourtListener check of *Caperton v. A. T. Massey Coal Co.*, 556 U.S. 868 (2009), confirmed that its majority characterized the campaign-influence circumstances as exceptional. That does not adjudicate these petitioners' facts, but it supports requiring a specific factual bridge rather than treating citation of constitutional-bias cases as sufficient.

These weaknesses overlap, so I do not multiply supposed independent rejection probabilities. Collectively they justify reducing the roughly 5% reference rate to 0.6%, with denial the clear modal outcome. The residual probability recognizes the constitutional subject matter, uncertainty about the complete record, and the possibility that petitioners' unprovisioned reply answers the BIO more effectively than their opening petition does.
