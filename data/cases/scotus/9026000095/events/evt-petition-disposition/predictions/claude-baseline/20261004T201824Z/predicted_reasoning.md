# Forecast: what the Court will do with No. 26-95

**Disposition.** The petition will be **denied** on the order list following the
September 28, 2026 conference (expected Monday, October 5, 2026). No grant, no
GVR, no summary reversal.

**Relists.** The docket shows **one distribution** (for the Conference of
9/28/2026) and no relist. I expect **no further distribution**: the petition
is decided at its first conference. The grants from the long conference have
already issued (another OT2026 paid petition distributed for the same
conference is already recorded as granted in the corpus), and a relist for this
petition would ordinarily have been posted by now; the residual chance of a
late-posted relist (for example to await a Justice's statement, or because the
pro se stay application linked as 26A145 draws a second look) is small, on the
order of one in twenty.

**CVSG.** None. No federal party, no federal statute, no federal interest is
implicated; the Court will not call for the views of the Solicitor General.

**Which question, if any.** If the Court somehow took the case it would not
take the question as written, which asks whether a state court may "summarily
deny" a recusal motion "without any reasoned analysis". The BIO shows the trial
court issued a six-page reasoned order and a verified answer, so the premise of
the QP is not supported by the record. A plausible reformulated question would
be whether the Fourteenth Amendment required recusal on these facts, which is a
fact-bound Caperton application the Court does not grant on.

**Summary disposition versus plenary review.** Conditional on any grant, a
summary route (a GVR or a short per curiam) is likelier than briefing and
argument: there is no developed split and nothing to argue over, and the Court
would not set a four-page pro se petition down for plenary review. There is,
however, no intervening decision to GVR in light of, which is why the
conditional is only moderately above even.

**Dissent or statement respecting denial.** None expected. No Justice has
signaled interest in the summary-recusal-denial question in this posture, the
petitioner is pro se, and the record (adverse rulings plus a tentative ruling)
is far from Caperton or Williams. A statement respecting denial is very unlikely.

**If granted (conditional only).** The likely ground would be a narrow
Williams-style holding that due process requires some articulated consideration
of a preserved federal recusal claim. I give this essentially no weight because
the preservation and finality problems the BIO documents would almost certainly
be dispositive before the Court reached it.
