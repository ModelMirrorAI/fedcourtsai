## In brief

A former Shreveport firefighter asks the Court to revive her disability discrimination lawsuit after lower courts dismissed it on summary judgment.

## What happened

Felicia Scroggins worked for the Shreveport Fire Department for over two decades before the City fired her in January 2023, citing years of absences tied to what she describes as a disability. She sued, raising claims under the Americans with Disabilities Act, a retaliation claim under Title VII, and a procedural due process claim. A federal district court granted the City's request for summary judgment, ruling Scroggins was not a "qualified individual" under the ADA and had not shown her firing was retaliatory or that she was denied required procedures. The Fifth Circuit affirmed in a brief unpublished opinion, and later denied rehearing.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked whether an employee who has already returned to work and is performing her job under an accommodation can still be ruled unqualified for ADA purposes mainly because of her past absences and predictions about future attendance, without letting a jury weigh evidence of her actual return and ability to work. The petition also asks whether the lower courts improperly resolved disputed facts against Scroggins rather than leaving them for a jury, as the rules for deciding summary judgment require.

## Where it stands

The City has waived its right to respond to the petition. The case was distributed for the Court's November 13, 2026 conference, and the docket does not yet show any action taken on it.

## What each outcome would mean

Scroggins, as petitioner, is asking the Court to take up her case and reverse the rulings against her. If the Court grants the petition, it would agree to hear the case and decide the legal questions, without yet deciding who wins. If the Court denies the petition, the Fifth Circuit's decision affirming summary judgment for the City stands, and Scroggins's lawsuit remains dismissed.
