## In brief

The Supreme Court declined to hear a former Shreveport firefighter's bid to revive her disability discrimination lawsuit against the city.

## What happened

Felicia Scroggins worked for the Shreveport Fire Department for over two decades before the city fired her in January 2023, citing excessive absences tied to sick leave. She sued, claiming the firing violated the Americans with Disabilities Act and Title VII's retaliation protections, and that she was denied due process. A federal district court granted the city summary judgment on all claims, finding Scroggins could not perform the essential functions of her job even with accommodation, could not show her firing was retaliatory, and had received adequate process. The Fifth Circuit affirmed in a brief, unpublished opinion, and later denied rehearing.

## What the Court is being asked

Scroggins, representing herself, asked the Court to decide whether a court may rule, without letting a jury weigh in, that an employee is not capable of doing her job because of past absences and predictions about future attendance, even though the record showed she had already returned to work and was performing assigned duties under a modified schedule at the time she was fired.

## Where it stands

The petition was distributed for the Court's November 13, 2026 conference, and the Court denied it on November 16, 2026. The city had earlier waived its right to respond to the petition.

## What each outcome would mean

Scroggins, as petitioner, asked the Court to take up her case and overturn the lower court rulings against her. The denial leaves the Fifth Circuit's decision in place, meaning the summary judgment dismissing all her claims against Shreveport stands, and her lawsuit remains closed. Had the Court instead granted the petition, it would have agreed to hear the case fully and decide the legal question, without yet determining which side should win.
