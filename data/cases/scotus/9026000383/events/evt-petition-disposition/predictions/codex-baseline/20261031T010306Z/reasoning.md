I move substantially below the 5.09% anchor for case-specific reasons:

- **No demonstrated split.** QP 1 concerns ADA qualification after a return to limited-duty work; QP 2 concerns drawing factual inferences at summary judgment. The petition chiefly alleges incorrect application of existing standards, rather than identifying competing appellate rules needing resolution. Its own reasons section acknowledges the Court's usual reluctance to grant merely for error correction (petition, printed pp. 20–22).
- **Narrow appellate decision.** The Fifth Circuit's unpublished opinion affirms without substantive analysis (appendix, App. 1). It does not announce a new categorical rule that temporary light-duty work can never establish qualification. The petition's broader formulation is therefore not clearly embodied in a precedential appellate holding.
- **Material vehicle obstacles.** The district court considered more than historical absence: it discussed fitness-for-duty evidence, unpredictable symptoms, and the feasibility of performing the actual Fire Engineer position (App. 13–17). Working a temporary four-hour light-duty schedule does not, on this record alone, establish ability to perform that position's essential functions. More importantly, the district court independently rejected the ADA claim for failure to show pretext (App. 17 n.1–18). Even a favorable qualification ruling would have to overcome that alternative ground. The petition contests it under Rule 56, but doing so makes the case still more record-dependent.
- **Additional claims do not repair the vehicle.** The due-process claim was also deemed abandoned; the petition acknowledges that preservation problem (printed p. 19; appendix, App. 21). The retaliation reasoning is separately tied to causation and pretext. These are not clean substitutes for the two principal questions.
- **Little institutional attention so far.** One ordinary conference distribution after a waiver, with no requested response, amicus filing, or CVSG shown, supplies no affirmative signal offsetting the weak vehicle. This is an observation about the staged record, not a claim that attention cannot develop later.

The best argument for review is that the actual return to work creates a concrete tension with the district court's inference of continuing inability to attend, and its alternative pretext discussion may itself be vulnerable to the petition's summary-judgment objection. I therefore retain a nonzero grant tail. The historical **Tolan v. Cotton, 572 U.S. 650 (2014)** opinion, verified through CourtListener, demonstrates that the Court can summarily correct failure to credit a nonmovant's evidence. It does not establish that this different ADA record warrants the same treatment.
