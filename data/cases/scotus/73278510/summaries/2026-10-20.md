## In brief

Michael Salazar asked the Supreme Court to decide whether people who subscribe to a company's non-video offerings count as "consumers" under a federal video privacy law.

## What happened

Michael Salazar sued Paramount Global, which owns the sports website 247Sports.com, claiming it violated the Video Privacy Protection Act by sharing his Facebook ID and video-watching history with Facebook without his consent. Salazar had signed up for 247Sports's free email newsletter and watched videos on the site. The law only protects "consumers," defined as people who rent, purchase, or subscribe to "goods or services" from a "video tape service provider." A federal district court dismissed the case, and the Sixth Circuit Court of Appeals affirmed, holding that Salazar was not a "consumer" because the newsletter he subscribed to was not itself video or audiovisual content. Other appeals courts had reached different conclusions on similar facts.

## What the Court is being asked

The dispute centers on a phrase in the law defining who counts as a "consumer" of a video provider. Salazar argues the law covers anyone who rents, buys, or subscribes to anything from a business that also provides video content, even unrelated items like a newsletter. Paramount argues the law protects only those who transact specifically for video-related goods or services. The Court must decide which reading is correct.

## Where it stands

The Supreme Court granted review, the case was argued on October 14, 2026, and it now awaits decision on the merits.

## What each outcome would mean

Salazar, who brought the case, asked the Court to reverse the lower court's ruling against him. If the Court affirms, Paramount's win stands, and Salazar's lawsuit remains dismissed because he would not qualify as a "consumer" under the law. If the Court reverses, Salazar's broader reading of "consumer" would prevail, and his lawsuit could proceed. If the Court vacates, it would set aside the Sixth Circuit's decision and send the case back for further proceedings without deciding which side is right.
