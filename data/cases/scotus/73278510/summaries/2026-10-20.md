## In brief

Michael Salazar sued Paramount under a video-privacy law over data sharing with Facebook; the Supreme Court heard the case on what makes someone a protected "consumer."

## What happened

Michael Salazar signed up for a free email newsletter from 247Sports, a sports website owned by Paramount, and later watched free videos on the site while logged into Facebook. He claims the site shared his Facebook ID and viewing history with Facebook without consent, violating the Video Privacy Protection Act, a 1988 law restricting video rental companies from disclosing customers' viewing records. A federal district court dismissed his case, and the Sixth Circuit Court of Appeals agreed, holding that Salazar was not a "consumer" under the law because his newsletter subscription was not itself a video-related product. Other appeals courts have split on this question.

## What the Court is being asked

The case turns on how to define who counts as a "consumer" protected by this privacy law. The law covers people who rent, buy, or subscribe to "goods or services" from a company that also provides video content. The Court must decide whether that phrase covers only video-related offerings, such as movie rentals or streaming subscriptions, or any offering at all from such a company, including something unrelated to video like a free newsletter.

## Where it stands

The Court granted review in January 2026, and the case was argued on October 14, 2026. It now awaits decision.

## What each outcome would mean

Salazar, who sued Paramount and is asking the Court to overturn the lower court's ruling against him, would have his case revived and sent back for further proceedings if the Court rules in his favor, meaning his lawsuit could continue. If the Court sides with Paramount instead, the lower court's dismissal of Salazar's lawsuit would stand, ending his case. The Court could also vacate the lower court's decision and send the case back without deciding the question for either side, leaving the dispute to be resolved in further proceedings.
