## In brief

The Court will decide how broadly to read "consumer" under a 1988 video-privacy law, resolving whether Michael Salazar's data-sharing lawsuit against Paramount's 247Sports can proceed.

## What happened

Michael Salazar signed up for a free email newsletter from 247Sports, a sports website owned by Paramount. He later watched free videos on the site while logged into Facebook, and Paramount allegedly shared his Facebook ID and viewing activity with Facebook without his consent. Salazar sued under the Video Privacy Protection Act, which lets a "consumer" sue a "video tape service provider" for disclosing certain viewing information without consent. The lower courts dismissed his case, holding that Salazar was not a "consumer" because the newsletter he subscribed to was not itself a video-related product. Other appeals courts have read "consumer" more broadly to include anyone who buys or subscribes to anything from such a company, creating disagreement among courts nationwide.

## What the Court is being asked

The law protects "consumers," defined as people who rent, buy, or subscribe to "goods or services" from a company that also deals in video materials. The Court is asked whether that phrase covers only video-related purchases and subscriptions, or whether it covers any purchase or subscription at all from such a company, even something unrelated to video, like a newsletter.

## Where it stands

The Court granted review in January 2026. Briefing concluded in the summer of 2026, and the case was argued on October 14, 2026. A decision has not yet been issued as of the snapshot date.

## What each outcome would mean

Salazar, who sued Paramount and lost in the lower courts, asked the Court to take up the case. A ruling for Salazar would adopt the broader reading of "consumer," reviving his lawsuit and sending the case back for further proceedings under that standard. A ruling for Paramount would uphold the lower court's narrower reading, leaving the dismissal of Salazar's claims in place. The Court could also vacate the lower court's decision and send the case back without resolving the question for either side.
