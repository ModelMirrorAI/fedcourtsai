## What happened

Michael Salazar signed up for a free email newsletter from 247Sports, a sports news website owned by Paramount Global. He alleges that when he later watched free videos on the site while logged into Facebook, 247Sports used a tracking tool that sent his Facebook ID and viewing history to Facebook without his consent. Salazar sued under a federal law that lets a "consumer" of a "video tape service provider" sue for disclosing such information without permission. The district court dismissed his case, and the Sixth Circuit Court of Appeals agreed, holding that Salazar was not a "consumer" because a newsletter isn't a video product. Other appeals courts have split on this question, with some ruling the opposite way.

## What the Court is being asked

The law defines a "consumer" as someone who rents, buys, or subscribes to goods or services from a video-related business. The Court is asked to decide whether that phrase covers any goods or services someone gets from such a business, even something unrelated to video like a free newsletter, or whether it only covers video-related goods or services. Paramount argues that reading the law more broadly would turn a decades-old video privacy law into a sweeping rule affecting ordinary internet advertising practices, while Salazar argues the law's plain wording is not limited to video-related transactions.

## Where it stands

The Court granted cert and the case has been argued (heard on October 14, 2026, per the docket) and briefed by both sides along with numerous outside groups; it now awaits a decision.
