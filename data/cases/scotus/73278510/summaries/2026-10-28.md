## In brief

Michael Salazar argues the Video Privacy Protection Act protects anyone who buys anything from a video provider, not just video customers; Paramount Global disagrees.

## What happened

Michael Salazar sued Paramount Global, alleging that its 247Sports.com website shared his video-watching history and Facebook ID with Facebook without his consent, violating the Video Privacy Protection Act (VPPA), a 1988 law restricting video rental companies from disclosing customer viewing records. Salazar had signed up for 247Sports' free email newsletter. A federal district court dismissed his suit, and the Sixth Circuit Court of Appeals affirmed, holding that Salazar was not a "consumer" protected by the law because he had not subscribed to video-related goods or services, only a newsletter. Other circuit courts had reached different conclusions on similar facts, with the Second and Seventh Circuits interpreting the law more broadly.

## What the Court is being asked

The law protects "consumers" of video providers from having their viewing history disclosed without consent, and defines a consumer as someone who rents, buys, or subscribes to "goods or services" from such a provider. The Court is asked whether that phrase covers only video-related goods or services, or any goods or services at all, even ones unrelated to video, such as a free email newsletter.

## Where it stands

The Court granted the petition and the case was argued on October 14, 2026. It now awaits a decision on the merits.

## What each outcome would mean

Salazar, who brought the original lawsuit and asked the Court to take up the case, is now asking the Court to reverse the lower court's ruling against him. If the Court affirms, Paramount's win in the lower courts stands, and Salazar's lawsuit remains dismissed; people who acquire only non-video goods or services from such companies would not be protected by the law regarding later disclosures of their video-viewing activity. If the Court reverses, Salazar's case would go forward, and his newsletter subscription would count toward making him a "consumer" under the law. The Court could also vacate the ruling and send the case back for further proceedings without deciding the question for either side.
