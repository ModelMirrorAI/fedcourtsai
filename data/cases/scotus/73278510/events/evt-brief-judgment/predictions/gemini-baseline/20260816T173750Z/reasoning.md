**Base Rates & Anchoring:**

**Adjustments & Case Specifics:**
I adjusted the probability of reversal upward from the 69.7% base rate to 85% based on the strength of the petitioner's textualist argument. The case presents a clean circuit split over the interpretation of "goods or services" in the VPPA's definition of "consumer". The Sixth Circuit adopted a purposive limitation, restricting the phrase to "audiovisual goods or services," which diverges from the plain text. The petitioner's argument relies heavily on the presumption of meaningful variation—Congress specified "video materials" in the definition of protected information but omitted it in the definition of consumer.

Given the current Court's strong textualist methodology, I expect a clear majority to reject the Sixth Circuit's atextual limitation. While the respondent (represented by Paul Clement) mounts a strong structural argument that interpreting "goods or services" literally creates a mismatch in the statute's interlocking definitions, the Court's recent jurisprudence consistently prioritizes plain text over structural purposivism when the text is unambiguous.
