# Predicted Reasoning

The Court will reverse the judgment of the Sixth Circuit. The majority will rest its decision squarely on the plain text of the Video Privacy Protection Act (VPPA), interpreting the statutory definition of "consumer"—a "renter, purchaser, or subscriber of goods or services from a video tape service provider"—as unqualified and reaching all goods or services such a provider offers. The majority will apply the presumption of meaningful variation, noting that while Congress explicitly limited the definition of "personally identifiable information" to "video materials," it omitted any such audiovisual limitation from the definition of "consumer." 

The Court will establish a categorical rule that any individual who transacts with a covered video tape service provider qualifies as a "consumer," rejecting the Sixth Circuit's narrower, purposive reading. The Court will likely emphasize that its role is to apply the text as written, even if the result applies a video-privacy statute to a plaintiff subscribing to a newsletter.

The decision is expected to be a 7-2 or 6-3 split. The conservative textualist wing (Justices Thomas, Alito, Gorsuch, Barrett) along with the liberal Justices (Sotomayor, Kagan, Jackson), who generally favor broad constructions of consumer privacy statutes, will form the majority. Justices Roberts and Kavanaugh are the most likely dissenters, potentially adopting the respondent's structural argument that "goods or services" must be read in context to mean only the video-related services that generate the protected privacy interests. We do not expect the Court to dismiss the case as improvidently granted or affirm by an equally divided Court.
