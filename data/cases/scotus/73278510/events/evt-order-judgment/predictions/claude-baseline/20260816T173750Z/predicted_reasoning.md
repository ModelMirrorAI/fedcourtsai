# Forecast: Salazar v. Paramount Global, dba 247Sports (No. 25-459) — merits judgment

**Judgment class: affirmed** (probability the judgment below is disturbed: 0.45).
The Court will hold that a "consumer" under the Video Privacy Protection Act,
18 U.S.C. § 2710(a)(1), must rent, purchase, or subscribe to *audiovisual*
goods or services from a video tape service provider, and that Salazar's
subscription to 247Sports' free online newsletter does not qualify — adopting
the Sixth and D.C. Circuits' reading over the Second and Seventh Circuits',
and affirming the Sixth Circuit's judgment of dismissal.

**Vote lineup: 6–3.** Majority: Roberts, Thomas, Alito, Kagan, Kavanaugh,
Barrett (author). Dissent: Gorsuch (author), joined by Sotomayor and Jackson,
on plain-text grounds — "goods or services" is unmodified, and Congress used
"audio visual materials" elsewhere in the statute when it meant to limit.
A lopsided or even unanimous defendant-side result on the Facebook v. Duguid
model is the main alternative to this split; a plaintiff-side reversal, if it
comes, would likely be a cross-ideological textualist coalition led by
Gorsuch or Kagan. No recusal is apparent on the docket, so an
equally-divided affirmance is a negligible risk.

**Ground.** The majority rests on statutory context rather than bare plain
meaning: the prepositional phrase "from a video tape service provider"
tethers "goods or services" to what the provider offers in its defined
capacity; the renter/purchaser/subscriber triad in § 2710(a)(1) parallels
"rental, sale, or delivery" of "prerecorded video cassette tapes or similar
audio visual materials" in § 2710(a)(4); and § 2710(a)(3)'s definition of
personally identifiable information ("specific video materials or services")
confirms the statute's video-transaction focus. Expect the opinion to invoke
the Duguid line about not repurposing a 1988 privacy statute into a general
statutory-damages engine for pixel-tracking class actions.

**Breadth.** Categorical: a general construction of "consumer" that
forecloses non-audiovisual-subscription VPPA suits nationwide, not a holding
confined to these facts. The Court will likely reserve the question both
courts below assumed — whether Paramount is a "video tape service provider"
in the first place — and will not need to decide whether Salazar's *use* of
the newsletter to view videos could qualify, since his theory rests on the
newsletter subscription itself.

**Separate writings.** A Gorsuch dissent is the most likely separate
writing. A short concurrence emphasizing that the PII definition would
independently limit liability is possible but not expected to split the
majority's rationale from its result.

**Procedural exits.** A DIG is unlikely (~2%): the vehicle was chosen over
the interlocutory NBA petition (No. 24-994) precisely because it presents a
final judgment on a clean record with a single question presented, and the
merits briefing raises no vehicle problems visible on the docket.

**Timing** (context, not a scored claim): argued October 14, 2026 — the
first argument session of OT2026. A statutory case argued in October
typically comes down between January and April; expect a decision in the
first quarter of 2027, and by June 2027 at the outside.
