# Salazar v. Paramount Global (No. 25-459) — forecast of the judgment

**Judgment: reversed** (remanded for further proceedings). The Court rejects the
Sixth Circuit's holding that a VPPA "consumer" must have rented, purchased, or
subscribed to *audiovisual* goods or services. A "vacated and remanded" label is
the close alternative — the petitioner asked for vacatur — and would be the same
result on the disturbed axis. A DIG is live but unlikely (about 4%): Justice
Barrett pressed both counsel on Article III standing for a pixel disclosure, and
respondent offered supplemental briefing, but neither party briefed it and every
circuit to reach VPPA standing has found it.

**Vote: 7–2.** Majority: Roberts, Sotomayor, Kagan, Gorsuch, Kavanaugh, Barrett,
Jackson. Dissent: Thomas and Alito. The Chief Justice is the least certain
member of the majority. He opened with Justice Breyer's "no pets in the park"
point about purpose, but he also pushed respondent off its easy hypotheticals.
A 6–3 with Roberts in dissent is the main alternative; a 5–4 needs Barrett or
Kavanaugh to move as well.

**Who writes.** The opinion of the Court is most likely Kagan's. At argument
she put the textual case most crisply: three definitions, two carrying a video
modifier and one without it, read most naturally give the petitioner's answer.
A textualist from the conservative side (Gorsuch, Kavanaugh, Barrett) is the
next likeliest group. October-sitting assignments are unknown to me. I expect
Alito to write the dissent: a 1988 statute built around video-tape technology,
and "from a video tape service provider" read as limiting what is supplied
(his Costco-gas hypothetical). Thomas may join him or write separately, perhaps
questioning whether streaming is "similar audiovisual materials." A concurrence
is plausible from Kavanaugh or Barrett, stressing that the VTSP ("engaged in
the business"), PII and standing limits remain open. Jackson may write about
statutory purpose.

**Ground.** The Court treats "goods or services" as unmodified. "From a video
tape service provider" names the source, not the subject matter, and the
contrast with "video materials or services" in (a)(3) and "audiovisual
materials" in (a)(4) is decisive. The respondent's structural argument — that
the transaction making someone a consumer must be the one that generates PII,
so that consent works coherently — loses to the text and to (b)(1)'s "any
consumer of such provider." The Court answers the consequences argument by
pointing to the elements left for remand and to easy prospective consent.

**Breadth.** The holding is narrow. It decides only the "consumer" definition.
It expressly reserves whether 247Sports is a "video tape service provider,"
whether a Facebook-ID disclosure is PII (a 3–1 split the petitioner conceded
cuts against them), and standing. It remands without any broader rule on
online video or ad-tech data sharing.
