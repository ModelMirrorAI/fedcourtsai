## In brief

Pennsylvania asks the Court to review a ruling that struck down the state's requirement that mail-in voters handwrite a date on their ballot envelopes.

## What happened

Voters and Democratic-aligned groups sued Pennsylvania's county election boards, arguing that rejecting mail-in ballots over missing or incorrect handwritten dates on return envelopes violates the First and Fourteenth Amendments. A district court agreed and blocked enforcement of the date requirement. The Third Circuit affirmed, finding the requirement imposes at least a minimal burden on voters that Pennsylvania's asserted interests, including fraud prevention, did not justify. The full Third Circuit narrowly declined to rehear the case, over two dissents. Pennsylvania, which had intervened to defend the law, petitioned the Supreme Court, arguing the panel misunderstood state law and that federal appeals courts disagree on how to evaluate minor voting burdens. Various county election boards and outside groups have taken differing positions on the petition.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked to decide whether Pennsylvania's rule requiring mail-in voters to date their ballot envelopes violates the Constitution's protections for voting rights, and separately whether the case should instead be sent back for reconsideration in light of a Pennsylvania Supreme Court decision issued after the appeals ruling.

## Where it stands

The petition has been distributed for conference twice, and after the second distribution the Court invited the Solicitor General to file a brief giving the federal government's views. Briefing, including amicus filings and waivers by many county respondents, is otherwise complete.

## What each outcome would mean

Pennsylvania, joined by the Republican National Committee and allied groups, asks the Court to take up the case. If the Court grants review, it would hear full arguments and decide whether the date requirement is constitutional, or whether to send the case back for further review without deciding the question. If the Court denies review, the Third Circuit's decision striking down the date requirement stands, meaning Pennsylvania could not reject mail ballots solely for dating errors. Some county election boards and the voters who sued support leaving that ruling in place; others take no position.
