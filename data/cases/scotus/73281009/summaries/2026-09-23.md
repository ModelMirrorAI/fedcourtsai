## What happened

Pennsylvania requires voters casting mail-in ballots to handwrite a date on the return envelope, alongside their signature. Voter Bette Eakin and several organizations sued the state's county election boards, arguing that rejecting ballots for missing or incorrect dates violates the U.S. Constitution. A federal district court agreed and blocked enforcement of the date requirement; the Third Circuit Court of Appeals affirmed, and later denied rehearing by the full court over two dissents. Pennsylvania, which had intervened to defend the law, asks the Supreme Court to review that decision. A related petition was filed by the Republican National Committee and allied parties.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked whether requiring mail-in voters to handwrite a date on their ballot envelope, when officials disqualify ballots that lack a correct date, violates voters' constitutional rights to free speech and equal treatment under the law. Pennsylvania also argues the case should be sent back to the lower court in light of a Pennsylvania Supreme Court ruling issued after the appeals court's decision, and separately asks the Court to clarify how much scrutiny courts should apply to election rules that impose only minor burdens on voters. Some respondents argue the timing of any decision this close to the next election counsels against the Court stepping in at all.

## Where it stands

The case has been fully briefed, with multiple counties and other parties filing briefs supporting or opposing review, or taking no position, and some amicus briefs have been accepted. The Court has invited the Solicitor General to submit the federal government's views on the case, meaning no decision on whether to hear the case will occur until that brief is filed.
