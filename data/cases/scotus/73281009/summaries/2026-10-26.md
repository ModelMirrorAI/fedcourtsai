## In brief

Pennsylvania asks the Supreme Court to review a ruling that its mail-in ballot date requirement violates voters' constitutional rights.

## What happened

Pennsylvania law requires mail-in voters to handwrite a date on their ballot return envelope; ballots with a missing or incorrect date are discarded. Bette Eakin and other voters and organizations sued Pennsylvania's county election boards, arguing the requirement violates federal law and the Constitution. The Republican National Committee and allied groups intervened to defend the requirement. A federal district court ruled for Eakin, and the Third Circuit Court of Appeals affirmed, holding that the requirement imposes a minimal but unjustified burden on voting rights. Pennsylvania, which had intervened on appeal to defend the requirement, and the Republican National Committee separately petitioned the Supreme Court to review that decision.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked whether a state's requirement that mail-in voters handwrite a date on their ballot envelope, with noncompliant ballots discarded, violates the First or Fourteenth Amendment. Pennsylvania also asks the Court, as an alternative to full review, to send the case back to the lower court in light of a Pennsylvania Supreme Court decision issued after the Third Circuit's ruling, which Pennsylvania says undercuts the lower court's reasoning about voters' ability to fix ballot errors.

## Where it stands

The petition has been fully briefed, including responses from various county election boards and a reply from Pennsylvania, and was distributed for the Court's June 25, 2026 conference. The Court has since invited the Solicitor General to file a brief expressing the federal government's views on whether the case should be heard; the docket shows no decision yet on whether to grant review.

## What each outcome would mean

Pennsylvania, supported by the Republican National Committee, asks the Court to take up the case and reverse the Third Circuit's ruling, which Eakin and other voters won below. If the Court grants review and later reverses, Pennsylvania's date requirement would be upheld and county boards could again discard ballots with missing or incorrect dates. If the Court denies review, the Third Circuit's decision stands, and county boards must continue counting ballots despite date errors. A grant without a decision on the merits would mean only that the Court will hear full arguments before deciding the question.
