# Forecast: what the Court will do with No. 25-967, Pennsylvania v. Eakin

**Vantage.** The docket shows two distributions (Conference of 4/17/2026, pulled by a
response request on 4/1; Conference of 6/25/2026) and a CVSG issued 6/29/2026. The
Solicitor General has not filed. The petition is vided with No. 25-962 (RNC v. Eakin),
which drew the same CVSG the same day; the two will be disposed of together.

**Disposition.** P(any grant) = 0.55. The more likely path to a grant is plenary
review rather than a GVR; the single most likely *label* is close to a coin flip
between `granted` and `denied`, with `gvr` a real third route.

**Relists.** At least one further distribution is near-certain (0.96): a CVSG petition is
always redistributed once the SG's brief arrives. Expect the SG brief in December 2026
or early January 2027 (the customary timing for a late-June invitation, aimed at the
January conferences), redistribution in January, and a disposition between mid-January
and March 2027. If the SG slips to the May filing window, disposition comes in June
2027. A further relist or two after the SG's brief is likely if the Court is drafting
a dissent from denial or holding for the Pennsylvania Supreme Court's decision in
*Baxter v. Philadelphia Board of Elections*.

**CVSG.** Already on the docket; nothing further to forecast. The SG will most likely
side with the Commonwealth and the RNC on the merits (the date requirement is
constitutional under Anderson-Burdick) and recommend that the Court grant (roughly
three chances in four), either plenary review or a GVR in light of *Center for
Coalfield Justice*; a recommendation to deny would rest on vehicle grounds (the
pending *Baxter* state-constitutional challenge, and the Commonwealth's own election
officials disclaiming any interest in the requirement).

**Which question the Court would take.** The QP as written, though a grant would in
practice be framed around 25-962's broader Anderson-Burdick question: whether a
generally applicable, minimally burdensome ballot-casting rule (the "usual burdens of
voting") gets rational-basis review or heightened Anderson-Burdick balancing. The Court
is unlikely to take the case merely to reweigh Pennsylvania's interests in the date;
the interest is in the standard.

**Summary disposition.** Conditional on a grant, P(disposed in the cert order) = 0.30.
The petition's lead request is a GVR in light of *Coalfield Justice* (Pa. Sept. 26,
2025), which undercut the panel's premise that an undated-ballot voter has no recourse.
The Court passed on that route at the 6/25 conference and asked the SG instead, which
reads as interest in the merits standard; but a GVR remains a cheap exit the SG may
itself recommend, and the Court has GVR'd on intervening state-high-court decisions
in analogous posture (*Lords Landing*, *Thomas v. American Home Products*). Plenary
review would be argued in April 2027 at the earliest, more likely OT2027.

**If denied.** P(some Justice writes) = 0.40. Justice Alito, who as Circuit Justice
has already written that the Third Circuit's earlier date-requirement ruling was very
likely wrong (*Ritter v. Migliori*, 2022, joined by Justices Thomas and Gorsuch), is the
likeliest author of a dissent from denial or a statement respecting denial; a denial
that follows a *Baxter* ruling mooting the question would more likely draw a short
statement than a dissent.

**Mootness path.** If the Pennsylvania Supreme Court decides *Baxter* against the date
requirement under the Free and Equal Elections Clause before the Court acts, the
likeliest outcome is a denial (or a hold followed by denial); a Munsingwear-style
vacatur is a smaller possibility.

**If granted, conditionally.** The likely ground is that "usual burdens of voting"
that are generally applicable and minimal do not trigger heightened Anderson-Burdick
balancing, and the Third Circuit's judgment would be reversed or vacated; the Court
would not reach whether the date serves any interest beyond rational basis.
