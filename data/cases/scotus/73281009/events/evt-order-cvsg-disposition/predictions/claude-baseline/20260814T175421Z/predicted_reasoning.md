# Forecast: Pennsylvania v. Eakin, No. 25-967 (CVSG-moment cell)

## Where the docket stands

The snapshot shows two distributions (Conferences of 4/17/2026 and 6/25/2026),
a response requested on 4/1/2026, cert-stage amicus briefs on both sides of the
question, respondent-side briefs *in support* of certiorari (the RNC
respondents and two county boards), and the CVSG order of 6/29/2026. The
petition is linked with No. 25-962 (RNC v. Eakin), a companion petition from
the same Third Circuit judgment. I forecast from that state.

## The disposition

**The grant family is slightly more likely than not (0.52), and plenary grant
is the single likeliest route within it.** The Solicitor General will file a
brief — on the usual CVSG clock for a late-June invitation, most likely
between December 2026 and May 2027 — and I expect the recommendation to favor
the petitioners: the current administration's Justice Department has
consistently sided with state ballot-integrity requirements, the petitioner is
a State defending its own election code against federal constitutional
invalidation, and the petition presents an acknowledged 7–4 circuit split on
Anderson-Burdick step two that the en banc dissents below certified as real.
If the SG recommends grant, expect a grant within one or two conferences of
redistribution, with argument in October Term 2027; the case would be
consolidated with or heard alongside No. 25-962.

**The main path to a non-grant is mootness, not lack of interest.** Baxter v.
Philadelphia Board of Elections — argued in the Pennsylvania Supreme Court in
September 2025 and still undecided as of this forecast — asks whether the same
date requirement violates the state constitution's Free and Equal Elections
Clause. If that court strikes the requirement on independent state grounds
before this petition is disposed of (a live possibility over the long CVSG
timeline), the federal question becomes academic. Even then, I expect the
Commonwealth and the RNC to seek Munsingwear vacatur of the Third Circuit's
judgment rather than simple denial — this very litigation lineage produced
exactly that disposition in Ritter v. Migliori (2022) — so a meaningful share
of the mootness branch still resolves as a grant-and-vacate rather than a
denial. Approximate scenario weights: plenary grant ~0.37, GVR/Munsingwear
vacatur ~0.15, denial ~0.45, dismissal ~0.03.

## Relist and CVSG claims

- **Further distribution: near-certain (0.97).** A CVSG'd petition is
  redistributed once the SG's brief arrives; only a withdrawal or an
  extraordinary summary disposition before the brief would prevent at least
  one more conference. Expect two to three further distributions in total
  (the post-SG-brief distribution, plus the customary relist before a grant).
- **A further CVSG: effectively nil (0.02).** The invitation is already on the
  docket; this claim is vacuous for this cell and the harness masks it.

## Which question, and how

If the Court grants, it takes the Commonwealth's QP essentially as written —
whether the handwritten-date requirement violates the First or Fourteenth
Amendment — because the QP already frames the Anderson-Burdick scrutiny
question the split is about. A GVR *in light of Coalfield Justice* (the
petition's lead ask) is possible but less likely than plenary review: the
Court called for the SG's views on the merits question rather than GVR'ing on
the state-law point when it had the chance at two conferences.

## If denied

Expect a denial to be accompanied by a statement or dissent from denial —
Justice Alito (the Circuit Justice, who granted the extension application) or
Justice Thomas are the likeliest authors, on the theme that Anderson-Burdick's
scope needs resolution — unless the denial follows a state-court mootness
ruling, in which case a quiet denial or a Munsingwear order is likelier than a
separate writing.
