# Predicted reasoning — Pennsylvania v. Eakin (25-967), forecast at the CVSG moment

**Disposition.** The Court will more likely than not grant this petition in
some form (P ≈ 0.55, grant family including a GVR). The modal path is a
plenary grant, probably consolidated with the companion petition in No.
25-962 (RNC v. Eakin, vided on this docket), with argument in the winter or
spring of OT2026 and a decision by June 2027 — in time to govern the 2028
election cycle.

**Relists.** The docket shows two distributions (Conferences of 4/17/2026 and
6/25/2026) before the June 29 CVSG. Nothing more will happen until the
Solicitor General files: expect the SG's brief in the late fall or winter of
2026 (a June invitation typically returns in five to eight months, often near
the December filing window), then one to three further distributions before a
disposition, most likely an order between January and April 2027. The
relist-increment claim is near-certain (0.97): every live path — grant, GVR,
or denial after the SG weighs in — runs through at least one further
distribution.

**CVSG.** Already on the docket (June 29, 2026); the increment claim is
vacuous for this cell and a second invitation essentially never issues.

**The SG's position.** The current Solicitor General is very likely to
support the petitioners' side — the federal government under this
administration has aligned with enforcing state ballot-integrity rules, and
the RNC (a respondent here) supports certiorari. Expect a recommendation to
grant, or to GVR in light of Center for Coalfield Justice; an outright
deny recommendation is the less likely branch, and even then the Court's
demonstrated interest (a called-for response after universal waivers, then a
CVSG) makes a follow-the-SG denial not automatic.

**Which question presented.** If the Court grants plenary review it takes the
QP essentially as written, or reformulates slightly toward the methodological
question the en banc dissents framed: whether a minimally burdensome,
generally applicable election regulation triggers anything more than
rational-basis review under Anderson-Burdick. (Context-only; not scored.)

**Summary disposition.** Conditional on a grant, the cert-order route is a
real possibility (≈ 0.35): the petition's *lead* ask is a vacate-and-remand in
light of Coalfield Justice, and if the Pennsylvania Supreme Court's pending
decision in Baxter strikes the date requirement on state constitutional
grounds first, a Munsingwear-style vacatur of the Third Circuit's judgment is
the natural exit. The CVSG cuts the other way — the Court rarely asks for the
SG's views merely to GVR — so plenary review remains the modal grant path.

**If denied.** A denial most plausibly follows a mooting Baxter ruling or an
SG deny recommendation. Expect a reasonable chance (≈ 0.45) of a dissent from
denial or a statement respecting denial — this issue has drawn separate
writings before (the Ritter v. Migliori sequence), and Justices Alito, Thomas,
or Gorsuch are the likeliest authors (the "from whom" half is context-only).

**If granted and argued.** Conditionally: the likely ground of decision is
that the panel misapplied Anderson-Burdick to a de minimis, generally
applicable rule, with reversal more likely than affirmance given the current
Court's election-law docket — but this is conditional context, not a claim
this cert-stage event resolves.
