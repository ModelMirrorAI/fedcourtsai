# Forecast

The petition has been distributed twice, for the April 17 and June 25, 2026 conferences. After calling for the Solicitor General's views on June 29, the Court will almost certainly distribute it at least once more after the United States files. One further post-brief distribution is most likely, although an additional relist remains possible. No further CVSG is expected because the invitation has already issued.

Denial is slightly more likely than a grant-family disposition. The Court's request for the United States' views shows substantial interest, but the case presents two obstacles to plenary review: the parties dispute whether *Center for Coalfield Justice* actually changed the state-law premise of the Third Circuit's decision, and a parallel Pennsylvania constitutional case could alter or moot the controversy. The asserted Anderson–Burdick split also may look more like divergent application of a balancing framework to an unusual ballot-envelope rule than a clean conflict requiring plenary review.

If the Court grants relief, a grant-vacate-remand order is more likely than plenary briefing and argument. The petition leads with a request for reconsideration in light of *Coalfield Justice*, and the Solicitor General can address whether that intervening state decision undermines the Third Circuit's premise about notice and provisional voting. If the Court instead takes the case for plenary review, it will likely take the single question presented substantially as written, while focusing it on how Anderson–Burdick treats a facially minimal, generally applicable ballot-completion requirement.

Disposition will likely follow the Solicitor General's brief and redistribution during October Term 2026. If certiorari is denied, no separate writing is expected. The most plausible exception is a statement or dissent from Justice Alito, potentially joined by Justice Thomas, emphasizing state control of election administration and the claimed effect of the intervening Pennsylvania decision.
