# Rationale

I assign a 0.46 probability to a grant-family disposition and predict denial as the most likely disposition. The leakage-safe anchor is the prior-Term sal-v3 `high` risk set in the committed statpack: pooling OT2017 through OT2024 gives 439 grant-family outcomes over a denial-reweighted denominator of 1,074, or 40.88%. The broader paid CVSG cut is directionally consistent but not leakage-matched by Term: 57 of 163 resolved petitions with a CVSG reached the grant family, or 34.97%. The frozen `high` risk-set rate is therefore the governing anchor.

I adjust above 40.88% because the record shows several affirmative signals: the Court requested a response after initial waivers, the petition was distributed twice, and the Court then invited the Solicitor General's views. The Commonwealth's petition also supplies a facially plausible summary route. It argues that the intervening Pennsylvania Supreme Court decision in *Center for Coalfield Justice* undercut the Third Circuit's understanding that an undated-ballot voter necessarily lacked notice and recourse, and it points to a closely divided denial of rehearing and an asserted circuit disagreement over scrutiny of minimal election-law burdens.

I limit the adjustment because the complete provisioned opposition materials identify substantial vehicle and certworthiness problems. They argue that *Coalfield Justice* applies only when a county voluntarily reviews and segregates defective ballots before election day, that the Third Circuit had the decision before it during rehearing, and that the panel's conclusion did not depend solely on notice or cure. They also dispute the asserted circuit split, characterize the handwritten-date rule as an obsolete and unusually fact-bound measure, and identify a pending Pennsylvania constitutional challenge that could independently eliminate the rule. The CVSG itself already drives the frozen high band, so treating it as a separate large uplift would double-count the strongest docket signal.

The 0.97 relist-increment probability is from the exact frozen state of two distributions. The terminal relist-count cut is not a forward hazard and therefore is not used as the answer. Here, however, a post-CVSG disposition ordinarily requires the United States to file and the petition to be placed before a later conference; mootness, withdrawal, or another exceptional termination accounts for the residual. The CVSG-increment claim is 0 because the June 29 invitation is already on the docket; the harness masks that claim as vacuous at this moment.

Conditional on a grant, I put 0.62 on a cert-order summary route. The current prior-Term paid control is about 35.14% (279 GVR labels among 794 grant-family rows), with a known downward bias because summary reversals can remain labeled `granted`. This case is materially more summary-shaped than that control because the petition's primary request is a GVR based on an intervening state decision. The competing possibility is a plenary grant to address Anderson–Burdick. Conditional on denial, I put 0.22 on any noted dissent or statement: the election-law stakes and the Court's demonstrated interest raise the chance, but most denials, including post-CVSG denials, still issue without separate writing.

I used the provisioned June 30 snapshot, the questions-presented text, the complete petition, and the complete combined opposition filing. A limited CourtListener opinion search returned general Anderson–Burdick authorities from several circuits but did not materially change the filing-based assessment. The main uncertainty is the future recommendation of the Solicitor General, followed by the status and effect of the parallel Pennsylvania litigation.
