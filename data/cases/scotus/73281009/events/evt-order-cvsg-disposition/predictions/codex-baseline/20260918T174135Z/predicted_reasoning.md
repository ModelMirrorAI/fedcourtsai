# Forecast of the Court's action

The Court will most likely grant review after receiving the Solicitor General's views. I assign **58% to any grant**, including summary relief. Among grant routes, a merits proceeding is narrowly favored over disposition in the cert order: **55% versus 45%, conditional on a grant**.

The starting record contains **two conference-distribution entries** and a **June 29, 2026 CVSG**. I expect the petition to return to distribution after the invited brief and responsive submissions, with **one to three additional distributions** before resolution. The probability of at least one additional distribution is **97%**. The earlier response request interrupted the initial briefing trajectory, so the two recorded distributions should not be read as two substantive relists already completed.

No new CVSG is forecast: that step has already occurred, and the required increment claim is **0% and vacuous**. The Court will use the existing invitation rather than promptly dispose of the petition without the requested views. My central timing forecast is a petition disposition between **December 2026 and June 2027**, not an immediate resolution at the June 2026 baseline. This is a forecast, not a report that the SG has or has not filed since that baseline.

If review proceeds to briefing and argument, the Court will likely take the substance of Pennsylvania's single question presented: whether enforcing the handwritten ballot-envelope date requirement against otherwise valid mail ballots violates the First or Fourteenth Amendment under Anderson-Burdick. The grant is more likely to focus on the level and application of scrutiny for an ordinary ballot-casting rule than to announce that all mail-voting regulation escapes constitutional review. Conditional on reaching the merits, the likely direction is greater deference to neutral administrative rules imposing modest compliance burdens, with the availability of alternative voting or cure procedures relevant to the burden assessment.

If the Court instead grants summary relief, a **GVR for reconsideration in light of Coalfield Justice** is more likely than an outright summary reversal. That route would return the state-law effect and its significance for constitutional balancing to the Third Circuit rather than decide the full election-law question in a short order. The objections that Coalfield Justice is limited and was available on rehearing make that route plausible rather than dominant.

If the Court denies review, expect an ordinary denial without a separate writing. I assign **22%**, conditional on denial, to a noted dissent or statement respecting denial, without predicting an individual Justice's vote or authorship. Denial would leave the challenged judgment standing without a Supreme Court merits endorsement.
