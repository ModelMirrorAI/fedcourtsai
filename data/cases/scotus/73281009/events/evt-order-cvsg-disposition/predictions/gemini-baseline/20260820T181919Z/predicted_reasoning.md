Distributed twice so far, and the Court has called for the views of the Solicitor General (CVSG). Expect the petition to be distributed at least once more after the SG files their brief, likely followed by a grant of certiorari or a summary disposition.

If granted for plenary review, the Court would likely take the question presented as written, addressing whether the handwritten date requirement for mail-in ballots violates the First or Fourteenth Amendment under the Anderson-Burdick test. 

However, a summary GVR (grant, vacate, and remand) is a highly plausible alternative route. An intervening decision by the Pennsylvania Supreme Court (Center for Coalfield Justice) has reportedly altered the state-law landscape upon which the Third Circuit relied. The Court may choose to remand the case for the Third Circuit to reconsider its ruling in light of that intervening decision.

Should the Court deny review, expect a dissent from denial (likely from Justice Alito or Justice Thomas) raising federalism concerns and arguing that the Third Circuit misapplied the Anderson-Burdick balancing test to strike down a de minimis state election regulation.
