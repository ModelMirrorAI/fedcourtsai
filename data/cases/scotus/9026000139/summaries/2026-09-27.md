## In brief

Monsanto asks the Court to send a California jury verdict over Roundup's cancer warnings back for review under a new ruling on federal pesticide law.

## What happened

Mike Dennis sued Monsanto in California state court, claiming decades of Roundup use caused his cancer and that Monsanto failed to warn consumers of the risk. A jury found for Dennis on his failure-to-warn claims and awarded damages, later reduced by the trial court. The California Court of Appeal affirmed, rejecting Monsanto's argument that federal pesticide law barred the claims, and the California Supreme Court denied further review. Monsanto then asked the U.S. Supreme Court to take the case, pointing to its own recent decision in another case, Monsanto Co. v. Durnell, which addressed the same legal question.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked whether, given its ruling in Durnell about when federal pesticide labeling law overrides state failure-to-warn lawsuits, it should set aside the California court's decision and send the case back for that court to reconsider Dennis's claims in light of Durnell.

## Where it stands

The petition was distributed for conference twice, and the Court asked Dennis to respond after the first distribution. Dennis has now filed a response saying he does not oppose Monsanto's request. The case is awaiting the Court's decision on the petition.

## What each outcome would mean

Monsanto is asking the Court to act. If the Court grants the petition, it would set aside the California Court of Appeal's ruling and send the case back for that court to apply the Durnell decision to Dennis's claims, without the Supreme Court deciding who ultimately wins. If the Court denies the petition, the California Court of Appeal's judgment for Dennis, including the damages award, would stand. Dennis has told the Court he does not oppose the vacate-and-remand outcome Monsanto seeks.
