## In brief

The Supreme Court vacated a California ruling against Monsanto in a Roundup cancer-warning lawsuit and sent the case back for reconsideration under its recent Durnell decision.

## What happened

Mike Dennis sued Monsanto, claiming it failed to warn that its Roundup weed killer could cause cancer. A California jury agreed, awarding him damages later reduced to $28 million, and the California Court of Appeal affirmed, ruling that federal pesticide law did not preempt his claim. The California Supreme Court denied further review. While Monsanto's appeal to the U.S. Supreme Court was pending, the Court decided a separate case, Monsanto Co. v. Durnell, holding that federal law does preempt this type of failure-to-warn claim when the Environmental Protection Agency has not required a cancer warning. Monsanto then asked the Court to apply that ruling here.

## What the Court is being asked

The question is whether the lower court's decision, which let Dennis's warning-label claim go forward, should be set aside and reconsidered now that the Court has ruled on the same legal issue in another Monsanto case. Dennis's side told the Court it did not oppose this outcome, agreeing that the California court should take another look at the case in light of the new ruling.

## Where it stands

The Supreme Court granted Monsanto's petition, vacated the California Court of Appeal's judgment, and sent the case back for further proceedings consistent with its Durnell decision.

## What each outcome would mean

Monsanto, which lost in the California courts, asked the Supreme Court to act. By vacating the judgment and remanding, the Court set aside Dennis's win below without deciding the case itself. The California Court of Appeal must now reconsider Dennis's failure-to-warn claim applying the legal rule from Durnell, which could lead it to rule for either side depending on how that precedent applies to this record.
