## In brief

The Court vacated a California judgment against Monsanto over Roundup cancer warnings and sent the case back for reconsideration under its recent preemption ruling.

## What happened

Mike Dennis sued Monsanto in California state court, claiming the company failed to warn that its Roundup weedkiller could cause cancer. A jury found for Dennis on his failure-to-warn claims, awarding damages later reduced to a total of $28 million. The California Court of Appeal affirmed, holding that a federal pesticide law did not preempt, or override, his state-law claims. The California Supreme Court denied further review. Monsanto then asked the U.S. Supreme Court to take the case, pointing to the Court's intervening decision in a different case, Monsanto Co. v. Durnell, which held that the federal law does preempt this type of failure-to-warn claim.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court was asked whether, given its ruling in the Durnell case that federal pesticide law overrides these state-law warning claims, it should set aside the California court's judgment and send the case back for a fresh look in light of that ruling.

## Where it stands

The Court granted the petition, vacated the California Court of Appeal's judgment, and remanded the case for further proceedings consistent with its decision in Durnell.

## What each outcome would mean

Monsanto, which lost in the California courts, asked the Court to act. Dennis's brief said he did not oppose this outcome. The Court's action sets aside the California Court of Appeal's judgment in Dennis's favor and sends the case back to that court. This does not decide who ultimately wins; instead, the California Court of Appeal must now reconsider the preemption question applying the reasoning from the Durnell decision, which could lead it to rule either for Monsanto or again for Dennis.
