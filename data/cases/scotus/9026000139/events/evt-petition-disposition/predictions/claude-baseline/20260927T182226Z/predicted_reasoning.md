# Forecast: Monsanto Co. v. Dennis, No. 26-139 (evt-petition-disposition)

**Disposition.** The Court will grant the petition, vacate the judgment of the California Court of Appeal, and remand for further proceedings not inconsistent with *Monsanto Co. v. Durnell* (U.S. June 25, 2026). This is a GVR, not plenary review: the petition asks for nothing else, and the Court will not set a case down for argument on a question it decided three months ago. Expect the order on the order list following the Conference of October 9, 2026 — Tuesday, October 13 (the Monday is a federal holiday), possibly with a one-line dissent from the GVR by a Justice who dissented in *Durnell*, but no relist of substance and no separate opinion of length.

**Relists.** The docket shows two distributions: August 5 (for the September 28 Conference) and September 16 (for the October 9 Conference, after the Court called for a response and received it). I do not expect a third. The intervening distribution was a redistribution forced by the response request, not a relist reflecting deliberation, and the three post-*Durnell* Monsanto petitions already disposed of (*Salas*, *Johnson*, *Anderson*, all GVR'd June 30, 2026) went out at their first post-decision conference. The residual chance of a further distribution comes from two shapes: a Justice wanting time to note a dissent from the GVR, or the Court holding this petition to dispose of it in a batch with other post-*Durnell* Roundup petitions arriving this fall. I put that at roughly one in five.

**CVSG.** None. The Solicitor General has already had his say on the FIFRA question in *Durnell* itself, and a GVR petition on a decided question presents nothing to invite views on.

**Question presented.** If the Court "takes" anything it takes the petition's single question exactly as written — whether to vacate and remand in light of *Durnell* — which is not a question for argument but a request for a GVR. Nothing will be reformulated or narrowed.

**Summary disposition versus plenary review.** Summary disposition is overwhelmingly the likelier route. The only non-summary shapes are (a) a denial, if the Court concluded that the California judgment rests on a ground *Durnell* does not reach, and (b) a dismissal under Rule 46 if the parties settle before the Conference acts. Neither is likely: respondent's court-requested response affirmatively states that he does not oppose the GVR, and the Court of Appeal's opinion rests entirely on the *Bates* parallel-requirements reading that *Durnell* rejected.

**Dissent from denial.** A denial is improbable. Conditional on one, I would expect a short statement or dissent from a Justice in the *Durnell* majority explaining why the California judgment escapes *Durnell*, or objecting that it does not — call it a bit under one in three. If the petition is granted, the writing to watch for is the opposite one: a noted dissent from the GVR by a *Durnell* dissenter, which resolves no claim in this cell.

**Conditional merits.** On remand the Court of Appeal will have to decide whether any part of the failure-to-warn verdict can survive on a non-label theory. The jury rejected the design-defect claims and found only failure to warn, and the petition characterizes the theory as label-based, so the likely end state is vacatur of the $28 million judgment in full; that is for the state court, not this Court.
