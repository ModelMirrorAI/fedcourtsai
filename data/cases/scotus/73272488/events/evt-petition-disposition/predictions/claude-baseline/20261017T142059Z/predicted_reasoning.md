# Forecast — Abrams v. United States, No. 25-1236 (petition disposition)

**State the docket shows (snapshot 2026-10-10):** distributed once (for the
June 18, 2026 conference, after the United States waived response); the Court
then **requested a response** (June 9, 2026); after three extensions the
United States' brief in opposition was submitted October 9, 2026. Two amicus
briefs (NACDL; former federal judges). No CVSG — the United States is the
respondent, so a CVSG has no role here.

- **Further distribution: yes (near-certain).** With the BIO in, the petition
  will be redistributed for a conference in late October or November 2026 —
  its second distribution. I expect it to be decided at that first post-BIO
  conference or after one or two relists; a grant, if it comes, is most likely
  after at least one relist.
- **CVSG: no.** The Solicitor General is already the opposing party.
- **Disposition: more likely denied than granted (P(grant) ≈ 0.30).** The
  question — whether a general Rule 29 motion preserves de novo sufficiency
  review — is an openly acknowledged circuit conflict (the Third Circuit
  expressly declined to follow its sister circuits; petition counts 7 vs. 4),
  and the government itself invited the Third Circuit to resolve it. Against
  that, the expected BIO lines are that the standard of review would not change
  the outcome here (the panel found the fraud evidence ample), that the
  "minority" circuits are less settled than claimed (the Fifth Circuit is
  internally inconsistent), and that a rules question is better left to the
  Advisory Committee on Criminal Rules.
- **If granted:** the Court takes the QP as written (general motion → de novo
  review), with the companion petition Tovar v. United States (No. 25-6344)
  held for it. Plenary review, not a summary disposition; no intervening
  decision supports a GVR.
- **If denied:** most likely without a noted dissent or statement; a statement
  respecting denial (inviting the Rules Committee to act) is the most plausible
  separate writing, but unlikely.
