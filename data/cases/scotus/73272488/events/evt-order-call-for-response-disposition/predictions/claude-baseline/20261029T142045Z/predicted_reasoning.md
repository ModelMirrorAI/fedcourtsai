# Predicted course of the Court — Abrams v. United States, No. 25-1236

**State forecast from.** My snapshot stops on 9 June 2026. It shows one distribution, for the conference of 18 June 2026. The government waived a response, and the Court called for one on 9 June. Two amicus briefs are on the docket (NACDL and Former Judges), and no CVSG. Since the snapshot, the live docket shows the response deadline extended three times, the government's brief in opposition filed on 9 October 2026, the petitioner's reply filed on 23 October, and a distribution for the conference of 13 November 2026.

**Disposition.** I expect the Court to **deny** the petition, most likely after the 13 November conference or after one relist. I put the chance of a grant at about 12%.

**Further distribution.** The petition is already set for another conference, so a further distribution is all but certain. I expect zero or one relist after that before the Court disposes of the petition. Relisting twice or more would signal a likely grant or a written dissent.

**CVSG.** None will issue. The United States is the respondent and has already filed a brief, so there is no reason to call for its views.

**Question taken, if granted.** If the Court grants, it would take the QP as written: whether a general Rule 29 motion preserves de novo review of sufficiency. It might reformulate the question to deal with the government's point that the motion was never renewed at the close of all the evidence.

**Summary disposition.** This is unlikely. No intervening decision would support a GVR, and the court below found no error under plain error at all, so a summary reversal has nothing to work with. A grant would go to plenary review.

**Separate writing on a denial.** No writing is the most likely result. If there is one, the likeliest is a short statement respecting denial noting the acknowledged split while agreeing the vehicle is poor, most plausibly from Gorsuch, Jackson or Sotomayor.
