The Supreme Court is unlikely to hear this appeal (about a one in four chance, or 25%). James Abrams was convicted of federal fraud and identity-theft charges and argues that his general request for an acquittal at the end of his trial was enough to get a fresh, full review of the evidence on appeal, rather than the much stricter "plain error" standard the appeals court announced. Although federal appeals courts are genuinely divided on how specific a trial objection must be under Federal Rule of Criminal Procedure 29, the appeals court in this case also explained why the evidence of Abrams's guilt was overwhelming under either standard, making this a poor case for the Supreme Court to take up.
