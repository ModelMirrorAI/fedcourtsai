## Why I move substantially above the anchor

The petition presents a concrete, recurring preservation question: does a general Rule 29 acquittal motion preserve ordinary appellate sufficiency review? Its argument identifies multiple circuits accepting general motions and several requiring specificity, while acknowledging conflicting Fifth Circuit decisions. More importantly than counsel's count, the provisioned Third Circuit opinion expressly decides the preservation question and rejects the contrary approach. This is not merely an asserted split over applying evidence to one defendant. The motion's general character is undisputed, and the legal question is the petition's sole question presented. Those features, two institutional amicus filings, and an affirmative response request justify moving well above an unselected baseline petition. The request is attention, not evidence that four Justices favor review.

## Why denial remains more likely

The principal discount is materiality. In the appendix, the panel describes the trial record as comfortably supporting the verdicts, notes that even ordinary sufficiency review defers to the jury, finds no first-prong error on the fraud counts, and finds sufficient evidence on the identity-theft counts. See petition appendix pp. 12a, 23a, 29a, and 38a. That permits a government argument that correcting preservation law would not change the judgment. This is my inference, **not** an express alternative holding that every conviction would be affirmed under de novo review. Indeed, part of the identity-theft analysis separately relies on the absence of clear error, so the standard is not demonstrably irrelevant. See pp. 33a–34a. A remand to apply the correct standard remains possible.

The petition's characterization of this as an ideal vehicle is therefore not dispositive. Its discussion of Tovar, No. 25-6344, and of an April 7 government brief is advocacy I read in the provisioned filing, not independent verification that the government supports review here. I have not determined Tovar's later status and do not rely on it. Without this case's opposition, I cannot test the government's strongest vehicle argument. These uncertainties keep the call at 30%, rather than treating a broad split as sufficient for a likely grant. The adjustment is judgment, not a fitted conditional estimator.
