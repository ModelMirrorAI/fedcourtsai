# Forecast of the Court's disposition

The Court will most likely deny the petition. I assign a 30% probability to any grant and 70% to the combined non-grant outcomes, dominated by denial. The mature disagreement about what a general Rule 29 motion preserves will draw scrutiny, but the sufficiency analysis below will make this case less attractive than its abstract legal question.

The record shows **one distribution**, for June 18, 2026, followed by a response request on June 9. It shows no completed conference, no response yet, two amicus-brief entries, and no CVSG. I expect the United States to respond and the petition to be distributed again after the response process. P(at least one additional distribution beyond the recorded one) is **0.98**; that routine redistribution need not be a true relist after conference. The modal path is one additional distribution and denial, rather than a long sequence of repeated conferences. If the petition receives serious grant consideration, one or two additional conference distributions beyond that routine redistribution are plausible.

I do not expect a CVSG: **0.002** for a new invitation. The Solicitor General can address the federal interest in the United States' own response; a response request to a party is not a request for the government's views as a nonparty.

If granted, the Court will most likely take the single question as presented: whether a general acquittal motion under Rule 29 preserves ordinary appellate review of evidentiary sufficiency without separately specifying each defect. It will address preservation, not undertake a free-standing reconsideration of the fraud evidence. Conditional on grant, I expect full briefing and argument (**0.92**) rather than disposition in the cert order (**0.08**). No intervening decision in the supplied record requires a GVR, meaning vacating the judgment and returning the case for reconsideration. If the Court reaches the merits, the omission of a particularity requirement from Rule 29, in contrast to the civil rule, is the petitioner's strongest route; that is conditional merits context, not a prediction that a denial explains its reasons.

Conditional on denial, I expect no separate statement or noted dissent (**0.90**); P(some dissent or statement respecting denial) is **0.10**. If a Justice writes, Gorsuch is my leading individual possibility, but no separate writer is the clear modal forecast. The structured order-writer probabilities are independent, unconditional probabilities, not a distribution summing to one and not predictions of how the Justices vote.

From the June 10 information set, my timing forecast is completion after the July 9 response deadline, likely at the first fall conferences following summer briefing rather than immediately at the originally scheduled June conference. This is a horizon forecast from the provisioned moment, not an assertion about what happened later or the present docket status.
