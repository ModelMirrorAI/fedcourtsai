## In brief

A man convicted of investment fraud asks the Court to decide what a routine trial motion must say to preserve a full appeal of the evidence against him.

## What happened

James Abrams was convicted on 48 counts, including wire fraud and aggravated identity theft, after a jury trial in Pennsylvania federal court. At trial his lawyer moved for acquittal under a rule allowing such motions when evidence is insufficient, but said only that she was making the motion and would not elaborate. The Third Circuit Court of Appeals held that this general motion did not preserve detailed sufficiency arguments for full appellate review, so it reviewed his claims under a stricter "plain error" standard and affirmed his convictions on that basis.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked whether a general trial motion challenging the sufficiency of the evidence, without identifying specific flaws, is enough to get a full, fresh review of that evidence on appeal, or whether a defendant must spell out each specific weakness to get anything beyond the more limited error review used for problems not raised below.

## Where it stands

The United States opposed review, and Abrams filed a reply. The case was distributed for the Court's conference on November 13, 2026, after an earlier conference in June 2026; the docket shows no decision yet on whether the Court will hear it.

## What each outcome would mean

Abrams, who lost in the lower court, is asking the Court to take up his case. If the Court grants review, it would hear full arguments on the question and decide it, without yet deciding who wins. If the Court denies review, the Third Circuit's ruling stands, leaving Abrams's convictions in place and keeping intact the stricter review standard applied to his case and others in that circuit.
