## In brief

A man convicted of investment fraud asks the Supreme Court to decide how specific his trial objection had to be to preserve his appeal.

## What happened

James Abrams was convicted after a jury trial of wire fraud, mail fraud, aggravated identity theft, money laundering, and related charges involving a renewable-energy startup. At trial, his lawyer asked for a judgment of acquittal but did not specify any grounds, saying only that he was moving under the relevant rule and waiving argument. The trial court denied the motion, and the jury convicted him. On appeal, the Third Circuit Court of Appeals held that because his motion was general rather than specific, his sufficiency-of-evidence arguments could only be reviewed under a more forgiving "plain error" standard, not fresh ("de novo") review, and it affirmed his convictions.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked whether a general request for acquittal, made without spelling out the specific weaknesses in the government's evidence, is enough to let an appeals court fully re-examine the evidence later, or whether a defendant must spell out each specific flaw at trial to get that fuller review on appeal.

## Where it stands

The petition was distributed for conference, after which the Court asked the government to respond. The government has filed a brief opposing review, Abrams has filed a reply, and the docket shows no further action since.

## What each outcome would mean

Abrams is asking the Court to take up his case and reverse the Third Circuit's decision. If the Court grants review, it would hear full arguments and decide the legal question itself, without yet deciding who wins. If it denies review, the Third Circuit's decision stands, meaning Abrams's convictions and sentence remain in place, and the stricter preservation rule continues to apply in that circuit.
