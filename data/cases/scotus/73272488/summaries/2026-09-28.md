A man convicted of investment fraud asks the Court to decide how specific a trial motion challenging the evidence must be to get full appellate review.

## What happened

James Abrams was convicted on 48 counts, including wire fraud, mail fraud, and aggravated identity theft, after prosecutors said he used forged documents and false statements to obtain investor funds for a clean-energy startup. At trial he moved for a judgment of acquittal but did not specify any particular flaw in the government's evidence. The Third Circuit held that this general motion did not preserve his sufficiency arguments for full review, applied a more limited "plain error" review instead, and affirmed his convictions, while ruling in his favor on a separate restitution issue.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked to decide what a defendant must say in a trial-court motion challenging the sufficiency of the evidence in order to get thorough appellate review later, rather than the more limited review that applies when an argument was not properly raised below. Abrams's petition says courts of appeals are divided: some hold that a general motion preserves full review of all evidence arguments, while others, including the court that ruled against him, require the defendant to spell out each specific weakness in the evidence.
