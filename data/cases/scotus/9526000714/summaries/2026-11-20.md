## In brief

The federal government asked the Supreme Court to pause a lower court order reinstating Venezuela's temporary protected status, then withdrew the request the next day.

## What happened

This case concerns the Secretary of Homeland Security's decision to end Venezuela's Temporary Protected Status (TPS) designation, which had let eligible Venezuelans stay and work in the United States. A district court set aside that termination, and the Ninth Circuit affirmed. After the Supreme Court later ruled in a different case that courts generally cannot review TPS termination decisions, it sent this case back to the Ninth Circuit for another look. While that court considered the issue, its temporary administrative stay was set to expire, which would let the district court's order reinstating TPS take effect. The government asked the Supreme Court to step in before that happened.

## What the Court is being asked

The government asked the Court to pause the district court's order while the case continues in the lower courts, arguing that a recent Supreme Court ruling shows courts lack authority to second-guess the Secretary's TPS termination decisions, and that reinstating TPS status immediately would cause serious, hard-to-reverse harm.

## Where it stands

The government filed this emergency application on November 18, 2026. The next day, it withdrew the application, and the docket records it as withdrawn.

## What each outcome would mean

The government, as applicant, had asked the Court to pause the lower court's order reinstating Venezuela's TPS designation while the case proceeded. Because the application was withdrawn rather than decided, the Court took no action on it: it neither paused the lower court's order nor left it in effect through a ruling. The underlying dispute between the government and the National TPS Alliance and other respondents continues in the lower courts without Supreme Court intervention at this stage.
