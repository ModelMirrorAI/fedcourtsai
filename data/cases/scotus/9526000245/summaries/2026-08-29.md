## In brief

Applicants facing Wisconsin criminal proceedings asked a single Justice to block their arrest pending appeal; the Justice denied the request.

## What happened

Katherine Lou Hobbins Forester and James Hobbins Forester, representing themselves, face Wisconsin criminal prosecutions and related bond conditions. They filed a federal habeas petition challenging their custody, which the district court dismissed, also declining to issue a certificate of appealability (permission needed to pursue certain habeas appeals). The Seventh Circuit denied a certificate of appealability and related emergency motions, finding the applicants had not exhausted state-court remedies or shown that doing so was impossible. The applicants then sought rehearing in the Seventh Circuit and, with hearings scheduled in Wisconsin courts, asked the Supreme Court's Circuit Justice for emergency relief to stop enforcement of appearance requirements while the appeal proceeded.

## What the Court is being asked

The application asks whether an order temporarily blocking arrest, detention, or other enforcement is needed to preserve the applicants' ability to obtain federal court review, while they argue that Wisconsin's court processes have been ineffective at addressing their claims in time, and that the lower federal courts were wrong to require a showing that using state remedies was impossible rather than just ineffective.

## Where it stands

The application was submitted to Justice Barrett on August 17, 2026, and she denied it on August 27, 2026. The underlying appeal and rehearing request continue in the Seventh Circuit.

## What each outcome would mean

The applicants, Katherine and James Hobbins Forester, asked for emergency protection from arrest and other enforcement while their appeal continued. Denying the application, which is what happened, leaves the Seventh Circuit's orders in place and does not stop the Wisconsin proceedings or appearance requirements the applicants sought to block. It does not decide the underlying legal questions about exhaustion of state remedies or the habeas claims, which remain before the Seventh Circuit. Had the application been granted instead, it would have temporarily paused enforcement while that litigation continued.
