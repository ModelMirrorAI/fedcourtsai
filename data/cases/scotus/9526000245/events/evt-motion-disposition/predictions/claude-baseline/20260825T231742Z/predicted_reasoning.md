# Forecast — 26A245, Hobbins Forester v. Gerol (application for injunction pending appeal)

**Disposition: denied, by Justice Barrett acting alone.** The application
(submitted to Justice Barrett on August 17, 2026, docketed August 25) asks for
an injunction pending appeal from the Seventh Circuit (No. 26-2577), after the
Eastern District of Wisconsin denied the applicants' pro se emergency habeas
petition challenging state pretrial bond restraints, dismissed the action, and
declined a certificate of appealability (July 28, 2026). Nothing in that
posture supports the "significantly higher justification" an injunction
pending appeal demands, and I expect a one-line denial.

Procedural claims that resolve against this docket:

- **No response will be called for.** Justice Barrett will act on the papers;
  a call for a response is an affirmative act of attention this application
  will not draw. P ≈ 0.04.
- **No referral to the full Court.** The commonest route for a plainly
  deficient pro se application is single-Justice denial without referral;
  referral happens mostly on applications with a live prospect of relief or a
  renewed filing. P ≈ 0.10.
- **No amicus briefs.** The docket shows zero and I expect it to end at zero.
  P(any rises) ≈ 0.02.
- **Timing.** Directly comparable pro se substantive applications this Term
  were denied within about four to ten days of docketing (e.g. 26A237, denied
  four days after filing, no response, no referral). Expect a denial entry by
  roughly the first week of September 2026. (Timing is unscored; it is stated
  to discipline the rest.)

Conditional merits-shaped content: were relief somehow entertained, it would
come only after a requested response and a referral — which is why the
ladder claims above and the disposition claim move together. There is no
plausible route to an unqualified grant that does not first pass those rungs,
and I put almost no weight on the first rung firing.
