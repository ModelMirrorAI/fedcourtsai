# Rationale

I assign a 0.5% probability to an unqualified grant and predict denial. The frozen arrival record shows an injunction-pending-appeal application submitted to Justice Barrett, with no response requested, no referral to the full Court, and no amicus briefs. The application itself was not provisioned, so the precise requested injunction and the applicants' best presentation of irreparable harm are important uncertainties.

The committed interim statpack supplies a valid baseline for this OT2026 cell. Pooling strictly prior application Terms gives 30 grants among 226 resolved substantive applications (14/48 in OT2024 and 16/178 in OT2025), or 13.3%, above the 50-case floor. I adjust sharply downward because that heterogeneous pool is not conditioned on prediction-time escalation signals and the predicted cohort is selected higher on the escalation ladder than the raw cohort. This application is instead at the bottom of that ladder and has unusually severe merits and vehicle defects.

The official CourtListener record for the underlying Eastern District of Wisconsin case, *Hobbins Forester v. Gerol*, No. 2:26-cv-01287, shows a joint, self-represented pretrial habeas petition arising from pending Wisconsin criminal charges. The district court's July 28 order dismissed it on Rule 4 review. It identified improper form, the need for separate petitions arising from separate state proceedings, and the individual nature of habeas relief. More decisively, it found that neither applicant had attempted to exhaust state remedies, that amendment would therefore be futile, and that their allegations did not satisfy the heavy futility exception. It dismissed the emergency motions as moot and declined a certificate of appealability. Those threshold defects leave very little prospect of appellate reversal or of extraordinary Supreme Court relief, while ordinary state review remains available.

The district docket describes the emergency request as including protective and preservation relief and relief from allegedly unconstitutional bond restraints. That establishes a serious claimed personal interest, but not the exceptional federal basis needed to interrupt ongoing state proceedings. A related, voluntarily dismissed civil action contains sprawling RICO and governmental-conspiracy allegations; I use it only as a party-and-presentation signal, not as the content of this application. The absence of experienced Supreme Court counsel, a focused nationally important issue, a response request, or amici further reduces both grant and attention probabilities.

I assign 1.5% to a later response request, 6% to later referral, and 0.3% to an increase from zero amici. Referral is higher because a Circuit Justice can refer an application even without finding it persuasive, but the visible exhaustion defect makes an individual chambers denial much more likely. I expect disposition within several days, with a smaller tail of roughly one to two weeks if Justice Barrett refers it.

CourtListener returned the district order and docket, but it had no indexed Seventh Circuit docket or opinion for No. 26-2577. An August 5 district-docket entry labels a Seventh Circuit order, but its text was unavailable, so I did not infer its contents. One later opinion search was throttled. The forecast therefore rests on the provisioned Supreme Court snapshot, the available district record, related pre-application litigation used cautiously, and the committed statpack; it does not use any disposition of this application.
