### Upward Factors (+15 percentage points combined)
1. **Prior Supreme Court stay on the identical policy (*United States v. Shilling*, 145 S. Ct. 2695 (May 6, 2025)):** Eighteen months ago, over three noted dissents (Justices Sotomayor, Kagan, and Jackson), the Court stayed a universal preliminary injunction against this exact Hegseth Policy (No. 24A1030). Under *Hollingsworth v. Perry*, 558 U.S. 183, 190 (2010), that stay necessarily reflected at least five Justices' judgment that the constitutionality of the Hegseth Policy was both certworthy if invalidated below and likely to succeed on the merits. Certiorari requires only **four** votes.
2. **Post-reply issuance of the D.C. Circuit's mandate (Nov. 17, 2026) and active D.D.C. discovery intrusion (Nov. 16, 2026):** Primary-source docket entries retrieved from CourtListener reveal two material developments on November 16–17, 2026:
   - In the cert reply (filed Nov. 16, p. 6 n.*), the Solicitor General noted the D.C. Circuit had not yet issued its mandate while the government's motion to stay the mandate pending certiorari remained pending. The **next day (Nov. 17, 2026)**, the D.C. Circuit denied the stay motion 2–1 (Wilkins and Rogers, over Walker's dissent) and issued the mandate forthwith (`Dkt. 185` in D.D.C. on Nov. 19). Thus, unlike during the appeal, the preliminary injunction is **actively in force** against the Department of Defense/War when the Justices meet on December 4.
   - On **November 16, 2026** in *Talbott v. Trump*, No. 1:25-cv-00240-ACR (D.D.C.), Judge Reyes denied the government's motion for a protective order, ordered production within 28 days of materials withheld under the deliberative-process privilege and answers to deposition questions previously blocked on privilege grounds, and ordered a two-hour deposition of Army Secretary Daniel P. Driscoll ahead of the January 4, 2027 bench trial. That directly substantiates the Solicitor General's *Rostker v. Goldberg* warning (`cert-reply.txt` at 9) about intrusive proceedings probing military judgment.
3. **Facial Fifth Amendment invalidation in a universal-venue circuit + no cleaner vehicle in *Shilling*:** Unlike *Trump v. Thakur* (No. 26-210, cert. denied Nov. 16, 2026)—where the Ninth Circuit sided with the government on its broad Tucker Act/APA defense and left only a fact-bound First Amendment preliminary injunction over specific university grants—the D.C. Circuit in *Talbott* (`176 F.4th 720`) published a precedential holding that a flagship Executive Order military readiness policy is facially irrational and motivated by unconstitutional animus, over a 45-page dissent by Judge Walker and in tension with Judge Katsas's 25-page stay concurrence, *Rostker*, *Trump v. Hawaii*, and *West Virginia v. B.P.J.*, 146 S. Ct. 2356 (2026). Because any service member nationwide can sue the Secretary in D.D.C., leaving `176 F.4th 720` in place during a 2027 trial and a second D.C. Circuit appeal would effectively bind the D.C. federal courts until OT2028. Moreover, CourtListener entries for the only parallel appeal, *Shilling v. Trump* (9th Cir. No. 25-2039), show that on November 6, 2026 the Ninth Circuit **vacated** its November 10 oral argument to receive supplemental briefing on changes in the *Shilling* plaintiffs' military status—leaving *Talbott* as the sole viable vehicle for OT2026 review.

### Downward Factors and Key Uncertainties (−10 percentage points combined)
1. **Interlocutory posture on the eve of a January 4, 2027 bench trial:** Respondents' strongest argument (`brief-in-opposition.txt` at 11–18) is institutional restraint: the D.C. Circuit already vacated both the universal scope of the injunction and all relief for accession plaintiffs, leaving an interim injunction covering only ~30 currently serving plaintiffs, with a full bench trial scheduled to begin on January 4, 2027 (pretrial statements and motions in limine were filed Nov. 20, 2026). Notably, the Court recently denied an emergency stay in *Trump v. Kingdom*, No. 26A416 (Oct. 21, 2026), and denied interlocutory certiorari in *Trump v. Thakur*, No. 26-210 (Nov. 16, 2026), confirming that institutionalist Justices remain selective when immediate practical harm is narrow.
2. **Record dispute over the "attempted to transition" waiver bar:** Both the panel opinion (`176 F.4th at 736 & n.8`) and Judge Wilkins's November 17, 2026 statement (`pp. 4–5`) lean heavily on the government's district-court refusal at the preliminary-injunction stage to narrow the phrase "attempted to transition" (e.g., past social transition or clothing). Several conservative Justices could prefer letting the government clarify how the policy and its waivers actually operate on a completed summary-judgment/trial record rather than reviewing a facial challenge colored by a rushed preliminary-injunction concession.
3. **Absence of a circuit split:** Because *Shilling* remains undecided in the Ninth Circuit, there is no inter-circuit conflict yet.
