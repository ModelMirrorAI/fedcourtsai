The docket currently shows **one distribution** (`distribution_count: 1`), entered November 17, 2026, setting the Solicitor General's petition for its first consideration at the **December 4, 2026 Conference**, with **no relists** to date.

- **Further relists (`relist-increment: 0.68`):** Expect the Court to **relist the petition at least once**—most likely once to the December 11, 2026 Conference (or twice into early January 2027)—before disposing of it. Consistent with the Court's routine vetting of prospective grants (including the great majority of OT2025–OT2026 Solicitor General grants) and the need for chambers to assess late-moving procedural developments below—specifically the D.C. - **Call for the Views of the Solicitor General (`cvsg-increment: 0.001`):** No CVSG appears on the docket and none will issue. The United States, represented by the Solicitor General, is the petitioner.

## Disposition and Route

- **Certiorari grant (`disposition: 0.78`, `predicted_disposition: granted`):** The Court will **grant the petition for plenary review**, setting the case for merits briefing and oral argument in the Spring 2027 sitting (March or April 2027) for decision before the summer recess. Having already stayed a preliminary injunction against the same Hegseth Policy in *United States v. Shilling*, 145 S. Ct. 2695 (2025), and now facing an active preliminary injunction following issuance of the D.C. Circuit's mandate alongside a published D.C. Circuit precedent (*Talbott v. United States*, 176 F.4th 720) declaring a flagship military personnel policy facially unconstitutional under rational-basis review, at least four Justices will vote to hear the government's appeal now rather than leave *Talbott* standing as binding D.C. Circuit law through a 2027 bench trial and a second multi-year appellate cycle.
- **Question presented taken (context-only):** If granted, the Court will take the petition's single Fifth Amendment equal-protection question presented **as written**, without adding a separate remedial question, because the D.C. Circuit already narrowed the preliminary injunction to currently serving respondents under *Trump v. CASA, Inc.*, 606 U.S. 831 (2025).
- **Plenary review over summary disposition (`summary-disposition-route: 0.08` conditional on grant):** Conditional on a grant, plenary review (~92%) is far likelier than a summary disposition in the cert order (~8%). Although *West Virginia v. B.P.J.*, 146 S. Ct. 2356 (June 30, 2026), issued four weeks after the D.C. Circuit's June 1, 2026 opinion, the Solicitor General requests plenary review rather than a GVR, *B.P.J.* expressly reserved distinct transgender classifications (146 S. Ct. at 2378), and Judge Wilkins's November 17, 2026 statement accompanying the denial of a stay of the mandate already examined *B.P.J.* and concluded it does not alter the panel's facial rational-basis/animus holding. Only a modest chance exists that the Court uses a compromise GVR in light of *B.P.J.* to vacate the published circuit precedent while district-court proceedings conclude.

## Conditional Merits Outlook

If granted for plenary review, the Court will likely **reverse or vacate** the D.C. Circuit's judgment insofar as it affirmed the preliminary injunction for currently serving respondents. The likely ground is that under *Rostker v. Goldberg*, 453 U.S. 57 (1981), *Trump v. Hawaii*, 585 U.S. 667 (2018), *United States v. Skrmetti*, 605 U.S. 495 (2025), and *West Virginia v. B.P.J.*, 146 S. Ct. 2356 (2026), judicial deference to military personnel and medical-readiness judgments forecloses invalidating the Hegseth Policy as irrational or motivated by unconstitutional animus, and that a general military disqualification tied to gender dysphoria, medical transition, and biological sex standards satisfies Fifth Amendment review without requiring individualized retention exemptions.

## Separate Writings on the Order

- **On a grant:** If the Court grants plenary review, expect an unsigned grant order with no separate writings; if it enters a compromise GVR, a brief dissent from Justice Alito or Justice Thomas (favoring plenary review) or Justice Sotomayor or Justice Jackson (defending the remand refusal) is possible but unlikely.
- **On a denial (`dissent-from-denial: 0.38` conditional on denial):** Should fewer than four Justices vote to grant review at this interlocutory stage, there is a substantial (~38%) chance that the denial order carries a separate writing—most plausibly either a dissent from the denial of certiorari authored by Justice Alito and/or Justice Thomas arguing that the D.C. Circuit defied *Rostker* and *Shilling*, or a short statement respecting the denial authored by Justice Kavanaugh emphasizing that the denial turns on the imminent January 2027 bench trial and leaves the government free to return after final judgment.
