# Forecast: United States v. Talbott, No. 26-272

**State forecast from.** The docket shows one distribution (for the conference of December 4, 2026), full cert-stage briefing (petition, brief in opposition, reply), two amicus briefs supporting the petition, and no CVSG.

**Disposition.** The Court will grant the Solicitor General's petition (about 86%). The most likely route is a plenary grant on the question as the government wrote it, with argument in the spring of 2027.

**Relists.** The petition will be distributed at least once more (about 82%). The Court's modern practice is to relist once before a plenary grant, so I expect a relist from December 4 to December 11 and a grant on or shortly after that conference, or at the January conferences. A longer run of relists (three or more) would point to a summary reversal or to a denial with a writing being prepared.

**CVSG.** None. The United States is the petitioner, so there is no one to invite (1%).

**Question presented.** If granted, the Court takes the government's question as written: whether the Hegseth policy violates the equal-protection component of the Fifth Amendment. The Court may in effect confine it to retention, since the D.C. Circuit vacated the injunction as to accession and non-parties, but I do not expect a formal reformulation. A direction to brief mootness is possible if the district court's January 2027 trial goes forward.

**Summary route.** Conditional on a grant, a cert-order disposition (a summary reversal or vacatur resting on the Shilling stay, Trump v. Hawaii and the recent Skrmetti / B.P.J. line) has about a 15% chance. Plenary review is the likelier route: the question is a significant constitutional one, three judges wrote separately below, and the government asks for plenary review, not summary reversal.

**If granted, the merits.** The likely ground is that the policy is subject to rational-basis review under the deference owed to military judgments and is not inexplicable by anything but animus, reversing the judgment below.

**If denied.** A denial would most likely rest on the vehicle objections (interlocutory posture, a 28-person injunction, a trial set for January 4, 2027). In that case I expect a noted dissent or a statement from the Court's more conservative members, most likely Justices Alito or Thomas (about 60% that some writing or noted dissent accompanies a denial).
