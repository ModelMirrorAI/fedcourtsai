The strongest case-specific evidence favoring review is what the Court did in United States v. Shilling. It stayed an injunction against the same policy in full, including as to the eight individuals who sought to preserve it. The justices have already confronted the practical consequences of letting the disqualification take effect. Here two D.C. Circuit judges concluded that even deferential review would likely expose unconstitutional hostility. That gives the Court a recurring legal question about how to evaluate the military's stated readiness grounds when the policy documents themselves disparage the people affected. The Solicitor General argues that rational-basis review permits the government to rely on plausible military judgments without proving that each individual poses a risk. The servicemembers point to the elimination of the prior exception for troops already serving successfully. This dispute over what evidence the Constitution requires could be decided even on a preliminary record. (Petition pp. 14–21, 24–32. Opposition pp. 16–24. Reply pp. 2–5, 8–11.)

The best reason to deny now is the prospect of a better case for review soon. The June 1 decision left an injunction covering only 28 named serving members. The government prevailed on applicants for enlistment and on nationwide relief. Each judge wrote separately, and the equities of retaining troops with established records mattered to the result. The opposition identifies no contrary appellate ruling on the current policy and reports a January 4 trial. A final judgment could moot the appeal from the interim order before the Supreme Court could decide it. The reply cites instances in which a district judge paused proceedings after a grant, including one before this same judge. That possibility and the national significance of the question keep a grant more likely than denial. The Shilling stay carries only so much weight here. The district court there relied on heightened scrutiny and expressly reserved the question of animus. Under the earlier Mattis policy the Court granted a stay in Karnoski but denied the government's petition for review before appellate judgment. Talbott has already been through appellate review. (Decision below, No. 25-5087, pp. 3–4. Petition pp. 9–10, 16 n.2. Opposition pp. 9–17. Reply pp. 9–12.)
