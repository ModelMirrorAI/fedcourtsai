# Forecast — United States v. Talbott, No. 26-272 (petition disposition, forecast at arrival)

The Court will **grant certiorari** and set the case for briefing and argument,
most likely taking the question presented as the Solicitor General wrote it.

**Disposition and route.** Grant, with plenary review rather than a summary
route. The petition asks the Court to decide whether the Hegseth policy —
disqualifying from military service individuals with gender dysphoria, a
history of it, or related interventions, under Executive Order 14,183 —
violates equal protection. The Court has already granted a full stay of the
parallel injunction in *United States v. Shilling*, 145 S. Ct. 2695 (2025),
"pending further review," which under *Hollingsworth v. Perry* signals that a
majority already judged the question likely cert-worthy; the D.C. Circuit has
now held the policy likely unconstitutional on the merits and affirmed an
injunction as to serving members, so denial would leave in place a circuit
ruling against a policy the Court itself allowed to take effect. A summary
disposition is the less likely route (~12% conditional on a grant): a GVR in
light of *West Virginia v. B.P.J.* or *Skrmetti* is conceivable, but the D.C.
Circuit already had *Skrmetti* before it and the military-deference question
is not resolved by either, and the SG asks for plenary review of a recurring
question the Mattis-policy round left undecided. Expect the grant during
October Term 2026 — plausibly by January 2027, with argument in the spring —
unless the Court briefly holds the petition for developments in *Shilling*
(the Ninth Circuit has ordered reargument after *B.P.J.*), which would delay
but not change the disposition.

**Distribution and relists.** The docket shows **zero distributions** — this
forecast is made at docketing, before any conference exists. The petition will
certainly be distributed once a response arrives (BIO due October 1, 2026;
respondents are well-resourced institutional litigants and will file, likely
after one extension). Expect a first distribution in late 2026 and **one to
two relists** before the grant — the Court routinely relists once to vet a
high-profile grant — rather than the long relist strings that precede summary
dispositions or dissents from denial.

**CVSG.** None. The United States is the petitioner, represented by the
Solicitor General; there is no one to invite. (~1%, effectively the chance the
event is re-pinned to something unusual.)

**Question taken.** QP as written. It is a single, cleanly framed
equal-protection question drafted by the SG; the Court has no need to narrow
or reformulate it. (Registered context-only.)

**If denied.** A denial would be a substantial surprise given the Shilling
stay. Conditional on one, a separate writing is more likely than not not to
appear from the Justices who supported the stay — but a statement or dissent
from denial (Thomas or Alito most plausibly) is a real possibility (~35%),
since denial would abandon a question the Court already intervened on.

**If granted (conditional merits context, not a claim).** The likely judgment
is reversal, on military-deference grounds under rational-basis review,
tracking the Shilling stay lineup (the three-Justice bloc that would have
denied the stay dissenting).
