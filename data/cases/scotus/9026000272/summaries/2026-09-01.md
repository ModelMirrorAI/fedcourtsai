## What happened

Nicolas Talbott and other plaintiffs, some already serving in the military and others hoping to join, sued the government over a Department of War policy that generally disqualifies people who have gender dysphoria, a history of it, or related medical treatment from military service. A federal district court blocked the policy nationwide, finding it likely reflected bias against transgender people. The government appealed. The D.C. Circuit upheld the block for people currently serving but lifted it for those seeking to join, with the judges disagreeing over whether the policy was a reasonable military judgment or an unjustified, biased ban. The government, represented by the Solicitor General, now asks the Supreme Court to take the case.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked to decide whether this policy, which sets who may serve based on gender dysphoria and related medical history, treats people unequally in a way the Constitution's fairness guarantee forbids. The government argues the policy targets a medical condition, not transgender status, and deserves deference as a military personnel judgment. The plaintiffs' side, reflected in the lower courts' rulings, views the policy as effectively excluding transgender people based on bias rather than any legitimate reason.

## Where it stands

The case is a new petition asking the Supreme Court to grant cert, meaning agree to hear the case; the respondents' answer is due October 1, 2026, and the Court has not yet acted on the request.
