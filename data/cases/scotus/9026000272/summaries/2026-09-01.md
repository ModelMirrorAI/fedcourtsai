## In brief

The federal government asks the Court to overturn a lower court ruling that blocked, for current servicemembers, a military policy generally disqualifying people with gender dysphoria from service.

## What happened

The Department of War adopted a policy disqualifying from military service people with gender dysphoria, a history of it, or related medical treatment. Nicolas Talbott and other plaintiffs, some serving and some seeking to join the military, sued, arguing the policy violates constitutional equal-protection guarantees. A district court issued a nationwide preliminary injunction blocking the policy. A federal appeals court panel later ruled that the challengers were likely to succeed on their claim, upholding the injunction for people already serving but narrowing it to let the policy apply to new applicants. The government now asks the Supreme Court to review that decision.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked whether the military's policy screening out people with gender dysphoria or related treatment history amounts to unconstitutional discrimination, or whether it is instead a permissible medical classification that courts should treat with deference given the military context.

## Where it stands

The government filed its petition for certiorari on August 27, 2026. A response from the challengers is due October 1, 2026, and the Court has not yet acted on the petition.

## What each outcome would mean

The government, as petitioner, is asking the Court to take up and reverse the ruling against it. If the Court grants review, it would hear full arguments and decide whether the policy is constitutional, without yet deciding who wins. If the Court denies review, the appeals court's decision stands, meaning the policy remains blocked as applied to current transgender servicemembers while remaining in effect for new applicants, and the case would continue in the lower courts on that basis.
