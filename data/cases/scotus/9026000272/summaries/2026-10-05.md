## In brief

The federal government asks the Court to review a ruling that partly blocked a military policy disqualifying people with gender dysphoria from service.

## What happened

After an executive order directed new military service standards, the Department of War adopted a policy generally disqualifying people with gender dysphoria, a history of it, or related medical treatment. Nicolas Talbott and other current and prospective servicemembers sued, arguing the policy violates equal protection. A district court blocked the policy nationwide. The court of appeals, reviewing that order, agreed the policy likely violates equal protection, but narrowed the block to apply only to servicemembers already in the military, not to new applicants. The government now asks the Supreme Court to take up the case and reverse.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked whether this military policy, which screens out people based on gender dysphoria and related treatment, unlawfully discriminates in violation of constitutional equal-protection guarantees, or whether it instead reflects a permissible, medically-grounded judgment entitled to deference given the military context.

## Where it stands

The government's petition was filed in August 2026, and the deadline for Talbott and the other respondents to reply was extended, at their request, to November 2026. Several amicus briefs have been filed supporting the government. The docket shows no action yet on whether the Court will hear the case.

## What each outcome would mean

The United States, the petitioner, is asking the Court to take up and reverse the lower court's decision. If the Court grants review, it would hear full arguments and decide whether the policy is constitutional, deciding nothing about the outcome at this stage. If it denies review, the court of appeals' decision stands: the policy remains blocked as to current transgender servicemembers with gender dysphoria, while applicants seeking to join the military may still be screened out under the policy, as that part of the lower injunction was already vacated.
