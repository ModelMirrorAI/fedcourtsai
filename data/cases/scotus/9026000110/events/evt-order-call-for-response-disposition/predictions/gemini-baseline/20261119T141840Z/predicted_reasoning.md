# Predicted Court Action — *Tedford's Tenancy, LLC v. City of New York* (No. 26-110)

## Disposition and Procedural Trajectory

The Court will **deny** the petition for a writ of certiorari (`P(any grant) = 0.09`, `P(denied) = 0.91`).

- **Further distributions (`relist-increment = 0.98`):** The docket as of September 10, 2026 shows a single distribution (`distribution_count = 1`), entered on September 2, 2026 for the September 28, 2026 Long Conference, followed by a September 9, 2026 request for a response (due October 9, 2026) after both the State and Municipal respondents waived. Beyond that post-opposition distribution, expect **one to two additional conference relists** as interested chambers examine the case alongside vided-amicus petition *Walls v. - **Solicitor General invitation (`cvsg-increment = 0.01`):** The Court will **not** call for the views of the Solicitor General. The dispute involves Fifth and Fourteenth Amendment regulatory-takings ripeness in a state-court challenge to New York State and New York City rent-stabilization provisions; no federal agency, federal statute, or federal proprietary interest is implicated.

## Scope of Review and Route Conditional on a Grant

- **Questions presented if granted:** If the Court grants plenary review, it will **not** take Question 1 (whether final-decision ripeness is jurisdictional under Article III standing or prudential), because this case arises from New York state courts not governed by Article III and because the Appellate Division considered equitable futility on its merits anyway. Instead, the Court would **limit review to Question 2** (or reformulate it narrowly) to address whether *Pakdel v. City & County of San Francisco*, 594 U.S. 474 (2021), permits dismissing a regulatory-takings claim as unripe for failure to seek an administrative hardship rent increase where state regulations bar an application or cap relief below alleged operating losses. Conditional on plenary review, the likely merits ground would reinforce *Pakdel*'s de facto finality standard—holding that landowners need not pursue administrative variance mechanisms for which they are facially ineligible—while remanding all *Penn Central* merits and state-law eligibility disputes to the New York courts.
- **Summary disposition vs. plenary review (`summary-disposition-route = 0.38`):** Conditional on the Court granting review (`0.09`), plenary argument remains somewhat likelier (`0.62`), carry-over from Question 2's recurring land-use framing, while a **cert-order summary disposition carries a substantial `0.38` conditional share**. That `0.38` conditional probability splits primarily between two routes: (1) holding No. 26-110 behind federal-court companion *Walls v. Prince George's County*, No. 26-107, and entering a **GVR** if *Walls* is granted and decided in the landowner's favor, or (2) a standalone ***Pakdel*-style summary per curiam vacatur** (or narrow GVR) faulting the Appellate Division's two-sentence ripeness dismissal without full merits briefing.
