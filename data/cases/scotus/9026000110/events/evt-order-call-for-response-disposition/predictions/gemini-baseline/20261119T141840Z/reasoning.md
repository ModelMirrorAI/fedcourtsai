## 2. Upward Case-Specific Signals

1. **Demonstrated interest by multiple Justices in Takings finality and municipal rent regulations:** In *Pakdel v. City & County of San Francisco*, 594 U.S. 474 (2021), the Court summarily vacated a Ninth Circuit decision that turned *Williamson County* finality into an administrative-exhaustion trap incompatible with *Knick v. Township of Scott*, 588 U.S. 180 (2019). More recently, Justice Thomas issued a statement respecting denial in *74 Pinehurst LLC v. New York*, Nos. 22-1130 & 22-1170 (Feb. 20, 2024, after 13 distributions), calling the constitutionality of NYC's Rent Stabilization Law (RSL) an "important and pressing question" awaiting "an appropriate future case" with concrete, non-generalized pleadings, and dissented from denial (joined by Justice Gorsuch) in *GHP Management Corp. v. City of Los Angeles*, No. 24-435 (June 30, 2025, after 14 distributions). And in *First Choice Women's Resource Centers, Inc. v. Davenport*, No. 24-781 (Apr. 29, 2026)—decided *after* both New York appellate orders below—the Court unanimously reinforced *Knick*'s rule against forcing constitutional claimants through state proceedings while discussing *Pakdel* finality.
2. **Concrete financial pleading on QP2:** Unlike the generalized facial/as-applied allegations in *74 Pinehurst*, 59 F.4th 557, 565 (2d Cir. 2023), Tedford's Verified Complaint (App. 21a–44a) pleads specific rent rolls ($3,465.45/month across three stabilized residential units), operating expenses and negative cash flow, >$1 million in estimated code-mandated renovations, and specific regulatory bars under 9 NYCRR § 2522.4(b)–(c) that it argues foreclose DHCR comparative or alternative hardship relief or cap increases at 6% (~$2,500/year). Yet the Appellate Division, First Department dismissed the claim as unripe in two sentences solely because Tedford never applied to DHCR for a hardship exemption (App. 2a).

## 3. Downward Adjustments: Severe Vehicle and Posture Defects on the Record

Inspection of `appendix.txt` and `petition.txt` reveals five substantial obstacles to four votes for certiorari (and to five votes for summary vacatur):

1. **QP1 is an illusory vehicle from a state court:** Question 1 asks whether final-decision ripeness in Fifth Amendment regulatory takings cases is a jurisdictional requirement of Article III standing or a non-jurisdictional rule of prudence. First, **New York state courts are not Article III courts** (*ASARCO Inc. v. Kadish*, 490 U.S. 605, 617 (1989)), so the federal boundary between Article III standing (FRCP 12(b)(1)) and federal prudential ripeness (FRCP 12(b)(6)) did not govern the state courts below (which ruled under CPLR 3211). Second, the jurisdictional-vs.-prudential label was completely **outcome-neutral** below: the Appellate Division expressly entertained the equitable futility exception on its merits and held Petitioner failed to establish it (App. 2a). Third, this Court already described *Williamson County* ripeness as prudential in *Suitum v. Tahoe Regional Planning Agency*, 520 U.S. 725, 733–34 (1997), and *Stop the Beach Renourishment, Inc. v. Florida Dept. of Environmental Protection*, 560 U.S. 702, 729 (2010).
2. **Disputed state-law regulatory premises and self-inflicted posture on QP2:** Tedford never submitted *any* application to DHCR—not for comparative hardship, alternative hardship, Major Capital Improvements (MCI), or Individual Apartment Improvements (IAI). Its claim of "statutory ineligibility" for alternative hardship rests heavily on (a) its own pending 2021 property-tax certiorari lawsuit (`Index No. 266045/2021`, App. 29a ¶ 68, 35a ¶ 133) under 9 NYCRR § 2522.4(c)(2)(iv)—a temporary coordination rule preventing double-counting contested property taxes while a tax reduction proceeding is pending—and (b) the fact that Tedford's Tenancy, LLC took title via inter-family transfer in **December 2021** and sued **five months later** in May 2022 (App. 23a ¶ 17, 44a), raising unsettled state-law ownership-tacking questions under N.Y.C. Admin. Code § 26-511(c)(6-a)'s 36-month ownership requirement that no New York agency has ever construed.
3. **Unfavorable *Penn Central* record and abandoned physical-takings claim:** Although the Appellate Division affirmed dismissal of the as-applied regulatory-takings claim on ripeness alone, the N.Y. Supreme Court also rejected the claim on the *Penn Central* merits across five pages (App. 12a–16a), emphasizing that **4 of the 7 residential apartments sit vacant** (generating zero rent because Plaintiff has not renovated and leased them), an unregulated ground-floor commercial unit yields **$103,901/year** ($8,658.42/month), and the LLC acquired title two years *after* the 2019 HSTPA. Moreover, whereas Justice Thomas's *74 Pinehurst* statement specifically invited a future case testing **per se physical takings** (eviction restrictions), Tedford **expressly abandoned any physical-takings claim** at oral argument in the Appellate Division (App. 3a).
4. **State-court procedural posture:** Tedford pleaded both federal Fifth Amendment and New York Constitutional (Art. I, § 7) takings claims in a single cause of action (App. 40a ¶¶ 179–180), and the N.Y. Court of Appeals first dismissed its appeal as of right for lack of a direct substantial constitutional question (App. 19a) before denying leave to appeal (App. 20a). While *Michigan v. Long* likely preserves § 1257 jurisdiction because the Appellate Division relied on *74 Pinehurst*, *Williamson County*, and *Knick*, the intertwined state-pleading and CPLR posture further diminishes vehicle appeal.
5. **Comparison to vided-amicus companion No. 26-107 (*Walls v. Prince George's County*):** `record/related/related.json` stages `26-107.json` under `relation: "vided-amicus"` (`shared_briefs: 3`), showing that No. 26-107 was distributed on September 2, 2026 and drew a CFR that same day. Inspection of the Fourth Circuit's unpublished decision below (*Walls v. Prince George's County*, No. 25-1121, 2026 WL ____, decided Feb. 23, 2026) shows that *Walls* arises from **federal court** (where Rule 12(b)(6) prudential ripeness was expressly applied, slip op. at 2 n.1) and involves a landowner who *did* obtain a written agency waiver denial before declining to petition the County Council for a *legislative* water-and-sewer plan amendment. As a standalone plenary vehicle, No. 26-110 is weaker than No. 26-107; however, No. 26-107's parallel pendency slightly supports No. 26-110's tail grant probability (`~2%` of the `9%` total) via a potential hold-and-GVR if No. 26-107 were granted.
