# Forecast — No. 26-110, Tedford's Tenancy, LLC v. City of New York

The docket shows **one distribution** (conference of 28 Sept 2026), after which the Court called for a response (due 9 Oct 2026).

- **Disposition.** The Court will deny certiorari. Once the response is filed the petition will be redistributed and denied at that conference or, at most, after one relist.
- **Further distributions.** One more distribution is near certain (the post-response conference). I expect no relist beyond that — at most a single one if a Justice is drafting a short statement.
- **CVSG.** None. The dispute is between a private landlord and New York State and City agencies over state rent stabilization; no federal interest invites the Solicitor General's views.
- **Question presented.** If the Court took anything, it would be QP1 (whether final-decision ripeness for regulatory takings claims may operate as a discretionary "prudential" bar once standing is shown), probably leaving QP2's futility question behind as fact-bound under Pakdel. The Court is unlikely to do so here because the Appellate Division also held, in the alternative, that the complaint states no taking on the merits, so the ripeness question is not outcome-determinative, and the decision below never labels its ripeness ruling prudential or jurisdictional.
- **Summary disposition.** Unlikely. No intervening decision supports a GVR, and the state court's reliance on Williamson County finality is not the sort of clear error that draws a summary reversal. A grant, if there were one, would be plenary.
- **Separate writing.** Most likely none. If anyone writes on the denial it would be Justice Thomas or Justice Gorsuch, noting that the takings-ripeness question deserves review in a case without an alternative merits holding.
