I put the chance of any grant at 2.5%. The strongest reason to expect denial is in the First Department's opinion. After finding the claim premature because Tedford's had never sought a hardship rent increase, the court unanimously affirmed dismissal on the merits as well. The building still yielded residential and commercial rent, and the LLC acquired it in 2006 subject to rent stabilization. The court found the allegations insufficient under both the total-loss rule and *Penn Central*. Neither question presented asks for review of that holding. Tedford's suggests it could amend its complaint on remand, but a favorable answer on ripeness alone would leave the second ground for dismissal intact. (Petition appendix 1a–3a, petition 13 n.15, City opposition 29–32, State opposition 11–13.)

The best argument for review is that the hardship process may ask this owner to apply for relief whose limits are already fixed. Tedford's alleges that its pending property-tax challenges bar relief and that even the maximum increase would leave rents insufficient to cover costs. *Pakdel* requires only a definitive government position. The first question also identifies appellate decisions using different terms for the final-decision requirement. But the First Department did not decide whether the requirement was jurisdictional or prudential, and Article III does not govern the jurisdiction of New York's courts. The City and State say the distinction was never presented below. On the second question, they dispute both the owner's ineligibility and whether that contention was pursued on appeal. The possibility that an increase could change the building's economics makes this a difficult record on which to decide when an application would be futile. (Questions presented 1–2, petition 10–13, 21–34, City opposition 13–29, State opposition 10–20.)

That denial reduces the chance of a grant on the broad question here and removes a possible reason to hold Tedford's.
