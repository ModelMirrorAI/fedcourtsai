I expect the Court to deny the petition after one more distribution. The docket shows one distribution, for the September 28 conference. The Court then requested responses, and the City and State have now filed them. The next distribution should put the fully briefed petition before the Justices. I expect a decision at that conference, without an invitation to the U.S. Solicitor General or a separate writing on the denial.

If the Court grants review, I expect it to focus on the second question, narrowed to whether an owner must apply for a hardship rent increase when the complaint plausibly alleges that the agency cannot materially change the rent the owner may charge. Plenary briefing is the likelier route. A summary ruling under *Pakdel* would have to account for the state court's separate decision that the complaint failed to plead a taking. The related *Walls* petition ended in denial and supplies no intervening merits decision for a remand.
