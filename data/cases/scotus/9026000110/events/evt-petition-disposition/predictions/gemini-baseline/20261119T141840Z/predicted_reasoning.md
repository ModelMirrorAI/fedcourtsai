# Forecast of the Court's Action

## Disposition

The Court will **deny** the petition for a writ of certiorari (`Petition DENIED`) on the order list immediately following its first post-opposition conference in December 2026.

## Conference Distribution and Relist Trajectory

As of the November 19, 2026 snapshot, the docket records **one distribution** — the pre-response distribution on September 2, 2026 for the September 28, 2026 conference, entered after respondents waived their right to respond and shortly before the Court requested a response on September 9, 2026. Both the City of New York and the State of New York filed their Briefs in Opposition on November 10, 2026, and no post-opposition distribution entry has yet appeared on the docket.

Barring an unexpected voluntary dismissal under Rule 46 before the Clerk's routine circulation window, the petition will be **distributed once more** in late November 2026 under Rule 15.5 so that the petition, the two Briefs in Opposition, and any forthcoming reply brief reach a December 2026 conference. That routine post-opposition circulation will bring the total distribution count from `1` to `2` (resolving `relist-increment` in the affirmative). Past that first post-opposition conference, however, the Court will **not relist the petition further**: expect zero conference-to-conference relists (no third distribution), matching the trajectory of companion petition No. 26-107 (*Walls v. Prince George's County*), which was denied at its first post-opposition conference on November 9, 2026.

## Call for the Views of the Solicitor General

The Court will **not** call for the views of the Solicitor General. This litigation comes from the New York state courts, challenges municipal and state rent-stabilization provisions under the Fourteenth and Fifth Amendments, and implicates no federal statute, treaty, agency program, or federal enforcement interest. Moreover, the state-court preservation and independent-merits defects exposed in respondents' oppositions cannot be cured by federal executive input.

## Questions Presented and Conditional Merits Scope

In the unlikely event the Court grants review, it would **not** take Question 1 — which asks whether final-decision ripeness for Fifth Amendment regulatory takings claims is jurisdictional under Article III or prudential — because Article III's case-or-controversy constraints do not govern New York state courts of general jurisdiction and the question was neither raised nor passed upon below. At most, the Court would grant limited to Question 2 (or reformulate it narrowly) to address whether *Pakdel v. City & County of San Francisco*, 594 U.S. 474 (2021), permits dismissal on final-decision ripeness grounds where an owner contends that available administrative rent-hardship procedures cannot alter the challenged tenancy restrictions.

Conditional on a grant, if the Court reached the merits of Question 2, it would evaluate whether New York's alternative hardship rent-increase mechanism under N.Y.C. Admin. Code § 26-511(c)(6-a) and 9 N.Y.C.R.R. § 2522.4 provides an ordinary, non-futile avenue capable of altering the regulation's economic impact when the property owner has never submitted any application to the Division of Housing and Community Renewal, while remanding or leaving undisturbed the Appellate Division's separate determination that the complaint failed to plead a regulatory taking under *Lucas* and *Penn Central*.

## Summary Disposition Versus Plenary Review

Conditional on the remote event that the Court grants review at all, a **summary disposition** in the cert order carries nearly half (45%) of the conditional grant weight against 55% for plenary briefing and argument. *Pakdel* itself was resolved by a per curiam vacatur at the certiorari stage rather than full merits briefing, making a narrow summary vacatur on finality more conceivable relative to plenary review than in an ordinary doctrinal dispute, although no pending Supreme Court merits case exists for a hold-and-GVR (companion No. 26-107 having already been denied) and the Appellate Division's independent alternative merits holding weighs heavily against summary intervention as well.

## Separate Writings Respecting Denial

The denial order will almost certainly issue **without any noted dissent or separate statement**. If any Justice writes separately on the denial order, the likeliest author is **Justice Thomas** (followed distantly by Justice Gorsuch or Justice Alito), issuing a short statement respecting the denial of certiorari along the lines of Justice Thomas's February 2024 statement in *74 Pinehurst LLC v. New York*, noting ongoing interest in takings challenges to New York City's rent-stabilization framework while observing that the absence of any administrative application, state-court preservation problems, and the unchallenged alternative merits dismissal make this case an unsuitable vehicle. Even such a statement is unlikely here given that the cleaner federal companion petition in *Walls* (No. 26-107) drew no noted dissent or statement when denied on November 9, 2026.
