## In brief

A landlord asks the Court to revive its claim that New York's rent stabilization law took its property, after state courts dismissed the suit as premature.

## What happened

Tedford's Tenancy, LLC owns a Manhattan building with six rent-stabilized apartments and one commercial unit. After a long-term ground lease expired in 2020, Tedford's sued New York City and State officials, claiming the rent law's limits on rent increases left it unable to cover costs and effected an unconstitutional taking of its property. New York trial and appellate courts dismissed the suit, holding the claim was not yet ripe because Tedford's never applied for a hardship exemption that might allow higher rents, and alternatively that the complaint failed to allege a taking since the property still generates income. New York's highest court declined to hear a further appeal.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked to decide whether a rule requiring property owners to show a government agency has reached a final, definitive decision before a court can hear their takings claim is a strict jurisdictional limit on a court's power to act, or instead a flexible rule courts can choose not to apply. It is also asked whether that final-decision requirement can be enforced against an owner who says seeking the agency's permission would have been pointless.

## Where it stands

The petition has been fully briefed, including opposition briefs from both the City and State respondents and several amicus briefs supporting Tedford's Tenancy, and a reply from Tedford's Tenancy. It was distributed for a September 2026 conference, after which the Court asked the respondents to respond; it is now scheduled for a conference in December 2026, and the docket shows no decision yet on whether the Court will hear the case.

## What each outcome would mean

Tedford's Tenancy is asking the Court to take up its case. If the Court grants review, it would hear the case in full and decide the legal question, without yet deciding who wins. If the Court denies review, the New York courts' dismissal of Tedford's Tenancy's lawsuit stands, leaving the City and State's win in place and the case over. The City and State respondents, who prevailed below, oppose review and argue the case is a poor vehicle for deciding the question because the state court also rejected the claim on a separate ground Tedford's Tenancy does not challenge.
