## In brief

A landlord asks the Court to hear its challenge to a New York court ruling that its rent-stabilization takings claim was not ready for judicial review.

## What happened

Tedford's Tenancy, LLC owns a Manhattan apartment building subject to New York's Rent Stabilization Law. It sued the City and State, claiming the law's rent restrictions deprived it of economic use of the property, violating the Constitution's limits on government takings of property. New York trial and appellate courts dismissed the suit, holding the claim was not yet ripe for review because Tedford's had not applied for a hardship rent increase, and separately that the complaint failed to state a valid claim because the property still generated income. New York's highest court declined further review. Tedford's then asked the Supreme Court to take the case.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked to decide whether the rule requiring a property owner to show the government has reached a final position before a takings claim can be heard is a strict jurisdictional limit or a more flexible judge-made rule, and whether courts may require owners to pursue relief that is unavailable to them or too small to matter before allowing such a claim to proceed.

## Where it stands

The petition was distributed for conference, after which the Court asked the respondents to respond; both the City and State respondents have since filed briefs opposing review. The case has not yet been scheduled for another conference in the record shown.

## What each outcome would mean

Tedford's Tenancy, the petitioner, asks the Court to take up the case and reverse the dismissal of its claim. If the Court grants review, it would hear full arguments on when a takings claim is ready for court and whether Tedford's needed to seek a rent increase first; a decision could revive Tedford's suit or confirm the dismissal. If the Court denies review, the New York courts' dismissal stands, leaving Tedford's without a path to pursue its takings claim as filed, and the City and State prevail.
