## In brief

Louisiana asks the Court to lift an appeals court order that paused state-court proceedings against Devon Energy while Devon's appeal continues.

## What happened

Cameron Parish and Louisiana sued Devon Energy in state court over coastal damage. Devon moved the case to federal court, arguing it had acted under a federal officer's direction. A federal district court disagreed and sent the case back to state court, formally transmitting that order to the state court clerk. Only after that transmittal did Devon file a notice of appeal and ask for a stay. The district court refused, reasoning the state court already had jurisdiction back and a federal stay would improperly block state proceedings. The Fifth Circuit reversed, ruling that Devon's appeal automatically paused both the remand order and the state-court case.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked whether an automatic pause takes effect — stopping a state case from going forward — when a company appeals a federal judge's order sending a case back to state court, even though the federal court had already notified the state court it could proceed before the appeal was filed. Louisiana also asks the Court, as an alternative step, to treat this request itself as an early request for full review and to take up that question now, before the Fifth Circuit finishes the appeal.

## Where it stands

Louisiana's filing asks a single justice to vacate the Fifth Circuit's order; that justice has asked Devon Energy to respond by October 21, 2026. No ruling on the request has yet been recorded.

## What each outcome would mean

Louisiana and Cameron Parish are asking the Court to act. If the Court grants the request, the pause on state-court proceedings would lift, letting Louisiana's case against Devon Energy move forward in state court while the Fifth Circuit's appeal continues. If the Court denies it, the Fifth Circuit's pause stays in place, keeping the state case on hold until the appeal is resolved. Either way, this is a temporary decision; it would not decide the underlying legal question or the lawsuit itself, which would continue in the lower courts.
