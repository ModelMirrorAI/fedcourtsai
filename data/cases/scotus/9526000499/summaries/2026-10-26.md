## In brief

Louisiana asked the Supreme Court to lift a lower court's pause on returning a coastal-damage lawsuit to state court; a single justice denied the request.

## What happened

Cameron Parish and Louisiana sued Devon Energy over claims that decades of oil operations damaged Louisiana's coastline. Devon removed the case to federal court, arguing it had acted under federal-officer direction. The district court disagreed and sent the case back to state court. Devon appealed that remand and asked for a stay; the district court refused, but the Fifth Circuit ruled that Devon's appeal automatically paused the case, keeping it out of state court for now. Louisiana then asked the Supreme Court to undo that pause.

## What the Court is being asked

The question is whether filing an appeal of an order sending a case back to state court automatically freezes that return to state court, even after the transfer paperwork has already gone out, while the appeal is pending. Louisiana argues no automatic freeze should apply once the transfer has happened; Devon argues the appeal necessarily pauses the whole case.

## Where it stands

A single justice asked Devon to respond to Louisiana's application, received Devon's opposition and Louisiana's reply, and then denied the application. The underlying appeal remains pending in the Fifth Circuit, and no petition asking the full Court to hear the case has been acted on in this docket.

## What each outcome would mean

Louisiana, joined by Cameron Parish, asked the Court to lift the pause. Granting that request would have let the case return immediately to state court while Devon's appeal continued. Denying it, which is what happened, leaves the Fifth Circuit's pause in place, so the case stays out of state court until Devon's appeal of the remand order is resolved. The denial does not decide whether the Fifth Circuit's stay was legally correct.
