## In brief

Louisiana asks the Court to lift a Fifth Circuit order that paused a lawsuit's return to state court while Devon Energy appeals.

## What happened

Cameron Parish and Louisiana sued Devon Energy and other companies in state court over coastal damage they attribute to decades of oil and gas operations. Devon removed the case to federal court, arguing it had acted under federal-officer direction. The district court rejected that argument and ordered the case sent back to state court, mailing the remand order the same day. Devon then appealed and asked for a stay. The district court refused, but the Fifth Circuit ruled that Devon's appeal automatically paused the remand and blocked the state case from proceeding, joining two other circuits on this question while a fourth disagrees.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked to decide whether an appeal of an order sending a case back to state court automatically pauses that return and blocks state-court proceedings, even after the remand paperwork has already been sent to the state court. Louisiana separately asks the Court to treat this emergency request as a full petition for review and decide that question now, rather than later through the ordinary process.

## Where it stands

Justice Sotomayor requested a response from Devon, which it filed, and Louisiana has filed its reply. The application is now fully briefed and awaiting the Court's action.

## What each outcome would mean

Louisiana and Cameron Parish, as applicants, are asking the Court to lift the pause so their case can continue in Louisiana state court while Devon's appeal proceeds separately. If the Court grants that request, the state lawsuit would resume immediately. If the Court denies it, the Fifth Circuit's pause stays in place, keeping the case out of state court until Devon's appeal is decided. Either way, this ruling would not decide whether Devon's appeal itself succeeds, and Louisiana's separate request that the Court treat this as a full case for review and decide the underlying legal question now remains a distinct option the Court could also grant or deny.
