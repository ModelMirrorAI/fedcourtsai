# Forecast

The Court will most likely deny Louisiana and Cameron Parish's application to vacate the Fifth Circuit's October 5, 2026 stay. I assign a 28% probability to an unqualified grant of the requested interim relief. A denial will leave the appellate stay in place without resolving the ultimate removal question or endorsing an automatic-stay rule on the merits. The most likely disposing document is a short order rather than a reasoned merits opinion.

The application raises a substantial question about whether an appeal filed after transmission of a remand order automatically interrupts state-court proceedings. Nevertheless, the Court is more likely to leave that contested question for ordinary review than to lift the stay immediately. The state-sovereignty and delay arguments will not, in the most likely outcome, overcome the obstacles to vacating an appellate stay while the underlying jurisdictional appeal remains pending. If relief is granted, its likely basis is the distinction between protecting an ongoing federal proceeding and interrupting state jurisdiction after remand, not a final adjudication of Devon's liability or every asserted basis for removal.
