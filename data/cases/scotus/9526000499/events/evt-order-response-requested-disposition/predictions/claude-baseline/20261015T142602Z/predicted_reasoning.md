# Forecast: Louisiana v. Devon Energy Production Co. (26A499), application to vacate the Fifth Circuit's stay

**Disposition: denied (P(unqualified grant of vacatur) = 0.14).** The record shows a response was
requested by Justice Sotomayor on Oct 14, due Oct 21. Justice Alito is recused, so she is acting for
the Fifth Circuit. I expect the Court to deny the vacatur request, most likely in a short unexplained
order. It probably comes within about one to three weeks after the response arrives: late October to
mid-November 2026.

- **The ladder from here.** A response has already been called for, so that rung has fired (the
  harness will mask that claim). I expect the application to be **referred to the full Court**
  (about 55%). Three things point that way: the Circuit Justice slot is unusual because of the
  recusal, there is an acknowledged split, and the applicants also ask for certiorari before
  judgment, which a single Justice cannot grant. A single-Justice denial in chambers is the main
  alternative. I think it is somewhat **unlikely that an amicus entry appears** before disposition
  (about 30%). The window is short. Still, a coastal-parish or state amicus group, or an industry
  group backing the respondent, could file.
- **Why denial.** Vacating a court of appeals' stay is an extraordinary act. Here the question is
  pending in its cleaner form in *City of Brunswick v. Honeywell* (No. 26-289), which is distributed
  for the Oct 16 conference. The likeliest paths all leave this application ungranted. (a) The Court
  grants cert in Brunswick or another vehicle and denies the vacatur, leaving the Fifth Circuit's stay
  in place while it decides the question. (b) The Court denies cert in Brunswick, which signals it
  will not disturb the majority circuit rule on the interim docket. (c) The Court holds the
  application or denies it without prejudice. Vacatur becomes plausible mainly if the Court is
  already inclined to reverse the Fourth, Fifth and Eleventh Circuits' reading of *Griggs*. Even
  then, it would more likely grant review than vacate on an interim basis.
- **Certiorari before judgment.** I expect it to be denied, or overtaken by action in Brunswick. If
  the Court does take up the question, I expect it to take Brunswick's QP (the post-transmittal
  automatic stay under *Griggs*/*Coinbase* in a § 1447(d) appeal), not this application. If it ever
  reached the merits, the likely ground would be the § 1447(c) and § 1446(d) text plus the
  Anti-Injunction Act, set against *Coinbase*'s background rule.
- **Separate writings.** On a denial, a short dissent or statement is possible, most plausibly from
  Justice Jackson (the *Coinbase* dissent) or Justice Sotomayor. It is not my modal expectation.
