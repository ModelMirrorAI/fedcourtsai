# Forecast of the Court's action

The Court will most likely **deny the application to vacate the Fifth Circuit's October 5, 2026 stay**, leaving the remand appeal to proceed. The probability of an unqualified grant is **27%**. Denial will not settle whether federal-officer remand appeals automatically suspend state-court proceedings after transmission of the remand order.

The arrival record shows **no response request, no full-Court referral, and zero amici**. From that state:

- **Response request: 85%.** I expect Justice Sotomayor or the Court to call for Devon Energy's response before disposition, allowing a short briefing exchange on the statutory basis for the stay and the claimed sovereign injury.
- **Referral to the full Court: 90%.** I expect collective consideration rather than a final disposition by the receiving Justice alone. Justice Alito will not participate, consistent with the express recusal in the snapshot.
- **Additional amicus entry: 42%.** The modal forecast is no amicus increment; if an increment occurs, one or two supporting filings from interested sovereigns or repeat litigants are more likely than a large briefing campaign. A qualifying submitted brief counts without waiting for formal acceptance.
- **Disposition: denial.** A short order is more likely than an opinion resolving the automatic-stay question. I expect disposition in late October or early November 2026, roughly October 16–November 5; a hold for related proceedings is a secondary possibility. Timing is an unscored forecast, not a docket fact.

The likely reason for withholding emergency relief is that the application presents a substantial but disputed procedural question without a sufficiently compelling showing that the existing appellate stay must be undone immediately. The Court can preserve the appellate process without endorsing the Fifth Circuit's entire theory. A response request or referral therefore should not be mistaken for a forecast of a grant.

If the Court instead grants vacatur, the likeliest ground is narrow: the remand had already been transmitted, and the federalism and statutory limits on stopping state-court proceedings distinguish that posture from Coinbase's arbitration appeal. That would not decide coastal-restoration liability or necessarily resolve the different pre-transmittal situation. I do not predict conversion of the application into plenary review as the modal route, and do not forecast a per-Justice vote division or an unconditional merits opinion.
