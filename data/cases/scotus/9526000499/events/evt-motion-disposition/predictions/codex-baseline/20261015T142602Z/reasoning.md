## Adjustment above the anchor

The application presents a developed legal dispute rather than an unsupported request. Its introduction and pages 15–20 describe disagreement over automatic stays after a remand has already been transmitted to state court. Importantly, the Fourth Circuit's pre-transmittal decision is not a square holding on this post-transmittal question. Pages 21–30 develop substantial statutory and federalism objections under 28 U.S.C. §§ 1446(d), 1447(c), and 2283. Pages 33–35 identify Louisiana's sovereign enforcement interests and alleged long-running delay. Those features warrant a meaningful upward adjustment from 9.65% and make response and referral more likely than immediate single-Justice rejection.

But review-worthiness is not entitlement to emergency vacatur. The longstanding vacatur formulation requires serious irreparable injury and an appellate stay that is demonstrably wrong under accepted standards: *Western Airlines, Inc. v. Teamsters*, 480 U.S. 1301, 1305 (1987) (O'Connor, J., in chambers), quoting *Coleman v. Paccar, Inc.*, 424 U.S. 1301, 1304 (1976), verified through CourtListener. A genuine division over an unsettled extension can support eventual review while making immediate correction less compelling.

*Coinbase, Inc. v. Bielski*, 599 U.S. 736, 743–744 (2023), supplies a substantial competing rationale: preserving appellate determination of a forum question through an automatic stay, subject to contrary statutory indications. Its holding concerns arbitration and federal district-court proceedings, not this state-court remand posture. Nonetheless, its reasoning makes the Fifth Circuit's position more defensible than the application's rhetoric suggests. The verified Coinbase lineup also cautions against assuming a ready majority for vacatur: Alito's recusal removes one member of its five-Justice majority, but the remaining Justices need not repeat their arbitration votes on either federalism or emergency relief. I do not mechanically map those votes onto this application.

The described harms are serious but center on further litigation delay and sovereign interests. The provisioned application identifies no imminent execution, fixed trial deadline, or comparable irreversible event demanding intervention this week. The Court can leave the stay in place while the appeal proceeds or consider the legal issue through ordinary review. The separate Brunswick petition mentioned in the application may offer another vehicle; I have not checked its later treatment. These considerations keep the grant estimate well below one-half despite the substantial legal question. The principal uncertainty is how the Court weighs the post-transmittal statutory distinction against preserving appellate jurisdiction, without the respondent's full position before me.
