# Forecast: Louisiana v. Devon Energy Production Co., No. 26A499

**Disposition: denied.** I expect the application to vacate the Fifth Circuit's October 5 stay order to be denied, and the alternative request to treat it as a petition for certiorari before judgment to be denied with it. I expect no noted dissent, or at most one statement. The order should come soon, within days to about two weeks of the October 21 response, possibly after a short reply from Louisiana.

**Where the record stands.** Louisiana applied on October 9 to Justice Sotomayor, acting because Justice Alito (the Fifth Circuit's Circuit Justice) is recused. She called for a response on October 14, and Devon filed its opposition on October 21. The record shows no referral to the full Court and no amicus filings.

**Why denial.**
- The same question was before the Court in *City of Brunswick v. Honeywell*, No. 26-289, and certiorari was denied on October 19, 2026 (as the response reports). The Court also denied *Express Scripts v. California*, No. 25-327, in January 2026. The Court has just declined plenary review of this exact question, so the first prong of the vacatur test (a reasonable probability of certiorari) is very hard to meet.
- With Justice Alito recused, the Court would sit eight-handed on a question that turns on reading *Coinbase*, a 5-4 decision. A divided Court is a real possibility, which argues for waiting for a full-Court vehicle.
- Vacating a court of appeals' stay of a remand order pending that court's own expedited appeal is rare. The equities claimed are delay in a state-court forum, not anything irreversible.

**Ladder claims.**
- *Response requested*: already happened (October 14), so the claim is vacuous for this cell.
- *Referral to the full Court*: possible but less likely than not. Justice Sotomayor can deny alone. A referral becomes more likely if she wants the full bench's view because a State is the applicant, or if Louisiana renews the application to another Justice after a denial in chambers.
- *Amicus*: unlikely in the short window left. Possible sources are other coastal parishes or States, but none has appeared so far.

**If the Court did act for Louisiana** (unlikely), it would most likely be a full-Court order vacating the stay on the Ninth Circuit's reasoning in *Harrison*: once the remand order is mailed, nothing is left in the district court to stay. A grant of certiorari before judgment is even less likely, given the *Brunswick* denial.
