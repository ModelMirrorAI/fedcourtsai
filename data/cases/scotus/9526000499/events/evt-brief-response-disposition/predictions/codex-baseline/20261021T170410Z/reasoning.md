## Adjustments

The requested response is positive evidence of attention, and the application presents a genuine, potentially recurring legal disagreement. Louisiana's strongest argument is that statutory restoration of state-court jurisdiction upon transmission differs from the continuing district-court proceedings addressed by Griggs and Coinbase. Its federalism and Anti-Injunction Act arguments make relief possible rather than negligible. Those features would initially move this application above the unconditioned prior.

The opposition materially reverses that upward adjustment. Its introduction and discussion of reviewability report that the Court declined review of the related City of Brunswick issue on October 19, just two days before this snapshot; the application also identifies the earlier Express Scripts denial. I treat these as pre-decision context about other cases, not as dispositions of this application and not as holdings approving an automatic stay. Nevertheless, two reported opportunities to review the issue being declined weaken the claim that immediate intervention here is likely. The October 19 development is especially probative because it follows the application and is addressed by the response. It was not independently verified outside the provisioned materials.

Devon also identifies a disputed Rule 62 threshold issue, an alternative discretionary-stay request, and recusal-related vehicle concerns. The application offers meaningful answers, including its narrower post-transmittal question, but these complications make this a poor emergency vehicle even if Louisiana has the better eventual statutory argument. I do not adopt Devon's Rule 62 theory as settled law. On equities, Louisiana identifies sovereign and enforcement injuries, while Devon invokes preservation of its claimed federal forum and appellate remedy. A longstanding damages action presents less immediate urgency than an imminent execution, election deadline, or compelled disclosure. Together these factors pull my final probability below the 9.32% pool, to 6%.
