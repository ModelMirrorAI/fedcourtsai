## What happened

Calvary Chapel San Jose and its pastor, Mike McClure, refused to comply with Santa Clara County's COVID-era public health rules, including a requirement that face coverings be worn indoors. The county fined the church, and after Calvary Chapel did not pay, sued to collect. California courts ruled against the church: a state trial court and then the California Court of Appeal upheld fines of over $1.2 million, finding the face-covering rule applied neutrally to everyone and that the fines were not excessive given the church's repeated, intentional violations. The California Supreme Court declined further review. Calvary Chapel now asks the U.S. Supreme Court to take the case. California, opposing review, argues the decision is unpublished, fact-specific, and involves a rule dropped nearly five years ago.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked whether a public health rule with several exceptions—for young children, people with medical conditions, or specific activities like dining—counts as neutral and generally applied, or whether such exceptions mean stricter constitutional review should apply to a church's objection. Calvary Chapel also asks the Court to recognize a new exception letting religious organizations control their own worship practices free from such rules, or, alternatively, to abandon the longstanding legal framework for evaluating free-exercise claims altogether. Finally, the Court is asked whether fining the church more than a million dollars for its violations was so disproportionate to its conduct that it amounted to an unconstitutionally excessive fine.

## Where it stands

The petition has been fully briefed, with multiple outside groups filing supporting briefs and the petitioner filing a reply. As of the snapshot date, the case had been distributed for the Court's conference on April 24, 2026, and the docket shows no action taken since that distribution.
