## What happened

Calvary Chapel San Jose and its pastor, Mike McClure, refused to follow Santa Clara County's COVID-era public health rules, including a requirement that face coverings be worn indoors. The county fined the church over a million dollars for these violations and sued to collect. California courts had earlier struck down separate county orders limiting indoor gathering sizes as unconstitutional, but the courts upheld the face-covering fines, finding the rule applied equally to everyone and that the fines matched the church's conduct. California's appeals court and supreme court both rejected the church's challenges. The church now asks the U.S. Supreme Court to take the case, while the county argues the fines and legal rulings were correctly decided.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked several related questions. First, whether a public health rule with exceptions for things like medical conditions or specific low-risk activities should have been subject to the toughest constitutional review because those exceptions supposedly created comparable risks to what the church was doing. Second, whether religious organizations should have a specialized freedom, beyond existing protections for hiring clergy, covering how they conduct worship services. Third, whether the Court should abandon or narrow the longstanding rule that neutral, broadly applicable laws do not need special justification to apply to religious conduct. Finally, whether the size of the fines imposed on the church was constitutionally excessive given its conduct and the harm caused.

## Where it stands

The case has not yet been scheduled for argument. The petition, opposition brief, and reply have all been filed, along with several amicus briefs supporting the church, and the case was distributed for the Court's conference on April 24, 2026, meaning the justices will consider then whether to take up the case.
