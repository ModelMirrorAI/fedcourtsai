## In brief

A church fined over $1 million for violating COVID-era mask rules asks the Court to revisit how courts evaluate religious-freedom challenges to public health orders.

## What happened

Calvary Chapel San Jose and its pastor refused to comply with Santa Clara County's COVID-19 rules requiring face coverings indoors. The county fined the church, and after the church did not pay, sued to collect. California courts had earlier struck down separate county orders limiting indoor gathering capacity as unconstitutional, but a state trial court later upheld more than $1.2 million in fines specifically for violating the face-covering requirement, rejecting the church's arguments that the rule violated its religious freedom and the ban on excessive fines. The California Court of Appeal affirmed, and the California Supreme Court denied further review.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked whether health rules with several exceptions for non-religious activity, but none for religious worship, must meet the toughest constitutional test before they can restrict religious conduct. The petition also asks whether churches have a right to control the details of their worship services free from such rules, whether the Court should abandon the current framework for religious-freedom cases altogether, and whether the fines imposed were so large compared to the church's conduct that they violated the constitutional ban on excessive fines.

## Where it stands

The petition was fully briefed, including several outside groups filing supporting briefs, and the case was distributed for the Court's conference on April 24, 2026. The docket shows no action taken since that distribution.

## What each outcome would mean

Calvary Chapel is asking the Court to take up the case. If the Court grants review, it will hear full arguments and decide the questions itself, without that decision being determined in advance. If the Court denies review, the California Court of Appeal's ruling upholding the fines against Calvary Chapel stands, and the church remains liable for the fines as ordered. A denial would not mean the Court agrees the lower court was right; it simply leaves that decision in place.
