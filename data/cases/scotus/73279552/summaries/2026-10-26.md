## In brief

A California church asks the Court to overturn over $1 million in fines imposed for violating a county mask rule during the COVID-19 pandemic.

## What happened

Calvary Chapel San Jose and its pastor refused to comply with Santa Clara County's COVID-era rule requiring face coverings indoors, which applied to businesses, including churches, with narrow exceptions for things like young children and people with medical conditions. The county fined the church, and when the church did not pay, a state court upheld fines of $1,228,700. The California Court of Appeal affirmed, finding the rule neutral and generally applicable and the fines proportionate to the church's conduct. The California Supreme Court denied further review.

## What the Court is being asked

The church raises several related questions. It asks whether a health rule with various exceptions for other activities must be treated as not generally applicable, which would require the government to show a compelling reason for applying it to religious services. It also asks the Court to recognize a new exception letting churches control their own worship practices without such rules applying at all, or alternatively to abandon the current framework for evaluating free exercise claims altogether. Finally, it asks whether the fines imposed were so large compared to the church's conduct and the harm caused that they violate the constitutional limit on excessive fines.

## Where it stands

The petition has been fully briefed, including a reply filed by the church in April 2026, and the case was distributed for the Court's conference on April 24, 2026. No decision on whether to hear the case has yet been recorded.

## What each outcome would mean

Calvary Chapel San Jose, the church, is asking the Court to take up the case and reverse the lower court's ruling. If the Court grants review, it would hear full arguments and decide the free exercise and excessive fines questions, deciding nothing about who wins simply by taking the case. If the Court denies review, the California Court of Appeal's decision stands, leaving the fines against the church in place and leaving California's rule in effect as applied here.
