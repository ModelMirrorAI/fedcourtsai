## In brief

A church fined over $1 million for ignoring a county's pandemic mask rule asks the Court to find the rule unconstitutional and reduce the penalty.

## What happened

During the COVID-19 pandemic, Santa Clara County required face coverings in indoor spaces, including churches, with narrow exceptions for young children and people with medical conditions. Calvary Chapel San Jose and its pastor refused to comply, and the county fined them more than $1.2 million. California's trial court and court of appeal both upheld the fines, finding the mask rule neutral and generally applicable and the fines proportional to the church's conduct. California's supreme court denied further review.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked whether a health rule with several narrow exceptions still counts as evenhanded, or whether those exceptions mean the rule should instead face the toughest level of judicial review when it burdens religious practice. The petition also asks the Court to create a new exemption for religious ceremonies from the general rule that neutral laws don't need special justification, or to abandon that general rule entirely, and whether fines of this size for violating a mask rule are so large compared to the conduct involved that they cross a constitutional line against excessive financial penalties.

## Where it stands

The petition and the county's brief opposing review have both been filed, along with the church's reply and several outside groups' supporting briefs. The case was distributed for the justices' conference on April 24, 2026, and the docket shows no action since.

## What each outcome would mean

Calvary Chapel, the church, is asking the Court to take up the case and reverse the rulings against it. If the Court grants review, it would hear full arguments and decide the questions itself, deciding nothing yet about who is right. If it denies review, the California Court of Appeal's decision upholding the fines and the mask rule stands, leaving the county's win in place and the church responsible for the fines as ordered.
