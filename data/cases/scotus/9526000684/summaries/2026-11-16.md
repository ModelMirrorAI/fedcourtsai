## In brief

A pro se bankruptcy litigant asked the Chief Justice for an emergency stay to block a foreclosure and a pending court ruling; the Chief Justice denied it.

## What happened

Paul Schultz, a pro se debtor, has been involved in extended bankruptcy and related litigation involving BOKF, N.A. and other creditors, including disputes over a loan and a property called Kapalua. A bankruptcy court granted BOKF relief from the automatic stay affecting the property, and a foreclosure sale was scheduled for December 17, 2026. Schultz sought rehearing and emergency relief in the Fourth Circuit, which denied mandamus and had not yet ruled on rehearing. The district court and bankruptcy court also denied his requests to pause proceedings. Schultz then asked the Chief Justice, as Circuit Justice for the Fourth Circuit, to step in.

## What the Court is being asked

Schultz asked the Chief Justice to temporarily block two things while his appeals proceed: a federal judge's ruling on his bankruptcy appeal, and enforcement of the order that allowed the foreclosure sale to go forward. He argued this was needed to preserve his ability to get appellate review of claims that the judge should be disqualified and that earlier rulings never actually decided key legal questions about who held valid rights to enforce his loan.

## Where it stands

The application for a stay was submitted to the Chief Justice on November 10, 2026, and was denied by the Chief Justice on November 16, 2026.

## What each outcome would mean

Schultz, the applicant, asked the Chief Justice to pause the foreclosure sale and the district court's ruling on his appeal. Granting the stay would have temporarily blocked the foreclosure and delayed a decision in Schultz's bankruptcy appeal while his related requests for rehearing and emergency relief proceeded in the Fourth Circuit. Denying it, which is what happened, leaves the lower courts' orders in effect: the foreclosure sale and the district court proceedings may go forward without the pause Schultz requested. The denial does not decide the underlying merits of his claims about judicial recusal or the validity of the loan.
