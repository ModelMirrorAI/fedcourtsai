## In brief

A Virginia debtor asks the Chief Justice for emergency orders blocking a foreclosure sale and a district judge's ruling while his related appeals continue.

## What happened

Paul Schultz, a pro se bankruptcy debtor, has been litigating for years over claims by creditor BOKF, N.A. and a related entity, Clear Sky Financial, concerning a loan and a property called Kapalua. The bankruptcy court granted BOKF relief from the automatic stay that normally protects a debtor's property during bankruptcy, clearing the way for a foreclosure sale scheduled for December 17, 2026. Schultz sought a stay first from the bankruptcy court and the district court, both of which denied his requests, and from the Fourth Circuit, which denied a related mandamus petition and has a rehearing petition still pending. Schultz now brings this application directly to the Chief Justice, the Justice assigned to handle emergency matters from the Fourth Circuit.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked to temporarily pause two things while Schultz's appeals continue: a federal district judge entering a final ruling on his bankruptcy appeal, and any enforcement of the bankruptcy court's order that would allow his home to be sold at foreclosure. Schultz argues the district judge should not rule because a pending challenge to her continuing to handle the case has not yet been resolved, and that losing the home before that challenge is decided would make later review meaningless.

## Where it stands

The application was submitted to the Chief Justice on November 10, 2026, and docketed November 13, 2026. The docket shows no order yet granting or denying the requested stay.

## What each outcome would mean

Schultz, the applicant, is asking the Court to pause the lower proceedings. If the Chief Justice grants a stay, the district judge's ruling and the foreclosure sale would be put on hold temporarily while Schultz's appeals proceed, without deciding who is ultimately right. If the stay is denied, the district judge could rule and the foreclosure sale could proceed as scheduled, while Schultz's underlying appeals continue separately. Either way, this request does not decide the merits of Schultz's claims against BOKF or his challenge to the judge's participation.
