# Predicted Court Action and Reasoning

The Court will deny the petition for a writ of certiorari without calling for a response, without further relisting, and without any separate writing.

## Conference Trajectory and Relists

The docket currently records **one** distribution—entered on November 17, 2026, for the Conference of December 4, 2026—following the Commissioner of Internal Revenue's waiver of the right to respond on November 13, 2026, and zero relists to date. Expect **zero further distributions or relists**. Neither a Call for a Response (CFR) nor a conference holdover will occur; the petition will be denied on the routine order list issuing from the December 4, 2026 Conference (unless terminated slightly earlier by a voluntary dismissal under Rule 46 if the petitioner resolves his parallel administrative tax proceeding).

## Views of the Solicitor General (CVSG)

The Court will **not** call for the views of the Solicitor General. Beyond the absence of certworthy issues, the respondent is the Commissioner of Internal Revenue, already represented on the docket by the Solicitor General. Were the Court ever inclined to hear from the government, its procedural mechanism would be a Call for a Response directed to the party respondent rather than a non-party CVSG invitation.

## Questions Presented and Conditional Merits Posture

If the Court were to grant review, it could **not** take any of the petition's three questions presented as written. All three questions presented challenge the substantive scope of "collected proceeds" under 26 U.S.C. § 7623(b) and (c) (specifically whether tax, penalty, and interest figures disclosed in a public company's SEC Forms 10-Q and 10-K qualify) and assert improper judicial deference to the IRS on the administrative record. Neither the Tax Court nor the D.C. Circuit reached those merits or evidentiary questions: both courts dismissed solely for lack of Tax Court subject-matter jurisdiction under 26 U.S.C. § 7623(b)(4) because the IRS Whistleblower Office rejected the claim at the threshold as speculative and never commenced an administrative or judicial action against any target taxpayer (*Li v. Commissioner*, 22 F.4th 1014 (D.C. Cir. 2022); *Kennedy v. Commissioner*, 142 F.4th 769 (D.C. Cir. 2025)). Consequently, if granted at all, the Court would have to reformulate or substitute a threshold jurisdictional question: whether 26 U.S.C. § 7623(b)(4) confers Tax Court jurisdiction over a Whistleblower Office threshold rejection where the IRS initiated no administrative or judicial action against a taxpayer. If the Court ever reached that reformulated question on plenary review, the dispositive debate would turn on whether *Li* and *Kennedy* survive *Boechler, P.C. v. Commissioner*, 596 U.S. 199 (2022), or whether the absence of an IRS enforcement action means there is no "determination regarding an award under paragraph (1), (2), or (3)" within the text of § 7623(b)(4).

## Summary Disposition vs. Plenary Review

No grant of any kind is expected, and no intervening Supreme Court decision or pending merits case on § 7623(b) currently exists to support a routine hold-and-GVR. Conditional on the remote event that the Court *does* grant review, however, a **summary disposition in the certiorari order—specifically a grant, vacate, and remand (GVR)**—is modestly likelier than setting the case for plenary briefing and oral argument. Because the petitioner proceeds *pro se* from an unpublished D.C. Circuit Rule 36 judgment and failed to brief the dispositive § 7623(b)(4) jurisdictional holding, the Court would strongly resist using this docket for plenary merits review and would grant primarily if an intervening jurisdictional precedent or post-settlement mootness (*Munsingwear*) warranted vacatur and remand. A summary per curiam reversal on the merits is effectively foreclosed both by the government's waiver and by the unaddressed jurisdictional bar below.

## Separate Writings on Denial

Should the Court deny certiorari as expected, **no Justice will file a dissent from, or statement respecting, the denial of certiorari**. Although Justices Gorsuch and Thomas occasionally write separately on tax administration and judicial review of agency action, the Court does not issue separate writings on un-CFR'd *pro se* petitions where the questions presented bypass the lower court's unpublished jurisdictional holding and no circuit split exists under the D.C. Circuit's default appellate venue in 26 U.S.C. § 7482(b)(1).
