## In brief

A taxpayer asks the Court to revive his bid for an IRS whistleblower award after lower courts rejected his claim for lack of jurisdiction.

## What happened

Don Hou submitted information to the IRS alleging tax noncompliance by individuals, entities, and a public company, and sought a whistleblower award under a federal tax statute. The IRS rejected his claim, and the Tax Court dismissed his case, concluding it lacked jurisdiction because the IRS had not pursued an administrative or judicial action against the taxpayers he identified, following circuit precedent on when such claims can be reviewed. The D.C. Circuit affirmed that dismissal, citing its own prior rulings. The D.C. Circuit later denied rehearing and rehearing en banc, and issued its mandate. Hou, representing himself, then petitioned the Supreme Court.

## What the Court is being asked

Hou asks the Court to decide whether courts may limit mandatory whistleblower award provisions by disregarding a related provision defining what counts as collected proceeds, whether those proceeds include tax amounts a public company discloses in securities filings when tied to IRS enforcement triggered by whistleblower information, and whether the appeals court wrongly deferred to the IRS without requiring it to fully justify its decision.

## Where it stands

The Commissioner waived the right to respond to the petition, and the case was distributed for the Court's December 4, 2026 conference. The docket shows no action since that distribution.

## What each outcome would mean

Hou, the petitioner, asks the Court to take up his case. If the Court grants review, it would hear the case in full and decide the questions about the whistleblower statute, without yet deciding who is right. If the Court denies review, the D.C. Circuit's ruling affirming dismissal of Hou's claim for lack of jurisdiction stands, and the Commissioner's position prevails, ending Hou's challenge.
