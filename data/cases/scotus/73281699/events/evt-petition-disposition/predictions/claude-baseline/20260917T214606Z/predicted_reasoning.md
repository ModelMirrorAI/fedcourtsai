# Forecast: what the Court will do with No. 25-1215, Cohen v. Judicial Conduct Board of Pennsylvania

**Bottom line.** The petition will most likely be **denied** at or shortly after the September 28, 2026 conference. P(any grant) = 0.16.

**State forecast from.** The docket shows **two distributions**: one for the May 21, 2026 conference (before the Court called for a response on May 12) and one for the September 28, 2026 long conference (after the brief in opposition and the reply). No relist in the ordinary sense has occurred; the second distribution is the post-response redistribution. No CVSG is on the docket. No amicus brief has been filed on either side.

**Further distributions.** Roughly one chance in three (0.35) that the petition is distributed at least once more past the two entries shown. The Court called for a response on a petition the respondent had waived, which means at least one chambers wanted a closer look, and petitions in that position are relisted after the long conference more often than the run of paid petitions, whether to draft a dissent from denial, to hold for a related case, or to consider a grant. The most likely single path is still a clean denial on the October 5 order list with no further distribution. If relisted, expect one to two further distributions, not a long string.

**CVSG.** Very unlikely (0.03). No federal party, no federal statute, and the Solicitor General has no institutional stake in a state judicial-conduct code. The federal judiciary's own Code of Conduct is not implicated by the question as framed.

**Which question, if granted.** The Court would take the question as the petition writes it, the level of scrutiny for content-based restrictions on a sitting judge's non-campaign speech, but would likely narrow it in practice to the partisan-political-activity rules (Rules 4.1(A)(3) and 4.1(A)(11)) rather than the six-rule tangle the Pennsylvania courts actually applied. A reformulation asking whether Republican Party of Minnesota v. White and Williams-Yulee extend beyond judicial candidates is the likeliest shape.

**Summary disposition.** Conditional on a grant, a summary route is improbable (0.05). There is no intervening decision to GVR against and the standard-of-review question is contested, so a grant would mean plenary review.

**Dissent or statement respecting denial.** Conditional on a denial, roughly 0.15 that some Justice notes a dissent or files a statement. The petition's theory, that content-based speech restrictions on judges draw strict scrutiny, resonates with Justices Thomas, Gorsuch, and Alito, and the Court's call for a response suggests interest. But the facts are unattractive for a writing: sustained partisan cheerleading on a page bearing the judge's robe photo and title, and an alternative holding below that the sanction survives strict scrutiny. Most likely the denial issues without comment.

**If granted (conditional only).** The likeliest outcome would be an affirmance or a narrow vacatur: a majority holding that Pickering-type balancing, adapted for the judicial office, governs non-campaign speech by sitting judges, with the "prestige of office" features of this record doing much of the work. A decision extending White's strict scrutiny to all sitting-judge speech is the minority scenario.
