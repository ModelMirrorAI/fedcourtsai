## Why 25%, rather than the baseline or a grant prediction

**Reasons to move upward:** The petition presents a defined federal constitutional standard expressly decided by a state court of last resort. Its argument is not merely dissatisfaction with a sanction: it identifies conflicting treatment of sitting judges' speech and disputes the extension of public-employee balancing outside judicial campaigns. The petition's main split discussion is at pages 13–23; its vehicle and merits arguments are at pages 23–33. Those considerations, together with the requested response, support a meaningful upward adjustment.

The strongest independently checked comparison is **Jenevein v. Willing, 493 F.3d 551, 557–61 (5th Cir. 2007)**. The opinion actually chooses strict scrutiny rather than directly adopting the Pickering-Garcetti framework, while also permitting censure for use of courtroom and robe. That supports a real standards disagreement but simultaneously supports a vehicle objection. I do not accept the BIO's proposed earlier-panel override as a settled elimination of the split merely because it is asserted by counsel.

The retrieved reply, pages 1–6, emphasizes direct disagreement with Jenevein and invokes Ohio's Rudduck decision concerning a sitting judge's Facebook endorsement. At pages 7–10 it argues that applying strict scrutiny could still change the judgment, distinguishes Jenevein's robe issue, and disputes the importance of Cohen's inability to seek reelection. These arguments make the conflict and reviewability stronger than the BIO alone suggests. I treat the Ohio characterization as advocacy in the reply and BIO, not as an independently verified holding. I did not read the separate Ohio opinion.

**Reasons to remain below even odds:** The BIO, pages 9–18, distinguishes campaign speech, religious-exercise claims, legislative testimony, and partisan advocacy using judicial prestige. It identifies six disciplinary rules, varied conduct, Cohen's mandatory retirement, and the disciplinary tribunal's alternative strict-scrutiny analysis. These are substantial reasons the Court could prefer a cleaner case. They are not a demonstrated jurisdictional bar: the state supreme court decided the federal standard, and the reply contests whether the same result necessarily follows under stricter review. The petition's pension discussion raises personal stakes, but the BIO explains that pension consequences arise in a separate administrative proceeding; I do not treat pension forfeiture as the precise judgment under review.

Taking both sides seriously, I set **P(any grant) = 0.25**: above the approximately 0.172 reached-band prior, but still favoring denial because clean outcome-determinative conflict is less certain than a disagreement about doctrinal labels. This is a judgmental adjustment, not a fitted likelihood model. The principal sensitivity is whether the Justices view this as a clean opportunity to settle the standard or an entangled as-applied discipline case.
