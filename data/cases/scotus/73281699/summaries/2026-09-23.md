## What happened

Mark Cohen, a Philadelphia judge, kept posting on a public Facebook page after joining the bench—commenting on politicians, legislation, and at least one candidate—while the page identified him as a judge and once showed him in his robe. Pennsylvania's Judicial Conduct Board charged him with violating the state's judicial ethics code. The Court of Judicial Discipline found violations and suspended him without pay for his final three months in office before mandatory retirement. The Pennsylvania Supreme Court unanimously affirmed, ruling that a balancing test used for public employees' speech, rather than the toughest constitutional test, applied to sitting judges' speech outside election campaigns, and that Cohen's conduct failed either way.

## What the Court is being asked

The case asks what standard courts should use when a judge argues that rules limiting a sitting judge's speech violate free speech protections. Cohen argues the toughest standard, reserved for content-based speech restrictions generally, should apply, pointing to decisions from several states and federal appeals courts he says used that tougher standard. The Judicial Conduct Board answers that the more flexible balancing approach the Pennsylvania court used is correct, that any disagreement among other courts is overstated, and that Cohen's suspension would hold up even under the stricter test he prefers.

## Where it stands
