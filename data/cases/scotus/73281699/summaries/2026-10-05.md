## In brief

The Court declined to hear a former Pennsylvania judge's challenge to his suspension over partisan Facebook posts, leaving his discipline in place.

## What happened

Mark Cohen, a Philadelphia judge, kept posting on a personal Facebook page that identified him as a judge and once featured him in his judicial robe. The posts praised Democratic politicians and criticized Republicans. Pennsylvania's Court of Judicial Discipline found this violated the state's judicial conduct code and suspended him without pay for his final three months in office. The Pennsylvania Supreme Court unanimously affirmed, ruling that a more flexible balancing test, rather than the toughest level of First Amendment scrutiny, applied to restrictions on a sitting judge's speech outside an election campaign. Cohen asked the U.S. Supreme Court to review that ruling.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court was asked whether rules limiting what a sitting judge may say must meet the most demanding First Amendment test, the one used for direct restrictions on political speech, rather than a more flexible balancing test weighing a judge's speech interests against the government's interest in an impartial judiciary.

## Where it stands

The petition was distributed for conference twice, the Court requested a response from the state judicial conduct board, and both sides filed briefs. The Court denied the petition.

## What each outcome would mean

Cohen, the judge seeking review, asked the Court to overturn the Pennsylvania Supreme Court's ruling against him. By denying the petition, the Court leaves that ruling in place: Cohen's suspension stands, and the lower court's approach to judges' speech remains the law in Pennsylvania. The denial does not mean the Supreme Court agreed with the Pennsylvania court's reasoning; it simply leaves that decision undisturbed without deciding the First Amendment question Cohen raised.
