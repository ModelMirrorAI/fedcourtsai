## In brief

West Virginia officials ask the Court to shut down foster children's lawsuit seeking court-ordered reform of the state's foster-care system.

## What happened

Foster children sued West Virginia officials in 2019, claiming the state's foster-care system violated their constitutional and statutory rights through understaffing, abuse, and inadequate placements. After years of litigation, a federal district court dismissed the case, ruling it lacked power to grant the sweeping institutional changes the children sought and so could not redress their claims. The Fourth Circuit reversed, holding that federal courts have both the power and duty to oversee reform of state institutions. It also declined to review West Virginia's challenge to the lower court's class-certification order, reasoning that order was no longer appealable once the dismissal was reversed. West Virginia now asks the Supreme Court to review both rulings.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked whether a lawsuit can proceed when the only remedy that would work requires a federal court to take over management of a state agency, and whether a rule letting courts quickly review class-certification orders prevents an appeals court from reviewing such an order later, after it becomes part of a final judgment on appeal.

## Where it stands

The petition was distributed for the Court's conference of November 6, 2026, after the children waived their right to respond; the Court then asked for a response, and the deadline for that response has since been extended to December 8, 2026. No conference has yet considered the petition on its merits.

## What each outcome would mean

West Virginia, which lost in the Fourth Circuit, is asking the Court to take up the case and reverse. If the Court grants the petition, it would hear the case and decide whether the lawsuit can proceed and whether the class-certification order was reviewable. If the Court denies the petition, the Fourth Circuit's ruling stands, and the case returns to the lower courts, where the children's lawsuit would continue and the class-certification order would not be reviewed on this appeal.
