## Questions Presented

The petition in *Patrick Morrisey, Governor of West Virginia, et al. v. Jonathan R., Minor, by Next Friend, Sarah Dixon, et al.*, No. 26-297, presents two questions:

1. **Article III Redressability and Institutional Reform Injunctions:** "Is a claim redressable when the only relief said to be effective requires a federal court to become the 'manager or policy planner' of a state agency?"
2. **Appellate Review of Class Certification on Conditional Cross-Appeal (Merger Rule vs. Rule 23(f)):** "When a class-certification order merges into a final judgment, does Federal Rule of Civil Procedure 23(f) nonetheless bar an appellate court from reviewing that order on a cross-appeal from the judgment?"

## Decision Below

In *Jonathan R. v. Morrisey*, 178 F.4th 139 (4th Cir. June 4, 2026) (amended June 8, 2026), a divided panel of the United States Court of Appeals for the Fourth Circuit (Senior Judge Floyd, joined by Judge Harris; Judge Rushing concurring in the judgment in part and dissenting in part) reversed the Southern District of West Virginia's *sua sponte* dismissal for lack of Article III standing and remanded for further proceedings:

- **Background and Procedural History:** Twelve minor plaintiffs in West Virginia's foster-care system filed this putative class action in September 2019 against the Governor of West Virginia and officials of the West Virginia Department of Human Services (formerly DHHR), alleging systemic substantive due process violations under the Fourteenth Amendment and statutory violations under the Americans with Disabilities Act (ADA) and the Rehabilitation Act. Plaintiffs sought declaratory relief and broad structural injunctive relief across the state foster-care system (including caseload caps, 30-day evaluation and 60-day case-planning timetables, placement-array reforms, and a neutral court-appointed monitor). After the Fourth Circuit reversed an initial dismissal based on *Younger* abstention and mootness in *Jonathan R. v. Justice* (*Jonathan R. I*), 41 F.4th 316 (4th Cir. 2022), *cert. denied*, 143 S. Ct. 310 (2022) (No. 22-240), the case was reassigned to District Judge Joseph R. Goodwin, who certified a General Class of all current and future West Virginia foster children and an ADA Subclass under Rule 23(b)(2), *Jonathan R. v. Justice*, 344 F.R.D. 294 (S.D.W. Va. 2023). Following discovery and full briefing on West Virginia's motion for summary judgment, the district court *sua sponte* dismissed the action with prejudice for lack of Article III standing under Rule 12(h)(3), holding that federal courts lack equitable power under Article III to issue structural injunctions that manage or overhaul a state agency and that declaratory relief would not independently redress Plaintiffs' injuries. *Jonathan R. v. Morrisey*, 768 F. Supp. 3d 756 (S.D.W. Va. 2025).
- **Majority Opinion (Floyd, S.J., joined by Harris, J.):**
  1. *Article III Standing (Redressability and Injury in Fact):* Applying the two-step redressability inquiry of *Buscemi v. Bell*, 964 F.3d 252, 259 (4th Cir. 2020), the majority held that longstanding Supreme Court and circuit precedent — including *Brown v. Board of Education II*, 349 U.S. 294 (1955), *Swann v. Charlotte-Mecklenburg Board of Education*, 402 U.S. 1 (1971), *Milliken v. Bradley* (*Milliken II*), 433 U.S. 267 (1977), *Hutto v. Finney*, 437 U.S. 678 (1978), and *Brown v. Plata*, 563 U.S. 493 (2011), as well as sister-circuit foster-care decisions in *B.K. ex rel. Tinsley v. Snyder*, 922 F.3d 957 (9th Cir. 2019), and *M.D. ex rel. Stukenberg v. Abbott*, 907 F.3d 237 (5th Cir. 2018) — establishes that federal courts possess both the equitable authority and the duty to remedy systemic constitutional and statutory violations in state institutions. Federalism and separation-of-powers concerns are addressed through three remedial guardrails (tailoring the remedy to the scope of the violation under *Missouri v. Jenkins*, 515 U.S. 70 (1995); accounting for state and local interests and financial constraints under *Milliken II* and *Rufo v. Inmates of Suffolk County Jail*, 502 U.S. 367 (1992); and modifying decrees upon changed circumstances under Rule 60(b)(5) and *Horne v. Flores*, 557 U.S. 433 (2009)), rather than by denying Article III jurisdiction at the outset. The majority also held that Plaintiffs' request for declaratory relief independently satisfied redressability under *Wells v. Johnson*, 150 F.4th 289 (4th Cir. 2025), and that the complaint adequately alleged ongoing injury in fact.
  2. *Reassignment:* The panel unanimously rejected Plaintiffs' request to reassign the case to a different district judge on remand.
  3. *Conditional Cross-Appeal of Class Certification:* Relying on pre-Rule 23(f) decisions from the Ninth and Sixth Circuits (*Blake v. City of Los Angeles*, 595 F.2d 1367, 1386 (9th Cir. 1979); *Milan Express Co. v. Western Surety Co.*, 886 F.2d 783, 785 n.1 (6th Cir. 1989)) and parting ways with the Seventh Circuit (*Council 31, AFSCME v. Ward*, 978 F.2d 373, 380 (7th Cir. 1992)), the majority held that its reversal of the district court's dismissal meant there was no longer a final judgment, rendering West Virginia's conditional cross-appeal of the 2023 class-certification order "effectively interlocutory and thus unreviewable" outside Rule 23(f)'s 14-day window.
- **Concurrence in the Judgment in Part and Dissent in Part (Rushing, J.):**
  1. *Standing:* Judge Rushing concurred in reversing the dismissal for lack of standing. While disagreeing with the majority's view that declaratory relief independently established redressability (App. 44a n.1) and emphasizing that the two district judges who had presided over the case voiced "legitimate" federalism and separation-of-powers concerns about the breadth of Plaintiffs' demands (App. 45a), Judge Rushing concluded that under binding precedent (*Swann*, *Plata*), "at least some of the injunctive relief Plaintiffs seek is within the district court's authority and would relieve at least part of Plaintiffs' injuries," which suffices for Article III redressability at the threshold stage (App. 44a).
  2. *Conditional Cross-Appeal:* Judge Rushing dissented from the majority's refusal to entertain West Virginia's conditional cross-appeal of the class-certification order. She explained that under 28 U.S.C. § 1291 and the merger rule, the interlocutory class-certification order merged into the final judgment of dismissal; reversing that judgment on appeal invigorated the conditional cross-appeal rather than stripping the appellate court of jurisdiction mid-opinion. She further noted that Rule 23(f) is a permissive expansion of interlocutory review whose nonjurisdictional 14-day deadline Plaintiffs had never invoked, and she highlighted two serious *Wal-Mart Stores, Inc. v. Dukes*, 564 U.S. 338 (2011), commonality defects in the district court's certification of the sprawling General Class and ADA Subclass (App. 53a–57a).

## Analysis of the Questions Presented

### 1. Question 1: Article III Redressability and Structural Injunctions

Petitioners' lead question asks the Court to hold that Plaintiffs' claims are non-redressable at the threshold under Article III because Plaintiffs seek a comprehensive structural injunction over West Virginia's foster-care agency, relying heavily on *Trump v. CASA, Inc.*, 606 U.S. 831 (2025), *United States v. Texas*, 599 U.S. 670 (2023), and Justice Thomas's concurrence in *Missouri v. Jenkins*, 515 U.S. 70, 126 (1995).

Despite the rhetorical breadth of parts of the Fourth Circuit majority's opinion (such as quoting *Thompson v. HUD*, 404 F.3d 821, 827 (4th Cir. 2005), on the district judge acting as a "manager or policy planner"), Question 1 is an exceptionally poor vehicle for Supreme Court review at this stage:
- **Unanimous 3-0 Judgment Below on Standing:** Judge Rushing — a conservative jurist who dissented on Question 2 and criticized the majority's dicta on declaratory judgments — fully concurred in reversing the district court's standing dismissal. As she concisely explained, "existing precedent instructs that district courts possess the power to enter injunctions that intervene into the operation of state governmental institutions once they find a constitutional violation," and "[a]t least some of the injunctive relief Plaintiffs seek is within the district court's authority and would relieve at least part of Plaintiffs' injuries. For our purposes, that is sufficient to show redressability" (App. 44a).
- **No Circuit Split:** Every circuit to address statewide foster-care reform suits — including the Fifth Circuit in *M.D. ex rel. Stukenberg v. Abbott*, 907 F.3d 237, 271–76 (5th Cir. 2018), and the Ninth Circuit in *B.K. ex rel. Tinsley v. Snyder*, 922 F.3d 957, 967 (9th Cir. 2019) — has held that federal courts have Article III jurisdiction to entertain such suits and grant tailored prospective injunctive relief, policing overreach at the remedial stage rather than dismissing the entire complaint for lack of standing before any liability determination.
- **Premature / Pre-Liability Posture:** The district court has not adjudicated West Virginia's pending summary judgment motion, held a trial, found any constitutional or statutory violation, or entered *any* injunction (let alone the 47 measures listed in Plaintiffs' prayer for relief). In *Trump v. CASA, Inc.*, 606 U.S. 831 (2025), *Horne v. Flores*, 557 U.S. 433 (2009), *Missouri v. Jenkins*, 515 U.S. 70 (1995), and *Brown v. Plata*, 563 U.S. 493 (2011), the Supreme Court reviewed actual injunctions or consent decrees entered by district courts. Converting disputes over the potential scope of an unentered remedy into a threshold Article III standing bar before liability is even decided would require upending basic remedial and standing doctrine in a case where West Virginia may still prevail on summary judgment or at trial.

### 2. Question 2: Conditional Cross-Appeals of Merged Class-Certification Orders

Question 2 is substantially stronger than Question 1 and accounts for most of the grant/summary-reversal probability in this case:
- **Genuine Circuit Split and Sharp Dissent:** As Judge Rushing's dissent demonstrates (App. 46a–51a), when the district court dismissed the action in full, its prior interlocutory order certifying the General Class and ADA Subclass merged into the final judgment under 28 U.S.C. § 1291 and Federal Rule of Appellate Procedure 3(c)(4). When Plaintiffs appealed that final judgment, West Virginia properly filed a conditional cross-appeal challenging class certification in the event of reversal. The Seventh and D.C. Circuits hold that reversal of the final judgment invigorates the conditional cross-appeal and supports appellate review of the class-certification order (*Council 31, AFSCME v. Ward*, 978 F.2d 373, 380 (7th Cir. 1992); *Henry v. Hulett*, 969 F.3d 769, 787 (7th Cir. 2020) (en banc); *Fink v. National Savings & Trust Co.*, 772 F.2d 951, 960–61 (D.C. Cir. 1985); *Hartman v. Duffey*, 19 F.3d 1459, 1466–67 (D.C. Cir. 1994)). By contrast, the Fourth Circuit majority joined the Sixth Circuit (*Milan Express Co. v. Western Surety Co.*, 886 F.2d 783, 785 n.1 (6th Cir. 1989)) and the Ninth Circuit (*Blake v. City of Los Angeles*, 595 F.2d 1367, 1386 (9th Cir. 1979)) in holding that reversal of the dismissal "unmerges" the class-certification order and renders the cross-appeal barred as an untimely interlocutory appeal under Rule 23(f). Moreover, the panel majority raised this Rule 23(f) bar *sua sponte* despite the nonjurisdictional nature of Rule 23(f)'s 14-day time limit (*Nutraceutical Corp. v. Lambert*, 586 U.S. 188, 192 (2019)).
- **Why Question 2 Still Likely Falls Short of a Grant:**
  1. *Discretionary Nature of Conditional Cross-Appeal Review:* Even Judge Rushing concluded that while the Fourth Circuit had *jurisdiction* over the conditional cross-appeal, deciding whether to reach the merits of class certification before remanding was a matter of appellate *discretion* ("Because we have jurisdiction and there is no procedural bar to our review, it is within our sound discretion to resolve West Virginia's appeal of the class certification order before remanding the case for further proceedings," App. 52a). A remand directing the Fourth Circuit that it has discretion to consider the cross-appeal may not even guarantee a merits ruling if the panel majority would decline as a discretionary matter (as the Ninth Circuit did in *Blake*).
  2. *Availability of Rule 23(c)(1)(C) Modification and Summary Judgment on Remand:* Both the majority (App. 42a n.17) and Judge Rushing (App. 57a) emphasized that under Rule 23(c)(1)(C), the district court retains full power on remand to decertify or modify the classes before or in conjunction with ruling on West Virginia's fully briefed summary judgment motion. Furthermore, District Judge Goodwin — who already expressed skepticism of Plaintiffs' sweeping theories — now has the benefit of Judge Rushing's detailed *Wal-Mart v. Dukes* critique (App. 53a–57a).
  3. *Interlocutory Posture of the Case as a Whole:* The Supreme Court generally prefers to await a final merits disposition or an actual Rule 23(f) posture rather than reviewing a procedural question about whether a court of appeals should have reached a conditional cross-appeal while remanding a threshold standing dismissal.
