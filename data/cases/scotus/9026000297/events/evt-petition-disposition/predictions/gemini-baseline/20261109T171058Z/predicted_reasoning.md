## Question Selection and Conditional Merits Path

- **Which Question Presented the Court Would Take:** If the Court were to grant review (`disposition = 0.12`), it would most likely **grant limited to Question 2 alone** (whether Federal Rule of Civil Procedure 23(f) bars an appellate court from reviewing a class-certification order that merged into a final judgment when the appellate court reverses the final judgment on appeal) and decline Question 1. Question 1 lacks any circuit split, drew a 3-0 panel judgment below reversing the *sua sponte* dismissal, and asks the Court to announce a novel Article III redressability barrier to institutional-reform litigation before the district court has ruled on summary judgment, held a trial, or entered a single remedial order. Question 2, by contrast, presents a clean procedural division among the circuits (putting the Fourth and Sixth Circuits at odds with the Seventh and D.C. Circuits) and a sharp dissent by Judge Rushing.
- **Conditional Merits Reasoning (If Granted):** If certiorari were granted on Question 2 (or on both questions), the Court would likely vacate or reverse the Fourth Circuit's holding that its reversal of the standing dismissal rendered West Virginia's conditional cross-appeal of class certification an unreviewable interlocutory appeal barred by Rule 23(f). Adopting the reasoning of Judge Rushing's dissent, the Court would hold that (1) under 28 U.S.C. § 1291 and the merger rule, the interlocutory class-certification order merged into the final judgment of dismissal, conferring appellate jurisdiction over the conditional cross-appeal; (2) Rule 23(f) expanded permissive interlocutory review rather than curtailing final-judgment cross-appeals; and (3) in any event, Rule 23(f)'s 14-day deadline is a nonjurisdictional claim-processing rule under *Nutraceutical Corp. v. Lambert*, 586 U.S. 188, 192 (2019), that Plaintiffs forfeited by never raising it, leaving the Fourth Circuit with discretion on remand to address the class-certification cross-appeal.

## Summary Disposition vs. Plenary Review (`summary-disposition-route`: 0.25)

Conditional on a grant, the probability of a summary disposition in the cert order itself (rather than plenary briefing and oral argument) is **0.25** (`summary-disposition-route = 0.25`, corresponding to ~3% unconditional probability). While plenary review of the circuit split on conditional cross-appeals of class certification would be the primary grant path (~75% conditional share), the Fourth Circuit majority's *sua sponte* invocation of Rule 23(f)'s 14-day window as a procedural bar despite *Nutraceutical*'s holding that Rule 23(f) is a nonjurisdictional claim-processing rule creates a plausible path for a short per curiam summary vacatur and remand (or GVR) on Question 2 if the Court calls for a response and concludes the panel treated a forfeited claim-processing rule as jurisdictional.
