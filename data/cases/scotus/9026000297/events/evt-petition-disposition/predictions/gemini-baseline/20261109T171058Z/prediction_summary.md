The Supreme Court is very unlikely to hear this appeal, with about a one-in-eight chance (12%) of granting review and an 88% chance of denying West Virginia's petition. In this lawsuit over West Virginia's foster-care system, a federal trial judge threw out the entire case before trial, ruling that courts lack power to order broad agency reforms, and all three appellate judges agreed that dismissing the whole lawsuit before deciding whether the state broke the law was premature. Although one appellate judge dissented over whether the appeals court should also review the trial court's "class certification" order—the ruling allowing thousands of foster children to sue together as a group—the case is still in the middle of proceedings in the trial court, which can reconsider the group status and pending motions on its own, making Supreme Court intervention unlikely now.
