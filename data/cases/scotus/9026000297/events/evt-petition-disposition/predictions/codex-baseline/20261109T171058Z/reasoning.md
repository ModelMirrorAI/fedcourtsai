## Why above the anchor

The **second question is the strongest cert vehicle**. The amended opinion itself acknowledges disagreement about reviewing a class-certification order on a conditional cross-appeal when the underlying final judgment is reversed. The petition, pp.28–34, identifies contrary Seventh and D.C. Circuit authority, and the majority discusses the Seventh Circuit rather than simply overlooking the issue. Council 31 v. Ward, 978 F.2d 373, 380 (7th Cir. 1992), independently retrieved through CourtListener MCP, actually exercised jurisdiction over such a cross-appeal after reversing the judgment. Thus there is more here than a petitioner's unsupported assertion of a split. The rule concerns recurring appellate procedure beyond foster care, is distinctly presented, and produced a reasoned dissent.

The State's sovereign interests, three amicus submissions including a multistate brief, and the Court's request for a response after waiver provide additional reasons to move above 23.3%. The first question raises consequential limits on structural relief, and the majority's expansive account of federal judicial authority gives that question salience. My adjustment to 42% is judgmental, not an estimate from a published response-request or issue-specific subgroup.

## Why denial still leads

The first question embeds a contested premise: that only a complete judicial takeover can provide redress. **Judge Rushing agreed that standing existed and that dismissal must be reversed**, because at least some requested relief could lawfully redress part of the injuries. Her disagreement concerns the majority's reasoning and, dispositively, its refusal to hear the certification cross-appeal; it is not a dissent favoring dismissal for lack of standing. App.46a–49a materially weakens the petition's framing of a divided standing holding.

Moreover, no structural injunction has actually been entered here. The Court can await findings of a violation and a concrete remedy rather than decide the permissible scope of hypothetical systemwide relief on a threshold dismissal. The first question principally alleges conflict with Supreme Court principles; the petition's developed inter-circuit conflict is on the second question. That narrower procedural issue also has qualifications: several cited cases predate Rule 23(f), the Ninth Circuit's approach may be discretionary, and certification can be revisited below. Those considerations keep a potentially attractive error-correction case below even odds. The absent opposition is a significant uncertainty, not a reason to resolve those qualifications in the State's favor.
