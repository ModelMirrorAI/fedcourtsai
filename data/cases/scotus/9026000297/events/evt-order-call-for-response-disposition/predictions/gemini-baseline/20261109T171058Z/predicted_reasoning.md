## 2. Call for the Views of the Solicitor General (`cvsg-increment`: 0.02)

No CVSG is currently on the docket, and the Court is very unlikely to invite the Solicitor General's views (`0.02`). Although the underlying complaint includes claims under the Americans with Disabilities Act and the Rehabilitation Act alongside Fourteenth Amendment Due Process claims, the two questions presented concern (1) Article III standing and federal equitable authority to oversee a state foster-care agency and (2) appellate review of a merged class-certification order on a conditional cross-appeal under 28 U.S.C. § 1291 and Federal Rule of Civil Procedure 23(f). Neither question turns on the administration of a federal regulatory scheme or implicates distinct federal programmatic interests that typically prompt a CVSG.

## 3. Question Presented Selection (Conditional on Grant)

If the Court grants plenary review, it is more likely to grant on **Question 2 alone** (whether Rule 23(f) bars an appellate court from reviewing a class-certification order that merged into a final judgment when the court of appeals reverses that judgment on the main appeal) than on Question 1. Question 2 is the sole issue on which the Fourth Circuit panel divided (with Judge Rushing dissenting at length) and presents a concrete procedural question regarding § 1291 merger, conditional cross-appeals, and the nonjurisdictional status of Rule 23(f) under *Nutraceutical Corp. v. Lambert*, 586 U.S. 188 (2019). By contrast, Question 1 asks the Court to adjudicate the permissible scope of structural injunctions before any trial, liability determination, or remedial decree exists, on appeal from a *sua sponte* standing dismissal that all three panel judges agreed was erroneous.

If the Court were to reach the merits of Question 2, it would likely hold that when a final judgment is entered under § 1291, prior interlocutory class-certification orders merge into that judgment, Rule 23(f)'s 14-day permissive appeal window does not strip the court of appeals of jurisdiction over a conditional cross-appeal upon reversal of the dismissal, and the court of appeals has discretion whether to address class certification or remand it to the district court.

## 4. Summary Disposition vs. Plenary Review (`summary-disposition-route`: 0.20)

Conditional on the Court granting the petition, there is roughly a **20% probability** that the Court disposes of the case in the certiorari order itself (via a GVR or a per curiam vacatur in part on Question 2) rather than setting the case for full merits briefing and oral argument. Because the Fourth Circuit majority raised Rule 23(f) *sua sponte* as a bar to considering West Virginia's conditional cross-appeal—without briefing from the parties and in tension with *Nutraceutical Corp. v. Lambert*—the Court could conceivably vacate the cross-appeal portion of the judgment and remand for the Fourth Circuit to consider whether to exercise its discretion to review class certification. Plenary review remains the likelier route (`0.80` conditional on a grant) if four Justices vote to grant.
