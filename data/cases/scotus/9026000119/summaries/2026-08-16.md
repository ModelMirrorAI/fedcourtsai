## In brief

Guam's Attorney General asks the Supreme Court to review the dismissal of a corruption appeal after the territory's high court disqualified his entire office from continuing to prosecute it.

## What happened

Guam prosecutors indicted five officials connected to a public transit agency on theft and misconduct charges. A trial court dismissed the case after disqualifying the Attorney General's office over a conflict of interest, finding the office had also given legal advice to the transit agency on the same issue without an effective screen between divisions. The Attorney General appealed to the Supreme Court of Guam, which likewise disqualified the office from handling the appeal unless conflict-free substitute counsel appeared within 30 days. When no substitute appeared, that court dismissed the appeal. The Attorney General, representing the People of Guam, now petitions for review of that dismissal.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked whether a rule against removing an entire government law office from a criminal case, which federal courts have applied to U.S. Attorney's offices, should also apply to state and territorial courts, so that territorial courts cannot dismiss otherwise valid prosecutions or appeals by disqualifying an elected prosecutor's whole office without adequate notice or a path to replacement counsel.

## Where it stands

The petition was filed July 20, 2026, after the Court granted an extension of time to file it. The respondents' response is due August 26, 2026, and the petition has not yet been distributed for a conference.

## What each outcome would mean

Guam, through its Attorney General, is asking the Court to take up the case. If the Court grants the petition, it would hear full arguments and decide whether the disqualification and dismissal violated constitutional limits, with no outcome yet decided. If the Court denies the petition, the Supreme Court of Guam's dismissal of the appeal stands, leaving the underlying criminal case against the officials dismissed and the disqualification of the Attorney General's office in place. A denial would not mean the Court agrees the lower court was right; it would simply leave that ruling undisturbed.
