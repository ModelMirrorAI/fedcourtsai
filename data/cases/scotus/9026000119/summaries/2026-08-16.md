## What happened

Guam prosecutors indicted five officials of the Guam Regional Transit Authority, including Richard Y. Ybanez, on theft, misconduct, and related charges. A trial court disqualified the entire Office of the Attorney General of Guam from the case, finding a conflict of interest because part of the office had earlier advised the transit authority on the same issue, and no effective conflict wall was in place. The court then dismissed the case with prejudice. The Attorney General appealed, but the Supreme Court of Guam also disqualified the office from handling the appeal unless conflict-free substitute counsel appeared within 30 days. When no substitute counsel came forward, the appellate court dismissed the appeal. Guam, as petitioner, now asks the Supreme Court to review that dismissal.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked whether a legal rule that discourages courts from removing an entire government prosecutor's office from a case should also apply to state and territorial courts. Guam argues this rule, already followed in federal cases, should limit when local courts can disqualify an elected attorney general's whole office and dismiss criminal cases or appeals as a result, saying doing so without adequate notice or a path to substitute counsel violates constitutional rights to due process and fair treatment.

## Where it stands

The petition for certiorari, the formal request that the Court agree to hear the case, was filed on July 20, 2026, after Guam received an extension of time to file. The respondents' response is due August 26, 2026, and the Court has not yet acted on the petition.
