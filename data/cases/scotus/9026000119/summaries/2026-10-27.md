## In brief

Guam's government asks the Supreme Court to review a Guam court ruling that disqualified its Attorney General's office and dismissed a corruption case against five officials.

## What happened

Guam prosecutors indicted five public officials connected to the Guam Regional Transit Authority on theft and misconduct charges. A trial court disqualified the entire Attorney General's office from the case, finding a conflict of interest because one part of the office had previously advised the transit authority on the same issue, and then dismissed the indictment entirely. On appeal, the Supreme Court of Guam likewise disqualified the Attorney General's office, gave it thirty days to find substitute counsel, and when no substitute appeared, dismissed the appeal. Guam now asks the U.S. Supreme Court to step in.

## What the Court is being asked

Guam asks the Court to decide whether a constitutional rule that disfavors removing an entire government prosecutor's office from a case should also apply to state and territorial courts, so that disqualifying an entire Attorney General's office does not lead to the unconstitutional dismissal of criminal cases.

## Where it stands

The respondent Anthony P. Chargualaf has waived his right to respond. The petition was distributed for the Court's November 6, 2026 conference, where the justices will decide whether to take the case; the docket does not yet show a decision.

## What each outcome would mean

Guam, as petitioner, is asking the Court to take up the case and overturn the Guam courts' rulings. If the Court grants review, it would hear the case in full and decide the constitutional question, without yet deciding who wins. If it denies review, the Guam Supreme Court's dismissal of the appeal and the underlying case stands, leaving the disqualification of Guam's Attorney General's office and the end of the prosecution against Ybanez, Badar Cruz, Sablan, Susuico, and Chargualaf in place.
