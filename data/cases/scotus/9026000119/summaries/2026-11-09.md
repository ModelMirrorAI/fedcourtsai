## In brief

Guam's government asked the Court to review a Guam court ruling that dismissed a corruption appeal after disqualifying the Attorney General's office; the Court denied review.

## What happened

Guam prosecutors indicted several officials of a public transit agency on corruption-related charges. A trial court disqualified the entire Attorney General's office from the case, finding a conflict of interest because one office division had advised the transit agency while another prosecuted it, and dismissed the indictment. The Attorney General, representing the People of Guam, appealed to the Supreme Court of Guam. That court likewise disqualified the Attorney General's office from handling the appeal unless conflict-free substitute counsel appeared within 30 days. When no substitute counsel appeared, the Guam Supreme Court dismissed the appeal entirely.

## What the Court is being asked

The petition asks the Court to say that courts may not summarily disqualify an entire Attorney General's office that holds exclusive or shared authority to prosecute crimes, and to apply that limit to state and territorial courts, so that criminal cases are not thrown out as a result of such disqualifications.

## Where it stands

One respondent, Anthony P. Chargualaf, waived his right to respond to the petition. The case was distributed for the Court's November 6, 2026 conference, and on November 9, 2026, the petition was denied.

## What each outcome would mean

Guam, acting for the People of Guam, asked the Court to take up the case. With the petition denied, the Supreme Court of Guam's dismissal of the appeal stands, meaning the disqualification of the Attorney General's office and the dismissal of the underlying criminal case against Ybanez, Cruz, Sablan, Susuico, and Chargualaf remain final. Had the Court granted the petition instead, it would have agreed to hear full arguments on the disqualification question, without yet deciding who was right.
