## In brief

Guam's government asks the Supreme Court to revive a corruption case that was dismissed after Guam's courts disqualified the entire Attorney General's office.

## What happened

Guam prosecutors indicted five officials tied to the Guam Regional Transit Authority on theft and misconduct charges. A trial court disqualified the entire Attorney General's office, finding a conflict of interest because one office division had advised the transit agency on the same issue another division was prosecuting, and then dismissed the case entirely. Guam's Attorney General appealed, but the Supreme Court of Guam also disqualified the office from handling the appeal and gave it 30 days to find conflict-free substitute counsel. When no substitute appeared, that court dismissed the appeal. Guam's government, as petitioner, now asks the U.S. Supreme Court to review that dismissal.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked whether a legal principle that discourages removing an entire government prosecutor's office from a case, which the petition says applies to federal prosecutors, should also apply to state and territorial courts, so that disqualifying an entire Attorney General's office does not result in unconstitutional dismissal of criminal cases.

## Where it stands

The petition was filed in July 2026. One respondent, Anthony Chargualaf, waived his right to respond. The case was distributed for the justices' November 6, 2026 conference, and the docket does not yet show any action taken at that conference.

## What each outcome would mean

Guam's government, as petitioner, is asking the Court to hear the case. If the Court grants review, it will decide the question in full after further briefing and argument, without yet deciding who is right. If the Court denies review, the Supreme Court of Guam's dismissal of the appeal stands, leaving the underlying criminal case against the five officials dismissed and the disqualification of the Attorney General's office in place.
