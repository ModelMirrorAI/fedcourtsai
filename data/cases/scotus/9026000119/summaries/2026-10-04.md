## In brief

Guam's government asks the Supreme Court to review a territorial court ruling that disqualified its Attorney General's office and dismissed a corruption appeal as a result.

## What happened

Guam prosecutors indicted several Guam Regional Transit Authority officials, including Richard Ybanez and others, on corruption-related charges. A trial court disqualified the entire Attorney General's office from the case, finding it had a conflict of interest because one of its divisions had given legal advice to the transit authority while another prosecuted its officials. The court then dismissed the case. On appeal, the Supreme Court of Guam also disqualified the Attorney General's office, ruled that the appeal would be dismissed unless conflict-free substitute counsel appeared within thirty days, and later dismissed the appeal when no substitute counsel appeared. Guam, through its Attorney General, now asks the U.S. Supreme Court to review that dismissal.

## What the Court is being asked

Guam asks the Court to decide whether a legal rule that discourages courts from disqualifying an entire prosecutor's office should also apply to state and territorial courts, so that dismissing a criminal prosecution or appeal over such a disqualification would be unconstitutional.

## Where it stands

Guam filed its petition on July 20, 2026. One respondent, Anthony Chargualaf, has waived his right to respond. The docket shows no further action since then.

## What each outcome would mean

Guam, the petitioner, is asking the Court to take up the case. If the Court grants the petition, it would hear full arguments and decide whether the disqualification rule applies to Guam's courts, without deciding in advance who wins. If the Court denies the petition, the Supreme Court of Guam's dismissal of the appeal stands, leaving the disqualification of Guam's Attorney General and the end of this prosecution in place.
