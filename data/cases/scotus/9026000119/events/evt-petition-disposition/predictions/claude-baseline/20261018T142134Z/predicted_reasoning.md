# Forecast — Guam v. Ybanez, No. 26-119

**Disposition: denied** at the conference of November 6, 2026 (the first distribution).

- **Relists.** The docket shows one distribution (for the 11/6/2026 conference) and no relist. I expect no further distribution. The more likely deviation from a clean denial is not a relist. It is a call for a response: only respondent Chargualaf has filed a waiver, and the Court sometimes asks for a response before it would act on a government petitioner's request. Even if the Court does call for a response, I expect a denial after it.
- **CVSG.** None. This is a territorial criminal prosecution in which the government of Guam is itself the petitioner. No federal regulatory interest calls for the Solicitor General's views.
- **Question presented, if granted.** The single QP asks whether federal constitutional limits on disqualifying an entire prosecuting office bind territorial courts. A grant would almost certainly be reformulated, most likely narrowed to whether the Supreme Court of Guam's disqualification and dismissal of the People's appeal without notice violated due process as applied to Guam through the Organic Act (48 U.S.C. § 1421b(u)).
- **Summary route.** If the Court grants at all, a summary disposition (a per curiam vacatur on a notice or due-process ground) is a real share of that small grant mass. No intervening decision supports a GVR.
- **Separate writing.** I expect none on denial.
