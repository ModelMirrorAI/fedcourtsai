# Reasoning

This is a forward-mode prediction for `Guam v. Richard Y. Ybanez, et al.`, a petition for certiorari at the distribution moment.

## Base Rates and Adjustments
The petition falls into the `state` salience band under `sal-v4`. According to the `metrics/statpack.md` per-Term base rates for the `state` band (pooling Terms up to OT 2025), the bracketed `reached` grant rate is approximately 23.5% ($99 / 421$). However, several case-specific factors strongly downward-adjust this baseline:

1.  **Weak Federal Question:** The petitioner (the People of Guam, represented by the Attorney General) asks whether federal constitutional rules disfavoring vicarious disqualification of a prosecutor's office apply to states and territories. However, state and territorial governments generally do not have Fourteenth Amendment due process rights against their own courts, making this primarily an internal separation-of-powers issue disguised as a federal question.
2.  **Lack of National Importance:** The dispute involves an idiosyncratic disqualification order by the Supreme Court of Guam. It lacks the broader national implications or clear circuit split necessary to command Supreme Court review.
3.  **Absence of Amici and BIO Waiver:** There are no amicus briefs supporting the petition, and the respondent has explicitly waived the right to respond. A waiver of response typically signals that the respondent views the petition as exceedingly weak and not worth defending unless the Court requests a response.

## Conclusion
Given the narrow, localized nature of the dispute, the structural weakness of the federal constitutional claim, and the lack of external interest (no amici, waived response), a grant is highly improbable. I estimate the likelihood of a grant at 1%.
