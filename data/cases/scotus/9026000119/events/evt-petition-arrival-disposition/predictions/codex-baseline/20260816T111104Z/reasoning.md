# Rationale

I assign a 2.5% probability to any grant-family disposition and predict denial. This is a forward-mode, cert-stage arrival cell. The provisioned snapshot dated August 16, 2026 shows a paid petition from the Supreme Court of Guam, zero distributions, and no CVSG. The 98-page petition and its extracted question presented were available in full and untruncated; no brief in opposition was yet provisioned.

The applicable arrival anchor is the weakest salience band's bracketed `reached` rate, which represents the whole paid scored segment at docketing. Pooling the rendered Terms strictly before OT2026 gives 863 grant-family dispositions among 13,163 denial-reweighted resolved paid petitions, or 6.556%. I did not use the much higher `state` risk-set rate: the arrival contract reserves a distinct class adjustment for federal petitioners, and Guam is not classified as a federal petitioner. I also did not use the terminal relist-zero cut, because zero distributions is the definition of this forecasting moment rather than evidence that the petition will end without distribution.

Several considerations move the forecast below 6.556%. The petition does not identify a split among state or territorial high courts. Its principal authority against office-wide disqualification is lower-federal-court precedent concerning federal prosecutors, and its question asks this Court to extend that principle rather than resolve conflicting authority. The Guam trial court found a concrete same-matter conflict: the attorney general's office advised the agency about the qualification issue, later prosecuted related conduct, disclosed communications, and had no effective screen. The Guam Supreme Court did not decide the merits of disqualification; it dismissed only after the petitioner failed to secure conflict-free substitute counsel. Those features create serious passed-upon, preservation, independent-ground, and redressability problems. The petition's theory that the governmental entity itself holds Fourteenth Amendment and petition rights is also weakly supported by the authorities it cites.

The counterweights are the territorial government as petitioner, the institutional severity of disqualifying an entire public-prosecution office, and the claimed recurrence in another Guam matter. Those factors keep the estimate above a near-zero frivolous-petition rate but do not overcome the vehicle defects.

The `relist-increment` probability is 0.97 because the frozen count is zero: at this arrival moment the claim first asks whether the docket will reach any distribution, not whether it will survive an already-held conference. I expect one ordinary distribution and no later relist. The 3% CVSG estimate reflects the federal interest in Guam's Organic Act but the predominantly local, record-bound controversy. Conditional on a grant, I assign a 10% summary-route probability, well below the prior-Term paid baseline of 300 cert-order dispositions among 863 grants (34.762%), because no intervening authority supports a GVR and the requested doctrinal extension would ordinarily require plenary review. I assign 1.5% to a dissent or statement conditional on denial because the issue lacks an evident nationwide conflict or salient ideological alignment.

The sanctioned CourtListener searches for broader disqualification precedent returned HTTP 429 rate-limit errors, so no live results informed the forecast. I therefore relied on the provisioned record and the committed statpack. The missing opposition brief and unavailable live search make the legal assessment less complete, but neither prevents a conservative forecast.
