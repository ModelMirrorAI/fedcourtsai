# Forecast: Guam v. Ybanez, No. 26-119 (petition disposition, forecast at arrival)

The docket shows **zero distributions** — the arrival state this cell forecasts
from. The petition was docketed July 27, 2026; the response is due August 26,
2026.

**Disposition.** The Court denies certiorari, most likely in October or
November 2026. Respondents (indicted former Guam officials, now unrepresented
on this docket) will either waive a response or file a short brief in
opposition; I do not expect the Court to request a response on its own if one
is waived, though a request is possible given a government petitioner.

**Distribution and relists.** The petition will be distributed at least once —
expect distribution for the late-September 2026 long conference or the first
regular October conference. I expect **no relist**: one conference, then a
denial on the next order list. The QP presents no circuit or state-court
conflict, and the cert-pool memo will flag both that gap and the doctrinal
problem with a government invoking Fourteenth Amendment rights as "the People."

**CVSG.** None. The federal government's interest in Organic Act housekeeping
is real but thin, the petition does not implicate a federal program or statute
the Solicitor General administers, and CVSGs at this posture are rare
(~1% of the paid segment). The Court will not invite the SG's views.

**Which question, if taken.** If the Court granted, it would not take the QP
as written — it is argumentative ("should be extended") and framed as a request
to create law rather than resolve a conflict. It would reformulate to something
like: whether the Due Process Clause (as extended to Guam by the Organic Act)
constrains a territorial court's summary disqualification of an entire elected
prosecuting office without notice or an opportunity to be heard. (Registered
context-only; not graded.)

**Summary disposition.** Unlikely. No intervening decision of this Court bears
on prosecutorial disqualification, so the GVR route is essentially unavailable;
if the Court acted at all, a plenary grant — or, less likely, a per curiam
reversal on the stark no-notice facts — is the shape. That conditional
judgment is the `summary-disposition-route` claim (0.12 | grant).

**Dissent from denial.** None expected. A short statement respecting denial
from a Justice interested in territorial questions or in judicial control over
elected prosecutors is conceivable but improbable (~4% conditional on denial).
