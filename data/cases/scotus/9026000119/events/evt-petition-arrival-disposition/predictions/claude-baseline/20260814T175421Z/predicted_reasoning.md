# Forecast — Guam v. Ybanez, No. 26-119 (petition disposition, forecast at arrival)

The docket shows **zero distributions** — the arrival state this cell forecasts
from. The petition was docketed July 27, 2026, with the response due August 26,
2026. Everything below is a claim about what accrues from that state.

**Disposition: denied, most likely without comment.** The Court will deny this
petition. Respondents — five individual defendants whose prosecution was
dismissed with prejudice — will most likely waive a response (they have nothing
to gain from engaging), and I do not expect the Court to call for one: the
petition's due-process theory belongs to a government entity claiming
Fourteenth Amendment rights against its own courts, a framing the cert pool
will treat as a threshold defect rather than a live question.

**Relists: distributed once, not relisted.** The petition will be distributed
for the long conference (late September 2026) or one of the first October
conferences, and denied on its first conference or at most its second — the
order should issue in October or early November 2026. I forecast **one
distribution and no relist** as the modal path; the probability that at least
one distribution accrues from the current zero-distribution state is near
certainty (0.96 — the residual is withdrawal or a procedural termination
before conference, both unlikely with a determined AG petitioner in a criminal
posture that cannot settle).

**No CVSG (0.03).** Guam's AG office is a creature of the Organic Act, so the
United States has a genuine structural interest, and that is the one route to
a CVSG here. But the Court calls for the SG's views on petitions it is already
taking seriously, almost always with a split or a substantial federal-statutory
question presented cleanly. This petition presents neither — it concedes the
federal courts are uniform and asks for an "extension" to state and territorial
courts — so I expect no call.

**If granted, the Court reformulates the QP.** The QP as written ("whether
settled United States constitutional jurisprudence ... should be extended to
state and territorial courts") is a request for error correction dressed as a
question, and the Court would not take it in that form. A grant, if one came,
would rest on a rewritten question — most plausibly whether a court that
disqualifies the sole officer empowered to prosecute, then dismisses the
prosecution's appeal for want of the substitute counsel no one was directed to
appoint, denies due process — or on the Organic Act separation-of-powers
question the petition gestures at but does not squarely present.

**No summary disposition.** A GVR requires an intervening decision and none
exists. A summary reversal requires a lower court defying settled Supreme
Court precedent; the petition's own framing concedes there is no Supreme Court
precedent on point to defy. Neither route is live.

**No separate writing expected.** A denial should come without dissent or
statement. The Justices who write on territorial questions (Gorsuch and
Sotomayor on the Insular Cases) have no hook here — the petition does not
challenge the territorial framework, it invokes it. P(any separate writing
respecting denial) ≈ 0.05.
