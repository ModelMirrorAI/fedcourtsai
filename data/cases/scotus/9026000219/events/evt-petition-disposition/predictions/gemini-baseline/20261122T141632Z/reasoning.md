### Downward signals that outweigh the CFR once the Brief in Opposition is filed
Comparing `petition.txt` against the CAAF and AFCCA opinions in `appendix.txt` reveals fatal vehicle and merits defects that the Solicitor General's forthcoming Brief in Opposition is virtually certain to spotlight:

1. **Severe waiver / non-preservation and express trial concession:** Petitioner's Question Presented—whether *Leon*'s good-faith exception applies when a commander has no legal training and received advice from the same prosecutor who advised law enforcement—was **neither pressed nor passed upon below**. Worse, CAAF explicitly recorded (App. 17a) that *"In his motion to suppress, Appellant conceded the commander was competent to issue the search authorization"* under `Mil. R. Evid. 315(d)` and `311(c)(3)(A)`, and noted (App. 20a) the *"absence of an allegation that the [commander] abandoned his detached and neutral role."* AFCCA likewise enforced strict waiver under `Mil. R. Evid. 311` and *United States v. Perkins*, 78 M.J. 381, 390 (C.A.A.F. 2019), for suppression grounds not raised with particularity at trial (App. 47a–48a). Even Chief Judge Ohlson's lone dissent at CAAF (App. 22a–28a) did not question non-lawyer commander authority or dual judge-advocate advice; he dissented solely on the fact-bound ground that the 7.5-month date window lacked nexus to the phones.
2. **Absence of any lower-court conflict and settled precedent under *Shadwick*:** The petition alleges no split among federal circuits or state high courts. On the "no legal training" prong, *Shadwick v. City of Tampa*, 407 U.S. 345, 350 (1972) (cited by AFCCA at App. 44a), settled more than half a century ago that Fourth Amendment issuing magistrates need not be lawyers or judges. On the "same prosecutor" prong, the petition relies heavily on testimony surrounding the *first* search authorization (August 17, 2022), whereas CAAF reviewed only the *second* search authorization (August 31, 2022), sought by an independent Headquarters OSI Internal Affairs agent from Quantico who consulted base legal counsel on probable cause, and reviewed by Col. 3. **Unresolved alternative grounds and narrow factual stakes:** Both AFCCA and CAAF assumed *arguendo* a lack of probable cause without disturbing the military judge's actual probable-cause ruling, and the military judge also denied suppression on an independent cost-benefit balancing ground under `Mil. R. Evid. 311(a)(3)` (App. 10a, 42a–43a) that CAAF's majority did not reach. Moreover, probable cause for the August 12, 2022 unlawful-entry and assault evidence on the phone was undisputed even by the dissent ("dead to rights," App. 22a), leaving only a single specification from two days earlier (an August 10, 2022 gym-shower recording) affected by pre-August 12 phone data.
