# Why I put the chance of review at 3.5%

The September 14 request for a response is the strongest sign of interest. The United States had waived its response on August 31, and the Clerk distributed the petition on September 2 for the September 28 conference. The Court asked to hear from the government before that conference. Two extensions moved the response deadline to December 7. The November 22 snapshot records one distribution, zero amicus briefs and no brief in opposition. The government's position on review remains unknown. Once the requested response is in or its time expires, the petition should go back on a conference list. That accounts for my 99.5% chance of another distribution even though I expect denial.

The frozen OT2026 private-petitioner baseline in `record/context.json` gives a 5.18% grant rate among prior paid petitions that reached this band. The corresponding rate for petitions that ended with zero amicus briefs is 2.69% (10,350 resolved). Johnson's count can still rise. For scale, the modern discretionary-cert table in `metrics/statpack.md` records 1,284 grants or grant-vacate-remand orders among 45,245 resolved petitions, about 2.8%. Its paid-segment relist table ranges from 1.8% granted or remanded with zero terminal relists (10,311 resolved) to 13.6% with one (2,546 resolved). A second distribution to circulate a requested brief would count in that statistic without showing that the Justices had twice considered a fully briefed petition. The response request and dissent below put my estimate above the zero-amicus reference. The obstacles to deciding the legal question keep it below the 5.18% band rate.

Johnson asks whether investigators can reasonably rely on a search approved by a commander without legal training who took advice from the same prosecutor who advised them. His petition identifies the practice in *Perkins* and *Lattin*. Chief Judge Ohlson's dissent gives a concrete reason for scrutiny. Even if two incidents months apart justified searching for pictures taken on those two dates, what justified searching the intervening months? The phone evidence mattered to the indecent-recording conviction. A ruling on the lawyer's dual role would reach military investigations across the services. The civilian cases cited in the petition discuss police seeking legal advice. Johnson identifies no conflicting appellate holding on the same lawyer advising the person who authorizes the search. *Shadwick* also upheld municipal court clerks' authority to issue certain arrest warrants, focusing on independence and capacity to assess probable cause. The shared adviser therefore matters more to the proposed question than the commander's lack of a law degree. (Questions presented, petition pp. 6–12, appendix pp. 22a–27a, and *Shadwick v. City of Tampa*, 407 U.S. 345, 350–52 (1972).)

The case is a difficult vehicle for that question. The Court of Appeals for the Armed Forces granted review on the connection between possession of two phones and the breadth of the search. Its four-judge majority assumed a lack of probable cause and upheld the officers' reliance on this authorization. Johnson had conceded the commander's competence under the military rule. The commander had received some instruction, initially declined to sign until inaccuracies were corrected, and heard evidence about a similar incident at another base. Those facts complicate the petition's premise that prosecutorial advice displaced independent judgment. The trial judge also found that exclusion would yield too little deterrence under Military Rule of Evidence 311(a)(3). A victory on good faith alone could leave the judgment standing. (Appendix pp. 3a–7a, 10a–11a, 16a–21a.)

I put the chance of a call for the Solicitor General's views at zero because the Solicitor General already represents the respondent whose brief the Court requested. The paid-segment CVSG table has only 166 resolved invitations, against 13,674 resolved petitions without one. Conditional on review, I put 10% on disposition in the grant order. The competing readings of the record and the absence of an identified intervening decision favor briefing and argument. Conditional on denial, I put 2.5% on a noted dissent or statement. Ohlson's dissent supplies a specific concern a Justice could raise, but it turns chiefly on the reach of this phone search and gives little basis to predict any particular author.
