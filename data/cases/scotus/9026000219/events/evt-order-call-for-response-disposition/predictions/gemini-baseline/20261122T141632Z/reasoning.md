## Case-Specific Adjustments from the Provisioned Record

### 1. Upward signal from the September 14, 2026 Call for a Response and Chief Judge Ohlson's dissent
After the Solicitor General waived a response on August 31, 2026 and the petition was distributed on September 2 for the September 28 Long Conference, the Court requested a response on September 14 (due October 14, 2026). Although no committed statpack table conditions directly on `response-requested`, a call for a response lifts a waived petition out of the zero-interest waiver pool into active chambers review (where paid CFR petitions generically resolve in the grant family roughly 7–11% of the time). It is easy to see what caught a chambers' eye on a petition-only read:
- CAAF issued a published 4–1 opinion accompanied by a sharp dissent from Chief Judge Ohlson (`appendix.txt`, Pet. App. 21a–28a), opening with his earlier dissent in *United States v. Lattin*, 83 M.J. 192, 199 (C.A.A.F. 2023), and condemning AFOSI's 7.5-month iPhone search as `"a classic and straightforward example of a blatantly unconstitutional fishing expedition"` (Pet. App. 22a).
- Cell-phone search overbreadth (*Riley v. California*, 573 U.S. 373 (2014)) combined with the petition's framing — a commander with no legal training beyond a new-commander PowerPoint approving a broad digital search on the advice of the same prosecutor (`Captain E.M.`) who assisted AFOSI and later allegedly testified (`Trial Tr. 79`) that there was no reason to believe the phones were used in an offense — looks doctrinally arresting before any opposition is filed.

### 2. Strong downward adjustments from vehicle, preservation, and doctrinal review of `appendix.txt`
Close inspection of both opinions in `appendix.txt` (`1a–84a`) reveals fatal vehicle flaws that were not foregrounded in the Petition's Question Presented and push `P(grant)` back down to **`0.04`**:
- **Severe non-presentation / concession below:** CAAF granted review solely on a narrow, case-specific evidentiary question: `"[w]hether it was error to rely on Appellant's incidental possession of two phones at the time of arrest as the basis [for the second search authorization] to search the phones' contents going back more than eight months"` (Pet. App. 3a, 11a). Even more damaging to vehicle quality, CAAF explicitly stated that `"The parties do not dispute the first requirement of the good faith exception was satisfied: the search authorization was issued by 'an individual competent to issue the authorization under Mil. R. Evid. 315(d).' M.R.E. 311(c)(3)(A). In his motion to suppress, Appellant conceded the commander was competent to issue the search authorization"` (Pet. App. 17a), and noted `"the absence of an allegation that the [commander] abandoned his detached and neutral role"` on structural grounds (Pet. App. 20a). Neither AFCCA (which enforced forfeiture under *United States v. Perkins*, 78 M.J. 381, 390 (C.A.A.F. 2019), against an unpreserved M.R.E. 315(d) control argument, Pet. App. 47a–48a) nor CAAF — nor even Chief Judge Ohlson's dissent — passed upon whether non-lawyer commander status or dual-hatted judge-advocate advice categorically defeats *Leon* good faith.
- **No circuit split and uphill doctrinal baseline:** CAAF exercises exclusive court-martial appellate jurisdiction under 10 U.S.C. § 867 and 28 U.S.C. § 1259(3), so there is no inter-circuit split. Substantively, *Shadwick v. City of Tampa*, 407 U.S. 345, 350 (1972), rejected the claim that Fourth Amendment warrant issuers must be lawyers or judges; *Messerschmidt v. Millender*, 565 U.S. 535, 553–54 (2012), held that pre-submission prosecutorial approval bolsters rather than undermines *Leon* objective reasonableness; and the Court accords substantial deference to presidentially promulgated Military Rules of Evidence under 10 U.S.C. § 836. Note also that *Lattin* itself — the 2023 3–2 CAAF precedent Petitioner cites (Pet. 12) involving the same Chief Judge Ohlson dissent and dual-hatted SJA advice — did not result in a Supreme Court grant.
- **Messy factual posture and independent alternative holdings:** Unlike a pure "rubber stamp" fact pattern, the military judge found (and both appellate courts credited) that Colonel J.F. **initially refused** to sign the second search authorization until inaccuracies on the form were corrected (Pet. App. 7a, 37a). Moreover, the military judge affirmatively upheld actual probable cause (which AFCCA and CAAF bypassed only by assuming *arguendo* its absence, Pet. App. 4a, 16a, 48a) and made an independent deterrence finding under Mil. R. Evid. 311(a)(3), while Petitioner did not challenge the first search authorization before CAAF (Pet. App. 8a n.6).

Balancing the genuine positive signal of a pre-Long-Conference Call for a Response + CAAF Chief Judge dissent against an unpreserved/conceded Question Presented, zero split, and independent trial-level grounds yields `0.04` (`~3.3%` plenary grant + `~0.7%` GVR/summary action), modestly below the `5.2%` `baseline` `[reached]` anchor and roughly `1.5x–2x` the `0`-amici (`2.7%`) and CAAF (`2.0%`) floors.
