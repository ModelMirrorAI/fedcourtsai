# Predicted Court Action — *Jaelen M. Johnson v. United States* (No. 26-219)

## Disposition and Conference Trajectory

The petition will be **denied**.

The docket as of September 15, 2026 records **one** distribution — on September 2, 2026, for the September 28, 2026 Conference — followed by the Court's September 14, 2026 call for a response (due October 14, 2026), which removed the petition from the September 28 Conference list. Once the Solicitor General files the Brief in Opposition, the Clerk will redistribute the petition for a subsequent Conference, adding at least one more distinct conference distribution beyond the single distribution currently on the docket. At that first post-response Conference, the Court will deny certiorari without a post-opposition relist (with only a small chance of one additional relist if a Justice circulates a brief separate writing).

Once the government's opposition is before the Conference, the vehicle defects in the record below will foreclose review:

1. **Non-presentation and concession below.** The petition's categorical Question Presented — whether *United States v. Leon*, 468 U.S. 897 (1984), applies to search authorizations issued by non-lawyer military commanders under Mil. R. Evid. 311(c)(3) and 315(d) who receive probable-cause advice from the same judge advocate who advised law enforcement — was neither within the narrow factual issue granted by the U.S. Court of Appeals for the Armed Forces (CAAF) nor passed upon by either military appellate court. On the contrary, Petitioner conceded at trial that the commander was competent to issue the authorization under Mil. R. Evid. 315(d) and 311(c)(3)(A), and litigated only fact-bound nexus and date-range objections below.
2. **Absence of conflict and settled Fourth Amendment baselines.** No split of authority exists, *Shadwick v. City of Tampa*, 407 U.S. 345 (1972), permits non-lawyer officials to issue warrants when neutral and detached, and *Messerschmidt v. Millender*, 565 U.S. 535 (2012), treats pre-submission prosecutorial review as supporting rather than defeating objective good faith.
3. **Alternative factual and legal grounds below.** The military judge found actual probable cause (which the appellate courts bypassed only *arguendo*), found independent admissibility under Mil. R. Evid. 311(a)(3)'s deterrence balancing, and credited testimony that the commander initially refused to sign the second search authorization until inaccuracies were corrected.

## Call for the Views of the Solicitor General (CVSG)

The Court will **not** issue a CVSG. The United States is the named respondent represented by the Solicitor General, who will file a party response directly pursuant to the September 14, 2026 request.

## Question Presented and Conditional Merits Scope

If the Court were to grant plenary review despite the preservation bar, it would narrow the Question Presented away from a facial challenge to non-lawyer commander search authority under *Shadwick* and confine review to whether objective good-faith reliance under *Leon* and Mil. R. Evid. 311(c)(3) is available when the same prosecution-aligned judge advocate both assists investigators in drafting a search-authorization request and advises the issuing commander on probable cause. If reached on the merits, the Court would likely affirm or resolve the case narrowly on the military record rather than categorically invalidating Mil. R. Evid. 311(c)(3)(A)'s application to commander authorizations.

## Plenary Review vs. Summary Disposition Route

Conditional on a grant, **plenary review** is substantially likelier than a summary disposition in the certiorari order (`18%` conditional share for GVR or summary merits action). No pending Supreme Court merits case presents a related military-search or *Leon* question that would prompt a hold-and-GVR, court-martial finality leaves negligible room for mootness vacatur, and neither a government confession-of-error GVR nor a summary per curiam reversal is likely given two appellate affirmances and the commander's initial rejection of the defective draft authorization.

## Separate Writings on the Certiorari Order

The denial order will most likely issue **without any noted dissent or separate statement**. There is a modest (`6.5%` conditional on denial) chance that the chambers that triggered the September 14 call for a response — most plausibly Justice Sotomayor, Justice Gorsuch, or Justice Jackson — notes a dissent or authors a short statement respecting the denial highlighting Chief Judge Ohlson's warning against overbroad military cell-phone searches and dual-hatted judge-advocate probable-cause advice while recognizing that forfeiture below makes this petition an unsuitable vehicle.
