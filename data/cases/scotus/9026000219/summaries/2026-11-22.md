## In brief

An Air Force servicemember asks the Court to review whether evidence from searches his commander approved should have been used against him at court-martial.

## What happened

Jaelen Johnson, a Senior Airman, was court-martialed after a civilian entered a fellow service member's room while he slept and was found there. Investigators searched Johnson's two phones under authorizations signed by his commander, who had no legal training and was advised by the same prosecutor who had helped investigators prepare the search request. The searches turned up evidence of other conduct, leading to convictions for assault, unlawful entry, and indecent recording. Military appellate courts, including the Court of Appeals for the Armed Forces, assumed without deciding that the searches lacked probable cause but held that investigators relied on the authorizations in good faith, so the evidence could still be used. Johnson now asks the Supreme Court to review that ruling.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked whether it is proper to excuse an otherwise unlawful search from the usual rule against using illegally obtained evidence when the official who approved the search was a military commander with no legal training, and that commander was advised by the same prosecutor who had already been helping investigators build their search request.

## Where it stands

The petition was distributed for conference, and the Court then asked the government to respond; the government has received two extensions and now has until December 7, 2026 to file its response. The case has not yet been scheduled for a conference to decide whether to grant review.

## What each outcome would mean

Johnson, the petitioner, is asking the Court to take up his case and ultimately overturn the lower military court's ruling. If the Court grants review, it would hear full briefing and argument on whether the good-faith exception applied here, deciding the question itself. If it denies review, the Court of Appeals for the Armed Forces decision upholding Johnson's convictions stands, and the evidence from the phone searches remains part of the case against him. Denial would not mean the Court agrees the lower court was correct, only that its decision remains in place.
