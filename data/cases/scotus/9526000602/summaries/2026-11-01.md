## In brief

Two homeowners ask the Court to pause eviction from their Miami property while an appeal of a denied stay remains pending in a lower appeals court.

## What happened

Micahiel Nichloson and James Buckman are defendants in a foreclosure case brought by U.S. Bank National Association in Florida state court, which was later removed to federal district court. The district court denied their motion to stay enforcement of a state-court writ of possession, ruling it lacked authority to block a state-court eviction and that the applicants had not shown they would be irreparably harmed. The applicants appealed to the Eleventh Circuit and asked that court for an emergency stay while their appeal proceeds. As of this filing, the Eleventh Circuit had not ruled on that request, and the applicants also raised concerns about judges' alleged conflicts of interest.

## What the Court is being asked

The applicants ask a single justice, acting for the Eleventh Circuit, to temporarily block enforcement of the eviction order at their home until the appeals court rules on their own pending stay request, so they are not removed from the property before that happens.

## Where it stands

The application for a stay was submitted to Justice Thomas and docketed on October 28, 2026. The docket shows no ruling on it yet.

## What each outcome would mean

Nichloson and Buckman, the applicants, are asking for temporary relief. If granted, the eviction would be paused while the Eleventh Circuit considers their stay motion, letting them remain in the property for now. If denied, U.S. Bank could proceed with enforcing the writ of possession and the eviction could go forward while the appeal continues. Either way, the underlying foreclosure appeal would continue in the lower courts, and this order would not decide who ultimately wins that case.
