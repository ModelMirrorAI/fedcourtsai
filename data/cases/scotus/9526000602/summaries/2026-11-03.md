## In brief

Homeowners facing eviction asked a justice to pause the sheriff's enforcement of a writ of possession while their appeal continued; the request was denied.

## What happened

Micahiel Nichloson and James Buckman, defendants in a Florida foreclosure case brought by U.S. Bank National Association, faced a writ of possession allowing their eviction from a Miami property. They asked the district court to stay enforcement pending appeal; the court denied the motion, finding it lacked authority to stay a state-court judgment or enjoin state enforcement proceedings, and that the applicants had not shown they would suffer irreparable harm from enforcement of the federal order itself. They then sought a stay from the Eleventh Circuit, which had not ruled. They asked the Supreme Court to step in temporarily.

## What the Court is being asked

The applicants asked a single justice to pause enforcement of the eviction while the appeals court decided their own pending stay request, so they would not be removed from the property before that court acted.

## Where it stands

Justice Thomas, acting as circuit justice for the Eleventh Circuit, denied the stay application on November 3, 2026.

## What each outcome would mean

Nichloson and Buckman, as applicants, asked for temporary relief pausing their eviction while the Eleventh Circuit considered a stay motion already pending there. Granting the application would have paused enforcement of the writ of possession until the appeals court ruled or until a further order. Denying it, which is what happened, leaves the writ of possession in effect, meaning U.S. Bank can proceed with enforcement unless the Eleventh Circuit itself grants a stay. The denial does not resolve the underlying appeal or the foreclosure dispute, which continues in the lower courts.
