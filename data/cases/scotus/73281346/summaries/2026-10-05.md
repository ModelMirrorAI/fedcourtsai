## In brief

Two police officers ask the Court to revive their bid for immunity from a lawsuit over a fatal shooting during an encounter with an unarmed man.

## What happened

Officers Jessica Pitts and William Lewis responded to a call about Thomas Gay, whose father said he was behaving erratically and unarmed. Lewis repeatedly used a taser on Gay, and Pitts then shot and killed him. Gay's estate, represented by Taylor Burke, sued the officers for excessive force. The district court denied the officers' request for qualified immunity, a legal protection that shields officials from lawsuits unless they violated clearly established rights. The Tenth Circuit Court of Appeals agreed, finding genuine factual disputes about what happened and ruling that prior cases had clearly established that this kind of force was unlawful. The officers then asked the Supreme Court to review that decision.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked whether the appeals court wrongly treated a specific legal theory about officers escalating a dangerous situation as a clearly established rule, whether it defined the relevant legal rule too broadly instead of pointing to cases closely matching these facts, and whether it improperly avoided reviewing the officers' legal arguments by calling them factual disputes.

## Where it stands

The case has been distributed for conference multiple times, most recently for October 9, 2026, after full briefing including a response and reply. The docket does not show that the Court has yet acted on the petition.

## What each outcome would mean

Pitts and Lewis, the officers, are asking the Court to take up the case. If the Court grants the petition, it would hear the case fully and decide the legal questions raised, without that step deciding who wins. If the Court denies the petition, the Tenth Circuit's ruling stands, meaning the officers remain without qualified immunity at this stage and Burke's lawsuit over Gay's death can continue in the lower courts.
