## In brief

The Supreme Court declined to review two police officers' bid to escape a lawsuit over the fatal shooting of Thomas Gay.

## What happened

Officers Jessica Pitts and William Lewis responded to a call about Thomas Gay behaving erratically at his father's home. Lewis repeatedly used a taser on Gay, and Pitts then shot and killed him. Taylor Burke, representing Gay's estate, sued the officers for excessive force. The district court denied the officers' request for qualified immunity, a legal protection that shields officials from lawsuits unless they violated clearly established rights. The Tenth Circuit Court of Appeals affirmed, finding disputed facts that, if resolved in Burke's favor, showed the officers violated clearly established Fourth Amendment law. The officers then asked the Supreme Court to take the case.

## What the Court is being asked

The officers asked the Court to decide whether the appeals court wrongly treated certain lower-court rules about escalating police encounters as settled constitutional law, whether it defined the relevant legal rules too broadly rather than tying them to the specific situation the officers faced, and whether it improperly avoided deciding legal questions by labeling them factual disputes.

## Where it stands

The petition was distributed for conference multiple times, with the Court twice asking for additional briefing (a response and a reply). After being rescheduled once, it was considered at a conference on October 9, 2026, and the Court denied the petition on October 13, 2026.

## What each outcome would mean

The officers, Pitts and Lewis, asked the Court to overturn the Tenth Circuit's decision. The Court's denial leaves that decision in place, meaning the lawsuit against the officers continues in the lower courts without qualified immunity shielding them at this stage. The denial does not mean the Court agreed with the Tenth Circuit's reasoning; it simply leaves that ruling undisturbed. Burke's estate may now proceed toward trial on the excessive-force claims.
