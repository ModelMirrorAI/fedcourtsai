## In brief

Two police officers asked the Court to revive their bid for qualified immunity in a lawsuit over a fatal shooting; the Court declined to hear the case.

## What happened

Officers Jessica Pitts and William Lewis responded to a call about Thomas Gay, who was behaving erratically at his father's home. Lewis tased Gay repeatedly, and Pitts then shot and killed him. Gay's estate, represented by Taylor Burke, sued the officers for excessive force. A federal district court found disputed facts that could show the officers used unreasonable force and denied them qualified immunity, a legal protection that shields officials from lawsuits unless they violated clearly established rights. The Tenth Circuit Court of Appeals affirmed, and the full appeals court declined to rehear the case.

## What the Court is being asked

The officers asked the Court to decide whether the appeals court wrongly treated a theory about officers recklessly creating dangerous situations as clearly established law, whether it defined clearly established law too broadly by relying on general principles and cases that differ from this one, and whether it improperly avoided reviewing legal questions by labeling them factual disputes.

## Where it stands

The petition was distributed for conference more than once, and the Court denied it without comment on October 13, 2026.

## What each outcome would mean

The officers, Pitts and Lewis, asked the Court to take up their case. With the petition denied, the Tenth Circuit's ruling stands: the officers are not entitled to qualified immunity at this stage, and the lawsuit over the shooting of Thomas Gay can continue in the lower courts. The denial does not mean the Court agreed the lower court was right; it simply leaves that decision in place without the Court deciding the underlying legal questions itself.
