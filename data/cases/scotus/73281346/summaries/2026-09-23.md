## What happened

Police officers Jessica Pitts and William Lewis were sued by Taylor Burke, representing the estate of Thomas Gay, after Lewis repeatedly tased Gay and Pitts fatally shot him during a 2019 encounter at Gay's father's home. The father had called police because Gay was behaving erratically, but told officers Gay was unarmed. The officers sought qualified immunity, a legal protection shielding officials from lawsuits unless they violated clearly established rights. The district court and the Tenth Circuit Court of Appeals both denied that protection, finding that a jury could conclude the officers used excessive force against someone who was unarmed and posed no threat. The officers now ask the Supreme Court to review that decision.

## What the Court is being asked

The case raises questions about how courts decide whether police conduct violated rights that were clearly spelled out in advance. The officers argue the appeals court set the bar too low by relying on a theory that officers can be held responsible for recklessly escalating a situation, and by using prior cases that they say do not closely enough match the facts here. They also argue the appeals court wrongly treated some of their legal arguments as mere factual disputes it could not review. Burke's estate responds that the officers are just repackaging factual disagreements that the Supreme Court has no authority to reconsider at this stage, and that the appeals court correctly applied existing rules to the specific facts.

## Where it stands

The case has been fully briefed, including a reply from the officers, and was distributed for the Court's conference on September 28, 2026, where the Justices will consider whether to grant the officers' petition for certiorari, the Court's permission to hear the case.
