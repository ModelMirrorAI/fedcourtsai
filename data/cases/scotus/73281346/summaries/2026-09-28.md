## In brief

Two police officers ask the Court to review a ruling that let a fatal shooting lawsuit against them continue, after lower courts found they were not entitled to qualified immunity.

## What happened

Officers Jessica Pitts and William Lewis responded to a call about Thomas Gay, whose father said he was behaving erratically and unarmed but making movements toward his back pocket. Within minutes, Lewis repeatedly tased Gay and Pitts shot and killed him. Gay's estate sued under a federal civil-rights law, claiming excessive force. The district court found disputed facts that could let a jury rule for the estate and denied the officers qualified immunity, a legal protection that shields officials from suits unless they violated clearly established rights. The Tenth Circuit Court of Appeals affirmed, and rehearing was denied.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked whether the appeals court wrongly let the case proceed by defining the officers' legal protection too broadly, by treating a theory that officers can be liable for recklessly escalating a situation as clearly established law, and by treating some of the officers' legal arguments as factual disputes it could not review on this early appeal.

## Where it stands

The petition has been distributed for conference more than once, most recently for September 28, 2026, and the docket shows no decision yet on whether the Court will hear the case.

## What each outcome would mean

The officers, Pitts and Lewis, are asking the Court to take up the case. If the Court grants review, it would hear full arguments and decide the legal questions itself, deciding nothing yet about who wins. If it denies review, the Tenth Circuit's decision stands, meaning the estate's lawsuit against the officers continues in the lower courts. Either outcome leaves the underlying factual disputes about the shooting to be resolved later at trial, not by this decision.
