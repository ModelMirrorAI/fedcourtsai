## In brief

Two police officers ask the Court to revive their bid for immunity from a lawsuit over a fatal shooting the appeals court let proceed to trial.

## What happened

Officers Jessica Pitts and William Lewis responded to a call about Thomas Gay, whose father said he was behaving erratically but was unarmed. Lewis repeatedly used a taser on Gay, and Pitts then shot and killed him. Gay's estate, represented by Taylor Burke, sued the officers for excessive force. The district court and the Tenth Circuit Court of Appeals both ruled that disputed facts, if resolved in the estate's favor, would show the officers violated clearly established constitutional limits on police force, so they denied the officers qualified immunity, a legal protection that shields officials from lawsuits unless they violated clearly established rights.

## What the Court is being asked

The officers argue the appeals court let the lawsuit continue by applying police-conduct rules too broadly instead of pointing to a prior case closely matching these specific facts, and by treating an unsettled legal theory about officers escalating a confrontation as if it were already established law. They also argue the appeals court wrongly treated their legal arguments as mere factual disputes it couldn't review. Burke responds that the officers are really just disputing the facts, which the Court cannot revisit at this stage, and that the lower court's decision was correct.

## Where it stands

The case was distributed for conference multiple times after full briefing, including a rescheduled conference in September 2026, and most recently was distributed for the conference of October 9, 2026. The docket does not yet show any decision on the petition.

## What each outcome would mean

The officers, as petitioners, are asking the Court to take up the case and overturn the ruling against them. If the Court grants the petition, it would hear full arguments and decide whether the officers are entitled to qualified immunity, which would decide nothing by itself. If the Court denies the petition, the Tenth Circuit's ruling stands, and the lawsuit against the officers continues in the lower courts. The officers have also asked the Court to summarily reverse or to send the case back for reconsideration in light of a recent decision, options that would avoid full briefing and argument.
