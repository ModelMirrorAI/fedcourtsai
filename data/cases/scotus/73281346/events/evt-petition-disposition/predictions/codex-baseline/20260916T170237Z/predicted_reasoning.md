# Forecast of the Court's action

The Court will deny the petition, most likely in the opening orders following the September 28, 2026 conference, without a reasoned merits opinion.

The record already shows **two distributions**: April 8 for the April 24 conference, and July 8 for the September 28 conference. The intervening response request and briefing explain why that count need not signify a completed substantive relist. From this state, I predict **no further distribution** before denial; the probability of at least one additional distribution is **24%**. If consideration continues, I expect one or two additional distributions rather than a long series.

The Court will **not call for the Solicitor General's views**. There is no existing CVSG, and I assign **1%** to a new request.

If the Court grants review, the likelier route is a **summary disposition in the cert order**, at **75% conditional on grant**. A narrow summary reversal concerning the clearly established law analysis, potentially limited to the shooting claim, is more likely than plenary briefing and argument. A routine GVR in light of Barnes is not the leading summary route because the decision below already considered Barnes.

If the Court instead takes a question for plenary review, I expect it to narrow the petition's second question to whether the precedents relied on by the Tenth Circuit clearly established the unlawfulness of the particular force used on the facts assumed at summary judgment. I do not expect the Court to accept all three questions wholesale or use this case to announce a comprehensive rule for officer-created danger. Any merits reasoning on that branch would emphasize the specificity of precedent and the division between legal immunity questions and factual disputes on interlocutory appeal.

Conditional on denial, I predict **no noted dissent or statement respecting denial**; the probability of at least one such writing is **6%**. A denial will leave the litigation to proceed without resolving the competing factual accounts or establishing Supreme Court approval of the lower court's reasoning.
