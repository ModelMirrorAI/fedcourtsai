# Forecast: what the Court will do with No. 25-1062, Pitts v. Burke

**Disposition.** The Court will deny the petition. P(any grant) = 0.20. If it grants, the route will almost certainly be a per curiam summary reversal on the "clearly established law" prong rather than plenary review: P(summary route | grant) = 0.70. A GVR is unlikely because no intervening decision of this Court bears on the panel's holding (Barnes v. Felix predates the Tenth Circuit's opinion), so the residual grant mass is a plenary grant, most plausibly to reach the officer-created-danger question Barnes reserved.

**Where the docket stands, and relists from here.** The snapshot shows two distribution entries: an April 8 distribution for the April 24, 2026 conference that was overtaken when the Court called for a response on April 20 after respondent had waived, and a July 8 distribution for the September 28, 2026 long conference on the fully briefed petition. The petition has therefore never actually been considered at a conference, and the September 28 conference is its first substantive look. I forecast a better-than-even chance the Court does not dispose of it on the October 5 order list: P(at least one further distribution) = 0.45. Two shapes drive that number. If any Justice is pressing for a summary reversal, the pattern this Term (Zorn v. Linton: waiver, call for response, then months of relists before a March per curiam) means several relists, likely three or more. If the Court is going to deny, a relist or two while a dissent from denial is drafted is the other route to a relist. A clean denial on October 5 is still the single most likely outcome.

**CVSG.** None. No federal party, no federal statute in play, and the Court does not call for the Solicitor General's views on § 1983 qualified-immunity petitions against municipal officers: P(CVSG called for) = 0.02.

**Which question, if any.** If the Court acts, it will act on the petition's second question, the level-of-generality point, in a per curiam that holds the Tenth Circuit's taser and deadly-force precedents (Cavanaugh, Lee, Ceballos, Tenorio, Carr) do not squarely govern a suspect who was warned about, and repeatedly reached toward, a back pocket in a confined room. It would not take the first question as written: the panel expressly analyzed each use of force at the moment it occurred, and the "reckless creation" discussion appears only in rejecting an argument about the district court's reliance on an expert. The third question (appellate jurisdiction) is underdeveloped in the petition and would be left behind. A plenary grant to answer the Barnes-reserved question is possible but unlikely on this record, because the panel did not rest its judgment on that theory. **Summary disposition versus plenary review.** Conditional on a grant, summary reversal: the petition itself asks for it, the Court's qualified-immunity interventions against the Tenth Circuit have taken that form (City of Tahlequah v. Bond), and this Term's Zorn v. Linton shows the six-Justice majority is again willing to use it. The obstacle to any grant is that the facts taken in respondent's favor, an unarmed man whom the officers had been told was unarmed, shot while moving toward a doorway after being tased without warning for holding a doll, are far less favorable to the officers than in any prior summary reversal, all of which involved a suspect with, or reasonably believed to have, a weapon.

**Dissent from denial.** If the Court denies, most likely no separate writing: P(dissent or statement respecting denial | denial) = 0.15. The plausible authors are Justice Thomas or Justice Alito, writing that the Tenth Circuit again defined clearly established law too generally, possibly joined by Justice Kavanaugh given his Barnes concurrence; Justice Gorsuch, a former Tenth Circuit judge, has been reluctant to expand qualified immunity. (The "from whom" half is context-only.)

**Timing.** Considered September 28, 2026. Denial would issue on the October 5, 2026 order list, or after one or two relists by early November. A summary reversal, if one comes, would issue as a per curiam between November 2026 and March 2027 after repeated relists.

**If granted, conditionally.** The likely ground is that no Tenth Circuit precedent placed beyond debate the unlawfulness of shooting a drug-impaired, non-compliant person who had pushed an officer and was reaching toward a back pocket in close quarters, with Officer Lewis's taser deployments likely swept into the same disposition or left to the district court on remand. A plenary opinion on the officer-created-danger question would divide the Court and is the low-probability branch.
