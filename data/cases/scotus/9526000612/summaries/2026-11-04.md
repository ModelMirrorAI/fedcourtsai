## In brief

Martin Akerman asked the Chief Justice to order preservation and production of federal employment records while he pursues further appeals; the Chief Justice denied the request.

## What happened

Martin Akerman, representing himself, has pursued a long-running dispute over his federal employment records, including documents about administrative leave, notice leave, and a disability-retirement application he says contained false information from his employer. A federal district court denied his request for a preliminary injunction ordering production of these records, finding part of his request outside the lawsuit and insufficient evidence of urgent harm. The D.C. Circuit Court of Appeals later denied reconsideration and an administrative injunction. Akerman then asked the Supreme Court to step in while he prepares a petition asking it to review the appeals court's refusal to grant interim relief.

## What the Court is being asked

Akerman asked the Chief Justice, acting as the justice responsible for emergency matters from the D.C. Circuit, to order preservation and production of specific employment and disability records, and to restore a procedural window that closed in April 2026, while his case continues in the lower courts.

## Where it stands

The application was submitted to the Chief Justice on October 20, 2026, and denied on November 3, 2026.

## What each outcome would mean

Akerman, the applicant, sought the injunction; the Merit Systems Protection Board and other federal respondents opposed it. Denying the application, which is what happened, leaves in place the lower courts' refusals to order preservation or production of the disputed records while Akerman's case proceeds. It does not decide the underlying disputes over his employment records or benefits claims, which remain pending in the lower courts and agencies.
