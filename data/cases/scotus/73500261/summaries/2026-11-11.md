## In brief

Louisiana officials ask the Court to hear a challenge to a Ten Commandments classroom-display law, but only if it also takes up a similar Texas case.

## What happened

Louisiana's H.B. 71 requires every public school to display the Ten Commandments in each classroom. Public-school parents and children sued, arguing the law violates the First Amendment's religion clauses. A federal district court blocked the law, and a Fifth Circuit panel agreed. The full Fifth Circuit, sitting en banc, then vacated that injunction, but only on the ground that the challenge was premature, since schools had not yet decided how to implement the displays; it did not rule on whether the law itself is constitutional. Louisiana filed a "conditional" petition asking the Supreme Court to hear this case only if it also takes up a similar Texas case, Nathan, where the Fifth Circuit upheld a comparable law on the merits.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked whether the parents' challenge was premature because the law's practical effects were not yet known, and, separately, whether the law violates constitutional protections against government establishment of religion or interference with religious exercise.

## Where it stands

The petition has been distributed for conference twice, most recently in December 2026, after rounds of extensions for the response and a reply from Louisiana; the docket shows no action yet on whether the Court will hear the case, and the case was rescheduled after the most recent distribution.

## What each outcome would mean

Louisiana's officials, who lost the prematurity ruling's practical effect by having their injunction merely lifted rather than the case dismissed, are asking the Court to act, but only if it also takes the Texas case; the parents who sued oppose review entirely. If the Court denies review, the Fifth Circuit's prematurity ruling stands, leaving the law's constitutionality undecided for now. If the Court grants review, it would hear full arguments and decide the prematurity question, the constitutional questions, or both, with no party's position prevailing in advance.
