## In brief

Louisiana officials ask the Supreme Court to hear their case only if it takes up a similar Texas Ten Commandments dispute, after winning below on procedural grounds.

## What happened

Louisiana's H.B. 71 requires every public school to display the Ten Commandments in each classroom. Parents sued, arguing the law violates the First Amendment's religion clauses. A district court and a Fifth Circuit panel agreed and blocked the law. But the full Fifth Circuit, sitting en banc, vacated that injunction on narrower grounds: it held the challenge was not yet ready for decision because too much about how schools would display the Commandments remained unknown. The court did not decide whether the law itself is constitutional. Louisiana officials, despite largely winning, filed a "conditional" petition asking the Supreme Court to step in only if it agrees to review a related Texas case.

## What the Court is being asked

The petition raises two issues: whether the parents' challenge to the Louisiana law was premature because the law had not yet been put into practice, and whether the law violates constitutional protections for religious freedom. But Louisiana's own filing asks the Court to take up the case only conditionally, if it separately decides to review the Texas dispute, and otherwise argues the Court should deny review entirely.

## Where it stands

The petition has been distributed for conference, and the Court asked for a response, which the parents have now filed opposing review. The case has not yet been scheduled for another conference or acted on since the opposition brief arrived.

## What each outcome would mean

Louisiana officials, who largely prevailed below, filed this petition asking the Court to act only if it also takes up the related Texas case. The parents, who lost the procedural ruling but face no live injunction problem of their own, urge the Court to deny review entirely, arguing the case is a poor vehicle since the lower court never ruled on the law's constitutionality. If the Court denies the petition, the Fifth Circuit's decision leaving the preliminary injunction vacated stands, and the case continues in the lower courts. If the Court grants it, the case would be heard alongside the Texas dispute, and the Court would decide the procedural and constitutional questions itself.
