# Forecast: what the Court will do with No. 25-1360 (Brumley v. Roake)

**Disposition.** Denied (P(any grant) = 0.20). This is Louisiana's *conditional* cross-petition from the en banc Fifth Circuit's ripeness vacatur in *Roake*. Louisiana won below, tells the Court to deny every petition in the cluster, and asks for a grant only if the Court grants a petition arising from the Texas companion case, *Nathan v. Alamo Heights ISD* (No. 26-257, filed Aug. 17, 2026, response due Oct. 28, 2026). The Louisiana plaintiffs filed no petition of their own from the *Roake* judgment and initially waived a response here. So this docket's fate is derivative: the Court will act on it only when it acts on 26-257, and the modal path is that 26-257 is denied and this petition is denied the same day, or that 26-257 is granted and this petition is held and later denied.

**Relists.** The docket shows **one** distribution (for the Sept. 28, 2026 conference), overtaken by the Court's July 28 call for a response, which is now due Oct. 19. The petition will necessarily be redistributed after the response and reply arrive, and it will then travel with 26-257, whose response is due Oct. 28. Expect a first joint distribution for a December 2026 or January 2027 conference and then at least one further relist as the Court weighs the cluster; two or more additional distributions in total is the likeliest shape. If the Court decides to await the Eighth Circuit's decision in *Stinson v. Fayetteville School District* (argued Sept. 22, 2026), the relist count could run much higher through a long hold.

**CVSG.** Unlikely (about 5%). Both cases involve state statutes with no federal party, and the current Solicitor General would almost certainly file as amicus in support of Texas and Louisiana without being asked, so an invitation serves little purpose.

**Which question, if granted.** If the Court grants 26-257 and takes this petition with it, it takes this petition's QP 1 (justiciability of the pre-enforcement facial challenge to H.B. 71, which the en banc court decided) and treats QP 2 (facial validity of H.B. 71) as subsumed in the *Nathan* merits question; the Court will not frame a free-standing merits question on a statute whose merits the court below never reached.

**Summary route.** Conditional on any grant, about 22% is a cert-order disposition rather than plenary review: the most plausible non-plenary path is a hold behind 26-257 followed by a GVR directing the Fifth Circuit to render dismissal if Texas prevails on the merits. The likelier grant form is a consolidated plenary grant with 26-257, because the Fifth Circuit heard the two cases together and the ripeness question in *Roake* is bound up with the standing and ripeness questions the Court would have to address in *Nathan* anyway.

**On denial.** No separate writing on this docket is expected (about 8%). Any dissent from or statement respecting denial in the cluster will be filed on 26-257, where the plaintiffs' petition squarely presents the *Stone v. Graham* question; a denial of a prevailing state's own conditional petition draws no writing. The one scenario for a writing here is a Justice using the Louisiana docket to comment on offended-observer standing, which Judges Oldham and Willett flagged below.

**If granted, conditionally.** A consolidated merits decision would turn on whether *Stone* survives *Kennedy v. Bremerton*; the Court would likely hold the Louisiana challenge unripe or the facial challenge unproven, giving Louisiana the dismissal it asks for, while deciding the Texas statute's facial validity on the merits.
