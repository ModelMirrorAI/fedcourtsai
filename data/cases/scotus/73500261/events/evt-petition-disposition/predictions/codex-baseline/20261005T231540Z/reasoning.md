## Adjustments and uncertainty

The petition's introduction and conclusion expressly seek a grant only if the Court grants a petition arising from Nathan. The officials obtained vacatur of the preliminary injunction on ripeness grounds but seek the additional relief of outright dismissal. I therefore do not apply the ordinary state-petitioner rate as though Louisiana had lost on the constitutionality of its law. Its favorable judgment, contingent request, and the possibility of addressing the controversy through another vehicle weigh downward. The supplied record does not establish the status of any companion Supreme Court petition; I do not assume a companion grant.

Countervailing considerations are the response request after waiver, the consequential classroom-religion questions, and the petition's explanation of the differences between Louisiana's and Texas's laws. If the Court reviews the related litigation, Louisiana's justiciability posture and request for additional relief could make this case a useful companion rather than a redundant one. The petition also frames a dispute about Stone, Kennedy, and the treatment of facial challenges. I treat its assertion of circuit consensus and its claims about those precedents as advocacy, not independently verified conclusions. A national controversy alone does not establish a clean split or a grant-worthy vehicle.

These effects roughly offset, leaving 22%, slightly below the 22.7% anchor. This is a judgmental probability, not a fitted conditional-petition model. The largest uncertainties are companion-case developments and the unavailable opposition.
