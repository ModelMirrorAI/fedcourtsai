# Predicted course: Brumley v. Roake, No. 25-1360 (conditional petition)

**Posture.** Louisiana won vacatur of the preliminary injunction against H.B. 71 on ripeness grounds before the en banc Fifth Circuit. It filed this petition only as a **conditional** one: it asks the Court to deny review of both Fifth Circuit Ten Commandments decisions, but to take this case too if it grants the Texas companion case, *Nathan v. Alamo Heights* (No. 26-257). The docket shows one distribution (for the 9/28/2026 conference). The respondents had waived a response, and the Court then called for one on 7/28/2026. This petition's fate is tied to *Nathan*.

**Forecast.**
- **Disposition: denied (about 76%).** The likeliest path is that the Court decides *Nathan* first, or with this case. If it denies *Nathan*, Louisiana's conditional petition is denied with it, because the condition fails. If it grants *Nathan*, which I put at somewhat better than even, the Court still has a cleaner option than taking a second case: review *Nathan* alone. That case was found ripe and was decided on the merits, so it presents the Establishment Clause and Free Exercise questions directly. This petition would add a preliminary-injunction ripeness posture. Louisiana's petition would then most likely be held and later denied, or granted only if the Court wants the ripeness question and the Louisiana statute together. I put the chance of a grant at about one in four.
- **Further distribution: near-certain.** The response came in after the first conference date passed, so the petition will be distributed again, most likely to be considered alongside or after *Nathan* once that case's response (due 11/10/2026) is in. Expect at least one more distribution. Relists or a hold pending *Nathan* are plausible, so several more are possible.
- **CVSG: no.** No federal interest requires the Solicitor General's views, and the United States can file uninvited.
- **If granted, which question:** both QPs as written, most likely consolidated with *Nathan*. QP 1 (justiciability/ripeness) is the question this vehicle uniquely adds. QP 2 (facial Establishment Clause / Free Exercise validity) duplicates *Nathan*.
- **Summary disposition:** unlikely as the route even on a grant (about 20%). A GVR or hold-then-vacate is the main summary path, if *Nathan* is decided against the States and the Court sends the Louisiana ripeness ruling back.
- **Dissent from denial:** unlikely. A denial here would most likely be the mechanical consequence of denying *Nathan* or of granting *Nathan* alone. Any separate writing would probably attach to *Nathan* rather than to this conditional petition. If one appears, Justices Thomas, Gorsuch or Alito (on revisiting *Stone v. Graham*), or Justice Sotomayor (on the classroom displays), are the likeliest writers.
