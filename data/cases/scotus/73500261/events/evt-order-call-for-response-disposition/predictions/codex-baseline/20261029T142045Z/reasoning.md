## Anchor and adjustments

The relevant committed prior is the **bracketed reached rate for state under sal-v4**, not the terminal state-band rate, the private-petitioner baseline, or the all-docket rate. I pooled every rendered Term strictly before this docket's 2025 Term, namely OT2017–OT2024. Using the exact prefix rates and weighted resolved denominators in metrics/statpack.json gives **94 grant-family outcomes / 395 resolved = 23.7975%**. The corresponding denominators, newest first, are 38, 32, 36, 97, 46, 53, 42, and 51. I excluded OT2025 and OT2026 even though those rows appear in the pack. This is a historical estimate from the committed statpack at this checkout, not a claim about today's live corpus population; I did not obtain a fresh corpus-wide vintage. I do not use the unrelated retrieved prior rows to estimate a current rate.

I place the final forecast at **22%**, close to but slightly below that 23.8% anchor, for opposing reasons:

- **Upward:** The Court affirmatively requested a response despite a waiver. The underlying policy applies religious text throughout public-school classrooms, and the papers raise an important disagreement about older school-religion precedent and the newer history-and-tradition approach. Those features warrant materially more attention than an ordinary fact-bound petition. There is no published response-request-conditioned rate in the pack, however, so this adjustment is judgment rather than a measured multiplier.
- **Downward:** This is not a losing state's straightforward request to overturn an injunction. The en banc Fifth Circuit already vacated the injunction on ripeness grounds. Louisiana expressly asks the Court to deny review unless it reviews Nathan; its additional requested relief is dismissal of the suit. The petition's introduction, pages 1–3, companion discussion, pages 33–36, and conclusion make that contingency explicit. A grant therefore depends substantially on another case and on the Court wanting this additional vehicle.
- **Downward:** The surviving Louisiana opinion, Appendix A pages 4a–11a in petition.txt, rests on unresolved display context and ripeness rather than a merits holding sustaining all mandatory classroom displays. Local discretion and the preliminary-injunction posture complicate this vehicle. A constitutionally significant dispute does not by itself require immediate review of this petition.
- **Counterweight:** The petition explains that the Louisiana and Texas cases were argued together below and that the statutes differ in contextualization and placement requirements. If the Court does take the Texas controversy, those differences make coordinated consideration of Louisiana plausible. I therefore retain a substantial grant probability instead of treating the petition's protective wording as making a grant negligible. The petition's assertion of no circuit conflict remains advocacy; I have not independently verified a present nationwide consensus.

The independent cert signals do not justify moving to a high-relist or CVSG-conditioned anchor. The snapshot has one distribution, not repeated post-conference relists. The pack's terminal relist-0 bucket has 10,203 weighted resolved petitions and roughly 1.8% grant-family outcomes; relist-1 has 2,538 and roughly 13.7%; relist-2 has 495 and roughly 41.2%; relist-3+ has 484 and roughly 37.4%. These are terminal populations pooled across Terms, not forward hazards from this response request. The CVSG cut similarly contrasts about 34.8% grant-family outcomes among 164 resolved invited cases with 6.4% among 13,556 without an invitation. Neither cut is a substitute for the strictly-prior state risk set. No amicus brief is shown in the July snapshot; I do not equate that early observation with the terminal zero-amicus bucket.

## Other forecast numbers

**Additional distribution, 0.98:** Respondent briefing ordinarily puts a response-requested petition back into circulation. The July 8 distribution was for a conference not yet held as of the snapshot. An additional distribution is therefore highly likely without implying a substantive relist after conference; the harness masks this claim at this moment.

**New CVSG, 0.04:** The question is chiefly school-religion doctrine and Article III, and the record contains no invitation. Federal constitutional importance alone does not make executive-branch consultation likely.

**Summary route conditional on grant, 0.45:** A protective companion may be disposed of in light of another ruling, but a genuinely different ripeness question may warrant joint merits proceedings. This is conditional on a grant, not an unconditional 45% chance of a summary order. Its unconditional grant-and-summary mass is 0.22 × 0.45 = 0.099. I identify no already-issued intervening decision as requiring a GVR.

**Writing conditional on denial, 0.06:** An unsigned order is much more likely. Denying the state's protective petition does not itself decide against the parents on the merits, limiting the reason for a dissent from denial. The order-writers probabilities cover both grant and denial routes; several Justices can write, so those numbers neither sum to one nor equal this conditional existence probability. I omit speculative cert votes.

**Stakes, 0.88:** The underlying policy reaches public-school children statewide and implicates the boundary between government religious expression and individual religious freedom. This rates potential importance if decided, not grant likelihood. The procedural limitations lower review odds without making the controversy low-stakes.
