# Predicted Court Reasoning and Procedural Trajectory: *Brumley v. Roake* (No. 25-1360)

1. **Further Distributions and Relists (`relist-increment: 0.95`):**
   The docket as of July 28, 2026 records a single distribution (`distribution_count: 1`, distributed July 22, 2026 for the September 28, 2026 Long Conference) followed by the Court's July 28, 2026 Call for a Response (due August 27, 2026). Once Respondents' brief in opposition is filed (or after any extension), the petition will be redistributed for an October or November 2026 conference, advancing its distribution count past 1. Furthermore, the Court will likely relist or hold No. 25-1360 across two or more conferences so that it can be considered alongside Respondents' forthcoming direct petition for certiorari from the same Fifth Circuit en banc judgment (extended to August 19, 2026 in No. 25A1239) and the parallel petitions arising from the Fifth Circuit's companion en banc decision in *Nathan v. Paxton* (Texas's S.B. 10).

2. **Solicitor General Invitation (`cvsg-increment: 0.005`):**
   The Court will not call for the views of the Solicitor General (CVSG). The dispute concerns the facial constitutionality under the First Amendment and Article III ripeness of a state statute governing public school classroom displays, implicating no federal statute, federal program, or federal administrative agency.

3. **Disposition and Vehicle Selection (`predicted_disposition: denied`, `probability: 0.14`):**
   The Court will deny Louisiana's conditional petition for a writ of certiorari. Petitioners (Superintendent Brumley and the BESE members) prevailed in the en banc Fifth Circuit, which vacated the district court's preliminary injunction and remanded with instructions to dismiss the complaint without prejudice as unripe. Petitioners explicitly frame No. 25-1360 as a protective conditional petition, advising the Court that review of H.B. 71 is premature and requesting a grant only if the Court decides to grant certiorari on the *Roake* plaintiffs' forthcoming petition or the parallel Texas litigation (*Nathan v. Paxton*). Under the rule of *Camreta v. Greene*, 563 U.S. 692 (2011), the Court ordinarily declines to grant review at the behest of a prevailing party merely to convert a jurisdictional dismissal without prejudice into a merits dismissal with prejudice. Even if the Court chooses to revisit *Stone v. Graham*, 449 U.S. 39 (1980), during the 2026 Term, *Paxton v. Nathan* (where the en banc Fifth Circuit reached the merits and affirmed a preliminary injunction against Texas's Ten Commandments statute under *Stone*) or the *Roake* plaintiffs' own petition challenging the Fifth Circuit's vacatur will serve as the primary vehicle, leaving No. 25-1360 to be denied.

4. **Questions Presented and Route Conditional on a Grant (`summary-disposition-route: 0.12`):**
   If the Court were nonetheless to grant No. 25-1360—most plausibly by consolidating it with the *Roake* plaintiffs' petition or *Paxton v. Nathan* for plenary review—it would take both Question Presented 1 (Article III justiciability/ripeness of pre-enforcement challenges to classroom display laws) and Question Presented 2 (whether H.B. 71 is facially unconstitutional under the Establishment Clause or Free Exercise Clause, including whether *Stone v. Graham* should be overruled in light of *Kennedy v. Bremerton School District*, 597 U.S. 507 (2022)). Conditional on a grant, plenary review (88%) is far more likely than a summary disposition in the cert order (12%), as a summary reversal in favor of a party that already won vacatur and dismissal below is procedurally inapt, leaving only a small chance of a GVR if the petition were held pending a merits decision in a companion case.

5. **Separate Writings on Denial (`dissent-from-denial: 0.15`):**
   Upon denial of certiorari, the order will most likely issue without a noted dissent because Petitioners won vacatur of the injunction below and themselves urged the Court to deny review unless it took up a companion petition. However, if the Court denies review across the entire Fifth Circuit Ten Commandments cluster (or leaves the Fifth Circuit's ripeness dismissal in *Roake* undisturbed while declining to reach the First Amendment merits), there is a meaningful chance (~15%) that Justice Thomas, Justice Alito, or Justice Gorsuch authors a dissent from the denial of certiorari or a statement respecting the denial arguing that the Court should have reached the merits and formally overruled *Stone v. Graham*.
