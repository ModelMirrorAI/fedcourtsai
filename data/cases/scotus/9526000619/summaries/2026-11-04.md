## In brief

A homeowner asked the Chief Justice to pause a Maryland eviction while she seeks Supreme Court review; the Chief Justice denied the request.

## What happened

Denise Smith lost a Maryland foreclosure dispute involving Nationstar Mortgage and related parties. After a 2018 auction, a bankruptcy court entered a 2019 consent order setting payment and default procedures. Smith says Nationstar's counsel told her to withhold payments and then used her nonpayment as a default, while a bankruptcy judge later declined to rule on that claimed default. Maryland's courts denied her appeal on procedural grounds, and the Supreme Court of Maryland denied review and reconsideration. With a writ of possession pending, Smith asked the Chief Justice, acting as Circuit Justice for the Fourth Circuit, to stay enforcement while she prepares a Supreme Court petition.

## What the Court is being asked

The application asks whether enforcing the Maryland possession judgment should be paused temporarily so Smith can pursue a petition asking the Court to review whether Maryland's appellate filing rules were applied in a way that unfairly blocked her from getting a hearing on her federal due-process claim about the disputed loan default.

## Where it stands

The Chief Justice denied the stay application on November 3, 2026, shortly after it was filed and submitted to him.

## What each outcome would mean

Smith, the applicant, asked for the stay. Granting it would have temporarily paused enforcement of the Maryland possession judgment against her while she prepared a certiorari petition, without deciding who is ultimately right. Because the Chief Justice denied it, the possession judgment favoring Nationstar and the other respondents remains enforceable, and nothing currently blocks execution of the writ of possession while Smith's case continues elsewhere.
