## In brief

The Supreme Court declined to revive a tribe's lawsuit seeking money damages from the federal government over a third party's upstream diversion of reservation water.

## What happened

The Winnemucca Indian Colony sued the United States for money damages, claiming the government breached its trust duties by failing, over many years, to recognize a lawful tribal government and, as a result, failing to stop a development company from diverting a stream that had supplied water to part of the reservation. The Court of Federal Claims dismissed the claim, finding no law required the government to pay damages for this, and also citing other jurisdictional bars. The Federal Circuit affirmed, agreeing the tribe had not identified a specific legal duty the government violated.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked whether rules recognizing a tribe's right to use water tied to its reservation, together with regulations controlling the sale of tribal trust land, create a specific duty requiring the government to pay money damages when it fails to stop a third party from diverting water before it reaches the reservation.

## Where it stands

The petition was distributed for conference twice, with the government first waiving its right to respond and then being asked to respond; after full briefing, including an opposition brief and a reply, the Court denied the petition.

## What each outcome would mean

The Winnemucca Indian Colony, which asked the Court to take the case, sought to overturn the Federal Circuit's decision against it. The denial leaves that decision in place: the tribe's claim for money damages over the water diversion remains dismissed, and the government is not required to pay damages on this claim. Had the Court instead taken up the case, it would have heard full arguments and decided the legal question itself, without that decision alone resolving who was right.
