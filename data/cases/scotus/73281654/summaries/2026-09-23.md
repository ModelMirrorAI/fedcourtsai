## What happened

The Winnemucca Indian Colony, a federally recognized tribe in Nevada, sued the United States for money damages in the Court of Federal Claims. The tribe says that during a two-decade internal leadership dispute, the Bureau of Indian Affairs refused to recognize either of two rival tribal governments, and that during this period the government failed to stop a private development company from diverting a stream that had supplied water to part of the tribe's reservation. The Court of Federal Claims dismissed the claim, and the Federal Circuit affirmed, holding the tribe had not pointed to a law creating a specific duty the government could be sued for breaching. The tribe asked for rehearing, which was denied.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked whether a legal doctrine recognizing that setting aside land for a tribe also reserves needed water, combined with a regulation requiring government approval before trust land is transferred, together create a specific duty for the government to stop outsiders from diverting water away from a reservation — and whether failing to enforce that duty can lead to a damages award. The government responds that neither source imposes such a duty, that the doctrine is judge-made rather than found in a statute or treaty, and that the regulation only concerns approving land transfers, not water or third-party diversions.

## Where it stands

The case has been distributed for conference twice, most recently for September 28, 2026, after the government filed its brief opposing review and the tribe filed a reply; the Court has not yet acted on the petition.
