## In brief

A tribe asks the Court to revive its claim for money damages against the federal government over a third party's diversion of water from its reservation.

## What happened

The Winnemucca Indian Colony sued the United States in the Court of Federal Claims, seeking money damages for, among other things, the government's alleged failure to stop a development company from diverting a stream that once supplied water to part of the tribe's reservation. The tribe blamed this on the government's decades-long refusal to recognize either faction in an internal tribal leadership dispute. The Court of Federal Claims dismissed the claim, finding no legal basis requiring the government to pay damages and citing other jurisdictional problems. The Federal Circuit affirmed, holding that neither the legal doctrine the tribe relied on nor the regulation it pointed to imposed a specific duty on the government that could support a damages claim.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked whether a longstanding rule recognizing that setting aside land for a tribe implicitly reserves some water rights, together with a regulation on approving land transfers, creates a specific government duty—one that can be enforced through a damages lawsuit—to stop outsiders from diverting water headed to a reservation.

## Where it stands

The petition has been distributed for conference twice, most recently for the conference of September 28, 2026, after full briefing including the government's brief opposing review and the tribe's reply. The docket does not yet show any decision on the petition.

## What each outcome would mean

The Winnemucca Indian Colony, as petitioner, asks the Court to take up the case. If the Court grants review, it will hear the case fully and decide the legal question; a grant by itself would not decide who wins. If the Court denies review, the Federal Circuit's decision affirming dismissal of the tribe's claim stays in place, leaving the United States's win below undisturbed and the tribe without this damages claim.
