First, the Federal Circuit's decision is unpublished and non-precedential, making it an unlikely vehicle for Supreme Court review. Second, the court dismissed the continuing trespass claims under the Section 1500 jurisdictional bar due to an overlapping 2011 district court lawsuit, and dismissed other claims under the six-year statute of limitations (28 U.S.C. 2501). These multiple, independent procedural hurdles would prevent the Court from reaching the merits. 

On the merits, the Federal Circuit's holding—that the common law Winters doctrine and 25 C.F.R. 152.22 do not create a money-mandating fiduciary duty—is a direct and faithful application of the Supreme Court's very recent decision in Arizona v. Navajo Nation, 599 U.S. 555 (2023). There is no plausible circuit split, and the United States (the respondent) already submitted its Brief in Opposition confirming the procedural bars and the application of Navajo Nation. With the US as the respondent, a CVSG is essentially impossible. Given the strength of the BIO, the recent controlling precedent, and the procedural bars, a grant is highly improbable.
