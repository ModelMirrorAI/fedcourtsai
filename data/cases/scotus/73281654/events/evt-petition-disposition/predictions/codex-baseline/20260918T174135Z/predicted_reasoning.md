# Forecast

The Court will deny certiorari in Winnemucca Indian Colony v. United States, No. 25-1170. My probability of any grant, including a GVR or summary reversal, is 6%.

The snapshot records two distributions: May 12 for the May 28, 2026 conference and September 9 for the September 28, 2026 conference. Thus the record does not establish two completed conferences considering a fully briefed petition.

- **Further distributions:** The modal forecast is zero additional distributions, followed by denial in the orders following the September 28 conference, most likely in early October 2026. I assign 25% to at least one further distribution beyond the frozen count of two; within that branch, one or two additional distributions are more likely than a prolonged hold. Timing is a forecast, not a scored claim.
- **Solicitor General's views:** No CVSG appears in the record. I assign 0.5% to a new CVSG. The United States is already the respondent and the Solicitor General has submitted its requested opposition; this is not a petition awaiting the government's first expression of its position.
- **Question if granted:** The Court would most likely address a narrowed combination of questions 1 and 3: whether the reservation's governing sources, the Winters reserved-water-rights doctrine, and 25 C.F.R. § 152.22 impose an enforceable, money-mandating federal duty to protect tribal water rights against third-party diversion. Question 2 is the case-specific consequence rather than an independent issue likely to attract review.
- **Route if granted:** Plenary briefing and argument are more likely than disposition in the cert order. The probability of the summary route, conditional on a grant, is 10%. Neither side's provisioned brief identifies a new intervening decision requiring a GVR. A grant to distinguish existing-resource protection from securing additional water would call for substantive consideration, not merely mechanical reconsideration below.
- **Writing if denied:** Expect no noted dissent or statement respecting denial; the conditional probability of some such writing is 14%. The distinction between safeguarding existing tribal resources and obtaining additional water supplies is the most plausible subject of a separate writing. I do not predict a particular Justice or an undisclosed cert vote.

The likely denial leaves the Federal Circuit's disposition intact without expressing a Supreme Court merits rationale. The forecast rests on the petition's lack of a demonstrated inter-circuit conflict, the government's text-based response to the asserted trust duty, and the independent jurisdictional obstacles described in the opposition. If review is granted, the central doctrinal contest will concern the source and specificity of the federal duty, not whether water matters to the Tribe or whether reserved water rights exist at all.
