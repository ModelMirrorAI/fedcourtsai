## In brief

A mother asks the Court to pause a Texas custody order and related enforcement action while she seeks review of her dismissed appeal.

## What happened

Elisha Holloway lost a Texas trial court case that gave sole custody of her children to Bryan Polk, restricted her to supervised visits, and imposed other conditions. She tried to appeal, but the Texas appeals court dismissed her appeal for not paying a $205 filing fee, after rejecting her request to reconsider whether she could afford it. The Texas Supreme Court declined to review that dismissal. Separately, the trial court later ordered law enforcement to locate the children and deliver them to Polk, citing the custody order as authority. Holloway's attempts to appeal that attachment order were also dismissed, on the ground that such orders cannot be appealed. She has now filed both a petition asking the Supreme Court to review the fee-related dismissal and this emergency application asking a single Justice to pause enforcement in the meantime.

## What the Court is being asked

The application asks a single Justice to temporarily block enforcement of the custody and attachment orders while the Court decides whether to hear Holloway's case. The underlying petition asks whether dismissing her only appeal of a major custody ruling, over an unpaid filing fee, while her request to be excused from that fee was still pending, violated her constitutional rights to a fair process.

## Where it stands

The docket shows only that the stay application was submitted to Justice Alito on September 27, 2026, and recorded by the Court on October 2, 2026. No response from Polk or ruling on the application appears in the record.

## What each outcome would mean

Holloway, who lost in the lower courts, is asking for emergency relief. If the stay is granted, enforcement of the January custody order and the July attachment order would pause temporarily, keeping the children's current living arrangement in place while the Court considers her separate petition asking it to hear the case. If the stay is denied, those orders could be enforced immediately, including law enforcement locating and delivering the children to Polk, while Holloway's petition proceeds separately. Either way, this application does not decide the underlying legal questions.
