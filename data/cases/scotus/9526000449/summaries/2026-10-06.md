## In brief

A mother asked a Justice to pause a Texas custody order and related enforcement while her appeal of a fee-based dismissal proceeds; the application was denied.

## What happened

Elisha Holloway sought to appeal a Texas trial court order that gave Bryan Polk sole custody and restricted Holloway to supervised visits. Texas appellate courts dismissed her appeal after she did not pay a $205 filing fee, and denied her request to reconsider her claimed inability to pay. The Texas Supreme Court declined further review. Separately, the trial court later issued an order and writ directing law enforcement to locate the children and deliver them to Polk, relying on the original custody order; Holloway's attempt to appeal that attachment order was also dismissed, since Texas courts held such orders are not appealable. Holloway then asked the U.S. Supreme Court to stay enforcement while she pursues a petition for review there.

## What the Court is being asked

Holloway asked a single Justice to temporarily block enforcement of the custody and attachment orders while the Court considers her petition asking it to review whether Texas courts violated her constitutional rights by closing off her only appeal over an unpaid fee before deciding her request to be excused from paying it.

## Where it stands

The application for a stay was submitted to Justice Alito on September 27, 2026, and he denied it on October 5, 2026. The underlying petition for a writ of certiorari, referenced in the application, is not itself reported as decided in this record.

## What each outcome would mean

Holloway, naming herself Applicant, asked the Court to pause enforcement of the custody and attachment orders while her broader appeal continues. Granting the stay would have temporarily blocked enforcement of those orders against her, preserving the children's current situation during further review. Denying it, which is what happened, leaves those orders in effect and enforceable by Texas authorities while Holloway's separate petition for certiorari, if still pending, proceeds on its own schedule.
