# Forecast: what the Court will do with No. 25-1388

**State forecast from.** The docket shows one distribution (Conference of
September 28, 2026) and a CVSG issued October 5, 2026. No relist preceded the
CVSG. The paired petition on the same question, *AstraZeneca Pharmaceuticals LP
v. Mosaic Health, Inc.*, No. 25-1070 (CA2, Illinois Brick and lost-profit
damages, plaintiffs prevailed below), sat on the same conference and received a
CVSG the same day.

**Relists.** The petition will be distributed again once the Solicitor General
files, which is close to certain; the only way it is not is a settlement or
withdrawal before the brief arrives. Expect the SG's brief in the late-May 2027
window that October invitations usually produce, a redistribution for a June
2027 conference, and one to two further distributions after that: the Court
will consider this petition and No. 25-1070 together, and a two-vehicle choice
on a question this technical tends to draw at least one relist. If the Court
grants No. 25-1070 alone, this petition will instead be held through the
OT2027 merits decision, which pushes its disposition into mid-2028 and adds
no distributions in the interval.

**CVSG.** Already on the docket; no further invitation will issue.

**Disposition.** More likely denied than granted, but not by much. The paired
CVSG shows the Court is seriously interested in whether *Illinois Brick*
reaches a non-purchaser's lost-profit claim; the question is which vehicle it
takes. The SG will most likely say the Sixth Circuit read *Illinois Brick* too
broadly (the Court's own cases frame it as a pass-on overcharge rule) but will
weigh this petition's vehicle problems: the respondents' claim that the panel
rested independently on proximate cause, the forfeiture of the joint-venture
framing Judge Bush supplied, and a summary-judgment record on the state-law
claims that undercuts the conspiracy story. No. 25-1070 is interlocutory but
has a defendant-petitioner and Chamber of Commerce support. The likeliest
grant shape is therefore a grant in No. 25-1070 with this petition held, then
GVR'd if the Court confines *Illinois Brick* to overcharge pass-on and denied
if the Court extends it. A plenary grant here (alone or consolidated with
No. 25-1070) is the next likeliest grant route, and the Court would take the
question presented as written; it is already framed narrowly as the
group-boycott, lost-profits situation.

**Summary disposition.** Conditional on any grant, roughly a quarter to a third
of the mass is a GVR in light of a merits decision in No. 25-1070 rather than
plenary review here; there is no intervening decision that would support a
GVR before the companion case is decided, and a summary reversal in a civil
antitrust case is very unlikely.

**Dissent from denial.** If denied, expect no separate writing in most worlds.
A denial that follows an SG recommendation to deny on vehicle grounds, with the
question alive in the companion case, is the kind the Court denies silently.
A short statement respecting denial is possible if the Court denies both
petitions and leaves the circuit disagreement standing; Justice Kavanaugh, who
wrote *Apple v. Pepper*, is the likeliest author, with Justice Gorsuch the
likeliest to write if the Court instead signals agreement with the Sixth
Circuit's bright-line reading.

**If granted, the likely ground** is that *Illinois Brick* governs only the
apportionment of a pass-on overcharge among purchasers in a distribution
chain, so a boycott target seeking its own lost profits is instead analysed
under the *Associated General Contractors* proximate-cause factors; that is
conditional on a grant and forecasts nothing on a denial.
