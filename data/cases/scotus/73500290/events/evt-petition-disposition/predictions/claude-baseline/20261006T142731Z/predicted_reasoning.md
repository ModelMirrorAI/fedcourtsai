# Forecast: what the Court will do with No. 25-1388

**Disposition.** More likely denied than granted, but close: P(any grant) = 0.42. The decisive fact is that the Court has already called for the Solicitor General's views (Oct. 5, 2026) and did so the same day in AstraZeneca v. Mosaic Health, No. 25-1070, the Second Circuit case on the other side of the claimed split. The Court is treating "does Illinois Brick reach lost-profits claims by parties not in privity with the violator" as a live question with two candidate vehicles. The SG's recommendation will carry the case. I expect the SG to acknowledge genuine tension between the Second and Sixth Circuits but to rate this petition the weaker vehicle of the two, because of the panel's separate speculative-damages holding on prospective-physician sales and the forfeiture fight over the joint-venture framing.

**Relists.** Distributed once (for the Sept. 28, 2026 conference), then removed from the calendar by the CVSG. The petition will not be considered again until the SG files, which for an October invitation typically lands between December and late May. Expect at least one further distribution, likely two or three, as the Court conferences the SG brief and then holds this case in step with Mosaic Health. The relist claim at 0.96 reflects only the small chance of withdrawal or settlement before redistribution.

**CVSG.** Already on the docket. No second invitation will issue; the harness masks this claim for the cell.

**Route if granted.** Three paths, in descending likelihood: (1) Mosaic Health alone is granted and this petition is held, with a GVR following only if the Court rules that Illinois Brick does not bar lost-profits recovery by the target of a boycott; (2) both petitions are granted and consolidated, giving the Court the plaintiff-side and defendant-side postures together; (3) this petition alone is granted as the cleaner group-boycott vehicle, which the reply brief argues for. Conditional on a grant, the chance the disposition rides in the cert order (a GVR after Mosaic Health) is about 0.30. No summary reversal: the Sixth Circuit's holding is reasoned and unanimous, and the Court would not reverse it without argument.

**Which question.** If the Court takes this case, it takes the QP as written, framed around a group boycott and lost-profits damages. It will not reach the forfeited joint-venture characterization or the state-law summary-judgment record. If it takes Mosaic Health instead, the operative question will be the overcharge-versus-lost-profits line in the 340B setting, with this case held.

**If granted, likely ground (conditional).** The Court would most plausibly confine Illinois Brick to indirect-purchaser overcharge claims, consistent with McCready and AGC treating remoteness as a separate inquiry, and reverse or vacate the Sixth Circuit. A minority (Gorsuch-style bright-line view from Apple v. Pepper) would extend the rule to indirect sellers.

**Dissent from denial.** Conditional on denial (most likely following an SG recommendation to deny both petitions or to take Mosaic Health alone and then decide it against the plaintiffs), a separate writing is possible but not expected: 0.20. The likeliest author is Justice Kavanaugh or Justice Gorsuch, both of whom wrote in Apple v. Pepper, with a short statement that the overcharge-versus-lost-profits line deserves resolution in a better vehicle.
