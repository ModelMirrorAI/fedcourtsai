# Rationale for the numbers

**Inputs read.** AGENTS.md, the predict prompt, the prediction schema, `event.yaml` (stage cert, moment distribution), `record/context.json` (forward mode, band `high` under sal-v4, Term 2025, distribution_count 1, cvsg_date 2026-10-05, no cutoff), the snapshot `2026-10-06.json`, and all three provisioned documents: `questions-presented.txt`, `petition.txt` (300 pages, `truncated: true`; the truncation falls inside the appendix, and the petition body through the conclusion is complete), and `brief-in-opposition.txt` (47 pages, complete). Beyond those I retrieved the petitioner's reply brief and the supremecourt.gov docket of the companion petition; see `retrieval.md`.

**Anchor.** The context freezes band `high` under sal-v4, and the statpack's "Segment base rate by salience band (sal-v4)" table matches that version. Pooling the bracketed `reached` figure for `high` over the Terms strictly before 2025 that the table renders (2017 through 2024) gives roughly 314 grants over n=898, about 35%. The CVSG cut on the paid scored segment says the same thing: among 163 resolved CVSG petitions the grant family (granted plus GVR) is about 35%. That is the yardstick I am scored against and my starting point.

**Adjustments up.**
- The same-day CVSG in AstraZeneca v. Mosaic Health, No. 25-1070, is the strongest signal in the record. A paired invitation on one doctrinal question means the Court has already decided the question merits the SG's attention, not just this petition; that is more than the ordinary CVSG the 35% rate averages over.
- Two Sixth Circuit judges flagged the case for review: Judge Bush's statement respecting denial of rehearing en banc said the case "may be appropriate for Supreme Court review," and Judge Kethledge concurred in the panel judgment while saying the result harms consumer welfare. A published 155 F.4th opinion, a dispositive Rule 12 ruling, and Paul Clement as counsel of record all fit the profile of a granted paid petition.
- The reply brief answers the BIO's lead vehicle point credibly: the panel's speculative-damages passage addresses only lost sales to prospective physicians, while the completed-sales lost profits were barred on Illinois Brick alone.

**Adjustments down.**
- Two vehicles for one question. The Court needs at most one, and Mosaic Health arrived first, was already relisted and rescheduled, and carries Chamber of Commerce amicus support. If the Court grants Mosaic Health and holds this case, this petition only enters the grant family through a later GVR, which requires the plaintiffs' side to win there. I put the chance that this petition is granted, consolidated, or GVR'd at roughly 0.8 conditional on the issue being taken at all.
- The BIO's independent-ground and forfeiture arguments are not frivolous. The joint-venture framing surfaced first in Judge Bush's statement, and the panel's "even apart from Illinois Brick" sentence gives the SG an easy reason to prefer the other vehicle.
- The state-law claims failed at summary judgment and respondents paint UAS as a serial litigant with a failed business model. That does not go to the legal question, but it shapes how sympathetic the SG and the Court find the plaintiff, and the SG's office has historically defended Illinois Brick's bright line (it sided with Apple in Apple v. Pepper).

**Arithmetic.** P(SG recommends grant somewhere on the issue) about 0.5; P(Court takes the issue | SG recommends grant) about 0.85; P(Court takes it anyway | SG recommends denial) about 0.15; so P(issue taken) about 0.50. Times 0.8 for this petition's share of that mass gives about 0.40. I round to 0.42 to reflect the paired-CVSG strength beyond what the raw CVSG cut captures. Predicted disposition is `denied` because 0.42 is below one half; `granted` is 0.

**Claims.** `disposition` restates 0.42. `relist-increment` 0.96: the CVSG guarantees a redistribution once the SG files, and only withdrawal or settlement prevents it. `cvsg-increment` 0.02: the invitation is already on the docket (context `cvsg_date` 2026-10-05), so the harness masks this claim as vacuous for the cell; the number is a formal statement that no second invitation issues. `summary-disposition-route` 0.30 conditional on grant: the held-then-GVR path after a plaintiff-side win in Mosaic Health is roughly a third of my grant-family mass, and no intervening decision supports a GVR today. `dissent-from-denial` 0.20 conditional on denial: antitrust-standing denials rarely draw writings, but a flagged circuit split after a CVSG raises it above the base.

**Big case score 0.45.** The scope of Illinois Brick matters to every antitrust plaintiff outside direct privity and to the business community, and a grant would be the Court's first look at the doctrine since Apple v. Pepper. It is not a case the general public will follow.

**Uncertainty and where to discount me.** The whole forecast rides on the SG's recommendation, which will not arrive for months, and on the Court's choice between two vehicles, which no base rate in the statpack conditions on. I did not read the Sixth Circuit opinion itself, only the parties' characterizations of it, so my read of whether the speculative-damages passage is an independent holding is inference from adversarial filings. The corpus queries I ran returned recency-ranked priors with no CVSG filter and did not materially move the number.
