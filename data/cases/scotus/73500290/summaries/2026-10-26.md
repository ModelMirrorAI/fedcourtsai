## In brief

United Allergy Services asks the Court to revive its antitrust suit claiming rivals conspired to drive it from the Tennessee allergy-care market.

## What happened

United Allergy Services (UAS) supplies equipment and technicians to primary care doctors who offer allergy testing and treatment, competing with Amerigroup and an allergy practice known as the Center. UAS sued them, claiming they conspired to push it out of the Tennessee market by pressuring doctors who worked with UAS. A federal district court dismissed UAS's antitrust claims, finding UAS could not sue because it was not a direct competitor, though related state-law claims proceeded and later lost at summary judgment. The Sixth Circuit Court of Appeals affirmed, ruling UAS's antitrust claims failed under a Supreme Court rule limiting who can sue over harms that pass through a chain of buyers and sellers, and separately because UAS's claimed losses were too speculative.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked whether a legal rule that normally stops a business from suing over losses passed down a chain of buyers and sellers also blocks a company that was itself the direct, intended target of a boycott from suing to recover its own lost profits, even though another business (the doctors) stood closer to the alleged wrongdoers in that chain.

## Where it stands

The petition has been fully briefed, and the case was distributed for the Court's September 28, 2026 conference. On October 5, 2026, the Court invited the Solicitor General to file a brief giving the federal government's views before deciding whether to hear the case.

## What each outcome would mean

United Biologics, LLC (UAS) is asking the Court to take up its case and reverse the Sixth Circuit's ruling. If the Court eventually grants review, it would hear full arguments on whether UAS can sue despite not being the most direct target of the alleged conspiracy, deciding nothing yet about who is right. If the Court denies review, the Sixth Circuit's decision stands, leaving UAS's federal antitrust claims dismissed and Amerigroup and the Center's win in place. The pending call for the Solicitor General's views means the Court has not yet decided whether to grant or deny the petition.
