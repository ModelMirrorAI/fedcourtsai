## In brief

United Allergy Services asks the Court to revive its antitrust lawsuit against insurers and a medical group it says conspired to drive it from the allergy-care market.

## What happened

United Allergy Services (UAS) supplies equipment and technicians so primary-care doctors can offer allergy testing and treatment. UAS sued Amerigroup and several Tennessee medical-practice defendants, alleging they conspired to push UAS out of the market by pressuring the doctors who worked with it. A federal district court dismissed UAS's antitrust claims, ruling UAS could not sue because it was not the direct, immediate victim of the alleged conduct; UAS's related state-law claims later failed at summary judgment. The Sixth Circuit Court of Appeals affirmed, and the full court declined to rehear the case, though some judges wrote separately questioning the result.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked whether a legal rule that normally stops indirect, downstream buyers from suing over a single antitrust overcharge should also block a business that says it was the deliberate target of a group boycott from suing for its own lost profits, when the boycott was carried out by pressuring that business's customers rather than the business itself.

## Where it stands

The petition has been fully briefed and was distributed for the Court's September 28, 2026 conference; the docket does not record what happened at that conference. On October 5, 2026, the Court invited the Solicitor General to file a brief giving the federal government's views on the case.

## What each outcome would mean

United Allergy Services, as the petitioner, asked the Court to take up the case. If the Court grants review, it will hear full arguments and decide the legal question itself, which would not by itself determine who wins. If the Court denies review, the Sixth Circuit's decision stands, leaving in place the dismissal of UAS's antitrust claims and the earlier judgment for Amerigroup and the other defendants. Because the Court has invited the Solicitor General's views, a decision on whether to hear the case is likely still some time away.
