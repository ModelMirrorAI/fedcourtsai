## What happened

Latino voters in Washington's Yakima Valley sued state officials, arguing that state legislative district LD-15 diluted Hispanic voting strength in violation of the Voting Rights Act. Two individuals, Jose Trevino and state representative Alex Ybarra, intervened to defend LD-15. A federal district court ruled the district unlawful and adopted a replacement map that lowered the district's Hispanic voting-age population somewhat. Trevino and Ybarra appealed, but the state itself did not. The Ninth Circuit held that the two intervenors lacked standing to challenge the liability ruling or to bring their own vote-dilution claim against the new map, though Trevino could challenge the new map as racially discriminatory; on that claim, the court found race did not drive the map's design, so heightened judicial review did not apply.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked two things: first, whether a voter moved into a new district, and a lawmaker whose district was reshaped by a court-ordered map, have the right to challenge either that map or the earlier ruling that led to it, even though the officials who administer elections chose not to appeal. Second, whether a redistricting map adopted to fix unlawful vote dilution must meet the toughest constitutional test for race-based government action whenever race played any role in shaping it, rather than only when race was the main driving factor.

## Where it stands

It was distributed for the Court's conference on April 2, 2026, and after a request for a response and further filings, including a denied motion to expedite, it was distributed again for the conference of September 28, 2026, where the Court will decide whether to take up the case.
