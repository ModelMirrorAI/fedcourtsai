## In brief

The Supreme Court vacated a Ninth Circuit ruling against voters challenging a Washington legislative map, sending the case back for reconsideration in light of a newer decision.

## What happened

Hispanic voters sued Washington over a state legislative district, Legislative District 15, claiming it diluted Latino voting strength in violation of the Voting Rights Act. A federal district court agreed, struck down the district, and adopted a replacement map. Jose Trevino and Alex Ybarra, who had intervened to defend the original district, appealed, arguing they had the right to challenge both the liability ruling and the new map, and that the new map itself was a racial gerrymander. The Ninth Circuit ruled against them, finding they mostly lacked standing to appeal and that the replacement map did not improperly rely on race. The state and the Secretary of State did not appeal.

## What the Court is being asked

The case asks whether voters and a legislator affected by a court-ordered remedial redistricting map have the legal right to challenge that map, and the liability finding behind it, in court. It also asks whether the new map should have faced the toughest level of judicial review because race played some role in drawing it.

## Where it stands

The Supreme Court granted the petition, vacated the Ninth Circuit's judgment, and sent the case back for further consideration in light of its recent decision in a related Louisiana redistricting case.

## What each outcome would mean

Trevino and Ybarra, who intervened to defend the original district, asked the Supreme Court to overturn the Ninth Circuit's decision, which had favored Soto Palmer and the other voters who successfully challenged the district. By vacating and remanding, the Court set aside the Ninth Circuit's decision without deciding who is right. The case now returns to the lower courts, which must reconsider the standing and gerrymandering questions in light of the newer ruling, with the outcome still undetermined for either side.
