## In brief

The Court vacated and sent back a Washington redistricting dispute over two voters' standing and a map's legality, instructing the lower court to reconsider in light of a newer ruling.

## What happened

Latino voters sued Washington over a state legislative district, LD-15, claiming it diluted Hispanic voting strength under the Voting Rights Act. A federal district court agreed, struck down LD-15, and adopted a replacement map. Two people who had intervened in the case to defend the original district, Jose Trevino and Alex Ybarra, appealed both the ruling against LD-15 and the replacement map, also arguing the new map was itself a racial gerrymander. The Ninth Circuit held they lacked standing to challenge most of this and rejected their gerrymandering claim on the merits. They then asked the Supreme Court to review that decision.

## What the Court is being asked

The case asks whether a voter moved into a new district, and a legislator whose district was redrawn, have enough personal stake to challenge a court-ordered map or the underlying finding that triggered it, and whether a map drawn to fix vote dilution must meet the strictest constitutional test when race was considered in drawing it.

## Where it stands

The Supreme Court granted the petition, vacated the Ninth Circuit's judgment, and sent the case back for further consideration in light of a recent decision in another case, Louisiana v. Callais.

## What each outcome would mean

Trevino and Ybarra, who sought review, do not get a final ruling in their favor from the Supreme Court. Instead, the Ninth Circuit's decision against them is set aside, and that court must reconsider the case taking the new Callais ruling into account. This does not decide who is right on standing or the map's legality; the Ninth Circuit could reach the same or a different result after reviewing the case again, and further proceedings would follow there.
