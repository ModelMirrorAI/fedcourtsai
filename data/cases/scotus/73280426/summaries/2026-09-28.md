## In brief

Two voters who intervened to defend a Washington legislative district ask the Court to revive their challenge to a court-ordered remedial map and the voting-rights ruling that produced it.

## What happened

Latino voters sued Washington, claiming state legislative district LD-15 diluted Hispanic voting strength in violation of the Voting Rights Act. Jose Trevino and state representative Alex Ybarra intervened to defend the district and, later, to argue it was itself a racial gerrymander. The district court ruled for the challengers, found a violation, and adopted a remedial map that redrew LD-15 and neighboring districts. Trevino and Ybarra alone appealed; the state and its Secretary of State did not. The Ninth Circuit held that neither man had standing to challenge the underlying violation finding or to bring his own vote-dilution claim against the new map, though Trevino could challenge the new map as a racial gerrymander—a claim the court said he likely never properly raised, and which it rejected anyway because race was not the main factor in the map's design.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked whether a voter moved into a new district and a legislator whose district was reshaped by a court-ordered map may challenge that map, or the voting-rights ruling that led to it, in court at all. It is also asked whether a redistricting map adopted to fix a voting-rights violation must meet the strictest constitutional test whenever race played any role in shaping it, rather than only when race was the main factor.

## Where it stands

The petition has been fully briefed, including a response opposing review and a reply, and the Court has requested a response from the state respondents. It was distributed for the Court's conference of September 28, 2026; the docket does not yet show any decision on it.

## What each outcome would mean

Trevino and Ybarra, who lost in the Ninth Circuit, are asking the Court to take up the case. If the Court grants review, it would hear the case and decide the standing and constitutional questions itself, without indicating who would ultimately win. If the Court denies review, the Ninth Circuit's decision stands: Trevino and Ybarra's challenges to the voting-rights finding and their own vote-dilution claims stay dismissed for lack of standing, and the ruling that the remedial map does not need the strictest scrutiny remains in place.
