# Forecast: what the Court will do with No. 25-918, Trevino v. Hobbs

**Bottom line.** The most likely single outcome is a grant, vacate, and remand
(GVR) to the Ninth Circuit "for further consideration in light of Louisiana v.
Callais," entered on or shortly after the October 5, 2026 order list following
the September 28 long conference. The next most likely outcome is a quiet
denial. Plenary review is unlikely.

**Relists.** The docket shows two distributions: for the April 2, 2026
conference (after which the Court called for a response) and, once briefing
closed, for the September 28, 2026 conference. The second is a post-response
redistribution, not a relist in the classic sense. From here I expect the
petition to be disposed of off the long conference or after at most one relist.
A GVR usually issues without a relist; a denial that draws a separate writing,
or a disagreement inside the Court over whether Callais reaches an
intervenor-standing holding, would add one or two. Roughly one chance in three
of at least one further distribution.

**CVSG.** None. The Solicitor General has no stake in a state legislative map
and the Court has already spoken on Section 2 this year; it will not slow this
petition down to ask.

**Route if granted.** Summary disposition, not argument. The State of
Washington, the respondent that administers the map, has asked for exactly
that: a GVR so the Ninth Circuit can apply Callais first. The Court GVR'd three
Section 2 matters in light of Callais in May 2026 (Allen v. Caster, Turtle
Mountain Band v. Howe, and the Mississippi State Board of Election
Commissioners case), including over Justice Jackson's objection that Callais
did not address the question actually decided below. That pattern fits this
petition. The companion Garcia v. Hobbs petition (No. 25-901), on the same
conference, will travel with it: the State asked for a matching GVR there.

**Which question, if plenary.** If the Court did set the case for argument it
would take a reformulated version of QP 1 (whether a voter re-sorted by a
court-drawn Section 2 remedial map may challenge the antecedent liability
judgment when the State does not appeal) and leave QP 2 alone, since Callais
reaffirmed that a racial-gerrymandering claim requires racial predominance and
the Ninth Circuit found none on a map its drawer built without racial data.
I put this at about one in twenty.

**If denied.** Expect no noted dissent. The grounds for denial would be the
threshold ones the Soto Palmer respondents press: permissive intervenors held
to lack standing to appeal a liability judgment, a forfeited equal-protection
theory, and a remedial map drawn race-blind. Those are not grounds a Justice
writes separately about. A statement from Justice Thomas or Justice Alito on
"dilution as a remedy" is conceivable but unlikely.

**On remand (conditional on GVR).** The Ninth Circuit will most likely
reinstate its standing holding as to the liability judgment, since Callais does
not speak to intervenor standing, and reconsider only Trevino's
equal-protection challenge to the remedial map under Callais's discussion of
when Section 2 compliance can justify race-conscious districting. The remedial
map is likely to survive that reconsideration.
