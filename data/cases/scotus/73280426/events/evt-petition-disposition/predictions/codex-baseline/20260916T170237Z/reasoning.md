## Why 30% rather than 17% or a likely grant

**Upward evidence.** The Court affirmatively requested responses on March 25 after both sets of respondents waived response. That is more informative than routine distribution alone. The petition challenges a separation between standing to contest liability and standing to contest the resulting remedy, invokes Bost on candidate injury, and expressly anticipates possible reconsideration after Callais. The Ninth Circuit decision predates both authorities as described in the briefs. That creates a credible, relatively inexpensive GVR route even if this is a poor vehicle for plenary review. These considerations lift my grant-family estimate above the elevated risk-set anchor.

**Downward evidence.** This is not a demonstrated clean circuit split. The petition characterizes the Ninth Circuit's result as unique and argues conflict with Supreme Court principles, while the opposition disputes the factual premise of concrete uncontested injury. The sovereign defendants did not appeal, so the private intervenors must establish their own appellate standing. The opposition argues that Ybarra's electoral injury is unsupported and distinguishes Bost's vote-counting context from an unopposed candidate's challenge to district boundaries. It also identifies likely forfeiture of Trevino's racial-gerrymandering challenge, although the Ninth Circuit elected to address the merits. These are substantial vehicle objections, not proof that petitioners' substantive position is necessarily wrong. Sources: petition printed pp. 15–26; opposition printed pp. 1–2, 11–15, 23–31.

The questions presented seek both standing review and strict scrutiny for a race-conscious remedial map. The petition treats the remedy's VRA purpose and attention to the Latino community as evidence of unconstitutional racial classification. The opposition says the mapdrawer used no racial data, the court selected the map for traditional criteria, and predominance remains the governing threshold. Its further contention that the evidence satisfies Callais is advocacy, not an independently verified factual finding adopted here. These competing descriptions make the broad second question less clean than its abstract formulation suggests. Sources: `questions-presented.txt`; petition printed pp. 24–28; opposition printed pp. 31–35.

The second distribution is not strong evidence of a second completed merits discussion: the first preceded the call for responses, and the next scheduled conference remains in the future. Nor do the months since filing establish a hold for another case; response extensions and the conference calendar explain considerable delay. I retain the frozen elevated band but discount any inference that this is a repeatedly relisted petition with sustained substantive consideration.

Balancing a meaningful intervention signal and an intervening-precedent remand route against the standing, preservation, and record obstacles yields **P(any grant) = 0.30**. The modal disposition is denial. The 80% summary-route probability is conditional on the grant, so approximately **0.24** of unconditional probability is assigned to a cert-order disposition and **0.06** to a grant opening plenary proceedings. Those are judgmental allocations, not estimated frequencies from the pack's historically unstable granted/GVR label split.
