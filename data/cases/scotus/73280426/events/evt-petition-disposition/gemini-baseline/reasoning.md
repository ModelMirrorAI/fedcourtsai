# Reasoning

This case involves a challenge to a court-drawn, race-conscious remedial redistricting map in Washington State (LD-15) under the Equal Protection Clause, along with a threshold question of whether the intervenors have standing to challenge it. The Ninth Circuit ruled that the intervenors lacked standing and that the map did not violate the Equal Protection Clause because race was not the "predominant" factor.

The Court requested a response, indicating initial interest. The "elevated" salience band reflects this requested response and the nature of the issue (Voting Rights Act / redistricting). However, the standing issue poses a significant vehicle problem. If the Ninth Circuit correctly determined that the intervenors lack standing, the Supreme Court cannot reach the merits of the Equal Protection claim. Often, the Court prefers to address these constitutional issues in cases with clean vehicle postures. 

While the conservative majority of the Court has shown a strong interest in curbing the use of race in redistricting (e.g., *Alexander v. SC NAACP*), the standing hurdle here decreases the likelihood of a grant. I assign a 15% probability of a grant (`probability: 0.15`), which translates to a `granted: 0` (deny) prediction.

As the case is distributed for the "long conference" on September 28, 2026, there is a reasonable chance it will be relisted if any Justice is considering a dissent from denial or if they are discussing the standing issue further, so I assign a 35% probability to `relist-increment`. A CVSG is very unlikely (5%) given this is a challenge to a state redistricting map, an area where the federal government's views are well known and often less dispositive at the cert stage than in commercial or federal regulatory cases.
