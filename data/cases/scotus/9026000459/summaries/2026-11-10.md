## In brief

Marshall Grace asks the Court to review whether an appeals court could itself decide a disputed jury-selection issue instead of sending it back to the trial judge.

## What happened

Marshall Grace was convicted of federal drug conspiracy charges after a trial in which prosecutors struck a Black potential juror, Edward Davis, using a peremptory challenge. Grace objected under the rule against race-based jury strikes. The trial judge, rather than ruling on the prosecutor's stated reason, questioned Davis directly and then denied the objection based on information the prosecutor had not known when exercising the strike. On appeal, the Fifth Circuit agreed the trial judge had used the wrong approach, but rather than sending the case back, the appeals court itself decided the prosecutor's original reason was not shown to be a pretext for discrimination, and upheld the conviction. The full court denied rehearing.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked whether, when an appeals court finds that a trial judge failed to properly decide whether a juror strike was racially motivated, the appeals court may resolve that factual question itself, or whether it must instead send the case back to the trial judge to decide.

## Where it stands

The petition was filed and the government waived its right to respond, but the Court later requested a response, due November 27, 2026. The case was distributed for the Court's November 6, 2026 conference, and the docket shows no action recorded since.

## What each outcome would mean

Grace, who lost in the lower court, is asking the Court to take up his case. If the Court grants review, it would hear full arguments and decide whether appeals courts may make this kind of finding themselves; a grant would not by itself decide who wins. If the Court denies review, the Fifth Circuit's decision upholding Grace's conviction stands, and the case ends there. A denial would not mean the Court agrees with the lower court's approach, only that it leaves that ruling in place.
