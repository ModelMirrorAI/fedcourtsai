## In brief

A man convicted of drug crimes asks the Court to decide whether appeals courts, not just trial judges, may resolve disputed claims of racial bias in jury selection.

## What happened

Marshall Grace was convicted of methamphetamine trafficking after a trial in which prosecutors struck a Black prospective juror, Edward Davis. Grace objected that the strike was racially motivated. The trial judge, instead of ruling on the prosecutor's stated reason, questioned Davis directly and then upheld the strike on a different basis the prosecutor had never given. The Fifth Circuit agreed the judge had erred by doing this, but rather than sending the case back, it independently reviewed the trial record itself and decided the strike was not discriminatory, affirming the conviction. The full court denied rehearing.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked whether an appeals court, after finding that a trial judge failed to properly decide whether a juror was struck because of race, may decide that question itself by reviewing the record, or whether it must instead send the case back to the trial court, which heard and saw the witnesses, to make that finding first.

Grace is asking the Court to overturn the Fifth Circuit's ruling.
