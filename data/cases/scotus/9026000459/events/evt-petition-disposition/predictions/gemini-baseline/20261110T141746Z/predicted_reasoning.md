## 2. Question Presented and Conditional Route (`summary-disposition-route`: `0.714`)

- **Question Presented**: The petition presents a single question: *"May a federal appellate court, after concluding that a federal district court has failed to make necessary findings under step three of Batson v. Kentucky, 476 U.S. 79 (1986), proceed to make those findings in the first instance?"* If the Court were to grant plenary review, it would take the single question presented as written.
- **Summary Disposition vs. Plenary Review Conditional on Grant (`summary-disposition-route`: `0.714`)**: Conditional on the Court granting the petition (`P(grant) = 0.035`), a summary disposition—specifically a **grant, vacatur, and remand (GVR)** in light of *Pitchford v. Cain*, 608 U.S. 391 (2026)—is substantially likelier (`0.025 / 0.035 = 0.714`) than plenary review (`0.010 / 0.035 = 0.286`). The petition explicitly requests a GVR in the alternative because the Fifth Circuit denied rehearing on May 5, 2026, and *Pitchford* was decided on May 28, 2026. Nonetheless, even a GVR is unlikely in absolute terms (`2.5%`) because *Pitchford* addressed state-court waiver under 28 U.S.C. § 2254(d) where a trial judge twice cut off defense counsel from arguing *Batson* step three, whereas Grace's counsel had a full opportunity to argue pretext and the Fifth Circuit affirmed on the merits of step three.
- **Conditional Merits Ground (if granted for plenary review)**: If the Court were to grant plenary review, the likely ground of decision would be narrow: clarifying whether, when a district court deviates from *Batson* step three by questioning a venireperson *sua sponte*, an appellate court may affirm on the ground that the challenger failed as a matter of law to carry his step-three burden of persuasion on the pre-colloquy record, or whether *Pullman-Standard v. Swint*, 456 U.S. 273 (1982), requires a remand unless only one finding is permissible on the record.
