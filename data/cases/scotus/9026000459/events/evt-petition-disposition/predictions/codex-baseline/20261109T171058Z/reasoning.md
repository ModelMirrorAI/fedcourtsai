Cain*, decided May 28, 2026, after the March appellate judgment and May 5 rehearing denial. I read the retrieved Court opinion's syllabus and opening discussion, not just the petition's description. ## Why denial nevertheless leads

The Fifth Circuit does not explicitly announce an across-the-board rule permitting first-instance factfinding. It introduces clear-error review, emphasizes Grace's burden, and could be characterized by the government as affirming a sufficiently supported ultimate ruling rather than replacing missing findings. The district judge did permit defense argument; *Pitchford*'s actual opportunity-to-rebut and waiver holdings therefore do not squarely dictate relief here. Some purported split cases may turn on distinct records or on whether only one conclusion was possible. The petition's stronger characterization remains advocacy, not an uncontested premise.

The opposition is not yet available, so I cannot resolve whether the precise remand theory was sufficiently presented below, whether the original rationale was expressly rejected rather than merely questioned, or whether the government can defend the result without independent credibility findings. The appendix supports preservation of a Batson objection, but that alone does not establish preservation of every appellate-remedy argument. No amici or companion grant reinforce the request. These limitations keep the estimate well below even odds despite the substantial upward adjustment.
