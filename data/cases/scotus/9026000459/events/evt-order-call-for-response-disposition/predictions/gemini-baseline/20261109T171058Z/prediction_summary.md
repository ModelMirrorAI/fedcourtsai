The Supreme Court is very likely to deny Marshall Grace's petition for review, with only about an 11% chance (roughly one in nine) of granting it or sending the case back to the lower court. During jury selection at Grace's federal drug-trafficking trial, the trial judge doubted the prosecutor's reason for removing a Black prospective juror—a challenge governed by *Batson v. Kentucky*, which forbids race-based jury strikes—and improperly questioned the juror himself before allowing the strike. Although the Court asked the federal government to file a response to Grace's petition, the trial transcript shows that the judge explicitly rejected the claim of racial targeting and that the juror stated during questioning that "only God can judge people" and failed to disclose his son's prior arrest, making the case a poor vehicle for Supreme Court intervention.
