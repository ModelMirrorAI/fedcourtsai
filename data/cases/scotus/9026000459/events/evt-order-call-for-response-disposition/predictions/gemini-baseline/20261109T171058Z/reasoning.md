## 3. Case-Specific Adjustments from the 5.16% Anchor

### Upward Factors (lifting P(grant or GVR) from ~5.2% to 11.0%)

- **Call for a Response after SG waiver:** The October 28, 2026 CFR shows active interest from at least one chamber before the first conference.
- **Supreme Court specialist counsel and clean legal framing:** Petitioner is represented by Paul W. Hughes (McDermott Will & Schulte LLP), who frames a concrete 4–2 circuit split on a pure question of appellate procedure: when a district court fails to make a valid credibility/pretext finding at step three of *Batson v. Kentucky*, 476 U.S. 79 (1986), must the court of appeals remand (as the Second, Fourth, Sixth, and Seventh Circuits hold under *Pullman-Standard v. Swint*, 456 U.S. 273 (1982)), or may it conduct step-three factfinding in the first instance (as the Ninth Circuit held in *United States v. Alvarez-Ulloa*, 784 F.3d 558 (9th Cir. 2015), and as petitioner argues the Fifth Circuit did here)?
- **Acknowledged trial-court *Batson* error below:** In a published opinion (`United States v. Grace`, No. 24-30730, decided March 27, 2026; App. 1a–12a), the Fifth Circuit panel (Stewart, J., joined by Jones and Willett, JJ.) expressly held that the district judge "strayed from Supreme Court and Fifth Circuit precedent" by questioning Juror 8 (Edward Davis) *sua sponte* at the bench "in an erroneous attempt to find a race-neutral reason on the Government's behalf" after initially stating that the prosecutor's proffered reason (that Davis had seven sons) was unconvincing (App. 9a–11a, 15a–23a).
- **Intervening decision in *Pitchford v. Cain*, 608 U.S. 391 (May 28, 2026):** Just three weeks after the Fifth Circuit denied rehearing in *Grace* (May 5, 2026), this Court reversed the Fifth Circuit in *Pitchford v. Cain*, holding that the trial court's failure to conduct *Batson*'s third step required relief and reiterating that "the job of enforcing *Batson* rests first and foremost with trial judges." That chronological sequence (rehearing denied May 5, *Pitchford* decided May 28) is a classic trigger for a chamber to call for a response from the Solicitor General to see whether the government acquiesces in a GVR or remand.

### Downward / Vehicle Factors (keeping P(grant or GVR) at 11.0% and favoring denial at 88.0%)

A close reading of `decision-below.txt` and the complete bench-conference transcript in `appendix.txt` (App. 15a–24a) reveals three substantial vehicle flaws that the Solicitor General is virtually certain to highlight in the forthcoming Brief in Opposition:

1. **The Fifth Circuit did not claim authority to make *de novo* credibility findings on a disputed record:** Unlike the Ninth Circuit in *Alvarez-Ulloa*, the Fifth Circuit did not announce a rule that appellate courts may resolve disputed prosecutor demeanor or credibility questions in the first instance. Instead, the panel relied on two case-specific points (App. 11a–12a):
   - First, the district court *had* expressly rejected Grace's step-three argument that the government was targeting Black venirepersons based on strike percentages: when defense counsel argued that striking two of the Black venirepersons showed the prosecutors "unfairly targeted black people," the district judge stated on the record, *"Well, I disagree with that. They're not. I disagree with that."* (App. 18a).
   - Second, once the prosecutor gave a facially race-neutral reason at step two (that Davis had seven sons and virtually nothing else was known about him), the burden of persuasion at step three rested on Grace (*Purkett v. Elem*, 514 U.S. 765, 768 (1995)), and defense counsel offered no comparative juror analysis or other evidence of pretext—merely conjecturing that Davis looked old enough that his sons were not young children (App. 19a). Meanwhile, the district court's initial skepticism of the "seven sons" rationale explicitly conflated step two with step three (App. 16a, 19a).
2. **Damaging voir dire admissions by Juror 8:** During the bench conference, two facts emerged on the record that make this a deeply unattractive vehicle for plenary review:
   - When the district court asked Juror 8 (Davis) whether he could be fair and impartial as a juror in the case, Davis answered: *"Well, to me, you know, only God can judge people. You know, I can't really judge nobody. Only God can judge people."* (App. 22a).
   - Immediately after the court's ruling, the prosecutor noted for the record—and the district judge agreed—that the court had asked the venire twice during general voir dire whether they or any family members had been arrested, and Davis had remained silent despite his son having spent two-and-a-half years in jail and five years on probation on a gun charge, leading the district judge to state on the record: *"And he was less than candid."* (App. 23a–24a).
3. **Distinctions from *Pitchford v. Cain* and lack of amicus support:** *Pitchford* was a capital habeas case in which the trial judge twice cut off defense counsel from making a comparative-juror pretext argument at step three and the state supreme court found waiver. Here, defense counsel had full opportunity to speak both before and after the bench examination (App. 15a–19a, 22a–23a), made no comparative-juror argument, and was not held to have waived anything. No amicus briefs have been filed in support of the petition.
