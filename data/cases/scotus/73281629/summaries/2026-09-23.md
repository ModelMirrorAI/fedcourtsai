## What happened

Mark Zavislak, a beneficiary of Netflix's employee health plan through his spouse, asked Netflix under a federal benefits law (ERISA) to turn over documents governing the plan, including contracts Netflix has with outside companies that process medical, dental, and vision claims. Netflix gave him several summary documents but refused to hand over the four claims-processing contracts, saying those only govern Netflix's relationship with its vendors, not participants' benefits. After a bench trial, a federal district court agreed with Netflix, and the United States Court of Appeals for the Ninth Circuit affirmed. Zavislak asked for rehearing, which was denied. He then asked the Supreme Court to review the case.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked whether a contract between an employer's benefit plan and the outside company that processes claims counts as one of the plan's governing documents that must be disclosed to beneficiaries who request it, or whether such contracts can be withheld because they mainly describe administrative arrangements rather than spelling out what benefits members are entitled to. Zavislak's petition says other appeals courts require disclosure of this kind of contract and that the Ninth Circuit is out of step with them; Netflix's brief in opposition disputes that any such disagreement among the courts exists and says the lower court's decision was correct and unremarkable.

## Where it stands

The petition has been distributed for conference twice, most recently for the September 28, 2026 conference, after the Court asked Netflix for a response and received Netflix's opposition brief and Zavislak's reply. The case awaits the Court's decision on whether to grant review.
