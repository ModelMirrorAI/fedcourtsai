## In brief

The Court declined to hear a benefits plan beneficiary's bid to force Netflix to disclose certain administrative contracts under a federal pension and benefits law.

## What happened

Mark Zavislak, a beneficiary of Netflix's health plan through his spouse, asked Netflix for documents governing the plan, including contracts between Netflix and its claims administrators. Netflix gave him seven plan documents but refused to turn over four claims administration agreements, saying those contracts only addressed Netflix's business relationships with outside administrators, not participants' benefits. Zavislak sued. After a bench trial, the district court ruled for Netflix, and the Ninth Circuit affirmed, applying its own prior rulings on what counts as a disclosable plan document.

## What the Court is being asked

The case asks whether a federal law requiring benefit plan administrators to disclose contracts and instruments that establish or operate a plan also covers contracts that merely govern how outside companies process and administer benefit claims, rather than contracts that define what benefits participants are entitled to.

## Where it stands

The petition was distributed for conference twice, most recently in September 2026, after full briefing including a reply from Zavislak. On October 5, 2026, the Court denied the petition.

## What each outcome would mean

Zavislak, the beneficiary, asked the Court to take up the case and overturn the Ninth Circuit's ruling in favor of Netflix. By denying the petition, the Court leaves the Ninth Circuit's decision in place: Netflix was not required to disclose the four claims administration agreements, and Zavislak's lawsuit seeking them does not proceed further. The denial does not mean the Court agreed the lower court was right; it simply leaves that ruling standing.
