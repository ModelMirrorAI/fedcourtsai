## In brief

The Court declined to hear a benefits plan beneficiary's bid to force Netflix to disclose contracts with its claims administrators.

## What happened

Mark Zavislak, a beneficiary of Netflix's employee health plan, asked Netflix in 2021 for documents governing the plan's operation, including contracts with its claims administrators. Netflix provided summary documents but not the four claims administration agreements Zavislak wanted. He sued, arguing federal benefits law required their disclosure. After a bench trial, the district court ruled the agreements did not have to be disclosed, finding they governed only Netflix's relationships with its service providers, not participants' benefits. The Ninth Circuit affirmed in an unpublished decision.

## What the Court is being asked

The case asks whether a contract that governs how benefit claims are administered counts as one of the governing documents that a benefits plan must hand over to a beneficiary who requests it under federal pension and benefits law. Zavislak's petition argued the Ninth Circuit uses a different, narrower test for what counts than other appeals courts, while Netflix answered that no real disagreement among the courts exists and that the agreements here were correctly found not to control participants' benefits.

## Where it stands

The petition was distributed for two conferences, with Netflix first waiving and then filing a response after the Court requested one, and further briefing followed. The docket shows the Court denied the petition on October 5, 2026.

## What each outcome would mean

Zavislak, the plan beneficiary, asked the Court to take up his case after losing in the lower courts. The denial means the Court will not review the question, leaving the Ninth Circuit's ruling in place: Netflix was not required to disclose the claims administration agreements to Zavislak. The denial does not mean the Court agreed the lower court was correct, only that it left that decision standing.
