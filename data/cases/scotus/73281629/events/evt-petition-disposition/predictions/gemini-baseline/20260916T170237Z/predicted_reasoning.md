Distributed twice so far (once before the BIO was requested, and once for the September 28 long conference after full briefing). Expect at least one further relist before a decision on the petition, as cases emerging from the long conference that draw serious attention are often relisted at least once to finalize the grant or a dissent from denial. A CVSG is possible given that the question presented involves the interpretation of an ERISA disclosure provision (29 U.S.C. 1024(b)(4)) where the Department of Labor might have an interest, but the Court may also just grant it outright given the acknowledged 1-9 circuit split.

If granted, the Court would likely take the single question presented as written to resolve the lopsided split between the Ninth Circuit's "information test" and the strict textual approach of nine other circuits. A summary GVR is unlikely because there is no obvious intervening Supreme Court decision that bears on the split. Should the Court deny the petition, there is a moderate chance of a dissent from denial, given the depth of the split and the affirmative act of requesting a response from the respondent.
