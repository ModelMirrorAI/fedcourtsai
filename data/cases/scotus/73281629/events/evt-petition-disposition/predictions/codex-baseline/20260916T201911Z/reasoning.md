## Why 16%, rather than the anchor or a large split premium

The petition's strongest feature is a concrete, recurring statutory question. Its QP and argument at printed pages 6–11 contrast the Ninth Circuit's beneficiary-information approach with other circuits' treatment of governing contracts, especially the Seventh Circuit's Mondry and the Tenth Circuit's Premera. The appellate memorandum at appendix pages 4a–5a actually applies the information test and rejects classification by document type. This is more substantial than a bare disagreement over whether a particular benefits claim should have been paid. The April 24 request for a response after waiver is also affirmative attention, although not equivalent to four votes for review. These considerations support keeping the probability materially above the ordinary petition population.

The opposition at printed pages 6–16 contests both the purported nine-to-one methodological split and the narrower disagreement about claims-administration agreements. Its vehicle objection has support in the reproduced memorandum: this is an unpublished decision applying circuit precedent to document-specific findings after a bench-trial procedure. The panel distinguishes contracts governing provider relationships from terms establishing participants' benefits or claim procedures, while acknowledging limited duplicative benefit provisions in the vision agreement. Those features permit the Court to view this as an application dispute rather than a clean categorical conflict.

The BIO further distinguishes Mondry and Premera by their denied-benefits settings and their records, and argues that disclosure tied to a benefits denial remains independently available. I treat those as the respondent's arguments, not as independently verified holdings that conclusively eliminate the split. Conversely, the petition's description of an acknowledged nine-circuit conflict is advocacy, not an established count of decisions answering this exact question. The memorandum's reliance on en banc precedent means its unpublished status does not erase the issue, but it does make the claimed square conflict less clean. Balancing credible textual tension and a requested response against these vehicle problems produces a modest net reduction from 17.22% to 16%.
