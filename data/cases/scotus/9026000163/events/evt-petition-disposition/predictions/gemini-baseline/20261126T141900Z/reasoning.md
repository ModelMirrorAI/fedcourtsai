## Case-Specific Adjustments

Starting from the `2.69%` zero-amicus `baseline` reference rate (and comparing against the `5.18%` unconditional `baseline` risk-set anchor), two genuine strengths push upward, but four substantial counterweights pull the forecast back to **`0.05` (5.0%)**:

### Upward Factors
- **Acknowledged circuit conflict on Question Presented 1:** Both sides agree—and the Solicitor General's opposition expressly concedes (BIO 10–11)—that the Second Circuit (*United States v. Svoboda*, 347 F.3d 471 (2d Cir. 2003); *United States v. Tang Yuk*, 885 F.3d 57 (2d Cir. 2018)) requires proof that it was reasonably foreseeable to the defendant that a co-conspirator's overt act would occur in the district of prosecution, whereas the Third, Fourth, Sixth, and Ninth Circuits reject a foreseeability limitation on overt-act conspiracy venue.
- **Elite Supreme Court representation and constitutional framing:** Petitioner is represented by Paul W. Hughes (McDermott Will & Schulte LLP) and Eugene R. Fidell with the Yale Law School Supreme Court Clinic, alongside the Federal Public Defender. Their framing ties Question Presented 1 to the Court's recent attention to the Venue and Vicinage Clauses in *Smith v. United States*, 599 U.S. 236 (2023), and *Abouammo v. United States*, 608 U.S. 597 (2026).

### Downward Factors
1. **Repeated, unanimous denials on the exact same stale 1-to-4 split:** The Second Circuit has stood alone since *Svoboda* in 2003 without a single additional circuit adopting its foreseeability gloss in over twenty years. As the opposition notes (BIO 12) and CourtListener records confirm, the Court has repeatedly denied certiorari on this exact split—including *Iossifov v. United States*, 143 S. Ct. 2562 (2023), *Renteria v. United States*, 139 S. Ct. 1326 (2019), *Kirk Tang Yuk v. United States*, 139 S. Ct. 342 (2018), *Gonzalez v. United States*, 568 U.S. 1214 (2013), and *Ebersole v. United States*, 546 U.S. 1139 (2006)—without a single recorded dissent or statement respecting denial.
2. ***Abouammo* (June 2026) cuts against petitioner's theory and complicates the statutory posture:** Full inspection of Justice Kagan's unanimous slip opinion in *Abouammo* (decided June 11, 2026, with zero separate concurrences) reveals three headwinds:
   - First, no Justice wrote separately in *Abouammo* to question *Hyde v. United States*, 225 U.S. 347 (1912), or *Whitfield v. United States*, 543 U.S. 209, 218 (2005); instead, the Court cited *Whitfield*'s overt-act conspiracy rule as settled law governing inchoate offenses (slip op. at 8).
   - Second, *Abouammo* squarely stated that *"This Court has never looked to a statute's mens rea elements in considering venue. Nor would it make much sense to do so"* (slip op. at 6), rejecting the Ninth Circuit's reliance on "contemplated effects"—reasoning in direct tension with petitioner's request to engraft a defendant-specific mens-rea/contemplation ("reasonable foreseeability") requirement onto constitutional venue.
   - Third, *Abouammo* footnote 3 expressly distinguished statutes where Congress enacted a specific venue provision, noting that Congress's specification may affect the constitutional analysis (slip op. at 5 n.3). Unlike 18 U.S.C. § 1519 in *Abouammo*, petitioner's conviction under 18 U.S.C. § 1956(h) is governed by an explicit congressional conspiracy-venue statute, **18 U.S.C. § 1956(i)(2)**, which authorizes prosecution "in any other district where an act in furtherance of the attempt or conspiracy took place" with no foreseeability requirement—yet petitioner barely confronts the constitutionality of invalidating or narrowing § 1956(i)(2) as an Act of Congress.
3. **Severe vehicle and outcome-determinativeness problems:**
   - The decision below is **unpublished and non-precedential** (with rehearing en banc denied without a poll), merely applying settled Fourth Circuit precedent (*United States v. Bowens*, 224 F.3d 302 (4th Cir. 2000)).
   - Question Presented 1 is entangled with Question Presented 2's fact-intensive *Jackson v. Virginia* challenge: petitioner's primary factual argument for why Western District of North Carolina venue was unforeseeable is that the June 2016 Charlotte real-estate wire into Uzomba's account belonged to a separate spoke rather than a single conspiracy with petitioner. Reviewing Question Presented 1 therefore risks bogging down in a disputed multi-day trial record where a properly instructed jury found a single ongoing business-email-compromise (BEC) money-laundering conspiracy run by common hub principals across rotating mule accounts.
   - Even if the Second Circuit's foreseeability standard applied, the government makes a credible showing (BIO 11–13) that venue would still be affirmable: petitioner laundered proceeds from interstate real-estate closing wires (including a $542,640.92 wire from Texas victims into his Florida account and further laundering through a mule he recruited in Georgia) while leaving victim targeting to his co-conspirators—facts under which the Second Circuit itself routinely finds out-of-district co-conspirator acts foreseeable (*Tang Yuk*, 885 F.3d at 70; *United States v. Rommy*, 506 F.3d 108, 123 (2d Cir. 2007)).
4. **Absence of amicus backing or GVR tail:** Despite Yale Supreme Court Clinic and experienced private counsel—who regularly marshal criminal-defense amici when targeting a clean vehicle—zero amicus briefs were filed (`cert_amicus_briefs = 0`). In addition, `record/same-question.json` confirms no parallel pending petitions (`petitions: []`), eliminating the hold-and-GVR pathway that accounts for roughly half of the grant-family base rate in zero-amicus criminal petitions. Question Presented 2 (*Kotteakos*) claims no circuit split and seeks pure fact-bound error correction of an unpublished sufficiency ruling, making summary reversal minimal (`~0.3%` unconditional).
