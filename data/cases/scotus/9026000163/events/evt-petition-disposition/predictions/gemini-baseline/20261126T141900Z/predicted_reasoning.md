# Predicted Court Action — *Pierre Yvelt Almonor v. United States* (No. 26-163)

## Disposition and Conference Trajectory

The Court will **deny** the petition for a writ of certiorari without further briefing or argument.

- **Further distributions (relists):** The docket currently records **one distribution** (distributed November 24, 2026, for the Conference of December 11, 2026) and zero relists. The petition will most likely resolve at its first scheduled conference without being relisted, with a secondary possibility of a single relist if one chambers conducts a closer check of the trial record or the interaction between *Abouammo v. United States*, 608 U.S. 597 (2026), and 18 U.S.C. § 1956(i)(2).
- **Call for the Views of the Solicitor General (CVSG):** The Court will **not** issue a CVSG. The United States is already the party respondent and the Solicitor General has already filed a Brief in Opposition on the merits of certiorari.

## Conditional Route If Granted

- **Plenary review over summary disposition:** Conditional on the Court granting review at all, plenary briefing and oral argument are substantially likelier than a summary disposition in the certiorari order.
  - A **GVR** in light of *Abouammo* is unlikely because *Abouammo* was decided nearly two months before the petition was filed, is already fully addressed in the certiorari briefing, expressly distinguished conspiracy's overt-act venue rule (*Whitfield v. United States*, 543 U.S. 209 (2005)), rejected mens-rea-based venue inquiries, and reserved express statutory venue provisions such as 18 U.S.C. § 1956(i)(2). Nor is any parallel merits case pending on the Court's docket for which this petition would be held.
  - A **summary reversal** on the second question presented under *Kotteakos v. United States*, 328 U.S. 750 (1946), is likewise unlikely because the Fourth Circuit's unpublished per curiam affirmance reviewed a properly instructed jury's single-conspiracy finding under *Jackson v. Virginia* across an eighteen-month multi-transaction money-laundering record.
- **Question presented if granted:** If the Court grants plenary review, it will limit the grant to **Question Presented 1**—whether the Venue and Vicinage Clauses (or 18 U.S.C. § 1956(i)(2) construed in light of them) require that a co-conspirator's overt act in the district of prosecution be reasonably foreseeable to the defendant—and leave behind **Question Presented 2**'s fact-bound sufficiency challenge to the single-conspiracy verdict. If granted on Question Presented 1, the likely merits ground would track historical and statutory overt-act conspiracy principles under *Hyde v. United States*, 225 U.S. 347 (1912), *Whitfield*, and § 1956(i)(2), or narrow review once the statutory venue text and record foreseeability are confronted.

## Separate Writings on Denial

Should the Court deny certiorari, the order will most likely issue **without any noted dissent or statement respecting the denial**, matching the Court's unadorned denials in *Iossifov v. United States* (2023), *Renteria v. United States* (2019), and *Kirk Tang Yuk v. United States* (2018). In the small tail event that a separate writing accompanies the denial, the likeliest authors are **Justice Gorsuch** or **Justice Thomas** (noting originalist tension between *Hyde*'s co-conspirator overt-act rule and the Venue and Vicinage Clauses while acknowledging vehicle complications in this record) or **Justice Sotomayor** or **Justice Jackson** (addressing conspiracy breadth and remote-venue fairness).
