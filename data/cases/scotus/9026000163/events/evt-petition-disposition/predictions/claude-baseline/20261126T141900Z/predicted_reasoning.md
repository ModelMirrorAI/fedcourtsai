# Forecast — Almonor v. United States, No. 26-163

The docket shows one distribution (for the Conference of December 11, 2026) and no relist. The Court will deny the petition at or shortly after that conference, without a further distribution. A relist is unlikely (about 7%); if one happens it would most plausibly signal a Justice drafting a statement about conspiracy venue or the Court weighing a GVR, not a plenary grant.

No CVSG will issue: the United States is the respondent and has already stated its position in a brief in opposition.

If the Court acted on the petition at all, the two realistic forms are (a) a GVR in light of *Abouammo v. United States*, 608 U.S. 597 (2026), which the petition and reply request in the alternative, or (b) plenary review of question 1 as written (whether a co-conspirator's overt act must be reasonably foreseeable to the defendant to support venue). Question 2 (the *Kotteakos* single-conspiracy / hub-and-spoke challenge), framed as a request for summary reversal, would not be taken; it is a fact-bound sufficiency claim. Conditional on any grant, a cert-order disposition (GVR) is nearly as likely as plenary review (about 45%), because the decision below is unpublished and predates *Abouammo*, but the government opposes a GVR and *Abouammo* by the government's account reaffirmed the overt-act venue rule for conspiracy.

On a denial, no separate writing is expected (about 2%). If anyone wrote, the likeliest author would be Justice Gorsuch, given his interest in jury-trial and vicinage rights, with Justices Sotomayor or Jackson next.
