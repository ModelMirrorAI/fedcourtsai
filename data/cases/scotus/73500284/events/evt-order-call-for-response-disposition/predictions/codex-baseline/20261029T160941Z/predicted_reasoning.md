# Forecast of the Court's action

The Court will most likely **deny certiorari without explanation or a separate writing**. The question of which law governs nonsignatory equitable estoppel under the New York Convention is substantial, but the Court will more likely decline this vehicle than use these domestic-insurer disputes to decide it.

The snapshot shows **one distribution**, for the September 28, 2026 conference, and a subsequent July 27 response request after waiver. The petition will receive another distribution after the requested response or expiration of the response period. I expect roughly **one routine redistribution**, with denial at the first effective consideration of the completed response-stage papers rather than a long sequence of conference relists. Additional substantive relists are possible, especially on the grant branch, but a second distribution is not by itself evidence that the Court has considered and relisted the case. This forecast concerns the next proceedings after the provisioned July state; it does not assert that nothing has happened by the October run date.

The Court will probably **not call for the Solicitor General's views**. If it does, that would most plausibly follow review of the requested response and would extend the petition's life into later 2026 or 2027. Without a CVSG or extended holding pattern, disposition in the fall 2026 conference cycle is the likelier timetable. The response itself is not in the record, so no argument or concession is attributed to it.

**If review is granted**, the Court will most likely take the petition's single question substantially as presented: whether federal common law or state law governs the equitable-estoppel inquiry for a nonsignatory seeking to enforce a Convention-covered arbitration agreement. A narrower framing could focus on domestic insurers seeking to invoke a foreign insurer's separate agreement. Ordinary merits briefing and argument are considerably more likely than a summary disposition; conditional on grant, I assign only **8%** to a judgment in the cert order itself. No identified intervening decision supplies an evident GVR trigger. A grant would not, by itself, establish entitlement to arbitration or decide the insurance claims.

**Conditional on denial**, I assign **6%** to any noted dissent or statement respecting denial. The central forecast is no writing. If there is one, Thomas or Gorsuch is a more plausible candidate than most colleagues, but no Justice's interest is disclosed in the snapshot and neither is an affirmative author prediction. No cert-stage vote lineup or majority-opinion author is forecast.
