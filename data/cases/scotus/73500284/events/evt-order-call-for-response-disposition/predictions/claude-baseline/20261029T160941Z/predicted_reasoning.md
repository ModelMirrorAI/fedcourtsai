# Forecast — Indian Harbor Insurance Co. v. Town of Vinton, No. 25-1383

**Starting state.** My provisioned snapshot ends on the call-for-response day (Jul 27, 2026). It shows one distribution, for the 9/28/2026 conference, which came after respondents waived a response. It also shows two amicus briefs supporting the petition and no CVSG. The Court's live docket has moved since then. The brief in opposition came on Aug 26 and the reply on Sep 9. On Oct 21 the petition was distributed again, for the November 6, 2026 conference. The petition is still pending.

**Disposition.** I expect a denial, but grant odds are well above the usual rate for a petition with a called-for response. I put the grant chance at about 27%.

**Relist.** A second distribution has already happened, so the masked `relist-increment` claim is all but settled. Past that, I expect the Court to act at or soon after the November 6 conference. Either it denies outright, or it relists once or twice before granting. A relist after November 6 would point toward a grant or a CVSG.

**CVSG.** The chance of a call for the Solicitor General's views is meaningful, at about 20%. The question is how a treaty (the New York Convention) and Chapter 2 of the FAA interact with state insurance law. In *GE Energy* the Court decided a closely related Convention question after asking for the government's views. A CVSG at the November conference would push the disposition into 2027.

**Question the Court would take.** If the Court grants, it would take the single QP as written: does federal common law or state law govern a nonsignatory's equitable-estoppel claim to enforce a Convention arbitration agreement? It might reformulate the QP to cover how 9 U.S.C. § 208 interacts with *Arthur Andersen v. Carlisle*.

**Summary disposition.** Unlikely. No intervening decision supports a GVR, and the question is one of first impression that calls for plenary review. Conditional on a grant, I put a summary route at about 3%.

**Separate writing on denial.** Unlikely, at about 4% conditional on a denial. This is a commercial arbitration choice-of-law case with no constitutional or criminal stakes that usually draw a dissent from denial. If anyone writes, Thomas, Gorsuch or Alito are the likeliest.

**If granted.** The likely ground would be whether § 208 carries *Arthur Andersen*'s state-law rule into Chapter 2 cases.
