## Conditional Route and Scope If Granted

- **Plenary Review vs. Summary Disposition (`summary-disposition-route: 0.12`)**:
  - Conditional on the 7% chance that the Court grants review, plenary merits review (`88%`) is far likelier than a summary disposition or GVR (`12%`). There is no pending Supreme Court merits case on the *GE Energy* choice-of-law question for which this petition could be held, and resolving a multi-circuit choice-of-law conflict left open in *GE Energy* would require full merits briefing and oral argument rather than summary reversal.
- **Which Question Presented the Court Would Take**:
  - If the Court were to grant certiorari, it would likely **limit the grant to Question Presented 2** (whether federal common law or state law determines whether a nonsignatory to an arbitration agreement governed by the New York Convention can invoke equitable estoppel) or take both questions with the focus on Question Presented 2. Question Presented 1—whether the policy's Contract Allocation Endorsement creates separate bilateral insurance contracts between the insured and each subscribing insurer—presents no circuit split, as both the Fifth Circuit below (App. 8a–9a) and the Second Circuit in *Certain Underwriters at Lloyds, London v. 3131 Veterans Blvd LLC*, 136 F.4th 404, 411 (2d Cir. 2025), interpret the standard-form endorsement the same way.
- **Conditional Merits Ground**:
  - Even if the Court granted review on Question Presented 2 and held that federal common law rather than state law governs equitable estoppel under Chapter 2 of the Federal Arbitration Act, the Court would likely either vacate and remand for the Fifth Circuit to apply federal common-law equitable-estoppel standards in the first instance (including the effect of the with-prejudice dismissal of the foreign insurers, the local-government status of the insureds, and the McCarran-Ferguson Act) or affirm on the alternative ground that dismissing the foreign insurers with prejudice left no foreign arbitration with which domestic litigation could conflict.
