## 2. Case-Specific Analysis of the Questions Presented and Vehicle Flaws

### A. Question Presented 1: No Circuit Split and Threshold Contract-Text Ground

Question Presented 1 asks whether an arbitration clause in an overarching policy subscribed to by both domestic and foreign insurers "falls under" the Convention on the Recognition and Enforcement of Foreign Arbitral Awards (`9 U.S.C. § 202`) when the insured dismisses the foreign insurers to litigate against the domestic insurers only.

1. **The Fifth Circuit's Decision Rests on Plain-Text Contract Construction**:
   Judge Ho's unanimous panel opinion (joined by Judges Haynes and Oldham, App. 1a–11a) did not hold that a party to a single indivisible multi-party international contract can unilaterally defeat the Convention by dropping foreign defendants. Instead, the panel held that under the policy's express **Contract Allocation Endorsement**—which states that `"[t]his contract shall be constructed as a separate contract between the Insured and each of the Underwriters"` and that each insurer's liability is strictly several and never joint (App. 8a, 21a–22a, 62a–63a)—the insured never had a single overarching contract with the domestic and foreign insurers in the first place, and under settled Fifth Circuit insurance-law principles an endorsement controls over conflicting general policy text (*Bennett v. Hartford Ins. Co. of Midwest*, 890 F.3d 597, 605 (5th Cir. 2018), App. 9a).
2. **Absence of Any Circuit Conflict**:
   As the Petition itself concedes (Pet. 21, 31), the only other circuit to address this standard-form surplus-lines allocation endorsement—the Second Circuit in *Certain Underwriters at Lloyds, London v. 3131 Veterans Blvd LLC*, 136 F.4th 404, 411 (2d Cir. 2025)—**agreed** with the Fifth Circuit that the endorsement creates separate bilateral contracts with each insurer, such that the domestic insurers' contracts are governed by Chapter 1 of the FAA (and reverse-preempted by `La. R.S. § 22:868(A)(2)` under the McCarran-Ferguson Act) rather than the Convention.

### B. Question Presented 2: Real Circuit Split Offset by Severe Vehicle and Preservation Defects

Question Presented 2 asks whether federal common law or state law determines whether a nonsignatory to an arbitration agreement governed by the Convention can use equitable estoppel to enforce the agreement.

1. **The Underlying Split Is Genuine**:
   In *GE Energy*, 590 U.S. at 445, this Court held that the New York Convention does not conflict with domestic equitable-estoppel doctrines, while expressly leaving open "which body of law governs that determination" (see also *id.* at 446 n.* (Sotomayor, J., concurring)). The First (*InterGen N.V. v. Grina*, 344 F.3d 134, 143 (1st Cir. 2003)), Second (*Smith/Enron Cogeneration L.P. v. Smith Cogeneration Int'l, Inc.*, 198 F.3d 88, 96 (2d Cir. 1999)), Fourth (*Aggarao v. MOL Ship Mgmt. Co.*, 675 F.3d 355, 373–75 (4th Cir. 2012)), and Ninth Circuits (*Setty v. Shrinivas Sugandhalaya LLP*, 3 F.4th 1166, 1168 (9th Cir. 2021)) apply federal common law to determine whether equitable estoppel applies under the Convention. By contrast, in *Bufkin Enterprises, L.L.C. v. Indian Harbor Insurance Co.*, 96 F.4th 726, 730 (5th Cir. 2024), and the decision below (App. 9a–11a), the Fifth Circuit held that state law governs equitable estoppel under *Arthur Andersen LLP v. Carlisle*, 556 U.S. 624, 631 (2009), and applied the Louisiana Supreme Court's intervening decision in *Police Jury of Calcasieu Parish v. Indian Harbor Insurance Co.*, 395 So. 3d 717, 730 (La. 2024), holding that `La. R.S. § 22:868(A)(2)` precludes domestic insurers from invoking equitable estoppel.
2. **Why This Case Is a Poor Vehicle for Question Presented 2**:
   Once Respondents file their Brief in Opposition, at least four independent vehicle flaws will weigh heavily against certiorari:
   - **Self-Inflicted Waiver / Estoppel in Prior Litigation**: In the district court proceedings in these consolidated cases, Petitioners themselves invoked the Fifth Circuit's state-law *Grigson* / *Bufkin* framework (App. 20a–21a, 25a, 65a–68a). Moreover, as Petitioners admit in Footnote 2 of the Petition (Pet. 13 n.2), when these very insurers briefed *Bufkin* in the Fifth Circuit (before the Louisiana Supreme Court rejected *Bufkin*'s *Erie* guess in *Calcasieu Parish*), **they argued that Louisiana state law governed equitable estoppel**. Having helped establish *Bufkin*'s state-law rule in the Fifth Circuit when they believed Louisiana law favored them, Petitioners are poorly positioned to complain after the Louisiana Supreme Court rejected their reading of Louisiana law.
   - **Dismissal with Prejudice and Full Release of Foreign Insurers**: All four Respondents dismissed the foreign insurers (Certain Underwriters at Lloyd's, London and HDI Global Specialty SE) **with prejudice** and released all claims against them, forfeiting the foreign insurers' several share (~17.5%) of the loss (App. 26a, 67a). As the district court explained in the Vinton ruling (App. 66a–68a), even under the *Grigson* intertwined-claims standard (the same standard Petitioners ask the Court to adopt as federal common law), equitable estoppel fails when the foreign signatories have been dismissed with prejudice and each insurer's liability is strictly several: there is no pending or potential arbitration with the foreign signatories that could be rendered meaningless.
   - **All Four Respondents Are Local Government Entities**: Each Respondent is a Louisiana political subdivision (Town of Vinton, Police Jury of Cameron Parish, Cameron Parish Recreation District #6, and School Board of Cameron Parish). Both Louisiana law (*Showboat Star Partnership v. Slaughter*, 789 So. 2d 554, 561–62 (La. 2001); `La. R.S. § 9:2778`; App. 25a & n.2, 68a) and federal common law (*OPM v. Richmond*, 496 U.S. 414, 419 (1990)) impose heightened barriers to asserting equitable estoppel against governmental entities, creating an alternative ground for affirmance specific to public bodies.
   - **McCarran-Ferguson Act Reverse-Preemption Complication**: Unlike standard international commercial disputes (*GE Energy*, *Setty*), this dispute involves domestic insurance policies where the McCarran-Ferguson Act (`15 U.S.C. §§ 1011–1012`) shields state insurance anti-arbitration laws (`La. R.S. § 22:868(A)(2)`) from federal statutory preemption. Whether federal courts can fashion a federal common-law equitable-estoppel doctrine under FAA Chapter 2 to allow domestic insurers (whose own separate contracts do not fall under the Convention) to override a state statute regulating insurance raises a thorny McCarran-Ferguson complication that would cloud any general ruling on *GE Energy*'s reserved question.
   - **Unanimous En Banc Denial and Closed Historical Cluster**: All 15 active judges on the Fifth Circuit denied rehearing en banc without a poll (App. 82a), and these suits stem from a finite set of 2020 Hurricane Laura/Delta claims in Louisiana.
