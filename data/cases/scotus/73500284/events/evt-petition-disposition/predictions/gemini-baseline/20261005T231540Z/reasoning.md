The petition presents a well-developed 4-1 circuit split over whether state or federal common law governs equitable estoppel for nonsignatories under the New York Convention, a question explicitly left open by the Supreme Court in *GE Energy Power Conversion France SAS, Corp. v. Outokumpu Stainless USA, LLC*. The Court requested a response on July 27, indicating serious interest, and the petitioner is represented by veteran Supreme Court counsel at Skadden, supported by two amicus briefs at the cert stage. 

However, the Brief in Opposition raises significant vehicle problems: the claims are state-law insurance claims between domestic parties over property damage in Louisiana; the respondents are public entities (which complicates the application of equitable estoppel); and the McCarran-Ferguson Act's protection of state insurance regulation adds complexity that is atypical for an international arbitration case. Furthermore, three of the four respondents never sued the foreign insurers in the first place, meaning the requested estoppel doctrine might not apply regardless of whether state or federal common law governs. Given these vehicle issues, the Court is likely to wait for a cleaner case involving actual foreign parties to resolve the Convention question. I adjust the baseline probability up significantly from the docket's base rate (~4% for a baseline paid petition) due to the clear split and the requested response, but keep it at 0.25 (overall predicted as denied) because of the vehicle flaws.
