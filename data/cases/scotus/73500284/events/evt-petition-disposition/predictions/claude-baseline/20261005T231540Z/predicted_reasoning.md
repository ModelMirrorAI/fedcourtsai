# Forecast: Indian Harbor Insurance Co. v. Town of Vinton, No. 25-1383

**Bottom line.** The Court will more likely deny than grant. P(any grant) = 0.20.
If it grants, it will set the case for briefing and argument on the question
presented as written, not dispose of it in the cert order.

## Where the docket stands, and what happens next

The docket shows **one distribution** (Jul 8, 2026, for the Sep 28 long
conference), entered after respondents waived. That distribution was overtaken
by the Court's **call for a response** on Jul 27. The brief in opposition came
in Aug 26 and the reply Sep 9. As of today the supremecourt.gov docket shows no
redistribution and no order, so the petition was not acted on at the long
conference.

- **Further distribution: near-certain (0.97).** The petition must be
  redistributed now that briefing is complete. Expect a "DISTRIBUTED for
  Conference" entry for a late-October or November 2026 conference. That entry
  is mechanical, not a relist in the deliberative sense. After that first real
  consideration I expect roughly a 35-40% chance of at least one genuine relist
  (deliberation over the vehicle objections, a possible CVSG, or a Justice
  considering a separate writing), so the likeliest terminal count is two
  distributions, with three plausible.
- **CVSG: possible but not likely (0.18).** The question is treaty-adjacent
  (what body of law fills the Convention's silence, and whether 9 U.S.C. § 208
  routes it through Chapter 1 and Arthur Andersen). The Solicitor General
  participated as amicus at the merits stage of GE Energy, and a Court unsure
  about the vehicle has an easy reason to ask. If a CVSG issues it would come
  at the first conference after redistribution, in November 2026, and push the
  disposition into spring 2027. More likely the Court decides the petition
  without one.
- **Timing.** Absent a CVSG, an order on the petition by December 2026 or
  January 2027.

## The disposition

**Denial is the likelier outcome (0.80).** The petition is well made: a
question the Court itself reserved in GE Energy, a published unanimous Fifth
Circuit opinion, en banc denied, elite counsel, two cert-stage amici (the
property-casualty insurers' trade association and a group of international
arbitration scholars), and a split that the BIO's own authority deepens, since
the Seventh Circuit's August 2026 Kim decision joins the Fifth in holding that
state law governs estoppel under Chapter 2. Those are the features that drew
the call for a response.

But the respondents' vehicle objections are the kind the Court takes
seriously. No live claim exists against either foreign insurer, so the
Convention reaches the case only through estoppel in the first place. Three of
the four respondents never sued the foreign insurers, which cuts against the
intertwined-claims form of estoppel even under the federal rule petitioners
want. The respondents are public entities, against whom estoppel runs
differently. And the insurance setting, with McCarran-Ferguson in the
background and a Louisiana statute voiding insurance arbitration clauses, makes
this an idiosyncratic case for announcing a general Convention choice-of-law
rule. The Court can also see a cleaner vehicle on the horizon in Kim itself,
which carries a reasoned Easterbrook opinion and no insurance overlay. The
likeliest path is a denial after one or two conferences, with the question
left to arrive again.

**Summary disposition is very unlikely (0.03 conditional on grant).** There is
no intervening decision to GVR in light of, and a reserved question on a
mature split is not summary-reversal material.

**Which question, if granted.** The question presented as written: whether
federal common law or state law governs a nonsignatory's equitable-estoppel
enforcement of an arbitration agreement under the Convention. The Court might
reframe it around 9 U.S.C. § 208 and whether Chapter 1's incorporation of
state law (Arthur Andersen) carries over, which is how both sides actually
argue it.

**Conditional merits.** If granted, the textual path the BIO maps (§ 208
imports Chapter 1 absent conflict; GE Energy found the Convention "simply
silent"; Hencely's demand for a significant conflict before federal common law
displaces state law) gives the Court a straightforward route to affirm, and
the Seventh Circuit has now written that route out. A reversal would rest on
Scherk's uniformity purpose. I would put affirmance ahead of reversal if the
Court takes the case, which is itself one reason the grant is less likely: the
Court grants more readily to correct than to confirm.

**Dissent from denial: unlikely (0.07).** This is not an issue with a
committed dissenter. If there is a writing it would be a short statement
respecting denial noting the split and inviting a better vehicle.
