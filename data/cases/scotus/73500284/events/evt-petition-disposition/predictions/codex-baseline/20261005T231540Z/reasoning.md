## Why 25%, rather than the 5.1% anchor

**Reasons to move upward.** The petition identifies a concrete choice-of-law question with consequences beyond these insurance claims, and alleges a four-to-one circuit disagreement. Its discussion at printed pages 15–24 explains why the choice changes whether the insurers can invoke foreign insurers' arbitration agreements. The requested response after waiver and the two amicus filings add evidence of attention and broader interest, though neither demonstrates four votes for review. The insurance association and arbitration scholars' briefs themselves were not provisioned; I infer no particular argument from their titles.

The legal uncertainty is real, not merely a petitioner's characterization. In *GE Energy Power Conversion France SAS, Corp. v. Outokumpu Stainless USA, LLC*, 590 U.S. 432, 445 (2020), the Court left both estoppel's application and the governing body of law for remand. I verified that passage through CourtListener opinion 9889180. In *Setty v. Shrinivas Sugandhalaya LLP*, 3 F.4th 1166, 1168–69 (9th Cir. 2021), the majority applied federal substantive law in the Convention setting, whereas Judge Bea's dissent favored the relevant domestic contract law. CourtListener opinion 4701830 confirms a genuine post-*GE Energy* disagreement about the approach. This is enough to increase the odds appreciably above an ordinary paid petition.

**Reasons not to make grant the favorite.** The asserted four-to-one split is not a verified count of materially identical post-*GE Energy* holdings. The opposition, printed pages 16–22, distinguishes older authorities and emphasizes that *Setty* involved federal claims and foreign parties. The retrieved *Setty* majority expressly frames its rule around federal claims and ultimately rejects estoppel on the facts. Thus a difference in governing-law language does not itself establish opposite outcomes on this record.

More importantly, the opposition's pages 23–26 raise substantial vehicle concerns: separate bilateral contracts; no remaining claims against foreign insurers; the respondents' governmental status; and the possibility that intertwined-claims estoppel would fail even under federal law. At page 16 it also identifies service-of-suit and agreement-signature issues not reached below. These are respondents' arguments, not independently adjudicated alternative holdings. Petitioners answer at pages 21–24 that allegations of concerted misconduct and the comparable *Bufkin* litigation make the legal choice dispositive, and at page 35 that the question was litigated and decided below. I do not simply accept the opposition's claim that the question cannot matter. Nevertheless, the need to work through those objections makes a cleaner future vehicle a substantial alternative to review now.

My **25% probability of any grant** is a judgmental adjustment, not a fitted estimate: the preserved legal issue, verified doctrinal disagreement, and requested response deserve a large increase, but disputed split depth and vehicle quality keep denial distinctly more likely. The main uncertainty is whether the Court sees this as the natural follow-up to *GE Energy* or as insurance-specific litigation that will not reliably resolve the broader issue.
