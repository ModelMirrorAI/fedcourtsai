# Forecast

The Court will deny the petition for certiorari. The dispute presents a substantial question about the governing law for nonsignatory arbitration under the New York Convention, but the domestic insurance contracts, public-entity respondents, and contested entitlement to estoppel make this a less attractive vehicle than a straightforward international commercial dispute. A denial will not resolve that legal question or endorse the Fifth Circuit's reasoning.

The provisioned docket records **one distribution**, on July 8, 2026, for the September 28 conference, and **no CVSG**. From that recorded state, the modal forecast is **zero additional distributions**. I assign 42% to at least one additional distribution; if that occurs, one or two additional distributions are more likely than a long series. This is an increment forecast, not a claim that a relist has already occurred. The baseline does not establish what happened at the listed conference.

The Court will most likely dispose of the petition during the opening fall 2026 conferences, by late November, without requesting the Solicitor General's views. I assign **12%** to a new CVSG: the Convention's international implications make consultation plausible, but the dispute's private-party and state-insurance features weigh against it. A CVSG would instead delay final disposition for several months. Timing is context, not a scored claim.

If review is granted, the Court will most likely take the petition's single question substantially as written: whether federal common law or state law governs a nonsignatory's equitable-estoppel enforcement of a Convention arbitration agreement. It will not need to decide every disputed prerequisite to arbitration to answer that choice-of-law question. This forecast does not predict that the insurers necessarily win under the selected law.

Conditional on a grant, **plenary review is substantially more likely than a disposition in the cert order**; I assign the latter route 6%. No identified intervening decision supplies a straightforward GVR ground, and the disputed choice-of-law issue calls for fuller briefing rather than summary error correction.

Conditional on denial, I assign **5%** to any noted dissent or statement respecting denial. The forecast is an unexplained denial without separate writing; I do not forecast individual cert votes or a particular author.
