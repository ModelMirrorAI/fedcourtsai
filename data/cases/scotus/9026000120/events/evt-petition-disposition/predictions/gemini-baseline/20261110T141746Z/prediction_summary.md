The Supreme Court is likely to deny review in this case, with about a one-in-ten chance of agreeing to hear it. The challengers—advocacy groups and individuals who were cut off during Colorado state legislative hearings for refusing to use a transgender person's preferred name and pronouns—ask the Court to rule that "legislative immunity" (a legal protection that shields lawmakers from being sued over their lawmaking duties) does not block lawsuits seeking a court order against enforcing public-comment speech rules. Although the free-speech issue is prominent, the federal appeals court unanimously agreed that longstanding Supreme Court precedent protects state lawmakers from federal lawsuits over how they run their own committee hearings, and there is no real disagreement among the federal appeals courts on that rule for state legislators.
