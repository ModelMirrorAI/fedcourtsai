# Forecast: Rogne v. City of Catoosa, No. 25-1320 (evt-petition-disposition)

**Disposition.** The Court will deny certiorari. The petition sits at a single
distribution, for the Conference of September 28, 2026 (the long conference),
with the City's waiver of response on file; the denial should appear on the
order list that follows that conference, on or about October 5, 2026, with no
call for a response and no separate writing.

**Relists.** Distributed once so far; this is the ordinary case of a petition
that has never been relisted. Expect no further distribution. The two ways a
second distribution could arise are a reschedule off the long conference or a
call for a response from the City, and neither is likely here: the Court
requests a response in only a small share of waived cases, and it does so where
a petition presents a plausible split or a federal-interest question, which this
one does not.

**CVSG.** None. No federal party, no federal statute, and no federal program is
implicated; the dispute is between a private landowner and an Oklahoma
municipality over state-court preclusion, so the Solicitor General's views will
not be sought.

**Which question the Court would take, if it took one.** Not the question as
written. The petition's question, whether a state-court dismissal on prudential
exhaustion and rescission grounds is a decision "on the merits" of a Takings
Clause claim, is really a question about how Oklahoma's savings statute and
Oklahoma preclusion law read a particular Oklahoma judgment, and the Court would
not grant to answer that. Any interest would be reformulated toward the
Knick-adjacent question the petition gestures at: whether a state court may
dispose of a takings claim for past physical occupation on voluntary-cessation
or exhaustion grounds without ever adjudicating compensation. Nothing on this
record suggests the Court sees that question presented cleanly here.

**Summary disposition versus plenary review.** Conditional on a grant, a summary
route is somewhat more likely than plenary review: the petition is an
error-correction petition with a sympathetic takings narrative and a short
unpublished order below, which is the shape the Court has occasionally handled
per curiam in the takings area rather than set for argument. No intervening
decision of this Court bears on the question, so a GVR is not in view; the
summary form, if any, would be a per curiam reversal. This is a conditional
forecast about an unlikely branch.

**Dissent from denial.** None expected. A statement respecting denial from a
Justice attentive to takings claims is conceivable but improbable: the record
runs through two state proceedings and two federal proceedings, the dispositive
ruling below is a preclusion holding grounded in state law, and the City did not
even respond. This is not a vehicle a Justice would use to flag the issue.
