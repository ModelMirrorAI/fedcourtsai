## In brief

The Supreme Court declined to hear a property owner's bid to revive his lawsuit seeking compensation for a city's years-long occupation of his land.

## What happened

John Rogne says the City of Catoosa built a barrier fence on his property in 2011 after issuing a cease-and-desist order, blocking his use of the land for six years without paying him anything. He sued in Oklahoma state court claiming an unconstitutional taking without compensation. The state courts ruled against him, holding he had not exhausted administrative remedies and that the city's later rescission of its order left no live claim. Rogne then refiled in federal court, arguing Oklahoma's savings statute let him do so because his first case failed on procedural grounds, not on the merits. The federal district court and the Tenth Circuit disagreed, ruling that the state court's decision counted as a ruling on the merits, so the suit could not proceed.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked whether a state court's refusal to hear a compensation claim—because the property owner hadn't used an administrative process, and because the government later voluntarily ended its occupation—counts as a decision on the substance of the compensation claim itself, as opposed to a procedural dismissal that would let the owner refile.

## Where it stands

The petition was distributed for the Court's September 28, 2026 conference, and on October 5, 2026, the Court denied the petition.

## What each outcome would mean

Rogne, who lost in the lower courts, asked the Court to take up his case. The denial means the Court will not review the Tenth Circuit's decision, so that ruling stands: Rogne's federal lawsuit remains dismissed, and he cannot pursue his compensation claim further under the theory he raised. The City of Catoosa's win in the lower courts is unaffected. A denial does not mean the Court agreed with the Tenth Circuit's reasoning, only that the case will not be reviewed.
