## In brief

The Court declined to hear a property owner's bid to revive his lawsuit seeking compensation for a city's years-long occupation of his land.

## What happened

John Rogne says the City of Catoosa built a barrier fence on his vacant lots and barred him from stockpiling dirt there, first in 2009 and again in 2011. He sued in Oklahoma state court claiming this was a taking of his property without compensation, but lost after the city rescinded its order and the courts found he had not exhausted administrative remedies first; state appellate courts agreed and the state supreme court declined further review. Rogne then refiled his claim in federal court, arguing an Oklahoma statute let him do so because his earlier case had failed on procedural grounds rather than on the merits. A federal district court dismissed the case, and the Tenth Circuit Court of Appeals affirmed, holding the state court's decision had in fact resolved the claim on the merits.

## What the Court is being asked

Rogne asks the Court to decide whether a state court's reliance on a procedural rule requiring a property owner to first seek an administrative remedy, where the only relief available there was the government voluntarily stopping its conduct rather than paying money, counts as a decision on the substance of a compensation claim. That question would determine whether Rogne can still pursue his claim in federal court.

## Where it stands

The petition was distributed for the Court's September 28, 2026 conference, and on October 5, 2026 the Court denied it.

## What each outcome would mean

Rogne, as petitioner, asked the Court to take up his case. With the petition denied, the Tenth Circuit's ruling stands: Rogne's federal lawsuit remains dismissed, and he does not get another chance to pursue his claim for compensation from the City of Catoosa over its past occupation of his property. The denial does not mean the Court agreed the Tenth Circuit was right; it simply leaves that decision in place without the Court deciding the question itself.
