## In brief

A property owner asks the Court to revive his lawsuit seeking compensation for the city's years-long occupation of his land.

## What happened

John Rogne says the City of Catoosa built a barrier fence on his land in 2011, blocking his use of it, after issuing a cease-and-desist order. Rogne sued in Oklahoma state court claiming an uncompensated taking of his property. The city later rescinded the order and removed the fence, and the state courts dismissed his case, ruling he had not exhausted administrative remedies and that the dispute was resolved. Rogne then refiled his claim in federal court, arguing Oklahoma's savings statute let him do so because his earlier case had not been decided on the merits. The federal district court dismissed the case, and the Tenth Circuit affirmed, holding that the state court's decision had in fact resolved the claim on the merits.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked whether a lower court's decision, based only on a rule requiring a property owner to first pursue administrative remedies and on the fact that the government voluntarily stopped its conduct, counts as a decision on the substance of a claim that property was taken without compensation, or whether such a claim can still be refiled afterward.

## Where it stands

The City of Catoosa waived its right to file a response. The petition was distributed for the Court's September 28, 2026 conference, and the docket does not yet show any action taken on it.

## What each outcome would mean

Rogne is asking the Court to hear his case. If the Court grants the petition, it will consider the question in full and decide whether the earlier ruling barred his claim; a grant does not decide who wins. If the Court denies the petition, the Tenth Circuit's ruling against Rogne stands, and his case seeking compensation from the City of Catoosa remains dismissed.
