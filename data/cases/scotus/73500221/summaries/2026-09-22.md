## What happened

John Rogne owns vacant lots in Catoosa, Oklahoma. The City issued a cease-and-desist order barring him from stockpiling dirt there and, according to the petition, built a barrier fence on his land that blocked his use of it for six years before rescinding the order. Rogne sued the City in state court for taking his property without paying him compensation. The state courts ruled against him, holding he had not first pursued an available administrative remedy, and treating the City's later rescission as resolving the matter. Rogne then refiled the same claim in federal court, relying on an Oklahoma law that lets a lawsuit be refiled if the earlier one failed for reasons other than the merits. The federal district court dismissed the case, and the Tenth Circuit Court of Appeals affirmed, agreeing with the state appellate court that the claim had been decided on the merits.

## What the Court is being asked

The dispute is over how to classify the earlier state-court loss. Rogne argues that court never actually ruled on the substance of his compensation claim — it only found he skipped an administrative step, and then treated the City's voluntary rescission of its order as ending the case. He says that kind of ruling is procedural, not a decision on the merits, so he should be allowed to pursue his claim again in federal court. The City has not yet filed its own arguments on this point.
