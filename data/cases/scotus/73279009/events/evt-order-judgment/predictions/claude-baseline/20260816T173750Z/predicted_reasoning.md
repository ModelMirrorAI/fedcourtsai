# Forecast: Grant v. Higgins, No. 25-566 (consolidated with Viramontes v. Cook County, No. 25-238)

**Judgment class: reversed.** The Court will disturb the Second Circuit's judgment
upholding Connecticut's assault-weapons ban. The likeliest single label is an
outright reversal — the Court holding the possession ban unconstitutional as
applied to AR-15-type rifles — rather than a vacatur-and-remand, because the
question presented is a pure legal question the Court limited the grant to, the
record is fully developed after summary judgment below, and a remand for
"reapplication" of a common-use rule the Court announces would leave nothing
for the Second Circuit to do. My rough distribution over the six labels:
reversed 0.55, vacated 0.25, affirmed-in-part-reversed-in-part 0.05, affirmed
0.10, DIG 0.03, equally divided 0.02 — P(disturbed) 0.85.

**Vote lineup: 6–3.** Roberts, Thomas, Alito, Gorsuch, Kavanaugh, and Barrett
in the majority; Sotomayor, Kagan, and Jackson in dissent. Thomas and Alito
publicly urged review of exactly this question at the Snope v. Brown denial in
June 2025, and Kavanaugh's statement respecting that denial said the AR-15
question would be before the Court "in the next Term or two" while calling the
Fourth Circuit's protected-arms analysis questionable — that is three votes
telegraphed, Gorsuch joined the Bruen majority without reservation, and a grant
here after those signals implies the movants counted five. The least certain
votes are Roberts and Barrett, who narrowed Bruen's method in Rahimi; I expect
both to join a judgment against the ban while pressing the majority toward the
bounded ground below, with a small chance one writes separately to concur only
in the judgment. No recusal is in prospect, so an equally divided affirmance is
a trace outcome.

**Ground.** The majority will hold AR-15-platform rifles are "Arms" in common
use for lawful purposes and protected under Heller's common-use principle as
applied through Bruen: tens of millions lawfully owned means they are not
"dangerous and unusual," and no historical tradition supports banning arms in
common civilian use. It will reject the Seventh Circuit's Bevis line — that
military-derived weapons fall outside the Amendment's text — and the Second
Circuit's kindred reasoning, treating common use as dispositive against a
possession ban rather than one input to be balanced.

**Breadth.** Categorical as to the weapon class, bounded as to everything
else: the rule will condemn possession bans on AR-15-type semiautomatic rifles
generally (reaching the parallel bans of roughly ten states), while expressly
reserving magazine-capacity limits — litigated separately in Ocean State
Tactical-style cases and outside the limited question — other weapon
categories, and regulations short of possession bans.

**Separate writings.** Expect a Kavanaugh concurrence (his pattern in Bruen and
Rahimi) emphasizing what the holding does not disturb — licensing, background
checks, machine-gun and dangerous-and-unusual doctrine. A Sotomayor principal
dissent joined by Kagan and Jackson, on Sandy Hook's legislative response and
the tradition of regulating especially dangerous weapons. Thomas is the natural
majority author if in the majority (he wrote Bruen); authorship is unscored, so
this is context.

**Timing.** Petitioners' merits brief is due August 28, 2026 and respondents'
October 21, 2026, pointing to argument in the December 2026 sitting. A decision
of this magnitude lands late: expect the judgment in June 2027, near the end of
OT2026.

**Procedural exits.** A DIG is unlikely — the grant was limited and deliberate,
taken after seventeen distributions, and there is no vehicle defect visible in
either consolidated record; standing was uncontested below. Nothing about the
grant is in play here; certiorari is settled history.
