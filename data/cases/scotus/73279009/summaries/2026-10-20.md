## In brief

The Court agreed to hear a challenge to Connecticut's ban on AR-15s and similar semiautomatic rifles, consolidated with a related case.

## What happened

Connecticut restricts possession of "assault weapons," including AR-15-style rifles, and certain other firearms. Eddie Grant, Jr. and other individuals and gun-rights groups sued Connecticut officials, arguing the ban violates the Second Amendment, and asked for a preliminary injunction blocking enforcement while the case continued. A federal district court denied the injunction, finding the challengers were unlikely to succeed on the merits. The Second Circuit Court of Appeals affirmed, holding the ban fits within the country's historical tradition of restricting unusually dangerous weapons, and that the challengers also had not shown the other factors needed for a preliminary injunction. Grant and the other plaintiffs then asked the Supreme Court to review that decision.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked whether the Constitution protects the right to possess semiautomatic rifles, including the AR-15, that are commonly owned for lawful purposes, meaning whether Connecticut's ban on such rifles is constitutional.

## Where it stands

The Court granted the petition, consolidated it with a related case, and the case has been argued; briefing and amicus filings continued through the fall, with argument held and the case now awaiting decision.

## What each outcome would mean

Grant and the other challengers are asking the Court to overturn the Second Circuit's ruling, which upheld Connecticut's law and left the preliminary injunction denial in place. If the Court affirms, Connecticut's ban remains in effect and the challengers cannot obtain the preliminary injunction they sought while the underlying lawsuit continues. If the Court reverses, it would hold the ban likely unconstitutional, clearing the way for an injunction against enforcement while the case proceeds. If the Court vacates, it would send the case back to the lower courts without deciding the constitutional question for either side.
