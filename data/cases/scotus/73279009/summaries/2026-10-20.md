## In brief

The Court agreed to decide whether the Second Amendment protects the right to possess semiautomatic rifles like the AR-15.

## What happened

Connecticut residents and gun-rights groups sued to block Connecticut's ban on certain semiautomatic rifles and other firearms, arguing the ban violates the Second Amendment. A federal district court denied their request to block the law while the case proceeded, and the Second Circuit Court of Appeals affirmed, holding the challengers were unlikely to succeed because the restricted weapons fall within a historical tradition of regulating unusually dangerous arms. The challengers, led by Eddie Grant Jr., then asked the Supreme Court to review that ruling. Connecticut officials urged the Court not to take the case, saying every appeals court to consider similar bans has upheld them.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked whether the right to keep and bear arms covers semiautomatic rifles that are widely owned for lawful purposes, including the AR-15, and whether such rifles can be banned consistent with that right.

## Where it stands

The Court granted the petition, consolidated this case with a related one, and heard oral argument; the case now awaits a decision on the merits.

## What each outcome would mean

Grant and the other challengers asked the Court to take up the case. If the Court rules for the challengers, it would find the ban unconstitutional as applied to these weapons, likely leading to further proceedings favorable to the challengers. If the Court rules for Connecticut officials, the ban would be upheld and the lower court's decision affirmed. The Court could also vacate the lower court's ruling and send the case back without deciding the constitutional question for either side.
