## In brief

Gun owners and gun-rights groups asked the Court to review whether Connecticut can ban certain semiautomatic rifles, including AR-15s; the Court granted review, consolidating the case with a similar petition.

## What happened

Eddie Grant, Jr. and other Connecticut residents, along with two gun-rights organizations, sued Connecticut officials over state laws banning "assault weapons," including AR-15-style rifles and certain other firearms. They sought a preliminary injunction blocking enforcement while the case proceeded, arguing the ban violates the Second Amendment. A federal district court denied the injunction, and the Second Circuit Court of Appeals affirmed, holding the challengers were unlikely to succeed on the merits and had not shown the balance of harms favored them. The residents then asked the Supreme Court to review that decision.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked whether the Constitution protects the right to possess semiautomatic rifles that are commonly owned for lawful purposes, including the AR-15, or whether states may ban such rifles despite their popularity.

## Where it stands

The Court granted the petition, consolidating this case with a related petition (No. 25-238) for one hour of combined oral argument. Further filings will appear on the docket of the other case.

## What each outcome would mean

Grant and the other challengers asked the Court to take up the case after losing below. Because the Court granted review, it will now hear full arguments and decide the constitutional question itself; granting cert does not mean either side has won. If the Court ultimately rules for Grant and the other challengers, Connecticut's restrictions on these rifles could be struck down or narrowed. If it rules for Connecticut officials, the Second Circuit's decision upholding the ban would stand. The Court could also send the case back for further proceedings without deciding the question either way.
