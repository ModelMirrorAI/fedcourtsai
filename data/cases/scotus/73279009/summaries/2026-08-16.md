## What happened

Eddie Grant, Jr. and other Connecticut residents and gun-rights groups sued state officials over Connecticut's laws restricting so-called assault weapons, including AR-15-style rifles and certain other firearms. They sought a preliminary injunction blocking enforcement while their lawsuit continued, arguing the laws violate the Second Amendment. A federal district court denied the injunction, and the Second Circuit Court of Appeals affirmed, holding the challengers had not shown they were likely to win on the merits and had also failed to show the balance of harms favored blocking the law. The petitioners then asked the Supreme Court to review that decision. Connecticut officials oppose review, arguing the record is incomplete and that petitioners did not challenge all the grounds the appeals court relied on.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked whether the Constitution protects the right to possess semiautomatic rifles, including the AR-15, when such rifles are widely owned and used for lawful purposes. Petitioners argue that popularity for lawful use should settle the constitutional question in their favor, while the state argues the rifles can still be restricted as unusually dangerous weapons, and that unresolved factual disputes about how these weapons are actually used make this a poor case for deciding that question now.

## Where it stands
