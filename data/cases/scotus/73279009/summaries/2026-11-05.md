## In brief

The Court agreed to hear a challenge to Connecticut's assault weapons ban, consolidated with a related case, after gun owners and advocacy groups asked it to strike down the law.

## What happened

Connecticut restricts certain semiautomatic firearms, including AR-15-style rifles, that its legislature classifies as assault weapons. Eddie Grant, Jr. and other individuals and gun-rights groups sued Connecticut officials, arguing the ban violates their right to keep and bear arms. A federal district court denied their request to block the law while the case proceeded, and the Second Circuit Court of Appeals affirmed, holding the restrictions fit within a historical tradition of regulating unusually dangerous weapons. The challengers then asked the Supreme Court to review that decision.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked whether the Constitution protects the right to possess semiautomatic rifles that are commonly owned for lawful purposes, including the AR-15, or whether a state may ban such weapons as unusually dangerous despite their widespread ownership.

## Where it stands

The Supreme Court granted review, consolidating this case with a related petition, and the case has been briefed and argued; it now awaits a decision on the merits.

## What each outcome would mean

Grant and the other challengers are asking the Court to overturn the Second Circuit's ruling, which upheld Connecticut's law. If the Court reverses, it would find the ban likely unconstitutional, a result favoring Grant and the other challengers. If it affirms, Connecticut's restrictions would stand, a result favoring the state officials who defended the law. If the Court vacates the decision, it would send the case back to the lower courts without deciding the constitutional question for either side.
