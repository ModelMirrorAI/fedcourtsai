## Why 8%, rather than 5% or a high-confidence grant

The main upward adjustment is the warrant question. Petition pages 9–17 describe a published Pennsylvania decision sustaining charging materials that identify loci shared by everyone but omit the unique DNA profile. The petition acknowledges a reference to an existing crime-scene profile held elsewhere; that qualification matters and prevents treating the warrant as literally devoid of identifying context. Nevertheless, the comparison to State v. Belt, 179 P.3d 443 (Kan. 2008), is concrete. I checked Belt's opinion through CourtListener: its discussion rejects reliance on externally maintained profile information where neither warrant nor affidavit supplies the unique profile. I also checked the operative passage in State v. Police, 343 Conn. 274 (2022), through CourtListener: it requires supporting information assuring identification through a unique profile and calls for statistical rarity information. These primary-source checks support a plausible particularity conflict, not a verified conclusion that the Pennsylvania decision cannot be distinguished. Sources: Belt, CourtListener opinion 2635856, discussion around *960–961; Police, opinion 11297773, passage beginning with its conclusion on the affidavit requirement.

The broader privacy issue supplies significance, but less certain cert traction. Petition pages 17–26 argue that collecting a discarded object and extracting its genetic information are separate events for Fourth Amendment purposes, invoke DNA-analysis precedent, and analogize to digital and bodily-sample privacy. Its own presentation also acknowledges decisions upholding analysis of discarded genetic material and relies in part on dissents. I have not independently verified its asserted Davis/Raynor conflict and do not treat it as a clean conflict on identical facts. The parent-child theory is particularly undeveloped: page 25 says counsel found no analogous authority. Combining it with the petitioner's own discarded saliva complicates rather than improves the vehicle.

I therefore move modestly above the 5.12% anchor to 8%. The published decision and concrete Belt comparison justify an uplift. Against a much higher number are the single ordinary distribution, lack of visible institutional support, absence of a provisioned adversarial response, the unusual historical warrant facts, and uncertainty about how the actual warrant, affidavit, and remedy affect the claimed conflict. I do not assume a waiver, harmless-error holding, independent state ground, or other procedural bar that the provided material does not establish. A stipulated trial and completed state appellate review, as described at petition pages 7–8, are preferable to an unresolved interlocutory posture.
