## In brief

Lawyers sanctioned for frivolous election-lawsuit filings ask the Court to overturn the sanctions against them.

## What happened

Petitioners are attorneys who sued Arizona election officials in 2022 on behalf of candidates challenging the state's vote-counting procedures. A district court dismissed the suit for lack of standing and separately sanctioned the attorneys, finding they made false and misleading factual claims without adequately investigating them first, and that they improperly pursued emergency relief close to the election. The Ninth Circuit affirmed the sanctions over a dissent, and later denied rehearing by the full court over a dissent from six judges. The attorneys now ask the Supreme Court to review the sanctions.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked whether judges may sanction lawyers to discourage lawsuits the judge views as damaging to public confidence, whether sanctions may rest on arguments merely viewed as long-shot or novel, whether judges may interpret a complaint in the manner least favorable to the lawyers who wrote it before sanctioning them, and whether a request for an election-related court order can be punished as improperly delayed when filed months before an election while also covering future elections.

## Where it stands

The case has been distributed for conference multiple times, most recently for October 9, 2026, after the respondents filed their brief opposing review. The docket does not show that the Court has yet acted on the petition.

## What each outcome would mean

Parker and Parker Daniels Kibort, the attorneys, are asking the Court to take up their case. If the Court grants review, it would hear full arguments and decide the legal questions about when sanctions are proper, with no outcome assured either side. If it denies review, the Ninth Circuit's decision upholding the sanctions against the attorneys stands, meaning they remain responsible for paying the opposing county officials' attorneys' fees. A denial would not mean the Supreme Court agrees the sanctions were correct, only that the lower court's ruling remains in place.
