## In brief

The Supreme Court declined to review sanctions imposed on lawyers for Kari Lake and Mark Finchem over their 2022 Arizona election lawsuit.

## What happened

Attorneys Andrew Parker and his firm represented candidates Kari Lake and Mark Finchem in a 2022 lawsuit challenging Arizona's electronic vote-counting procedures. A federal district court dismissed the suit for lack of standing and separately sanctioned Parker and co-counsel under court rules, finding they made false and misleading factual claims and pursued an injunction request that was both frivolous and poorly timed before the election. The Ninth Circuit affirmed the sanctions in a divided panel decision; rehearing before the full court was denied, though six judges dissented, warning the sanctions could chill legitimate litigation. Parker then asked the Supreme Court to review the sanctions ruling.

## What the Court is being asked

The petition raised four questions: whether a court may sanction attorneys partly to discourage future lawsuits it views as politically unpopular; whether sanctions may rest on finding that legal arguments are novel or unlikely to succeed; whether a court may interpret a complaint in the way least favorable to the attorneys who wrote it before sanctioning them; and whether a request for an election-related court order can be sanctioned as poorly timed when it was filed roughly four months before the election and also sought relief for future elections.

## Where it stands

The petition was distributed for conference multiple times, with the Court requesting a response from the opposing parties along the way. After further conferences, the Court denied the petition.

## What each outcome would mean

Parker and his firm, as petitioners, asked the Court to take up the case and overturn the sanctions. Maricopa County officials, the respondents, opposed review, defending the lower courts' rulings. Because the Court denied the petition, the Ninth Circuit's decision upholding the sanctions against Parker and his co-counsel stands, and the case is not reviewed further by the Supreme Court. A denial does not mean the Court agreed the sanctions were proper, only that it chose not to hear the case.
