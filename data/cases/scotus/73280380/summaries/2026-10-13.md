## In brief

The Court declined to hear attorneys' bid to overturn sanctions imposed for false statements made in a lawsuit challenging Arizona's 2022 election procedures.

## What happened

Attorneys for two candidates in Arizona's 2022 elections sued county and state election officials, seeking an order requiring hand-counted paper ballots instead of electronic voting equipment. A federal district court dismissed the lawsuit for lack of standing and later sanctioned the attorneys under court rules for making false and misleading factual claims, including claims that Arizona did not already use paper ballots and that its voting equipment was untested. The Ninth Circuit affirmed the sanctions, over a dissent, and later declined to rehear the case as a full court, with six judges dissenting from that denial.

## What the Court is being asked

The attorneys asked the Court to decide whether a court may sanction lawyers to discourage future lawsuits it considers harmful to public confidence, whether sanctions may rest on novel or aggressive legal arguments, whether a court may interpret a complaint in the manner least favorable to the people who wrote it before sanctioning them, and whether seeking a pre-election court order counts as improperly rushed when filed months before the vote.

## Where it stands

The petition was distributed for conference multiple times, with the Court asking the opposing side to respond before considering it further. The Court denied the petition, leaving the sanctions in place.

## What each outcome would mean

The attorneys, Andrew Parker and his firm, asked the Court to take up their case and overturn the sanctions ordered against them. By denying the petition, the Court leaves the Ninth Circuit's ruling in place: the sanctions requiring the attorneys to pay the county officials' legal fees stand, and the lower courts' findings that the attorneys made unsupported factual claims are not disturbed. The denial does not mean the Court agreed with the lower courts' reasoning; it simply leaves that outcome as the final word in this case.
