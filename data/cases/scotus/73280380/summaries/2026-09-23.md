## What happened

Lawyers Andrew Parker and his firm represented Kari Lake and Mark Finchem in a 2022 lawsuit challenging Arizona's use of electronic voting equipment. A federal district court dismissed the suit for lack of standing and then sanctioned the lawyers, finding their filings contained false and misleading factual claims and were not backed by a reasonable pre-filing investigation. The court ordered them to pay the opposing county officials' legal fees. The Ninth Circuit affirmed the sanctions, and after a panel dissent, the full appeals court declined further review over the dissent of six judges. The lawyers now ask the Supreme Court to step in.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked whether the lower courts overstepped in punishing these lawyers. The petition frames this as several questions: whether judges may sanction attorneys partly to discourage similar lawsuits in the future, whether sanctions can rest on arguments a court sees as weak or untested, whether a complaint should be read in the light most unfavorable to the people who filed it when deciding on sanctions, and whether asking a court to change election procedures months before an election can be penalized under a rule against needlessly dragging out litigation. The county officials respond that the sanctions were simply based on demonstrably false statements in the lawsuit, not on its subject matter or its boldness, and that the case raises no broader legal question needing the Court's attention.
