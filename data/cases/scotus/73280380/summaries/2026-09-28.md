## In brief

Two lawyers who were sanctioned for their conduct in an election lawsuit ask the Court to overturn those sanctions.

## What happened

Andrew Parker and his law firm represented candidates Kari Lake and Mark Finchem in a 2022 lawsuit challenging Arizona's vote-counting procedures. After the case was dismissed for lack of standing, the district court sanctioned Parker and his firm, finding they made false and misleading factual claims without adequately checking them first, and improperly delayed seeking urgent court action. The Ninth Circuit affirmed the sanctions. Parker sought rehearing before the full appeals court; it was denied, though six judges dissented, arguing the sanctions improperly punished disfavored litigation. Parker and his firm now ask the Supreme Court to review that decision.

## What the Court is being asked

The case asks whether courts may penalize lawyers for filing suits a judge disfavors, for making legal arguments that are novel or unlikely to succeed, or for how a complaint is read when deciding whether to impose sanctions. It also asks whether a request for a court order affecting future elections can be penalized under a rule limiting last-minute election challenges, when part of that request concerned elections beyond the immediate one.

## Where it stands

The Court asked the other side to respond after considering the case at a March 2026 conference, and that response has been filed. The case was distributed for a September 28, 2026 conference, which the docket shows was rescheduled.

## What each outcome would mean

Parker and his firm are asking the Court to take up their case. If the Court grants review, it would hear full arguments and decide whether the sanctions were proper, with no side winning automatically. If the Court denies review, the Ninth Circuit's decision upholding the sanctions against Parker and his firm stays in place, though that denial would not itself mean the Court agrees the sanctions were correct. The county officials who defended the sanctions, named as respondents, are urging the Court to deny review.
