## In brief

Lawyers sanctioned for false statements in an election lawsuit ask the Supreme Court to overturn the sanctions against them.

## What happened

Attorneys Andrew Parker and his firm represented candidates who sued Arizona officials before the 2022 election, challenging the state's vote-counting procedures. A federal district court dismissed the suit for lack of standing and then sanctioned the attorneys under Rule 11 and a related statute, finding they made false and misleading factual claims without adequate investigation and improperly pursued injunctive relief close to the election. The Ninth Circuit affirmed the sanctions, and rehearing en banc was denied over a dissent from six judges. The attorneys now ask the Supreme Court to review that ruling. The county officials who sought the sanctions oppose review, saying the lower courts correctly applied established standards to the facts of this case.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked whether a court may sanction lawyers partly to discourage future lawsuits it considers harmful to public confidence, whether sanctions can rest on legal arguments a court sees as novel or unlikely to succeed, whether a court may interpret a complaint in the way least favorable to the lawyers who filed it before sanctioning them, and whether a request for election-related court orders can be sanctioned under a rule limiting last-minute election litigation when the request also covered future elections.

## Where it stands

The petition was distributed for conference in March 2026, after which the Court asked the opposing side to respond. That response was filed in June 2026, and the case was distributed again for a September 2026 conference; the docket shows it was then rescheduled, with no decision yet recorded.

## What each outcome would mean

Parker and his firm, as petitioners, ask the Court to take up the case. If the Court grants review, it would hear full arguments and decide the sanctions questions itself, without yet favoring either side. If it denies review, the Ninth Circuit's decision upholding the sanctions against the attorneys stands, and the county officials' win is preserved. The petition also asks the Court to summarily reverse the sanctions outright without full briefing; that outcome would set the sanctions aside without the case going through argument.
