## Base rate and adjustments

The committed `metrics/statpack.md` carries a matching `sal-v4` band table. Pooling **all eight displayed Terms strictly before Term 2025**, namely 2017-2024, gives an elevated-band reached denominator of approximately **2,810** and an approximately **17.24%** reached grant rate. This is a denominator-weighted calculation from the published rounded rates, not an exact reconstruction of raw grant counts. I exclude Terms 2025 and 2026 and use the bracketed reached figures, not the lower terminal elevated-band rates. This is a private attorney petition, not a government-petitioner case merely because county officials are respondents.

The modern-cert disposition table's much lower overall grant rate is not the best anchor for this selected paid petition. I also read the paid-segment relist and CVSG cuts. Their terminal-state estimates describe population shape, not the forward probability of another distribution from this cell. In particular, the pack warns that rescheduling adds distribution entries. Here a request for opposition intervened between the two distributions. I therefore do not treat this as a clean post-conference merits relist or turn the summer interval into evidence of a hold.

I move from the approximately 17% band anchor to **28% P(any grant)** for three reasons. First, the Court affirmatively requested a response after initial distribution. Second, the petition describes a panel dissent and a six-judge dissent from denial of rehearing, with a concrete concern about sanctions chilling advocacy. Those opinions provide a developed competing account, although disagreement within one circuit is not itself an intercircuit split. Third, the parties discuss Bost, a January 2026 candidate-standing decision that postdates the March 2025 sanctions affirmance and potentially undermines part of its premise. That supplies a plausible GVR route without requiring a full new merits case.

The opposition keeps the number well below 50%. Its strongest contention is that the sanctions rested independently on materially misleading factual assertions about paper ballots and equipment testing, inadequate investigation, and continued litigation after a warning, rather than on candidate standing alone. It directly distinguishes Bost on that basis (opposition p. 17). It also argues that the challenged application of Purcell was only one reason for section 1927 sanctions (pp. 21-24). If those descriptions are right, correcting a standing rationale would not necessarily undermine the sanctions judgment. The petition invokes other circuits' protective formulations of Rule 11, but the material supplied does not establish a clean, unavoidable disagreement over a single rule of law rather than its application to these pleadings.

My main uncertainty is therefore the significance of Bost to the actual grounds sustaining sanctions. I do not accept either the petition's political-retaliation characterization or the opposition's characterization of falsehoods as an independently established fact. The forecast prices both accounts. The 28% grant probability includes summary relief; it is not a 28% forecast of a plenary hearing.
