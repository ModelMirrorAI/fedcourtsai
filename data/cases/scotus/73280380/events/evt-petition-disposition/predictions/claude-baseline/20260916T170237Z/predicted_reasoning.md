# Forecast: what the Court will do with No. 25-882, Parker v. Gates

**Disposition.** The petition will be **denied**, most likely on the order
list following the September 28, 2026 long conference or one conference
after it. P(any grant) = 0.12.

**Relists.** The docket shows **two distributions**: the first for the
March 27, 2026 conference, which the petition never actually reached because
the Court called for a response four days earlier; the second, on July 8,
for the September 28 long conference after the brief in opposition arrived.
So this is a petition at its *first real* conference, not a relisted one. I
expect it to be **distributed at least once more** with probability 0.38:
a grant would almost certainly come after a relist, and a denial with a
separate writing typically takes one or two extra conferences while the
writing circulates. Most likely single path is still a clean denial on the
first order list without any further distribution.

**CVSG.** No call for the views of the Solicitor General (P = 0.03). No
federal party, no federal statute at issue beyond 28 U.S.C. § 1927 as a
sanctions vehicle, and the Court does not invite the SG on Rule 11 disputes
between private attorneys and county officials.

**Which question, if any, the Court would take.** If the Court grants, it
would not adopt any of the four questions as written. QP 1 ("send a message
... politically disfavored lawsuits") and QP 2 ("novel or a long shot")
characterize the sanctions order in a way the panel majority expressly
rejected, and QP 4 (Purcell) rests on a § 1927 finding that also stands on
persistence in false paper-ballot allegations, so it is not outcome-
determinative. The only question with a doctrinal core is QP 3: whether a
pleading is construed in the signer's favor when Rule 11 is applied to it.
A plenary grant would reformulate around that question. A summary route is
at least as likely as plenary review conditional on a grant (P = 0.5): the
petition itself asks for a McKnight-style summary vacatur on the strength of
the en banc dissent, and a GVR "in light of Bost v. Illinois State Board of
Elections" (Jan. 14, 2026), which post-dates the Ninth Circuit's March 2025
sanctions opinion, is the shape a reluctant Court would use rather than
setting a fee dispute for argument.

**Dissent or statement respecting denial.** P = 0.22. The six-judge Ninth
Circuit en banc dissent (VanDyke, joined by Callahan, R. Nelson, Collins,
Lee, and Bumatay) hands a Justice a ready-made opinion on the "weaponization
of sanctions," and Justices Thomas, Alito, or Gorsuch are the plausible
authors of a short statement or dissent. Against that, the Court denied the
underlying merits petition in Lake v. Fontes, No. 23-1021, without noted
dissent, and separate writing here would associate a Justice with the 2022
Arizona voting-machine litigation; most of the time the denial will be
silent.

**If granted (conditional only).** The likely ground would be that Rule 11's
objective standard requires reading the challenged pleading as a whole and
in context before finding a factual assertion baseless, with the § 1927 award
vacated and remanded for reconsideration under that reading. The Court would
not reach Purcell.
