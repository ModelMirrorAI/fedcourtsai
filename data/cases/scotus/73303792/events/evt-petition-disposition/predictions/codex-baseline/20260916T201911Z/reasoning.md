## Why 0.6%, rather than 5.1%

The largest downward adjustment is substantive vehicle quality, not the caption alone. In the petition's Statement of the Case, pp. 3–7, petitioner describes a nonprecedential affirmance resting on Rooker-Feldman, pleading insufficiency, immunity, amendment futility, and filing restrictions. The pleaded wrongs involve judicial assignment, recusal, and custody-related proceedings. Even accepting the asserted jurisdictional error for forecasting purposes, the other grounds may independently prevent relief. Without the appendix I cannot determine their precise reach, so I treat them as a vehicle risk rather than an established complete bar.

The petition's strongest claimed cert hook is its split discussion at pp. 8–10. That discussion places **VanderKodde v. Mary Jane M. Elliott, P.C.**, 951 F.3d 397 (6th Cir. 2020), on the expansive side of Rooker-Feldman. I checked the Sixth Circuit's February 26, 2020 opinion through CourtListener, opinion 4510506, especially slip pp. 5–8. Its majority instead uses an injury-source inquiry and distinguishes injury caused by a state judgment from injury attributable to other conduct. This directly weakens the petition's characterization of its own circuit's rule. It does not prove every asserted conflict nonexistent, but it substantially reduces confidence that this unpublished application presents the clean inter-circuit disagreement claimed.

QP 2 combines two different subjects. The petition itself acknowledges that its liberal-amendment authorities allow denial for futility, while arguing that the courts misapplied that exception here (pp. 5, 10–12). Its prefiling-restriction discussion lists authorities and procedural safeguards without demonstrating in detail that comparable records yield incompatible categorical rules. On this record, both branches look more like contested applications than a clearly documented rule conflict. I have not independently verified every authority and do not treat the petition's summaries as judicial findings.

The absence of affirmative attention beyond an ordinary first distribution, together with the recorded response waiver, reinforces that assessment modestly. It is not conclusive: the Court can request a response later. I do not multiply separate statistical penalties for waiver, counsel status, unpublished disposition, and lack of amici; those signals overlap, and the pack supplies no fitted coefficients for them. My final **P(any grant) = 0.006** is a judgmental downward adjustment from the 0.0512 anchor, leaving room for a jurisdictional correction or an unobserved vehicle feature. The categorical prediction is denial, not dismissal.
