# Forecast: what the Court will do with No. 25-1258, Gomez v. Ryan

**Disposition.** The Court will deny the petition in the order list following
the September 28, 2026 conference (issued Monday, October 5, 2026, or the
Friday before). No opinion, no separate writing.

**Relists.** The docket shows exactly one distribution (June 17, 2026, for the
September 28 conference — the "long conference"). This petition has never been
relisted. I expect no further distribution: it will be denied at its first
consideration. A single reschedule or relist is a small possibility (roughly one
in fourteen), driven mostly by the noise of the long conference rather than by
any Justice's interest in the questions.

**CVSG.** None. No federal party, statute, or program is implicated; the
respondents are Ohio county judges, a clerk of courts, and a municipal police
officer, and the questions concern Rooker-Feldman and Rule 15. The Court will
not call for the Solicitor General's views.

**Which question, if any.** If the Court took anything, it would be a
reformulated version of Question 1 — the post-*Exxon Mobil* boundary of
Rooker-Feldman where a section 1983 plaintiff alleges injury from official
conduct surrounding, rather than embodied in, a state judgment. Question 2 (a
Rule 15(a)(2) futility denial coupled with prefiling restrictions) is
fact-bound, reviewed for abuse of discretion, and would not be taken. But the
Court will not take either: the Sixth Circuit's disposition is a nonprecedential
order adopting the district court's reasoning in full, the underlying claims run
against judges acting in their judicial capacity (absolute immunity is an
independent ground the petition does not seriously engage), and the respondents
waived their response without the Court requesting one.

**Summary disposition.** Not the likelier route in absolute terms, because no
disposition other than denial is realistically in play. Conditional on any
grant, though, a summary route (a GVR in light of some intervening decision, or
a summary order) would be more likely than plenary review, since this is not a
vehicle the Court would set for argument; no intervening decision I am aware of
bears on the questions.

**Dissent from denial.** None expected. No Justice writes on a pro se
Rooker-Feldman petition from a nonprecedential affirmance where the respondents
did not even file a brief in opposition.
