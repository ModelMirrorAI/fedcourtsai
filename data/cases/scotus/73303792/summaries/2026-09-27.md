## In brief

John Paul Gomez asks the Court to revive his civil-rights lawsuit against Ohio judges, a court clerk, and a police officer, after lower courts dismissed it.

## What happened

Gomez sued state and local officials, including judges and a court clerk, claiming their conduct in connection with Ohio family-court proceedings violated his constitutional rights. He says the harm came from the officials' own misconduct, not from any state-court ruling itself. A federal district court dismissed the case, ruling that some claims were barred by a doctrine that limits federal courts from reviewing state-court judgments, and that the rest failed to state a valid claim. The court also denied Gomez a chance to amend his complaint and barred him from making certain future filings. The Sixth Circuit affirmed.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked to decide two things: first, whether the rule barring federal courts from reviewing state-court judgments applies here, given that Gomez says his injuries came from officials' conduct rather than from any judgment itself. Second, whether a rule allowing courts to freely permit changes to a complaint lets a court refuse that chance, and bar someone from future filings, without ever letting them try to fix the problems in their case.

## Where it stands

## What each outcome would mean

Gomez, who lost in the lower courts, is asking the Court to take up his case. If the Court grants the petition, it would hear the case and decide these procedural questions itself, without yet deciding who is right. If it denies the petition, the Sixth Circuit's ruling against Gomez stands, and his lawsuit remains dismissed along with the filing restrictions imposed on him. A denial would not mean the Court agrees with the lower courts, only that their decision remains in place.
