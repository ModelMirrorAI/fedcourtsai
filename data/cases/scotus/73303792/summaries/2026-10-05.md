## In brief

The Supreme Court declined to hear John Paul Gomez's bid to revive his civil rights lawsuit against Ohio judges, a clerk, and a police officer.

## What happened

Gomez sued Ohio state and local officials, alleging that misconduct connected to state court proceedings, including irregularities in how a judge was assigned to his case, violated his constitutional rights. A federal district court dismissed his complaint, ruling that some claims were barred by a doctrine that limits federal courts from reviewing state-court judgments, and that the rest failed to state a valid claim. The court also denied Gomez permission to amend his complaint and barred him from future filings without meeting certain conditions. The Sixth Circuit Court of Appeals affirmed that ruling.

## What the Court is being asked

Gomez asked the Court to decide two things: first, whether the rule limiting federal review of state-court judgments should have blocked his claims, given that he says his injuries came from officials' conduct rather than from any court judgment itself; and second, whether courts may refuse to let a plaintiff fix problems in a complaint, and bar future filings, without first giving the plaintiff a chance to amend.

## Where it stands

The petition was distributed for the Court's conference on September 28, 2026. On October 5, 2026, the Court denied the petition.

## What each outcome would mean

By denying the petition, the Court leaves the Sixth Circuit's ruling in place: Gomez's lawsuit remains dismissed, the denial of his request to amend his complaint stands, and the restrictions on his future court filings continue to apply. The denial does not mean the Court agreed with the lower court's reasoning; it simply leaves that decision as the final word in this case.
