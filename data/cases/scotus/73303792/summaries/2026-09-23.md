## What happened

John Paul Gomez, representing himself, sued various state and local officials, including judges and a court clerk, under a federal civil-rights law, claiming they caused constitutional harm through their handling of state domestic-relations and enforcement proceedings. A federal district court in Ohio dismissed the case, holding that some claims were barred because they were too closely tied to state-court rulings, and that the rest failed to state a valid legal claim. The court also refused to let Gomez fix the complaint through an amendment and barred him from filing further suits without meeting certain conditions. The Sixth Circuit Court of Appeals affirmed that outcome.

## What the Court is being asked

The case raises two issues. First, it asks when federal courts may refuse to hear a civil-rights lawsuit on the theory that it is really just an attack on a state court's judgment, as opposed to a claim about separate wrongdoing by officials that happens to relate to state proceedings. Second, it asks whether a court can deny someone the chance to fix or amend their complaint, and can restrict their ability to file future lawsuits, without first giving them a meaningful opportunity to address the problems the court identified.
