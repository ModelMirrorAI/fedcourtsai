# Forecast: what the Court will do with No. 26-339, FS Medical Supplies, LLC v. Tanner Pharma UK Limited

**Disposition.** More likely denied than granted, but this is a live grant
candidate: P(grant) 0.30. The call for a response issued on October 6, 2026
means the petition will not be decided at the October 16 conference it was
first distributed for. Expect the brief in opposition in late November or early
December (a 30-day extension past the November 5 due date is routine), a reply
about two weeks later, and a decision on an order list in January or February
2027.

**Distributions and relists.** The snapshot shows one distribution (September
30, 2026, for the October 16 conference). The response request pulls the
petition off that conference, so it will be redistributed once the opposition
and reply are in; that redistribution alone resolves the relist-increment
claim. From there I expect one or two further distributions in total: a
petition that drew a call for a response and an amicus brief is the kind the
Court holds over at least once while a chambers checks the depth of the split,
so a single relist after the redistribution is roughly an even bet. The only
route to no further distribution is a settlement or withdrawal in the next two
months, which the underlying $200 million dispute and the parties' investment
in this question make unlikely.

**CVSG.** No. The question is a pure reading of the diversity statute between
private commercial parties, with no federal program, agency, or sovereign
interest at stake. The Court has decided its recent diversity-jurisdiction
cases (Hertz, Americold, Lincoln Property, Grupo Dataflux) without inviting
the Solicitor General, and nothing here distinguishes it.

**Which question, if any.** The petition presents one question, and if the
Court takes the case it will take it as written: whether section 1332(a)(3)
confers jurisdiction where the sole plaintiff is an LLC with both state and
foreign citizenship, the defendants include state and foreign citizens, and the
state citizenships on opposite sides are completely diverse. There is nothing
to narrow. If granted, the likely ground of decision is textual: whether
"citizens of different States" in paragraph (a)(3) is satisfied by an entity
whose attributed citizenships include a State, or whether (as the Fourth
Circuit held) each attributed citizenship must independently fit the
paragraph. I would expect a reversal if granted, since the Court generally
grants in this posture to disturb an outlier rule, but that is conditional and
not a forecast about the petition.

**Summary disposition versus plenary review.** Conditional on a grant, plenary
review, not a summary route: there is no intervening decision for a GVR, and
the Court does not summarily reverse a reasoned, published, unanimous circuit
holding on a question the petition itself calls an open split. Conditional
probability of a cert-order disposition: 0.04.

**Dissent from or statement respecting denial.** Unlikely. A technical
diversity question draws separate writings on denial only rarely, even when a
Justice was interested enough to call for a response. If one appears it would
most plausibly be a short statement respecting the denial noting the split and
inviting a better vehicle, from a Justice with federal-courts interests
(Thomas or Gorsuch), not a dissent. Conditional probability: 0.06.
