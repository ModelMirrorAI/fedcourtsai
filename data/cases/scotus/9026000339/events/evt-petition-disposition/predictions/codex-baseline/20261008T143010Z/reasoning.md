## Why denial remains the modal disposition

The central uncertainty is whether the Court regards the disagreement as sufficiently settled and consequential to warrant review now. Tango Music dates to 2003, and the Fourth Circuit reportedly relies on the intervening Grupo Dataflux decision. My separate MCP read of Grupo Dataflux v. Atlas Global Group, L.P., 541 U.S. 567, 568–71 (2004), opinion 9434608, confirms that its principal issue concerns whether a post-filing citizenship change cures an initial diversity defect; its defendant was a Mexican corporation rather than the mixed defendant alignment presented here. That supports the petition's distinction, but does not itself resolve this petition's statutory interpretation. A respondent could still contend that the older Seventh Circuit treatment did not analyze the precise entity-versus-member objection and needs reconsideration rather than Supreme Court intervention.

The Fifth Circuit comparison involves corporate citizenship and the Second Circuit comparison a different permanent-resident question. I therefore do not count the petition's whole collection of authorities as a mature, broad, four-circuit split. Without the opposition or the full decision below, I leave substantial probability on a denial despite the strong petition. The 42% estimate is sensitive to those missing adversarial materials and should not be read as finely measured precision.
