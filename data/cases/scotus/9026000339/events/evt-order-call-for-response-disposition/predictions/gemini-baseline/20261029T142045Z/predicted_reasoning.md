# Predicted Reasoning: *FS Medical Supplies, LLC v. Tanner Pharma UK Limited, et al.* (No. 26-339)

## Procedural Trajectory and Disposition

The Court will **deny** the petition for a writ of certiorari without separate opinion after receiving the respondents' briefs in opposition.

1. **Further Distribution (`relist-increment`)**: The docket currently records **1 distribution** (distributed on September 30, 2026, for the October 16, 2026 Conference) following the respondents' September 21, 2026 waivers of the right to respond. Because the Court called for a response on October 6, 2026 (due November 5, 2026), the petition was pulled from the October 16 Conference list and is virtually certain to be **distributed at least once more** once the briefs in opposition and petitioner's reply are filed. Beyond that mandatory post-response redistribution, the petition is unlikely to draw multiple true conference-to-conference relists; it will likely be resolved at the first conference following post-response distribution (a terminal distribution count of 2), or at most after a single conference relist.

2. **Call for the Views of the Solicitor General (`cvsg-increment`)**: No CVSG has been issued, and the Court will **not** invite the Solicitor General to file a brief. The question presented concerns a pure statutory construction of the diversity jurisdiction statute, 28 U.S.C. § 1332(a)(3), in private commercial litigation between an LLC and corporate/individual defendants. No federal agency administers § 1332, and no federal programmatic, regulatory, or foreign-relations interest is implicated that would warrant soliciting the Executive Branch's views.

3. **Question Presented if Granted**: If the Court were to grant certiorari, it would take the single Question Presented as framed in the petition: whether 28 U.S.C. § 1332(a)(3) confers diversity jurisdiction over a civil action in which the sole plaintiff is an LLC with both state and foreign citizenship (through its members), the defendants include both state and foreign citizens, and the state citizenships on opposite sides of the caption are completely diverse.

4. **Summary Disposition vs. Plenary Review (`summary-disposition-route`)**: Conditional on a grant, the Court will set the case for plenary merits briefing and oral argument rather than disposing of it summarily in the cert order. There is no intervening Supreme Court precedent calling for a grant, vacate, and remand (GVR), and the Fourth Circuit's published opinion relies directly on *Grupo Dataflux v. Atlas Global Group, L.P.*, 541 U.S. 567 (2004), and *Carden v. Arkoma Associates*, 494 U.S. 185 (1990), foreclosing any argument that the lower court committed an obvious error warranting summary reversal. If granted and decided on the merits, the Court would likely affirm or narrowly construe § 1332(a)(3) in light of the traditional complete-diversity rule and *Carden*'s member-by-member attribution rule for unincorporated entities.

5. **Dissents or Statements Respecting Denial (`dissent-from-denial`)**: When the Court denies certiorari, no Justice is expected to author a dissent from or statement respecting the denial. While Justices Gorsuch and Thomas occasionally write separately on federal jurisdiction or civil procedure questions, this fact-bound commercial dispute—where petitioner already filed a third lawsuit in the district court after restructuring its LLC membership—is an unlikely candidate for a separate writing at the cert stage.
