The stronger reason for the adjustment is the apparent Fourth-Seventh Circuit conflict. The petition describes Tango Music as a sole, mixed domestic/foreign LLC plaintiff suing domestic and foreign defendants, with section 1332(a)(3) sustaining jurisdiction. Different foreign countries and defendant configurations do not obviously distinguish the relevant rule. The Fourth Circuit's contrary published holding presents a tractable federal statutory question with recurring consequences for LLC litigation. The appendix confirms a published, unanimous panel decision; this is not an unpublished error-correction petition. The QP and petition's account of Tango Music support the conflict assessment (petition pp. i, 2-5, 14-16; appendix pp. 1a-2a, 8a-10a).

Denial nevertheless remains the modal result. Tango Music predates Grupo Dataflux, and the Fourth Circuit reportedly disputed both its direct relevance and its consistency with that later decision. The claimed Fifth Circuit support involves a dual-citizen corporation rather than this LLC posture; the Second Circuit support is less square. Those authorities do not establish an uncontested, mature three-way split. The unanimous panel and absence of an opposition brief limit confidence that the petition's characterization will survive adversarial briefing. Jurisdictional citizenship corrections, a post-filing ownership transfer, and attempted alternative cures complicate the surrounding proceedings, even though the central statutory issue appears preserved and outcome-determinative.
