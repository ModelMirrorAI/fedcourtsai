**Anchor.** The band is `baseline`. Its bracketed `reached` rate in the sal-v4
segment table, pooled over OT2017–OT2025 (the rows strictly before OT2026),
comes to roughly 5% (Term values 4.0–5.9%, each n≈1,200–1,700). That is the
yardstick. For the paid scored segment as a whole, the statpack's
relist-0/1 and amicus cuts put a petition with one amicus brief at about 10%
grant and 5% GVR at disposition. Those cuts are measured where petitions ended
up, not at this moment.

**Adjustments up.**
- *The call for a response.* No committed table conditions on it. From general
  knowledge of the Court's practice, a paid petition that draws a CFR after a
  waiver is granted several times more often than the paid base rate, though
  most are still denied. A CFR means at least one Justice (or a clerk's pool
  memo) flagged the petition. I take that alone to lift the anchor to around
  10–12%.
- *Petition quality.* The split is acknowledged: the Fourth Circuit expressly
  rejected the Seventh Circuit's *Tango Music* (Posner, J.) on, as the petition
  puts it, materially identical facts. The question is pure law, decided in a
  published opinion, and outcome-determinative (the suit was dismissed for
  lack of jurisdiction). Subject-matter jurisdiction questions under § 1332 are
  a category the Court grants comparatively readily because they recur and need
  uniform answers. Experienced counsel (Arnold & Porter) and a Washington Legal
  Foundation amicus brief filed on the call day add a little.

**Adjustments down.**
- The split is shallow and old. Only one published circuit decision (2003) is
  squarely on the other side. The Fifth Circuit authority is an unpublished
  1993 table decision, and the Second Circuit case is only "in tension." A
  respondent will argue the question arises rarely at the appellate level and
  that the Fourth Circuit reading is textually defensible: the LLC is not *a*
  citizen of a State unless every member is, by analogy to complete-diversity
  doctrine under *Strawbridge*/*Carden*. The Court may agree the Fourth Circuit
  got it right and see no need to intervene. The respondents' waivers suggest
  they see little cert-worthiness, though now they must engage.
- The Court's grant rate has been low lately (OT2026 row 1.5% to date).

Net: about 0.20, above the CFR-alone figure because of the acknowledged split
and clean vehicle, but well short of even odds.
