## In brief

A medical-supplies company asks the Court to revive its contract lawsuit after lower courts found that one of its members' foreign citizenship defeated federal jurisdiction.

## What happened

FS Medical Supplies sued Tanner Pharma UK, TannerGAP, and related individuals in federal court, claiming they secretly cut FS Medical out of a profit-sharing deal involving COVID-era medical supply sales worth hundreds of millions of dollars. FS Medical is a limited liability company (LLC) whose members include citizens of Texas, California, and, it later disclosed, China. The defendants are citizens of North Carolina and the United Kingdom. A magistrate judge and the district court dismissed the case, finding no diversity jurisdiction because a citizen of a foreign country appeared on both sides. The Fourth Circuit Court of Appeals affirmed that dismissal.

## What the Court is being asked

The law lets federal courts hear lawsuits between citizens of different states even when foreign citizens are also involved, so long as there is genuine diversity among the state citizens on each side. The Court is asked whether that law still allows a federal lawsuit when the only plaintiff is a company that counts as a citizen of both a U.S. state and a foreign country, so long as its state citizenship is still completely different from the state citizenship of everyone on the other side.

## Where it stands

The petition was distributed for the Court's October 16, 2026 conference. Rather than act on it then, the Court asked the respondents to file a response, due November 5, 2026; the docket shows no further action since.

## What each outcome would mean

FS Medical, as the petitioner, is asking the Court to take up its case. If the Court grants the petition, it will hear full arguments and decide whether this kind of lawsuit belongs in federal court, without yet deciding who wins the underlying contract dispute. If the Court denies the petition, the Fourth Circuit's ruling stands, meaning FS Medical's case stays dismissed from federal court on jurisdictional grounds. The request for a response does not itself decide anything; it only means the Court wants more information before acting.
