## In brief

A medical-supply company asks the Court to revive its federal lawsuit after appeals judges ruled its own mixed citizenship blocked diversity jurisdiction.

## What happened

FS Medical Supplies, an LLC with members from Texas, California, and China, sued Tanner Pharma UK, TannerGAP, and the Bournes in federal court in North Carolina, alleging the defendants secretly cut it out of a profitable deal to supply COVID test kits to the United Kingdom government after promising not to. The defendants are citizens of North Carolina and the United Kingdom. Lower courts dismissed both of FS Medical's related suits, ruling that because FS Medical counts as a citizen of a foreign country as well as of two states, the case does not qualify as one between "citizens of different states" under the federal diversity statute. The Fourth Circuit affirmed that dismissal.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked whether a company that counts as a citizen of both a U.S. state and a foreign country can still use a federal law allowing suits between citizens of different states, where foreign citizens appear only as added parties, when its state citizenships are completely different from the state citizenships on the other side.

## Where it stands

The petition was distributed for the Court's October 16, 2026 conference; the docket shows no action from that conference, but the Court asked the respondents to respond by November 5, 2026, a deadline later extended to December 2, 2026.

## What each outcome would mean

FS Medical, which lost in the lower courts, is asking the Court to take up its case. If the Court grants the petition, it would hear full arguments and decide whether this kind of mixed-citizenship company can sue in federal court on these facts; granting review would not by itself decide who wins. If the Court denies the petition, the Fourth Circuit's ruling stays in place, and FS Medical's lawsuit remains dismissed, leaving it to pursue its claims, if at all, elsewhere.
