## In brief

A medical-supply company asks the Court to revive its lawsuit after lower courts found no federal jurisdiction because one of its owners is a Chinese citizen.

## What happened

FS Medical Supplies sued Tanner Pharma UK, TannerGAP, and two individuals in North Carolina federal court, claiming the defendants secretly cut it out of a profitable deal to sell COVID test kits to the United Kingdom government, costing it over $200 million. FS Medical is a company whose owners are citizens of Texas, California, and, as it later discovered, China; the defendants are citizens of North Carolina and the United Kingdom. The district court dismissed the case, ruling it lacked jurisdiction because a foreign citizen appeared on each side. The Fourth Circuit Court of Appeals affirmed that dismissal.

## What the Court is being asked

Federal law lets companies sue in federal court, rather than state court, when the dispute is between citizens of different states and foreign citizens are only additional parties on one side or both. The question is whether that rule covers a company whose owners include both American and foreign citizens, suing opponents whose American owners are based in a different state, even though a foreign citizen also happens to be linked to the company itself. FS Medical argues yes, since its American owners and the defendants' American owners are from different states; the Fourth Circuit held no, reasoning that the company's foreign owner means it cannot count as an American citizen at all for this purpose.

## Where it stands

The petition was distributed for the Court's October 16, 2026 conference. Before that conference, on October 6, the Court asked the opposing parties to respond to the petition, with a response due November 5, 2026; some respondents had earlier waived their right to respond, and a friend-of-the-court brief supporting the petition has also been filed.

## What each outcome would mean

FS Medical, which lost in the lower courts, is asking the Court to take up its case. If the Court grants the petition, it will hear full arguments and decide whether this kind of lawsuit belongs in federal court, without yet deciding who wins the underlying contract dispute. If the Court denies the petition, the Fourth Circuit's ruling stands, and FS Medical's case stays dismissed, though it could still try to pursue its claims in state court.
