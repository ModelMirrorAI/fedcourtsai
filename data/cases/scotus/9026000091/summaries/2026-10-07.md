Richard Ross asks the Court to revive his bid for attorneys' fees after the government seized his funds, then dropped its forfeiture case and returned his money.

## What happened

The government seized money from Richard Ross's bank account, suspecting it was tied to a fraud scheme, and sued to forfeit it. After Ross fought the case for about a year and prepared to seek summary judgment, the government dropped its case and returned his funds. Ross then sought attorneys' fees under a federal law letting claimants who "substantially prevail" in forfeiture cases recover fees. The district court denied fees, reasoning Ross never got a court judgment in his favor. The Second Circuit Court of Appeals affirmed, holding Ross did not qualify because the dismissal did not conclusively and permanently reject the government's forfeiture claim.

The Court is also asked whether losing the chance to collect those fees, because the case was dismissed without conditions protecting that right, counts as a legal harm that judges must guard against when they approve such dismissals.
