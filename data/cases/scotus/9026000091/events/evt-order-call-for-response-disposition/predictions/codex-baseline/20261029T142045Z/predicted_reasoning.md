# Forecast of the Court's action

The Court will most likely deny the petition after obtaining the requested
response. It will issue an ordinary certiorari order, not an opinion
endorsing the Second Circuit's interpretation of CAFRA. The statutory issue
and attention from eight amicus briefs will not, on balance, overcome the
absence of a clear binding circuit conflict on entitlement to fees and the
case's alternative fee-bar and dismissal-posture complications.

The record currently shows **one distribution**, for the September 28, 2026
conference, and **no CVSG**. After the response process, I expect at least
one additional distribution entry (98%), most likely one. My modal path is
denial at the first conference that considers the completed papers, without
an extended sequence of genuine conference hold-overs. A redistribution
after the requested response is not itself evidence that the Court held the
petition over after considering it. I expect disposition in late 2026 or
early 2027, rather than a year-long invited-views process.

A CVSG is extremely unlikely (0.5%): the United States is respondent and
its Solicitor General can address the federal interest through the response
already requested. That response's contents are not in the record, and this
forecast does not assume a recommendation.

If the Court grants review, the most likely question is QP1, focused on
whether a claimant who obtains return of property after a Rule 41(a)(2)
dismissal without prejudice substantially prevails under CAFRA. The Court
could leave QP2's broader legal-prejudice question aside or limit it to the
forfeiture context. If granted, an opinion would likely distinguish the
statutory fee standard from prevailing-party precedents and address the
role of judicial finality; this is conditional legal context, not a
prediction that such an opinion will issue.

Conditional on any grant, plenary briefing and argument is substantially
more likely than disposition in the cert order: 88% versus 12%. No
identified intervening decision supplies a straightforward GVR instruction,
and a summary reversal would have to resolve contested statutory and
procedural questions.

Conditional on denial, I expect **no noted dissent or statement** (90%).
If one appears, Thomas or Gorsuch is the leading prospect given the
forfeiture concerns discussed in the petition; Sotomayor is another plausible
writer. The structured authorship probabilities reflect that low-probability
possibility, not a forecast of a merits-opinion author or a public vote tally.
