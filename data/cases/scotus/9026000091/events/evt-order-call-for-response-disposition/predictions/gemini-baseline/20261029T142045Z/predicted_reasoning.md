## 2. Disposition and Scope of Review If Granted

- **Disposition (`disposition`: 0.10; `predicted_disposition`: `denied`)**: Once the Solicitor General files the Brief in Opposition, the Court will deny certiorari. Although eight *amicus curiae* briefs prompted a call for a response, the opposition will demonstrate that (1) every court of appeals to address Question 1 (the Second, Fifth, Eighth, and Ninth Circuits) agrees that a voluntary dismissal without prejudice does not make a CAFRA claimant a "substantially prevailing" party under 28 U.S.C. § 2465(b)(1)(A); (2) *Lackey v. Stinnie*, 604 U.S. 192 (2025), reaffirmed just last Term that statutory prevailing-party fee-shifting requires an enduring, judicially sanctioned change in the parties' legal relationship; (3) there is no genuine circuit conflict on Question 2 regarding Rule 41(a)(2) "plain legal prejudice"; and (4) the District Court's alternative holding that CAFRA's multiple-claims exception, 28 U.S.C. § 2465(b)(2)(C), independently bars fee recovery makes this case a poor vehicle.
- **Questions Presented If Granted (Conditional)**: If the Court were to grant certiorari, it would likely grant on both Questions Presented—or reformulate them into a single combined question asking whether the government's voluntary dismissal without prejudice of a civil asset forfeiture action either confers "substantially prevailing" status under 28 U.S.C. § 2465(b)(1)(A) or inflicts "plain legal prejudice" requiring dismissal with prejudice or fee conditions under Federal Rule of Civil Procedure 41(a)(2)—because the two questions operate as interlocking halves of Petitioner's challenge to non-preclusive forfeiture dismissals. If granted on the merits, the Court would likely affirm the Second Circuit under *Buckhannon* and *Lackey*, or else remand for consideration of whether a dismissal that effectively bars refiling due to the expiration of the statute of limitations alters the legal relationship.
- **Plenary Review vs. Summary Disposition (`summary-disposition-route`: 0.05)**: Conditional on a grant, the Court would almost certainly set the case for plenary briefing and oral argument rather than issuing a summary disposition (GVR or summary reversal). No pending or intervening Supreme Court decision favors Petitioner's position, and reversing the unanimous consensus of four circuits under a strict sovereign-immunity waiver statute would not be done summarily.

## 3. Separate Writings on Denial

- **Dissent or Statement Respecting Denial (`dissent-from-denial`: 0.12)**: There is a meaningful (~12%) probability that the denial order is accompanied by a statement respecting the denial of certiorari or a dissent from denial. Eight cert-stage amicus briefs from organizations such as the Institute for Justice, NACDL, Goldwater Institute, and Americans for Prosperity Foundation highlight systemic concerns that the government can seize property, litigate for months or years, and avoid CAFRA's mandatory fee-shifting provision by moving for a voluntary dismissal without prejudice under Rule 41(a)(2). Justice Thomas (`0.07`) and Justice Gorsuch (`0.06`) are the most likely authors of a separate statement or dissent, given their prior separate writings highlighting constitutional and statutory concerns with civil asset forfeiture practices in *Leonard v. Texas*, 580 U.S. 1178 (2017), and *Culley v. Marshall*, 601 U.S. 377 (2024).
