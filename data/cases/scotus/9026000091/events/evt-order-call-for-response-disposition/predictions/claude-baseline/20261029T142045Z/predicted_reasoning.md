# Ross v. United States (No. 26-91): forecast

**Call: denied (P(grant) 0.13).**

The docket shows one distribution: the 9/28/2026 conference, scheduled after the
United States waived its response. On Sep 9, 2026 the Court called for a response.
The government has since had its deadline extended to Nov 9, 2026. The petition
will be distributed again once the response and any reply are in, most likely for a
conference in early-to-mid December 2026. I expect one more distribution before a
decision, with a modest chance of a relist after that. No CVSG: the United States is
the respondent, so the Court gets the government's views through its own brief.

**Most likely outcome.** The Court denies cert, probably in December 2026 or January
2027. QP 1 (whether a forfeiture claimant who makes the government voluntarily
dismiss and return the property "substantially prevails" under 28 U.S.C.
2465(b)(1)) has no circuit split: the Second Circuit is the fourth court of appeals
to read the phrase as Buckhannon's "prevailing party". QP 2 (whether losing a fee
entitlement is "plain legal prejudice" under Rule 41(a)(2)) carries a real
three-way split (2d/8th, 11th, 9th). But it is a procedural side-question, and
whether it decides this case depends on how QP 1 comes out. The government will
likely argue that the circuits agree on QP 1 and that the case is a poor vehicle.

**If granted**, the Court would most likely take QP 1 as written, perhaps
reformulated around whether Buckhannon's "prevailing party" test governs CAFRA's
different wording (the Hardt and Lackey footnote line). It would take QP 2 only as a
fallback. Plenary review would be the route. A summary disposition is unlikely:
there is no intervening decision to GVR on, and a summary reversal against four
agreeing circuits is implausible.

**If denied**, a separate writing is possible but unlikely. Justice Gorsuch, and
less likely Justice Thomas, have written critically about civil-forfeiture practice,
so a statement respecting denial is the most plausible separate writing.
