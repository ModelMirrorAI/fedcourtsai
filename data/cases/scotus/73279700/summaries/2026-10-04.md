## In brief

A man seeks emergency Supreme Court orders stopping child-support enforcement and restoring his suspended licenses while he pursues a separate mandamus petition.

## What happened

Allen Watkins, representing himself, says an arbitration award from February 2024 should have been confirmed by the United States District Court for the District of Arizona under federal arbitration law. He says the district court refused to confirm it and the Ninth Circuit Court of Appeals has not acted on his mandamus petition, which he says it improperly converted into an appeal. Meanwhile, Arizona and Illinois child-support agencies have suspended his driver's licenses and garnished income, which he says has caused severe financial hardship for him and his children.

## What the Court is being asked

Watkins asks a single justice to pause all child-support enforcement actions against him, including wage garnishment and license suspensions, and to order his licenses reinstated immediately, while a separate request asking the Court to order lower courts to confirm his arbitration award is pending.

## Where it stands

The application for a stay was submitted to Justice Kagan, the circuit justice for the Ninth Circuit, on November 18, 2025. The docket shows no order yet resolving the application.

## What each outcome would mean

Watkins, the applicant, is asking for emergency relief. If granted, the order would pause enforcement by the Arizona and Illinois child-support agencies and restore his suspended licenses temporarily, while his related mandamus petition and the underlying arbitration dispute continue in the lower courts. If denied, the current suspensions, garnishments, and other enforcement actions would remain in effect while those proceedings continue. Either way, the stay decision itself would not resolve whether the arbitration award must be confirmed.
