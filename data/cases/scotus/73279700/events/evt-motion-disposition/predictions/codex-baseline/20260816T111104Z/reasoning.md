# Rationale

I assign a 0.002 probability to an unqualified grant and predict denial. There is no committed interim baseline for this OT2025 cell: the strictly prior pool contains only OT2024, with 14 grants among 47 resolved substantive applications (29.8%), below the pre-registered 50-resolution floor. I therefore did not substitute that descriptive rate, the pack-wide rate, the current-Term rate, or a cert rate as a baseline.

The case-specific showing warrants a very large downward adjustment from the broad descriptive application cohort. The six-page pro se application asks Justice Kagan to halt Arizona and Illinois child-support enforcement, restore driver and several other licenses, halt adverse reporting, and keep that relief in place while a companion mandamus matter proceeds and until a district court confirms a claimed arbitration award. It supplies no award, arbitration agreement, lower-court order, or record support. Its assertion that confirmation is a ministerial duty does not address the necessary agreement, jurisdiction, service, venue, or defenses. It also seeks sweeping affirmative relief against state agencies rather than preservation of an identified lower-court judgment, while its own account identifies an available Ninth Circuit proceeding. Those defects make a clear and indisputable right, a fair prospect of reversal or certiorari, and the requested mandatory relief exceptionally unlikely.

The application alleges substantial irreparable harm: loss of work, threatened homelessness, garnishment, suspended driver licenses, and harm to dependent children. I keep the grant probability above zero for that asserted urgency, but the filing says the harm had already continued for more than twenty months and offers no supporting exhibits. It also does not engage the interests of child-support beneficiaries or the states.

The frozen record shows no response request, no referral to the full Court, and zero amicus briefs. The weak and individualized filing makes a response request (0.003) or additional amicus filing (0.001) especially unlikely. Referral (0.02) is somewhat more plausible because a Circuit Justice can refer an application without first requesting a response, but direct denial remains overwhelmingly more likely.

No filed-document text was provisioned under `record/documents/`. I downloaded only the application PDF linked in the snapshot. It is a scanned six-page filing with no extractable text layer, so I rendered and visually reviewed all six pages. I did not search for this case's present docket or disposition. The snapshot is dated August 16, 2026 but contains only the November 2025 application entry; I treated that silence as potentially incomplete or stale, not as evidence that the application remained pending or as outcome evidence. The lower-court and arbitration posture is therefore the applicant's unverified account, which is the main remaining uncertainty.
