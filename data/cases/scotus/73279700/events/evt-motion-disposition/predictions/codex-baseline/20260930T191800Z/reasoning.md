## Why 4%, rather than 20%

The adjustment is judgmental, not an estimated conditional rate. The tiny prior pool is not stratified by individual-applicant status and may represent a materially different case mix; I did not inspect its constituent applications. This record supplies only an individual applicant's apparent self-represented request involving a district court. It supplies no identifiable broad legal question, supported harm showing, or affirmative attention signal. Absence of those details is not proof that the application lacks merit, and zero escalation at arrival is normal, not an adverse finding by the Court. Nevertheless, my subjective assessment of this sparse individual-application profile places relief substantially below the pooled rate. I retain 4% rather than near-certainty of denial to allow for unobserved urgency, meritorious error, or a significant underlying question.

For legal context only, I retrieved the general stay standard in *Hollingsworth v. Perry*, 558 U.S. 183, 190 (2010), through CourtListener's opinion search and a targeted passage lookup (opinion 9413203). A stay pending certiorari requires sufficient prospects of review and reversal and likely irreparable harm; close cases also involve balancing equities. That authority is not a factual analogue and does not establish what procedural vehicle Watkins ultimately seeks. It explains why a bare filing entry provides little affirmative support for extraordinary relief. The opinion predates this application's arrival. The attempted official-site web retrievals returned no usable content and contributed no evidence.

The increment probabilities are likewise subjective: **8%** for a response request, **18%** for full-Court referral, and **2%** for an amicus-count increase. They reflect the individual-applicant profile and missing evidence of broader participation, with referral more plausible than a response request because referral need not signal merits interest. None is presented as a published conditional base rate. No Justice-level vote is forecast. I leave the stakes score explicitly null because an unknown subject and unknown practical reach do not justify equating low estimated relief odds with low significance.

The principal reasons to discount this forecast are the absent application and lower-court reasoning, the poorly matched and incompletely parsed base-rate population, and the unverified present status of an old forward-labeled arrival record.
