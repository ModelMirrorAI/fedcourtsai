## In brief

StandWithUs and two students ask the Court to revive their lawsuit claiming MIT was deliberately indifferent to campus antisemitism, after lower courts dismissed it.

## What happened

Two MIT students and an advocacy group sued MIT, alleging that campus protests following the October 7, 2023 attacks created a hostile environment for Jewish and Israeli students, and that MIT's response violated Title VI, a federal law barring discrimination at schools receiving federal funds. A federal district court dismissed the suit, and the First Circuit Court of Appeals affirmed, ruling both that the complaint did not plausibly allege an actionable hostile environment and, separately, that MIT had not been deliberately indifferent to it. The students and StandWithUs then asked the Supreme Court to review the hostile-environment ruling.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked to decide two things: first, whether a student bringing this kind of discrimination claim must show that the people doing the harassing acted with discriminatory intent, or whether it is enough that a reasonable student would find the environment hostile; and second, whether speech otherwise protected by the First Amendment can still count toward a hostile-environment claim, or whether such speech must be left out of that analysis entirely.

## Where it stands

The petition has been distributed for several conferences, most recently October 16, 2026, after full briefing including a brief in opposition from MIT, a reply from the petitioners, and multiple amicus briefs; the docket does not yet show a decision on whether the Court will hear the case.

## What each outcome would mean

StandWithUs and the two students are asking the Court to take up the case and reverse the First Circuit's ruling. If the Court grants review, it would hear full arguments and decide the legal questions itself, without yet deciding who wins. If the Court denies review, the First Circuit's decision affirming dismissal of the lawsuit stands, meaning MIT's win in the lower courts remains in place and the case ends there.
