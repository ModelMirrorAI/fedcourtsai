## In brief

A Jewish advocacy group and two students ask the Court to revive their discrimination lawsuit against MIT over its response to campus protests following the October 7, 2023 attacks.

## What happened

StandWithUs Center for Legal Justice and two MIT students sued MIT, claiming campus protests after October 7, 2023 created a hostile environment for Jewish and Israeli students and that MIT responded with deliberate indifference, violating a federal law barring discrimination at schools that receive federal funding. A federal district court dismissed the case, and the First Circuit Court of Appeals affirmed on two separate grounds: that the complaint did not plausibly describe an actionable hostile environment, and that, even if it did, MIT's escalating response to the protests was not clearly unreasonable. MIT opposes further review; the petitioners say the ruling conflicts with other appeals courts' decisions.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked to decide whether a plaintiff bringing this kind of claim must show that the people engaging in harassment personally intended to discriminate, or whether it is enough that the conduct objectively created a hostile environment. It is also asked whether speech otherwise protected by the First Amendment can still count as harassment supporting such a claim, or whether the Constitution bars treating that speech as a basis for liability.

## Where it stands

The petition has been distributed for several conferences, most recently one scheduled after the snapshot date, and the docket does not yet show any decision on whether the Court will hear the case.

## What each outcome would mean

StandWithUs and the two students, as petitioners, are asking the Court to take up the case. If the Court grants review, it would hear full arguments on these questions and decide them, without yet determining who wins. If the Court denies review, the First Circuit's decision affirming dismissal of the case stands, leaving MIT's win in place. A denial would not mean the Court agrees with the lower court's reasoning, only that the case ends there.
