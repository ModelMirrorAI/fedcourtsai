## In brief

Jewish MIT students and an advocacy group ask the Court to revive their civil rights lawsuit against MIT over its response to campus protests following the October 7, 2023 attacks.

## What happened

StandWithUs Center for Legal Justice and two MIT students sued MIT under Title VI, a law barring race and national-origin discrimination at federally funded schools, claiming campus protests after October 7, 2023 created a hostile environment for Jewish and Israeli students and that MIT was deliberately indifferent to it. A federal district court dismissed the case, and the First Circuit Court of Appeals affirmed, ruling both that the allegations did not show an actionable hostile environment and, separately, that MIT's response was not clearly unreasonable. The First Circuit denied rehearing.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked whether someone claiming a hostile environment under this law must show that the people harassing them acted with discriminatory intent, and whether the free-speech guarantee prevents such a claim from resting on speech that would otherwise be constitutionally protected.

## Where it stands

The petition has been distributed for several conferences, most recently one scheduled after the snapshot date, after full briefing including MIT's opposition, the students' reply, and numerous amicus briefs; the docket shows no decision yet on whether the Court will hear the case.

## What each outcome would mean

StandWithUs and the students are asking the Court to take up the case and reverse the First Circuit's ruling, which favored MIT. If the Court grants review, it would hear full arguments and decide the legal questions; if it denies review, the First Circuit's decision affirming dismissal of the students' lawsuit stands, and MIT prevails. Denial would not mean the Court agrees the lower court was right, only that the case ends there without further review.
