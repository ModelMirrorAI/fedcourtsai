## Why not simply use 35%?

**Reasons to take review seriously.** The petition's two questions concern whether a harasser's subjective discriminatory intent is required for a Title VI hostile-environment claim and whether protected speech can support such a claim. The petition alleges conflicts across circuits; the reply presses the distinction between discriminatory causation and subjective animus, and between considering speech as context and punishing speech. Those are consequential legal questions rather than merely a demand that the Court reweigh one protest. Multiple amici, a requested response, and continued conference consideration support genuine interest. I treat the descriptions of campus incidents as allegations in litigation, not adjudicated facts.

**The principal downward adjustment is vehicle quality.** The appended First Circuit opinion, pp. 35a-37a, expressly proceeds on the alternative assumption that the alleged protests constitute actionable harassment and concludes that MIT was not deliberately indifferent. It describes escalating measures, including policy changes, organizational discipline, police presence, fencing, suspensions, arrests, and eventual removal of the encampment. MIT's opposition, pp. 15-18, argues that this independently sustains the judgment regardless of the questions presented. That is a substantial obstacle to using this case to settle either broader issue.

The reply, pp. 2-4, supplies a meaningful answer: the reasonableness of MIT's response depends on the severity of the harassment, so allegedly misclassifying the harassment could also taint the alternative holding. This is not frivolous, but it asks the Court to look past a deliberately stated alternative ground. As general precedent, Davis v. Monroe County Board of Education, 526 U.S. 629, 648-649 (1999), confirms both that the response is assessed in its known circumstances and that a court can find a response insufficiently unreasonable for liability at the dismissal stage. I verified that passage through the CourtListener copy of the lead opinion, opinion ID 9433803. Davis is a Title IX decision; its application here is the framework used in the provisioned Title VI briefing, not a claim that the statutes are interchangeable in every respect.

**The splits and preservation are contested.** The opposition distinguishes the petition's employment-law authorities from educational funding-recipient liability and disputes whether the First Circuit adopted the categorical rules attributed to it. It also raises preservation problems for the protected-speech question. The reply answers that the hostile-environment standards should align across statutes and that the panel's treatment of speech itself created the challenged rule. I do not accept either party's circuit-split count as independently established. These disputes make the petition less clean than its questions-presented page suggests.

Balancing national importance and sustained attention against the alternative holding and contested framing leaves the grant estimate modestly below the high-band anchor, at 32%, rather than near the ordinary petition floor. The adjustment is judgmental, not a fitted statistical estimate. A Justice could regard the alternative ground as infected by the same error and vote to grant; conversely, the relists could reflect a contemplated separate writing rather than a grant coalition. I cannot distinguish those explanations from the docket alone.

## Other probabilities and stakes

- **Further distribution: 55%.** With three distinct conferences already reflected, another distribution is slightly more likely than not. The point forecast is one additional conference, while resolution following the already scheduled October 16 conference remains a substantial alternative. This is a forward increment estimate, not the terminal relist table read as a transition matrix.
- **New CVSG: 8%.** Interpretation of a federal funding statute gives the government a plausible interest, but the record contains no invitation or government merits position. I expect resolution without a CVSG; its absence does not imply a lack of federal interest.
- **Summary route given any grant: 15%.** No controlling intervening decision requiring a GVR is identified in the materials I used. Resolving contested legal standards and the vehicle objection would more naturally call for briefing and argument. A summary correction remains possible, but is not the principal grant scenario. This number is conditional, not an additional unconditional 15% grant chance.
- **Separate writing given denial: 38%.** The combination of a salient campus civil-rights dispute and repeated consideration makes a statement or dissent plausible. Nevertheless, no separate writing is the modal denial forecast. This is an aggregate existence forecast, not a forecast of particular Justices' votes.
- **Stakes: 0.82.** The potential rule would affect educational access, university responses to harassment, and political expression nationally. That is distinct from the chance this procedurally complicated petition obtains review.
