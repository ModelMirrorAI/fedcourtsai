# Forecast of the Court's action

The Court will most likely **deny certiorari**, leaving the First Circuit's judgment in place without a merits opinion. My probability of any grant is **32%**.

The baseline records **three distinct conference distributions**, ending with the already scheduled October 16, 2026 conference. I forecast **one further distribution** beyond that state before disposition, with **55%** probability of at least one additional distribution. I expect resolution in late October or November 2026 rather than a prolonged hold. The timing is a forecast, not a statement that an order has occurred.

I expect **no call for the Solicitor General's views**; the probability of a new CVSG is **8%**. If one issues, the disposition would likely move outside the short horizon just forecast.

The central barrier to plenary review will be the lower court's alternative conclusion that MIT's response was not deliberately indifferent even assuming actionable harassment. A denial would not establish the Court's agreement with either the lower court's hostile-environment analysis or that alternative ground, and an unexplained denial would not reveal which concern prevailed.

**If review is granted**, I expect plenary briefing and argument rather than immediate disposition: **15% probability of a summary disposition conditional on a grant**, versus 85% for a non-summary grant route. The most likely focus would be a narrowed version of the first question: whether plaintiffs must plead the nonparty harassers' subjective discriminatory animus, beyond conduct tied to a protected trait, to establish the hostile-environment element under Title VI. The Court would likely avoid a categorical answer that all protected political speech either must or cannot count; any speech issue would instead concern its contextual role in the pleaded hostile environment and the distinction between considering expression and compelling its suppression. Any eventual correction would have to address why the alternative deliberate-indifference ground does not independently sustain dismissal. This is conditional merits context, not an unconditional forecast of a merits opinion.

**If review is denied**, no separate writing is the more likely result, but I assign **38%** to at least one dissent from, or statement respecting, denial. Such a writing would most likely emphasize the importance of the campus-harassment questions or explain why this vehicle is unsuited to resolving them. I make no per-Justice vote or authorship prediction.
