# Forecast — StandWithUs Center for Legal Justice v. MIT (No. 25-1404)

**State the docket shows.** Three distributions: for the 9/28/2026 long
conference (re-distributed on Sep 9 after the reply), then for 10/9/2026, then
for 10/16/2026. The petition has been considered and relisted twice; no CVSG.
MIT initially waived, the Court requested a response (Jul 28), and eight amicus
briefs support the petitioners.

**Disposition.** More likely denied than granted (P(grant) ≈ 0.30). The Court
is plainly giving the petition close attention, but the First Circuit rested on
an independent, alternative ground — no plausible allegation of deliberate
indifference — that the questions presented do not challenge. That makes any
answer to the hostile-environment questions potentially non-outcome-determinative,
and the second QP faces a waiver argument (counsel disclaimed at oral argument
below that Title VI could compel suppression of protected speech).

**Relists.** I expect at least one further distribution after the 10/16
conference (≈55%): the two relists after the long conference look more like a
separate writing being prepared, or a Justice holding out for a grant, than a
quick resolution, and either path typically takes additional conferences.

**CVSG.** Unlikely (≈6%). The Court already called for a response and has
relisted on its own; a CVSG this late in a twice-relisted posture is rare,
though the United States does have an enforcement interest in Title VI campus
antisemitism.

**If granted.** The Court would most likely take QP 1 (whether the harassers'
subjective discriminatory intent/animus is an element of a Title VI hostile
environment claim), possibly reformulated to also reach whether the deliberate
indifference holding stands independently; QP 2 (First Amendment "protected
speech" bar) is the weaker vehicle given the waiver dispute and might be left
aside. Plenary review rather than a summary route: there is no intervening
decision to GVR on, and a summary reversal of a pleading dismissal with an
unchallenged alternative ground is improbable (≈7% conditional on grant).

**If denied.** A dissent from denial or statement respecting denial is about a
coin flip (≈50%) — the twice-relisted, no-CVSG pattern is the classic signature
of a writing in progress. If one issues, the likeliest authors are Justices
Thomas, Alito, and/or Gorsuch, possibly a statement noting the alternative
ground as the reason review is unwarranted now.
