## What happened

Morris Mullins was sentenced to life without parole for a murder he committed at 17. Years later, citing Supreme Court rulings on juvenile sentencing, he argued his sentence was illegal because the judge's remarks suggested he believed Mullins could change. The Utah Supreme Court, divided 3-2, vacated Mullins's sentence, reasoning that if a juvenile is capable of change, a life-without-parole sentence is unconstitutional. The court sent the case back for resentencing. Utah asked for rehearing, and the state's highest court amended its opinion, adding a second, independent reason for vacating the sentence; Utah's second rehearing request was denied. Utah then asked the Supreme Court to review the decision.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked whether the Constitution's ban on cruel and unusual punishment allows a judge to sentence a juvenile convicted of murder to life without parole even where the judge has found, or suggested, that the juvenile is not beyond rehabilitation.
