## In brief

Utah's bid to review a ruling that threw out a man's juvenile life-without-parole sentence over hints he could change was denied.

## What happened

In 2002, Morris Mullins was sentenced to life without parole for a murder he committed at 17. Years later, citing new Supreme Court rulings on juvenile sentencing, Mullins argued his sentence was illegal because the sentencing judge had suggested he had "a chance to change." The Utah Supreme Court, divided 3-2, agreed and vacated his sentence, holding that judges' comments raised significant concerns the judge believed Mullins capable of change and had not properly considered his youth, making life without parole unconstitutional. The court sent the case back for resentencing. Utah asked the Utah Supreme Court to reconsider; it refused.

## What the Court is being asked

Utah asked the Court to decide whether a sentencing judge can still impose life without parole on a juvenile convicted of murder even if the judge finds, or suggests, that the juvenile is capable of change rather than permanently beyond hope of reform. Mullins countered that the Court lacked authority to hear the case at all because no final sentence has yet been imposed, since his case was sent back for resentencing.

## Where it stands

The petition was distributed for several conferences between September and October 2026. On October 19, 2026, the Court denied the petition.

## What each outcome would mean

Utah, which asked the Court to take the case and overturn the ruling below, sought to restore the possibility of a life-without-parole sentence for Mullins. By denying the petition, the Court leaves the Utah Supreme Court's decision in place: Mullins's original sentence stays vacated, and the case returns to a Utah court for resentencing, where a lesser sentence may now be considered. The denial does not mean the Court agreed with the Utah Supreme Court's reasoning, only that it left that ruling undisturbed.
