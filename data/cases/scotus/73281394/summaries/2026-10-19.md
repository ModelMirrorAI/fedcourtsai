Morris Mullins was sentenced to life without parole for a murder he committed at 17, before the U.S. Supreme Court limited such sentences to juveniles whose crimes show permanent incorrigibility. The majority found the sentencing judge's remark about Mullins having "a chance to change" raised serious concerns that the judge believed Mullins capable of reform and had not properly considered his youth, making the sentence illegal under Utah procedural rules. Two justices dissented, reading Supreme Court precedent to allow the sentence regardless. Utah's request for rehearing was denied, and Utah then asked the U.S. Supreme Court to review the case. Mullins opposed, arguing the Court lacked jurisdiction and that the case was a poor vehicle for deciding the issue.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court was asked whether a judge can still sentence a juvenile convicted of murder to life without parole even after finding, or suggesting, that the juvenile is capable of change rather than permanently beyond redemption.
