## What happened

Morris Mullins was 17 when he pleaded guilty to aggravated murder in Utah and received a sentence of life without parole. Years later, relying on Supreme Court rulings limiting harsh sentences for juveniles, Mullins challenged that sentence. The Utah Supreme Court, divided 3-2, vacated his sentence, reasoning the judge's comments at sentencing raised concerns he may have viewed Mullins as capable of change, which the majority said would make life without parole unconstitutional. The court sent the case back for resentencing. Utah asked for rehearing, then petitioned the U.S. Supreme Court.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked whether the Constitution's ban on cruel and unusual punishment forbids sentencing a juvenile to life without parole once a court finds, or suggests, that the juvenile could still change, or whether courts may still impose that sentence as long as they considered the offender's youth. Mullins's brief separately argues the Court has no authority to hear the case yet because the Utah court's ruling was not a final judgment, and that the case is a poor vehicle to decide the question.
