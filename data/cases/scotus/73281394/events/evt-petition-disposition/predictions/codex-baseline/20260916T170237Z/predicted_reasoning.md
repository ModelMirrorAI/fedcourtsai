# Forecast

The Court will deny Utah's petition, most likely without a separate writing. The principal obstacles are the unfinished resentencing proceedings, uncertainty whether answering the question presented would eliminate the separate youth-consideration basis for remand, and a less square conflict than the petition's numerical description suggests. A denial will not endorse either side's interpretation of the Eighth Amendment.

The snapshot shows **one conference distribution**, entered July 8 for the September 28, 2026 conference, and **no CVSG**. The August 25 reply marked distributed is not a second conference distribution. From this state, I assign **30%** to at least one further distribution. My modal forecast is zero further distributions and an order in late September or early October 2026. If the petition receives additional consideration, one or two further distributions are likelier than a prolonged hold. Timing is contextual, not a scored claim.

I assign **1.5%** to a new CVSG. The Court can assess this state criminal-sentencing dispute from the opposing briefs and its own precedents without soliciting the federal government's views. The ordinary path is disposition without that intervening step.

If the Court grants, it will focus on whether a discretionary sentencing court may impose juvenile life without parole after affirmatively finding, or implicitly indicating, capacity for reform. The distinction between an actual finding and an ambiguous expression of hope will matter; I expect a grant to center on the latter feature of this record rather than reopen juvenile sentencing generally.

Conditional on any grant, I assign **35%** to disposition in the cert order and **65%** to further briefing and argument. Summary reversal enforcing Utah's reading of Jones's discretionary-sentencing rule is a plausible grant-side route, but the threshold and alternative-ground issues make plenary clarification likelier conditional on review. No identified intervening decision makes a routine GVR the natural path. If the Court reaches the merits, my directional expectation is rejection of a rule that an ambiguous expression of hope alone invalidates an otherwise constitutionally adequate discretionary sentence; that would not necessarily settle whether this sentencing proceeding adequately considered youth.

Conditional on denial, I assign **12%** to any noted dissent or statement respecting denial. My forecast is no such writing, and I do not predict a particular Justice or an unobservable cert vote lineup.
