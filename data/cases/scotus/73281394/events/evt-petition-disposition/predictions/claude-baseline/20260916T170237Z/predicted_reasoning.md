# Forecast: Utah v. Mullins, No. 25-1115 — petition disposition

**Bottom line.** The Court denies certiorari. P(any grant) = 0.14.

**Relists.** The docket shows **one** distribution, for the Conference of
September 28, 2026 (the long conference); the petition has never been relisted.
I put the chance of at least one further distribution at 0.38. A state
petitioner pressing a Jones v. Mississippi question is the kind of petition the
Court sometimes carries over a conference to weigh summary reversal or to let a
Justice write, but the §1257 finality defect and the fact-bound, state-rule
framing of the decision below make a clean denial out of the first conference
the modal path. If it is relisted, expect one or two further distributions, not
a long string.

**CVSG.** None. There is no federal party and no federal statute, and the
Solicitor General has no stake in a Utah resentencing — P = 0.03.

**Which question, if granted.** The State's question as written, likely
reformulated to the pure legal issue: whether, after Jones, the Eighth
Amendment bars life without parole for a juvenile murderer where the sentencer
finds or suggests the juvenile is capable of change. The Court would not engage
the Utah rule 22(e) "imposed in an illegal manner" framing, and it would have to
resolve the final-judgment question under Cox Broadcasting's third category
before reaching the merits.

**Summary disposition versus plenary review.** Conditional on a grant,
P(summary disposition in the cert order) = 0.30. The route to a summary
reversal is that the Utah majority's rule — an affirmative finding of
corrigibility bars JLWOP — reads as inconsistent with Jones's statement that a
discretionary sentencing procedure is "both constitutionally necessary and
constitutionally sufficient." Against it, the Court rarely summarily reverses
on a record this ambiguous (no affirmative finding below, an alternative
consider-youth-properly rationale in the opinion, and a jurisdictional
question the state court never addressed). No GVR: nothing intervening bears
on the question. If granted for plenary review, the case would be argued in
the October 2026 Term and decided by June 2027; the likely ground would be
Jones's process-only reading of Miller, reversing, on a 6–3 line.

**Dissent from denial.** Conditional on a denial, P(some Justice notes a
dissent or statement) = 0.12. The plausible authors are Justice Thomas or
Justice Alito, writing that state courts continue to misread Jones or that
Montgomery should be revisited. But a denial that rests on the interlocutory
posture is the sort no Justice bothers to write about, and the liberal
Justices have no reason to object to a denial that leaves a defendant-favorable
ruling in place.
