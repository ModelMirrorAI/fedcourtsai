## In brief

Hamed Kian asks the Supreme Court to overturn a 1970 ruling and hold that the Constitution requires 12-person juries in serious criminal cases, not Florida's six.

## What happened

Hamed Kian was convicted by a six-person Florida jury of five felony counts of practicing chiropractic medicine with a suspended license and sentenced to prison followed by probation. Florida, like several other states, uses six-person juries in non-capital cases, a practice the Supreme Court upheld in 1970 in Williams v. Florida. Kian argued on appeal that the Sixth and Fourteenth Amendments actually require 12 jurors, pointing to a more recent Supreme Court decision requiring unanimous jury verdicts that he says undermines the reasoning behind the six-person-jury ruling. Florida's Fourth District Court of Appeal rejected this argument and affirmed his conviction without a written opinion, since it is bound by the Supreme Court's existing precedent.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked whether the Constitution's guarantee of a jury trial in serious criminal cases requires the jury to have 12 members, meaning a state could no longer convict a defendant using a jury of six.

## Where it stands

The Court granted Kian's petition and heard full briefing, including dozens of friend-of-the-court briefs on both sides; the Solicitor General has asked to take part in oral argument. The case is awaiting a decision after merits briefing closed.

## What each outcome would mean

Kian, the defendant convicted below, is asking the Court to reverse his conviction. If the Court affirms, Florida's six-person-jury system stands and Kian's conviction remains in place. If the Court reverses, it would hold that defendants in serious criminal cases are entitled to 12-person juries, which could call into question Kian's conviction and similar convictions in Florida and other states using smaller juries. If the Court vacates, it would send the case back to the lower court without deciding which side is right, for further proceedings there.
