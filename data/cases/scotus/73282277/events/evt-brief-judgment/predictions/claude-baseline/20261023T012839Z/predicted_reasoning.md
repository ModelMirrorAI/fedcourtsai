# Kian v. Florida (No. 25-6623) — predicted judgment

**Judgment class: reversed.** The Court reverses the Fourth District Court of
Appeal's affirmance of Kian's convictions by a six-person jury and remands.
P(judgment below disturbed) = 0.70.

**Vote lineup: 6–3.** Majority: Gorsuch (writing), Thomas, Sotomayor,
Kavanaugh, Barrett, Jackson. Dissent: Alito (writing), Roberts, Kagan. The
majority roughly tracks the Ramos v. Louisiana coalition minus departed
Justices; Roberts and Alito dissented in Ramos on stare decisis grounds, and
Kagan joined that dissent. The least certain votes are Barrett and Kavanaugh
(stare decisis / reliance) and Kagan (could join a pro-defendant majority).

**Ground.** The majority applies Ramos's method — the Sixth Amendment's jury
right carries the features the term "trial by jury" had at the founding — and
holds that a 12-member jury was one of them for serious crimes, as Thompson v.
Utah, Maxwell v. Dow and Patton v. United States said before Williams. It
formally overrules Williams v. Florida (1970) rather than holding Ramos had
already done so, rejecting Williams's functionalism as Ramos rejected Apodaca's.
Stare decisis is addressed in Ramos-style terms: Williams was egregiously
wrong, its reasoning is inconsistent with Ramos and Ballew, and reliance is
answered by Edwards v. Vannoy (no retroactivity on collateral review), which
confines disruption to cases on direct appeal.

**Breadth.** A categorical rule for all serious-offense prosecutions in every
State, not a Florida-specific holding; preservation and harmless-error
questions are left for remand and the lower courts.

**Procedural exits.** A DIG is unlikely: preservation was conceded at the cert
stage and the question is squarely presented. An equally divided Court
requires a recusal, and none is suggested on the record.

**If the Court affirms (≈30%).** The likely ground is stare decisis: reliance
of the roughly seven States that seat fewer than 12 jurors in felony cases, and
Ballew's six-juror floor as a workable line. A majority that also holds
Williams correct on the original meaning is less likely.
