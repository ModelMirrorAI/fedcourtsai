# Forecast of the Court's judgment

The Court will reverse the Fourth District Court of Appeal's judgment and remand for further proceedings. It will hold that a defendant tried for a serious criminal offense is entitled to a twelve-member jury, overrule Williams v. Florida's contrary criminal-jury holding, and reject Florida's defense of its ordinary six-person felony jury. This forecasts a merits judgment, not a certiorari disposition: review has already been granted.

## Vote lineup

I forecast a 6–3 result for Kian. Gorsuch, Kavanaugh, Barrett, Sotomayor, and Jackson will join the majority; Thomas will concur in the judgment. Roberts, Alito, and Kagan will dissent. Thomas's separate rationale will favor the Fourteenth Amendment's Privileges or Immunities Clause rather than the majority's incorporation route. Authorship is not part of this forecast.

## Ground

The majority will treat the historical meaning of the constitutional jury as controlling, rather than asking whether six people can perform approximately the same function as twelve. The relevant distinction will be between a historically constitutive feature of the criminal jury and procedural practices that can change without eliminating that feature. The Court will conclude that twelve members, like unanimity, falls on the constitutive side.

Ramos will supply the principal methodological bridge, but the Court will formally overrule Williams rather than accept the petitioner's claim that Ramos already eliminated its precedential force. The majority will address stare decisis, treating Williams's weakened historical and functional premises as sufficient justification to reconsider it. Empirical concerns about smaller juries will reinforce the holding, not become a test requiring defendants to demonstrate actual prejudice in each case.

Florida's settled practice, retrial burdens, and infrastructure costs will not carry the day. The majority will distinguish the operational consequences of correcting a criminal-procedure rule from reliance that warrants retaining a mistaken definition of a constitutional right. It will not require a finding that Florida's particular jury-size law had discriminatory origins.

## Breadth and remaining proceedings

The holding will categorically govern the initial jury complement in serious criminal prosecutions, including prosecutions outside Florida. It will not be confined to chiropractic-license offenses or to defendants who can establish that their particular panel deliberated inadequately. The Court will leave civil juries and the later excusal of a juror in an initially twelve-person criminal jury for another case. It will not decide collateral retroactivity.

The Court will not treat Kian's acceptance of the selected panel, under then-binding law, as a dispositive knowing relinquishment of a twelve-juror entitlement. Nevertheless, a remand will leave room for state-court treatment of any remaining procedural or remedial questions; reversal does not itself order an acquittal or guarantee a particular retrial outcome.

The dissent will emphasize that Williams was a genuine majority precedent, unlike Apodaca's fractured incorporation reasoning, and that its six-person minimum has supplied an administrable rule for decades. It will give substantially greater weight to reliance and resist equating Ramos's unanimity holding with a command to restore every historical jury attribute.

I expect an argued decision during the October 2026 Term, most likely by June 2027. The supplied record does not establish an argument date. A DIG remains a live minority possibility because of the preservation and acceptance-of-the-panel questions, but it is not the predicted judgment; there is no disclosed recusal supporting an equally divided affirmance.
