# Predicted Court Reasoning: *Hamed Kian v. Florida* (No. 25-6623)

## 1. Predicted Judgment Class and Vote Lineup

- **Predicted Judgment**: **`reversed`** (judgment of the Florida Fourth District Court of Appeal reversed and case remanded for further proceedings not inconsistent with the opinion).
- **Predicted Vote Split**: **6–3** (`Neil M. Gorsuch` [author], `Clarence Thomas`, `Sonia Sotomayor`, `Elena Kagan`, `Amy Coney Barrett`, and `Ketanji Brown Jackson` in the majority; `John G. Roberts, Jr.`, `Samuel A. Alito, Jr.` [dissenting opinion], and `Brett M. Kavanaugh` dissenting).

## 2. Question Reached and Procedural Thresholds

The Court will reach the single Question Presented as framed by the petition: whether the Sixth and Fourteenth Amendments guarantee a defendant charged with a serious felony the right to a trial by a twelve-person jury, requiring the Court to overrule *Williams v. Florida*, 399 U.S. 78 (1970).

The Court will reject Florida's vehicle and harmless-error objections:
1. **Preservation / Summary Affirmance Below**: Because Petitioner raised a facial constitutional challenge to `Fla. Stat. § 913.10` before the Florida Fourth District Court of Appeal—which under Florida appellate precedent may be raised for the first time on direct appeal—and the Fourth DCA issued an unelaborated *per curiam* affirmance without invoking a state procedural bar, no adequate and independent state ground blocks review.
2. **Structural Error vs. Harmless Error**: The Court will reject Respondent's contention under *Neder v. United States*, 527 U.S. 1 (1999), that empaneling a six-person jury instead of a twelve-person jury is subject to harmless-error review based on the weight of the trial evidence. Depriving a felony defendant of half the constitutionally required tribunal is a structural defect in the constitution of the trial mechanism under *Weaver v. Massachusetts*, 582 U.S. 286 (2017), and *Ramos v. Louisiana*, 590 U.S. 83 (2020), not a discrete evidentiary or instructional omission whose effect on twelve jurors can be reconstructed from a six-juror transcript.
3. **Procedural Exit Risk**: Neither a dismissal as improvidently granted (`dismissed-as-improvidently-granted`) nor an equally divided affirmance (`affirmed-by-an-equally-divided-court`) is likely. The trial-level acceptance of the jury was squarely disclosed in the Brief in Opposition prior to the cert grant, and no recusal is indicated.

## 3. Majority Ground (`majority-ground`)

The majority will overrule *Williams v. Florida*, 399 U.S. 78 (1970), on originalist grounds directly paralleling *Ramos v. Louisiana*, 590 U.S. 83 (2020):
- **Original Public Meaning of "Jury"**: Tracing the common-law right from Blackstone's *Commentaries*, Hale, Coke, and Founding-era state practice through seventy years of pre-*Williams* Supreme Court precedent (*Thompson v. Utah*, 170 U.S. 343, 349–50 (1898); *Maxwell v. Dow*, 176 U.S. 581, 586 (1900); *Patton v. United States*, 281 U.S. 276, 288 (1930); *Duncan v. Louisiana*, 391 U.S. 145, 151–52 (1968)), the majority will hold that at the adoption of the Sixth Amendment in 1791, the phrase "trial, by an impartial jury" carried an established legal meaning: a body of twelve citizens rendering a unanimous verdict.
- **Repudiation of *Williams*'s Functionalism and Drafting-History Inference**: Following *Ramos*, 590 U.S. at 98–100, the majority will reject *Williams*'s two pillars: (a) its negative inference from unratified Senate drafting edits that removed James Madison's "accustomed requisites" phrasing, and (b) its functionalist assessment that six jurors perform the same "commonsense judgment" role as twelve. Just as *Ramos* held that courts may not trade away an ancient component of the Sixth Amendment jury guarantee through functionalist cost-benefit balancing, *Williams* erred by substituting 1970 social-science assumptions for the constitutional text's original meaning—assumptions that *Ballew v. Georgia*, 435 U.S. 223 (1978), and subsequent empirical research on deliberation quality, verdict variance, and minority representation soon undermined.
- ***Stare Decisis* Analysis**: Applying the *stare decisis* factors from *Ramos*, the majority will conclude that *Williams*'s reasoning is egregiously wrong, places Sixth Amendment jurisprudence in irreconcilable internal conflict post-*Ramos*, and originated in state practices (including Florida's 1877 post-Reconstruction reduction of jury size from twelve to six) that diluted jury participation. While acknowledging substantial state reliance in Florida and five other states on direct review, the majority will note that retroactive collateral disruption is bounded by *Edwards v. Vannoy*, 593 U.S. 255 (2021) (holding that new procedural rules do not apply retroactively on federal collateral review).

## 4. Breadth of the Holding (`ground-breadth`)

The majority will announce a **categorical constitutional rule** requiring a twelve-person jury in all serious state and federal felony prosecutions to which the Sixth Amendment right to jury trial attaches (offenses punishable by more than six months' imprisonment under *Baldwin v. New York*, 399 U.S. 66 (1970)), rather than a narrow or fact-bound ruling limited to Petitioner's offense. At the same time, the majority will explicitly cabin its holding to the Sixth Amendment criminal jury clause, leaving Seventh Amendment civil jury practice under *Colgrove v. Battin*, 413 U.S. 149 (1973), untouched (because the Seventh Amendment has not been incorporated against the states and contains distinct textual phrasing).

## 5. Separate Writings

- **Concurrence(s)**: Justice Thomas will concur to reiterate his view that the Sixth Amendment right to a twelve-person jury applies to the States through the Fourteenth Amendment's Privileges or Immunities Clause rather than the Due Process Clause. A concurrence (likely joined by Justices Sotomayor, Kagan, and Jackson) will emphasize the empirical evidence of six-person juries' disparate impact on minority representation and the Reconstruction/Jim Crow origins of Florida's 1877 statute.
- **Dissent**: Justice Alito (joined by Chief Justice Roberts and Justice Kavanaugh) will dissent, arguing that *stare decisis* and massive reliance interests—particularly roughly 5,000 pending direct-appeal felony convictions in Florida and long-standing statutory reliance across six states—forbid overruling a 56-year-old 7–1 precedent, and defending *Williams*'s reading of the Sixth Amendment's drafting history.
