# Forecast of the Court

## Judgment and votes

The Court will **reverse** the Fourth District's judgment and remand for proceedings consistent with a twelve-person-jury requirement. The predicted division is **6-3**. Thomas, Sotomayor, Gorsuch, Kavanaugh, Barrett, and Jackson will form the prevailing coalition; Roberts, Alito, and Kagan will dissent. Thomas may explain a distinct incorporation rationale. The forecast commits to the judgment and sides, not an opinion author or particular writing assignments.

## Majority ground

The majority will overrule Williams v. Florida. It will hold that the historically understood Sixth Amendment jury-trial guarantee includes twelve jurors for serious criminal prosecutions and that the guarantee applies against the states. Williams's conclusion that six jurors adequately perform a jury's functions will not justify departing from that constitutional meaning. Ramos's historical approach will support this conclusion, but the Court will expressly reconsider Williams rather than pretend Ramos already silently decided jury size.

Stare decisis will not preserve Williams. The majority will characterize its historical reasoning and functional substitute as insufficient, while distinguishing ordinary administrative reliance from a sufficient basis for retaining an erroneous constitutional rule. The holding will not depend on proving that every smaller jury produces a less reliable verdict or that Florida's current law was enacted with discriminatory intent.

## Breadth and remedy

The twelve-juror rule will be categorical for nonwaived jury trials of serious crimes, including noncapital and nonviolent felonies. It will not be confined to this defendant, this offense, or Florida's particular history. The majority will leave civil-jury requirements and collateral-review retroactivity outside its holding; those are not necessary to resolve this direct criminal appeal.

Florida's claim that the evidence was overwhelming will not itself establish that a constitutionally undersized jury was harmless. The Court will reject that as a sufficient basis to leave this summary affirmance intact. It will not treat ordinary assent to the selected panel as conclusively demonstrating an informed waiver of the constitutional jury-size claim. The remand may still require the state courts to address remaining procedural questions; reversal here need not itself order an immediate new trial.

The principal alternative judgment is vacatur with further consideration of preservation and remedy under the new constitutional rule. Affirmance preserving Williams and a DIG because of the record's preservation problem are real alternatives, but neither is the point forecast. There is no record-based reason to predict an equally divided Court. These alternatives are already included in the complement or disturbed portion of the 0.72 probability, as appropriate.
