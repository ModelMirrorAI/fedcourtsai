# Predicted outcome — Kian v. Florida, No. 25-6623 (merits, grant moment)

**Judgment class: reversed** (P(disturbed) = 0.76). Florida's Fourth DCA
summarily affirmed a conviction returned by a six-person jury; I expect the
Court to overrule *Williams v. Florida* (1970) and hold that the Sixth
Amendment, incorporated against the States, requires a twelve-person jury in
a trial for a serious offense, then reverse and remand. A "vacated and
remanded" label is the main alternative form of the same outcome (both count
as disturbed), most likely if the Court explicitly remands for the State's
harmless-error argument.

**Vote lineup: 6–3.** Majority: Gorsuch (likely author — he has called for
revisiting *Williams* since *Khorrami v. Arizona*), Thomas, Sotomayor,
Kavanaugh, Barrett, Jackson. Dissent: Roberts, Alito, Kagan, on stare decisis
and reliance grounds (the three *Ramos* dissenters' positions on
unanimity-precedent overruling). Thomas may write separately on the
Privileges or Immunities route, as in *Ramos*. A 5–4 or 7–2 split is
plausible; the swing Justices are Kavanaugh and Barrett on the stare decisis
factors, and Kagan on the merits.

**Ground.** The majority follows *Ramos*'s method: the common-law jury the
Framers knew had twelve members, *Williams*'s functional analysis departed
from text and history, and later empirical work undercut its premises
(*Ballew*'s line drawing at five). Stare decisis is rejected because
*Williams* was poorly reasoned and reliance is limited — *Edwards v. Vannoy*
forecloses retroactive collateral relief, confining the cost to cases on
direct review.

**Breadth.** Categorical: a twelve-person jury for every serious state
felony prosecution, not a rule confined to Kian's case. The Court is
unlikely to decide harmlessness itself; Florida's argument that a jury-size
error is harmless under *Neder* will be left for remand (or rejected as a
reason not to decide the question).

**Procedural exits.** A DIG is unlikely (the Court took the case after five
conference distributions and a called-for response, knowing the BIO's
harmless-error vehicle argument). No recusal is apparent, so an equally
divided Court is remote.
