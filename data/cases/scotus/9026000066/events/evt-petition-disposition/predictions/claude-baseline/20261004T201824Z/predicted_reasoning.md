# Forecast: what the Court will do with No. 26-66, *Smith v. Smith*

**Disposition.** The petition will be denied. The docket shows a single distribution (September 2, 2026, for the September 28, 2026 conference, the Term-opening long conference) with no response, no waiver, and no call for a response recorded. The ordinary path from that state is a one-line denial on the order list that follows the long conference, in the first days of October Term 2026.

**Relists.** Distributed once so far; I expect no further distribution. The petition is a pro se paid filing from an unpublished state intermediate-court memorandum decision in a marital-dissolution case, with no respondent engagement — the profile the Court clears at first consideration. The residual chance of another distribution comes from a reschedule or an unexpected call for a response rather than from a Justice holding the case.

**CVSG.** None. The dispute is between two private former spouses over Arizona family-court procedure; no federal interest is implicated and the Solicitor General has nothing to say about it.

**Which question, if any.** None of the three as written. Question 1 is framed as an adequate-and-independent-state-ground argument under *Lee v. Kemna*, but it attacks a footnote in an unpublished memorandum decision about a miscited subsection of Arizona's family-law rules; Questions 2 and 3 ask the Court to review the harmlessness and missing-transcript-presumption rulings of a state appellate court on a private family-law record. None presents a split or a question of general importance the Court would take up, and no reformulation rescues a vehicle this fact-bound.

**Summary disposition.** Not the likelier route in absolute terms, because no grant of any kind is likely. Conditional on the Court granting at all, a GVR or other summary route would be far likelier than plenary review — there is no intervening decision on point today, so even that is speculative, but plenary argument on these questions is close to inconceivable.

**Separate writings on denial.** None. No Justice will note a dissent from or statement respecting the denial; nothing in the petition engages a Justice's known interest in a recurring issue.

**Conditional merits.** If, against the forecast, the Court granted plenary review, the only live question would be Question 1's inadequacy argument: whether Arizona's treatment of a subsection miscitation as waiver is a state ground applied in an unexpectedly strict way under *Lee v. Kemna*. The Court would not reach the harmlessness or record-presumption questions as framed.
