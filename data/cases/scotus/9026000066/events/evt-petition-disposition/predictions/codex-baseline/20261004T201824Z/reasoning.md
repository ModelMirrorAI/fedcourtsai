These are serious allegations for the parties. They do not, in the supplied petition, establish a concrete disagreement between appellate courts on a common federal rule.

The main downward adjustment is the petition's dependence on a disputed procedural record. Its own account requires determining precisely what claim was preserved, whether the subsection miscitation actually controlled the result, which substitution order governed notice, what evidence was unavailable, and whether any defect mattered. The petition identifies the decision below as a memorandum decision. Its reasons for review principally ask the Court to correct applications of existing principles in this individual case rather than resolve a demonstrated competing nationwide rule. These features make this a substantially weaker candidate than the full baseline risk set, which includes private petitions that later develop stronger signals.
