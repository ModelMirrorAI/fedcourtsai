# Forecast of the Court's disposition

The petition will be distributed once for conference and then denied without a further relist. The Court will not call for the views of the Solicitor General: the dispute is between private parties, arises from a domestic-relations record, and implicates no evident federal program or institutional interest.

The petition presents no developed split and asks the Court to revisit an unpublished state memorandum through record-specific claims about waiver, notice, prejudice, and missing transcripts. The state procedural grounds and the need to reconstruct what occurred below will prevent the questions from serving as clean vehicles for national rules.

If review were granted, the Court would narrow and reformulate the first question to ask whether Arizona's application of its briefing rule was an inadequate state ground under the exceptional-circumstances doctrine. It would not take the second and third questions as written. Plenary review would be more likely than a cert-order disposition; there is no intervening precedent that naturally supports a GVR and no clear error suitable for summary reversal.

The denial will carry no noted dissent and no statement respecting denial.
