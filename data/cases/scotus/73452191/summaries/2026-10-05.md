## What happened

Walter Bernard sued Philip Ignelzi, a Pennsylvania state trial judge, under a federal civil rights law, claiming Ignelzi personally directed sheriff's deputies to arrest him without a warrant at his home during a landlord-tenant dispute, and later orchestrated a second arrest that led to nineteen days in jail. A federal district court dismissed the claims against Ignelzi, finding he was protected by absolute judicial immunity, a doctrine shielding judges from suits over their judicial acts. The Third Circuit Court of Appeals affirmed, and also held that Bernard's argument that Ignelzi acted as a law-enforcement officer rather than a judge had not been properly raised earlier. Bernard then asked the Supreme Court to take the case.

## What the Court is being asked

Bernard's petition asked the Court to decide whether judicial immunity protects a judge who personally directs and oversees police officers carrying out a warrantless arrest inside someone's home, rather than performing traditional judicial duties from the bench. He argued that other appeals courts have refused to extend immunity in similar circumstances, while the Third Circuit allowed it here.

## Where it stands
