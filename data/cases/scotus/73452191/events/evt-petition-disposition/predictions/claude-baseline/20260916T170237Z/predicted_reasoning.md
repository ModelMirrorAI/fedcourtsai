# Forecast: what the Court will do with No. 25-1356, Bernard v. Ignelzi

**Disposition.** The petition will be denied at or shortly after the September 28, 2026 conference (the "long conference"), in the order list of October 5, 2026 or the one following. P(any grant) = 0.025.

**Relists.** The docket shows exactly **one** distribution (entered July 15, 2026, for the September 28 conference); the petition has never been relisted. I forecast it is **not** distributed again: P(at least one further distribution) = 0.15. The most likely route to a second distribution is not a relist on the merits but a call for a response, because the respondent judge waived. A CFR followed by redistribution is the shape an interested Court would give this docket, and I put most of the 0.15 there rather than in a substantive relist. A reschedule out of the long conference is the residual.

**Response.** No response has been requested. I forecast the Court does **not** call for one (roughly 0.10 to 0.12, folded into the relist figure above), because the petition's own account of the decision below shows the argument it presses was held forfeited, and the Court rarely asks a waiving respondent to defend a forfeiture ruling on an unpublished opinion.

**CVSG.** None. No federal party, program, or statute-administration interest is implicated by a section 1983 immunity suit between a private litigant and a state trial judge. P(CVSG after prediction time) = 0.005.

**Which question, if taken.** If the Court took the case, it would take the question presented roughly as written (whether absolute judicial immunity covers a state judge who personally directs and supervises officers in a warrantless home arrest), but it would first have to get past the Third Circuit's forfeiture holding, so any grant would likely be framed to reach the alternative merits holding. A reformulation narrowing to "directing" versus "personally participating in" law-enforcement action is the likeliest edit, since that is where the petition's four cited circuits actually sit.

**Summary disposition versus plenary review.** Conditional on any grant, I put the cert-order route (a GVR or a per curiam summary reversal) at 0.35. The petition asks for summary reversal in the alternative and the posture is a Rule 12(b)(6) dismissal, which is the shape summary reversals take, but the Court's summary reversals in immunity cases run overwhelmingly in favor of the officer, not the plaintiff, and there is no intervening decision of this Court on judicial immunity to GVR against (Zorn v. Linton, which the petition cites, is described by the petition as a section 1983 immunity per curiam, not a judicial-immunity ruling). So a grant here, if it came, would more likely be plenary.

**Dissent from denial.** I expect none. P(some Justice notes a dissent or statement respecting denial | denial) = 0.03. The Justices who write on immunity doctrine at the cert stage (Thomas and Gorsuch on the doctrine's common-law footing, Sotomayor on section 1983 remedies) would face a forfeiture problem and a disgruntled-litigant fact pattern; a statement is conceivable but unlikely.

**Conditional merits.** If granted and reached, the likelier ground is that Mireles v. Waco controls: a judge's order that officers bring a party before the court in a pending matter is a judicial act, and a judge who directs rather than personally executes an arrest does not become "an adjunct law enforcement officer" under Lo-Ji Sales. That reading favors affirmance, which is a further reason the Court has little appetite to grant a plaintiff-side petition here.
