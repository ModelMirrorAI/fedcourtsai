# Forecast of the Court's action

The Court will **deny the petition**, most likely in an orders list following the scheduled September 28, 2026 conference, around late September or early October. That timing is a forecast, not a retrieved order date. The Court will not explain its denial, and denial will not establish approval of the lower court's immunity reasoning.

The record currently shows **one distribution and no subsequent relist**. I expect **zero further distributions**. The chance of at least one further distribution is 8%; if the case receives additional consideration, one extra distribution is more likely than a long series. I expect no call for the Solicitor General's views: the probability of a new CVSG is 0.3%. Neither a response request nor a CVSG is shown on the supplied record, and neither is part of my modal forecast.

The petition's preservation difficulty and the difference between ordering officers to act in connection with litigation and personally performing an executive function will keep this case from serving as the Court's chosen vehicle. The alleged home arrest supplies a serious constitutional setting but does not eliminate those obstacles. These are predicted reasons to decline review, not reasons I expect a denial order to announce.

**If review is granted**, the Court will focus on the petition's single question: whether absolute judicial immunity protects a state judge's alleged personal direction and supervision of a warrantless arrest inside a litigant's home. The likely framing will distinguish adjudicative enforcement orders from operational participation and confront preservation, rather than reconsider judicial immunity generally. No additional question is predicted.

Conditional on any grant, **plenary consideration is more likely than disposition in the cert order**: 70% versus 30%. Within the summary alternative, the petition's requested summary reversal is more plausible than a GVR tied to an identified intervening case. If the Court grants and reaches the merits, its analysis will turn on the functional boundary illustrated by *Mireles v. Waco*, 502 U.S. 9, 12–13 (1991), rather than treating an allegation of unlawful conduct alone as sufficient to defeat immunity.

Conditional on denial, I assign **3%** to any noted dissent or statement respecting denial and expect **no separate writing**. I do not forecast a particular Justice's vote or authorship.
