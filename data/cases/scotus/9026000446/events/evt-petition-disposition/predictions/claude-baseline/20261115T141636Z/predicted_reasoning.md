# Forecast: No. 26-446, Tarquinii v. Cao (Acting Secretary of the Navy)

The docket shows one distribution so far (Nov. 10, for the Dec. 4, 2026 conference), made on the Solicitor General's waiver of the right to respond.

- **Disposition.** The Court will deny certiorari, most likely on the order list following the Dec. 4 conference (Dec. 7), without a response ever being filed.
- **Further distributions.** The only realistic path to another distribution is a call for a response: a Justice asks the government to respond before the conference, the petition comes off the Dec. 4 list, and it is redistributed once the response is in (January–February 2027). I put that at about one in four. A true relist after a conference vote without a response is very unlikely.
- **CVSG.** None. The United States is the respondent; the Court asks for a response, not for the Solicitor General's views.
- **Question presented.** If the Court did grant, it would take the single QP as written (whether a Title VII plaintiff must expressly invoke the motivating-factor standard at summary judgment to preserve it). The government would likely argue in any response that the federal-sector provision (§ 2000e-16) and the pro se forfeiture below make this a poor vehicle for a private-sector § 2000e-2(m) split, and that the panel separately found no evidence of discrimination.
- **Summary disposition.** Unlikely. No intervening decision supports a GVR; the only summary route would be a government confession of error on the preservation rule, which I do not expect. A grant, if any, would be plenary.
- **Separate writing.** Expect none on a denial. If anyone wrote, it would most plausibly be Justice Sotomayor or Justice Jackson on forfeiture rules applied to pro se civil-rights plaintiffs — Justice Jackson's own district-court decision (Sledge) is cited by the panel for the rule, which cuts against her writing to criticize it.
