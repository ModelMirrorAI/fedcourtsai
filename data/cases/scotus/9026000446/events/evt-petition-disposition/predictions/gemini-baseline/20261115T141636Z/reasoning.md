## Case-Specific Adjustments

### Upward Factors (Why P(grant) Is Not Near Zero and `relist-increment` Is 0.18)

- **Supreme Court Specialist Counsel:** Although Petitioner litigated *pro se* at summary judgment in the district court and through Georgetown Law's Appellate Courts Immersion Clinic in the D.C. Circuit, her certiorari petition is signed by **Roman Martinez V** and a Latham & Watkins LLP team. Specialist Supreme Court counsel materially raises the probability that clerks scrutinize the petition and that at least one chambers calls for a response (`relist-increment = 0.18`).
- **Recent Doctrinal Hook (*Ames* and *Hittle*):** The petition skillfully frames the D.C. Circuit's forfeiture holding as symptomatic of lower courts' rigid over-reliance on *McDonnell Douglas Corp. v. Green*, 411 U.S. 792 (1973), invoking Justice Thomas's concurrence (joined by Justice Gorsuch) in *Ames v. Ohio Department of Youth Services*, 605 U.S. 303, 319–26 (2025), and dissent from denial of certiorari in *Hittle v. City of Stockton* (2025). It also pairs two 2024 published appellate decisions—*Bart v. Golub Corp.*, 96 F.4th 566 (2d Cir. 2024), and *McCreight v. AuburnBank*, 117 F.4th 1322 (11th Cir. 2024)—to allege an active 2–2 split.

### Decisive Downward Factors (Why Net Adjustment Brings P(grant) to 0.02)

Even accounting for Latham & Watkins authorship, four independent defects make certiorari overwhelmingly unlikely—especially once the Solicitor General files a Brief in Opposition if a response is requested:

1. **Unaddressed Federal-Sector Statutory Mismatch (42 U.S.C. § 2000e-16(a) vs. § 2000e-2(m)):**
   Petitioner was a federal civilian employee of the Department of the Navy. Her Title VII claim is governed by **42 U.S.C. § 2000e-16(a)** ("All personnel actions affecting employees ... in military departments ... shall be made free from any discrimination based on race, color, religion, sex, or national origin"), **not** private-sector 42 U.S.C. §§ 2000e-2(a)(1), 2000e-2(m), and 2000e-5(g)(2)(B) (indeed, 42 U.S.C. § 2000e(b) expressly excludes "the United States" from the definition of "employer" used in § 2000e-2). In *Babb v. Wilkie*, 589 U.S. 399 (2020), this Court construed the materially identical text of the federal-sector ADEA (29 U.S.C. § 633a(a)) to establish a distinct federal-sector causation and remedial regime, and multiple circuits have since held that *Babb* governs § 2000e-16(a) federal-sector Title VII claims rather than § 2000e-2(m) (*e.g.*, *Babb v. Secretary, Department of Veterans Affairs*, 992 F.3d 1193, 1198–1205 (11th Cir. 2021); *Huff v. Buttigieg*, 42 F.4th 638, 645–46 (7th Cir. 2022)). Neither the parties below nor the unpublished D.C. Circuit order addressed *Babb*'s effect on § 2000e-16(a), and the Petition's statutory appendix (Pet. App. 71a–91a) reproduces §§ 2000e-2 and 2000e-5 while omitting § 2000e-16 entirely. The Court will not grant certiorari to interpret private-sector § 2000e-2(m) preservation in a federal-sector case where § 2000e-2(m) does not textually govern.
2. **Extreme, Fact-Bound Vehicle Defects (Unpublished Rule 36 Order + Zero Legal Argument Below + Redressability Doubts):**
   - The decision below is a 3-page **unpublished, non-precedential Rule 36(d) judgment** that created no new law in the D.C. Circuit.
   - At summary judgment, Petitioner (proceeding *pro se* after discovery counsel withdrew) filed 212 paragraphs of factual assertions and exhibits with **no legal brief, no legal citations, and no articulated theory of causation** (Pet. App. 3a–4a, 21a). Thus, the case does not cleanly test what a counseled Title VII plaintiff must argue to preserve mixed-motive causation; it turns on whether a district court must *sua sponte* construct a motivating-factor claim from raw factual denials.
   - Judge Cobb's thorough 65-page opinion (Pet. App. 6a–70a) shows an exceptionally weak evidentiary record: multiple supervisors, colleagues, and contractors contemporaneously documented Petitioner's probationary performance and conduct problems, while her sole alleged race/sex remark came from the supervisor who had hired her months earlier. Moreover, because Petitioner was terminated over 11 years ago (March 2015), cannot obtain damages, backpay, or reinstatement under the § 2000e-5(g)(2)(B)(ii) framework she invokes, and litigated *pro se* in district court, a standalone mixed-motive remand faces serious Article III redressability questions under D.C. Circuit precedent (*Seed v. EPA*, 100 F.4th 257 (D.C. Cir. 2024)).
3. **The Alleged 2–2 Circuit Split Dissolves Under Direct Inspection:**
   Reading the opinions cited in the petition via CourtListener shows no clean conflict over whether a plaintiff who advances *zero* legal argument preserves a motivating-factor theory:
   - In *Bart v. Golub Corp.*, 96 F.4th 566 (2d Cir. 2024), preservation/forfeiture was **not** at issue: the plaintiff had direct gender-based remarks by her firing manager and actively argued mixed-motive causation under *McDonnell Douglas*; the Second Circuit resolved a substantive question about step-three pretext standards.
   - In *Stegall v. Citadel Broadcasting Co.*, 350 F.3d 1061, 1068, 1072 (9th Cir. 2003), and *Dominguez-Curry v. Nevada Transportation Department*, 424 F.3d 1027, 1040–42 & nn.6–7 (9th Cir. 2005), the plaintiffs' summary-judgment briefing and evidence actively supported mixed-motive theories (and in *Stegall*, survived under *both* pretext and mixed-motive standards).
   - Conversely, *McCreight v. AuburnBank*, 117 F.4th 1322, 1331–34 (11th Cir. 2024), and *Ponce v. Billington*, 679 F.3d 840, 845 (D.C. Cir. 2012), explicitly disclaim any "magic words" or heightened pleading trap, requiring only ordinary party presentation ("connecting the dots") at summary judgment.
4. **Poor Coalition Dynamics on the Bench:**
   Even in *Hittle v. City of Stockton* (2025)—a counseled case squarely asking the Court to reconsider *McDonnell Douglas*—only Justices Thomas and Gorsuch voted to grant certiorari, and only those two joined the *Ames* concurrence (605 U.S. at 319–26). Here, Petitioner does *not* ask to overrule *McDonnell Douglas*; she asks the Court to curtail party-presentation/forfeiture principles (*United States v. Sineneng-Smith*, 590 U.S. 371 (2020)) by requiring district judges to *sua sponte* evaluate unbriefed motivating-factor claims under Rule 56. Neither the Court's conservative wing (which strongly enforces party presentation and Rule 56(c) burdens) nor its liberal wing (which has little appetite to risk a restrictive Title VII or Rule 56 precedent on an 11-year-old *pro se* federal-sector record) has an incentive to supply four votes.
