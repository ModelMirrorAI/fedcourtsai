# Predicted Court Action: *Tarquinii v. Cao* (No. 26-446)

## Procedural Trajectory and Disposition

The docket currently records **one distribution** (distributed on November 10, 2026, for the Conference of December 4, 2026) following the Solicitor General's November 2, 2026 waiver of the right to respond on behalf of Respondent Acting Secretary of the Navy.

- **Ultimate Disposition (`denied`, P(any grant) = 0.02):** The Court will deny the petition for a writ of certiorari. Most likely (~82%), the petition will be denied outright on the Monday order list immediately following its first conference (December 7, 2026) without calling for a response or relisting.
- **Further Distributions / Call for a Response (`relist-increment` = 0.18):** Because the petition is authored by experienced Supreme Court counsel (Roman Martinez) and pitches a circuit split tied to Justice Thomas's recent concurrence in *Ames v. Ohio Department of Youth Services*, 605 U.S. 303 (2025), there is a modest (~18%) chance that a Justice requests a response from the Solicitor General (or triggers a brief relist), which would remove the case from the December 4 conference list and result in at least one additional distribution after the government's Brief in Opposition is filed in early 2027. Even if a response is called for, however, the Solicitor General's opposition will expose severe vehicle and statutory obstacles—most notably an unaddressed mismatch between federal-sector Title VII (42 U.S.C. § 2000e-16(a)) and private-sector motivating-factor liability (§ 2000e-2(m)), an unpublished Rule 36 order below, and a *pro se* summary-judgment opposition containing zero legal argument—leading to denial after one or two post-response distributions.
- **Call for the Views of the Solicitor General (`cvsg-increment` = 0.001):** No CVSG will issue. Respondent is the Acting Secretary of the Navy, already represented of record by the Solicitor General. If the Court wishes to hear from the United States after its waiver, it will enter a standard Call for a Response (CFR), not a CVSG.

## Conditional Scope of Review and Route if Granted

- **Question Presented Taken (Conditional on Grant):** If the Court were to grant plenary review, it would take the single Question Presented in the petition—whether, and under what circumstances, a Title VII plaintiff must expressly invoke a motivating-factor causation standard at summary judgment to preserve that theory—or reformulate it slightly to address federal-sector Title VII causation and Rule 56 party-presentation obligations.
- **Plenary Review vs. Summary Disposition (`summary-disposition-route` = 0.22 conditional on grant):** Conditional on the Court granting review at all, plenary briefing and oral argument would be the primary route (~78%), because resolving the alleged circuit tension over summary-judgment preservation of motivating-factor theories would require full adversarial treatment. A summary disposition in the cert order (~22% conditional on a grant) would occur only in the unlikely event that, following a Call for a Response, the Solicitor General suggested a narrow grant, vacatur, and remand (GVR)—for instance, on federal-sector causation under *Babb v. Wilkie*, 589 U.S. 399 (2020)—or the Court held the petition behind an intervening procedural or Title VII case. Summary merits reversal is practically ruled out given the unpublished, fact-intensive record.
- **Conditional Merits Ground (If Granted):** If granted and argued, the Court would likely either dismiss the writ as improvidently granted once the federal-sector § 2000e-16(a) mismatch and *pro se* record came into focus, or affirm on ordinary party-presentation principles (*United States v. Sineneng-Smith*, 590 U.S. 371 (2020)) and Federal Rule of Civil Procedure 56(c), holding that a district court is not required to construct an unargued motivating-factor theory where the nonmovant offers no legal argument and challenges the employer's explanation solely as pretextual.

## Separate Writings on Denial

- **Dissent or Statement Respecting Denial (`dissent-from-denial` = 0.025 conditional on denial):** Expect an unsigned denial order with no noted dissent or statement respecting the denial (~97.5%). In the small tail (~2.5%) where a separate writing accompanies denial—almost certainly only after a Call for a Response and relist—Justice Thomas (potentially joined by Justice Gorsuch) is the likeliest author, using a brief statement or dissent to reiterate skepticism of how lower courts apply *McDonnell Douglas* at summary judgment while noting vehicle complications in this case. All nine Justices are sitting with no apparent recusal conflict (the district court action, *Tarquinii v. Harker*, No. 1:21-cv-01567, was assigned to Judges Contreras and Cobb, and the D.C. Circuit panel consisted of Judges Pillard, Wilkins, and Katsas).
