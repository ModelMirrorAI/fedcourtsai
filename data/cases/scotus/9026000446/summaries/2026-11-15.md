## In brief

A former Navy employee asks the Court to revive her job discrimination lawsuit after lower courts ruled she had not properly preserved one of her legal theories.

## What happened

Jawan Tarquinii, who worked as a human resources official at a Marine Corps base in Japan, sued the Navy under Title VII, the federal law barring workplace discrimination, alleging her supervisors harassed and fired her based on her race and sex. Representing herself, she opposed the Navy's summary judgment motion with detailed factual arguments but did not specifically label her claim as resting on a "motivating factor" theory, one of two ways Title VII lets plaintiffs prove discrimination. The district court granted summary judgment to the Navy, and the D.C. Circuit affirmed, holding Tarquinii had forfeited the motivating-factor theory by not expressly invoking it.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked to decide whether, and under what circumstances, a worker suing over job discrimination must specifically name a particular legal theory when opposing a summary judgment motion in order to have a court consider whether her evidence supports that theory, rather than having the court simply evaluate whatever evidence she submitted.

## Where it stands

The Navy waived its right to respond to the petition. The case was distributed for the Court's December 4, 2026 conference, where the justices will decide whether to take it up.

## What each outcome would mean

Tarquinii, who lost in the lower courts, is asking the Court to hear her case. If the Court grants review, it will decide the question in full after briefing and argument; granting review itself would not decide who is right. If the Court denies review, the D.C. Circuit's decision affirming summary judgment for the Navy stands, leaving Tarquinii's discrimination claim rejected without further proceedings in her case.
