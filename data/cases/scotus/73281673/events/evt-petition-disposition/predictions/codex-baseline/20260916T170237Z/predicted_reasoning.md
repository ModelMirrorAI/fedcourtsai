# Forecast

The Court will deny the petition, most likely in the orders following the September 28, 2026 conference, in late September or early October. The likely order will be unexplained; denial will not establish approval of Wisconsin's Fourth Amendment analysis.

The snapshot records **two distributions**: May 5 for the May 21 conference, and June 17 for the September 28 conference. A May 8 request for a response intervened. From that two-distribution state, I expect **zero additional distributions**, with an 18% probability of at least one more. If the petition is redistributed again, one additional conference is more likely than a prolonged series. The response cycle and summer calendar, rather than repeated completed consideration of a fully briefed petition, explain much of the elapsed time.

No CVSG is recorded. I expect none before disposition, assigning a 2% probability to a new invitation. Federal interests in CyberTip reporting make consultation conceivable, but this petition's threshold difficulties make that an unlikely next step.

If the Court grants review, it will most likely take a narrowed version of **Question 2**: whether law enforcement's first human viewing of a file identified through a private provider's automated hash matching exceeds the scope of the antecedent private search. I do not expect a grant of all three questions. The lower court left the reasonable-expectation-of-privacy and good-faith issues unresolved, and the broader question about all private cloud data extends beyond the particular reported video.

Conditional on any grant, I assign 12% to disposition in the cert order itself and 88% to plenary review. The competing interpretations of the private-search doctrine would more naturally receive briefing and argument than summary reversal. No intervening controlling decision supporting a GVR is identified in the materials consulted.

Conditional on denial, I assign 8% to a noted dissent or statement respecting denial. The modal forecast is no separate writing. If a writing occurs, I expect it to emphasize the unresolved digital-search conflict rather than endorse this petition as an uncomplicated vehicle. I make no individual-Justice vote prediction.
