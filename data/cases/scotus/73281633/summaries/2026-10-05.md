## In brief

The Court declined to hear a trade group's challenge to a New York law restricting sales of weight-loss and muscle-building supplements to minors.

## What happened

New York passed a law in 2023 barring sellers from offering weight-loss or muscle-building supplements to anyone under eighteen, identifying covered products by their ingredients, labeling, marketing, or how retailers display them. The Council for Responsible Nutrition, a trade association of supplement makers, sued New York's Attorney General, arguing the law unconstitutionally restricts commercial speech. A federal district court denied the Council's request to block the law while the case proceeded, and the Second Circuit Court of Appeals affirmed that denial, finding the law likely survives the constitutional test that applies to commercial-speech regulations.

## What the Court is being asked

The Council asked the Court to decide whether the appeals court applied the right standard for judging restrictions on commercial speech. Specifically, it asked whether the government must provide stronger evidence that a speech-based rule will actually reduce the harm it targets, and whether courts should defer to a legislature's chosen approach or instead require proof that no less-restrictive option would work as well.

## Where it stands

The petition was distributed for the justices' September 28, 2026 conference, and the Court denied it on October 5, 2026.

## What each outcome would mean

The Council for Responsible Nutrition asked the Court to take up its case and overturn the Second Circuit's ruling, which had gone against it. Because the Court denied the petition, the Second Circuit's decision stands: the law remains in effect, and the Council's bid for an injunction blocking it while the underlying lawsuit continues has failed. The denial does not mean the Court agreed with the lower court's reasoning, only that it will not review the case, and the underlying lawsuit may still continue in the lower courts.
