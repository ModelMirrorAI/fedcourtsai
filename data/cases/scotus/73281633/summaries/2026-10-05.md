## In brief

A supplements trade group's bid to challenge New York's ban on selling weight-loss and muscle-building supplements to minors was denied by the Court.

## What happened

New York passed a law barring the sale of diet pills and supplements marketed for weight loss or muscle building to anyone under eighteen. The Council for Responsible Nutrition, a trade group of supplement makers, sued New York's Attorney General, arguing the law violates the First Amendment by restricting commercial speech without adequate justification. A federal district court denied the Council's request to block the law while the case proceeds, and the Second Circuit Court of Appeals affirmed, finding the law likely satisfies the constitutional test for commercial speech regulation. The Council then asked the Supreme Court to review that ruling.

## What the Court is being asked

The case asks about the standard courts use when the government restricts commercial advertising or sales based on how a product is marketed. Specifically, it asks whether the government must produce specific evidence linking its restriction to the harm it targets, rather than relying on general reasoning or common sense, and whether courts owe deference to a legislature's choice of regulatory approach over less speech-restrictive alternatives. The Council says appeals courts disagree on these questions; New York disputes that any such disagreement exists.

## Where it stands

## What each outcome would mean

The Council for Responsible Nutrition asked the Supreme Court to take up its case and overturn the Second Circuit's ruling. By denying the petition, the Court leaves the Second Circuit's decision in place, meaning the lower courts' refusal to block New York's law stands, and the Council's lawsuit against the law continues in the lower courts without Supreme Court review of these particular questions.
