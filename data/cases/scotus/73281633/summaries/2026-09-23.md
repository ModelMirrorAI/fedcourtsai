## What happened

New York passed a law banning the sale of diet pills and weight-loss or muscle-building supplements to anyone under 18. The law identifies which products are covered partly by looking at their ingredients and partly by how they are labeled or marketed. The Council for Responsible Nutrition, a trade group for supplement makers, sued New York's attorney general, arguing the law violates the First Amendment by restricting truthful commercial speech. A federal district court denied the Council's request to block the law while the case proceeds, and the Second Circuit Court of Appeals agreed, finding the law likely satisfies the constitutional test that applies to restrictions on commercial speech. The Council then asked the Supreme Court to review that ruling.

## What the Court is being asked

The case turns on the legal test courts use to decide whether a government restriction on commercial speech is constitutional. That test asks, among other things, whether the government proved its rule will actually reduce a real harm, and whether the rule avoids restricting more speech than necessary when other options exist. The Council argues the appeals court applied this test too loosely, accepting "common sense" and general studies rather than requiring solid evidence tying the marketing-based restriction to actual harm, and deferring too readily to the legislature's choice not to regulate based on ingredients alone. New York responds that the appeals court applied the standard test correctly, that no real disagreement exists among appeals courts on this point, and that the case is a poor candidate for review because it involves only a preliminary, not final, ruling.

## Where it stands

The petition has been fully briefed, including multiple friend-of-the-court briefs, New York's opposition, and the Council's reply. The case was distributed for the Court's conference on September 28, 2026, where the Justices will consider whether to grant review.
