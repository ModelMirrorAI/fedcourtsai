## In brief

A supplements trade group asks the Court to review a ruling that upheld, at the preliminary-injunction stage, New York's ban on selling weight-loss and muscle-building supplements to minors.

## What happened

New York passed a law banning sales to minors of dietary supplements marketed for weight loss or muscle building, identifying covered products by their ingredients, labeling, and marketing. The Council for Responsible Nutrition, a trade association of supplement makers, sued New York's Attorney General, arguing the law unconstitutionally restricts commercial speech. A district court denied the Council's request to block the law while the case proceeds, and the Second Circuit affirmed, finding the law likely satisfies the Supreme Court's existing test for commercial-speech restrictions. The Council then asked the full Second Circuit to rehear the case; that request was denied.

## What the Court is being asked

The Council asks the Court to decide whether the appeals court applied the right level of scrutiny to New York's law. Specifically, it argues the lower court should have required New York to produce stronger evidence that restricting sales based on marketing would actually reduce harm to minors, and should have more closely questioned whether less speech-restrictive alternatives, like regulating by ingredient list, would work as well. New York responds that the appeals court applied the correct standard and that no conflict exists among lower courts on these questions.

## Where it stands

The petition, opposition brief, and reply have all been filed. The Court considered the case at its conference on September 28, 2026, and the docket does not yet show any decision on whether to hear it.

## What each outcome would mean

The Council for Responsible Nutrition is asking the Court to take up the case. If the Court grants review, it would hear full arguments and decide how strictly courts must scrutinize this kind of commercial-speech restriction; a grant would not itself decide who wins. If the Court denies review, the Second Circuit's decision upholding the law at this preliminary stage stands, and the case would continue in the lower courts on its ordinary track, without a ruling from the Supreme Court on the underlying constitutional question.
