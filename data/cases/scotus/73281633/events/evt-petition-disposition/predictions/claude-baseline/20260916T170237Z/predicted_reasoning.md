# Forecast: what the Court will do with No. 25-1145

**Council for Responsible Nutrition v. James** (CA2, No. 24-1343). Paid petition
from a national trade association challenging N.Y. Gen. Bus. Law § 391-oo, which
bars selling dietary supplements "labeled, marketed, or otherwise represented"
for weight loss or muscle building to minors. The Second Circuit affirmed the
denial of a preliminary injunction under *Central Hudson*. Two questions
presented: (1) whether the third prong can be satisfied without an evidentiary
showing that the specific restriction materially ameliorates harm, and
(2) whether the fourth prong permits deference to the legislature's choice of
means without meaningful consideration of less-intrusive alternatives.

## Disposition

**The petition will be denied**, most likely on the order list following the
September 28, 2026 Long Conference or, if relisted, in October. P(any grant)
= 0.11.

The Court will treat this as an interlocutory challenge to a preliminary
injunction ruling with two independent alternative grounds below (delay
eroding irreparable harm; the public interest), where the questions presented
are not outcome-determinative and the "split" is a disagreement about how much
record evidence is enough rather than about a stated rule. The Court has not
taken a *Central Hudson* evidentiary-burden case since *Greater New Orleans*
reserved the question, and *Free Speech Coalition v. Paxton* (2025) shows the
current Court comfortable upholding age-gating regimes aimed at minors under
intermediate scrutiny. A grant, if it comes, would be driven by the four
cert-stage amici and the recurring interest of Justice Thomas and others in
tightening *Central Hudson*; that is the minority branch.

## Relists

The docket shows **one distribution** (July 1, 2026, for the September 28
conference) and no relist. This is the ordinary single-distribution petition.
I expect **no further distribution** as the modal outcome, with a substantial
minority chance (about one in three) of exactly one relist: the Long Conference
routinely spills petitions with amicus support to the following conference for
a closer look at vehicle problems, and any dissent or statement respecting
denial would itself require a relist. Two or more relists is unlikely.

## Call for the views of the Solicitor General

**No CVSG.** The challenged law is a state statute, no federal party is
involved, the petition raises no preemption question, and the federal
government's regulatory interest in dietary supplements (FDA under DSHEA) is
tangential to the *Central Hudson* questions. Roughly 3%.

## Which question the Court would take, if it grants

If granted, the Court would take **QP 1 as reformulated**: what quality and
specificity of evidence *Central Hudson*'s third prong demands of a government
restricting truthful commercial speech, the question *Greater New Orleans*
left open and the *Borgner* dissent flagged. QP 2 (deference under the fourth
prong) would ride along as part of the same "how rigorous is intermediate
scrutiny" inquiry but would not be the driver; the Court would not reach the
heightened-scrutiny theme that petitioner and amici gesture at, which
respondent says was not pressed below.

## Summary disposition

**Unlikely.** No intervening decision of this Court bears on the *Central
Hudson* evidentiary or tailoring inquiry (*Chiles v. Salazar*, decided March
31, 2026, concerns professional speech and strict scrutiny and predates the
petition, so it is not a GVR hook). Conditional on a grant, the route is
plenary review, not a GVR or per curiam; P(cert-order disposition | grant)
= 0.08.

## Dissent from or statement respecting denial

Conditional on denial, I expect **no separate writing** as the modal outcome.
There is a real minority chance (about 8%) of a statement or dissent, and if
one appears it will come from Justice Thomas, who has repeatedly written that
*Central Hudson* under-protects truthful commercial speech and who dissented
from denial in *Borgner*; Justice Gorsuch is the likeliest joiner. The
interlocutory posture and the alternative grounds below make even a
sympathetic Justice more likely to wait for a final-judgment vehicle.
