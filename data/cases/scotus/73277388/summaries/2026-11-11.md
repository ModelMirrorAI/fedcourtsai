## In brief

Genesis asks the Court to decide whether federal arbitration law blocks Maryland's rule that arbitration clauses need their own separate consideration.

## What happened

Steve Ford sued Genesis Financial Solutions and Spring Oaks Capital, which serviced and later collected his credit card debt, claiming violations of Maryland lending laws. Genesis and Spring Oaks sought to force the dispute into arbitration under a clause in Ford's card agreement. A federal district court refused, ruling that a separate clause letting Genesis change account terms at any time made its promise to arbitrate illusory, leaving the arbitration clause without the independent consideration Maryland law requires for such clauses. The Fourth Circuit affirmed, following its own recent precedent, and denied rehearing as untimely.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked whether federal arbitration law allows a state to require arbitration clauses, specifically, to be backed by their own separate exchange of promises beyond what supports the rest of the contract, or whether treating arbitration clauses this way improperly singles them out compared to other contract terms.

## Where it stands

The petition was distributed for the Court's November 14, 2025 conference; the docket shows no action recorded from that conference. The Court then asked for a response, and the response deadline has since been extended several times, most recently to November 27, 2026.

## What each outcome would mean

Genesis, supported by Spring Oaks, asks the Court to take up the case; both companies lost in the lower courts. If the Court grants review, it would hear full arguments on whether Maryland's rule is valid and decide the question itself, without yet determining who wins. If it denies review, the Fourth Circuit's ruling stands, leaving Ford's lawsuit able to proceed in court rather than arbitration.
