## What happened

Steve Ford got a credit card serviced by Genesis Financial Solutions. The cardholder agreement included a clause requiring disputes to be arbitrated. After Ford failed to repay charges, his debt was transferred to Spring Oaks Capital, which pursued collection. Ford then sued Genesis and Spring Oaks in a proposed class action, arguing Maryland law required arbitration clauses to have their own separate contractual backing beyond what supports the rest of the agreement, and that this was missing here. A federal district court agreed and refused to send the case to arbitration. The Fourth Circuit Court of Appeals affirmed, following its own earlier rulings applying Maryland's rule. Genesis asked for rehearing by the full appeals court, but that request was rejected as filed too late.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked whether a federal law favoring arbitration allows a state to impose a special extra requirement that applies only to arbitration clauses within contracts, or whether that federal law rules out such extra requirements because they treat arbitration provisions less favorably than other contract terms. Genesis argues several other appeals courts have rejected this kind of arbitration-specific rule, while the Fourth Circuit alone allows it.

## Where it stands

The petition for cert, the Court's permission to hear the case, was filed in September 2025, and the Court has since asked the responding parties for their views, with the response deadline extended multiple times, most recently to October 30, 2026. No conference decision on whether to grant the petition has yet been recorded.
