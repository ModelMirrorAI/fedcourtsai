## In brief

Genesis Financial Solutions asks the Court to decide whether federal arbitration law blocks Maryland's rule requiring extra proof that arbitration clauses are backed by their own distinct consideration.

## What happened

Steve Ford sued Genesis Financial Solutions and Spring Oaks Capital, which later acquired his debt, claiming they operated as unlicensed lenders under Maryland law on a credit card loan. Genesis and Spring Oaks asked the court to dismiss the case and compel arbitration under the card agreement's arbitration clause. The district court refused, finding that a clause letting the company change contract terms at any time made its promise to arbitrate "illusory" and unsupported by its own consideration, as Maryland law requires for arbitration clauses specifically. The Fourth Circuit affirmed, relying on its own precedent, and denied rehearing as untimely.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked whether federal arbitration law allows a state to require arbitration clauses, alone among contract terms, to be backed by their own separate exchange of promises, or whether this special requirement improperly singles out arbitration agreements for treatment federal law does not allow.

## Where it stands

The petition was distributed for the Court's November 14, 2025 conference; the docket shows no action from that conference. The Court instead asked for a response, and the response deadline has since been extended several times, most recently to October 30, 2026.

## What each outcome would mean

Genesis asked the Court to take the case, and Spring Oaks has told the Court it supports that request; both seek to overturn the Fourth Circuit's ruling for Ford. If the Court grants the petition, it would hear full briefing and decide the arbitration question itself, deciding nothing yet about who wins. If it denies the petition, the Fourth Circuit's decision stands, leaving Ford's case in court rather than arbitration and leaving Maryland's consideration rule for arbitration clauses in place within that circuit.
