## In brief

Genesis Financial Solutions asks the Court to decide whether federal arbitration law bars Maryland's rule requiring arbitration clauses to have their own separate consideration.

## What happened

Steve Ford sued Genesis Financial Solutions and Spring Oaks Capital, claiming they marketed and collected on credit card loans without required Maryland licenses. Genesis and Spring Oaks asked the court to dismiss the case and send it to arbitration under a clause in Ford's credit agreement. The district court refused, finding that a clause letting Genesis change contract terms at any time made its promise to arbitrate too one-sided to count as valid consideration under Maryland law. The U.S. Court of Appeals for the Fourth Circuit affirmed, relying on its own recent precedent. Genesis then asked the Supreme Court to review that ruling.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked whether a federal law favoring arbitration agreements stops states from applying a special rule that only arbitration clauses must be backed by their own separate exchange of promises, even when the rest of the contract is valid, or whether states remain free to apply that kind of extra requirement specifically to arbitration clauses.

## Where it stands

The petition was distributed for the Court's November 14, 2025 conference, and the docket shows no grant or denial from that conference. Instead, the Court asked for a response, and the deadline for filing it has been extended several times, most recently to October 30, 2026.

## What each outcome would mean

Genesis, joined in support by Spring Oaks, asked the Court to take the case. If the Court grants review, it would hear full arguments and decide whether Maryland's rule survives federal arbitration law, without yet deciding who wins. If it denies review, the Fourth Circuit's decision favoring Ford stands, and the case returns to the lower courts without arbitration. The current posture, with a response still pending, means no action has yet been taken on the petition itself.
