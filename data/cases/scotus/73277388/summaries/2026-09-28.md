## In brief

Genesis Financial Solutions asks the Court to decide whether federal arbitration law bars Maryland's rule requiring arbitration clauses, alone among contract terms, to carry their own separate consideration.

## What happened

Steve Ford used a credit card serviced by Genesis Financial Solutions, then failed to repay the charges; his debt was later transferred to Spring Oaks Capital SPV, LLC, which sued to collect. Ford brought a separate class action against Genesis and Spring Oaks over the account terms, and they moved to compel arbitration under the card agreement's arbitration clause. Applying Maryland law, the district court held that clause unenforceable because it lacked its own independent consideration, given a provision letting the issuer change contract terms. The Fourth Circuit affirmed, following its own prior rulings applying the same Maryland rule. Genesis says every other circuit to consider similar rules has reached the opposite conclusion.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked whether federal law allows a state to require arbitration clauses specifically to be backed by their own separate exchange of promises, beyond the consideration that supports the rest of the contract, or whether that kind of arbitration-specific requirement improperly disfavors arbitration and is not allowed.

## Where it stands

The petition was distributed for the Court's November 14, 2025 conference. Shortly after, the Court asked the respondents to respond, and it has since granted several extensions of the response deadline, most recently to October 30, 2026. The petition has not yet been granted or denied.

## What each outcome would mean

Genesis, the petitioner, asks the Court to take up the case. If the Court grants review, it would hear full arguments and decide whether Maryland's rule survives federal arbitration law, without yet deciding who wins. If it denies review, the Fourth Circuit's ruling stands, leaving the arbitration clause unenforceable and Ford's suit against Genesis and Spring Oaks able to proceed in court rather than arbitration.
