# Forecast — Genesis Financial Solutions, Inc. v. Ford, No. 25-311

**Disposition: no grant.** The petition is most likely to end in either a plain
denial or a Rule 46 dismissal after the parties settle. Of the two, denial is
the more likely, but a dismissal is a real possibility (roughly 30%). P(grant) 0.07.

**Distributions so far: one.** The petition went to the 11/14/2025 conference
and then the Court requested a response. Respondents' brief in opposition is
now due October 30, 2026, after four extensions; the last one ran almost six
months and was granted with petitioner's consent. If a BIO is filed, the case
will be distributed again in November 2026 for a conference in late November
or December. I expect it to be decided at that first post-BIO conference with
no further relist. The most likely way the petition is *not* distributed again
is a stipulated dismissal before or around the BIO deadline.

**CVSG: not expected.** Both sides are private parties and the question is a
plain Kindred/Concepcion equal-treatment question. The Court does not need the
Solicitor General's view to see whether there is a split.

**If granted:** the Court would take the single QP as written: whether FAA § 2
preempts Maryland's *Cheek* rule, which requires independent consideration (mutual
promises to arbitrate) for an arbitration clause but not for other terms. A
plenary grant is the expected route, not a summary reversal. The lower-court
opinion is a short unpublished per curiam bound by *Johnson* and *Noohi*, and a
summary reversal of a circuit-wide rule is unusual. There is also no
intervening decision that would support a GVR.

**Should it deny:** I expect no separate writing. The likeliest exception would
be a short statement from a Justice who has written on arbitration hostility
(Justice Kavanaugh or Justice Gorsuch), but I do not commit to one.
