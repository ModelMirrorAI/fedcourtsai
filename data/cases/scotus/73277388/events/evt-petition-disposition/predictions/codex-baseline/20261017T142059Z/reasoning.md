## Baseline and adjustments

The frozen context supplies `baseline`, `sal-v4`, Term 2025, one distribution, and no CVSG. I retain that band even though the record separately contains a call for a response. The matching committed statpack publishes a sal-v4 reached-rate table. Pooling every displayed Term strictly before 2025 gives Terms 2017-2024. From the corresponding exact JSON values, the sum of `prefix_est_grant_rate * prefix_weighted_resolved` is 593 and the sum of `prefix_weighted_resolved` is 11,580: a 5.1209% prior-Term grant-family anchor. These are denial-reweighted estimates for private petitions reaching baseline, not raw counts or a terminal-baseline rate. I excluded Terms 2025 and 2026. The statpack's latest file commit is October 13, 2026; this is the checked-out historical reference pack, not a freshly queried corpus or a claim about current corpus-wide freshness.

I raise the forecast to 22% for three concrete reasons. First, the petition identifies a specific legal conflict, rather than merely alleging a bad application of settled law: independent consideration for arbitration clauses versus consideration supporting the contract as a whole. Its asserted First/Second/Third/Sixth/Eighth versus Fourth Circuit conflict is advocacy, not an independently verified five-to-one census. Second, the snapshot records the Court's November 6, 2025 request for a response after attempted waivers and an initial distribution. That is attention beyond the baseline state, although it is not a commitment to grant. Third, the American Bankers Association amicus entry and the credit-card contract setting support broader commercial significance. I have not read the amicus brief and infer no specific argument from its filing.

General precedent makes the federal issue credible: Kindred Nursing Centers, L.P. v. Clark, 581 U.S. 246 (2017), especially the opinion's contract-formation discussion at slip pages 7-9, rejects categorical exemption of formation rules from the FAA's antidiscrimination principle. I verified that discussion through CourtListener opinion 4168363. It supports the legal plausibility of petitioner's theory, not a conclusion that the Maryland rule is necessarily preempted or that this petition will be granted.

Several considerations keep the estimate well below 50%. The decision here is unpublished and follows Johnson rather than supplying a fully developed new circuit opinion. The petition itself identifies Johnson's separate cert petition, making this case a possible follower rather than the preferred lead vehicle. The underlying agreement contains a unilateral change-of-terms provision, and the record leaves room for a genuine illusory-promise or formation objection rather than pure hostility to arbitration. Differences among state-law rules and contractual language may narrow the claimed conflict. The absence of an opposition prevents testing the asserted vehicle cleanliness, preservation, and outcome-determinative effect. The petition also reports that rehearing was denied as untimely; I do not assume that fact defeats Supreme Court jurisdiction, given the separately stated filing extension. Finally, the lengthy response extensions are not repeated Court consideration and do not justify treating this as a multiply relisted petition. I do not infer their reason.

The movement from 5.12% to 22% is a judgmental adjustment for these case-specific signals, not a fitted likelihood ratio or a claim of measured forecasting skill. A denial remains the modal disposition; some non-grant probability also belongs to dismissal or withdrawal rather than denial alone.

## Other declared probabilities

The paid-segment relist cut has terminal grant-family shares of about 1.7% at zero relists, 13.3% at one, 40.6% at two, and 36.8% at three or more. The CVSG cut has about 34.8% for a CVSG versus 6.2% without. These pooled, denial-reweighted cuts describe the pack's terminal populations, including Terms not eligible for my numerical anchor. I use their shape only, not as time-conditional hazards or as a replacement for the strictly prior-Term reached rate.

The 88% distribution-increment forecast is driven primarily by the pending requested response: after it arrives, another distribution is a natural next procedural step. It does not mean an 88% chance of an unusually favorable substantive relist. Dismissal, withdrawal, or another procedural exit accounts for much of the complement. The 2.5% CVSG forecast reflects the private FAA issue and absence of a specific executive interest in the provisioned material; it is not inferred from a terminal CVSG grant rate.

The 60% summary-route probability is conditional on a grant. The unpublished follower posture and the separately docketed Johnson petition identified in the opening filing make a hold/GVR route plausible. I have not checked that petition's later trajectory and do not presume an intervening decision exists. This route estimate is especially uncertain. The 5% separate-writing estimate is conditional on denial; it reflects the possibility of concern over arbitration discrimination, while keeping a routine unexplained denial much more likely. Neither estimate is an unconditional probability.

The 0.48 significance score reflects potentially widespread consequences for consumer-credit arbitration and standard contracts, moderated by a technical statutory issue and a limited state-law setting. It is independent of the 22% grant estimate.
