# Forecast: Genesis Financial Solutions, Inc. v. Ford, No. 25-311

**Where the docket stands.** The petition was distributed once, for the
14 November 2025 conference. On 6 November 2025 the Court requested a response,
due 8 December 2025. No CVSG appears and no relist has happened. One amicus brief
is on file: the American Bankers Association's, supporting the petitioner. The
co-defendant below, Spring Oaks, has filed a Rule 12.6 letter supporting the
petition.

**Disposition.** I expect cert to be **denied**, at about 20% for any grant. The
call for a response shows that at least one chambers read the petition as more
than routine. Most petitions that draw such a call are still denied once the
brief in opposition arrives. I expect the brief in opposition to argue three
things: Maryland's *Cheek* rule follows from general severability doctrine
rather than from hostility to arbitration; the claimed split is older,
undeveloped dicta; and an unpublished one-paragraph per curiam is a weak
vehicle.

**Relists.** The petition will be distributed again once the response is in or
its time runs out, most likely for a conference in January or February 2026.
If it is headed for denial, I expect it to be denied at that first
post-response conference with no relist. A grant would most likely follow one
or two relists.

**CVSG.** Unlikely. No federal party is involved, and the question is
preemption of a state contract rule under the FAA, which the Court usually
decides without the Solicitor General's views.

**If granted.** The Court would take the question as written: whether
FAA § 2 preempts a state rule that demands separate consideration for an
arbitration clause alone. On the merits it would probably reverse under the
equal-treatment principle of *Kindred Nursing* and *Concepcion*. A summary
reversal is possible because the Court has resolved FAA equal-treatment
cases per curiam before (*Marmet*). Plenary review is still the likelier route,
because the case first has to settle what Maryland law actually requires. No
GVR is expected, because no intervening decision bears on the question.

**Separate writing on a denial.** Unlikely. If any Justice writes, it would be a
statement from a Justice favorable to enforcing arbitration, such as
Kavanaugh, Gorsuch or Alito, noting the issue for a better vehicle.
Thomas's long-held view that the FAA does not apply in state court does not
bear here, because this case arises in federal court.
