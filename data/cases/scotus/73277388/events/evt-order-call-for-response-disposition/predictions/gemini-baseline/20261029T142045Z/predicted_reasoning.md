Once redistributed after the brief in opposition arrives, the petition is unlikely to draw multiple conference relists before disposition.
- **Solicitor General Views (`cvsg-increment`: 0.01):** The Court will not call for the views of the Solicitor General. The dispute involves FAA Section 2 preemption of Maryland's common-law consideration rule (*Cheek v. United Healthcare of the Mid-Atlantic, Inc.*) in a private consumer credit-card contract and does not implicate federal regulatory administration or executive-branch enforcement interests.
- **Disposition (`disposition`: 0.08 — Denied):** After receiving Respondent Ford's brief in opposition, the Court will deny the petition for a writ of certiorari. Although the petition raises a recurring question regarding whether Maryland's independent-consideration requirement for arbitration clauses is preempted by the Federal Arbitration Act, the decision below is a two-paragraph unpublished Fourth Circuit per curiam affirmance entered without oral argument that relies on an earlier published decision (*Johnson v. Continental Finance Co.*, 131 F.4th 169 (4th Cir. 2025)) and a single footnote citation to *Noohi v. Toll Bros., Inc.*, 708 F.3d 599 (4th Cir. 2013). Moreover, the district court record turns heavily on contract-specific unilateral modification ("Change of Terms") language, choice-of-law disputes between Maryland and Utah law, and alternative statutory-fraud and third-party enforcement arguments.
- **Question Presented and Conditional Merits Ground:** If the Court were to grant certiorari, it would take the single question presented as written—whether FAA Section 2 preempts state contract-formation rules requiring separate, mutual consideration for an arbitration clause within an otherwise supported contract—and would likely reverse or vacate on the ground that applying a heightened mutuality requirement exclusively to arbitration provisions violates the FAA's equal-treatment principle under *Kindred Nursing Centers Ltd. v. Clark*, 581 U.S. 246 (2017), leaving contract-specific Utah choice-of-law issues for remand.
- **Summary Disposition Route (`summary-disposition-route`: 0.20 conditional on grant):** Conditional on the Court granting review, plenary briefing and oral argument would be the primary route (80%), though there is a 20% conditional probability of a summary reversal or GVR in light of *Kindred Nursing* and *AT&T Mobility LLC v. Concepcion*, 563 U.S. 333 (2011), given the Court's past willingness to summarily correct state-law rules that single out arbitration agreements.
- **Separate Writings on the Order (`dissent-from-denial`: 0.02 conditional on denial; `order-writers`):** Conditional on denial, the Court will almost certainly issue a standard, unsigned order with no noted dissent or statement respecting the denial. In the unlikely event a separate writing accompanies the denial (~2%), it would most plausibly come from Justice Thomas, Justice Gorsuch, or Justice Alito addressing FAA preemption or vehicle considerations.
