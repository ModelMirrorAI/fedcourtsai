## In brief

Tesla asks the Court to decide whether courts may review the Patent Office's refusal to reconsider patent challenges it denied based on a timing factor not found in the statute.

## What happened

Granite Vehicle Ventures sued Tesla for patent infringement. Tesla asked the Patent and Trademark Office to conduct inter partes review, a process for challenging issued patents, of the patents involved. The agency declined, citing the likely timing of the district court trial as a reason, among others. Tesla asked the Federal Circuit for a writ of mandamus, a court order compelling an official to act, arguing the agency had exceeded its statutory authority. The Federal Circuit denied the request, holding that such non-institution decisions are generally not reviewable absent a constitutional claim.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked whether a law making the Patent Office's decision not to review a patent "final and nonappealable" also blocks courts from considering a claim that the agency relied on a factor the patent review statute does not authorize, or whether courts may still check that the agency stayed within the limits Congress set.

## Where it stands

The petition has been distributed for conference twice, most recently for December 4, 2026, after being rescheduled from an earlier conference; the docket does not show that the Court has yet acted on it.

## What each outcome would mean

Tesla, which lost in the Federal Circuit, is asking the Court to take up its case. If the Court grants review, it would hear the case in full and decide whether courts can review this kind of challenge to the Patent Office's reasoning; a grant would not itself decide who wins. If the Court denies review, the Federal Circuit's decision stands, leaving in place the rule that such challenges are generally not reviewable, and Granite Vehicle Ventures' patents remain free from inter partes review on this record. The federal government, representing the Patent Office, opposes Tesla's petition.
