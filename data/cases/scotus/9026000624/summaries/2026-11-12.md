## In brief

Families of three women killed by a sheriff's deputy's car ask the Court to revisit whether the Charleston County Sheriff's Office can claim state immunity from their lawsuit.

## What happened

On Mother's Day 2022, a Charleston County sheriff's deputy, responding to a non-emergency call without lights or sirens, struck a car carrying Stephanie Dantzler and her adult daughters Shanice and Miranda Dantzler-Williams, killing all three. Their estates sued the Charleston County Sheriff's Office and others. The district court dismissed the claims against the Sheriff's Office, ruling it was protected by the Eleventh Amendment, a constitutional doctrine shielding states from certain lawsuits, because it counted as an arm of the state rather than a local agency. The Fourth Circuit affirmed. The Supreme Court earlier vacated that ruling and sent the case back for reconsideration in light of a new decision, Galette v. New Jersey Transit Corp. On remand, the Fourth Circuit again ruled the Sheriff's Office was immune, reasoning Galette did not apply.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked whether its recent ruling about when a government-created entity counts as part of the state for immunity purposes applies only to entities formally organized as corporations, or more broadly. It is also asked whether the Charleston County Sheriff's Office specifically should be treated as part of South Carolina's state government, shielding it from this lawsuit, or as a separate local entity that can be sued.

## Where it stands

The petition for certiorari was filed on November 9, 2026, with the Sheriff's Office's response due December 14, 2026. The Court has not yet acted on it.

## What each outcome would mean

The families, as petitioners, are asking the Court to reverse the Fourth Circuit's ruling that shielded the Sheriff's Office from suit. If the Court grants the petition, it would hear the case and decide whether the Sheriff's Office qualifies for state immunity; a grant alone would not resolve who wins. If the Court denies the petition, the Fourth Circuit's ruling stands, leaving the Sheriff's Office immune from these claims and the families unable to pursue this part of their lawsuit against it.
