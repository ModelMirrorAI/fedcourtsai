## In brief

Families of three traffic-accident victims ask the Court to decide whether a South Carolina sheriff's office can claim state immunity from their lawsuit.

## What happened

Sheriff's deputies responding to a non-emergency call struck and killed Stephanie Dantzler and her daughters Shanice and Miranda Dantzler-Williams. Their estates sued the Charleston County Sheriff's Office and others. The district court dismissed the claims against the sheriff's office, ruling it shares the state's immunity from suit as an "arm of the state," and the Fourth Circuit affirmed. The Supreme Court earlier granted review, set aside that ruling, and sent the case back for the Fourth Circuit to reconsider in light of a new Supreme Court decision, Galette, about when government entities count as arms of a state. On reconsideration, the Fourth Circuit again held the sheriff's office immune, reasoning in one paragraph that Galette applies only to entities organized as corporations.

## What the Court is being asked

The families ask the Court to decide two things: whether its recent ruling on state-entity immunity applies only to corporations, as the Fourth Circuit held, or more broadly; and whether the Charleston County Sheriff's Office is actually an extension of the state of South Carolina, shielded from suit, or instead more like a county agency that can be sued.

## Where it stands

The petition for a writ of certiorari was filed on November 9, 2026, with the respondent's response due December 14, 2026. The Court has not yet acted on it.

## What each outcome would mean

The families, as petitioners, are asking the Court to take up the case again and reverse the Fourth Circuit's ruling that favored the sheriff's office. If the Court grants the petition, it would hear full arguments and decide whether the sheriff's office is immune from the lawsuit. If it denies the petition, the Fourth Circuit's decision stands, leaving the sheriff's office immune from these claims and the families unable to pursue that part of their case against it.
