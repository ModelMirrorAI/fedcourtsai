## In brief

Relatives of a family killed by a speeding sheriff's deputy ask the Court to rule that the Charleston County Sheriff's Office cannot claim South Carolina's immunity from their lawsuit.

## What happened

A Charleston County sheriff's deputy, responding to a non-emergency call without lights or sirens, ran a stop sign and killed Stephanie Dantzler and her two daughters in 2022. Their representatives sued the Charleston County Sheriff's Office, among others, but the office argued it shares South Carolina's constitutional immunity from being sued in federal court as an "arm of the state." The district court and the Fourth Circuit agreed and dismissed those claims, relying on a 1996 Fourth Circuit precedent. The Supreme Court earlier sent the case back for the Fourth Circuit to reconsider in light of a new Supreme Court decision on this immunity question, but the Fourth Circuit again ruled for the sheriff's office, prompting this second petition.

## What the Court is being asked

The petitioners ask the Court to decide two things: whether its recent ruling on which government entities count as part of a state for immunity purposes applies only to entities organized as corporations, and whether the Charleston County Sheriff's Office itself qualifies as part of the state of South Carolina, rather than as a county agency that can be sued like any other local government body.

## Where it stands

The petition for a writ of certiorari was filed on November 9, 2026, and a response from the Charleston County Sheriff's Office is due December 14, 2026. The Court has not yet acted on the petition.

## What each outcome would mean

The family members who lost relatives in the crash are asking the Court to take up the case. If the Court grants the petition, it would hear full arguments and decide whether the sheriff's office can claim state immunity, with no outcome yet determined. If the Court denies the petition, the Fourth Circuit's ruling stands, leaving the sheriff's office immune from these claims and the lawsuit against it over.
