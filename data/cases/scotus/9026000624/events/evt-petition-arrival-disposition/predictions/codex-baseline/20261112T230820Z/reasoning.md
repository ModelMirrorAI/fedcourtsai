## Why above baseline

The questions presented and the appended March 23, 2026 order establish that the Supreme Court already vacated the first judgment in this litigation for reconsideration under Galette. That earlier petition, No. 25-640, is pre-existing procedural history, not the outcome of No. 26-624 now being forecast. The July 15 opinion then reinstated its prior result in a short discussion distinguishing Galette by its treatment of a corporation. This creates a focused possible conflict with the remand's governing precedent rather than an ordinary request to correct a fact-bound mistake. Sources: `questions-presented.txt`; `decision-below.txt`, pp. 3–6; `petition.txt`, appendices B and C.

Independent checking through CourtListener found Galette, opinion 11270334. Its legal-separateness analysis expressly extends beyond incorporated entities and discusses formal responsibility for judgments. This supports petitioners' objection to treating corporate form as dispositive; it does not itself establish that this sheriff's office is legally separate. Source: CourtListener `search_document`, phrase `not the only`, position 53133, with surrounding context. The supplied question likewise asserts that South Carolina has no legal liability for CCSO judgments; I treat that as the petitioners' position, not an adjudicated finding.

## Why denial remains more likely

The panel says it reviewed Galette and found consistency with its existing precedent, Cromer. A GVR directs reconsideration and does not command a new result. Galette's applicability beyond corporations does not by itself settle how state law structures this particular sheriff's office. The panel's conclusion could survive a fuller analysis. The July 15 decision is unpublished, the dispute is partly state-specific, and the supplied materials do not establish a clean, developed inter-circuit conflict on the same legal question. The petition's contents page asserts a growing conflict, but the supporting body text is unavailable. I do not accept the assertion as proof. No same-question petition exceeded the provisioned matching threshold; that is not proof no companion or split exists.

The appendix's August 11 order shows rehearing and rehearing en banc denied with no judge requesting a poll. It supplies no visible dissent. This is modest negative evidence, not a requirement for review. The appeal concerns a Rule 54(b)-certified subset of claims; other claims remain, and the appellate footnote declined to consider additional financial material as moot under circuit precedent. Those features make definitive summary resolution of CCSO's status less straightforward. Sources: `petition.txt`, appendix A; `decision-below.txt`, pp. 4, 6.

A rough allocation consistent with my headline is 15% summary reversal, 3% GVR, 6% plenary or partial review, 75% denial and 1% nongrant termination. Thus the conditional summary-route probability is (15+3)/24 = 75%. This is a transparency decomposition, not additional scored claims. Another GVR is possible, but simply repeating the same instruction has less value than correcting the legal approach directly if four Justices want to intervene.

## Other claims and limitations

`relist-increment = 0.98` means at least one future **distribution** relative to the frozen zero count, including the initial distribution. It does not mean a 98% chance of an actual second conference. I put the chance of at least one true repeat distribution around 32%, with zero or one such repeats most likely. A CVSG is unlikely (2.5%): the issue implicates governmental structure but involves no federal party and is framed around an already-decided precedent. Conditional on denial, the chance of any dissent or statement is 9%. Per-Justice order-writer probabilities are unconditional, low, and include possible writings on a grant order; they are not predictions of cert votes or a merits author.

The largest input limitation is that `petition.txt` jumps from front matter and appendix contents to appendix A: the advertised numbered merits-of-cert body, pp. 1–29, is not present as readable text despite `truncated: false` and `empty_text: false`. Thus my assessment uses the questions, contents headings and appendix, not an imagined full argument. The official PDF retrieval failed, so the gap remains. The lower-court docket summary also fails to find a rehearing petition/disposition although appendix A records the denial; I prefer the appendix. Both issues are flagged. No BIO has yet been staged; with its deadline in the future, that is not treated as nonopposition. The corpus query did not supply usable priors; the forecast rests on the frozen anchor, committed statpack, staged record and the focused precedent check.
