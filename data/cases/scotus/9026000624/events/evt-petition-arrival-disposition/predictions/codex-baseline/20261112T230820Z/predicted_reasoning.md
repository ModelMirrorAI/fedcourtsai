# Forecast of the Court's action

The Court will most likely deny the petition, leaving the Fourth Circuit's immunity judgment in place without explaining its reasons. The principal reason not to take the case is that the judgment can be treated as a state-specific application of existing arm-of-the-state law after reconsideration, rather than a suitable vehicle for another broad sovereign-immunity decision.

The baseline records **zero distributions**. I expect an initial conference distribution after the response process, most plausibly in early 2027. The probability of any additional distribution relative to zero is 98%; this includes the initial one and is not a prediction of a true relist. My modal path has one distribution and no true relist. The probability of at least one subsequent repeat distribution is about 32%; if review gains traction, one or two repeats are more likely than a long hold.

The Court will probably not call for the Solicitor General's views: I assign a 2.5% chance to a new CVSG. If one issues, it would most plausibly follow initial consideration in early 2027 rather than precede the response process. I predict no denial-related separate writing; conditional on denial, any dissent or statement has a 9% probability. Sotomayor or Gorsuch would be comparatively plausible authors if a Justice writes, but neither is expected to do so.

If the Court grants, I assign a 75% conditional chance to disposition in the grant order rather than plenary briefing and argument. A summary reversal requiring a legally sound immunity analysis is more likely than another remand simply repeating the earlier instruction. Such relief need not conclusively determine whether CCSO ultimately is an arm of the State.

If review is granted, the likely focus is a narrowed version of question one: whether Galette can be distinguished merely because the asserted state arm lacks corporate form. Question two would frame the application of the governing analysis, but I do not expect a broad unconditional holding that every South Carolina sheriff's office lacks immunity. A full merits ruling is conditional on a less likely plenary grant, not part of the denial forecast.
