# Rationale

I assign a 0.22 probability to any grant-family disposition and predict denial. The principal quantitative anchor is the committed statpack's sal-v3 `elevated` risk set, matching the frozen context. Pooling the bracketed `reached` rates over OT2017-OT2024 gives 693 estimated grant-family outcomes among 3,419 weighted resolved petitions, or 20.27%. That is the appropriate prior-Term anchor for this paid OT2025 petition with two distributions and no CVSG. For context rather than substitution, the paid segment's terminal one-relist cut has a 12.85% grant-family rate, and 27.40% of resolved petitions that reached at least one relist ended with at least two relists.

I adjust modestly above the 20.27% anchor because the snapshot shows more than routine docket activity. After respondents waived a response, the Court requested one on June 15. The complete petition and BIO were then filed, one amicus supported petitioners, and the case was redistributed for the September 28 conference. The petition presents a federal statutory question with significant international-comity and supply-chain implications. It also plausibly frames an express disagreement: the petition says the Ninth Circuit rejected the Seventh Circuit's treatment of materially similar foreign component purchases in *Motorola Mobility LLC v. AU Optronics Corp.*, 775 F.3d 816 (7th Cir. 2015).

The complete BIO materially limits the upward adjustment. It argues that the Ninth Circuit did not make the location of negotiations sufficient, but instead treated higher U.S. prices as the domestic effect and the common pricing agreement as a possible proximate cause of the foreign overcharges. More importantly for certiorari, the decision came on interlocutory review and remanded whether the quarterly prices actually constrained the foreign affiliates. The domestic plaintiff's indirect-purchaser/control theory and an import-effects theory also remain unresolved. Those fact-bound and alternative-ground problems make this a poor vehicle even if the doctrinal disagreement is genuine.

The petition, questions-presented extract, and BIO were complete and untruncated. I used their text rather than inferring the parties' positions from the docket. A CourtListener search confirmed that *Empagran* is the principal Supreme Court FTAIA decision and surfaced the relevant circuit authorities. A linked result purporting to be the Ninth Circuit decision returned unrelated Idaho parental-rights text when opened, so I discarded that result and relied on the provisioned filings' detailed descriptions of the decision. This retrieval defect did not expose an outcome and did not affect the forecast.

The 0.28 relist-increment probability is close to the statpack's empirical 27.40% transition approximation and reflects the clean split claim. The 0.12 CVSG probability is higher than a generic petition because federal antitrust enforcement and foreign-commerce concerns could benefit from the government's views, but no CVSG is yet shown and denial remains more likely. A summary disposition is unlikely conditional on grant because there is no identified intervening authority and the causation issue would benefit from plenary briefing. A denial would probably draw no separate writing because the dispute is technical and the vehicle objections provide a quiet basis to deny review.
