# Forecast

The petition has been distributed twice, corresponding to one relist from the first distribution shown in the snapshot. The most likely next event is denial after the September 28, 2026 conference, without another distribution. There is a meaningful minority chance of one further relist while the Justices consider the asserted circuit conflict; the probability of at least one additional distribution is 0.28.

The Court is unlikely to call for the Solicitor General's views. The federal government's prior prosecution and the international-antitrust implications make a CVSG plausible, but the petition is already fully opposed and set for conference. The probability of a post-prediction CVSG is 0.12.

If the Court grants review, it will most likely take a narrowed version of the sole question presented: whether the FTAIA's domestic-effects exception permits claims by foreign affiliates when a U.S.-negotiated master agreement and quarterly pricing process allegedly control their foreign purchases. Plenary review would be much more likely than a cert-order disposition because resolving the asserted disagreement with *Motorola Mobility* would require explaining the relevant domestic effect and causal connection. Conditional on a grant, the probability of a summary disposition is 0.04.

The overall forecast is denial. The Ninth Circuit characterized the facts as unusual and remanded unresolved causation and indirect-purchaser issues, giving the Court strong vehicle grounds despite the petition's express split framing. If review is denied, no Justice is likely to note a dissent or statement; the conditional probability of some separate writing is 0.04.
