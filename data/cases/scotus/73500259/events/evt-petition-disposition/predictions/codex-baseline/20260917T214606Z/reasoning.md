Neither cut supplies the forward probability of another distribution or a new CVSG. I use their shape, not their terminal rates as hazards or additional independent evidence.

My **23%** any-grant estimate is moderately above the 17.224% anchor. The question concerns recurring cross-border supply-chain arrangements rather than only an asserted error in damages calculation. The petition identifies explicit disagreement with the Seventh Circuit over a specific statutory limitation, and the requested response after waiver is a real act of judicial attention. The snapshot also identifies an economics-focused amicus. These support more interest than a routine private petition, but I have not treated amicus participation as proof of the breadth or correctness of its arguments.

The stronger reason not to go much higher is that the asserted conflict is contested at the level of the actual causal chain and holdings. The petition argues that negotiation of foreign prices in the United States cannot itself be a qualifying domestic effect. The opposition says the Ninth Circuit instead required U.S. price effects to control foreign affiliates' prices, with causation still to be established on remand; it expressly disputes the petition's characterization as a location-of-negotiations rule. These are the parties' competing accounts, not two independently established factual findings.

The limited independent check of **Motorola Mobility LLC v. AU Optronics Corp., 775 F.3d 816, 819, 822 (7th Cir.)**, through CourtListener opinion 2755741, confirms substantial tension: the opinion locates the purchases and initial price injury abroad, and discusses the U.S. parent's control over purchase prices without disregarding the subsidiaries' separate status. But its discussion also gives the opposition room to distinguish downstream U.S. effects and indirect-purchaser issues. I therefore regard the petition's split theory as serious, not conclusively square. I did not independently retrieve the Ninth Circuit opinion or the full underlying record.

Vehicle objections materially temper the increase. The opposition identifies unresolved causation on remand, an unchallenged ruling requiring reconsideration of the domestic parent's ownership/control theory under Illinois Brick, and an unaddressed import-effects alternative. Those arguments do not conclusively eliminate the foreign affiliates' statutory question, but they make immediate review less attractive than a final, cleaner disposition. The petition answers that its legal theory would dispose of the relevant foreign-purchase claims regardless of further causation findings. I give that answer weight without treating it as overcoming every alternative-ground objection. The guilty plea and restitution context described by the briefs further complicate presenting this as a pure territorial-limit dispute; they do not themselves settle private civil liability.

Finally, the second distribution followed a requested BIO and the summer interval. It is not reliable proof that the Justices twice examined a fully briefed petition and affirmatively retained it. I preserve the harness's count of two while discounting the stronger substantive-relist interpretation. This prevents the frozen elevated prior and the same docket entries from becoming two separate upward adjustments.
