# Forecast: NHK Spring Co. v. Seagate Technology LLC, No. 25-1358 (cert)

**State forecast from:** the docket shows **two distributions** — June 9, 2026
for the June 25 conference (overtaken when the Court requested a response on
June 15, after respondents had waived), and August 5, 2026 for the
**September 28, 2026 long conference**, with the BIO and petitioners' reply
on file. No CVSG is on the docket.

**Relist (further distribution past the two shown):** close to a coin flip,
slightly against — **P ≈ 0.45**. The September 28 consideration is effectively
the Court's first real look with full briefing. The modal single path is a
denial in the orders following the long conference with no third distribution.
But three live routes add distributions: (i) any grant — under current practice
a grant from the long conference almost always follows at least one relist;
(ii) a CVSG, which triggers redistribution after the Solicitor General files;
(iii) a relist-then-deny, common for petitions the Court itself called a
response on. Expect that if the petition survives September 28 at all, it draws
one to three further distributions rather than exactly one.

**CVSG:** **P ≈ 0.12** — a real possibility, above the docket-wide base rate by
an order of magnitude. The United States prosecuted the underlying cartel (NHK's
2019 guilty plea, E.D. Mich.), the DOJ/FTC international enforcement guidelines
are invoked by both sides, and the FTAIA's reach is a question on which the
Court has historically wanted the SG's views. If it comes, expect the invitation
in the orders after the September 28 or an early-October 2026 conference, with
the SG's brief in spring 2027 and disposition late in OT2026 or early OT2027.

**Which question, if taken:** the petition presents a single QP. The Court would
likely take it substantially as written, though a reformulation is plausible —
the BIO contests the premise that the Ninth Circuit adopted a
negotiations-situs rule, so a grant may recast the question onto the FTAIA's
"gives rise to" prong: whether a domestic price effect that contractually fixes
the price of wholly foreign transactions proximately causes a foreign
purchaser's Sherman Act claim.

**Summary disposition:** unlikely. No intervening decision of this Court bears
on the FTAIA question, so a GVR has nothing to ride on; a summary reversal of a
reasoned, published circuit decision creating a claimed split is not how the
Court treats this posture. If the petition succeeds it is a plenary grant.

**If denied:** expect **no dissent from denial and no statement**. Technical
antitrust/statutory-scope denials rarely draw separate writings, and no Justice
has a signalled stake in the FTAIA. The denial would most likely issue in
October–November 2026 (after the long conference or one relist); on the CVSG
path, disposition slips to spring–summer 2027.

**If granted (conditional):** the likely ground for reversal is that the
FTAIA's domestic-effects exception requires the plaintiff's own injury to be
proximately caused by a domestic effect, and that a foreign purchaser's
overcharge on foreign transactions — even at contractually U.S.-set prices —
arises from an effect on foreign commerce, in line with the Seventh Circuit's
Motorola reading of Empagran.
