# Predicted reasoning — NHK Spring Co. v. Seagate Technology LLC, No. 25-1358

**Bottom line: the Court denies certiorari, most likely on the October 5, 2026 order list following the September 28 long conference, or after a single relist. P(any grant) = 0.18.**

## Where the docket stands

The petition has been distributed twice, but has not yet been considered at any conference. The June 9 distribution (for the June 25 conference) was superseded when the Court called for a response on June 15 after Seagate had waived; the August 5 distribution puts the petition on the September 28, 2026 long conference for its first actual consideration. So the state I forecast from is: zero true relists, one call for response, one amicus brief (International Center for Law & Economics) supporting the petitioner, no CVSG.

## Relists

P(at least one further distribution) = 0.42. Three routes produce one: a grant (the Court nearly always relists once before granting to check the vehicle), a CVSG (which produces a fresh distribution after the Solicitor General files), and a relist or reschedule that ends in denial. The long conference clears the great majority of petitions on the first pass; a paid petition with a call for response and a circuit-split framing is in the minority that draws a second look. If relisted, expect one to two further distributions, not a long string.

## Call for the views of the Solicitor General

P(CVSG) = 0.22. The FTAIA is squarely a federal interest: the Antitrust Division prosecuted NHK for this very conspiracy, the Department of Justice has published guidelines on the domestic-effects exception that the petition invokes, and the Court has historically wanted the government's view before touching extraterritorial antitrust reach. Against that, the Court denied the Seventh Circuit's mirror-image case (Motorola Mobility v. AU Optronics, 2015) without asking. If a CVSG issues it will come from the September 28 conference or a single relist after it, with the SG's brief arriving in the spring of 2027 and disposition late in OT2026.

## Which question, if granted

The Court would not take the question as written. The petition frames the holding as "domestic negotiations are sufficient"; the Ninth Circuit's opinion rests on fixed U.S. prices as the domestic effect and contractual price-binding as proximate cause. A grant would reformulate around the FTAIA's second prong: whether a foreign purchaser's injury from foreign transactions "arises from" a domestic effect when the foreign price was contractually set by a U.S.-negotiated price-fixed agreement. The Court would review the legal standard only, leaving the remanded factual question (whether the RFQ prices truly bound the foreign affiliates) alone.

## Summary disposition

P(cert-order disposition | grant) = 0.04. No intervening decision of this Court bears on the FTAIA's second prong, and the Ninth Circuit opinion is a reasoned published decision. A grant here is plenary review, argued in OT2026 or OT2027.

## Dissent or statement respecting denial

P(some Justice writes | denial) = 0.06. Antitrust extraterritoriality has no reliable champion for separate writing on denial, and the interlocutory posture gives a would-be dissenter an easy reason to wait for a final judgment. Expect a bare denial.

## Conditional merits view

If the Court grants, reversal or vacatur is more likely than affirmance: the current Court reads extraterritorial reach narrowly (RJR Nabisco, Abitron) and the Ninth Circuit's "comity may have to be cast aside" language invites correction. But the fact-bound proximate-cause holding and the guilty-plea admissions give the Court room for a narrow affirmance, so this is closer to 60/40 than lopsided.
