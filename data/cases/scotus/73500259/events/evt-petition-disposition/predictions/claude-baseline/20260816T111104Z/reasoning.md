## Adjustments up

- **Call for response after a waiver.** Respondents waived on June 8; the Court
  requested a response on June 15. That is an affirmative act of attention
  beyond the distribution count that likely drives the band, and CFR'd paid
  petitions historically grant at several times the segment rate.
- **The split claim is concrete.** The petition pins an express rejection of the
  Seventh Circuit's Motorola (Posner, J.) on materially similar facts, with the
  panel's own quotable language ("[i]nternational comity may have to be cast
  aside"). The QP sits in a recurring, cert-worthy area (FTAIA
  extraterritoriality) the Court last addressed in Empagran (2004).
- **Professional posture.** Experienced Supreme Court counsel on both sides
  (Paul Hastings; Sidley/Wilson Sonsini), a cert-stage amicus, a clean paid
  petition with a fully developed record of the conflict.

## Adjustments down

- **Interlocutory posture, and a real one.** The Ninth Circuit vacated partial
  summary judgment and remanded open, potentially dispositive issues: the
  proximate-cause factual question, Seagate LLC's Illinois Brick
  control-exception claim, and the unreached import-effects exception. The
  Court strongly disfavors granting where the case may resolve below.
- **The BIO's "question not presented" rebuttal is colorable.** The panel
  disclaimed a situs-of-negotiations rule and repeatedly called the facts
  "unique"; respondents argue the split is illusory because Motorola turned on
  a different causal direction and standing posture. A fact-bound framing gives
  the Court an easy pass.
- **The Court has passed on this before.** It denied certiorari in Motorola
  itself (2015) despite a claimed conflict — general legal context I carry
  from training, not retrieved about this case.
- **Equities.** NHK's guilty plea (and its agreement that civil remedies would
  substitute for restitution) makes an unsympathetic petitioner.

Net: the up- and down-adjustments roughly offset, with a slight upward nudge
for the CFR, which I do not believe the band prices. **P(grant family) = 0.22**;
most likely single outcome remains **denied** (`granted = 0`).

## The other claims

- **relist-increment 0.45.** The two distributions shown overstate conference
  exposure: the CFR took the petition off the June 25 conference, so
  September 28 is its first real consideration. A grant usually (not always —
  see the recent granted priors below at distribution counts 1–2) runs through
  a relist, a CVSG would guarantee later distributions, and the CFR marks
  above-average survival odds; but the modal elevated petition still dies at
  its first conference. Statpack shape check: ~74% of the paid scored segment
  ends at relist 0, and my state is better-selected than that population.
- **cvsg-increment 0.12.** Base CVSG incidence in the paid scored segment is
  ~1.3% (173/13,596). This case's profile — U.S. enforcement interest (DOJ
  prosecuted NHK), the DOJ/FTC international guidelines, foreign-relations
  comity stakes, an interlocutory vehicle the Court might want the SG to vet —
  is an order of magnitude above that, and a CVSG is the natural hedge between
  grant and deny at the long conference.
- **summary-disposition-route 0.05 (conditional on grant).** No intervening
  decision exists to GVR against; the statpack's high gvr share of the grant
  family is dominated by criminal/immigration wash-back the Court GVRs in light
  of new decisions, which has no analogue here. A grant means plenary review.
- **dissent-from-denial 0.06 (conditional on denial).** Denial-stage writings
  are rare and concentrate in ideologically salient areas; a business
  extraterritoriality case draws at most an occasional statement respecting
  denial. No published baseline exists; banked honestly.
