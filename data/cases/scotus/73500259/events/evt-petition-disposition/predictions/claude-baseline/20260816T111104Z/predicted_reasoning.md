# Forecast — NHK Spring Co. v. Seagate Technology LLC, No. 25-1358 (petition disposition)

**State forecast from:** the docket shows two distributions — June 9, 2026 for the
June 25 conference, and August 5, 2026 for the September 28 long conference. The
first listing was effectively superseded: on June 15 the Court called for a
response (respondents had waived), which took the petition off the June 25
conference before it could be considered. So the September 28 long conference
will be the Court's first real look. The petition is fully briefed — BIO (July
22), petitioner's reply (August 5) — with one cert-stage amicus (International
Center for Law & Economics). No CVSG is on the docket.

**Relists.** If the Court is interested, the first move out of September 28 is a
relist or a CVSG, not a grant — the long conference grants sparingly and this
petition's vehicle wrinkles invite a closer look. I put the chance the petition
is distributed at least once more past the two distributions already shown at
about **45%**: the call for a response after a waiver shows someone in the
building wanted this examined, but most petitions in that position are still
denied at their first conference. If it survives September 28, expect one to
three further distributions before resolution.

**CVSG.** A real possibility, above the ordinary run of petitions: roughly
**12%**. The FTAIA's extraterritorial reach is classic Solicitor General
territory — the United States prosecuted NHK's underlying price-fixing, the
DOJ/FTC international enforcement guidelines are directly implicated, and the
comity stakes touch foreign relations. If the Court is not ready to deny at the
long conference but hesitant to grant an interlocutory case, inviting the SG's
views is the natural middle path; it would issue out of the September 28
conference or a relist shortly after.

**Which question, if taken.** The Court would not take the petition's QP quite
as written — the BIO makes a fair point that the Ninth Circuit disclaimed a
pure situs-of-negotiations rule. A grant would likely reformulate toward the
underlying conflict: whether the FTAIA's domestic-effects exception permits
foreign purchasers to recover for foreign purchases whose prices were fixed or
set in the United States — the question on which the panel expressly criticized
the Seventh Circuit's Motorola analysis. (Registered context-only.)

**Summary disposition.** Unlikely — about **5%** conditional on a grant. No
intervening decision of this Court bears on the FTAIA, so there is nothing to
GVR in light of, and the interlocutory, fact-laden record is a poor fit for
summary reversal. A grant here means plenary briefing and argument.

**If denied.** Expect a clean denial. A dissent from denial or statement
respecting denial is possible but improbable (**~6%**) — the case is a
low-valence business dispute; the likeliest separate writing would be a short
statement flagging the FTAIA split as worthy of resolution in a better vehicle,
and no particular Justice has staked out that ground.

**Timing.** If denied, most likely at or within a few weeks of the September 28
long conference (October 2026 order lists). A CVSG pushes resolution to spring
2027 or later.
