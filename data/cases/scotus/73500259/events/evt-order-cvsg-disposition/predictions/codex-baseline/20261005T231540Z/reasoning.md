## Why above the anchor, but below one-half

**Reasons to raise the grant probability.** The petition identifies a concrete disagreement with the Seventh Circuit's treatment of overseas component purchases rather than merely alleging erroneous application of settled law. Its discussion of Motorola and the Ninth Circuit's criticism of that decision gives the conflict argument substance (petition, printed pp. 15–21). The dispute concerns a recurring supply-chain structure and the relationship between domestic price-setting and foreign purchases. International comity and federal antitrust enforcement make the requested Solicitor General input particularly relevant. The actual CVSG is evidence of attention, but not evidence of what recommendation will follow.

**Reasons to stop short of a grant prediction.** The BIO offers substantial distinctions: a domestic price effect contractually determines foreign affiliates' prices here, whereas the asserted domestic downstream effect in Motorola did not cause the earlier foreign overcharge (BIO, pp. 1–4, 14–20). The historical Motorola opinion itself discusses both U.S. price determination and corporate separateness, making the conflict serious but not conclusively square (775 F.3d 816, 822–24; amended opinion dated January 12, 2015, PDF pp. 12–14). Empagran excludes claims resting on independent foreign injury; that leaves the asserted dependence of this injury a meaningful distinction rather than automatically deciding this petition (542 U.S. 155, 158–60 (2004), historical opinion inspected through CourtListener).

The BIO's vehicle objections further reduce the forecast: an interlocutory remand, unresolved factual application of causation, the domestic parent's ownership/control theory, and an alternative import-effects theory (BIO, pp. 25–28). These are respondents' arguments, not findings that every alternative necessarily defeats review. Nevertheless, they give the Court a reason to await a cleaner record even if some Justices question the Ninth Circuit's reasoning. Together these competing considerations support a moderate increase from approximately 0.35 to **0.42**, rather than treating a CVSG or alleged split as a near-grant. The unknown Solicitor General recommendation is the largest uncertainty.
