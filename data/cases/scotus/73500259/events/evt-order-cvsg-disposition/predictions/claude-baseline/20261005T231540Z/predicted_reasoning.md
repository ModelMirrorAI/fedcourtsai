# Forecast: NHK Spring Co. v. Seagate Technology LLC, No. 25-1358 (CVSG moment)

**Headline.** The Court will most likely deny the petition after the Solicitor
General files, but a grant is a live possibility: P(any grant) = 0.36. If the
Court grants, it will set the case for plenary briefing and argument rather than
dispose of it in the cert order.

## What the docket shows now

Two distributions (Conference of June 25, 2026, which was pre-empted by the
Court's own request for a response, and the September 28, 2026 long conference),
one amicus brief in support of the petition (International Center for Law &
Economics), and a CVSG entered October 5, 2026. No further distribution exists.

## Further distributions

The petition will be redistributed at least once more, almost certainly: a CVSG
petition returns to conference only after the Solicitor General's brief arrives
(P ≈ 0.96; the residual is settlement or withdrawal, which is not negligible in a
private damages case already remanded for further proceedings). Expect the SG's
brief in roughly May 2027, on the ordinary cadence for an October invitation,
with the petition distributed for a June 2027 conference. One additional relist
beyond that is plausible if a Justice is considering a separate writing, but the
modal path is a single post-CVSG distribution followed by disposition in June
2027 or at the following long conference.

## The Solicitor General's views

The SG's recommendation will drive the outcome, and it is genuinely uncertain.
The Antitrust Division prosecuted NHK and agreed to forgo restitution on the
premise that civil remedies were available; the Division's 2017 international
guidelines read the FTAIA's domestic-effects exception through proximate cause
rather than situs of negotiation, which is closer to the Ninth Circuit's framing
than to the petition's. Against that, the government has long defended the
comity-protective reading of Empagran and has an institutional interest in not
exposing U.S. firms to treble damages for wholly foreign transactions. The
likelier recommendation is to deny, on vehicle grounds (interlocutory posture,
"unique" facts, the unresolved Illinois Brick control-exception claim of the
domestic plaintiff) while agreeing the question is important; a recommendation
to grant is perhaps a 40% proposition.

## Which question the Court would take, if it takes one

The petition's QP as written, but the Court would understand it the way the
BIO frames it: whether a foreign affiliate's foreign purchase at a price fixed
in the United States is a claim that "arises from" a domestic effect under
15 U.S.C. § 6a(2). A grant would likely be accompanied by no reformulation; the
Court would decide the "gives rise to" prong and whether Motorola's rule or the
Ninth Circuit's proximate-cause inquiry governs.

## Route

No summary disposition. There is no intervening decision to GVR in light of,
and the Ninth Circuit's opinion is a reasoned, published decision on a question
of first impression in that circuit, not the kind of error the Court corrects
per curiam. Conditional on a grant, P(cert-order disposition) ≈ 0.04.

## If denied

Expect a plain denial. A statement respecting denial from a Justice attentive to
extraterritoriality concerns (Justice Gorsuch or Justice Kavanaugh are the
likeliest authors) is possible but uncommon after a CVSG recommending denial;
P(any noted dissent or statement | denial) ≈ 0.10.

## If granted (conditional)

The Court would likely reverse or vacate, holding that a foreign purchaser's
injury from a foreign transaction does not "arise from" a domestic effect merely
because the price was negotiated or set in the United States, and remanding for
the district court to consider the domestic plaintiff's indirect-purchaser claim
and the import-commerce theories the Ninth Circuit left open. That is
conditional on review being granted; a denial produces no opinion.
