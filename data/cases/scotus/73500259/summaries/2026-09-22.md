## What happened

NHK Spring, a Japanese maker of hard drive components, pleaded guilty to a criminal conspiracy to fix prices of parts called suspension assemblies. Seagate, a hard drive maker, then sued NHK for antitrust violations, including claims by Seagate's foreign affiliates in Thailand and Singapore over parts they bought overseas. A federal district court ruled that a federal law limiting when U.S. antitrust law reaches foreign transactions barred those foreign affiliates' claims. The Ninth Circuit reversed, holding the foreign affiliates could proceed because NHK's price-fixing set U.S. prices that, under the companies' supply contracts, also controlled what the foreign affiliates paid. NHK asked the Supreme Court to review that ruling, and Seagate opposes review.

## What the Court is being asked

The case concerns a law that limits when U.S. antitrust law covers business conduct connected to foreign countries. NHK's petition frames the question as whether simply negotiating deal terms inside the United States is enough to let foreign buyers sue over foreign purchases under U.S. antitrust law, even without a separate effect on U.S. commerce. Seagate answers that the appeals court's ruling did not rest on where negotiations happened, but on the fact that the conspiracy set U.S. prices that directly determined what its foreign affiliates paid, so the case does not actually present the question NHK describes.

## Where it stands

The petition has been distributed for conference more than once, most recently for the September 28, 2026 conference, after full briefing including a response, an amicus brief, and a reply from NHK. The Court has not yet acted on the petition.
