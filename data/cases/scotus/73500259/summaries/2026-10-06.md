## In brief

NHK Spring asks the Court to overturn a ruling that let Seagate's foreign affiliates sue NHK under U.S. antitrust law over price-fixed parts purchased abroad.

## What happened

NHK Spring, a Japanese manufacturer, pleaded guilty to a criminal conspiracy to fix prices of hard-drive components sold in the United States and elsewhere. Seagate Technology LLC and several of its foreign affiliates sued NHK under U.S. antitrust law. A federal district court ruled that a federal law limiting antitrust suits over foreign conduct barred claims based on purchases Seagate's foreign affiliates made abroad. The Ninth Circuit reversed, holding that the conspiracy's effects on U.S. prices could support the foreign affiliates' claims, and sent the case back for further proceedings. NHK then asked the Supreme Court to review that decision, and Seagate opposes review.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked whether a foreign company can be sued under U.S. antitrust law over foreign affiliates' purchases made entirely outside the United States, where the claim rests on the idea that U.S. price negotiations affected what those foreign affiliates paid, even though the foreign transactions themselves had no separate direct effect on U.S. commerce. Seagate disputes NHK's framing, arguing the real question is instead whether foreign affiliates of a U.S. company may sue based on price-fixing that admittedly affected U.S. prices directly.

## Where it stands

The petition has been distributed for conference twice, and after the second conference the Court invited the Solicitor General to file a brief giving the federal government's views. The case has not yet been granted or denied.

## What each outcome would mean

NHK Spring, which lost before the Ninth Circuit, is asking the Court to take up the case. If the Court grants review, it would hear full arguments and decide the legal question, without yet deciding who wins. If it denies review, the Ninth Circuit's ruling stands, and the case returns to the district court for further proceedings on Seagate's claims. The Solicitor General's views, once filed, may inform whether the Court acts at a later conference.
