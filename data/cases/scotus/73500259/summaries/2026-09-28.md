## In brief

NHK Spring asks the Court to review a Ninth Circuit ruling letting Seagate's foreign affiliates sue NHK under U.S. antitrust law over price-fixed parts purchased abroad.

## What happened

NHK Spring, a Japanese company, pleaded guilty to a criminal conspiracy fixing prices of hard-drive components called suspension assemblies, sold in the United States and elsewhere. Seagate Technology LLC and several of its foreign affiliates then sued NHK and other suppliers for antitrust damages. A federal district court ruled that a federal law limiting U.S. antitrust law's reach overseas, the Foreign Trade Antitrust Improvements Act, barred the foreign affiliates' claims because their purchases happened abroad. The Ninth Circuit vacated that ruling, holding NHK's price-fixing had a qualifying domestic effect and remanding for further fact-finding on whether that effect caused the foreign affiliates' losses. NHK sought rehearing, which was denied, then petitioned for Supreme Court review. Seagate opposes review.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked whether an antitrust law's limits on suing over foreign transactions are satisfied merely because part of the price-setting negotiations happened in the United States, even when the challenged purchases and deliveries occurred entirely abroad. NHK argues the appeals court wrongly allowed such claims; Seagate responds that the lower court's ruling did not rest on where negotiations occurred, but on the direct link between NHK's U.S. price-fixing and the prices its foreign affiliates paid.

## Where it stands

The petition has been distributed for conference twice, most recently for the conference of September 28, 2026, after the respondents' opposition brief and the petitioners' reply were filed. The docket does not yet show any action on the petition.

## What each outcome would mean

NHK Spring, as petitioner, asked the Court to take up the case. If the Court grants review, it will hear full arguments and decide the legal question itself, deciding nothing yet about who is right. If it denies review, the Ninth Circuit's ruling stands, and the case returns to the lower courts for further proceedings on Seagate's claims, including the factual questions the Ninth Circuit sent back on causation and on a separate indirect-purchaser theory.
