## In brief

NHK Spring asks the Court to decide whether its U.S. price negotiations can expose it to antitrust liability for sales completed entirely overseas.

## What happened

NHK, a Japanese manufacturer, pleaded guilty to a criminal conspiracy fixing prices of suspension assemblies, a hard-drive component, sold in the United States and elsewhere. Seagate, an American company, and its foreign affiliates sued NHK for antitrust violations, arguing the affiliates overpaid abroad for assemblies priced through negotiations conducted in the United States. The district court ruled that a federal antitrust law limiting lawsuits over foreign commerce barred most of these claims. The Ninth Circuit reversed in part, holding Seagate's foreign affiliates might proceed because the U.S.-negotiated prices allegedly set what they paid overseas, and sent the case back for further proceedings.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked whether a federal law restricting antitrust lawsuits over foreign transactions allows foreign companies to sue over purchases made entirely outside the United States, when the only U.S. connection is that part of the price-setting process happened here. NHK argues this conflicts with another appeals court's ruling that such domestic negotiations are not enough by themselves to trigger American antitrust law.

## Where it stands

The petition has been fully briefed, including a reply, and was distributed for the Court's conference in September 2026. The Court has since invited the Solicitor General to file a brief expressing the federal government's views on whether the case should be heard.

## What each outcome would mean

NHK, which lost before the Ninth Circuit, is asking the Court to take up the case and reverse that ruling. If the Court grants review and ultimately rules for NHK, it would narrow or eliminate the foreign affiliates' ability to sue under U.S. antitrust law for purchases made abroad, likely ending part of Seagate's case. If it rules for Seagate, the Ninth Circuit's decision allowing the case to proceed would stand. If the Court denies review, the Ninth Circuit's ruling remains in effect and the case returns to the district court for further proceedings on the merits.
