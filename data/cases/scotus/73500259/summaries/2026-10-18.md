## In brief

NHK asks the Court to review a Ninth Circuit ruling letting Seagate's foreign affiliates pursue antitrust claims over price-fixed parts purchased abroad.

## What happened

NHK Spring pleaded guilty to conspiring to fix prices of suspension assemblies, hard-drive components, sold in the United States and elsewhere. Seagate Technology LLC and its foreign affiliates in Thailand and Singapore sued NHK, claiming they overpaid for these parts under a supply agreement negotiated in the United States. A federal district court granted NHK partial summary judgment, ruling that a federal law limiting the reach of antitrust claims abroad barred claims based on purchases made overseas. The Ninth Circuit vacated that ruling, holding Seagate's foreign affiliates could proceed because pricing set through United States negotiations allegedly set the prices they paid abroad, and sent the case back for further proceedings.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked whether pricing terms negotiated in the United States, by themselves, can bring foreign companies' purchases of foreign-made goods within reach of American antitrust law, even when the transactions affected took place entirely outside the United States and had no separate effect on domestic trade.

## Where it stands

The case has been distributed for conference more than once, and the Court has invited the Solicitor General to file a brief giving the federal government's views; the case awaits that response before further action.

## What each outcome would mean

NHK, which lost below, is asking the Court to take up the case. If the Court grants review, it would hear full arguments and decide whether the lower court applied the antitrust law's reach correctly; a grant would not itself decide who wins. If the Court denies review, the Ninth Circuit's ruling stands, and Seagate's foreign affiliates could continue pursuing their claims against NHK in the lower courts. Because the Solicitor General has been invited to weigh in, the Court is not expected to act on the petition until after receiving that brief.
