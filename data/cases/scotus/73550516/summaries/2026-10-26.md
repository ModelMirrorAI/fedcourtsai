## In brief

An insurer's bid to have the Supreme Court decide which law governs arbitration disputes under an international insurance treaty ended when the insurer withdrew its case.

## What happened

Apex Hospitality bought hurricane insurance partly from Independent Specialty Insurance Company, a U.S. insurer, and partly from a foreign insurer. After a hurricane damaged its property, Apex sued only Independent Specialty in Louisiana court, not the foreign insurer. Independent Specialty asked the court to send the dispute to arbitration, relying on an international treaty, but Louisiana law generally bars arbitration clauses in insurance policies. The district court ultimately refused to order arbitration, and the Fifth Circuit Court of Appeals agreed, following its own recent precedent that state law, not federal law, controls whether a company that did not sign the arbitration agreement can still enforce it.

## What the Court is being asked

The case asked whether, under this international arbitration treaty, a court should use a federal rule or a state's own contract law to decide whether a company that is not a signatory to an arbitration agreement can still force arbitration based on it.

## Where it stands

The petition was distributed for conference, and the Court asked Apex to respond. Before the Court took any further action, Independent Specialty and Apex jointly asked to dismiss the petition, and the Court dismissed it on October 20, 2026.

## What each outcome would mean

Independent Specialty, the insurer, had asked the Court to take up the case and reverse the Fifth Circuit's ruling, which favored Apex by keeping the dispute in court rather than arbitration. Because the parties jointly agreed to dismiss the petition, the Court will not decide the legal question. The Fifth Circuit's decision stands, meaning Apex's lawsuit against Independent Specialty continues in court rather than being sent to arbitration.
