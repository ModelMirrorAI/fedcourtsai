## In brief

An insurer asks the Court to decide whether federal or state law governs when a company not party to an arbitration agreement can still enforce it.

## What happened

Apex Hospitality bought a surplus-lines property insurance policy from Independent Specialty and a foreign insurer, Lloyd's, covering a Louisiana hotel after hurricane damage. Apex sued Independent Specialty but not Lloyd's. Independent Specialty asked the court to compel arbitration, arguing that even though Apex's contract with it was a separate agreement from Apex's contract with Lloyd's, it could still enforce arbitration through a legal doctrine that lets a non-signatory invoke another party's arbitration clause. The district court eventually ruled against arbitration, applying Louisiana law, which generally bars arbitration clauses in insurance contracts. The Fifth Circuit affirmed, following its own recent precedent.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked to decide which body of law — a uniform national rule or the law of the state where the case arose — determines whether a company that did not sign an arbitration agreement can still force arbitration under principles of fairness and estoppel, in cases involving an international arbitration treaty. The petition says four other appeals courts use a national rule while the Fifth Circuit alone applies state law, producing different results for similar insurance disputes.

## Where it stands

The petition was distributed for the Court's September 28, 2026 conference. On August 17, 2026, after that conference, the Court requested a response from Apex Hospitality, due September 16, 2026.

## What each outcome would mean

Independent Specialty, the insurer, is asking the Court to take up the case. If the Court grants the petition, it would hear full arguments and decide which law governs, with no outcome assumed in advance. If it denies the petition, the Fifth Circuit's decision applying Louisiana law stands, meaning Apex is not required to arbitrate its dispute with Independent Specialty. The petition itself also asks the Court to hold the case until it rules on a related petition, Indian Harbor v. Town of Vinton, raising the same question.
