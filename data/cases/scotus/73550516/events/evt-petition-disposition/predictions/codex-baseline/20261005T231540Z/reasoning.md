## Prior and adjustments

The matching anchor is the committed statpack's **bracketed reached baseline rate**, not the much lower rate among petitions ending in baseline. For context only, the pack's all-Term modern-cert counts imply about 2.82% any-grant outcomes, and its Fifth Circuit cut is approximately 3.7% granted plus GVR. Neither supersedes the matched 5.12% prior. The paid relist cuts increase from roughly 1.7% grant-family outcomes at terminal relist bucket zero to 13.3% at one and about 41% at two. The paid CVSG cut is roughly 34.9% with a CVSG versus 6.3% without. These terminal, all-Term cuts describe selection and trajectory; they are not forward probabilities of another distribution or a CVSG, and I do not multiply them together.

My increase from 5.12% to 24% is a judgmental adjustment, not a fitted conditional estimate:

- **An identifiable unresolved legal question.** The QP asks whether federal common law or state law supplies nonsignatory equitable estoppel under the New York Convention. The petition alleges a 4–1 circuit conflict (petition pp. 8–9). In the historical precedent independently checked, *GE Energy Power Conversion France SAS, Corp. v. Outokumpu Stainless USA, LLC*, 590 U.S. 432, 445 (2020), expressly left the governing body of law unresolved. That supports treating this as an open question rather than a straightforward request to apply an already-announced Supreme Court rule. Source: CourtListener opinion 9889180, concluding section IV.
- **Some real support for disagreement, but not an audited 4–1 split.** *Setty v. Shrinivas Sugandhalaya LLP*, 3 F.4th 1166 (9th Cir. 2021), applies federal substantive law, while Judge Bea's dissent argues for the relevant state or foreign contract law. The majority speaks specifically of federal claims, and the case differs from this Louisiana insurance dispute. I verified that concrete disagreement, not the continuing scope of all four circuits cited by the petition. Source: CourtListener opinion 4701830, majority pp. 5–7 and Bea dissent. This qualification reduces the weight given to the petition's broad split characterization.
- **The choice can matter here.** The appended district-court order describes an initial decision compelling arbitration based on concerted-misconduct estoppel, then vacatur after the Louisiana Supreme Court rejected that route under state law (appendix D, pp. 18a–23a). That makes the QP more consequential than an abstract choice-of-law disagreement. Conversely, the appended Fifth Circuit lead opinion relies on separate-contract language and defends its state-law reading of earlier precedent; I do not simply adopt petitioner's account that its position is compelled (appendix B, pp. 11a–15a).
- **A requested response is attention, not four votes.** The August 17 response request is affirmative evidence that the initial unopposed posture did not end the Court's consideration. It warrants an upward adjustment, but the absent opposition prevents assessing preservation, contractual distinctions, or other vehicle objections.
- **This is principally a follower vehicle.** The two-page unpublished appellate decision says it follows *Town of Vinton* (appendix A, pp. 1a–2a). The petition itself asks for a hold pending a contemplated companion petition, then appropriate disposition (pp. 9–10). This lowers the chance of this case independently receiving plenary review while creating a meaningful conditional hold-and-GVR route. I have not retrieved the companion's current status and do not assume it was filed, granted, or decided. The lack of an en banc poll in this case is a modest counterweight, not proof the issue lacks importance (appendix C).
