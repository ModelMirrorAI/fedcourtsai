# Forecast: what the Court will do with No. 25-1427

**This petition will be decided in tandem with, and as a function of, the lead
petition in *Indian Harbor Insurance Co. v. Town of Vinton*, No. 25-1383.** The
petition itself asks for exactly that: a hold pending No. 25-1383 and
disposition "as appropriate in light of that decision". The Fifth Circuit
decision under review is a two-paragraph unpublished per curiam that simply
applies *Town of Vinton*, so the Court has no independent reason to take this
case on its own.

## Relists and distribution

The docket shows **one distribution** (August 12, 2026, for the September 28
long conference), after which the Court **requested a response** on August 17
(due September 16). The response request removed the petition from the
September 28 conference, so it was not acted on there. Once the brief in
opposition and any reply are in, the petition will be **redistributed at least
once more** — I put that near certainty (0.94); the only way it does not happen
is a settlement-driven dismissal before redistribution. Expect redistribution
for a conference in late October to December 2026, paired with No. 25-1383
(whose reply landed September 9 and which is itself awaiting redistribution).
If the Court grants the lead case, this petition will then be **held** — showing
as a sequence of further relists with no action — until the lead case is
decided, likely by June 2027. So the realistic relist trajectory is either two
distributions and a denial, or a long string of holds.

## CVSG

**No CVSG on this docket** (0.05). A call for the Solicitor General's views is
plausible in the *lead* case, because the question concerns the United States'
obligations under a treaty and the government has a uniformity interest the
amici press, but the Court would invite the SG there and simply hold this
companion. A CVSG here would be a surprise.

## Which question the Court would take

If it takes the question at all, it takes it as framed in both petitions:
**whether federal common law or state law determines whether a nonsignatory may
invoke equitable estoppel to enforce an arbitration agreement governed by the
New York Convention** — the question *GE Energy Power Conversion v. Outokumpu*
(2020) expressly left open. The Court would not reach the antecedent
contract-construction holding (that the allocation endorsement makes each
insurer's agreement a separate bilateral contract) or Louisiana's
anti-arbitration insurance statute; those stay as the Fifth Circuit left them.
If granted, the likely ground is that Chapter 2 of the FAA and the Convention's
uniformity purpose supply a federal rule for nonsignatory enforcement, with the
*Arthur Andersen v. Carlisle* "traditional principles of state law" rule
confined to Chapter 1 — but that is conditional on a grant that mostly happens
elsewhere.

## Summary disposition versus plenary review

Conditional on any grant here, the route is overwhelmingly a **GVR** (0.85):
the Court grants and hears the lead case, reverses or vacates the Fifth
Circuit, and then grants, vacates, and remands this held companion in light of
that decision. A consolidated plenary grant is the minority route; this
petition's record is thin (ten pages of argument, no appendix beyond the
opinions) and its unpublished per curiam adds nothing the lead case lacks. If
the lead case is affirmed, this petition is denied after the hold.

## Most likely disposition

**Denied** — most likely because No. 25-1383 is denied at the same conference,
and otherwise because the Court affirms in the lead case after a hold. The grant
family (a GVR, or a consolidated grant) is a one-in-five outcome.

## Dissent from denial

Unlikely (0.06). If a Justice writes respecting denial, it will be in the lead
case; the usual companion notation would be a bare "would grant" on this docket.
No Justice has a visible stake in this question; the candidates would be
Justices Gorsuch or Thomas on a treaty-uniformity theme, and even that is a
stretch for an insurance-arbitration petition.
