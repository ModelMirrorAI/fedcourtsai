The frozen amicus reference is **16.328%**, based on 2,315 resolved petitions that reached this band and ended with at least one amicus brief. I regard it as a useful intermediate reference, not a second independent multiplier. It can overstate the information in an early brief because its count is measured at disposition. This petition already has five briefs, however, so its support is observed rather than hypothetical. The paid-segment `2+` amicus bucket has 1,453 resolved petitions and approximately **22.7%** grants plus GVRs. That is a marginal comparison mixing stronger procedural states, not an estimated grant rate conditional on this entire record.

The paid-segment relist table likewise describes terminal states: grant-family shares are approximately 1.8% at zero relists (n=10,284 resolved), 13.5% at one (n=2,539), 41.4% at two (n=498), and 37.3% at three or more (n=483). I do not substitute the terminal zero-relist figure for this petition's forward hazard. The CVSG cut is about 35.5% grants plus GVRs with a CVSG (n=166) versus 6.3% without one (n=13,638). I move above the amicus reference because the requested response is affirmative attention after a waiver, the questions concern recurring class-action rules, and the record contains concrete grounds for scrutiny beyond abstract disagreement with the Ninth Circuit. The combined assessment is 30%, not a mechanically fitted estimate. I stop well short of an even chance because there is no substantive relist or CVSG, no opposition to test the petition's vehicle claims, and the decision below is unpublished and interlocutory.

## Legal and vehicle evidence

I read the provisioned questions presented, the petition's introduction, statement, split discussions and vehicle arguments, the matching appellate opinion and dissent, the appendix's identification of the decisions, and the lower-court docket summary. The CourtListener-derived appellate decision matches the caption, Ninth Circuit No. 24-7707, and January 9, 2026 judgment in the petition and appendix. Its actual text identifies the Clifton, Bybee and de Alba panel. The lower-court docket summary reports en banc rehearing denied, with no dissent noted in the entries it read; I do not infer universal judicial agreement from that limited observation.

**Prospective relief.** QP1 challenges standing for a previously deceived consumer seeking an injunction changing the same labeling. The majority expressly applies Davidson and relies on Falcone's desire to buy the products coupled with distrust of the sourcing representations (Pet. App. 2a–3a; `decision-below.txt`, majority pp. 2–3). The petition's comparison with Berni, Thorne, Conrad, Williams and Animal Legal Defense Fund presents a substantial claimed conflict (petition pp. 10–15). I have not independently verified every circuit comparison and do not treat the petition's description of a five-to-one split as an adjudicated fact. Whether future uncertainty over sourcing differs from already-known defects in other products remains an important possible distinction. The petition also acknowledges the earlier denial of review in Davidson; that history makes the claim of an automatically compelling grant less persuasive, even if the asserted conflict has developed since then.

**Uninjured class members.** QP2 overlaps the issue previously accepted in Laboratory Corp. of America Holdings v. Davis, 605 U.S. 327 (2025). I independently consulted that earlier opinion through CourtListener, opinion 11065594. The Court dismissed the writ without explaining why; Justice Kavanaugh attributed the likely problem to mootness and advocated resolving the Rule 23 question. His rationale is not the Court's holding. This supports renewed interest in a better vehicle, not four current votes for this petition. Here the majority invokes Olean but also identifies a purchase-based economic injury (Pet. App. 3a–4a n.1). Respondent could therefore dispute that the asserted uninjured population is established rather than dependent on the unresolved merits.

**Damages model.** QP3 has the strongest record-specific criticism: Judge Clifton's dissent challenges a full-refund model despite evidence that consumers received value, and objects to postponing evidentiary scrutiny until trial (`decision-below.txt`, dissent pp. 1–6). But the dissent is about the damages model, not an express disagreement on all three questions. The majority sees a sufficient relationship between the theory of deception and damages, and notes that a different price-premium model could be considered later (Pet. App. 5a–6a). That leaves a plausible respondent argument that this is a revisable certification decision and a fact-bound application of Comcast rather than the best vehicle for a broad new rule. The petition's Rule 23(f) posture is not inherently disqualifying, but multiple issues and possible model revision increase vehicle uncertainty.

`documents.json` lists readable, untruncated petition, appendix and decision-below text. No brief in opposition, cert reply or invited SG brief was provisioned. The missing opposition is expected given the pending response deadline, not an extraction failure; it still materially limits confidence. No amicus brief's substantive text was provisioned or retrieved, so I use their existence and identities, not imagined arguments.

## Other probabilities and limitations

The **96% further-distribution probability** principally reflects ordinary redistribution after a requested response that is due later than the currently listed conference. It is not a 96% probability of a genuine post-consideration relist. One further distribution is the modal path; additional consideration is plausible if review attracts support. A future CVSG receives **2.5%**: federal procedural and constitutional questions permit one, but the current private consumer dispute presents no identified specialized federal administrative interest.

Conditional on a grant, I put **10%** on disposition in the cert order and **90%** on plenary review. No provisioned lead case supplies an identified GVR trigger. Summary correction of the damages ruling remains possible but is less natural than briefing a disputed national certification rule. Conditional on denial, I put **8%** on a noted dissent or statement. Individual order-writing probabilities are unconditional, small and allow overlap; Kavanaugh is the most plausible writer given the earlier separate opinion, not a predicted vote lineup.

The stakes score **0.66** reflects the potential national effect on class certification and consumer injunctions, not the size of the grant probability or a view about the underlying labor allegations.
