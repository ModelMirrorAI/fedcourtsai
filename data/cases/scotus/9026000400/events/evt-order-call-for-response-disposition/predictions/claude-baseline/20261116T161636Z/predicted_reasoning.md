# Forecast — Nestlé USA, Inc. v. Falcone, No. 26-400

The docket shows one distribution (for the Nov 13, 2026 conference) and a response requested the same day, due Nov 27, 2026.

- **Disposition.** The Court will deny certiorari. The respondent will file a brief in opposition (probably after one extension, putting it in late December 2026 or January 2027), and the petition will be redistributed for a conference in roughly January–February 2027.
- **Further distributions.** One more distribution is essentially certain (after the response). Past that, the most likely path is a denial at that first post-response conference with no true relist; a relist after that conference would be the signal that the petition is seriously in play.
- **CVSG.** No call for the Solicitor General's views. The dispute is between private parties under California consumer-protection law.
- **Question presented, if granted.** The Court would most likely limit the grant to a single question: QP2 (certification of a damages class containing members without Article III standing — the *Labcorp* question) or, alternatively, QP1 (standing of a no-longer-deceived consumer to seek an injunction). QP3 (the *Comcast* damages-model question) would be left behind.
- **Summary disposition.** Unlikely. No intervening decision supports a GVR, and the unpublished memorandum below is not a candidate for summary reversal. A grant would be for plenary review.
- **Separate writing.** If the Court denies, expect no noted dissent or statement. If anyone writes, it would most likely be Justice Kavanaugh, on the uninjured-class-member question.
