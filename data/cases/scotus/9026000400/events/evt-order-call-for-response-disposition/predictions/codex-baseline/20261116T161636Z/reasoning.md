# Rationale for the probability

## Call and information set

I assign **32% to any grant of review**, including a summary grant, and predict **denial**. This is the cert-stage, response-requested moment, not a forecast of whether Nestlé will prevail on the merits. The common baseline is `record/snapshots/2026-10-29.json`, with entries strictly before October 29, 2026, as specified by `record/context.json`. The mode is forward. I used that baseline rather than retrieving this petition's present docket state.

The snapshot records one distribution, entered October 28 for the November 13 conference, and a response request entered the same day, due November 27. It records no completed relist, no CVSG, and five separate amicus briefs filed October 23. November 13 passing on the calendar does not establish that the petition was considered then, much less that it was relisted. The response request after the waiver is real attention, but not a four-Justice commitment to hear the case. No opposition is provisioned; its absence is expected at this moment, not evidence of concession. I do not assume what it will argue.

I read the questions presented, the petition's factual and certiorari arguments, and the appellate majority and dissent reproduced at appendix pages 1a–11a. The petition and appendix are listed as complete, non-OCR text in `documents.json`. There are no staged related dockets; `same-question.json` contains no matches at its 0.5 threshold. That does not establish the absence of other litigation, but supplies no basis for predicting a companion-case hold.

## What raises the probability

**The second question has demonstrated institutional demand.** In *Laboratory Corp. of America Holdings v. Davis*, No. 24-304, the Court took the question whether a damages class may include uninjured members, then dismissed the writ on June 5, 2025. Justice Kavanaugh's dissent would have reached the question and ruled against such certification. The primary disposition is reported at 605 U.S. 327, 328–334; I verified it through CourtListener opinion 11243430. The majority's order gives no reason for dismissal: avoiding a threshold mootness question is Kavanaugh's explanation, not an announced majority holding. Nestlé's petition, pages 19–27, claims this case avoids the amended-class-order complication. That offers a plausible replacement vehicle, though its claimed cleanliness is advocacy rather than a conceded fact. The corpus lookup also confirmed Labcorp's January 24, 2025 cert grant; its `granted` label describes that cert stage, not a merits victory.

**The prospective-injury issue is substantial.** The petition's first question contrasts the Ninth Circuit's *Davidson* rule with decisions rejecting injunctions for already-informed purchasers. I independently checked *Berni v. Barilla*, 964 F.3d 141, 147–149 (2d Cir. 2020), CourtListener opinion 4546719, which rejects the past-purchaser class's entitlement to prospective relief. The appendix majority here expressly applies *Davidson* to Falcone's continuing desire to purchase and distrust of the labels (2a–3a). That is a real doctrinal tension, though not proof that every factual variant would come out differently across circuits.

**The damages dispute has an actual dissent.** Judge Clifton explains why a universal full refund assumes that the chocolate has no value to every purchaser and why an unsupported model should not sustain certification (7a–11a). That makes the third question more concrete than an abstract request to enforce *Comcast*. Five supporting briefs from business and public-interest organizations corroborate broad interest; I use only their docketed existence and identities, not unstaged brief contents.

## What keeps denial the modal outcome

The appellate disposition is an **unpublished memorandum** following discretionary Rule 23(f) review, not a new published circuit rule. It applies existing circuit precedents. Interlocutory class-certification review is not itself disqualifying, but the order remains revisable: the majority expressly identifies a possible alternative price-premium model (6a n.2). That weakens the need to use this case to decide a broad damages-model rule now.

Most importantly, the majority does not simply find that uninjured consumers may recover. It treats materiality and reliance as common questions and describes the asserted pocketbook loss as an injury (3a n.1, 4a–5a). Whether not reading or relying on a label necessarily means no economic injury is thus contested; lack of subjective reliance is not something I automatically equate with absence of Article III injury. This provides an answer to the petition's claimed clean presentation of question two. Likewise, the majority reads Falcone's deposition as retaining uncertainty about the Cocoa Plan, while the petition stresses passages expressing satisfaction. Question one could attract fact-specific vehicle objections.

Clifton dissented on damages methodology, not on all three questions. His dissent helps the petition but does not establish a panel disagreement over each alleged split. The full-refund issue also involves California restitution law and the strength of evidence supporting valuation. A future respondent could defend those features, but no such brief is before me. I discount the vehicle for the opinion's actual reasoning, not an invented opposition.

## Other probabilities and limitations

The **0.99 redistribution increment** chiefly prices ordinary renewed distribution after a requested response, not a 99% chance of a substantive relist following a completed conference; this moment's claim is harness-masked. The **0.07 CVSG increment** allows for government interest in class-action procedure but expects the Court to decide this private consumer dispute without an invitation. The **0.12 summary route conditional on a grant** leaves plenary review dominant: the second question remains unresolved rather than mechanically controlled by Labcorp, and no intervening decision in the record supplies a GVR target. The **0.17 writing-or-noted-dissent probability conditional on denial** is modestly elevated by Kavanaugh's Labcorp dissent. Per-Justice order-writing probabilities are unconditional and allow writings on grants as well as denials; they are not forecasts of cert votes.

The significance score, **0.68**, concerns nationwide standing and class-action consequences, not grant likelihood or an adjudication of child-labor liability. No outcome of this petition or other person's forecast was sought or encountered. The broad corpus grant sample was useful only as a tooling and procedural check, not as a representative denominator. Web calls returned no usable content; the substantive external checks came from primary opinions through MCP.
