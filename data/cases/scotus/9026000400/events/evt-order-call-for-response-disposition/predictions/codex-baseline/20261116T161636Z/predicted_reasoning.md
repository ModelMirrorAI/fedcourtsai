# Forecast of the Court's action

The Court will most likely **deny certiorari after receiving the requested response**, without a separate opinion or noted dissent. The probability of any grant is **32%**. A denial will leave the certification ruling in place without endorsing its reasoning.

## Further docket steps

The baseline has **one distribution and no recorded relist**, plus an October 28 response request with a November 27, 2026 deadline. The petition will almost certainly be distributed at least once more after the response arrives or its time expires: **99%**. This is a redistribution increment from the baseline, not a claim that the originally scheduled November 13 conference actually considered the petition. The most likely path is one renewed distribution followed by denial; one or two additional substantive relists remain a plausible alternative. Absent an extension, disposition is more likely in December 2026 or January 2027 than before the response deadline.

The Court is unlikely to invite the Solicitor General's views: **7%** for a new CVSG. If one occurs, it will most naturally follow consideration of the response at a winter conference and delay disposition while the invited brief is prepared. No such invitation is present in the baseline.

## If review is granted

The most likely grant would focus on **question two**: whether a federal court may certify a Rule 23(b)(3) damages class containing members without Article III injury. The Court could frame the question to address the certification-stage inquiry without presupposing that every failure of subjective reliance proves absence of injury. This would revisit the issue left unanswered by the dismissal in Labcorp.

Question one, concerning prospective standing for an already-informed consumer, is the strongest alternative or additional question. Question three, concerning the evidentiary adequacy of a full-refund damages model under Comcast, is more likely to be omitted or narrowed than taken as an unrestricted review of California restitution calculations.

**Conditional on a grant, plenary review is 88% likely; a disposition accompanying the cert order is 12% likely.** A narrow summary reversal on the damages-model issue is more plausible than a GVR without an identified intervening authority. If the Court hears the case, the likely controversy will concern whether the certification record establishes common injury and a legally supportable measure of damages, not whether the Court should directly decide the truth of the challenged sourcing claims.

## Writings on denial

Conditional on denial, there is a **17%** chance of a noted dissent or statement respecting denial. The modal prediction remains no writing. If a Justice writes urging resolution of the uninjured-member question, Kavanaugh is the leading candidate in light of his Labcorp dissent; Thomas is another possibility. The `order-writers` entries separately give unconditional probabilities that each Justice authors a writing on the eventual order, not probabilities of joining another Justice or voting to grant.
