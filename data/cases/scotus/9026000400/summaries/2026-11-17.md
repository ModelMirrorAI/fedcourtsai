## In brief

Nestlé asks the Court to review a Ninth Circuit ruling that let a consumer fraud class action over chocolate sourcing labels proceed to trial.

## What happened

Marie Falcone sued Nestlé USA under California consumer-protection laws, claiming that labels describing cocoa as "sustainably" or "responsibly" sourced were misleading because Nestlé could not guarantee its supply chain was free of child labor. A federal district court certified both a class seeking an injunction to change the labels and a class seeking monetary refunds. A divided Ninth Circuit panel affirmed, with one judge dissenting on the damages issue. The full court of appeals later denied rehearing.

## What the Court is being asked

Nestlé raises three questions. First, whether a consumer who already understands the alleged deception can still sue for an order requiring label changes, when she faces no renewed risk of being misled. Second, whether a class seeking money damages can include people who were never actually affected by the label statements. Third, whether a damages model may be used to certify a class when it would award refunds to class members who could not have recovered that amount suing individually, given that the products were not shown to be worthless to everyone who bought them.

## Where it stands

The petition was filed, Falcone waived her right to respond, several outside groups filed briefs supporting Nestlé, and the Court then asked for a response anyway, due November 27, 2026. The case was distributed for the Court's November 13, 2026 conference, and the docket does not yet show any action taken at that conference.

## What each outcome would mean

Nestlé, which lost below, is asking the Court to take up the case and reverse. If the Court grants review, it would hear full arguments and decide the standing and class-certification questions itself, without presuming either side correct. If it denies review, the Ninth Circuit's decision stands, leaving both the injunctive-relief class and the damages class certified, and the case would continue toward trial in the lower courts.
