# Forecast: No. 26-430 (NHBPA v. HISA): cross-petition on HISA rulemaking

**Posture.** This is one of three cross-petitions (26-332 Gulf Coast, 26-335 Texas, 26-430 Horsemen). They answer the main petitions filed by the Authority (26-199) and the FTC/Solicitor General (26-201). Those main petitions ask the Court to review the Fifth Circuit's June 11, 2026 remand holding that HISA's *enforcement* provisions are unconstitutional. All five dockets are distributed for the same conference, December 4, 2026. This docket shows one distribution and no relist.

**Disposition.** The main petitions are near-certain grants. There is a federal statute held unconstitutional, the Solicitor General is the petitioner, and the Sixth Circuit disagrees. What happens to this cross-petition is the open question. I expect it **not** to be granted for plenary review. On the rulemaking question every circuit has upheld the amended statute (the Fifth on rulemaking, the Sixth in *Oklahoma*, 163 F.4th 294, and the Eighth in *Walmsley*), so there is no split. Both respondents oppose it. The likeliest path is that it is **held** pending the decision in 26-199/26-201 and then **denied** once the opinion issues, probably June 2027. A same-day denial on December 4 or a later conference is the main alternative. There is a minority path to a grant: the Court takes the whole statute and consolidates, with the Horsemen arguing that rulemaking and enforcement are intertwined and a State (Texas) cross-petitioning beside them. There is also a smaller hold-then-GVR path (grant, vacate and remand), if the merits opinion states a private-nondelegation test that bears on rulemaking.

**Relists.** Very likely distributed again. The main petitions will probably be relisted at least once before a grant, and a held cross-petition is redistributed after the merits decision.

**CVSG.** None. The United States is already a party and filed a memorandum opposing the cross-petitions.

**Which question.** If granted, the Court would take QP 2 roughly as written: whether rules can take effect without the FTC being able to revise or reject them at the time. It would most likely be consolidated with 26-199/26-201 and argued alongside the enforcement question. The Appointments Clause question in the companion Gulf Coast cross-petition is less likely to be taken, given the standing objection.

**Summary route.** Conditional on any grant, a GVR after a hold is a substantial share (about 40%). Plenary consolidation is the rest.

**Separate writing.** A denial of a held cross-petition rarely draws a writing. If one appears, Justice Gorsuch or Justice Thomas, who have written on private nondelegation, are the likeliest authors.
