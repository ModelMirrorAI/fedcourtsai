No. 26-430 is a conditional cross-petition for a writ of certiorari filed by the National Horsemen's Benevolent and Protective Association and twelve state affiliates ("the Horsemen") seeking review of the Fifth Circuit's judgment on remand in *National Horsemen's Benevolent & Protective Ass'n v. Black*, 178 F.4th 224 (5th Cir. 2026) (provisioned in `record/documents/appendix.txt`, App. 1a–50a). While the Supreme Court is very likely to grant certiorari on the principal petitions filed by the Horseracing Integrity and Safety Authority (No. 26-199) and the Federal Trade Commission (No. 26-201)—or the parallel petition in *Oklahoma v. United States* (No. 25-1325)—to resolve the square Fifth–Sixth Circuit split over the constitutionality of the Horseracing Integrity and Safety Act's ("HISA") **enforcement** provisions, the Court is strongly favored to **deny** the Horsemen's conditional cross-petition in No. 26-430 challenging HISA's **rulemaking** provisions.

On the rulemaking question presented by No. 26-430 (`record/documents/questions-presented.txt`), there is zero circuit split: the Fifth, Sixth, and Eighth Circuits have unanimously held that Congress's December 2022 amendment to 15 U.S.C. § 3053(e)—empowering the FTC to "abrogate, add to, and modify" the Authority's rules—cured the private-nondelegation defect in HISA's rulemaking scheme. Both the Fifth and Sixth Circuits reaffirmed that holding on remand after *FCC v. Consumers' Research*, 606 U.S. 656 (2025). Both the Solicitor General (on behalf of the FTC) and the Authority (`record/documents/brief-in-opposition.txt`) oppose certiorari on all three cross-petitions (Nos. 26-332, 26-335, and 26-430), and the Horsemen do not need a grant of their cross-petition in order to argue about the statutory scope of § 3053(e) as respondents defending the Fifth Circuit's enforcement holding in Nos. 26-199 and 26-201.

In 2020, Congress enacted the Horseracing Integrity and Safety Act, 15 U.S.C. §§ 3051–3060, creating a national regulatory framework for thoroughbred horseracing administered by a private nonprofit corporation, the Horseracing Integrity and Safety Authority ("the Authority"), under the oversight of the Federal Trade Commission ("FTC").

1. ***Horsemen's I* and the 2022 Congressional Amendment:** In *National Horsemen's Benevolent & Protective Ass'n v. Black* (*Horsemen's I*), 53 F.4th 869 (5th Cir. 2022), the Fifth Circuit held that HISA's original rulemaking structure facially violated the private nondelegation doctrine because 15 U.S.C. § 3053(c)(2) required the FTC to approve proposed Authority rules if "consistent with" the Act and applicable rules, without giving the FTC general authority to modify or abrogate the Authority's rules on policy grounds. In December 2022, Congress amended 15 U.S.C. § 3053(e) (modeled on the Maloney Act's SEC–FINRA oversight provision, 15 U.S.C. § 78s(c)) to authorize the FTC, by rule under 5 U.S.C. § 553, to "abrogate, add to, and modify the rules of the Authority promulgated in accordance with this chapter as the Commission finds necessary or appropriate to ensure the fair administration of the Authority, to conform the rules of the Authority to requirements of this chapter and applicable rules approved by the Commission, or otherwise in furtherance of the purposes of this chapter." Consolidated Appropriations Act, 2023, Pub. L. No. 117-328, div. O, tit. VII, § 701, 136 Stat. 4459, 5231–32 (2022).
2. ***Horsemen's II* and the *Consumers' Research* GVR:** Following a bench trial on remand, the Northern District of Texas rejected all facial and as-applied constitutional challenges to the amended Act. *Nat'l Horsemen's Benevolent & Protective Ass'n v. Black* (*Black II*), 672 F. Supp. 3d 220 (N.D. Tex. 2023) (App. 51a–114a). On appeal, in *National Horsemen's Benevolent & Protective Ass'n v. Black* (*Horsemen's II*), 107 F.4th 415 (5th Cir. 2024), the Fifth Circuit:
   - **Affirmed** that the 2022 amendment to § 3053(e) cured the private nondelegation defect in HISA's **rulemaking** provisions, joining the Sixth Circuit in *Oklahoma v. United States* (*Oklahoma I*), 62 F.4th 221, 229–31 (6th Cir. 2023);
   - **Affirmed** the rejection of the Horsemen's Fifth Amendment due process challenge and Gulf Coast Racing LLC's Appointments Clause challenge (under *Lebron v. National Railroad Passenger Corp.*, 513 U.S. 374 (1995)) and Tenth Amendment anti-commandeering challenge (for lack of standing); and
   - **Reversed** as to HISA's **enforcement** provisions, holding them facially unconstitutional under the private nondelegation doctrine because the Authority and its anti-doping contractor (HIWU) may investigate, issue subpoenas, search premises, levy sanctions, and bring civil actions for injunctions without front-end FTC control.
   On October 28, 2024, the Supreme Court stayed the Fifth Circuit's mandate pending certiorari (No. 24A287). On June 30, 2025, following its decision in *FCC v. Consumers' Research*, 606 U.S. 656 (2025), the Supreme Court granted, vacated, and remanded the first round of HISA petitions and cross-petitions (Nos. 24-429, 24-433, 24-465, 24-472, and 24-489; 145 S. Ct. 2837 (2025), App. 115a–118a), as well as the Sixth Circuit's decision in *Oklahoma* (145 S. Ct. 2836 (2025)) and the Eighth Circuit's decision upholding HISA in *Walmsley v. FTC*, 117 F.4th 1032 (8th Cir. 2024), *vacated and remanded*, 145 S. Ct. 2870 (2025).
3. **Decisions on Remand After *Consumers' Research*:**
   - On December 17, 2025, the Sixth Circuit issued *Oklahoma v. United States* (*Oklahoma II*), 163 F.4th 294 (6th Cir. 2025), again upholding both HISA's rulemaking and enforcement provisions under *Consumers' Research*. Oklahoma petitioned for certiorari solely on the enforcement question (No. 25-1325, filed May 29, 2026).
   - On June 11, 2026, the Fifth Circuit issued the decision below (*Horsemen's III*, 178 F.4th 224; Pet. App. 1a–50a), reissuing its *Horsemen's II* opinion and adding Part III(B)(6) (Pet. App. 34a–40a) explaining why *Consumers' Research* neither altered the private nondelegation standard nor changed the Fifth Circuit's holdings upholding HISA's rulemaking provisions while invalidating its enforcement provisions. On July 14, 2026, the Fifth Circuit stayed its mandate pending certiorari (Pet. App. 119a–120a).
4. **The Current Cluster of Petitions (Nos. 26-199, 26-201, 26-332, 26-335, 26-430):**
   - On August 17, 2026, the Authority (No. 26-199) and the FTC represented by the Solicitor General (No. 26-201) petitioned for certiorari on the enforcement question.
   - In September 2026, the three sets of plaintiffs filed cross-petitions: Gulf Coast Racing LLC on the Appointments Clause (No. 26-332, filed Sept. 11, 2026), Texas on the rulemaking provisions (No. 26-335, filed Sept. 14, 2026), and the Horsemen on the rulemaking provisions (No. 26-430, filed Sept. 9, 2026 and docketed Sept. 30, 2026 as a conditional cross-petition).
   - Both the Authority (Oct. 13, 2026 consolidated BIO, `record/documents/brief-in-opposition.txt`) and the FTC (Oct. 26, 2026 BIO) opposed all three cross-petitions, and the Horsemen filed their reply brief on October 28, 2026 (`record/documents/cert-reply.txt`). On November 4, 2026, No. 26-430 was distributed for the Conference of November 20, 2026 (`distribution_count: 1`).

### Signals Moving `probability` from the Anchor (`0.0515` -> `0.12`)

1. **Upward Signal — Companion Attachment to Principal Petitions Certain to Receive Plenary Review (+0.15 above baseline):**
   Unlike a standalone paid petition, No. 26-430 arises from the same Fifth Circuit judgment as two principal petitions—No. 26-201 (filed by the Solicitor General on behalf of the FTC) and No. 26-199 (filed by the Authority)—that challenge the Fifth Circuit's facial invalidation of a federal statute's enforcement scheme on which the Fifth and Sixth Circuits openly split. All parties agree certiorari should be granted on the enforcement question. Moreover, in June 2025, when the Court GVR'd the main petitions (Nos. 24-429, 24-433) in light of *FCC v. Consumers' Research*, 606 U.S. 656 (2025), it also GVR'd the accompanying cross-petitions (Nos. 24-465, 24-472, 24-489) as part of the package. There is a genuine tail possibility that four Justices (potentially the three dissenters in *Consumers' Research*—Justices Gorsuch, Thomas, and Alito—plus a fourth vote interested in evaluating HISA's statutory structure as a whole) vote to grant the rulemaking cross-petition(s) alongside the enforcement petitions, or that a merits decision in the enforcement case triggers a post-hold GVR.

2. **Strong Downward Signal — Complete Circuit Consensus on the Rulemaking Question (-0.05):**
   Every circuit court and district court to address HISA's rulemaking provisions since the December 2022 amendment to 15 U.S.C. § 3053(e) has held them constitutional:
   - Fifth Circuit: *Horsemen's II*, 107 F.4th at 423–26; *Horsemen's III*, 178 F.4th at 231–33 (Pet. App. 10a–15a, 39a n.23);
   - Sixth Circuit: *Oklahoma I*, 62 F.4th at 229–31; *Oklahoma II*, 163 F.4th at 308;
   - Eighth Circuit: *Walmsley v. FTC*, 117 F.4th 1032, 1038–39 (8th Cir. 2024) (and Judge Gruender's partial dissent at 1041 agreed on rulemaking).
   Even Oklahoma dropped its rulemaking challenge after *Oklahoma I* and did not renew it in No. 25-1325. The Supreme Court rarely expands a grant to encompass a second constitutional question on which the lower courts are unanimous.

3. **Downward Signal — Post-*Consumers' Research* Posture Eliminates the 2025 GVR Path and Weakens the Plenary Grant Case (-0.02):**
   In June 2025, the cross-petitions received GVRs only because the Court was remanding the entire Fifth Circuit judgment for reconsideration in light of *FCC v. Consumers' Research*, 606 U.S. 656 (2025). In *Consumers' Research*, the Court reaffirmed *Sunshine Anthracite Coal Co. v. Adkins*, 310 U.S. 381 (1940), holding that private entities may assist in developing regulatory proposals so long as they function subordinately to a government agency that retains decision-making authority (606 U.S. at 692–95). Both the Fifth and Sixth Circuits have now applied *Consumers' Research* on remand and held that it reinforces the constitutionality of HISA's rulemaking framework.

4. **Downward Signal — Opposition of Both the Solicitor General and the Authority, Facial-Challenge Vehicle Weakness, and Cross-Petition Redundancy (-0.015):**
   Both the FTC (Solicitor General) and the Authority oppose the cross-petitions (`record/documents/brief-in-opposition.txt`, at 11–18). As the Fifth Circuit explained (Pet. App. 14a), the Horsemen's sole remaining objection—that a proposed Authority rule approved under § 3053(c)(2) "consistency" review might briefly take effect before the FTC completes a § 3053(e) notice-and-comment rulemaking to modify it—rests on a hypothetical timing gap that has never occurred in practice and cannot satisfy the *United States v. Salerno*, 481 U.S. 739, 745 (1987), standard for a facial challenge. Furthermore, as respondents in Nos. 26-199 and 26-201, the Horsemen can already argue in their merits brief on the enforcement question that § 3053(e) does not enable the FTC to supervise enforcement actions; they do not need a grant of their cross-petition unless the Court wishes to reach out and invalidate HISA's post-2022 rulemaking framework as well. Finally, even if the Court were inclined to grant a cross-petition on rulemaking, Texas's separate cross-petition (No. 26-335) presents a broader framing of the rulemaking issue, creating potential redundancy between Nos. 26-335 and 26-430.
