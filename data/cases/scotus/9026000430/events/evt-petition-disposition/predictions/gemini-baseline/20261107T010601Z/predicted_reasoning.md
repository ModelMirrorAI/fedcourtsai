## 3. Disposition and Question Presented Scope (`disposition`: `0.12`)

The Court will deny the conditional cross-petition in No. 26-430 (either at the time it grants certiorari on the enforcement question in Nos. 26-199 / 26-201, or after holding No. 26-430 pending the merits of the enforcement case). Every court of appeals to consider the December 2022 amendment to 15 U.S.C. § 3053(e)—the Fifth, Sixth, and Eighth Circuits—has held that empowering the FTC to "abrogate, add to, and modify" the Authority's rules cured the private nondelegation defect in HISA's rulemaking provisions, and both the Fifth and Sixth Circuits reaffirmed that conclusion on remand after *FCC v. Consumers' Research*, 606 U.S. 656 (2025).

If the Court were to grant No. 26-430, it would take the single question presented as written—whether HISA's delegation of rulemaking power to a private corporation violates the Constitution because the corporation's rules can take effect under 15 U.S.C. § 3053(c)(2) consistency review before the FTC completes a separate § 3053(e) rulemaking to revise or reject them—and consolidate it for plenary briefing and argument with the enforcement question in Nos. 26-199 and 26-201. If granted and reached on the merits, the Court would likely affirm the Fifth Circuit's rulemaking holding under *Sunshine Anthracite Coal Co. v. Adkins*, 310 U.S. 381 (1940), and *FCC v. Consumers' Research*, 606 U.S. 656 (2025), concluding that the FTC's pre-effective-date approval authority under § 3053(c)(2) combined with its plenary power to abrogate, add to, and modify rules under § 3053(e) establishes the requisite agency subordination in a facial challenge under *United States v. Salerno*, 481 U.S. 739 (1987).
