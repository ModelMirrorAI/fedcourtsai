## In brief

Horse industry groups ask the Court to review whether a federal law's scheme letting a private authority draft binding horseracing rules improperly delegates lawmaking power.

## What happened

Congress created the Horseracing Integrity and Safety Authority, a private nonprofit, to write and enforce nationwide horseracing rules subject to Federal Trade Commission oversight. The National Horsemen's Benevolent and Protective Association and others sued, arguing this arrangement unconstitutionally hands government power to a private entity. After earlier rulings and a congressional amendment giving the FTC more rulemaking power, the Fifth Circuit held that the Authority's rulemaking power was constitutional but its enforcement powers were not. The horsemen's groups then asked the Supreme Court to also review the rulemaking question.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked whether a law violates the rule against delegating government power to private parties when it lets a private organization's proposed rules take legal effect before the responsible federal agency can revise or reject them, even though the agency can later modify or cancel those rules through a separate process.

## Where it stands

The petition has been fully briefed, including a brief in opposition and a reply, and was distributed for the Court's conference on December 4, 2026. The docket shows no action since that distribution.

## What each outcome would mean

The horsemen's groups are asking the Court to take up this rulemaking question, which the Fifth Circuit decided against them. If the Court grants review, it would hear the case and decide whether the FTC's after-the-fact power to change the Authority's rules is enough, with no outcome assumed in advance. If the Court denies review, the Fifth Circuit's ruling that the rulemaking scheme is constitutional stays in place, leaving the horsemen's groups without further recourse on this particular question while a related dispute over the Authority's enforcement powers proceeds separately.
