## In brief

Former hospital employees fired for refusing COVID-19 vaccination ask the Court to revive their lawsuit against the hospital and Oregon officials.

## What happened

Sara Boysen and other former PeaceHealth employees were terminated in 2021 after refusing to be vaccinated against COVID-19 under policies issued by PeaceHealth and by Oregon's governor and health authority director. They sued, arguing the vaccine was really an unapproved "investigational drug" and that requiring it, without allowing them to refuse without penalty, violated federal statutes, an international treaty, and the Constitution. The district court dismissed all claims, and the Ninth Circuit affirmed, relying on its recent decision in a similar case involving Washington State and the same hospital system, which held that none of the sources of law the employees cited created rights enforceable in this kind of lawsuit, and that a longstanding Supreme Court vaccine case forecloses the due-process claim.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked whether requiring healthcare workers to be vaccinated or get an exemption, on pain of losing their jobs, violates their constitutional right to due process, given that the vaccine was initially authorized only for emergency use and federal rules on such authorizations call for voluntary acceptance.

## Where it stands

The petition has been briefed and distributed for conference; the respondents oppose review, and the petitioners have filed a reply. The docket shows no decision yet on whether the Court will hear the case.

## What each outcome would mean

Boysen and the other former employees are asking the Court to take up their case. If the Court grants review, it would hear full arguments and decide the legal question itself; a grant would not by itself mean either side wins. If the Court denies review, the Ninth Circuit's decision affirming dismissal of the employees' claims stays in place, and PeaceHealth and the Oregon officials keep the win they already have; a denial would not mean the Supreme Court agrees the lower court was right, only that its ruling stands.
