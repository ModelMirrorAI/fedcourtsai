## What happened

Sara Boysen and several other former PeaceHealth employees were healthcare workers in Oregon who refused to get vaccinated against COVID-19 or seek an exemption, as required by both a state health authority policy and PeaceHealth's own employment policy. They were fired and later sued PeaceHealth, several of its executives, and two Oregon officials, arguing the vaccine requirement violated their constitutional rights. A federal district court dismissed the case, and the Ninth Circuit Court of Appeals affirmed in an unpublished ruling, relying on an earlier Ninth Circuit case involving similar claims against other PeaceHealth workers. The Ninth Circuit did not decide whether PeaceHealth counted as a government actor or whether officials were shielded by legal immunity.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked whether requiring healthcare workers to be vaccinated, or get an exemption, as a condition of keeping their professional license or job violates a constitutional right to bodily autonomy — even though a century-old Supreme Court case allows vaccine mandates that serve public health. The petitioners argue the vaccine here should be treated as an unapproved experimental drug, subject to stricter rules requiring voluntary consent, and that the appeals court wrongly decided this issue without both sides fully arguing it first. The respondents counter that the workers pleaded facts showing the vaccine was fully approved, not experimental, and that no lower courts disagree on this kind of claim.

## Where it stands

The petition for cert, asking the Court to take up the case, has been fully briefed, including responses from both sets of respondents and a reply from the petitioners. It has been distributed for the Court's conference on October 9, 2026, where the Justices will decide whether to grant or deny review.
