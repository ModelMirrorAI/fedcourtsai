## In brief

Former hospital employees fired for refusing COVID-19 vaccination ask the Court to revive their lawsuit against Oregon officials and their employer.

## What happened

Sara Boysen and other former PeaceHealth employees were fired after refusing to be vaccinated against COVID-19 or seek an exemption, under policies from both PeaceHealth and Oregon health officials. They sued, arguing the requirement violated their constitutional rights and various federal laws protecting informed consent to experimental drugs. A federal district court dismissed the case, and the Ninth Circuit affirmed in an unpublished decision, relying on its own recent ruling in a similar case involving different plaintiffs and officials, holding that none of the laws Boysen cited created enforceable rights.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked whether requiring healthcare workers to accept an unapproved or experimental drug as a condition of keeping their professional license or job, while also cutting off their ability to sue over resulting harm, violates constitutional guarantees of fair treatment by the government.

## Where it stands

The petition has been fully briefed, including opposition briefs from both sets of respondents and a reply from the petitioners, and was distributed for the Court's conference on October 9, 2026.

## What each outcome would mean

Boysen and the other former employees are asking the Court to take up the case. If the Court grants review, it will hear full arguments and decide the legal question itself, without yet deciding who wins. If the Court denies review, the Ninth Circuit's decision affirming dismissal of the lawsuit stands, leaving PeaceHealth and the Oregon officials as the prevailing parties and ending the case in their favor.
