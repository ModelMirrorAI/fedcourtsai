## In brief

Former hospital employees fired for refusing COVID-19 vaccination asked the Court to revive their lawsuit against the hospital and Oregon officials; the Court denied review.

## What happened

Sara Boysen and other former PeaceHealth employees were fired after refusing to be vaccinated against COVID-19 under PeaceHealth's policy and a related Oregon health authority mandate. They sued PeaceHealth, its executives, and Oregon's former governor and health authority director, arguing the vaccine was an unapproved "investigational drug" and that requiring it violated various federal statutes, an international treaty, contract terms, and the Constitution. The district court dismissed the case, and the Ninth Circuit affirmed, relying on its recent decision in a similar case involving the same hospital, which held that none of the cited laws created rights enforceable in such a lawsuit and that a Supreme Court precedent upholding vaccine mandates foreclosed their constitutional claims.

## What the Court is being asked

The petitioners asked whether the Constitution's guarantee of fair treatment by the government bars a state from conditioning a person's ability to keep working in a licensed profession on accepting an experimental drug and giving up the right to sue over any resulting harm.

## Where it stands

The Court denied the petition for review on October 13, 2026, after considering it at conference.

## What each outcome would mean

Boysen and the other former employees petitioned the Court to hear their case. By denying review, the Court leaves the Ninth Circuit's ruling in place, so the dismissal of their lawsuit against PeaceHealth and the Oregon officials stands. The denial is not a ruling that the lower court's reasoning was correct; it simply means the Ninth Circuit's decision is final and the case will not be reheard.
