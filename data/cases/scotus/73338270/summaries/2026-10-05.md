## In brief

Former hospital employees ask the Court to revive their lawsuit claiming a state and a private healthcare system wrongly fired them for refusing a COVID-19 vaccine.

## What happened

Sara Boysen and other former PeaceHealth employees were fired after refusing to be vaccinated against COVID-19 or seek an exemption, under policies adopted by PeaceHealth and by Oregon state officials. They sued under federal civil rights law, arguing their firing violated due process and various federal statutes and international agreements protecting informed consent to investigational drugs. A federal district court dismissed the suit, and the Ninth Circuit Court of Appeals affirmed, relying on its own recent decision in a similar case involving the same hospital system and Washington officials, which it found controlled the outcome.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked whether firing licensed healthcare workers for refusing an investigational drug, after conditioning their employment on getting the shot or waiving their ability to sue over resulting harm, violates their constitutional right to due process. The petitioners also argue the appeals court wrongly decided the case on legal grounds the parties themselves had not raised or argued.

## Where it stands

The petition has been fully briefed, including opposition briefs from both sets of respondents and a reply from the petitioners, along with a supporting brief from an outside group. The case was distributed for the Court's October 9, 2026 conference, where the justices will decide whether to take it up; the docket shows no action yet.

## What each outcome would mean

Boysen and the other petitioners are asking the Court to hear the case. If the Court grants review, it would hear full arguments and decide the due process question itself, without presuming either side correct. If the Court denies review, the Ninth Circuit's decision affirming dismissal of the lawsuit stands, leaving the firings and the lower court rulings against the petitioners in place. A denial would not mean the Court agrees the lower court was right, only that the case ends there.
