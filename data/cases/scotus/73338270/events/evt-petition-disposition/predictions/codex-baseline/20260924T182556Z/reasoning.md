## Why below the anchor

The petition's QP combines professional employment, refusal of allegedly investigational drugs, and the claimed surrender of judicial remedies for injuries. Its strongest presentation is that the Ninth Circuit's treatment of Jacobson and federal informed-consent protections warrants intervention even without a circuit conflict. The petition also presses party-presentation, unconstitutional-conditions, and federal supremacy theories, and proposes consolidated consideration with related petitions. Those are substantial asserted stakes, not evidence that its characterization of the law or medical products is correct. See petition pp. 7–8, 25–33 and the provisioned QP.

The oppositions provide several concrete reasons to reduce the anchor substantially:

- **Related review has already been declined.** The PeaceHealth opposition, pp. 9–11, reports the June 1, 2026 denial in Curtis v. Inslee, No. 25-1119, the published decision applied below and involving similar claims, the same employer, and the same petitioners' counsel. Its later discussion also reports the May 18, 2026 denial in Health Freedom Defense Fund v. Carvalho. These pre-snapshot facts weaken the petition's May consolidation strategy. I rely on the provisioned briefs' reports; I did not independently retrieve these dockets. A related denial is not a merits endorsement, but it is useful forecasting evidence.
- **No developed split shown.** The petition principally claims conflict with this Court's precedents and federal legislation, not a square opposing appellate holding on this QP. Both respondent groups argue there is no circuit split. I have not independently audited all appellate authority, so this is a conclusion about the competing presentations, not a comprehensive census.
- **Vehicle obstacles are substantial.** The state opposition, pp. 9–11, argues that the QP's investigational-drug premise does not match the decision below and identifies an alternative qualified-immunity ruling. The PeaceHealth opposition, pp. 1–2 and 12–16, stresses unresolved state-action and immunity issues and the distinction between its role as employer and its role as vaccine provider. Petitioners themselves acknowledge that the panel left state action and immunity undecided (petition pp. 7–8). Those obstacles can prevent resolution of the broad question even if a Justice is sympathetic to it.
- **The decision below is unpublished.** The parties agree on that feature. Alongside the absence of a demonstrated split and the related denials, it makes this look more like a request to correct an application of existing circuit authority than a clean national rule-setting vehicle.

These considerations are correlated, so I do not multiply separate numerical penalties. Four percent is a judgmental adjustment from 17.2%, retaining meaningful residual probability for the response request, amicus interest, or a narrower procedural concern. It is not a fitted estimate. I score the stakes at 0.55 independently of grant likelihood.
