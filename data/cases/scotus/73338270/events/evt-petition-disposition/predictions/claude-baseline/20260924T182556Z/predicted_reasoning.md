# Forecast: Boysen v. PeaceHealth, No. 25-1280 (evt-petition-disposition)

**Disposition.** The Court will deny the petition, most likely on the order
list following the October 9, 2026 conference (October 13), or one conference
later if it is relisted. P(any grant) = 0.06.

**Relists.** The docket shows two distributions: for the June 18, 2026
conference (after which the Court called for a response) and for the October 9,
2026 long conference (after the two briefs in opposition and the reply). I put
the chance of at least one further distribution at 0.30. The modal path is a
straight denial on the first post-conference order list. The minority path is a
single relist while a Justice who prompted the call for a response decides
whether to write separately; a second or third relist is unlikely because
nothing here needs a vote count to be worked out.

**CVSG.** No call for the views of the Solicitor General (0.02). No federal
party is involved, the mandates were state and private-employer measures that
have since been repealed, and the suit seeks only damages. The petition's
theory that the FDCA and PREP Act preempt state mandates of EUA products is
one the federal government has no docket-level stake in here.

**Which question, if any.** If the Court took the case it would not take the
QP as written. The question would have to be reformulated to the one the lower
courts actually decided: whether Jacobson forecloses a substantive due process
challenge to a State's COVID-19 vaccination condition on licensed healthcare
work. The "investigational drug" and "waiver of judicial remedies" framing was
rejected on the pleadings and was not the ground of the Ninth Circuit's
memorandum disposition.

**Summary disposition.** Conditional on a grant, a summary route (GVR or per
curiam) is unlikely (0.10). There is no intervening decision of this Court to
GVR in light of, and the merits are not a candidate for summary reversal given
a uniform body of circuit authority applying Jacobson.

**Dissent or statement respecting denial.** Conditional on denial, I put the
chance that some Justice notes a dissent from or statement respecting the
denial at 0.15. The Court called for a response after both respondents waived,
which signals at least one chambers' interest, and the amicus brief presses a
"Jacobson has been over-read" argument aimed at Justices who have previously
questioned reliance on Jacobson. But the Court denied the published decision
this case rests on (Curtis v. Inslee, No. 25-1119) on June 1, 2026 without a
noted dissent, along with two materially similar petitions the same day, and
denied Health Freedom Defense Fund v. Carvalho (No. 25-765) on May 18, 2026.
A separate writing on a weaker, unpublished vehicle for the same issue is
possible but not the expectation.

**If granted (conditional only).** The likely ground would be the scope of
Jacobson under Glucksberg's history-and-tradition test, and the likely result
would still be affirmance or a remand on the antecedent qualified-immunity and
state-action questions the Ninth Circuit did not reach. None of that is
forecast here as an unconditional claim.
