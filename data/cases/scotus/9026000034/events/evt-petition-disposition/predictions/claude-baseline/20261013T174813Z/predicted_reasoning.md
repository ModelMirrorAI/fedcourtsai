# Forecast: Hanaway v. Sanderson, No. 26-34 (cert stage)

**Where the docket stands.** The petition has been distributed twice: first for the
October 9, 2026 conference, then again on October 13 for the October 16 conference.
That second distribution came after a conference at which the petition was listed,
so it is a relist. There is no CVSG.

**Disposition.** I expect a **denial**, with about a one-in-four chance of a plenary
grant. The relist shows that at least one Justice is looking closely, either to grant
or to write about a denial. The petition is weak on the usual criteria, though.
The only court of appeals to rule on a materially identical Halloween-sign law, the
Eleventh Circuit in *McClendon v. Long*, agrees with the Eighth Circuit. The asserted
conflict with *United States v. Arnold* (5th Cir.) concerns SORNA registration with
the government, not a sign aimed at the public from a person's home. The
16-State amicus brief and a Missouri petitioner raise the profile, but they do not
create a split.

**Further relists.** About an even chance that the petition is distributed at least
once more. A denial with a written dissent usually takes several more relists while
the writing circulates. A grant could come at the October 16 conference itself or
after one more relist. A clean denial on October 16 would end the distributions.

**CVSG.** Not expected (~3%). The respondent is a private individual, the
challenged law is a state statute, and a CVSG after a relist would be unusual.

**If granted.** The Court would take the QP as written, possibly reframed as whether
a factually accurate, government-mandated disclosure that is incidental to a conduct
regulation (*FAIR*) triggers strict scrutiny under *Wooley*/*Barnette*. That would be
plenary review. A summary reversal is unlikely (~10% of grants) because the
district court's alternative holding that the sign also fails lesser scrutiny
complicates a per curiam. No GVR is in prospect, since no intervening decision bears
on the question.

**Dissent from denial.** If the petition is denied, I put a separate writing at
roughly 40%, given the relist. The likeliest author is Justice Thomas or Justice
Alito, perhaps joined by Justice Gorsuch, arguing that factual disclosures tied to
conduct regulation are not compelled speech.
