# Forecast

The Court will most likely deny Missouri's petition without an explanatory opinion. I assign a 24% probability to any grant, including a summary reversal or GVR. Denial would leave the compelled-speech ruling below undisturbed, not establish that the Supreme Court endorses its reasoning.

The provisioned docket contains **two distributions**, September 23 for the October 9, 2026 conference and October 13 for the October 16 conference. Thus one apparent relist has already occurred. From that state, I assign **40%** to at least one further distribution. The modal path is no additional distribution beyond the two already recorded; if it is distributed again, one or two additional conferences are more likely than an extended hold. I expect disposition in the next several weeks, most likely in October or November 2026. This timing is a forecast, not an observed schedule for the decision.

There is no CVSG in the baseline. I assign **3%** to a new invitation for the Solicitor General's views. The Court is more likely to decide without one: the immediate dispute concerns Missouri's notice requirement, while the claimed implications for federal registration law are indirect. In the less likely CVSG path, an invitation would likely follow a fall conference and materially delay disposition.

If review is granted, the Court will most likely take the petition's single question substantially as framed, focused on whether this home-posted factual notice is compelled speech or speech incidental to valid conduct regulation. I do not expect the Court to expand the case into a general decision about the validity of sex-offender registration. Conditional on a grant, I assign **12%** to disposition in the cert order itself and **88%** to plenary review. No identified intervening decision supplies a straightforward GVR trigger; resolving the disputed relationship between the sign mandate and the conduct restrictions would more naturally warrant briefing and argument.

Conditional on denial, I assign **18%** to a noted dissent or statement respecting denial, and **82%** to neither. No particular Justice's participation is predicted. The State's effort to characterize the case as a broader conflict over factual disclosures could attract a separate writing, but the absence of a square conflict over equivalent home-sign requirements favors an ordinary denial.
