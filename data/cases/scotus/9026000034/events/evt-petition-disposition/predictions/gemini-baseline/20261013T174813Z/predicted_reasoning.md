Distributed twice so far (one relist); expect one further relist before a decision on the petition. The QP asks whether Missouri's Halloween sign-posting requirement for convicted sex offenders unconstitutionally compels speech. The Eighth Circuit struck down the state law on First Amendment grounds, giving this petition high salience as a state defense of its statute. The split claimed with the Fifth Circuit is arguably distinguishable, but the invalidation of a state law alone often draws the Court's interest.

A CVSG is unlikely because the case involves a state statute and state interests, rather than a federal agency or federal statutory scheme. If granted, the Court would likely take the QP as written to address the compelled speech doctrine in the context of sex offender restrictions. A summary disposition route (like a GVR) is unlikely because there is no obvious intervening precedent to remand on; the issue appears cleanly presented for plenary review. Should the Court deny the petition, there is a modest chance of a dissent from denial (e.g., from Justices Thomas or Alito) given the invalidation of a state law and the broader context of state police powers.
