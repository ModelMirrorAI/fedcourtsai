## In brief

Missouri asks the Court to revive its Halloween sign-posting requirement for registered sex offenders after lower courts struck it down as compelled speech.

## What happened

Missouri law bars registered sex offenders from participating in Halloween, including requiring them to post a sign reading "No candy or treats at this residence." Thomas Sanderson, convicted of a sex offense years before the law passed, had been told by police the restrictions did not apply to him and continued hosting Halloween displays. After he was prosecuted, he sued, challenging only the sign requirement under the First Amendment. Following a trial, the district court found Missouri had not shown the sign added any protective value beyond the law's other restrictions, and struck it down as unconstitutional compelled speech. The Eighth Circuit affirmed, though it sent the case back to narrow the scope of the injunction.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked whether a state can require people convicted of sex offenses to post a specific sign at their homes on Halloween, given trial evidence that the requirement does not clearly add to protecting children or help enforce the law's other restrictions.

## Where it stands

The petition has been fully briefed, including a brief in opposition, amicus brief, and reply. It was distributed for conference on October 9, 2026, and again for the conference of October 16, 2026, with no action yet recorded since that later conference.

## What each outcome would mean

Missouri's attorney general and a local police chief, as petitioners, are asking the Court to take up the case. If the Court grants review, it would hear full arguments and decide whether the sign requirement is constitutional, deciding nothing yet about who wins. If it denies review, the Eighth Circuit's ruling striking down the sign mandate as unconstitutional compelled speech, a win for Sanderson, would remain in effect.
