## In brief

The heirs of the author of a 1983 magazine article ask the Court to revive their copyright suit against Paramount over Top Gun: Maverick.

## What happened

Shosh and Yuval Yonay inherited the copyright to "Top Guns," a 1983 magazine article about Navy fighter pilots that inspired the 1986 film Top Gun. After reclaiming the rights from Paramount, they sued the studio, arguing its 2022 sequel, Top Gun: Maverick, infringed their copyright and that Paramount breached a contract requiring it to credit the author. The district court granted Paramount summary judgment, finding the works not substantially similar once unprotected facts and ideas were set aside. The Ninth Circuit affirmed, applying its two-step test for comparing works, and denied rehearing.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked whether, in deciding that two creative works are not similar enough to support a copyright claim, a court may rule for the side accused of copying by comparing the works piece by piece without also asking how an ordinary viewer or reader would perceive the works as a whole.

## Where it stands

Paramount waived its right to respond, but the Court later requested a response, which Paramount filed. The petition was distributed for the Court's conference of September 28, 2026, and the docket shows no action on it since.

## What each outcome would mean

The Yonays, who lost in the lower courts, are asking the Court to take up their case. If the Court grants review, it will hear the case and decide the legal question itself, without yet deciding who wins. If the Court denies review, the Ninth Circuit's decision stands, leaving Paramount's win in place and the Yonays' lawsuit over.
