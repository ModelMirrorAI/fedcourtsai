## In brief

The heirs of a magazine writer ask the Court to revive their copyright suit claiming Paramount's Top Gun: Maverick copied his 1983 article.

## What happened

Ehud Yonay wrote "Top Guns," a 1983 magazine article about Navy fighter pilots, and sold Paramount the film rights, which became the 1986 movie Top Gun. After Yonay died, his widow and son, Shosh and Yuval Yonay, inherited the copyright and terminated Paramount's rights. When Paramount released Top Gun: Maverick in 2022 without crediting or paying them, they sued for copyright infringement and breach of contract. The district court granted Paramount summary judgment, finding the works not "substantially similar," and the Ninth Circuit affirmed. Paramount argues the article's expression does not appear in the film, while the Yonays argue the film copied its selection and arrangement of details.

## What the Court is being asked

The Yonays argue that some appeals courts decide copyright cases by comparing two works as a whole, through the eyes of an ordinary viewer, while the court here instead broke the works into separate pieces like plot, characters, and dialogue, and compared each piece on its own before ever asking how an ordinary viewer would see the works together. They ask the Court to decide whether a court may rule for the side accused of copying this way, without ever considering the works side by side as wholes. Paramount answers that the different courts' approaches amount to the same analysis in substance, so there is no real conflict to resolve, and that this case would be a poor one to use for deciding the question regardless.

## Where it stands

The petition was fully briefed as of October 16, 2026, when Paramount filed its brief opposing review, and it awaits the Court's decision on whether to hear the case.

## What each outcome would mean

The Yonays, who lost in the lower courts, are asking the Court to take up the case. If the Court grants review, it will hear the case in full and decide the legal question; granting review does not decide who is right. If the Court denies review, the Ninth Circuit's decision stands, leaving Paramount's win in place and the Yonays' lawsuit over.
