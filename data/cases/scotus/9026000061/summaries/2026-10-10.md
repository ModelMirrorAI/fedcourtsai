## In brief

The heirs of a magazine writer ask the Court to revive their copyright suit claiming Paramount's "Top Gun: Maverick" copied their father's 1983 story.

## What happened

Shosh and Yuval Yonay, heirs of writer Ehud Yonay, sued Paramount Pictures, claiming "Top Gun: Maverick" copied protected elements of his 1983 magazine story "Top Guns," which Paramount had licensed for the original "Top Gun" film. The district court granted summary judgment for Paramount, finding the works not "substantially similar" under the Ninth Circuit's test, which compares individual story elements like plot and characters separately before any broader comparison. The Ninth Circuit affirmed and denied rehearing. The Yonays now ask the Supreme Court to review that ruling.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked whether judges may rule, on their own, that two creative works are not similar enough to count as copying—without first considering how an ordinary viewer or reader would perceive the works as a whole. The petition says some appeals courts compare works piece by piece before any such overall comparison, while others compare the works' overall look and feel from the start, and that this split can determine the outcome of infringement cases.

## Where it stands

Paramount waived its right to respond, but the Court later requested a response, now due October 16, 2026, after amicus briefs supporting the Yonays were filed. The petition was distributed for the Court's September 28, 2026 conference, and the docket shows no action on it since.

## What each outcome would mean

The Yonays, as petitioners, are asking the Court to take up the case. If the Court grants review, it will hear full arguments and decide which legal test applies, without yet deciding who wins; the case would then continue under whichever standard it adopts. If the Court denies review, the Ninth Circuit's ruling for Paramount stands, and the Yonays' lawsuit remains dismissed. A denial would not mean the Court agrees the lower court was right—only that the decision stays in place.
