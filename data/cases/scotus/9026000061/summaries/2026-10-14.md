## In brief

The heirs of a magazine writer ask the Court to revive their copyright suit claiming Paramount's "Top Gun: Maverick" copied their father's article.

## What happened

Shosh and Yuval Yonay inherited the copyright to a 1983 magazine article about Navy fighter pilots that inspired the original "Top Gun" film. After they ended Paramount's rights to the article, Paramount released "Top Gun: Maverick" without compensating or crediting them. They sued for copyright infringement and breach of contract. The district court granted summary judgment to Paramount, finding the works not "substantially similar," and excluded the Yonays' literary expert. The Ninth Circuit affirmed, and later denied rehearing.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked whether judges may rule, on their own, that two creative works are not similar enough to support a copyright claim by comparing the works piece by piece—plot, characters, dialogue, and so on, separately—rather than asking how an ordinary viewer or reader would perceive the works as a whole. The petition says appeals courts are split on which method applies.

## Where it stands

The petition was distributed for the Court's September 28, 2026 conference; Paramount initially waived its right to respond but the Court later requested a response, which is now due October 16, 2026 after an extension. The docket does not show any further action since then.

## What each outcome would mean

The Yonays, as petitioners, are asking the Court to take up the case. If the Court grants review, it would hear the case and decide which legal test applies to comparing the works, without yet deciding who wins. If it denies review, the Ninth Circuit's decision stands, leaving in place the ruling that Paramount is entitled to summary judgment and the Yonays' claims fail.
