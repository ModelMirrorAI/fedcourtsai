## In brief

The heirs of a magazine writer ask the Court to revive their copyright suit claiming Paramount's Top Gun: Maverick copied his 1983 article about Navy fighter pilots.

## What happened

Shosh and Yuval Yonay, heirs of writer Ehud Yonay, sued Paramount Pictures, arguing that Top Gun: Maverick infringed the copyright in Yonay's magazine article "Top Guns" and that Paramount breached a related contract by not crediting him. A federal district court granted Paramount summary judgment, finding the works not "substantially similar," and the Ninth Circuit Court of Appeals affirmed, concluding that what the works share is unprotected facts and ideas, while the article's original expression does not appear in the film. The Ninth Circuit also denied rehearing en banc.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked whether a court may decide on its own, without a jury weighing in on how an ordinary viewer or reader would see the works as a whole, that two works are not similar enough to count as copyright infringement. The petition says this depends on which of two different tests courts use to compare works; Paramount answers that the tests are the same in substance and that no real disagreement among courts exists.

## Where it stands

The petition has been fully briefed, including a brief in opposition and a reply, and was distributed for the Court's conference on December 4, 2026. The docket shows no action since that distribution.

## What each outcome would mean

The Yonays, who lost in the lower courts, are asking the Court to take up their case. If the Court grants the petition, it will hear the case in full and decide the legal question; this would not itself decide who wins. If the Court denies the petition, the Ninth Circuit's ruling for Paramount stands, and the Yonays' copyright and contract claims remain rejected.
