## In brief

The heirs of a magazine author ask the Court to revive their copyright suit against Paramount over Top Gun: Maverick's alleged similarity to the article.

## What happened

Ehud Yonay wrote a 1983 nonfiction article about Navy fighter pilots; Paramount later used it as the basis for the 1986 film Top Gun. After Yonay died, his heirs, Shosh and Yuval Yonay, obtained the copyright and ended Paramount's rights to the article. When Paramount released Top Gun: Maverick in 2022 without crediting or paying them, they sued for copyright infringement and breach of contract. The district court granted summary judgment to Paramount, finding the works not "substantially similar," and the Ninth Circuit affirmed, also rejecting the contract claim and the Yonays' expert evidence. The Ninth Circuit denied rehearing.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked whether a court may rule, on its own, that a copyrighted work and an allegedly copying work are not similar enough to infringe without weighing how an ordinary viewer or reader would perceive the works overall, or whether that comparison must instead go to a jury. The petition says the Ninth Circuit's method of breaking works into separate pieces and comparing each piece conflicts with how several other appeals courts decide the same question.

## Where it stands

Paramount waived its right to respond, but the Court later asked for a response, which Paramount filed on October 16, 2026, opposing review. The petition has been distributed for the Court's conference but the docket does not yet show a decision on whether to hear the case.

## What each outcome would mean

The Yonays, as petitioners, are asking the Court to take up the case. If the Court grants review, it would hear full arguments on the proper test for comparing works in copyright cases, deciding nothing yet about who wins. If the Court denies review, the Ninth Circuit's ruling for Paramount stands, leaving the Yonays' infringement and contract claims rejected. Paramount's brief argues no real conflict exists among the appeals courts and that the case is a poor vehicle for deciding the question.
