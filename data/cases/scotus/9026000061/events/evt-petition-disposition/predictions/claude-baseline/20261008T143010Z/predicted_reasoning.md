# Forecast: what the Court will do with No. 26-61, Yonay v. Paramount Pictures

**Disposition.** The petition will most likely be **denied**, but this is a live
grant candidate: P(any grant) = 0.20.

**Relists.** The docket shows **one distribution** (for the September 28, 2026
conference), entered August 5, 2026. That distribution was overtaken on August
17 when the Court requested a response from Paramount, which had waived; the
response is now due October 16, 2026. Expect the petition to be **redistributed
at least once more** after the brief in opposition and reply arrive, most likely
for a conference in late November or December 2026 (P ≈ 0.97; the only path to no
further distribution is a withdrawal or settlement). Conditional on the Court
being interested, expect one or two further relists past that redistribution;
unconditionally I expect the petition to be resolved at or within one relist of
its first post-response conference.

**CVSG.** No CVSG is on the docket. This is a private copyright dispute with no
federal party and no statute the Executive administers at stake; the Copyright
Office has no direct role in the substantial-similarity test. A CVSG is
unlikely but not negligible given the Court's demonstrated attention (the
response request) and the recurring-importance framing: P ≈ 0.12. If one issues,
it would come after the post-response conference, in early 2027, which would push
resolution into OT2026's spring or later.

**Which question.** If the Court grants, it will take the question presented
essentially as written: whether a court may grant judgment for the defendant on
substantial similarity by dissecting the works element by element without
assessing how an ordinary observer would perceive the works as a whole. The
Court would not take the Seventh Amendment framing as a separate question; it
would treat the jury-trial point as an argument within the single methodological
question. The expert-exclusion and breach-of-contract rulings would not be
granted on; they would fall for reconsideration on remand if the test changed.

**Summary disposition.** Very unlikely conditional on a grant (P ≈ 0.03). No
intervening decision of this Court bears on the substantial-similarity test, so
there is nothing to GVR in light of, and a methodological question of this kind
is not a candidate for summary reversal. A grant means plenary briefing and
argument.

**If granted, the likely outcome (conditional only).** The Court would most
likely vacate rather than reverse, holding that the ultimate substantial-
similarity question must account for the works' total concept and feel as an
ordinary observer would perceive them, while preserving filtration of
unprotectable facts and ideas; the result on remand would remain uncertain
because the asserted work is a nonfiction magazine article whose overlap with
the film is heavily factual.

**Dissent from denial.** If the Court denies, I expect **no separate writing**
(P(some statement or dissent | denial) ≈ 0.08). The split has been tolerated for
decades and no Justice has staked out a position on the substantial-similarity
test; a short statement respecting denial from Justice Thomas or Justice Gorsuch
flagging the split is the most plausible form if any appears.
