## Why move to 18%?

The request reverses a waiver and requires adversarial briefing before dismissal of the petition becomes routine. It is a real attention signal, but not evidence that four Justices favor review. The petition also identifies a recurring methodological disagreement affecting copyright cases across media, presents a published appellate opinion, and has three amicus filings. The question is whether a court may dispose of infringement claims using the extrinsic comparison without the ordinary-observer assessment of the works as a whole; it is not simply whether this particular blockbuster needed a license. Those features justify a substantial upward adjustment from 5.1%. No committed table estimates that adjustment conditional on a response request, so its size is judgmental.

The principal downward consideration comes from reading the **actual opinion below**, rather than accepting the petition's characterization of it. Appendix A, pages 7a–8a and 14a–18a, expressly recognizes that an original selection and arrangement of individually unprotected elements can itself be protected. It then concludes that these works do not share the necessary expressive pattern. Pages 19a–21a also discuss the excluded expert's failure to distinguish expression from facts and generic ideas. That gives the Court a reason to regard this as an application-of-law dispute, or an imperfect vehicle for the broad methodological question, even if the circuits' formulations differ. Petition pages 16–17 themselves acknowledge filtration in the Second Circuit. I therefore do not treat an outcome-determinative split as independently established.

Petition page 30, footnote 7, identifies **Sedlik v. Von Drachenberg** as pending en banc and asks, alternatively, for a hold. That is a concrete reason the Court might await further circuit development instead of immediately granting plenary review. I use only the status described in the provisioned petition, not any later result. It also leaves a plausible later remand route. Balancing these considerations produces 18%, substantially above the band anchor but still strongly favoring no grant. The missing respondent argument and unsuccessful independent corroboration of the split limit confidence in a sharper estimate.
