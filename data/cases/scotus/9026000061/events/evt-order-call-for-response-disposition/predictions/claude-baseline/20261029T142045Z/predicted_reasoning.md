# Forecast — Yonay v. Paramount Pictures Corp., No. 26-61

**State forecast from.** One distribution so far (for the 9/28/2026 conference,
entered Aug 5); Paramount waived response on Jul 29; the Court called for a
response on Aug 17 (due Sep 16). No CVSG. Three amicus filings supporting the
petitioners are on the docket.

**Disposition: denied.** The Court will most likely deny the petition once the
response is in. The question — whether the Ninth Circuit's threshold
"extrinsic" test (element-by-element dissection decided by the court) can
dispose of a copyright claim without an ordinary-observer comparison of the
works as a whole — is a real and long-criticized divergence from the Second
Circuit's approach. But the Ninth Circuit has granted rehearing en banc in
*Sedlik v. Von Drachenberg* on the workings of the same two-step test, so the
Court can expect the circuit at the centre of the split to reconsider its own
rule first. That is the decisive vehicle-timing problem; the call for a
response signals only that someone wants Paramount's answer, most often a
low bar.

**Relisting.** The petition will be distributed again once the response and
any reply are in (the harness masks this claim). I expect it to be decided at
its first post-response conference without a further relist; a relist would
signal serious grant interest.

**CVSG.** Not expected: a private copyright dispute in which the United States
has no direct stake, and with the Ninth Circuit en banc pending the Court has
little reason to invite the Solicitor General.

**If granted.** The Court would take the question as written (whether courts
may grant judgment of no substantial similarity without an ordinary-observer,
works-as-a-whole comparison). A summary disposition is unlikely; the one
plausible route is a GVR if the en banc Ninth Circuit reworks the test while
this petition is pending.

**Dissent from denial.** Not expected; a statement respecting denial noting the
pending en banc proceedings is a remote possibility.
