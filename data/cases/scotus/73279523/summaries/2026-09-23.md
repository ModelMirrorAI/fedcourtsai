## What happened

Cleveland operates a water utility that places liens on properties when customers fall far behind on bills. Black homeowners in Cuyahoga County, led by Albert Pickett, Jr., sued Cleveland, arguing this facially race-neutral lien policy falls disproportionately on Black customers and violates the Fair Housing Act. A federal district court certified a class seeking damages, and the Sixth Circuit Court of Appeals affirmed, holding that class members had suffered a concrete injury for standing purposes because racial discrimination itself, and the lien placement itself, are cognizable harms—even though Cleveland says some class members suffered no measurable economic loss. Cleveland asked the full Sixth Circuit to rehear the case; that request was denied.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked whether a class of plaintiffs seeking money damages may include people whose only claimed harm is the general, statistical disproportionate effect of a neutral policy, without any showing that they personally suffered concrete harm. Cleveland argues this conflicts with how other appeals courts handle similar cases and with the Court's own standing rules. The homeowners respond that every class member did suffer a concrete injury—both the dignitary harm of racial discrimination and the practical burden of having a lien placed on their property—so the case does not actually present the broader legal question Cleveland raises.

## Where it stands

The petition has been distributed for several conferences without a decision, and most recently, in May 2026, Cleveland filed a joint motion asking the Court to defer considering the petition further.
