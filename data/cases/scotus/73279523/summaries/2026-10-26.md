## In brief

Cleveland asks the Supreme Court to overturn a lower-court decision allowing a class-action lawsuit over its water-lien policy to include homeowners who suffered no economic harm.

## What happened

Cleveland Water places liens on properties when customers fall far behind on water bills. A group of Black homeowners sued, claiming the policy violates the Fair Housing Act because it disproportionately burdens Black residents in Cuyahoga County, even though the policy does not mention race. A federal district court certified a class of affected Black homeowners and residents to pursue both an injunction and money damages. The Sixth Circuit Court of Appeals affirmed, holding that the class could include members who had not suffered any economic loss, because the alleged racial disparity itself was enough to give every class member standing to sue.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked whether a lawsuit seeking money damages for a whole group of people can include group members who were never actually harmed, so long as they claim a policy's effects fell unevenly across races even though the policy itself does not treat people differently by race. Cleveland argues each class member must show a concrete loss; the homeowners argue that being subjected to a racially skewed policy, or having a lien placed on their property, is itself enough of a harm.

## Where it stands

The petition has been fully briefed, including a brief opposing review and Cleveland's reply, and was distributed for several conferences in May 2026. Most recently, Cleveland filed a joint motion asking the Court to defer consideration of the petition; the docket does not show the Court's action on that motion.

## What each outcome would mean

Cleveland, which lost in the lower courts, is asking the Court to reverse the Sixth Circuit's decision. If the Court grants review and later sides with Cleveland, the class of homeowners allowed to seek damages would likely be narrowed to exclude those without an economic loss, though the separate class seeking only an injunction would be unaffected. If the Court instead sides with the homeowners, the broader damages class certified by the lower courts would stand. If the Court denies review, the Sixth Circuit's ruling allowing the broader class remains in effect, and the case continues in the lower courts without Supreme Court review of this issue.
