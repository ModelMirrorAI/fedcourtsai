The City of Cleveland asks the Court to review whether a class of homeowners suing over water liens was properly certified when some members may have suffered no economic harm.

## What happened

Black homeowners in Cuyahoga County sued Cleveland, alleging its policy of placing liens on properties with overdue water bills disproportionately burdens Black customers, violating the Fair Housing Act. A federal district court certified a class seeking damages, and the Sixth Circuit Court of Appeals affirmed, holding that class members had suffered a legally sufficient injury because racial discrimination itself is a concrete harm, even if some members had paid no penalties or interest on their liens. Cleveland argues that up to 20 percent of the class suffered no economic injury at all. The homeowners dispute that characterization of the record.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked whether a class of people can be grouped together to seek money damages when some members of that class may not have suffered any real-world harm beyond being subject to a policy that has an unequal effect by race. The dispute centers on how much harm each person in a lawsuit like this must show before a court can let them all proceed as one group.
