# Forecast

The Court will deny the petition. The snapshot records one distribution, on June 24, 2026, for the September 28, 2026 conference; it records no further distribution and no CVSG. I expect zero additional distributions and a denial in the early-October 2026 orders following that conference. The probability of at least one additional distribution is 7%; if there is one, a single additional distribution is more likely than a prolonged sequence.

The Court will not call for the Solicitor General's views (0.2% probability of a new CVSG). Wisconsin has waived its response, and I expect disposition without an intervening request for a response. This is an individual state criminal-evidence dispute rather than a petition presenting a distinctive federal administrative interest.

If review is granted, the Court will most likely narrow the question to whether admitting evidence of conduct charged in a separate, still-pending prosecution impermissibly burdens the defendant's privilege against self-incrimination or access to counsel. It will not treat every evidentiary finding concerning another charged act as itself an unconstitutional conviction. The separate structural-error question would follow only if the Court first identifies a constitutional deprivation.

Conditional on any grant, plenary briefing and argument are more likely than disposition in the cert order: the summary-route probability is 15%. The record identifies no intervening decision squarely requiring a GVR, and the petition seeks an extension of constitutional protections rather than straightforward application of a clearly controlling rule.

Conditional on denial, I expect no noted dissent or statement respecting denial (2% probability of any such writing). I do not forecast a particular Justice as an author. The expected denial will leave the conviction standing without an opinion resolving the constitutional question or endorsing the lower court's reasoning.
