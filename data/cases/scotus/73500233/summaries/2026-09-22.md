## What happened

Mark Pitzka was charged in Wisconsin with setting two house fires connected to his ex-girlfriend, A.V., in separate cases in Waukesha and Dodge Counties. Before his Waukesha trial, prosecutors got permission to tell the jury about the still-pending, unresolved Dodge County arson charge as "other acts" evidence suggesting a pattern. Pitzka's trial lawyer did not object. A jury convicted him in Waukesha; the Dodge County case was later dropped. Wisconsin's court of appeals upheld the conviction, and the Wisconsin Supreme Court declined further review.

## What the Court is being asked

Pitzka's petition asks the Court to decide whether it violates a defendant's constitutional rights to let a jury hear about another, still-pending and unresolved criminal charge against him as evidence supporting guilt in the case being tried. He argues this forces him to effectively answer for the other charge before it goes to trial, undermining his right to remain silent, his right to be presumed innocent in that other case, and his right to counsel's guidance on whether to testify.

## Where it stands

Wisconsin waived its right to respond to the petition. The case was distributed for the Court's conference on September 28, 2026, where the Justices will decide whether to grant certiorari, the formal step needed before the Court can hear the case.
