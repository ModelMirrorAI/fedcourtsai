## In brief

Mark Pitzka, convicted of arson in Wisconsin, asked the Court to decide whether his trial was constitutionally tainted; the Court denied review.

## What happened

Mark Pitzka was charged with arson in Waukesha County, Wisconsin, after fires at properties connected to his ex-girlfriend. At the same time, he faced a separate, unresolved arson charge in Dodge County. The Waukesha trial court allowed prosecutors to tell the jury about that pending Dodge County case as "other acts" evidence suggesting a pattern. A jury convicted Pitzka, and after his conviction, the Dodge County case was dismissed. Wisconsin's court of appeals affirmed his conviction, and the Wisconsin Supreme Court declined to review that decision.

## What the Court is being asked

Pitzka asked the Court to decide whether it violates a defendant's constitutional rights for a trial court to let prosecutors tell a jury about a separate, still-pending and unresolved criminal charge against the same defendant, as evidence of a pattern or history, before that other charge has been resolved. He argued this practice undermines the presumption of innocence and the rights to silence and counsel in the other pending case.

## Where it stands

The petition was distributed for the Court's conference on September 28, 2026, after Wisconsin waived its right to respond. The Court denied the petition on October 5, 2026.

## What each outcome would mean

Pitzka, who lost in the Wisconsin courts, asked the Court to take up his case. Had the Court granted review, it would have heard full arguments and decided the constitutional question itself, without presuming either side correct in advance. By denying the petition, the Court leaves the Wisconsin Supreme Court's decision in place, meaning Pitzka's conviction stands and the evidentiary practice he challenged remains unaddressed by this Court.
