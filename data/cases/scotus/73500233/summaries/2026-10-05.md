## In brief

The Supreme Court declined to hear Mark Pitzka's challenge to his arson conviction, which rested partly on evidence from another pending, unresolved criminal case against him.

## What happened

Mark Pitzka was convicted of arson in Waukesha County, Wisconsin, after prosecutors introduced evidence of a second, still-pending arson charge against him in Dodge County as "other acts" evidence supporting his guilt. The Dodge County case was later dismissed after the Waukesha conviction. Pitzka argued on appeal that using an unresolved charge this way violated his constitutional rights, and that his trial lawyer was ineffective for not objecting more forcefully. Wisconsin's court of appeals rejected these arguments, and the Wisconsin Supreme Court denied further review.

## What the Court is being asked

Pitzka asked the Court to decide whether letting a jury hear about a separate, still-pending and unresolved criminal charge against a defendant, as evidence supporting guilt in the case on trial, violates the defendant's rights to remain silent, to the presumption of innocence, and to counsel, before that other charge has been resolved.

## Where it stands

Wisconsin waived its right to respond to the petition. The Court distributed the case for its September 28, 2026 conference, and on October 5, 2026, it denied the petition.

## What each outcome would mean

Pitzka, as petitioner, asked the Court to take up his case and overturn the Wisconsin courts' rulings. By denying the petition, the Court leaves the Wisconsin Supreme Court's decision in place, meaning Pitzka's arson conviction stands and the evidentiary ruling he challenged remains valid in Wisconsin. The denial does not mean the Court agreed with the lower court's reasoning, only that it chose not to review the case further.
