## In brief

Mark Pitzka asks the Court to review whether using evidence tied to his separate, still-pending criminal case against him at trial violated his constitutional rights.

## What happened

Mark Pitzka was charged with arson in Waukesha County, Wisconsin, and separately in Dodge County, for fires linked to his ex-girlfriend and her boyfriend. Before the Waukesha trial, prosecutors won permission to tell the jury about the still-pending, unresolved Dodge County arson charge against him as "other acts" evidence supporting his guilt. A jury convicted him. Wisconsin's court of appeals affirmed, applying the state's ordinary test for admitting other-acts evidence without addressing whether using a pending, unproven charge this way was different. The Wisconsin Supreme Court denied further review.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked whether letting a jury hear about a separate criminal charge against a defendant, one that has not yet been resolved by trial or plea, as evidence of guilt in a different, ongoing prosecution violates the defendant's rights to be presumed innocent, to stay silent, and to have counsel's guidance on the pending charge.

## Where it stands

Wisconsin waived its right to file a response to the petition. The Court distributed the case for its conference of September 28, 2026, and the docket does not yet show any action taken since that conference.

## What each outcome would mean

Pitzka, who lost in the Wisconsin courts, is asking the Court to take up his case. If the Court grants review, it will hear full arguments and decide whether using a pending, unresolved charge as trial evidence this way is constitutional; granting review does not by itself decide who wins. If the Court denies review, the Wisconsin Supreme Court's decision leaving Pitzka's conviction in place stands, and the case ends there without the Court deciding the underlying constitutional question.
