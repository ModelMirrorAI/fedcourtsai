# Predicted Reasoning — *Donald J. Trump, et al. v. Hillary R. Clinton, et al.* (No. 26-518)

## Procedural Trajectory (Relists and CVSG)

The docket currently records **one conference distribution** (distributed on October 21, 2026 for the Conference of November 6, 2026, following Respondents Hillary R. Clinton et al.'s October 19, 2026 waiver of the right to respond). On October 27, 2026, however, the Court requested a response, due November 27, 2026.

- **Further distributions (`relist-increment`):** Because the Court called for a response after the initial distribution, the petition will be redistributed for a subsequent Conference after the brief in opposition is filed (or the response deadline expires). Accordingly, at least one additional distribution beyond the single distribution currently recorded on the snapshot is nearly certain. Once redistributed with the brief in opposition in hand, the most likely trajectory is one post-response distribution (bringing total distributions to two) followed by denial, with a smaller chance of one or two additional relists if a Justice circulates a separate statement or dissent from denial.
- **Call for the Views of the Solicitor General (`cvsg-increment`):** The Court will **not** issue a CVSG. The United States of America is already a named respondent on the docket (substituted under the Westfall Act for the federal defendants below and represented by the Solicitor General), and the dispute involves private civil claims and litigation sanctions rather than a federal regulatory or statutory scheme warranting an invited amicus brief.

## Disposition and Route

The Court will **deny** the petition for a writ of certiorari.

- **Questions Presented (if granted):** If the Court were to grant review, it would take **Question Presented 2** alone (or narrowed to whether a district court's inherent-authority bad-faith finding and fee award under *Chambers v. NASCO, Inc.*, 501 U.S. 32 (1991), and *Goodyear Tire & Rubber Co. v. Haeger*, 581 U.S. 101 (2017), must be vacated when the district court treated an unresolved Article II equitable-tolling argument as one piece of evidence of bad faith) and leave behind **Question Presented 1** (whether Article II responsibilities equitably toll a sitting President's personal civil RICO claims), which is foreclosed by Petitioners' concession that deferring suit was a voluntary choice and by independent alternative grounds supporting dismissal of the underlying RICO claims.
- **Summary disposition vs. plenary review (`summary-disposition-route`):** Conditional on any grant, a **summary disposition (GVR)** is slightly more likely than plenary merits review (`0.55`). As the petition itself suggests in the alternative, if a majority concluded that the lower courts improperly counted Petitioners' Article II tolling argument toward the bad-faith determination, the natural remedy in a fact-intensive sanctions appeal would be to grant, vacate, and remand for reconsideration of the inherent-authority sanctions award without relying on that ground, rather than setting a record encumbered by shotgun-pleading findings and forfeited objections for full briefing and oral argument.
- **Conditional merits ground (if granted for plenary review):** If the Court granted plenary review, it would likely hold that while a novel constitutional argument for extending Article II principles to equitable tolling is not by itself evidence of subjective bad faith under *Chambers*, a district court's inherent-authority sanctions order may stand where independent, unchallenged findings of shotgun pleading and objectively frivolous counts support the award and the sanctioned party forfeited apportionment objections below.

## Separate Writings on Denial

Conditional on a denial of certiorari, there is a meaningful but minority probability (`0.12`) of a separate writing (a dissent from denial or statement respecting the denial of certiorari). The October 27, 2026 call for a response indicates that at least one chambers flagged the petition—most plausibly to examine the nearly $1 million inherent-authority sanctions award and the district court's sweeping commentary on the President's other litigation. If a separate statement or dissent from denial is filed, it is most likely to be authored by **Justice Thomas** (the Circuit Justice for the Eleventh Circuit) or **Justice Alito**, expressing concern about lower courts using inherent-authority sanctions in politically sensitive cases or chilling novel Article II arguments while recognizing the vehicle and forfeiture obstacles that prevent plenary review.
