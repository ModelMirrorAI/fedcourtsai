# Predicted petition handling

The Court will most likely deny the petition after receiving the requested response and redistributing it for a later conference, likely in winter 2026–27. The supplied docket shows exactly one distribution, October 21 for November 6, and an October 27 response request due November 27. I forecast a 98% chance of at least one additional distribution from that state. A return to conference following the requested response is the expected administrative course, not necessarily a true merits-driven relist. The modal path is one additional distribution followed by denial; several substantive relists are not the central forecast.

There is no CVSG on the record. I forecast only a 1% chance of a new invitation for the Solicitor General's views. The federal respondent can address the petition through ordinary party briefing. A response request is already present and is not something this cell predicts will first occur later.

Any grant has a 9% probability. If the Court takes the case for plenary review, it will most likely focus on the first question as presented—whether presidential responsibilities justify tolling private claims—with the sanctions question either accompanying it or narrowed to whether reasonable constitutional advocacy can support this particular inherent-authority award. A broad ruling immunizing presidential counsel from sanctions is not the expected scope.

Conditional on a grant, I assign 35% to disposition in the cert order itself and 65% to plenary review. The principal summary route would be a limited vacatur and remand of the sanctions judgment for reconsideration of the permissible grounds and causal basis of the award, rather than revival of every underlying claim. No intervening controlling decision on the provisioned record makes a conventional GVR the modal result. If granted for argument, the dispute would concern the boundary between accommodation of official duties and ordinary rules for a President acting as a private litigant, together with the independent bases supporting the fee award.

Conditional on denial, there is a 9% chance of a dissent or statement respecting denial. The modal order is an unsigned denial without separate writing. If a Justice does write, Thomas or Alito is the leading authorship possibility, followed by Gorsuch; this does not predict or purport to reveal their cert votes. Denial would leave the lower-court result in place without a Supreme Court merits opinion endorsing its reasoning.
