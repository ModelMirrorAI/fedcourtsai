## In brief

Donald Trump asks the Court to revive a dismissed lawsuit against Hillary Clinton and others and overturn sanctions imposed on him and his lawyers.

## What happened

After his first presidential term, Trump sued Hillary Clinton and dozens of others, alleging they conspired to falsely tie him to Russian collusion before the 2016 election. He argued the lawsuit's deadline should be paused (tolled) because his presidential duties kept him from filing sooner. The district court dismissed the case as untimely and, separately, as lacking merit, and imposed about $1 million in sanctions against Trump and his attorneys for filing what it called a frivolous, disorganized pleading. The Eleventh Circuit Court of Appeals affirmed those rulings, though it sent one defendant's dismissal back to be entered without prejudice on jurisdictional grounds.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked whether a president's official duties justify pausing the deadline for filing his personal lawsuits while he is in office, and whether a court may rely on a party's advocacy of that tolling argument as evidence of bad faith supporting sanctions.

## Where it stands

The petition was distributed for the Court's November 6, 2026 conference; the Court then asked Clinton's side to respond, and that response was filed November 13, 2026. The docket shows no decision yet on whether the Court will hear the case.

## What each outcome would mean

Trump and his co-petitioners are asking the Court to take up the case. If the Court grants review, it would hear full arguments on the tolling and sanctions questions and decide them, deciding nothing yet about who prevails. If the Court denies review, the Eleventh Circuit's ruling stands, leaving the lawsuit's dismissal and the sanctions against Trump and his attorneys in place, a result favoring Clinton and the other respondents, including the federal government as a respondent in part of the case.
