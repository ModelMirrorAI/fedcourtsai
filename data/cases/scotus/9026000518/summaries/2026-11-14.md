## In brief

Donald Trump asks the Court to revive civil claims a lower court dismissed as untimely and to overturn sanctions imposed on him and his lawyers.

## What happened

After his first presidential term, Trump sued dozens of people and organizations, alleging they conspired to falsely tie him to Russian election interference. The district court dismissed the suit, finding the claims filed too late and otherwise legally deficient, and it sanctioned Trump and his attorneys for filing what it called a sprawling pleading containing frivolous claims. The United States Court of Appeals for the Eleventh Circuit affirmed the dismissal and the sanctions, though it sent one defendant's case back to be dismissed on different grounds. The Eleventh Circuit later denied rehearing.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked whether the time limit for filing Trump's lawsuit should have been paused during his presidency because of the demands of the office, even though he filed other personal lawsuits while in office. It is also asked whether the lower courts improperly punished Trump and his lawyers with sanctions for making that argument about pausing the deadline, rather than for other problems with the lawsuit.

## Where it stands

The respondents waived their right to respond, but the Court later requested a response, which has now been filed opposing review. The case has not yet been scheduled for a conference to decide whether the Court will hear it.

## What each outcome would mean

Trump and his co-petitioners are asking the Court to reverse the Eleventh Circuit's decision, which upheld dismissal of the lawsuit and the sanctions against them. If the Court grants review, it would hear full arguments on whether the deadline should have been paused and whether the sanctions were proper, without yet deciding who wins. If the Court denies review, the Eleventh Circuit's decision stands, leaving the dismissal and the sanctions against Trump and his attorneys in place.
