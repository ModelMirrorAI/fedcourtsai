## In brief

Donald Trump asks the Court to review sanctions and a dismissal arising from his lawsuit alleging a conspiracy to spread false Russia-collusion claims against him.

## What happened

After his first term, Trump sued Hillary Clinton and numerous others, alleging a racketeering conspiracy and related claims over the 2016 "Russia collusion" allegations. The district court dismissed the suit as untimely, rejecting Trump's argument that his presidential duties should pause the filing deadline, and separately imposed about $938,000 in sanctions against Trump and his lawyers, finding the complaint was filed in bad faith. The Eleventh Circuit affirmed the dismissal and the sanctions, though it sent one defendant's claims back to be dismissed without prejudice for lack of jurisdiction over that defendant. Rehearing was denied.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked whether a President's constitutional duties can pause the deadline for filing his own private lawsuits while he holds office, and whether a court may treat a lawyer's advocacy of that argument, even if ultimately rejected, as evidence of bad faith justifying financial sanctions against the President and his attorneys.

## Where it stands

The respondents waived their right to respond, but the Court later requested a response, due November 27, 2026. The petition was distributed for the Court's November 6, 2026 conference, and the docket shows no action recorded since that conference.

## What each outcome would mean

Trump and his co-petitioners are asking the Court to overturn the Eleventh Circuit's rulings. If the Court grants the petition, it would hear full briefing and argument on whether the tolling theory is valid and whether the sanctions were proper, deciding nothing yet about who wins. If it denies the petition, the Eleventh Circuit's decision stands: the dismissal of Trump's claims as untimely and the sanctions against Trump and his lawyers remain in effect, and Clinton and the other respondents keep their win below.
