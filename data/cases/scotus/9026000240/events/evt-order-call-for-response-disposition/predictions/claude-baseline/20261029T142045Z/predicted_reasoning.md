# Forecast: Johnson v. Fleming, No. 26-240 (cert before judgment), after the call for a response

**State forecast from:** one distribution (for the 10/16/2026 conference). The Court then called for a response on 10/5/2026, after Virginia waived. There is no relist and no CVSG on the record.

**Disposition.** This petition will not be decided on its own. It travels with the companion *Hall v. Fleming*, No. 26-193, which raises the same challenge to Virginia's VTAG religious-degree exclusion from a final Fourth Circuit judgment (175 F.4th 510). The two dockets look like they are being lined up. *Hall* is fully briefed and was distributed for 11/13/2026, then rescheduled, and Johnson's response is now due 11/17/2026. Expect the Court to take both petitions up at the same conference, probably in December 2026 or January 2027. The likeliest single outcome for this docket is still **not a plenary grant**. The Court rarely grants certiorari before judgment, and this petition comes up on a preliminary-injunction and dismissal posture. *Hall* is the cleaner vehicle for the Locke question. The most probable sequence:
- The Court grants *Hall* (better than even) and **holds** Johnson for it. Once *Hall* is decided, likely late in OT2026, Johnson is GVR'd if the students' side prevails in *Hall*, or denied if it does not.
- Less likely, the Court grants both and consolidates them. Johnson adds the second grant program (the Tuition Assistance and Opportunity grant, as the petition describes it) and the denominational-favoritism theory.
- Or the Court denies Johnson outright while acting on *Hall*. That is most likely if the Fourth Circuit decides the appeal first and the before-judgment request loses its point.

Overall, a grant-family disposition (plenary grant or GVR) is somewhat less likely than a denial. A GVR after a hold is about as likely as a plenary grant.

**Further relists.** Near-certain. The petition will be distributed again once the response (due 11/17) and any reply are in, and a hold would add more.

**CVSG.** None. The respondent is a state, and no federal program is at issue. The United States may file as amicus on its own, but the Court has no reason to invite it.

**Question taken, if granted.** QP 1 as written (whether a state may fund some religious degrees and exclude similar ones), with QP 2 (overrule *Locke*) likely folded into it or reached through *Hall*. The Court is more likely to narrow *Locke* to its facts than to overrule it outright.

**Summary disposition.** Conditional on any grant, a GVR in light of *Hall* is about as likely as plenary review. No per curiam summary reversal is expected.

**Separate writing on a denial.** Unlikely if *Hall* is granted, since there is nothing to dissent from. If both are denied, a statement or dissent from Justice Thomas, Gorsuch or Alito is plausible, but it would more likely attach to *Hall* than to this before-judgment petition.
