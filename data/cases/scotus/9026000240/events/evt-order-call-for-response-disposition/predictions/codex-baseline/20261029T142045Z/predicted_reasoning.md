# Forecast of the Court's action

The Court will most likely deny this petition for certiorari before judgment, leaving the Fourth Circuit appeal to proceed. The response request will lead to fuller consideration but not necessarily to immediate Supreme Court intervention. The petition's separate request to overrule Locke v. Davey will most likely not be taken up in this proceeding.

The baseline records one distribution, on September 30 for the October 16, 2026 conference, followed by the October 5 response request with a November 4 deadline. It records no completed relist and no CVSG. I forecast at least one additional distribution with probability 0.98, principally the routine redistribution after the response or its deadline, not a claim that the Justices have already considered and relisted the petition. The modal path is one further distribution and then denial, with one additional substantive relist a plausible alternative. A hold connected to the related Hall petition described in the filing could produce a longer path. I expect disposition in late 2026 or early 2027 on the ordinary path; a companion-case hold could last substantially longer.

I forecast no invitation to the Solicitor General, assigning a 0.02 probability to a new CVSG. The questions, as framed in the petition, concern state educational grants and constitutional religious neutrality, rather than an unresolved federal agency position requiring the government's views.

If review is granted, the more likely route, narrowly, is briefing and argument: the conditional probability of disposition in the cert order itself is 0.45. The summary alternative is substantially live because the petition identifies a related, already-decided appellate case, Hall v. Fleming, No. 26-193, as another vehicle. A grant, vacatur, and remand here could follow a controlling resolution elsewhere. I do not forecast that such an intervening decision already exists, or that Hall will itself be granted. A direct summary reversal is less attractive than a companion-linked remand because the petition presents distinct grant programs and an unfinished appellate proceeding.

On plenary review, the Court would most likely focus on the first question: whether the Religion Clauses permit funding some religious degree programs while excluding materially comparable ones. A decision distinguishing Locke would be a narrower route than accepting the second question's invitation to overrule it. Conditional on reaching the merits, the likely objection would concern the allegedly unequal treatment of comparable religious programs, rather than a categorical entitlement to state funding of all ministerial training. This is a conditional merits forecast, not a prediction that an opinion will issue.

If review is denied, I expect an unsigned denial without an accompanying writing. The conditional probability of a dissent from, or statement respecting, denial is 0.10. Thomas, Alito, and Gorsuch are my leading candidates for a rare separate writing; none is individually likely to write. Their unconditional writing probabilities, and those of the other Justices, appear in prediction.json.
