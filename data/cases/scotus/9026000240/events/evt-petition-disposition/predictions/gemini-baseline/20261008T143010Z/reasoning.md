The baseline salience band for a modern cert petition carries a grant rate around 4-6%. However, I adjust this up significantly for three reasons. First, this case has attracted seven amicus briefs at the petition stage, including from West Virginia and 21 other states, the American Center for Law and Justice, and Notre Dame Education Law Project. This level of coordinated amicus support is a strong signal of vehicle quality and ideological importance. Second, the Court actively pulled the petition from the October 16 conference to request a response from the respondents (who had previously waived their right to respond). A requested response indicates that at least one Justice is closely scrutinizing the petition. Third, the petition directly asks the Court to overrule *Locke v. Davey*, a long-standing target of the conservative legal movement in its ongoing effort to expand Free Exercise rights in the context of state funding.

I adjust down because this is a petition for certiorari *before judgment* (the Fourth Circuit has only denied an injunction pending appeal and deferred a motion to accelerate). Certiorari before judgment is rarely granted, requiring a showing of imperative public importance. While the issues are salient, the Court may prefer to wait for the Fourth Circuit to rule on the merits or for a final judgment. Therefore, I place the grant probability at 20%—much higher than a typical petition, but still below 50% due to the procedural posture. 

The `big_case_score` is high (0.85) because overruling or narrowing *Locke v. Davey* would be a major doctrinal shift in First Amendment jurisprudence.
