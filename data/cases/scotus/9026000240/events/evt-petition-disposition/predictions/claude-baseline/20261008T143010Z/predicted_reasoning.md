# Forecast: what the Court will do with No. 26-240, Johnson v. Fleming

**State forecast from.** The docket shows **one** distribution (Sept 30, 2026, for the Oct 16 conference), **no CVSG**, a respondent waiver (Sept 23), and a **response requested by the Court on Oct 5** (due Nov 4, 2026). Eight amicus briefs support the petition, including West Virginia and 21 other states. The petition is for certiorari **before judgment** under Rule 11; the Fourth Circuit appeal (No. 26-1437) is fully briefed, the motion to submit on the briefs is deferred pending panel assignment, and the court has circulated December 8–11, 2026 argument dates. The companion petition Hall v. Fleming, No. 26-193, from the Fourth Circuit's published May 13, 2026 opinion, has its brief in opposition due Oct 14, 2026 after an extension, with thirteen amicus briefs.

**Relists.** The petition will be removed from the Oct 16 conference and redistributed once Virginia's response (due Nov 4) and a reply are in, so at least one further distribution is near-certain. Expect the Court to line this petition up with Hall for the same conference, most likely in early-to-mid December 2026, and then one of two paths: (a) a decision on both at that conference or the one after, which adds one or two distributions in total; or (b) a hold of this petition while Hall is argued, in which case no further distribution entries appear until Hall is decided (June 2027) and this petition is then disposed of on the order list. Either way the distribution count rises above one.

**CVSG.** No. The questions are constitutional claims against a state program; no federal statute or federal party is implicated and the Court has not called for the Solicitor General's views in the Trinity Lutheran–Espinoza–Carson line. A CVSG would also be inconsistent with the Court's evident intent to move the pair together on a compressed calendar.

**Disposition and route.** The most likely single outcome is **denial** of this cert-before-judgment petition, with the Court taking Hall (if it takes anything) as the final-judgment vehicle: Hall presents the "overrule Locke" question on a published opinion, a dismissal with prejudice, and the devotional-degree facts closest to Locke itself. The grant paths, which together I put at a little over a third: (1) the Court grants Hall and also grants this petition before judgment and consolidates, because this case adds the Virginia National Guard program, the CIP-38-versus-CIP-39 denominational-line theory, and the entanglement claim that Hall's single VTAG claim does not; or (2) the Court grants Hall alone, holds this petition, and on deciding Hall for the petitioner grants, vacates, and remands. Conditional on any grant, I put the cert-order (hold-and-GVR) route at about 0.45 and plenary consolidation at about 0.55. If the Court denies Hall, this petition is denied with it. Timing: a decision on the petition in December 2026 or January 2027, or, under the hold, a GVR or denial on the final order lists of June 2027.

**Which question, if taken.** With consolidation the Court would take Hall's question (whether to overrule Locke) as the lead and this petition's QP 1 as the narrower alternative: whether Locke permits a state to fund some religious degrees while excluding others. The Court would not grant on QP 2 alone here. The Court might reformulate to ask whether Locke should be overruled or limited to its facts.

**Dissent from denial.** If the Court denies both petitions, expect a dissent from denial or a statement respecting denial from Justice Thomas, likely joined by Justices Alito and Gorsuch, attached to Hall and referencing this case; a separate writing on this petition alone is unlikely. If the Court grants Hall and simply denies this petition as the duplicative companion, expect no writing.

**If granted, conditionally.** The likely ground is that Locke is confined to its facts (training for the clergy) and does not license exclusions that draw lines among religious degrees, with at least three Justices writing to overrule Locke outright.
