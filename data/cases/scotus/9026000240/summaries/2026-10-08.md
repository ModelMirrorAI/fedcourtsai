## In brief

Three Virginia students ask the Court to hear their challenge to state tuition grant programs that fund some religious degrees but not others.

## What happened

Cameron Johnson, Luke Thomas, and Trace Stevens are Virginia students at Liberty University seeking state tuition grants. Virginia funds some religious degree programs but excludes others, including the degrees these students chose. A federal district court dismissed Johnson's and Thomas's claims and denied Stevens a preliminary injunction, relying on the Supreme Court's 2004 decision in Locke v. Davey, which the district court read as barring any challenge to such exclusions. The Fourth Circuit Court of Appeals denied a request to block the policy while the appeal proceeds and put off a request to speed up the appeal. The students then asked the Supreme Court to take the case before the Fourth Circuit rules.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked whether the Constitution's religion protections stop a state from funding some religious degree programs while refusing to fund other, similar religious degree programs. The students also ask the Court to reconsider and overturn its past ruling that allowed a state to exclude a devotional theology degree from a scholarship program.

## Where it stands

The respondents waived their right to formally respond, but the Court later requested a response, due November 4, 2026. The case was distributed for the Court's October 16, 2026 conference, and the docket shows no action on the petition since that request.

## What each outcome would mean

Johnson, Thomas, and Stevens, as petitioners, are asking the Court to take up their case now, before the Fourth Circuit rules. If the Court grants the petition, it would hear the case in full and decide the questions about religious funding exclusions, without yet deciding who wins. If the Court denies the petition, the case would continue in the lower courts, where the district court's dismissal of Johnson's and Thomas's claims and denial of an injunction to Stevens would remain in place for now, with the Fourth Circuit's appeal still pending.
