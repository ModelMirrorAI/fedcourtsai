## In brief

Three Virginia students ask the Court to hear their challenge to state tuition grant programs that fund some religious college majors but not others.

## What happened

Cameron Johnson, Luke Thomas, and Trace Stevens are Virginia students at Liberty University who sought state tuition grants. Virginia's programs fund some religious degrees but exclude others the state classifies as religious training or theological education. A federal district court dismissed Johnson's and Thomas's claims and denied Stevens a preliminary injunction, relying on the Supreme Court's decision in Locke v. Davey, which it read as barring any challenge to such exclusions. The Fourth Circuit denied an injunction pending appeal without explanation. The students then asked the Supreme Court to take the case before the Fourth Circuit finished deciding the appeal, noting a related case, Hall v. Fleming, already pending at the Court.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked whether the Religion Clauses let a state fund some religious college majors through a grant program while excluding other, similar religious majors, and whether the Court should overturn its own prior ruling that the students say wrongly allows states broad power to exclude religious vocational training from such programs.

## Where it stands

The petition was distributed for the Court's October 16, 2026 conference, after which the Court asked the respondents to file a response, due as extended to November 17, 2026. No decision on whether to hear the case has been recorded.

## What each outcome would mean

The students are asking the Court to take up their case now, before the Fourth Circuit rules, and to reverse the lower courts' rulings against them. If the Court grants the petition, it would hear the case and decide the question itself, without yet deciding who wins. If it denies the petition, the lower court rulings rejecting the students' claims and denying their preliminary injunction request would stand, and the case would continue, if at all, in the Fourth Circuit.
