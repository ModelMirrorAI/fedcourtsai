## In brief

Three Virginia students ask the Court to decide whether state tuition grant programs may fund some religious degrees while excluding others like theirs.

## What happened

Cameron Johnson, Luke Thomas, and Trace Stevens are Virginia students pursuing religious degrees at Liberty University. Virginia's tuition grant programs fund some religious degree programs but not others that the students say are materially similar. A federal district court dismissed Johnson's and Thomas's claims and denied Stevens a preliminary injunction, ruling that a Supreme Court decision called Locke v. Davey bars such challenges. The Fourth Circuit Court of Appeals denied a request to block the programs while the case was appealed and put off a request to speed up the appeal. The students then asked the Supreme Court to take the case before the Fourth Circuit rules on the full appeal.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked whether the Constitution's religion protections let a state fund some religious college degree programs while refusing to fund other religious degree programs that are similar in content and purpose. The students also ask the Court to reconsider and overturn the precedent the lower courts relied on to reject their challenge.

## Where it stands

The petition was distributed for the Court's October 16, 2026 conference. Afterward, the Court asked the respondents to file a response, due November 17, 2026 after an extension; the Court has not yet acted on the petition itself.

## What each outcome would mean

Johnson, Thomas, and Stevens are asking the Court to take up their case. If the Court grants the petition, it would hear the case in full and decide whether Virginia's funding distinctions, and the precedent allowing them, are constitutional; a grant alone would not decide who wins. If the Court denies the petition, the lower court rulings rejecting the students' claims and denying their request to block the programs would remain in place, and the case would continue, if at all, in the lower courts.
