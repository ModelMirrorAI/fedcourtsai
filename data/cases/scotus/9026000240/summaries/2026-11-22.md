## In brief

Three Virginia college students ask the Court to hear their challenge to state tuition grant programs that fund some religious degrees but not theirs, before a federal appeals court rules.

## What happened

Cameron Johnson, Luke Thomas, and Trace Stevens are Virginia students who were denied state tuition grants because their chosen majors (Pastoral Leadership, Music and Worship, and a Master of Divinity) are classified as religious training or theological education. Virginia's grant programs fund some religious-studies majors but exclude others. A federal district court dismissed two of the students' claims and denied the third a preliminary injunction, relying on a 2004 Supreme Court case, Locke v. Davey, which allows states to decline funding religious vocational training. The students asked the Fourth Circuit Court of Appeals for an injunction while their appeal proceeds; that request was denied, and the appeal remains pending there. The students then asked the Supreme Court to take the case directly, before the Fourth Circuit decides it. Virginia's state officials oppose review, saying the case is premature and the factual record is incomplete.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked whether the Constitution's religion protections let a state fund some religious college degrees while refusing to fund other religious degrees in the same grant program, and separately, whether the Court should abandon its 2004 ruling that allowed states to withhold funding specifically for degrees preparing students for religious ministry.

## Where it stands

The petition was distributed for a conference, and the Court asked Virginia's officials to respond; after an extension, Virginia filed its brief opposing review. The docket shows no decision yet on whether the Court will hear the case.

## What each outcome would mean

The students, as petitioners, are asking the Court to take up their case now, before the Fourth Circuit rules. If the Court grants the request for certiorari before judgment, it would hear and decide the case directly, bypassing further proceedings in the lower appeals court; this would not by itself decide who wins. If the Court denies the request, the case continues in the Fourth Circuit, which would rule on the students' appeal before any Supreme Court review, and the district court's rulings against the students would remain in effect in the meantime.
