# Forecast — Loop v. Washington, No. 26-369

**State forecast from:** one distribution (for the November 13, 2026 conference), no relist, no CVSG. Both sets of respondents (the State and Attorney General; JAMS and the arbitrator) waived their response. Five amicus briefs support the petitioner.

- **Disposition:** denied, most likely on the November 16, 2026 order list after the 11/13 conference. Grant probability is about 2%.
- **Call for response / relist:** the main uncertainty. With two waivers, five amici and experienced counsel (Gene Schaerr), some Justice could ask for a response, and the petition would then be redistributed after the response comes in. I put the chance of any further distribution (a response request or a reschedule) at about 25%. If a response is requested, a denial is still the most likely result.
- **CVSG:** no. The respondents are a State and private arbitration parties, and no federal interest is at stake that would draw a call for the Solicitor General's views (<1%).
- **Question, if granted:** QP 1 (whether Rooker-Feldman bars a §1983 challenge to the procedures that led to a state judgment, as opposed to the judgment itself) is the only plausible candidate. It ties to *Exxon Mobil v. Saudi Basic* and *Skinner v. Switzer*. QP 2 (whether a State may delegate parental-rights adjudication to a private arbitrator) would probably be left aside, or reached only on remand.
- **Summary disposition:** unlikely. The likeliest summary route would be a per curiam vacatur saying the Ninth Circuit read Rooker-Feldman's "inextricably intertwined" test too broadly after *Exxon*. Conditional on a grant, about 12%.
- **Dissent from denial:** not expected (~3%). If anyone writes, Thomas or Gorsuch, out of interest in parental rights or in limiting Rooker-Feldman, is the likeliest.
