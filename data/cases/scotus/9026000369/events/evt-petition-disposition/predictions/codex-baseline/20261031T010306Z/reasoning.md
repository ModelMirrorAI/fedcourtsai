## Petition and vehicle

QP1 asks whether Rooker-Feldman bars a section 1983 action for unconstitutional procedures preceding a state judgment; QP2 challenges private adjudication of substantial parental-rights restrictions without constitutional safeguards (`questions-presented.txt`; petition pp. i, 15–21, 26–28). These are important subjects, but the appendix makes this a difficult vehicle for either.

First, the Ninth Circuit's unpublished memorandum characterizes this case as a de facto appeal and expressly declines to consider unpreserved or newly raised matters (appendix App-1–2). The petition cites cases allowing genuinely independent claims, including cases from the Ninth Circuit itself. It does not establish a clean conflict between precedential holdings on materially equivalent claims. The disagreement is substantially about how this complaint is characterized.

Second, the district court quotes the operative complaint as asking the federal court to review the Washington decision and identifies the primary requested relief as preventing enforcement of the restraining order and declaring it unconstitutional (App-6–7). That creates a concrete obstacle to the petition's claim that its injuries are wholly independent of the judgment. The district court also found immunity and failure-to-state-a-claim grounds (App-7–9). Although the appellate affirmance rests on jurisdiction, those alternative obstacles diminish the practical payoff from correcting jurisdiction alone. QP2 was not resolved on its constitutional merits below and sits behind that jurisdictional threshold.

Third, the petition's allegations of no adequate notice, no consent to these restrictions, and parental fitness are not uncontested findings establishing an ideal vehicle. The district court describes an agreement to arbitrate; the appended restraining order contains notice/opportunity-to-be-heard language and a credible-threat finding (App-3, App-11–14). These do not conclusively resolve the federal claims, but they make factual framing and preservation important. The reproduced order also expired on **September 1, 2025** (App-11), before this petition. Other parenting orders or surviving claims could preserve a live controversy, so I do not declare the entire case moot; the limited record nevertheless creates a further remedial uncertainty.

## Intervening authority and remaining claims

The petition itself discusses *T. M. v. University of Maryland Medical System Corp.*, decided **June 18, 2026**. I independently retrieved the revised opinion through CourtListener (opinion 11345156, opening syllabus). It holds that a state judgment need not have exhausted state appellate review for Rooker-Feldman to bar a federal action seeking its review and rejection. It also applies that rule to a due-process challenge seeking to invalidate and enjoin a consent order. That is adverse context for this vehicle, not an intervening reversal that obviously requires a favorable GVR. It does not eliminate the distinction between an independent injury and an injury caused by a judgment. The opinion predates both this petition and the snapshot, and is not this case's outcome.
