## 2. Case-Specific Adjustments

### A. Upward Signals (Modest)
1. **Supreme Court Counsel Appearance & Amicus Support:** Although the printed petition (`petition.txt`) was filed and signed by Petitioner John Torsten Loop *pro se*, Supreme Court practitioner Gene C. Schaerr (Schaerr | Jaffe) subsequently entered an appearance and is listed as Counsel of Record on the docket (`2026-10-31.json`), alongside five supporting amicus briefs. That level of organized amicus activity and experienced counsel involvement explains why the probability is set at `0.01` rather than near zero (`0.001`).

### B. Downward Signals (Decisive)
1. **Distributed on Waivers Without a Call for Response (CFR):** Both the state respondents (State of Washington and Attorney General Bob Ferguson, on Oct. 2, 2026) and the private arbitration respondents (JAMS and retired Judge Helen Halpert, on Oct. 8, 2026) waived their right to respond. The petition was distributed on October 28, 2026, for the November 13, 2026 Conference without the Court requesting a response. Under settled Court practice, certiorari is virtually never granted at a first conference without a brief in opposition or a prior CFR.
2. **Unpublished Two-Paragraph Ninth Circuit Memorandum Below:** The decision below (`appendix.txt`, App-1 to App-2) is a non-precedential, two-paragraph memorandum disposition (*Loop v. State of Washington, et al.*, No. 24-5020, Mar. 20, 2026) that creates no circuit split and establishes no circuit law.
3. **Foreclosure by *T. M. v. University of Maryland Medical System Corp.* (June 2026):** Just four months before this petition was distributed, the Supreme Court decided *T. M. v. University of Maryland Medical System Corp.*, 608 U.S. ___, No. 25-197 (June 18, 2026) ( retrieved via CourtListener MCP, opinion ID `11345156`). In *T. M.*, the Court reaffirmed *Rooker-Feldman* in an analogous posture where a plaintiff sued in federal district court while state appellate proceedings were pending, seeking a declaration that a state-court order violated due process and an injunction barring its enforcement. As Judge Evanson documented in the district court order below (`appendix.txt`, App-6 to App-7), Loop's operative *pro se* complaint explicitly stated that *"Mr. Loop is appealing to the US District Court to review the Washington State decision that affirmed an arbitration award issued without a proper de novo hearing"* (Dkt. No. 6 at 6), framed the issues as assignments of error in the state court's ruling, and sought an injunction barring enforcement of the restraining order and a declaration that it was unconstitutional. Far from conflicting with Supreme Court precedent, the Ninth Circuit's application of *Rooker-Feldman* sits squarely within *T. M.*'s core holding.
4. **Multiple Fatal Vehicle Flaws:**
   - **Recasting / Forfeiture:** The petition attempts to recharacterize Loop's *pro se* complaint as an "independent action" under *Skinner v. Switzer*, 562 U.S. 521 (2011), challenging state procedures rather than the state judgment itself. That framing contradicts the literal text of the operative complaint quoted by the district court (`App-6`), and the Ninth Circuit expressly declined to reach arguments raised for the first time on appeal (`App-2`, citing *Padgett v. Wright*, 587 F.3d 983, 985 n.2 (9th Cir. 2009)).
   - **Question 2 Was Never Reached Below:** Neither the district court nor the Ninth Circuit passed on the merits of Question 2 (whether the Fourteenth Amendment restricts a State from confirming a private family-law arbitration award affecting parental rights).
   - **Independent Rule 12(b)(6) Immunity Grounds:** The district court expressly dismissed the complaint on the alternative ground of failure to state a claim under Rule 12(b)(6) (`App-7` to `App-8`), holding that the State of Washington and Attorney General Ferguson enjoy absolute/sovereign immunity for judicial actions of the King County Superior Court, and that retired Judge Halpert and JAMS enjoy **arbitral immunity** under *Sacks v. Dietrich*, 663 F.3d 1065, 1070 (9th Cir. 2011) (in addition to not being state actors under 42 U.S.C. § 1983). Even if *Rooker-Feldman* did not apply, the dismissal would be affirmed on these threshold immunity and Rule 12(b)(6) grounds.
   - **Expiration of the Restraining Order (Mootness) and Preclusion:** The King County Superior Court restraining order entered on February 23, 2024 expired by its own terms on **September 1, 2025** (`appendix.txt`, App-3, App-11), and the minor child (who was 16 in February 2024, `App-11`) reached the age of majority by 2026. Any prospective declaratory or injunctive relief against the restraining order is therefore moot, while any damages claim is barred by sovereign and arbitral immunity, as well as by *res judicata* following the conclusion of Loop's parallel state appeals (`petition.txt` at ii–iii, noting the Washington Supreme Court denied review in No. 103830-5 on Sept. 3, 2025, and the Washington Court of Appeals affirmed final dissolution orders in No. 88157-4-I on Apr. 20, 2026).

## 4. Uncertainties and Limitations
The primary uncertainty is whether the five amicus briefs and Gene Schaerr's post-filing appearance as Counsel of Record could prompt a single Justice to request a response (CFR), which would trigger a subsequent distribution (`relist-increment`) after the brief in opposition is filed. Even if a CFR were issued, however, the fatal vehicle flaws documented in the district court's opinion (explicit de facto appeal language in the *pro se* complaint, sovereign and arbitral immunity under Rule 12(b)(6), expiration of the September 1, 2025 restraining order, and preclusion from the completed Washington state appeals) make an eventual grant of certiorari extraordinarily unlikely.
