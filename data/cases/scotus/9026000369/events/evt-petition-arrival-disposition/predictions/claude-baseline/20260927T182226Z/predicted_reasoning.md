# Forecast: what the Court will do with No. 26-369, Loop v. Washington

**Disposition.** Certiorari will be denied. The petition will be distributed once,
considered at a single conference, and denied in a routine order with no separate
writing. Expected timeline: the State of Washington (and the JAMS respondents) waive
the right to respond within the October 19, 2026 response window; the petition is
distributed in late October or November 2026 and denied at the first conference it
reaches, most likely in November or early December 2026.

**Relists.** The docket shows zero distributions today (the arrival moment by
definition). I expect exactly one distribution and no relist. A second
distribution would only come from a reschedule or a Justice's brief hold, and
nothing in the petition invites one.

**CVSG.** None. No federal party, no federal statute at issue beyond §1983 itself,
and no Solicitor General interest in a state domestic-relations arbitration.

**Which question, if any.** If the Court took anything, it would be QP1
(Rooker-Feldman's reach over §1983 claims attacking the process that produced a
state judgment), reformulated around the "inextricably intertwined" formulation the
lower courts used, which *Exxon Mobil* (544 U.S. at 286 n.1) disparaged. It would
not take QP2: the courts below never reached the Fourteenth Amendment delegation
question, so there is no ruling on it to review.

**Summary disposition versus plenary review.** Conditional on any grant, the route
would more likely be a GVR than argument: the decision below is an unpublished
Ninth Circuit memorandum, the petitioner is pro se, and the Court decided
*T.M. v. University of Maryland Medical System* (June 18, 2026) on Rooker-Feldman
only three months ago, so the plausible grant shape is a hold-and-GVR should some
pending Rooker-Feldman case be decided this Term. I know of no such pending case,
which is why the unconditional grant probability is so low.

**Dissent from denial.** None expected. A statement respecting denial from a
Justice interested in parental-rights procedure is conceivable but very unlikely on
a pro se, jurisdiction-dismissed vehicle.

**Conditional merits note.** If the Court somehow granted plenary review on QP1, the
likely outcome would be a narrow reaffirmation of *Exxon Mobil* and *Skinner*: a
§1983 claim alleging injury from an adverse party's conduct or a defective process
is not a "de facto appeal" merely because it overlaps with a state judgment. That
forecast is conditional only; a denial produces no opinion.
