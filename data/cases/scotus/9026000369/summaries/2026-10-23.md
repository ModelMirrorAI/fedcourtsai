## In brief

A father asks the Court to revive his federal lawsuit challenging a restraining order that an arbitrator imposed during his divorce case.

## What happened

John Torsten Loop and his then-wife arbitrated their divorce. The arbitrator, a retired judge working for JAMS, issued an award that included a restraining order keeping Loop away from his ex-wife and daughter. A state court entered that order over Loop's objection, and Washington's appellate courts upheld it. Loop separately sued the State of Washington, its Attorney General, JAMS, and the arbitrator in federal court, arguing the process violated his constitutional rights as a parent. The district court dismissed the suit for lack of jurisdiction, reasoning it was really an improper appeal of the state court's decision. The Ninth Circuit affirmed, and rehearing was denied.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked two things. First, whether a legal rule that blocks federal courts from reviewing state-court judgments also blocks a separate lawsuit claiming that the procedures leading up to that judgment, not the judgment itself, violated the Constitution. Second, whether a state may let a private arbitrator decide, without added safeguards, a matter that significantly limits a fit parent's relationship with a child.

## Where it stands

The petition was filed in September 2026, and the state respondents and the JAMS respondents each waived their right to respond. Several outside groups have since filed amicus briefs supporting Loop. The docket does not show that the Court has acted on the petition.

## What each outcome would mean

Loop, who lost in the lower courts, is asking the Court to take up his case. If the Court grants the petition, it will hear full arguments and decide whether his lawsuit can proceed and whether the arbitration process violated his constitutional rights; granting review decides nothing by itself. If the Court denies the petition, the Ninth Circuit's ruling stands, leaving Loop's federal lawsuit dismissed and the restraining order's underlying process unreviewed in federal court.
