## In brief

A father asks the Court to revive his civil-rights lawsuit over a family-court arbitration process that restricted his contact with his child.

## What happened

John Torsten Loop and his former spouse were ordered into arbitration during their divorce. The arbitrator issued a restraining order ending Loop's contact with his child, which a Washington state court confirmed; Washington's appellate courts affirmed that result. Loop separately sued the State of Washington and others in federal court, arguing the process violated his constitutional parental rights. The district court dismissed the suit, holding it was really an improper attempt to appeal the state-court judgment, a result barred by a rule called the Rooker-Feldman doctrine. The Ninth Circuit affirmed, and Loop, representing himself, now petitions the Supreme Court.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked two things: whether the rule barring federal lawsuits that amount to appeals of state-court judgments also blocks a lawsuit claiming the procedures leading up to that judgment were themselves unconstitutional, and whether a state can let a private arbitrator decide an issue as significant as a parent's contact with a child without the procedural protections the Constitution would otherwise require.

## Where it stands

## What each outcome would mean

Loop, the petitioner, is asking the Court to take up his case. If the Court grants review, it would hear full arguments and decide the two legal questions, deciding nothing about who wins until it rules. If the Court denies review, the Ninth Circuit's decision stands, leaving Loop's federal lawsuit dismissed and the state-court arbitration result undisturbed. A denial would not mean the Court agrees with the lower court's reasoning, only that the case ends there.
