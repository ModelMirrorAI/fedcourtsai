## What happened

John Torsten Loop and his former spouse were ordered by a Washington state court to arbitrate their divorce case. Loop says he was never told the arbitration would decide whether he could keep contact with his child, and that he was never found unfit as a parent. The arbitrator entered a restraining order cutting off Loop's contact with his child, and state courts confirmed and enforced it. Loop then sued the State of Washington, the arbitration provider JAMS, the arbitrator, and the state attorney general in federal court, arguing this violated his constitutional rights as a parent. A federal district court dismissed his suit, and the Court of Appeals for the Ninth Circuit affirmed, both ruling that hearing the case would amount to reviewing the state court's judgment, which federal trial courts are not allowed to do.

## What the Court is being asked

The case raises two questions. First, whether a legal rule that blocks federal lawsuits from second-guessing state court judgments also blocks a lawsuit like Loop's, which he says targets the flawed process that led to the judgment rather than the judgment itself. Second, whether the Constitution allows a state to hand off decisions that seriously affect a fit parent's relationship with their child to a private arbitrator, without first providing the procedural protections the Constitution normally requires when the state interferes with that relationship.
