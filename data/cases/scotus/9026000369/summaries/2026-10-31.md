## In brief

A father asks the Court to revive his federal lawsuit challenging a restraining order that an arbitrator and state courts imposed limiting his contact with his child.

## What happened

John Torsten Loop and his then-wife were ordered to arbitrate their divorce. The arbitrator, a retired judge working with JAMS, issued an award that included a restraining order keeping Loop away from his daughter for over two years; a state court entered that order over his objection, and Washington's appellate courts upheld it. Loop then sued the State of Washington, its Attorney General, JAMS, and the arbitrator in federal court, claiming the process violated his constitutional rights. The district court dismissed the suit for lack of jurisdiction under a doctrine that bars federal courts from reviewing matters that amount to appeals of state-court judgments, and the Ninth Circuit affirmed.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked two questions. First, whether that jurisdictional bar should block a federal civil-rights lawsuit when the claimed injury comes from flawed procedures leading up to a state-court judgment, rather than from the judgment itself. Second, whether a state may let a private arbitrator decide an issue that substantially affects a fit parent's rights to their child without the procedural protections the Constitution otherwise requires before the state interferes with that relationship.

## Where it stands

The petition and supporting amicus briefs have been filed, both sets of respondents waived their right to respond, and the case was distributed for the Court's November 13, 2026 conference; the docket does not yet show any action taken at that conference.

## What each outcome would mean

Loop, who lost in the lower courts, is asking the Court to take up his case. If the Court grants review, it will hear full arguments and decide the two questions itself, without yet deciding who wins. If it denies review, the Ninth Circuit's ruling stands, meaning Loop's lawsuit remains dismissed and the restraining order process is not reopened to federal constitutional review.
