# Predicted reasoning — Anderson v. Intel Corp. Investment Policy Committee, No. 25-498 (merits, forecast at grant)

**Judgment class: vacated.** The Court will disturb the Ninth Circuit's judgment —
most likely a vacatur with remand, in the mold of *Hughes v. Northwestern*: the
majority will reject a categorical "meaningful benchmark" pleading requirement for
ERISA duty-of-prudence claims predicated on fund underperformance, hold that the
inquiry is context-specific under *Dudenhoeffer* and *Hughes*, and remand for the
Ninth Circuit to reassess the complaint holistically rather than itself declaring
the complaint sufficient. Outright reversal (holding the complaint states a claim)
is the less likely form of disturbance; the Court in this line of cases corrects
the standard and remands. The main undisturbed routes are (a) affirmance on the
theory Intel's merits brief presses — that the Ninth Circuit imposed no categorical
rule, only ordinary *Twombly*/*Iqbal* plausibility for claims resting *solely* on
underperformance (Judge Berzon's concurrence below gives the Court a narrowing
handle) — which the Solicitor General and the Department of Labor support as amici
for respondents; and (b) a DIG if argument reveals the parties largely agree on the
legal standard and dispute only its fact-bound application here (the *NVIDIA* /
*Facebook* pattern in securities pleading cases). I put roughly 0.60 on
vacate/reverse, 0.30 on affirm, 0.07 on DIG, and the remainder on a mixed judgment.

**Vote lineup.** Conditional on vacatur, expect a lopsided or unanimous Court —
*Hughes* was 8–0 and *Cunningham v. Cornell* 9–0 in the same genre of
circuit-made ERISA pleading screens — likely with one or more separate
concurrences (Alito or Kavanaugh) stressing that comparator allegations remain
the ordinary route to plausibility and that district courts retain tools against
meritless suits, echoing the *Cunningham* concurrence. Conditional on affirmance,
expect a 6–3 split with Sotomayor, Kagan, and Jackson dissenting. My stated
per-Justice votes reflect the modal (vacatur) scenario: all nine in the majority.
Authorship is unscored; my guess is Kagan, who wrote *Hughes*.

**Ground and breadth.** The majority will rest on the anti-categorical logic of
*Dudenhoeffer*/*Hughes*: §1104(a)(1)(B)'s "like capacity ... like aims" language
states the fiduciary's standard of conduct, not a pleading element, and a
meaningful benchmark is one way — not the only way — to plead an inference of
flawed process. The rule announced will be transsubstantive across
underperformance claims (not limited to Intel's alternative-asset target-date
funds), but operationally modest: the Court will preserve that a complaint
resting on nothing but relative underperformance may still flunk plausibility
without a sound basis for comparison, which is how it will accommodate the SG's
and respondents' settlement-pressure concerns without adopting their rule.

**Which question the Court reaches.** The QP as granted (petitioners' framing).
The Court will decide only the pleading-element question; it will not resolve
whether these petitioners' pleaded comparators (retail target-date funds against
Intel's hedge-fund/private-equity-tilted custom funds) were in fact meaningful —
that goes back on remand.

**Timing** (unscored, stated for discipline): argued October 6, 2026 (first day of
the October 2026 sitting); decision most likely between January and April 2027,
earlier if unanimous.
