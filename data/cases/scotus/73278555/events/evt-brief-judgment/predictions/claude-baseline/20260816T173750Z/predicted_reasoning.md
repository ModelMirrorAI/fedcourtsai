# Predicted reasoning — Anderson v. Intel Corp. Investment Policy Committee, No. 25-498 (evt-brief-judgment)

Forecast of what the Court will do with the judgment below (Ninth Circuit,
No. 22-16268, affirming Rule 12(b)(6) dismissal of ERISA duty-of-prudence
claims), made after full merits briefing, before the October 6, 2026 argument.

## Judgment class

**Vacated.** The Court will reject the Ninth Circuit's "meaningful benchmark"
requirement as a categorical pleading element for underperformance-based
imprudence claims and remand for a holistic *Twombly*/*Iqbal* plausibility
assessment. This is the *Hughes v. Northwestern* template applied to the next
circuit-crafted screen: the Court granted the plaintiffs' petition — framed
squarely in *Hughes*'s "categorical rules are inconsistent with the
context-specific inquiry ERISA requires" language — over a BIO whose lead
argument was that no split exists and percolation should continue. It did not
take the pending defense-side vehicle presenting the same question
(*Parker-Hannifin Corp. v. Johnson*, No. 24-1030, CVSG'd and still held,
presumably for this case). The likeliest opinion rejects "meaningful benchmark"
as a magic-words prerequisite while emphasizing that comparator quality remains
highly probative and that apples-to-oranges comparisons may still fail
plausibility review — a both-ways opinion in the *Dudenhoeffer*/*Hughes* mold —
and remands rather than deciding whether this complaint survives.

The main alternative is an **affirmance** (~1 in 3): the United States (SG,
aligned with DOL) filed at the merits stage supporting respondents and urging
affirmance, will argue as amicus with divided argument time, and the Court could
hold that on these allegations — custom risk-mitigation funds compared only to
equity-heavy retail indices — no plausible inference of flawed process arises,
however the "benchmark" label is understood. An outright **reversal** (holding
the complaint states a claim) is unlikely (~1 in 12); the Court almost never
does the district court's plausibility work itself in this line. A DIG or
equally divided affirmance is negligible: the QP is clean, the vehicle was
vetted at the cert stage, and a full nine-member bench heard no recusal issue
(Kagan handled the extension application without incident).

## Vote lineup

**Unanimous or near-unanimous, all nine in the majority.** Every modern case in
this line — *Dudenhoeffer* (9-0, vacated), *Tibble* (9-0, vacated), *Hughes*
(8-0, vacated), *Intel v. Sulyma* itself (9-0, against Intel, in this same
litigation), *Cunningham v. Cornell* (9-0, reversed) — was unanimous, whichever
way the pleading-policy winds blew, because the Court prefers narrow
standard-clarifying opinions here. Expect one or two separate concurrences
(most plausibly Alito or Kavanaugh, as in *Cunningham*) stressing that district
courts retain robust screening tools against in terrorem ERISA suits; a lone
dissent from a Justice who would affirm outright (Alito or Thomas) is possible
but not expected. Authorship is unscored context: Sotomayor is the Court's
repeat ERISA-pleading author (*Hughes*, *Cunningham*) and is my modal pick.

## Question taken, route, and timing

The Court will decide the QP as written — whether underperformance-predicated
prudence claims require pleading a "meaningful benchmark" — and will not reach
the fact-bound question whether petitioners' own comparators sufficed
(respondents concede review was not granted on it). No summary route is
available at this stage; the case was argued in the ordinary course. Argued
October 6, 2026 (the Term's first sitting), the decision should land between
January and April 2027 — first-sitting cases with lopsided votes typically come
down early; a decision after May 2027 would itself be evidence of a closer,
affirm-leaning split than I forecast. On the remand I expect the Ninth Circuit
to reapply the clarified standard and quite possibly dismiss again — the
disturbance I forecast is about the standard, not petitioners' ultimate
recovery. *Parker-Hannifin* (No. 24-1030) will be GVR'd or denied within weeks
of the decision.
