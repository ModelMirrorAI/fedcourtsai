The Court is likely to vacate the Ninth Circuit's decision. Consistent with its approach in *Hughes v. Northwestern University* and *Fifth Third Bancorp v. Dudenhoeffer*, the Court will likely reject the Ninth Circuit's categorical rule that a "meaningful benchmark" must always be pleaded to state an ERISA duty-of-prudence claim. However, aligning with the Solicitor General's amicus brief, the Court will likely hold that where a plaintiff's claim of imprudence rests entirely on a fund's relative underperformance, the plaintiff must identify a suitable comparator to make the claim plausible. Because the Ninth Circuit applied a rigid, categorical rule rather than a context-specific inquiry, the Court will vacate the judgment and remand for the lower courts to evaluate the complaint under the correct standard. Expect a unanimous or near-unanimous opinion, likely authored by Justice Kagan or Justice Sotomayor, emphasizing that ERISA pleading standards remain context-dependent.
