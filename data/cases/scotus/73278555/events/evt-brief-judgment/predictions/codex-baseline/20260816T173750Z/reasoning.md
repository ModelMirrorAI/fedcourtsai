# Rationale

I assign a 0.62 probability that the Court disturbs the judgment below and predict a vacatur rather than a merits reversal. The point forecast is above 0.5 because petitioners' proposed disposition is a remand for ordinary Rule 8 analysis, a restrained remedy that fits the Court's unanimous instructions in Dudenhoeffer and Hughes to apply context-sensitive pleading review. Petitioners also have a concrete methodological objection: the Ninth Circuit required a qualifying comparator whenever performance allegations supported the claim even "in part," although the complaint also alleged unusual allocations, contemporaneous warnings, high fees, and failures to react over time. A majority can reject that threshold rule without deciding whether Intel acted imprudently.

Numeric pool-guard exclusions total 57, and the 515 parsed judgments exceed the 30-judgment minimum. This rate covers only the parsed slice, though the high coverage reduces the concern that the anchor is driven by unresolved recent cases.

I adjust below that anchor because Intel's narrower description of the rule is substantial: relative performance may be uninformative unless the compared funds share materially similar objectives and risk. The United States supports affirmance and argues that this is ordinary Twombly/Iqbal analysis rather than a categorical ERISA rule. Intel also stresses that its funds disclosed a volatility-reduction objective, the GDF exceeded its stated five-percent real-return target, and petitioners did not compare the correct customized benchmarks or identify defects in the particular alternative investments. Those points make a 5-4 or 6-3 affirmance quite plausible.
