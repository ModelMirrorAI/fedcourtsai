# Why P(disturbed) = 0.10

**Inputs read.** I read the provisioned snapshot `2026-10-07.json`, `context.json` (mode `forward`, cutoff 2026-10-07) and the questions-presented text. I read the full argument transcript, which is the evidence this moment adds. I also read `argument-features.json` (coverage: 131 of 132 Justice turns attributed, 4 of 4 segments) and the cover and table of contents of the United States' merits amicus brief, which **supports respondents**. I did not read the parties' merits briefs, replies, the petition, the BIO or the appendix in full. My view of their positions comes from how counsel and the Justices described them at argument. A reader should discount the doctrinal detail accordingly. I did no corpus query and no CourtListener or web retrieval.

**Anchor.** I used the statpack's merits section, which publishes an `excluded` count. The grant was on 2026-01-16, so the grant Term is OT2025. The ten-Term window is OT2015–OT2024. The table renders OT2017–OT2024; OT2015 and OT2016 have no parsed rows. Pooled, that is 378 disturbed of 537 parsed, or **70.4%**. Coverage is near-complete in those Terms (e.g. OT2024 76/79 parsed, OT2023 56/56). This clears the 30-judgment floor and is the bar I am scored against.

**Adjustment, large and downward.** The argument was one-sided:
- The petitioners' counsel conceded that underperformance alone is not a violation. They also conceded that performance allegations "inherently" require some comparison. That largely concedes the question presented and moves the fight to how the Ninth Circuit applied it.
- Gorsuch accused the petitioners of shifting ground away from their own question presented. Alito, Kavanaugh and Thomas were plainly skeptical.
- The petitioners' expected allies did not help them. Kagan described the decision below as reasonable and asked the SG only for refinements "consistent with" affirmance. Jackson read the Ninth Circuit as having considered the allocation theory. Sotomayor said she agreed with respondents that the Ninth Circuit did not require a matching asset allocation.
- The United States supports respondents. Respondents conceded that comparators need not be identical, which removes the main reason to vacate.
- `argument-features.json` agrees with my reading. Justice turns while the petitioners held the lectern: 84 turns / 1,994 words. While respondents' side argued: 47 turns / 1,311 words. That fits the usual heavier questioning of the losing side, but it is a count and not a vote. I treat questions as leans, not votes.

**Residual risk (about 0.10).** The main route to "disturbed" is a vacatur. The Court could announce a refined "meaningful" standard (similar aims, allocation not required) and remand for the lower courts to apply it, rather than affirming outright. Kagan's interest in guidance makes this live, and I put it at about 7–8%. Outright reversal is about 2%. A DIG is about 3–4%, given Gorsuch's complaint that the petitioners moved off their question presented, but a DIG leaves the judgment standing, so it sits in the complement.

**Votes.** All nine are forecast in the majority. I took the per-Justice votes section as background only. OT2024 and earlier are the strictly-prior Terms, and every Justice disturbed about 58–77% of the time there. The transcript outweighs that history here.

**Authorship.** The October-sitting assignments are unknown. My lifts for Sotomayor and Kagan are judgment calls, and the distribution stays close to flat.
