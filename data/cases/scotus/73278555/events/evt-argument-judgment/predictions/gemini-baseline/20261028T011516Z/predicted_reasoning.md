# Predicted Court Reasoning: *Anderson v. Intel Corporation Investment Policy Committee*, No. 25-498

## 1. Judgment and Vote Lineup

The Supreme Court will **affirm** (`affirmed`) the judgment of the Ninth Circuit in a **9–0** decision joining all nine Justices (`Roberts`, `Thomas`, `Alito`, `Sotomayor`, `Kagan`, `Gorsuch`, `Kavanaugh`, `Barrett`, and `Jackson`) in the judgment. Neither a dismissal as improvidently granted (`dismissed-as-improvidently-granted`) nor an equally divided affirmance (`affirmed-by-an-equally-divided-court`) is live: all nine Justices participated actively at oral argument, the Question Presented squarely resolves an acknowledged circuit conflict with *Johnson v. Parker-Hannifin Corp.*, 122 F.4th 205 (6th Cir. 2024), and no vehicle barrier prevents the Court from reaching and affirming the Ninth Circuit's holding.

## 2. Question Presented Reached and Doctrinal Ground (`majority-ground`)

The Court will reach the single Question Presented directly and answer it in the affirmative. Grounding its holding in the text of ERISA's duty of prudence, 29 U.S.C. § 1104(a)(1)(B)—which requires a fiduciary to act with the care, skill, prudence, and diligence that a prudent person would use "in the conduct of an enterprise of a like character and with like aims" (reinforced by the Department of Labor's longstanding investment-duties regulation, 29 C.F.R. § 2550.404a-1(b)(2)(i), evaluating an investment against "reasonably available alternatives with similar risks")—and in the general pleading standard of *Bell Atlantic Corp. v. Twombly*, 550 U.S. 544 (2007), *Ashcroft v. Iqbal*, 556 U.S. 662 (2009), *Fifth Third Bancorp v. Dudenhoeffer*, 573 U.S. 409 (2014), and *Hughes v. Northwestern University*, 595 U.S. 170 (2022), the Court will hold that when an ERISA plaintiff seeks to infer an imprudent fiduciary process from circumstantial evidence of a fund's investment underperformance, the complaint must identify a "meaningful benchmark."

Specifically, the majority opinion will explain that ERISA's duty of prudence measures fiduciary conduct and process at the time of the investment decision, not hindsight investment results. Because funds with different objectives and risk tolerances—such as a fund designed to mitigate downside volatility during market downturns versus an equity-heavy fund designed to maximize returns during a bull market—will naturally perform differently in any given market environment, comparing the returns of two funds with different aims and risk profiles is an "apples-to-oranges" comparison that fails to rule out the obvious, lawful alternative explanation for the return gap. Without a comparator sharing similar aims and a similar risk-return profile, an allegation of relative underperformance adds no plausible inference of imprudence under Rule 8.

## 3. Breadth of the Holding (`ground-breadth`)

The Court will announce a general pleading rule governing ERISA underperformance allegations nationwide (disapproving the Sixth Circuit's contrary approach in *Parker-Hannifin*), while carefully bounding its holding in two respects discussed extensively at oral argument:

1. **Similar Aims and Risk Profile, Not an Identical "Twin" or Identical Asset Allocation**:
   The Court will clarify that "meaningful benchmark" is not a heightened pleading standard beyond *Twombly* and *Iqbal*, and does not require a plaintiff to identify an identical "twin" fund or a comparator with the exact same underlying asset allocation. Rather, a meaningful benchmark must share similar investment aims and a similar risk-return profile—and when a plaintiff challenges a fund's asset allocation as the source of imprudent underperformance, a comparator that shares the same risk-mitigation or return objective and risk profile but employs a different asset allocation can serve as a valid benchmark.
2. **Non-Comparative / Direct Process and Strategy Allegations**:
   Consistent with Judge Berzon's concurrence below, the Court will note that a performance benchmark is required only insofar as a plaintiff relies on comparative underperformance to support an inference of imprudence; a plaintiff may still state an ERISA imprudence claim without a performance benchmark through direct allegations of a flawed fiduciary process, self-dealing, or an objectively imprudent investment strategy. However, the Court will conclude that the Ninth Circuit properly evaluated Petitioners' complaint here: Petitioners' proposed benchmarks (equity-heavy retail funds, Morningstar category averages, and Intel's own composite indices) did not share the Intel funds' disclosed downside-risk-mitigation objective and risk profile, and the Ninth Circuit separately and correctly concluded that Petitioners' non-performance allegations challenging the use of hedge funds and private equity failed to state a plausible claim on their own terms.

## 4. Opinion Authorship (`majority-author` and `separate-writers`)

- **Majority Opinion Author (`majority-author`)**:
  Chief Justice Roberts will assign the majority opinion, most likely to **Justice Barrett** (`0.215`) or **Justice Kagan** (`0.195`), with **Justice Kavanaugh** (`0.140`), **Justice Gorsuch** (`0.110`), **Chief Justice Roberts** (`0.100`), and **Justice Sotomayor** (`0.090`) as secondary possibilities. At oral argument, both Justice Barrett and Justice Kagan focused directly on how to formulate the doctrinal rule in the majority opinion: Justice Barrett tested language anchoring "meaningful benchmark" in statutory "like aims and strategies" where "like" means similar rather than an identical twin, while Justice Kagan pressed Respondents and the Solicitor General on how the opinion should affirm the Ninth Circuit while making clear that a meaningful comparator requires the same general approach and risk/reward profile rather than identical asset allocations.
- **Separate Writings (`separate-writers`)**:
  Although the Court is likely to be unanimous on the judgment and core opinion, there is a moderate probability (~18–20% each) of a brief concurrence from **Justice Jackson** (`0.20`) (emphasizing that holistic or plan-level asset-allocation challenges remain viable without a fund-by-fund performance comparator), **Justice Kavanaugh** (`0.18`), or **Justice Gorsuch** (`0.18`) (highlighting the broader question noted by Judge Murphy in *Parker-Hannifin*—whether hindsight underperformance alone can ever establish an imprudent fiduciary process—which the majority opinion will leave for another day).
