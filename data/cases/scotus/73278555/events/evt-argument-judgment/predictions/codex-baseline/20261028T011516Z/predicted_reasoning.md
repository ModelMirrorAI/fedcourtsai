# Forecast of the Court's judgment and reasoning

## Judgment and lineup

The Court will **affirm** the Ninth Circuit's judgment dismissing the prudence claim. The predicted lineup is **9-0**: Roberts, Thomas, Alito, Sotomayor, Kagan, Gorsuch, Kavanaugh, Barrett, and Jackson join the judgment and its central rationale. Kagan will deliver the opinion of the Court. The author forecast is considerably less certain than the predicted disposition; Barrett and Kavanaugh are the strongest alternative assignments.

No particular separate opinion is the central forecast. Kavanaugh is the most likely separate writer, addressing the limited relevance of investment performance to prudence. Gorsuch could emphasize reserving the broader question whether underperformance alone ever suffices; Jackson could stress preserving the whole-complaint inquiry. Those are possible concurrences, not predicted dissents from the result.

## Majority ground

The Court will characterize a meaningful comparison as an application of ordinary Twombly/Iqbal plausibility to an underperformance-based inference, not a heightened ERISA pleading rule. ERISA requires a prudent decision-making process under the circumstances at the time; it does not guarantee that a fund will outperform funds pursuing different investment objectives. Different returns between differently situated funds do not themselves make a deficient fiduciary process plausible.

The majority will therefore reject petitioners' request to abolish the meaningful-benchmark requirement. It will treat the Ninth Circuit's rejection of these comparative allegations as compatible with its consideration of the other allegations, rather than requiring remand to aggregate allegations the lower court already examined. The affirmance will not adjudicate Intel's ultimate prudence on a developed evidentiary record.

## Breadth and limits

The opinion will give guidance beyond Intel's specific funds but will confine the comparator requirement to inferences that depend on relative performance. It will not require every plaintiff alleging an imprudent process or strategy to identify a benchmark. Direct process allegations or other facts supporting imprudence can state a claim without comparative returns.

Meaningful does not mean identical. The opinion will permit different allocations or implementation methods when a comparison remains probative in light of the investment objectives and risks relevant to the pleaded theory. It will not demand matching exposure to the very asset class whose over-allocation is challenged, and it will not turn objectives, risk, strategy, and allocations into a rigid cumulative checklist. Conversely, ordinary plausibility will not be satisfied simply by identifying any fund with a better historical return.

The Court will reserve whether underperformance, even with a sound comparator, can by itself establish a plausible prudence claim. It will not decide that hedge funds or private equity are categorically prudent or imprudent retirement investments, and it will not use this case to replace Hughes or Dudenhoeffer with a universal safe harbor for fiduciaries.

## Alternative route

The principal alternative is **vacatur and remand** for a genuinely holistic assessment if the Court regards the Ninth Circuit's treatment of comparative allegations as an improper separate-sufficiency requirement. That would not itself establish a valid claim or liability. A DIG is a small undisturbed alternative if the Court concludes the argument has drifted from the question granted. Neither is the central forecast, and no recusal-based equally divided outcome is indicated by the provisioned record.
