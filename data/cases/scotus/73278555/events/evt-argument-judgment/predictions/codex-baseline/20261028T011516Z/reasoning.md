## Why affirmance is the central forecast

Petitioners' strongest point is methodological. Their opening brief describes allegedly unusual allocations, expense, illiquidity, and performance as mutually reinforcing indicators of an imprudent process, rather than a per se prohibition on alternative assets (`merits-brief-petitioner.txt`, introduction and argument concerning holistic pleading; `merits-reply-petitioner.txt`, opening argument). If a comparator prerequisite removes otherwise relevant performance facts before considering the complaint as a whole, Hughes and ordinary pleading principles provide a plausible route to vacatur without deciding that the complaint succeeds.

Intel instead argues that a return comparison must control for relevant differences in investment objectives before it can support an inference about imprudent process. It says materially identical assets are unnecessary and that the nonperformance allegations independently fail (`merits-brief-respondent.txt`, introduction and discussion of the decision below). The United States expressly supports affirmance, similarly emphasizes process rather than realized returns, and disputes petitioners' treatment of Intel's disclosed benchmarks (`merits-amicus-united-states.txt`, summary of argument). I follow the government's proposed result and contextual comparison framework, not a categorical rule requiring comparators in every prudence suit. Its presence is substantive support, not a counted cert-stage CVSG signal.

The argument makes that defense more persuasive. Kagan rejects the portrayal of the Ninth Circuit as demanding a twin fund and later proposes guidance distinguishing meaningful similarity from identical allocations. Sotomayor questions the lack of comparators pursuing the asserted long-term risk objective and expressly agrees the Ninth Circuit did not demand identical allocations. Jackson tests the claimed exclusion of strategy allegations against what the lower court actually discussed; her questions are not a clear endorsement of petitioners' holistic-pleading theory. Roberts distinguishes a dispute about applying a rule from a basis to invalidate it. Thomas, Alito, Gorsuch, Barrett, and Kavanaugh each probe why unlike performance establishes anything about prudence or how petitioners' broader strategy theory fits the question granted. These are qualitative readings of the exchanges, not votes inferred mechanically from speaking volume.

The most consequential residual risk is the government's concession that the Ninth Circuit evaluated the comparative allegations first and then the remaining allegations. Petitioners highlight precisely that sequencing in rebuttal. A Court concerned that the lower court demanded separate sufficiency could vacate for integrated reconsideration. I assign that risk most of the disturbed mass. But no Justice expressly adopts that remedy in the transcript, and several defend the Ninth Circuit's contextual analysis. A broader rejection of meaningful comparisons is less likely still.
