## In brief

Former Intel employees ask the Court to decide what retirement-plan participants must allege to claim their plan's fiduciaries invested imprudently.

## What happened

Former Intel employees who participated in Intel's retirement plans sued the plans' fiduciaries, claiming they violated a federal pension law, ERISA, by investing heavily in hedge funds and private equity starting after the 2008 financial crisis. The employees said these investments underperformed and cost more than comparable options, breaching the fiduciaries' duty to invest prudently. The fiduciaries countered that they adopted a deliberate risk-reduction strategy, fully disclosed to participants, that performed as intended. The district court dismissed the case twice, and the Ninth Circuit affirmed, holding that claims based on comparing an investment's performance to other funds require identifying a benchmark fund with similar goals and risks. The employees appealed to the Supreme Court.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked whether employees claiming a retirement-plan fiduciary invested imprudently, based on an investment underperforming other options, must identify a comparably similar fund as a point of comparison in order for their lawsuit to proceed, or whether courts should instead weigh all the allegations in a complaint together without requiring that specific kind of comparison.

## Where it stands

The Court granted review, and the case was argued on October 6, 2026. It now awaits decision.

## What each outcome would mean

The employees who sued are asking the Court to overturn the Ninth Circuit's ruling, which upheld dismissal of their lawsuit against Intel's plan fiduciaries. If the Court affirms, the dismissal stands and the employees' claims end, with lower courts continuing to require a comparable benchmark fund in similar cases. If the Court reverses, the case would likely return to the lower courts for them to reconsider the employees' allegations under a different standard, without deciding whether the fiduciaries actually acted imprudently. If the Court vacates the ruling, the lower court would reconsider the case without a predetermined outcome for either side.
