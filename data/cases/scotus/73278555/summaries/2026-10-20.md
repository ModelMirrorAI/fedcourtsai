## In brief

Former Intel employees ask the Court to decide what retirement-plan participants must allege to show a pension fund manager invested their money imprudently.

## What happened

Two former Intel employees sued the committees and individuals who managed Intel's retirement plans, claiming they violated a federal pension law by investing heavily in hedge funds and private equity starting after the 2008 financial crisis. The employees said this strategy underperformed other retirement funds and cost participants money. The fiduciaries countered that they adopted the strategy deliberately to reduce risk, disclosed this to participants, and that the funds performed as intended. The trial court dismissed the case, and the U.S. Court of Appeals for the Ninth Circuit affirmed, ruling that the employees had not identified a sufficiently similar comparison fund to support their claim that the investments were managed imprudently.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked whether employees suing over a retirement plan's poor investment performance must identify a comparably similar investment fund that performed better, in order for their claim to proceed, or whether other kinds of allegations can support such a claim without that specific comparison.

## Where it stands

The Court granted review of the case, and it was argued on October 6, 2026. The case now awaits a decision on the merits.

## What each outcome would mean

The employees, Anderson and the other petitioners, asked the Court to take up the case after the lower court ruled against them. If the Court rules for the employees, it would set aside the Ninth Circuit's decision and send the case back, without deciding for either side, for the lower courts to reconsider the employees' claims under a different legal standard. If the Court rules for the Intel fiduciaries, the Ninth Circuit's dismissal of the employees' lawsuit would stand, leaving them unable to pursue these claims further.
