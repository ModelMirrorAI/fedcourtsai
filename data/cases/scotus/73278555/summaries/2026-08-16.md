## In brief

Retirement plan participants ask the Court to decide what a lawsuit must allege to claim a fiduciary imprudently invested workers' retirement savings.

## What happened

Winston Anderson and other former Intel employees sued the committees and individuals who managed two Intel retirement plans, claiming they breached their duty under a federal pension law to invest prudently. The plans had shifted heavily into hedge funds and private equity after 2008, a strategy the petition says underperformed comparable funds while charging higher fees. The district court dismissed the claims, and the Ninth Circuit affirmed, holding that a claim based on a fund's underperformance must identify a comparable "benchmark" fund with similar goals and risks. The plans' fiduciaries defend that requirement as consistent with the law's text and this Court's precedent.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked whether a lawsuit claiming a retirement plan manager invested imprudently, based on a fund's underperformance, must point to a comparable fund with similar goals and risks as a basis for comparison, or whether other kinds of allegations can support such a claim without one.

## Where it stands

The Court granted the petition in January 2026. The case has been fully briefed, argument is set for October 6, 2026, and the government has asked to participate in that argument supporting one side.

## What each outcome would mean

Anderson and the other former employees, as petitioners, asked the Court to take up the case after losing below. If the Court rules for Anderson, plans challenging fund performance could proceed without pointing to a closely comparable fund, potentially reviving this suit. If the Court rules for the Intel committees, the requirement to identify a comparable benchmark fund would stand, and dismissal of these claims would likely be affirmed. The Court could also send the case back for further proceedings without deciding the question either way.
