## What happened

Winston Anderson and other Intel employees sued the committees and individuals who managed two Intel retirement plans, claiming they violated a federal pension law by investing plan money in hedge funds and private equity in a way that lost participants money compared to more stock-heavy fund options. The plan managers said they were pursuing a deliberate risk-reduction strategy, adopted after the 2008 crash, that they had disclosed to employees. The trial court dismissed the claims, ruling the employees had not compared the funds to any truly similar alternative investments. The United States Court of Appeals for the Ninth Circuit agreed and upheld the dismissal.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked whether employees who claim a retirement plan manager acted carelessly by choosing funds that performed worse than other investments must, to keep their lawsuit alive, point to a genuinely comparable investment option as a basis for that comparison, or whether they can proceed without identifying such a comparison.

## Where it stands

The Court granted review in January 2026 after the case was distributed for more than one conference. The parties have since filed merits briefs and numerous outside groups have submitted supporting briefs, including one from the United States government. The case has been scheduled for oral argument on October 6, 2026, and awaits that argument.
