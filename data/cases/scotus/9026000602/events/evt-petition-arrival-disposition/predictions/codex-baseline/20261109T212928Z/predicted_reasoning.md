# Forecast of the Court's action

The Court will deny certiorari in an ordinary order without an explanatory opinion. It will not resolve the merits of the bankruptcy or fraud allegations. The petition will receive its first conference distribution after the response period or any associated response process, then will be denied without a second conference distribution. A conference in early 2027 is more likely than immediate action, although the response deadline can move.

The starting record has **zero distributions, zero relists, and no CVSG**. The declared `relist-increment` probability is **98.5%**: from this state, even the first distribution counts as an increment. The modal trajectory is one additional distribution in total, not a true relist. I assign roughly 3% to a second distribution. The Court will not seek the Solicitor General's views; the probability of a new CVSG is **0.2%**.

If review is granted, the most plausible narrowed question is whether a debtor-creditor's unscheduled state-court judgment remains estate property such that postpetition prosecution or enforcement requires trustee authority or implicates section 362(a)(3). That is a more plausible focal issue than the petition's premise that a debtor's discharge extinguishes obligations owed to him by nondebtor defendants. The Court would not take all four questions as a package of bankruptcy, fraud, and due-process accusations.

Conditional on a grant, I assign **35%** to disposition in the cert order itself, including a GVR or summary reversal, and **65%** to plenary review. No specific intervening decision in the available record makes a GVR the expected route. The unconditional forecast remains denial, not summary relief.

Conditional on denial, the probability that any Justice notes a dissent or statement respecting denial is **1%**. The forecast is no such writing. No individual Justice is predicted to write; the small independent author probabilities are recorded in `prediction.json`, not predictions of private cert votes.
