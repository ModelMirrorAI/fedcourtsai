2. **The debtor is the judgment creditor, not these petitioners.** The original judgment in Appendix F identifies Keita and Tompkins as tenant-plaintiffs and the Gnanams as landlord-defendants. The petition says Keita filed bankruptcy on March 28, 2025, after the August 2024 judgments. Sections 362(a) and 524(a), reproduced in the appendix, require that distinction. Petitioners do raise a more substantial, separate estate-property contention under sections 362(a)(3), 541 and 554: an unscheduled judgment allegedly remained the trustee's asset. I do not dismiss that contention as equivalent to the debtor-protection argument. But it would require resolving asset ownership, authority to pursue collection, and the effect of particular state acts on the estate; the unexplained affirmance does not cleanly decide those questions.

3. **The proposed Taggart conflict is not squarely presented.** The petition, pages 16–17, invokes differing approaches in *In re Jeong* and *In re Spiech Farms* to applying Taggart to automatic-stay violations. I have not independently verified those two decisions and do not treat their citation as proof of a mature circuit split. I verified *Taggart v. Its objective civil-contempt rule addresses a creditor's violation of a discharge order; its automatic-stay discussion distinguishes statutory remedies and purposes. This petition seeks wholesale invalidation of state judgments, rather than review of a clean bankruptcy contempt judgment choosing between competing standards. Whether a stay or discharge barred the conduct is antecedent to the contempt standard.
