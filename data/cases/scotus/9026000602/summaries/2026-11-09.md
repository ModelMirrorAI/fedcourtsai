## In brief

A Florida couple who lost a landlord-tenant lawsuit ask the Supreme Court to overturn that judgment, arguing it was entered in violation of the tenants' bankruptcy case.

## What happened

Palanikumar and Mullairani Gnanam, former landlords, lost a Duval County lawsuit brought by their tenants, Nathaniel Chibuzor Keita and Lana Paige Tompkins, over a lease dispute. A trial judge entered judgment against the Gnanams in 2024, and an appeals court affirmed and awarded additional fees in August 2025. The Florida Supreme Court declined further review the same month. While the case was on appeal, Keita filed for Chapter 7 bankruptcy; the Gnanams say this was hidden from them and from the state courts, and that later orders continued to enforce the judgment despite the bankruptcy. They represent themselves throughout.

## What the Court is being asked

The Gnanams ask the Court to decide whether a state court can enforce a judgment, and reject a challenge to that judgment, without accounting for a federal bankruptcy filing by the party who won the judgment — specifically, whether doing so conflicts with federal rules that automatically pause collection efforts once someone files for bankruptcy, and with federal rules about what a bankruptcy discharge wipes out. They also ask whether the state courts denied them a fair chance to raise these arguments.

## Where it stands

The petition for certiorari was filed January 14, 2026, after the Court granted two extensions of time to file. The docket shows no indication yet of a response being filed or any action by the Court.

## What each outcome would mean

The Gnanams, as petitioners, are asking the Court to overturn the Florida courts' rulings against them. If the Court denies review, the judgment and fee awards against the Gnanams, which Keita and Tompkins won below, would stand. If the Court grants review, it would hear full arguments and decide the legal questions about bankruptcy's effect on the state court proceedings; a ruling could then affirm the lower decision, reverse it in the Gnanams' favor, or send the case back without resolving it for either side.
