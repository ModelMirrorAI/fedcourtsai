# Forecast: what the Court will do with No. 25-1335, *bin 'Atash v. United States*

**Bottom line: the petition will be denied**, most likely in the same order
list that disposes of the companion petition by Khalid Sheikh Mohammad and
Mustafa al Hawsawi (No. 26-13), without the Court calling for anything further.

## Distributions and relists

The docket shows **two distribution entries** (for the September 28 and
October 9, 2026 conferences), and **each was followed by a "Rescheduled"
entry before the conference met** (September 22 and October 5). So the
petition has not yet been considered at any conference; the two entries are
reschedules, not relists. The reschedules line up with the later-docketed
companion petition in No. 26-13, whose brief in opposition the Solicitor
General filed on the same day as this one, and the Court is almost certainly
holding this petition to consider the two together.

Expect **at least one further distribution** — a rescheduled petition is
always redistributed for a later conference — and probably the petition is
decided at the first conference where both petitions are before the Court
together, so a single additional distribution (two at most) is the modal
path. I do not expect a true relist (consideration followed by
redistribution) beyond that: the sequence should end in a denial within
roughly four to six weeks, i.e. by mid-November 2026.

## CVSG

**No call for the views of the Solicitor General.** The United States is the
respondent and the Solicitor General has already filed a 24-page brief in
opposition; a CVSG is not a live possibility on this docket.

## Which question the Court would take, if it took one

If the Court granted, it would take the petition's question essentially as
written but reframed around the mandamus standard, as the government's own
restatement does: whether the D.C. Circuit properly found a *clear and
indisputable* right to relief on the "begins performance" question under
R.M.C. 705(d)(4)(B). The Court would not take up the unlawful-influence or
delegation issues, which Judge Wilkins conceded to the government and the
petition does not press. A grant would almost certainly be paired with a
grant in No. 26-13 and the two consolidated.

## Summary disposition

**Unlikely.** There is no intervening decision of this Court bearing on the
"begins performance" question, so a GVR has nothing to GVR in light of, and
a summary reversal of a published, divided D.C. Circuit mandamus opinion on
a question the Court has never addressed is not this Court's practice.
Conditional on a grant, plenary review (consolidated with No. 26-13) is the
route.

## Dissent from or statement respecting denial

**Probably none.** The stakes and the forceful Wilkins dissent give a
Justice material for a statement, and Justices Sotomayor or Jackson are the
plausible authors if one appears. But the Court's long run of Guantanamo
denials since 2010 has been almost entirely silent, the legal question is
narrow and tied to a rule that now exists only in the military-commission
code, and a separate writing defending the 9/11 accused's plea bargains
against the Secretary of Defense carries obvious costs. I put a written
dissent or statement at a modest probability.

## If granted (conditional only)

The likely ground would be the mandamus standard rather than the merits of
Rule 705(d)(4)(B): that a question on which the highest military court had
ruled the other way under identical text, and on which two military
tribunals agreed with the accused, cannot be "clear and indisputable" error.
That would vacate the writs without deciding whether performance had begun.
