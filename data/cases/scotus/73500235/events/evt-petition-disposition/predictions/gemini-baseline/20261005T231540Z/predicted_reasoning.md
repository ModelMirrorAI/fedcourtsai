The petition has been distributed and rescheduled twice so far. Expect at least one further distribution or reschedule before the Court reaches a decision, as the Justices will likely need more time to review the complex record regarding the military commission rules and the mandamus standard. 

The Court will not call for the views of the Solicitor General (CVSG) because the United States is already a party to the case.

If the Court grants certiorari, it would likely take the question presented as written: whether the D.C. Circuit misapplied the standard for mandamus relief in determining that the accused had not "begun performance" under Rule for Military Commissions 705(d)(4)(B). A summary disposition (such as a GVR) is highly unlikely, as there is no intervening precedent and the issue requires plenary review.

Should the Court deny review, expect a written dissent from denial from the liberal wing (likely Justice Jackson, Sotomayor, or Kagan), emphasizing the D.C. Circuit's aggressive use of mandamus to overturn a trial judge's factual/contractual findings in a capital case.
