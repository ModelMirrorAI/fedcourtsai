## In brief

A man charged in the September 11 attacks asks the Court to undo a ruling that let the government withdraw from his plea deal sparing him the death penalty.

## What happened

Walid bin 'Atash is charged before a military commission at Guantanamo Bay in connection with the September 11, 2001 attacks. In 2024 a military official approved a pretrial agreement under which bin 'Atash would plead guilty and the government would not seek the death penalty. Two days later the Secretary of Defense withdrew from the agreement. A military judge and a military appeals court both ruled that bin 'Atash had already begun carrying out promises in the agreement, so the withdrawal came too late. The United States Court of Appeals for the District of Columbia Circuit disagreed, ruling for the government and blocking the guilty pleas from being entered, over one judge's dissent.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked whether it was clear and beyond reasonable dispute that bin 'Atash had not yet started carrying out his promises under the agreement when the government pulled out, given that he had signed a detailed factual confession and taken other steps the government itself said the agreement required. That question matters because a court may only order this kind of emergency relief, called mandamus, when the requesting party's right to it is clear and indisputable, not merely the better view.

## What each outcome would mean

Bin 'Atash, who lost in the appeals court, is asking the Supreme Court to hear his case. If the Court grants review, it will decide the question itself after full briefing and argument, deciding nothing yet about who is right. If it denies review, the appeals court's ruling stands, meaning the Secretary of Defense's withdrawal from the plea agreement remains in effect and the case returns to the military commission without the agreed guilty plea and sentencing terms.
