## In brief

A man charged in the September 11 attacks asks the Court to review a ruling letting the Defense Secretary withdraw from a plea deal sparing him the death penalty.

## What happened

Walid bin 'Atash is charged before a military commission in connection with the September 11, 2001 attacks. In 2024 a military official overseeing his case accepted a pretrial agreement under which bin 'Atash would plead guilty and the government would not seek the death penalty. Two days later, the Secretary of Defense withdrew from the agreement. A military judge and a military appeals court both ruled that bin 'Atash had already begun carrying out promises in the agreement, so the withdrawal came too late. The government then asked the D.C. Circuit for emergency court orders overriding those rulings, and that court agreed, holding the withdrawal valid and blocking the guilty plea from being entered.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked whether it was clear and indisputable, under the demanding standard for the kind of emergency court order the D.C. Circuit issued, that bin 'Atash had not yet begun performing promises in his plea agreement—even though he had signed a detailed factual confession and taken other steps the government itself treated as required by the deal.

## Where it stands

The petition has been distributed for two conferences, most recently October 9, 2026, and the docket does not yet show any action taken on it.

## What each outcome would mean

Bin 'Atash, as petitioner, is asking the Court to hear his case. If the Court grants review, it would hear full arguments and decide whether the D.C. Circuit was right to let the Secretary's withdrawal stand; a grant would not itself decide who wins. If the Court denies review, the D.C. Circuit's decision stays in place, meaning the Secretary's withdrawal from the plea agreement is effective, the death penalty remains available, and bin 'Atash cannot enter his guilty plea under that agreement's terms.
