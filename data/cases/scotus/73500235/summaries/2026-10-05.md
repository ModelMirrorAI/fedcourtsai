## In brief

A man charged in the September 11 attacks asks the Court to review a ruling that let the Secretary of Defense withdraw from his plea deal.

## What happened

Walid bin 'Atash is charged before a military commission for the September 11 attacks. In 2024 he signed a pretrial agreement sparing him the death penalty in exchange for a guilty plea, and the agreement was accepted by a military official. Two days later the Secretary of Defense withdrew from the agreement. A military judge and the Court of Military Commission Review both ruled that bin 'Atash had already begun performing his side of the deal, so the withdrawal came too late. The government then asked the D.C. Circuit for an order overriding that ruling, and the appeals court granted it, finding the military judge's decision clearly wrong.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked whether it was clear and indisputable, under the demanding standard for the kind of emergency court order the government sought, that bin 'Atash had not yet begun performing his promises when the government pulled out of the deal, given that he had signed the agreement, provided a detailed factual confession, and taken other steps the government itself treated as required.

## Where it stands

The petition has been distributed for the Court's conference twice, most recently October 9, 2026, after full briefing including the government's opposition and bin 'Atash's reply. The docket does not yet show any action taken at either conference.

## What each outcome would mean

Bin 'Atash is asking the Court to take up his case. If the Court grants review, it would hear full arguments and decide whether the lower military-judge ruling in his favor should have been overturned, deciding nothing yet about who wins. If the Court denies review, the D.C. Circuit's decision favoring the government stands, meaning the Secretary's withdrawal from the plea agreement remains in effect and bin 'Atash's case proceeds without that agreement. A denial would not mean the Court agrees the D.C. Circuit was right, only that its ruling remains in place.
