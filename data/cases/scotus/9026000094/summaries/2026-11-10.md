## In brief

A California city asks the Court to revive a civil-rights lawsuit bar after a man it tased sued over the force used, following his no-contest plea to resisting arrest.

## What happened

Adolfo Cardenas led police on a chase, fled on foot, and was tased by Officer Brice while running up a stairwell; he later pleaded no contest to resisting arrest among other charges. Cardenas then sued the City of Santa Maria and Brice, claiming the tasing was excessive force. The district court ruled his conviction barred the suit under a rule that blocks civil claims that would imply a criminal conviction was invalid. A divided Ninth Circuit panel reversed, holding the plea record did not clearly show the tasing was part of what Cardenas admitted to, and alternatively citing a now-vacated circuit decision limiting use of no-contest pleas as evidence. A separate opinion disagreed, citing Cardenas's own statements conceding the point.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked whether the appeals court was wrong to let Cardenas's lawsuit proceed despite his earlier guilty-type plea, given his lawyers' statements about what that plea admitted, and whether an evidence rule about no-contest pleas should have kept the conviction from blocking the suit in the first place.

## Where it stands

The petition has been distributed for conference multiple times, most recently for November 13, 2026, after the Court requested and received a response from Cardenas and a reply from the City; no decision on whether to hear the case has been recorded yet.

## What each outcome would mean

The City of Santa Maria and Officer Brice are asking the Court to reverse the Ninth Circuit's decision, which favored Cardenas. If the Court grants the petition, it would hear full arguments and decide the legal questions itself, deciding nothing yet about who wins. If it denies the petition, the Ninth Circuit's ruling stands, and Cardenas's excessive-force lawsuit against the City and Brice continues in the lower courts.
