## In brief

A city and police officer ask the Court to review a ruling that let a man's excessive-force lawsuit proceed despite his earlier no-contest plea to resisting arrest.

## What happened

Adolfo Cardenas led police on a chase, then pleaded no contest to resisting an officer, among other charges, pointing the state court to a police report as the factual basis for his plea. He later sued the City of Santa Maria and Officer Andy Brice, claiming Brice used excessive force by tasing him during the encounter. The city argued the lawsuit was barred because a ruling in Cardenas's favor would undermine his conviction. The district court agreed and ruled for the city. A divided Ninth Circuit panel reversed, in an unpublished decision, finding the record unclear about which of Cardenas's acts the plea actually covered, and alternatively citing a now-vacated circuit decision about a rule of evidence. The full Ninth Circuit denied rehearing.

## What the Court is being asked

The city and officer ask the Court to decide whether the appeals panel was wrong to let the lawsuit go forward, arguing the panel should have treated Cardenas's own statements in earlier proceedings as settling what his plea covered, and that its alternative reasoning relied on a decision the appeals court has since set aside.

## Where it stands

The petition has been distributed for conference twice, most recently for November 6, 2026; the Court asked for and received a response from Cardenas, and the city filed a reply. An entry dated the day before that conference shows the case was "rescheduled," and no decision on whether to hear the case has been recorded yet.

## What each outcome would mean

The city and Officer Brice, who lost below, are asking the Court to take up the case and reverse. If the Court grants review, it would hear full arguments and decide whether the lawsuit can proceed; a grant alone would not decide who wins. If the Court denies review, the Ninth Circuit's ruling stands, and Cardenas's lawsuit against the city and officer continues in the lower courts. The city also asks, as a fallback, that the Court set aside the ruling and send the case back for another look, which would decide nothing on the merits.
