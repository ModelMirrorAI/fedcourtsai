## In brief

A city asks the Court to review a ruling that let a man's excessive-force lawsuit proceed despite his earlier no-contest plea to resisting arrest.

## What happened

Adolfo Cardenas led police on a chase, fled on foot, and was tased by Officer Andy Brice before being arrested. Cardenas pleaded no contest to resisting Brice, among other charges, then sued the City of Santa Maria and Brice, claiming the tasing was excessive force. The district court ruled that this claim was barred because Cardenas had conceded that a police report describing the whole encounter, including the tasing, formed the factual basis for his plea. A divided Ninth Circuit panel reversed, holding the record did not clearly show which specific acts underlay the conviction, and alternatively pointed to a now-vacated circuit ruling about a evidence rule. A separate opinion disagreed with the panel's reading of the record.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked to decide whether the appeals court could let the lawsuit go forward despite a legal rule that normally blocks damages claims that would necessarily contradict an existing criminal conviction, given disputes over what the plea record showed and over a since-withdrawn evidentiary theory the lower court had also relied on.

## Where it stands

The petition has been distributed for conference twice, most recently for November 6, 2026, after the Court asked for and received a response and the city filed a reply. No order on the petition has yet been recorded.

## What each outcome would mean

The City of Santa Maria and Officer Brice are asking the Court to take up the case and reverse. If the Court grants review and ultimately rules for the City, Cardenas's lawsuit would likely be barred, consistent with the district court's original ruling. If it rules for Cardenas, the case would continue in the lower courts. If the Court denies review, the Ninth Circuit's decision allowing the lawsuit to proceed stands, and the case returns to the district court.
