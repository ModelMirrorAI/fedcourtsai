# Forecast: City of Santa Maria v. Cardenas (No. 26-94), after the call for a response

**Where the docket stands at my baseline (cutoff 2026-09-04).** One distribution, for the 9/28/2026 conference. The next day the Court called for a response, due October 5. No CVSG. One amicus brief: the International Municipal Lawyers Association and League of California Cities, supporting the petitioners.

**Disposition.** I expect a denial. I put the chance of any grant (plenary, GVR or summary reversal) at about 15%.

**Further distributions.** The petition will be distributed again once the response is in. The call for a response all but guarantees that. I expect it to be decided at its first post-response conference or after one relist. A run of three or more relists would point to a summary reversal being drafted, or to a hold for the Ninth Circuit's en banc decision in *King v. Villegas*.

**CVSG.** I don't expect one. Only a city and its officer are on one side, and the Solicitor General has no institutional stake in how a Heck defense applies to a no-contest plea.

**Route if granted.** The petition asks for summary reversal of the plea-clarity holding and, as a fallback, a GVR in light of the en banc vacatur of *King*. If the Court acts, I expect it to do so in the cert order: either a per curiam summary reversal on party-presentation grounds, or a GVR or hold-and-GVR once the en banc Ninth Circuit rules in *King*. I put that at about 60% of the grant mass, against roughly 40% for plenary review of the Rule 410(a) / Heck question. Plenary review becomes more likely if the en banc court keeps a categorical Rule 410(a) bar, which would cement a split with the Third, Fifth, Sixth and Eleventh Circuits.

**Which question.** If the Court grants plenary review, it would take the petition's single QP narrowed to the Rule 410(a) / no-contest-plea issue. It would not take the record-specific question of what the plea's factual basis was.

**Dissent from denial.** If the petition is denied, a separate writing is unlikely (about 10%). A statement respecting denial, most plausibly from Justice Thomas or Justice Alito criticizing the Ninth Circuit's Heck case law, is the likeliest form. Justice Sotomayor writing on the other side is less likely.
