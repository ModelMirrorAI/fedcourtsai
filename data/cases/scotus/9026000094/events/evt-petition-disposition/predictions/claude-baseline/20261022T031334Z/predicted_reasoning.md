# Predicted course — City of Santa Maria v. Cardenas, No. 26-94

**State of the docket.** The docket shows two distributions. The first (for the
9/28/2026 conference) came before any response. The Court then requested a
response on Sept 3, the BIO was filed Oct 5 and the reply Oct 20, and the
petition was distributed again for the **11/6/2026 conference**. So the
November conference is in substance the petition's first real consideration.
No CVSG is on the docket.

**Disposition.** Most likely **denied**, probably at or soon after the Nov 6
conference. The decision below is an unpublished, fact-bound memorandum
disposition. It applies the Ninth Circuit's settled Heck framework (Lemos,
Martell) to one plea record. Its alternative Rule 410(a) ground rests on King v.
Villegas, which the en banc court has since vacated, so the only conflict the
petition names no longer exists.

**Relists.** I expect most likely no further distribution. Where there is one,
it will be the Court holding the case to consider a **summary disposition**: a
per curiam summary reversal in the mold of its recent per curiams policing lower
courts on police-liability and party-presentation issues, or a GVR tied to the
King en banc proceedings. If the Court acts, that is the likelier route than
plenary review. A clean grant for argument is unlikely given the unpublished,
record-bound vehicle.

**CVSG.** None expected. No federal party or program is implicated beyond
Rule 410(a), which has dropped out after King's vacatur.

**Question presented, if taken.** The petition's QP would be reframed narrowly:
whether a court may decline to apply Heck where the plaintiff stipulated that
the whole police report, including the force incident, was the factual basis of
his no-contest plea. The Rule 410(a) point would be left behind or handled by
GVR.

**Dissent from denial.** It is modestly possible that a Justice concerned about
the Ninth Circuit's narrowing of Heck writes separately (Justice Thomas or
Justice Alito being the most plausible). But Olivier v. City of Brandon (2026)
cuts against an appetite to broaden Heck, so I expect no separate writing more
likely than not.
