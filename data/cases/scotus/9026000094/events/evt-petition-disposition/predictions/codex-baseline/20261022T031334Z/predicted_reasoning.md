# Forecast of the Court's action

The Court will most likely deny the petition without a separate writing. The unpublished decision presents a dispute about what this particular plea record and counsel's concessions establish, rather than a clean, continuing conflict over a governing rule. The panel majority's independent plea-record ground makes the vacatur of the King panel opinion an insufficient reason, by itself, to disturb the judgment.

The snapshot records **two distributions**: September 2 for the September 28, 2026 conference, and October 21 for the November 6, 2026 conference. Between them, the Court requested a response and received the opposition. Thus the second distribution is not compelling evidence of a substantive relist after full consideration of the completed briefing. From this two-distribution state, I assign a **30% probability of at least one further distribution**. My modal forecast is no further distribution; if the Court needs additional consideration, one further conference is more likely than a long succession of relists. A hold associated with King is a less likely source of extended delay.

There is no CVSG in the baseline. I assign **1.5% probability of a new CVSG**: this local-government damages dispute offers little apparent need for a distinct federal executive-branch view. The expected disposition is an order following the November 6 conference, during November 2026; additional consideration could move it into December or later. Timing is a forecast, not a scored claim.

I assign **20% probability to any grant-family disposition**, including a GVR or summary reversal. **Conditional on a grant**, the probability of disposition in the cert order rather than plenary briefing and argument is **85%**. Within that branch, a GVR addressing the changed status of King is more plausible than a broad merits ruling; summary reversal based on the treatment of counsel's concessions is the other substantial route. Neither route is my overall prediction.

If plenary review is granted, the Court is more likely to narrow the petition's question to whether the acknowledged factual basis and litigation concessions establish a Heck bar notwithstanding the no-contest plea, rather than reopen Heck generally. If it summarily reverses, the likely ground is the treatment of those concessions. If it GVRs, the likely instruction is reconsideration in light of the change in the Rule 410(a) precedent. These are conditional forecasts, not a prediction that a merits opinion will issue.

**Conditional on denial**, I assign **10% probability** that any Justice notes a dissent or statement respecting denial. The modal forecast is an unsigned denial with no recorded separate writing; I do not forecast individual cert votes.
