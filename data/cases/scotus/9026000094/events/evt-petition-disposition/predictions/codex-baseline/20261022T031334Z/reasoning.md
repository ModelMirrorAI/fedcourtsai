## Why 20%, rather than the anchor alone

The response request is meaningful affirmative attention, and the petitioner has identified a concrete potential basis for summary correction, not simply urged the Court to revisit Heck at large. The most consequential point for intervention is Judge Koh's separate opinion: although she concurred in the judgment because of King, she disagreed with the majority about the factual basis of the plea and described counsel's repeated concessions. Petition pages 15–21 press that dispute as a party-presentation error. The subsequent vacatur of the King panel opinion, documented in both provisioned briefs, also gives a GVR request an intelligible target. Experienced Supreme Court counsel and municipal amici are supporting, not dispositive, signals.

The principal counterweight is an independent ground supporting the judgment. The appended majority memorandum, pages 2a–5a, treats the plea form as referring the criminal court to a report, rather than expressly adopting every act in it. The majority therefore concludes that the excessive-force claim need not contradict the act supporting the misdemeanor conviction. It invokes King only as an alternative. Removing that alternative does not mechanically remove the majority's primary reasoning.

The opposition, especially pages 8–17, presents a credible vehicle objection: an unpublished application of a shared Heck framework is a weak platform for resolving a national rule. It also distinguishes the identification of a source document from a binding concession about which acts legally supported which conviction. The petition's oral-argument account and Koh's concurrence make that response contestable, but the dispute itself remains intensely record-specific. I do not treat the panel as divided over the judgment: all three judges reversed, with Koh disagreeing over the grounds. Nor do I treat the vacated Rule 410(a) holding as an established, presently operative circuit split.

For the GVR branch, I checked the general standard in Lawrence v. Chater, 516 U.S. 163, 167–68 (1996), through CourtListener: an intervening development must offer a plausible route to a different result below, with the equities also relevant. That standard leaves room for a remand, but the independent plea-record ground substantially weakens the inference that King's changed status would change the judgment. I did not seek King's later en banc disposition. Its current status beyond what these briefs establish remains an uncertainty, not a fact filled in from memory.

Taken together, these considerations support a modest upward adjustment from 16.848% to **20%**, not a grant prediction. This is a judgmental adjustment, not a fitted posterior or a measured causal effect of a response request. Most of the probability of intervention belongs to a summary route because the petition's strongest arguments concern case-specific correction and the changed authority, while a durable split supporting plenary review has not been demonstrated. The **85% conditional summary-route** forecast is separate from the unconditional grant number: it corresponds to 17% unconditional summary-route probability and 3% plenary-route probability.
