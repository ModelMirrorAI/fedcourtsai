## Questions Presented and Vehicle Assessment

If the Court were to grant review, it would focus on the first branch of Petitioners' Question Presented—whether the Ninth Circuit panel majority violated party-presentation principles and *Heck v. Humphrey*, 512 U.S. 477 (1994), by holding that the state-court plea record did not establish which acts of resistance formed the factual predicate of Respondent's no-contest plea. It would not grant plenary review on the second branch concerning Federal Rule of Evidence 410(a), because the Ninth Circuit opinion cited in Footnote 1 of the decision below (*King v. Villegas*, 156 F.4th 979 (9th Cir. 2025)) was already vacated for rehearing en banc on April 3, 2026 (171 F.4th 1163), leaving no live circuit split.

However, the Court will conclude that the petition is an unsuitable vehicle for plenary review or summary intervention:
1. The decision below is an unpublished, non-precedential memorandum disposition applying settled Ninth Circuit standards (*Lemos v. County of Sonoma*, 40 F.4th 1002 (9th Cir. 2022) (en banc), and *Martell v. Cole*, 115 F.4th 1233 (9th Cir. 2024)) to a specific Santa Barbara County Superior Court plea form.
2. The panel did not decide an unraised legal issue in violation of *Clark v. Sweeney*, 607 U.S. 7 (2025), or *United States v. Margolin*, 146 S. Ct. 1288 (2026); rather, both parties briefed whether the plea record indivisibly encompassed all four phases of the encounter under *Sanders v. City of Pittsburg*, 14 F.4th 968 (9th Cir. 2021), and the district court itself had requested supplemental briefing on the ambiguity of the plea form's "may consider" language.
3. Because Footnote 1's citation to *King* was explicitly framed as an "[a]lternative[]" ground (`App. 3a n.1`), vacating and remanding in light of *King*'s en banc vacatur would not alter the panel majority's primary holding that the plea record failed to establish that the fourth (stairwell/tasing) phase was the necessary factual predicate for the single § 148(a)(1) conviction.
