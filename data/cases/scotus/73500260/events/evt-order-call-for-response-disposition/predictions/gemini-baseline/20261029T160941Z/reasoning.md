## Case-Specific Adjustments from the Provisioned Record

### Upward Factors (Supporting Attention Above a Typical Denied Petition)
1. **Call for a Response (`moment: response-requested`) & State Amicus Brief:** After Respondent Wendy Spicher (Secretary of the Pennsylvania Department of Banking and Securities) waived the right to respond on July 8, 2026, the State of South Carolina filed an amicus brief the same day supporting Petitioners, and on July 29, 2026 the Court requested a response (due August 28, 2026). This confirms that at least one chambers flagged the petition as warranting a brief in opposition rather than summary denial on the waiver list.
2. **Experienced Counsel & Framed Horizontal Federalism Question:** Petitioners are represented by experienced Supreme Court counsel (Misha Tseytlin) and frame a clean doctrinal question under *Younger v. Harris*, 401 U.S. 37 (1971), and *New Orleans Public Service, Inc. v. Council of City of New Orleans* (*NOPSI*), 491 U.S. 350, 365 (1989): whether a State's "generic" interest in enforcing its consumer-protection and usury laws suffices for *Younger*'s second *Middlesex* factor when the targets are out-of-state brick-and-mortar lenders contracting with Pennsylvania residents in Virginia, Delaware, and South Carolina.

### Downward Factors (Why Denial Remains Strongly Favored at `0.04`)
1. **Unpublished, Non-Precedential Decision Below with Zero En Banc Votes:** The Third Circuit panel decision (`petition.txt`, App. 1a–10a, *TitleMax of Virginia, Inc. v. Secretary Pennsylvania Department of Banking and Securities*, No. 24-2136, Jan. 7, 2026) is an unpublished, non-precedential opinion under Third Circuit I.O.P. 5.7, and the Third Circuit denied rehearing en banc with zero recorded dissents across fourteen active judges (App. 49a–50a). The Court rarely grants plenary review of an unpublished affirmance that binds no future panel.
2. **Severe Vehicle Entanglement with Prior Published Precedent (*Weissmann*):** In an earlier published decision involving the very same TitleMax entities and Pennsylvania investigation, *TitleMax of Delaware, Inc. v. Weissmann*, 24 F.4th 230, 239–41 (3d Cir. 2022), *cert. denied sub nom. TitleMax of Delaware, Inc. v. Vague*, 142 S. Ct. 2837 (2022), the Third Circuit already held on the merits that TitleMax's loan transactions with Pennsylvanians did *not* occur "wholly outside" Pennsylvania (because TitleMax recorded liens with PennDOT, collected payments from borrowers in Pennsylvania, and repossessed vehicles in Pennsylvania) and that Pennsylvania had a strong interest in enforcing its usury laws without violating the Dormant Commerce Clause. As Judge Fisher's panel opinion emphasized (App. 6a–8a), *Weissmann* had already substantively rejected the factual and legal premise of Petitioners' *Younger* argument—namely, that the transactions were wholly extraterritorial. Because Respondent's brief in opposition will inevitably highlight that Petitioners' threshold *Younger* framing depends on relitigating a factual and constitutional premise they already lost on the merits in *Weissmann* (where this Court denied certiorari), this case is a poor vehicle for addressing an abstract *Younger*/*NOPSI* question.
3. **Weakness and Prematurity of the Alleged Circuit Split:**
   - The Tenth Circuit case Petitioners invoke (*Seneca-Cayuga Tribe of Oklahoma v. Oklahoma ex rel. Thompson*, 874 F.2d 709 (10th Cir. May 3, 1989)) was decided *before* *NOPSI* (June 19, 1989) articulated the "generic proceedings" test and turned on unique federal Indian sovereignty preemption.
   - The Fourth Circuit cases (*Harper v. Public Service Commission of West Virginia*, 396 F.3d 348 (4th Cir. 2005), and *Life Partners, Inc. v. Morrison*, 484 F.3d 284 (4th Cir. 2007)) are two decades old and predate *Sprint Communications, Inc. v. Jacobs*, 571 U.S. 69 (2013). More importantly, as the petition itself discloses (`petition.txt` at 12 n.4), a parallel lawsuit brought by TitleMax of South Carolina challenging the exact same Pennsylvania Order to Show Cause (*TitleMax of South Carolina, Inc. v. Spicher*, No. 25-2027) is currently pending in the Fourth Circuit. Even if the Justices were interested in whether the Fourth Circuit still adheres to *Harper*/*Life Partners* after *Sprint*, waiting to see how the Fourth Circuit resolves that parallel appeal involving the identical enforcement proceeding is far more prudent than granting an unpublished Third Circuit decision encumbered by *Weissmann*.
4. **Ongoing State Administrative Proceedings Favoring Petitioners on State Law:** As the petition notes (`petition.txt` at 14), in April 2026 the Pennsylvania administrative hearing examiner issued a proposed order dismissing the Department's Order to Show Cause against Petitioners on state-law evidentiary grounds (pending review before the Banking and Securities Commission). That ongoing state administrative path—which may moot the enforcement threat without reaching any federal constitutional question—further counsels against certiorari.

Weighing the `5.2%` `baseline` reached anchor and the modest positive signal of the Call for a Response + South Carolina amicus brief against the unpublished opinion below, the *Weissmann* issue-preclusion/law-of-the-case entanglement, the pending Fourth Circuit parallel appeal, and the potential administrative mootness in Pennsylvania, I set `probability = 0.04` (`denied`).
