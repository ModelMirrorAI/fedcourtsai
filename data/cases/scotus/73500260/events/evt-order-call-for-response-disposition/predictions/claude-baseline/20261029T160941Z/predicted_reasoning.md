# Predicted course — TitleMax of Virginia, Inc. v. Spicher, No. 25-1359

**Call: denied (P(grant) ≈ 0.12).**

- **Distributions so far.** The docket shows one distribution (for the 9/28/2026
  conference), after Pennsylvania waived its response; on 7/29/2026 the Court
  called for a response. That pulls the petition off the 9/28 conference. Once
  Pennsylvania's brief in opposition is in (due date extended into early November
  2026) and the reply time runs, the petition will be **redistributed** — near
  certain — for a conference in late November or December 2026. I expect it to be
  decided at that first post-response conference or after at most one relist;
  further relists beyond one are unlikely (< 20%) and would signal a grant or a
  hold for the companion TMX Finance Corporate Services petition (CA5).
- **No CVSG.** The respondent is a state officer, the dispute is a federal-courts
  question about Younger abstention, and no federal program is implicated; the
  Court has no reason to invite the Solicitor General.
- **If granted**, the Court would take the single question as written — whether a
  State's "generic" interest (NOPSI) in regulating out-of-state transactions with
  its residents suffices for the important-state-interest step of Younger/Middlesex
  — likely together with (or holding) the forthcoming CA5 TMX FCS petition. The
  likely ground would tighten Sprint's insistence that Younger is a narrow
  exception, requiring the State's interest to be assessed specifically against
  the competing sovereignty of the States where the loans were made.
- **Summary disposition** is unlikely: no intervening decision bears on the
  question, so a GVR is improbable, and a summary reversal of a Younger
  abstention ruling on a fact-specific interest assessment is rare.
- **Dissent from denial.** Most likely none. If anyone writes, the likeliest are
  Justices Thomas or Gorsuch (federalism/extraterritoriality concerns about one
  State regulating commerce in another) or Justice Alito.
