# Forecast

The Court will most likely **deny certiorari**, without a separate writing. The probability of any grant, including a summary grant or GVR, is **13%**. The case presents an arguable tension over Younger's important-state-interest requirement, but the Court is more likely to regard this nonprecedential decision as an application of the established civil-enforcement exception than as a suitable occasion to redraw it.

The docket currently shows **one distribution**, for September 28, 2026, and **no CVSG**. The later response request and extension through November 2 mean that I expect **at least one additional distribution** after the opposition and any reply, probably one before disposition in late November 2026 through January 2027 if briefing proceeds on the existing schedule. That first additional distribution would not itself show a substantive relist after conference consideration. I expect no prolonged series of substantive relists, although one further conference after redistribution is plausible. My probability of any additional distribution is **96%**.

I expect **no invitation to the Solicitor General**; the probability of a new CVSG is **3.5%**. If the Court requests the government's views, it will probably do so after considering the completed briefing, moving resolution substantially later than the ordinary winter timetable.

If review is granted, the Court is most likely to address the petition's single abstention question, potentially reformulated neutrally: whether the important-state-interest inquiry under Younger requires considering the asserted extraterritorial character of a State's civil enforcement action, rather than only the generic importance of the regulatory interest. It would not ordinarily resolve the underlying Commerce Clause and personal-jurisdiction claims in deciding that question. Conditional on a grant, the likelier route is **plenary briefing and argument**, not a disposition in the cert order; the probability of the summary route is **20%**. A companion-driven remand is a possibility, not a prediction that a companion has been granted.

If granted and decided on the merits, the principal doctrinal dispute would be the proper level of generality for the state-interest inquiry within Younger's limited civil-enforcement category. That merits forecast is conditional; an ordinary denial would decide no such doctrinal question. Conditional on denial, the probability of any noted dissent or statement is **6%**, with **no separate writing** the committed modal prediction and no particular Justice forecast to write.
