# Forecast — TitleMax of Virginia, Inc. v. Spicher (No. 25-1359)

**State forecast from:** one distribution on the docket (for the 9/28/2026
conference), followed on Jul 29 by a **Court-requested response**, which
Pennsylvania has twice extended, now due **November 2, 2026**. No relist has
yet occurred in the ordinary sense; no CVSG.

- **Disposition: denied** (P(grant) ≈ 9%). The called-for brief in opposition
  will very likely contest the split — the Fourth Circuit cases (*Harper*,
  *Life Partners*) and the Tenth Circuit's 1989 *Seneca-Cayuga* decision turn on
  a predominant federal interest (interstate commerce, IGRA) rather than a
  general rule about how to characterize the state interest under *NOPSI* —
  and will stress the parallel Pennsylvania proceedings already under review in
  the Commonwealth Court, where the federal defenses can be raised.
- **Further distribution: yes, almost certainly.** Once the response is filed
  the petition will be distributed again, most likely for a conference in late
  November or early December 2026. I expect a decision at that first
  post-response conference; one further relist after it is possible but not
  the modal path.
- **CVSG: no.** The dispute is between private lenders and a State over state
  enforcement; no federal program or federal party interest is implicated.
- **If granted:** the Court would take the single QP as written (whether a
  State's "generic" interest in regulating its residents' out-of-state
  transactions with out-of-state firms suffices for *Younger* under *NOPSI* /
  *Sprint*), possibly held or paired with the forthcoming Fifth Circuit *TMX
  FCS* petition that the petition flags. Plenary review, not a summary
  disposition — no intervening decision supports a GVR.
- **Should it deny:** expect no noted dissent or statement; a short statement
  respecting denial is a low-probability possibility given the federalism
  framing.
