## In brief

TitleMax asks the Court to review whether federal courts must step back from a Pennsylvania enforcement action against it under a rule favoring state proceedings over federal ones.

## What happened

Pennsylvania's banking regulator subpoenaed TitleMax, a car-title lender, over loans to Pennsylvania residents that allegedly carried interest rates far above the state's cap. After TitleMax complied, the state brought an administrative enforcement proceeding seeking tens of millions in penalties. TitleMax sued in federal court, arguing the proceeding improperly reached across state lines in violation of the Constitution's limits on state interference with interstate commerce. The district court declined to hear the claim, applying a doctrine that keeps federal courts from interfering with certain ongoing state proceedings, and the Third Circuit affirmed. TitleMax also lost parallel appeals in other circuits.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked whether a state's general interest in enforcing its consumer-protection laws is "important" enough to justify a federal court stepping back from a case, when the state is applying that law to transactions companies say happened in other states. TitleMax argues courts should weigh the state's interest against the competing interest in letting other states regulate business conducted within their own borders, rather than accepting any broadly stated state interest as sufficient.

## Where it stands

The petition was distributed for conference, and the Court later asked for a response, which Pennsylvania's regulator filed opposing review. The case has not yet been scheduled for another conference in this record.

## What each outcome would mean

TitleMax, the company facing the state enforcement action, asked the Court to take the case. If the Court grants review, it would hear full arguments on whether the lower court applied the right standard, without yet deciding who wins. If it denies review, the Third Circuit's decision stands, leaving in place the ruling that federal courts should not interfere with Pennsylvania's enforcement proceeding, and TitleMax would continue litigating in the state process.
