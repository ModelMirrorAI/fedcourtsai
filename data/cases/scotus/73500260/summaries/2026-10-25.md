## In brief

TitleMax entities ask the Court to let their constitutional challenge to a Pennsylvania enforcement action proceed in federal court rather than being blocked by a judicial-abstention doctrine.

## What happened

Pennsylvania's banking regulator accused several TitleMax-affiliated companies of charging illegal interest rates on loans made to Pennsylvania residents, even though the loans were made at stores outside Pennsylvania. The companies sued in federal court, arguing the state proceeding violated the Constitution's limits on regulating out-of-state commerce and on haling companies into states where they have no contacts. The district court dismissed the suits under a doctrine, from the case Younger v. Harris, that tells federal courts to stay out of certain ongoing state proceedings when the state has an important interest at stake. The Third Circuit Court of Appeals affirmed, and also denied rehearing.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked whether a state's broadly described interest in regulating conduct is enough to let the state keep a dispute out of federal court, when the conduct at issue is a transaction between an out-of-state business and the state's resident that took place in another state's borders. TitleMax argues courts must look more specifically at whether the state's interest holds up once the fact that other states have their own authority over transactions happening within their own borders is taken into account.

## Where it stands

The petition was distributed for a conference on September 28, 2026. Before that conference, the Court asked for a response from Pennsylvania's Secretary, which was originally due August 28, 2026 and has since been extended, most recently to November 2, 2026. The docket shows no further action since.

## What each outcome would mean

TitleMax, which lost in the Third Circuit, is asking the Court to take up the case. If the Court grants the petition, it will hear the case and decide whether the abstention doctrine was wrongly applied, with no outcome decided yet. If the Court denies the petition, the Third Circuit's ruling stands, meaning the companies' federal lawsuits remain dismissed and their constitutional challenges would have to be pursued, if at all, within the Pennsylvania enforcement proceeding.
