## In brief

TitleMax entities ask the Court to review whether Pennsylvania's enforcement action against them deserves federal court deference under a doctrine called Younger abstention.

## What happened

Pennsylvania's banking regulator accused several affiliated TitleMax companies of charging illegal interest rates on car-title loans to Pennsylvania residents and began an administrative enforcement proceeding against them. Rather than contest that proceeding directly, the companies sued Pennsylvania's secretary of banking in federal courts in their home states, arguing the state action improperly reached across state lines and violated their constitutional rights. The federal district court and the Third Circuit Court of Appeals both declined to intervene, holding that a doctrine requiring federal courts to step back from certain ongoing state proceedings (Younger abstention) applied because Pennsylvania had an important interest in enforcing its usury laws. Other appeals courts reached the same result in related cases.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked whether, in deciding if a state's interest in a pending enforcement proceeding is important enough to justify a federal court stepping back, courts must look only generally at whether the state is enforcing some legitimate law, or must also weigh whether the proceeding reaches into conduct and companies based in other states, in tension with the federal system's division of power between states.

## Where it stands

The petition has been fully briefed, including a brief opposing review, an amicus brief, and a reply brief, and was distributed for the Court's conference on December 4, 2026. No order on the petition has yet been recorded.

## What each outcome would mean

TitleMax, which lost in the lower court, is asking the Court to take up the case and reverse. If the Court grants review, it will hear full arguments and decide the legal question itself; a grant alone would not decide who wins. If the Court denies review, the Third Circuit's decision stands, leaving in place the ruling that federal courts should defer to Pennsylvania's enforcement proceeding, and TitleMax's federal lawsuit challenging that proceeding would remain dismissed.
