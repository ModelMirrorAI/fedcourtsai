## What happened

United Airlines required employees to be vaccinated against COVID-19 in 2021 but allowed religious and medical accommodation requests. Customer-facing employees who received religious accommodations, including pilots and flight attendants, were placed on unpaid leave rather than being allowed to keep working. A group of these employees sued under Title VII, arguing United failed to reasonably accommodate them. A federal district court certified a class of employees put on unpaid leave, and the Fifth Circuit affirmed, while also directing the trial court to create a new multi-step process to separately determine which class members had sincere religious beliefs before proceeding further. United asked the full Fifth Circuit to reconsider; that request was denied.

## What the Court is being asked

The case is about the rules for grouping many employees together into a single lawsuit, known as a class action. United argues that such a group can only be formed when a single yes-or-no answer would decide the outcome for everyone at once, and that here, deciding whether employees sincerely held their religious beliefs would necessarily produce different answers for different people. United says the lower court wrongly allowed the class to move forward anyway, creating a new procedure to sort out individual differences later. The petition also asks, if the Court does not decide this case outright, that it be held until the Court acts on a related case involving similar religious-accommodation questions.

## Where it stands

United Airlines filed its petition for certiorari, the request that the Supreme Court take up the case, on August 7, 2026, after receiving an extension of time to file. The other side's response is due September 11, 2026, and the Court has not yet acted on whether to hear the case.
