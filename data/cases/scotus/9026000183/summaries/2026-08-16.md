## In brief

United Airlines asks the Court to review a lower-court ruling allowing class certification of employees' religious-accommodation claims over its COVID-19 vaccine policy.

## What happened

United required employees to be vaccinated for COVID-19 in 2021 but allowed religious and medical exemption requests. Pilots and flight attendants who received religious exemptions were placed on unpaid leave, though they could apply for other roles. A group of these employees, led by Genise Kincannon and David Sambrano, sued United for failing to accommodate their religious beliefs. A federal district court certified a class of employees who received religious accommodations and were put on unpaid leave. The Fifth Circuit affirmed, while directing the trial court to create a new procedure to sort out, person by person, which class members had sincere religious objections. United asked the Fifth Circuit to reconsider that decision; it declined.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked whether a lawsuit can proceed as a class action when plaintiffs offer evidence a jury could accept for the group as a whole, even though rejecting that evidence would not defeat the whole class at once but would instead require deciding each person's claim separately based on individual facts.

## Where it stands

United filed its petition asking the Court to hear the case on August 7, 2026, after receiving an extension of time. The respondents' response is due September 11, 2026. The Court has not yet acted on the petition.

## What each outcome would mean

United, the employer, is asking the Court to take up the case. If the Court agrees to hear it, both sides will file full briefs and argue the case, and the Court will decide the legal standard for certifying classes like this one, without yet deciding who wins. If the Court declines to hear the case, the Fifth Circuit's ruling stands, leaving the class certified and allowing the case, including the new "class rostering" procedure the appeals court ordered, to continue in the trial court. The petition also asks the Court, if it does not grant review outright, to at least hold the case until it resolves a related petition, Detwiler, from a different circuit.
