# Forecast: United Airlines, Inc. v. Kincannon (No. 26-183)

Forecast at arrival: the petition was docketed August 12, 2026, the record shows
zero distributions, no CVSG, and no response yet (BIO due September 11, 2026).
Everything below forecasts forward from that state.

## Distribution and relists

The docket shows **zero distributions** — the arrival state, not a signal. The
petition will be distributed for conference with near certainty (~97%): it is a
paid petition by a solvent, committed petitioner represented by a former
Solicitor General, and the only paths to never-distributed are settlement or
withdrawal, neither likely in a certified ~1,000-member class action United is
fighting to unwind. Expect the first distribution in late September or October
2026, for an October or early-November conference, assuming the BIO arrives on
schedule or after one extension.

Beyond the first conference, I expect this petition's distribution count to run
higher than the typical paid petition's: roughly a 45% chance of at least one
true relist (a second distribution), through either of two routes — the Court
seriously considering a grant (this is a serial-relist profile: asserted circuit
split, published opinion, separate opinion below, top counsel), or a **hold for
Detwiler v. Mid-Columbia Medical Center**, which the petition itself requests as
fallback and which would show up as repeated redistributions or rescheduling
into 2027. If the hold route fires, the count could climb well past two.

## CVSG

Unlikely (~3%). The question presented is Rule 23 procedure in a private Title
VII suit; the federal government is not a party and no statute the government
administers is being construed in a way that turns on its views. If the Court
wants the SG's input on the underlying religious-accommodation questions, the
natural vehicle is Detwiler, not this petition.

## Which question presented

The petition presents a single QP. If the Court takes it, I expect it
substantially **as written** — it is already framed as a clean doctrinal
question about Wal-Mart/Amgen commonality and predominance — though the Court
might trim the framing to ask directly whether classwide evidence a jury could
reject member-by-member can satisfy Rule 23(a)(2) and (b)(3). (Registered
context-only; stated because it disciplines the rest.)

## Summary disposition vs. plenary review

Conditional on a grant, I put ~30% on the disposition riding in the cert order
rather than plenary review — almost all of that a **GVR in light of Detwiler**
(or of another intervening Rule 23 or Title VII decision), the route the
petition's own Part IV invites. A per curiam summary reversal is unlikely: the
Fifth Circuit's opinion is long, published, and fact-engaged, and the Court's
recent class-certification interventions (LabCorp v. Davis, OT2024) show it
wants plenary vehicles for Rule 23 doctrine, not summary correction. Note the
GVR route depends on Detwiler actually being granted and decided — as of this
forecast no SCOTUS docket for a Detwiler petition is visible on CourtListener,
so the hold request runs ahead of a petition I can confirm.

## If denied

If the Court denies, a **dissent from or statement respecting denial is
possible but not likely (~7%)** — somewhat above the background rate because
Judge Willett's separate opinion below ("no putative class would ever fail the
commonality requirement") gives a ready-made hook, and because Justices who
dissented in LabCorp's DIG (most plausibly Kavanaugh or Gorsuch) have shown
appetite to write on Rule 23 rigor. The cross-cutting valence — the class
members are religious objectors, whose claims several Justices view
sympathetically — cuts against a written dissent from the employer-favoring
side.

## Timing

No disposition before late 2026 on any route. A straight denial most plausibly
lands November 2026–January 2027; a grant most plausibly follows one or more
relists in the same window; a Detwiler hold pushes resolution to spring or
June 2027. (Timing is unscored; stated to discipline the rest.)

## If granted (conditional)

If granted for plenary review, the likely ground of decision is that Rule
23(a)(2)/(b)(3) commonality requires a question whose classwide answer resolves
liability-relevant issues "in one stroke," and that certification cannot be
rescued by deferring individualized adjudication to post-certification
mini-trials — i.e., reversal of the Fifth Circuit, in line with Wal-Mart and
Amgen. That is conditional context, not an unconditional claim.
