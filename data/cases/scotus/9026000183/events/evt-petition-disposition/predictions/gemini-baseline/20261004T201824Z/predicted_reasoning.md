The petition is currently at its first conference (the September 28 long conference) and has not yet been relisted. Given the high-profile nature of the petitioner (United Airlines), the presence of elite Supreme Court counsel, and support from major business amici (Airlines for America, IFA), this petition presents a strong vehicle for the Court to address Rule 23 class certification standards. However, because it is currently at its first conference, it is highly likely to be relisted if it is a serious candidate for a grant. I expect the Court to relist this petition at least once before disposing of it. A CVSG is unlikely because the dispute is primarily between private parties over procedural rules, without a strong federal programmatic interest.

If the Court grants review, it will likely take the single question presented as written, to clarify whether Rule 23(b)(3) predominance is satisfied by evidence that could lead to individualized, differing liability determinations, especially in a novel three-stage trial plan context. A summary disposition is highly unlikely given the need for plenary briefing on this complex procedural issue. Should the Court deny the petition, there is a reasonable possibility of a dissent from denial by one of the conservative Justices (such as Justice Alito or Justice Thomas) focused on curbing expansive class action certifications that diverge from Wal-Mart v. Dukes.
