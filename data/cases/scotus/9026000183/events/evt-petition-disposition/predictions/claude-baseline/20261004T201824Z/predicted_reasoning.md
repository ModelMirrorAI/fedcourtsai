# Forecast: what the Court will do with No. 26-183, United Airlines v. Kincannon

**Bottom line: denial is the likely outcome (P(any grant) = 0.13).** The petition
was first distributed for the September 28, 2026 long conference, and the docket
I was provisioned shows exactly one distribution, no CVSG, and two amicus briefs
in support (Airlines for America; International Franchise Association).

## Relists

The docket shows **one distribution** (for the 9/28/2026 conference). I expect the
petition **not** to be distributed again (P(at least one more distribution) =
0.27). This is a serious paid petition with elite counsel and amicus support, so
a relist is more likely than for the ordinary baseline-band petition, but the
modal path for a Rule 23(f) petition arguing abuse-of-discretion error on a
fact-heavy record is a single conference and a denial on the Term's opening order
list. If it is relisted, one or two further distributions is the likely shape —
either the Court working toward a grant or a Justice preparing a statement
respecting denial.

## CVSG

No CVSG is on the docket and I expect none (P = 0.04). No federal party is
involved and the question is Rule 23 procedure; the EEOC's Title VII interest is
peripheral to the commonality question actually presented.

## Which question the Court would take, if it grants

If the Court grants, it would take the petition's single question essentially
**as written** — whether commonality and predominance are satisfied when
plaintiffs offer a common theory and evidence a jury *could* credit even though
rejecting it would yield member-by-member liability outcomes — probably trimmed
of the "class rostering" framing and recast as a clean Wal-Mart/Amgen
"one stroke" question. The Court would not reach the damages-formula or
Title VII § 706(g) arguments as separate questions.

## Summary disposition versus plenary review

Conditional on a grant, plenary review is the likely route
(P(summary route | grant) = 0.12). The only summary path is a hold for the
anticipated *Detwiler v. Mid-Columbia Medical Center* petition (religious versus
secular belief under Title VII) followed by a GVR. That petition was not yet
filed when United's petition was filed and would be considered later in the fall,
so a hold at the long conference is unlikely; and *Detwiler*'s single-plaintiff
question bears only on the sincerity prong, which Judge Willett and the BIO both
say is unnecessary to the certification. No per curiam reversal: the decision
below is a deferential affirmance, not a candidate for summary correction.

## Dissent from denial

Conditional on denial, I expect **no separate writing** (P(some dissent or
statement respecting denial) = 0.10). The Justices most exercised about class
certification of partly uninjured classes (Justice Kavanaugh's *Lab Corp v.
Davis* dissent) are cross-pressured here: the class consists of religious
objectors to a vaccine mandate, and a writing urging stricter Rule 23 screening of
their claims is an awkward vehicle. If anything is written, a short statement
respecting denial noting the interlocutory posture is the likeliest form.

## If granted (conditional)

The likely ground would be that Wal-Mart's "one stroke" requirement is not met
where the common evidence, if rejected, leaves individualized liability
determinations — reversing or vacating the Fifth Circuit and disapproving the
"class rostering" workaround, with the undue-hardship question under Groff
possibly carved out as genuinely common. A 6–3 split along the familiar
class-action lines would be the expectation, though the religious-liberty
valence could scramble it.
