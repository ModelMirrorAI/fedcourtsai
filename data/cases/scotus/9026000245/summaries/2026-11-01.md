## In brief

AstraZeneca and other drug and medical-device makers ask the Court to shut down a lawsuit by Americans harmed by an Iraqi militia's attacks.

## What happened

American service members, civilians, and their families sued AstraZeneca and other pharmaceutical and medical-device companies, claiming the companies paid bribes and supplied extra free goods to Iraq's Ministry of Health while it was controlled by the militia Jaysh al-Mahdi, financing attacks that killed or injured them. A district court dismissed the case. The D.C. Circuit reversed, finding the claims adequately alleged aiding-and-abetting and direct liability under the Anti-Terrorism Act. After the Supreme Court sent the case back for reconsideration in light of its decision in a different terrorism case, the D.C. Circuit again ruled for the plaintiffs, prompting this petition.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked whether companies can be held responsible for aiding terrorism when their assistance was not tied to any specific attack and they did not intend to help attacks succeed, and whether a company's dealings with a foreign government agency, later said to have been infiltrated by a terrorist group, can count as directly causing the resulting attacks under the statute's causation rule.

## Where it stands

The petition has been fully briefed, including a brief opposing review, a letter correcting a citation, and a reply brief, and was distributed for the Court's conference on November 6, 2026. The docket does not yet show any action taken at or after that conference.

## What each outcome would mean

AstraZeneca and the other companies, as petitioners, are asking the Court to reverse the lower court's ruling, which favored Atchley and the other plaintiffs. If the Court agrees to hear the case, it would decide the legal standards for aiding-and-abetting and direct liability under the Anti-Terrorism Act, with no outcome yet determined. If the Court denies review, the D.C. Circuit's decision stands, and the plaintiffs' lawsuit against the companies continues in the lower courts.
