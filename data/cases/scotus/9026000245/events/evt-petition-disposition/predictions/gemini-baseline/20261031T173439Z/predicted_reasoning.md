# Predicted Court Trajectory and Disposition: *AstraZeneca UK Limited, et al. v. Joshua Atchley, et al.* (No. 26-245)

## 1. Conference Distribution and Relist Trajectory

The docket currently records **one distribution** (distributed on October 21, 2026, for the Conference of November 6, 2026) and zero relists. Because this case returns to the Court following the June 2024 GVR in *AstraZeneca UK Ltd. v. Atchley*, No. 23-9, and features elite Supreme Court advocates challenging the D.C. Circuit's unanimous reinstatement of both secondary and primary Antiterrorism Act (ATA) liability, there is a meaningful chance (`0.32`) that the Court relists the petition at least once after the November 6 conference to allow chambers to closely check the D.C. Circuit's remand opinion against *Twitter, Inc. v. Taamneh*, 598 U.S. 471 (2023), and *Smith & Wesson Brands, Inc. v. Estados Unidos Mexicanos*, 605 U.S. 280 (2025), or to permit a Justice to circulate a short statement or dissent from denial. Most likely, however, the Court will deny certiorari either directly out of the November 6, 2026 conference or after a single relist.

## 2. Call for the Views of the Solicitor General (CVSG)

The docket shows **no CVSG** in No. 26-245. In the prior petition (No. 23-9), the Court invited the Solicitor General's views in October 2023 and received a comprehensive brief in May 2024 addressing both JASTA aiding-and-abetting liability and direct ATA causation in this exact dispute. Although a change in Administration has occurred since the May 2024 amicus brief, the United States' legal and foreign-policy equities were already placed before the Court in No. 23-9, and no amicus briefs have been filed in No. 26-245 urging another invitation. Accordingly, the Court is unlikely (`0.08`) to issue a second CVSG; if it did call for the Solicitor General's views, that order would issue in November or December 2026 following one or two conferences.

## 3. Questions Presented and Scope of Review (Conditional on a Grant)

If the Court were to grant certiorari (`0.14`), it would grant both Question Presented 1 (aiding-and-abetting liability under 18 U.S.C. § 2333(d)(2)) and Question Presented 2 (direct liability and proximate causation under 18 U.S.C. § 2333(a)) as written in the petition. Because the D.C. Circuit held that the complaint independently states claims under both secondary and primary liability theories—and left its 2022 direct-liability holding intact verbatim—reviewing only Question Presented 1 would not dispose of respondents' action against the petitioners. If granted on the merits, the Court would likely reverse the D.C. Circuit on both questions, holding that under *Twitter* and *Smith & Wesson* allegations of corrupt commercial kickbacks and in-kind medical-supply bonuses paid to a terrorist-controlled government ministry do not establish culpable participation in a "near-common enterprise" to murder Americans without a more direct nexus to the terrorist attacks, and that furnishing fungible goods and payments to a dual-function government ministry does not satisfy proximate causation for direct ATA liability.

## 4. Summary Disposition vs. Plenary Review (Conditional on a Grant)

Conditional on a grant of certiorari, plenary review with full merits briefing and oral argument is overwhelmingly more likely than a summary disposition in the cert order itself (`summary-disposition-route: 0.07`). The Court already GVR'd *Atchley I* in June 2024 for reconsideration in light of *Twitter*, and the D.C. Circuit's 30-page remand opinion in *Atchley II* analyzed both *Twitter* (2023) and *Smith & Wesson* (2025) at length. Petitioners' passing suggestion of a second GVR in light of *Cisco Systems, Inc. v. Doe*, 609 U.S. ___ (2026), will not gain traction because *Cisco Systems* concerned whether the Alien Tort Statute and Torture Victim Protection Act imply an aiding-and-abetting cause of action at all, whereas JASTA expressly provides a statutory aiding-and-abetting cause of action under the ATA. Nor is summary reversal likely in a complex, fact-intensive appeal involving a 200-page complaint and two distinct statutory liability theories.

## 5. Dissent or Statement Respecting Denial (Conditional on a Denial)

Conditional on a denial of certiorari, the overwhelmingly likely outcome is an unsigned denial order with no separate writing (`0.96`). There is a modest probability (`dissent-from-denial: 0.04`) that one or more conservative Justices—most plausibly Justice Thomas (who authored *Twitter v. Taamneh*), joined by Justice Alito or Justice Gorsuch—files a brief dissent from the denial of certiorari or a statement respecting the denial emphasizing that the D.C. Circuit's expansive "near-common enterprise" reasoning stretches *Twitter* and *Smith & Wesson* at the pleading stage and inviting petitioners to renew their challenges after summary judgment or final judgment.
