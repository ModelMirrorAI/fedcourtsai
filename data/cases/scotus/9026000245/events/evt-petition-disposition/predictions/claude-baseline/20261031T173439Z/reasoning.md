# Reasoning — AstraZeneca UK Ltd. v. Atchley, No. 26-245 (P(grant) = 0.22)

**Inputs read:** snapshot `2026-10-31.json`; `context.json` (forward mode, band `baseline` under sal-v6, distribution_count 1, no CVSG, `cert_anchor.rate` 0.0519); the questions presented, petition, BIO (with the Oct. 9 letter correcting a citation), cert reply, and skimmed the appendix (truncated at the cap; the D.C. Circuit opinion on remand is at its front). `same-question.json` lists no matching petitions.

**Posture.** This is the second time the case has reached the Court. In No. 23-9 the Court called for the SG's views. The SG recommended a GVR in light of *Twitter v. Taamneh*, and the Court entered one (the corpus row for 23-9 confirms the SG appeared). On remand (Jan. 23, 2026; rehearing en banc denied Apr. 23, 2026) the D.C. Circuit again let the aiding-and-abetting and direct-liability ATA claims go forward. It held that a nexus to specific attacks was unnecessary because "money is fungible" and attacks were foreseeable. It also held that culpability did not require intent to aid terrorism. The case was distributed once, for the Nov. 6, 2026 conference. Two amicus filings are on the docket (the U.S. Chamber, and a motion from Charity & Security Network). Lisa Blatt is counsel for petitioners, and Kellogg Hansen (Branson) for respondents.

**Anchor.** The frozen anchor is 5.2%: the baseline band's pooled reached rate. The statpack's paid-segment cuts give useful context: relist bucket 1 is about 13.5% grant+GVR; the 2+ amicus bucket is about 22.7% grant family; the CVSG bucket is about 35%.

**Adjustments up.**
- There is a credible asserted split with the Second Circuit's *Ashley v. Deutsche Bank*, 144 F.4th 420. The petition also asserts a conflict with the Second and Seventh Circuits on proximate cause. The BIO says the split "rests on a single decision" and that the cases differ on their facts. Even so, two circuits that hear most ATA litigation applying *Taamneh* differently is a real problem.
- The Court has already spent a GVR on this case, and the SG's earlier brief criticised the D.C. Circuit's nexus and culpability analysis. A lower court that arguably repeated the error after a GVR draws attention.
- The petition has elite counsel, a Chamber amicus, and sophisticated corporate petitioners. That puts it in a population far above the baseline band's.

**Adjustments down.**
- The posture is interlocutory: a motion to dismiss on appeal. The BIO presses this as a final vehicle problem.
- The disputes are factbound applications of *Taamneh* at the pleading stage.
- The Court may prefer to let *Taamneh* percolate.

**Path.** I think a second CVSG is the single likeliest non-denial outcome, about 42%. The United States has a strong interest here: reconstruction contracting, foreign policy, and the ATA's reach. The Court asked for the SG's views last time, and the current administration's views may differ from the 2023 brief. My decomposition is P(CVSG) 0.42 × P(grant | CVSG) about 0.33, plus P(no CVSG) 0.58 × P(grant | no CVSG) about 0.13, which gives roughly 0.21. I round to 0.22. A GVR is unlikely because no intervening decision is apparent.

**Increments.** For relist-increment (0.55), the petition is distributed again if the Court calls for the SG's views (it is redistributed after the SG's brief), relists it, or reschedules it. It is not distributed again only if it is denied outright at the Nov. 6 conference, which I put at about 45%. For dissent-from-denial (0.08): a denial in a statutory business case seldom draws a writing, but a statement from Thomas, Alito, Gorsuch or Kavanaugh about ATA overreach is possible.

**Uncertainty.** I could not check the current administration's position on ATA suits. It has backed terrorism victims in other contexts, such as *Fuld*, which could cut against petitioners at a CVSG stage. I did not verify the citations in *Ashley* independently.
