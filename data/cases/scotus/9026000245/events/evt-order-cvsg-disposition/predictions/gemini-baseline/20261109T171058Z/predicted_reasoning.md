## 2. Disposition and Scope of Review If Granted

- **Overall Disposition (`disposition`: 0.43; `predicted_disposition`: `denied`):** The Court is slightly more likely to deny certiorari (55% denied, 2% dismissed) than to grant review (41% plenary grant, 2% GVR), following the Solicitor General's expected recommendation that interlocutory review at the Rule 12(b)(6) stage remains premature while threshold direct-liability elements and disputed facts regarding the operation of the Iraqi Ministry of Health are on remand in the district court.
- **Questions Presented Taken If Granted:** If the Court grants certiorari, it is most likely to grant review limited to **Question 1**—the scope of aiding-and-abetting liability under JASTA, 18 U.S.C. § 2333(d)(2), and whether the D.C. Circuit's reliance on the fungibility of money and rejection of specific intent to further terrorism conflicts with *Twitter, Inc. v. Taamneh*, 598 U.S. 471 (2023), *Smith & Wesson Brands, Inc. v. Estados Unidos Mexicanos*, 605 U.S. 280 (2025), and *Ashley v. Deutsche Bank Aktiengesellschaft*, 144 F.4th 420 (2d Cir. 2025)—while declining Question 2 (ATA direct-liability proximate causation), which the Solicitor General previously advised in No. 23-9 turns on case-specific allegations that Jaysh al-Mahdi physically controlled the Ministry. If the Court grants on Question 1, it would likely reverse or vacate the D.C. Circuit's aiding-and-abetting holding by reaffirming that when commercial transactions are not linked to specific terrorist attacks, plaintiffs must plead pervasive and systemic culpable participation rather than relying on financial fungibility across a multi-year insurgency.

## 3. Summary Disposition vs. Plenary Review

- **Summary Disposition Route (`summary-disposition-route`: 0.05 conditional on grant):** Conditional on granting certiorari, the Court will almost certainly (95%) set the case for full merits briefing and oral argument rather than disposing of it summarily in the cert order. The Court already issued a GVR in this exact case in June 2024 (`AstraZeneca UK Ltd. v. Atchley`, 144 S. Ct. 2675 (2024)) so the D.C. Circuit could apply *Taamneh* in the first instance, and the D.C. Circuit responded with a 38-page published opinion addressing both *Taamneh* and *Smith & Wesson*. Neither a second GVR nor a summary reversal without oral argument is a realistic vehicle for resolving this complex statutory dispute.
