# Forecast of the Court's action

The Court will most likely deny the petition after receiving the Solicitor General's invited brief and the parties' responses. The probability of any grant is 46%, so this is a narrow denial forecast, not a routine low-interest petition. The strongest reason to deny will be the interlocutory posture and the possibility that the supposed circuit conflict reflects different factual allegations rather than different governing rules.

The docket already records **one distribution**, for the November 6, 2026 conference, and the **November 9 CVSG**. The Court will wait for the invited brief, likely for several months, and then redistribute the petition. I assign 98% to at least one additional distribution; the modal path is one post-brief redistribution, with a further relist possible before final action. This prediction does not count the existing distribution as a future relist. There is no new CVSG to predict: the invitation has issued, and the increment claim is vacuous.

If the Court grants review, it will most likely take the first question in a neutral formulation: what connection between assistance and particular attacks, and what culpable participation, are required for aiding-and-abetting liability under 18 U.S.C. § 2333(d)(2) after Taamneh. That formulation will not assume that the pleaded aid was neither systemic nor pervasive, or that the allegations establish no relevant intent. The direct-liability/proximate-cause question is a less likely independent basis for review and could be left outside a limited grant.

Conditional on granting, plenary briefing and argument are substantially more likely than summary action: 85% plenary, 15% summary. The Court has already sent the case back for application of Taamneh, making a second remand solely to consider that same decision less useful. If it grants, the likely merits dispute will concern whether sustained, tailored terrorist financing sufficiently connects the defendants to the attacks despite the absence of transaction-by-transaction tracing; no unconditional merits holding is forecast here.

If the petition is denied, expect no published dissent or statement; the conditional chance of some such writing is 14%. If one appears, Thomas or Kavanaugh is a plausible author expressing concern about the reach of aiding-and-abetting liability, with Alito also a plausible writer. Those names are contextual expectations, not predicted cert votes.
