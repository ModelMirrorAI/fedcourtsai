# Forecast: Mendenhall v. City and County of Denver, No. 25-1205

**Disposition.** The Court will deny certiorari. The petition asks the Court to
overrule *Jones v. United States* (1960) and hold that the Fourth Amendment's
"Oath or affirmation" requires a witness with firsthand knowledge to swear
before a warrant issues. There is no circuit split and cannot be one, because
every lower court is bound by *Jones*; the Court will treat that as a reason to
wait for a signal from its own members rather than to grant.

**Relists.** The docket shows two distributions: June 2 (for the June 18,
2026 conference, pre-empted when the Court called for a response on June 15)
and July 29 (for the September 28, 2026 long conference). So the petition has
never been considered and held over at a conference; its second distribution is
the ordinary post-response redistribution, not a relist. From here I expect,
more likely than not, a decision on the first order list of October Term 2026
(October 5, 2026) with no further distribution. A single relist is the main
alternative, most plausibly because a Justice is writing a statement about the
denial. Two or more relists are unlikely.

**CVSG.** No call for the views of the Solicitor General. The United States is
not a party, and the Court already chose its intervention at the June
conference by calling for Denver's response rather than the SG's views. Federal
warrant practice would be transformed by the rule the petition proposes, so a
CVSG is conceivable, but the SG's position on overruling *Jones* is predictable
and the Court rarely asks a question whose answer it knows.

**Which question, if taken.** If the Court did grant, it would take the question
as written: whether to overrule *Jones*. There is no narrower version, since the
petitioner concedes *Jones* is dispositive and the lower courts decided nothing
else. The Court would not reformulate it into a totality-of-the-circumstances
sufficiency question, because the petitioner disclaimed that route.

**Summary disposition.** Not the route. No intervening decision bears on the
oath question, so a GVR has nothing to point to, and the Court would not
overrule a 65-year-old precedent per curiam. Any grant would be plenary.

**Dissent or statement on denial.** More likely than not the denial is silent.
The realistic alternative is a short statement or dissent from Justice Gorsuch
or Justice Thomas, framed around the text-and-history argument and the
*Crawford*/*Ramos* analogy; a statement from Justice Sotomayor on the
police-accountability facts (the Breonna Taylor and Anjanette Young amici) is a
less likely second path. I put the existence of any such writing at roughly one
in five.

**Conditional merits note.** If granted and argued, the likelier judgment is an
affirmance reaffirming *Jones* on stare decisis and workability grounds, with an
originalist dissent. That is conditional on a grant I do not expect.
