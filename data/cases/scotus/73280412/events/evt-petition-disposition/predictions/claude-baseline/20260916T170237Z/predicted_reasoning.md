# Forecast: what the Court will do with No. 25-901, Garcia v. Hobbs

**Docket state forecast from.** Three `DISTRIBUTED` entries (Mar 4 for 3/20, Mar 17 for 4/2, Jun 17 for 9/28), one `Rescheduled` (Mar 11), a `Response Requested` order (Mar 25) after the State had waived, the State's brief filed Jun 2, petitioner's reply Jun 10. No CVSG. The petition now sits on the September 28, 2026 long conference alongside its companion, Trevino v. Hobbs, No. 25-918 (same counsel, same Ninth Circuit panel, same decision day, same response-requested date, same conference).

**Disposition.** The likeliest outcome is a **grant, vacate, and remand** to the Ninth Circuit "for further consideration in light of Louisiana v. Callais" (decided April 29, 2026), entered on the same order list as a GVR in Trevino. The State of Washington, the only respondent taking a position, asks for exactly that in both cases; no party in this docket opposes vacatur; and the Court has already GVR'd a run of Section 2 judgments after Callais (Turtle Mountain, Bd. of Election Comm'rs v. NAACP, Allen v. Caster). The mootness holding below rests entirely on the Soto Palmer Section 2 judgment, so if that judgment goes back to the Ninth Circuit, so should this one. P(any grant) = 0.60.

The main alternative is a plain **denial** of both petitions, most likely because a majority accepts the Soto Palmer plaintiffs' argument that the Trevino intervenors lack standing to appeal the liability determination, in which case the State's conditional GVR request in Garcia lapses and the Court leaves an unpublished, split-free mootness memorandum alone. A **plenary grant** on the mootness question standing alone (Covington and Moore v. Harper) is possible but a distant third: the Court asked for a response before Callais came down, so at least one chambers saw a live issue, but the cleaner post-Callais path is a remand, not argument over whether a claim was moot under a judgment that may itself be vacated.

**Summary route, conditional on a grant.** Roughly four in five grants here are a GVR rather than plenary review (`summary-disposition-route` = 0.80). A hold pending a plenary grant in Trevino followed by a GVR still resolves as a summary route.

**Relists.** Expect a meaningful chance of exactly one further distribution: the pair is procedurally tangled (a standing objection in Trevino, a consent-to-vacatur by the State, a contested intervening decision), and any Justice wishing to write a statement or dissent on either petition would cause a relist from the long conference. I put P(at least one more `DISTRIBUTED` entry) at 0.40; two or more relists are unlikely, since this is not a case the Court would sit on for weeks.

**CVSG.** None. No federal party, no federal statute at issue in this petition (the mootness question is Article III), and the Court has already had the Solicitor General's views on Callais itself. P(CVSG) = 0.03.

**Which question, if plenary.** If the Court took the case for argument it would take the petition's single QP substantially as written, probably trimmed of the "intensifies the plaintiff's racial classification injury" characterization, i.e. whether a racial-gerrymander claim becomes moot when the district is replaced by a court-ordered remedy in a separate proceeding that remains under appellate review. If granted, the likely ground is Moore v. Harper's snap-back rule rather than the Covington continuing-injury theory.

**Dissent or statement if denied.** If the Court denies, there is a real chance (0.30) that Justice Thomas or Justice Alito writes, either a statement respecting denial noting the Callais question left open or a dissent adopting Judge VanDyke's view that a remedy ordering more racial sorting cannot moot a claim against racial sorting. Justice Kagan's side has no reason to write on a denial.

**Timing.** Orders from the September 28 conference issue in early October 2026; a relisted petition would resolve in October or early November 2026.
