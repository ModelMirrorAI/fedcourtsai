## In brief

Benancio Garcia asks the Court to revive his lawsuit claiming Washington's Legislative District 15 was an unconstitutional racial gerrymander, after lower courts dismissed his claim as moot.

## What happened

Garcia, a resident of Legislative District 15 in Washington, sued the Secretary of State and the State, arguing that the district was drawn by sorting voters based on race in violation of the Constitution's equal protection guarantee. While his case was pending, a different court, in a separate lawsuit called Soto Palmer, ruled that the same district violated the Voting Rights Act and ordered the state to redraw it. The three-judge panel hearing Garcia's case then dismissed his claim as moot, reasoning the new district replaced the old one. Garcia appealed, and the Ninth Circuit affirmed.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked whether a lawsuit challenging a voting district as racially discriminatory becomes moot, and can no longer be decided, once a separate court case replaces that district with a new one, when the plaintiff says the replacement map continues or worsens the same racial sorting he objected to and that map remains under appeal elsewhere.

## Where it stands

The petition has been distributed for the Court's conference on September 28, 2026, after full briefing including the state's response and Garcia's reply; the docket does not yet show any action taken at that conference.

## What each outcome would mean

Garcia is asking the Court to take up his case. If the Court grants review, it would hear full arguments and decide whether his claim was wrongly dismissed as moot; a grant would not itself decide who is right. If the Court denies review, the Ninth Circuit's ruling that his claim is moot stands, leaving his constitutional challenge dismissed without a decision on its merits. A denial would not mean the Court agreed the lower court was correct, only that the case ends there for now.
