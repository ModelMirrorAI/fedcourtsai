## In brief

The Court vacated a Ninth Circuit ruling that Benancio Garcia's challenge to a Washington legislative district was moot, sending the case back for reconsideration.

## What happened

Benancio Garcia III sued Washington's Secretary of State and the State, arguing that Legislative District 15 was an unconstitutional racial gerrymander that sorted him by his Hispanic ethnicity. While his case was pending, a different judge, in a separate lawsuit over the same district brought under the Voting Rights Act, ordered the district redrawn. A three-judge panel then dismissed Garcia's claim as moot, reasoning that the new district replaced the one he challenged. Garcia appealed, and the Ninth Circuit affirmed the dismissal.

## What the Court is being asked

The question is whether a lawsuit claiming a voting district improperly sorted people by race becomes moot, meaning there is nothing left for a court to decide, once that district is replaced through a separate court order, when the replacement map keeps or deepens the same kind of racial sorting the plaintiff objected to, and when that separate order is still being appealed.

## Where it stands

The Court granted the petition, set aside the Ninth Circuit's judgment, and sent the case back for further consideration in light of its decision in a different redistricting case, Louisiana v. Callais.

## What each outcome would mean

Garcia, who brought the original lawsuit, asked the Court to revive his case after the lower courts dismissed it as moot. By sending the case back, the Court did not decide whether his claim is moot or who should win; it only told the Ninth Circuit to reexamine its ruling using the standards set out in the newer Callais decision. The dismissal of Garcia's claim is no longer in effect, and the Ninth Circuit must now reconsider the mootness question.
