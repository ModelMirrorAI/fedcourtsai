## In brief

The Court granted a Hispanic voter's challenge to his Washington legislative district's dismissal, vacating the lower ruling for reconsideration in light of a newer decision.

## What happened

Benancio Garcia III sued Washington's Secretary of State and the State, claiming his legislative district, District 15, was an unconstitutional racial gerrymander. While his case was pending, a different judge in a separate voting-rights lawsuit struck down the same district and ordered a replacement map. A three-judge panel then dismissed Garcia's claim as moot, reasoning the new map left nothing more to decide. After this Court sent the case back once before for a procedural fix, the panel reissued the same dismissal, and the Ninth Circuit affirmed it.

## What the Court is being asked

The question is whether Garcia's claim became moot once his district was replaced, even though he says the replacement map still sorts voters by race and arguably makes that problem worse, and even though an appeal in the other case could still restore the original district.

## Where it stands

After briefing and more than one distribution for conference, the Court granted the petition, set aside the Ninth Circuit's judgment, and sent the case back for further consideration in light of a recently decided redistricting case.

## What each outcome would mean

Garcia, the petitioner, asked the Court to revive his claim after lower courts dismissed it as moot. The Court's action sends the case back to the Ninth Circuit without deciding whether Garcia's claim is actually moot or whether the district violated his rights. The lower court must now reconsider the case using guidance from the newer ruling the Court cited. Washington and its Secretary of State, who defended the dismissal below, do not yet have a final win, and Garcia does not yet have his claim restored; both questions remain open for the Ninth Circuit to address on remand.
