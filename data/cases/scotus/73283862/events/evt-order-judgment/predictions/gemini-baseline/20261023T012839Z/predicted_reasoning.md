# Predicted Court Reasoning: *Corey Duran Berry v. United States* (No. 25-7026)

## 1. Judgment Class and Vote Lineup

- **Judgment Class:** **`vacated`** — The judgment (order) of the United States Court of Appeals for the Eleventh Circuit denying a certificate of appealability (COA) will be vacated, and the case remanded for further proceedings consistent with the Court's opinion.
- **Vote Lineup:** **7–2** to vacate and remand.
  - **Majority (7):** Chief Justice Roberts (delivering the opinion of the Court), joined by Justices Sotomayor, Kagan, Gorsuch, Kavanaugh, Barrett, and Jackson.
  - **Dissent (2):** Justice Thomas (filing a dissenting opinion), joined by Justice Alito.

## 2. Scope of Review (Questions Presented Reached)

In accordance with the October 13, 2026 order granting certiorari limited to Question 2 presented by the petition, the Court will address only Question 2: whether a certificate of appealability under 28 U.S.C. § 2253(c) may be denied solely because binding precedent of the court of appeals forecloses the constitutional claim the applicant seeks to raise, when another federal court of appeals has resolved that constitutional question in the applicant's favor. The Court will not reach Question 1 (whether a 28 U.S.C. § 2255 movant raising a claim under *United States v. Davis*, 588 U.S. 445 (2019), must prove as a matter of historical fact that the sentencing court relied solely on 18 U.S.C. § 924(c)(3)(B)'s residual clause).

## 3. Majority Ground (`majority-ground`)

The Court will hold that under the plain text of 28 U.S.C. § 2253(c)(2) and its settled precedents in *Lozada v. Deeds*, 498 U.S. 430, 432 (1991) (per curiam), *Slack v. McDaniel*, 529 U.S. 473, 484 (2000), *Miller-El v. Cockrell*, 537 U.S. 322, 336–38 (2003), and *Buck v. Davis*, 580 U.S. 100, 115–16 (2017), a habeas applicant makes a "substantial showing of the denial of a constitutional right" whenever "jurists of reason could disagree with the district court's resolution of his constitutional claims or that jurists could conclude the issues presented are adequate to deserve encouragement to proceed further."

Specifically, the majority will ground its holding on three doctrinal points:
1. **Objective Nationwide Inquiry Into "Reasonable Jurists":** As the Court held in *Lozada*, 498 U.S. at 432, the fact that another federal court of appeals has resolved the same constitutional question in the petitioner's favor demonstrates that the question is debatable among jurists of reason. The Eleventh Circuit's contrary rule in *Hamilton v. Secretary, Florida Department of Corrections*, 793 F.3d 1261, 1266 (11th Cir. 2015), improperly conflates whether a three-judge panel of the court of appeals is bound by prior circuit precedent with whether the underlying constitutional question is objectively debatable among reasonable jurists.
2. **Distinction Between Threshold COA Debatability and Panel-Level Merits Disposition:** Relying directly on *Miller-El*, 537 U.S. at 338—which explained that "a claim can be debatable even though every jurist of reason might agree, after the COA has been granted and the case has received full consideration, that petitioner will not prevail"—the majority will reject the Government's argument that a COA is pointless whenever a three-judge panel is bound by circuit precedent.
3. **Preservation of Full Appellate and En Banc Review:** The majority will note that treating binding circuit precedent as an automatic bar to a COA insulates debatable constitutional holdings from normal appellate and en banc reconsideration in habeas cases (especially where circuit rules restrict rehearing en banc of single-judge COA denials) and deprives both the court of appeals and this Court of a complete appellate record. The Court will also reject footnote 1 of the Eleventh Circuit's order, explaining that when the underlying § 2255 claim alleges an unconstitutional conviction under *Davis*, debating the application of the *Beeman* framework to that claim directly concerns "the denial of a constitutional right" under § 2253(c)(2).

## 4. Breadth of Ground (`ground-breadth`)

The Court will announce a general, categorical rule rejecting the Fifth and Eleventh Circuits' doctrine that adverse circuit precedent automatically precludes issuance of a COA under 28 U.S.C. § 2253(c) when other circuits have resolved the constitutional issue in the prisoner's favor. At the same time, the Court will keep its disposition narrow with respect to the case-specific defenses raised by the Government: following *Welch v. United States*, 578 U.S. 120, 134–35 (2016), the Court will decline to decide in the first instance whether Berry's § 924(c) conviction remains valid under § 924(c)(3)(A)'s elements clause (because attempted federal carjacking under 18 U.S.C. § 2119 requires "intent to cause death or serious bodily harm") or whether Berry's claim is barred by procedural default, leaving those unaddressed threshold or alternative grounds for the Eleventh Circuit to consider on remand.

## 5. Separate Writings and Procedural Exits

- **Dissent:** Justice Thomas, joined by Justice Alito, will dissent on the ground that § 2253(c) was enacted in AEDPA to prevent futile habeas appeals, that issuing a COA when a three-judge panel is legally bound to affirm serves no statutory purpose when a prisoner may petition for certiorari directly from a COA denial under *Hohn v. United States*, 524 U.S. 236 (1998), and that Berry's underlying § 2255 motion plainly fails on alternative grounds because attempted carjacking under 18 U.S.C. § 2119 is a crime of violence and his claim is procedurally defaulted.
- **Procedural Exits:** A dismissal as improvidently granted (`dismissed-as-improvidently-granted`) or affirmance by an equally divided Court is unlikely. The vehicle objections raised in the Brief in Opposition (procedural default and the § 2119 elements-clause argument) were fully aired in the cert-stage pleadings before the Court granted review limited to Question 2, and no Justice has a known recusal trigger (all participating Eleventh Circuit and Southern District of Florida judges remain on the lower courts).
