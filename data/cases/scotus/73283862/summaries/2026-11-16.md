## In brief

The Supreme Court agreed to hear a federal prisoner's challenge to how appeals courts decide whether he may appeal the denial of his sentence challenge.

## What happened

Corey Berry pleaded guilty to carjacking offenses and a related firearm charge. After the Supreme Court later struck down part of the firearm law as too vague, Berry asked a federal court to throw out his firearm conviction, arguing it may have rested on the invalid part of the law. Courts in his circuit, applying circuit precedent, said he had to prove his conviction rested only on the invalid provision, something an unclear record made hard to show. The district court and the Eleventh Circuit Court of Appeals both denied him a certificate of appealability, a step required before he could appeal further, reasoning that circuit precedent foreclosed his claim. Berry asked the Supreme Court to review that denial.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked whether an appeals court may refuse to let a prisoner appeal when his argument is blocked by binding precedent in his own circuit, even though other circuits have accepted the same argument and would have ruled in his favor.

## Where it stands

The Supreme Court granted Berry's petition for certiorari and his request to proceed without paying filing fees. The case has not yet been argued; the parties are now preparing their merits briefs, with deadlines extended into December 2026 and January 2027.

## What each outcome would mean

Berry, the prisoner, asked the Court to take the case and is asking it to reverse the lower court's refusal to let him appeal. If the Court ultimately rules for Berry, it would hold that a certificate of appealability must issue when other circuits disagree with the prisoner's home circuit, letting his appeal go forward without deciding whether his underlying sentencing claim succeeds. If it rules for the government, the circuit's refusal to allow an appeal would stand, and his case would end. The Court could also send the case back without resolving the question either way.
