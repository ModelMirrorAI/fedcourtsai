## In brief

Corey Berry asked the Court to decide whether he was wrongly denied permission to appeal a ruling against him on his challenge to a firearm conviction.

## What happened

Berry pleaded guilty to carjacking offenses and a related firearm charge. After the Supreme Court later struck down part of the law underlying that firearm charge, Berry tried to challenge his conviction, but lower courts ruled he had not shown his conviction actually rested on the invalid part of the law. To appeal that ruling, he needed a "certificate of appealability," which requires showing that reasonable judges could disagree with the lower court's decision. The appeals court denied him that certificate, reasoning that circuit precedent foreclosed his claim, even though other circuits apply a more favorable rule. Berry asked the Supreme Court to review that denial.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked whether a prisoner should be allowed to appeal when the legal rule that defeated his claim is binding in his circuit but other circuits have adopted a more favorable rule. The question is whether such a conflict among circuits by itself means judges could reasonably disagree with the lower court, entitling the prisoner to pursue the appeal.

## What each outcome would mean

Berry, the prisoner, asked the Court to overturn the lower court's denial of permission to appeal.
