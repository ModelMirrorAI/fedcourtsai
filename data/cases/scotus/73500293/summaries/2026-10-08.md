## In brief

Music publishers ask the Court to decide whether an author who reclaims his copyright under federal termination law gets back only U.S. rights or rights worldwide.

## What happened

Cyril Vetter co-wrote "Double Shot (Of My Baby's Love)" in 1962 and assigned worldwide copyright rights to a publisher. Decades later he invoked a federal copyright law provision letting authors terminate old grants and reclaim rights. Vetter sued, claiming he recaptured ownership of the song everywhere, not just in the United States. The publisher's successor, Resnik, disputed this; after Resnik lost, music companies including BMG bought Resnik's interest specifically to pursue this appeal. The district court and the Fifth Circuit Court of Appeals both ruled for Vetter, holding that termination returned all rights, foreign and domestic, because they all originated under U.S. copyright law.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked whether a law letting authors cancel old copyright deals and get their rights back gives them back only the U.S. portion of those rights, or also the rights to the same work in other countries.

## Where it stands

The petition was distributed for the Court's October 16, 2026 conference after full briefing, including a brief in opposition, multiple amicus briefs, and a reply; the docket does not yet show any action taken at or after that conference.

## What each outcome would mean

BMG and the other music companies, as petitioners, are asking the Court to take up and reverse the Fifth Circuit's ruling. If the Court grants review, it will hear full arguments and decide the scope of termination rights itself, deciding nothing yet. If it denies review, the Fifth Circuit's decision favoring Vetter stands, meaning his termination recaptured worldwide rights to the song; a denial would not mean the Court agrees with that result, only that the lower ruling remains in place.
