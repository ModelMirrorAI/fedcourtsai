# Forecast: what the Court will do with No. 25-1391, BMG Rights Management (US) LLC v. Vetter

**State forecast from.** The docket shows **one** distribution, for the Conference of October 16, 2026. No CVSG is on the docket. Five cert-stage amicus briefs (Paramount, the Motion Picture Association, 20-plus intellectual-property law professors, RIAA/NMPA/A2IM, and IFPI with other international federations) support the petition. The reply brief was filed and distributed on September 30, 2026.

**Relists.** The petition will not be denied outright at its first conference. I expect at least one further distribution: either a relist from the October 16 conference, or a CVSG order followed by a redistribution once the Solicitor General files. Most likely path is one to two further distributions over the coming weeks; if a CVSG issues, the redistribution comes months later (spring 2027) and would then be followed by a relist or two before the grant/deny decision.

**CVSG.** A call for the views of the Solicitor General is a live possibility, around one in three. The reply brief itself invites one in its final footnote. The federal interest is real: the limiting language in sections 203(b)(5) and 304(c)(6)(E) came verbatim from the Copyright Office's 1965 Supplementary Report, the Office has long read termination as reaching only U.S. rights, and the petition frames the Fifth Circuit's "single international copyright" theory as a Berne Convention and comity problem. If the Court does not grant or deny on the first or second conference, the CVSG is the probable off-ramp, and it would most likely issue in late October or November 2026.

**The question presented.** If the Court takes the case, it takes the single question as written: whether reversion by operation of U.S. law (termination under section 304(c), and by the petition's framing the renewal-term reversion as well) reaches foreign copyrights. There is no second question to shed. The Court would not reformulate to add the section 101 "place of effect" definition argument the BIO leads with; that is a merits argument inside the same question.

**Summary disposition.** No. There is no intervening decision bearing on the question, the Fifth Circuit opinion is published and reasoned, and the respondent is ably represented. If review is granted it is plenary, with argument in the October 2027 Term if the grant comes after a CVSG, or late in the October 2026 Term if it comes by January 2027.

**Dissent from denial.** If the petition is denied, expect a bare denial. The question is commercial and statutory, with no constitutional or criminal-justice hook that typically draws a dissent from denial; the likeliest separate writing would be a short statement noting the issue's importance and the absence of a circuit split, and even that is improbable.

**Overall disposition.** Denial remains more likely than not, chiefly because this is the first and only appellate decision on the question and the BIO's percolation and vehicle arguments are the kind the Court routinely credits. But this is a high-quality petition on a question with international and industry-wide stakes, and a grant, with or without a CVSG first, is a substantial possibility (roughly 30 percent). If granted, the likely ground of decision would be the text of section 304(c)(6)(E) ("rights arising under ... foreign laws"), read with the presumption against extraterritoriality, and the likely result is reversal of the Fifth Circuit.
