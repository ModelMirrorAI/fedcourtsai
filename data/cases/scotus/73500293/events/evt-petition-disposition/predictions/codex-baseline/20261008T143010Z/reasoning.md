## Why the probability rises to 32%, but not above 50%

**Reasons for a substantial upward adjustment.** The petition presents a concrete statutory question with effects beyond the immediate ownership of Double Shot. Its main argument is that § 304(c)(6)(E), together with the analogous § 203 provision, preserves foreign-law rights when a U.S. grant is terminated. The petition links the contrary rule to uncertainty in international licensing and catalog transactions, and identifies a substantial body of contrary commentary and practice. See provisioned petition, printed pp. 18–35. The docket's five amicus filings from film, music, international-industry, and academic participants support treating those commercial stakes as more than an isolated litigant's concern. This is much stronger substantive evidence of certworthiness than the typical petition in the private baseline risk set.

**The principal restraint is a weak circuit-conflict showing.** Respondents argue that the principal Second Circuit authority concerns the derivative-works exception and merely assumes the publisher retained foreign rights; the directly contrary termination ruling cited is a district-court decision, not a square appellate conflict. See opposition, printed pp. 13–17. I checked Fred Ahlert Music Corp. v. Warner/Chappell Music, Inc., 155 F.3d 17 (2d Cir. 1998), using CourtListener opinion 6974949. Its opening identifies the derivative-works exception as the issue, and its foreign-rights discussion at approximately pp. 20–21 occurs in the factual account of domestic and foreign licenses. This supports treating the petition's conflict argument as tension with established understanding rather than a verified holding-to-holding circuit split. I did not independently verify every authority cited by either side.

**There is also a genuine, but not necessarily fatal, vehicle concern.** The Fifth Circuit separately affirmed Vetter's recaptured interest and the renewal interest purchased from Smith's heirs. See petition appendix, pp. 1–2 and 25–34; opposition, printed pp. 17–18. Respondents say the separate renewal ruling is not adequately challenged, so success on termination would not yield the unilateral worldwide control petitioners seek. The broadly phrased question presented does mention reversion generally, which makes the scope of the challenge less categorical than the opposition suggests. Moreover, partial ownership and economic rights can still matter even if exclusive control remains unavailable. I therefore discount for issue entanglement and practical-relief concerns without treating the opposition's no-meaningful-relief assertion as an established jurisdictional defect.

**The merits are contested, not a substitute for certworthiness.** Respondents defend a domestic ownership/transfer rule, invoking § 101's transfer definition, statutory purpose, and the distinction between ownership and foreign infringement. See opposition, printed pp. 22–30. Petitioners emphasize the express foreign-law limitation and territorial copyright structure. I find the latter sufficiently powerful to support review interest, but a plausible merits objection to the Fifth Circuit does not establish that four Justices will intervene before more appellate consideration.

The resulting 32% is a judgmental adjustment from the 5.12% risk-set anchor, not a fitted likelihood model. The increase reflects the unusually substantial legal and commercial question; the remaining 68% no-grant probability reflects the lack of a clean circuit split, the vehicle complication, and the absence of any observed relist or invitation. Denial is the modal disposition. The main uncertainty is how strongly the Court will prioritize immediate commercial uniformity over percolation.

## Other probabilities and significance

- **Further distribution, 45%:** the serious question and possible governmental consultation make additional consideration plausible, but there has been only one distribution, not a demonstrated pattern of repeat conferences. This claim includes later distribution after a CVSG as well as an ordinary relist.
- **New CVSG, 22%:** copyright administration and international effects create a specific reason to consult the government despite the private-party caption. Most probability remains on deciding without one. The CVSG and distribution claims overlap; they are not competing outcomes.
- **Summary route given grant, 4%:** no intervening controlling development identified in the permitted record supplies an obvious remand trigger. A fully briefed statutory dispute is the stronger grant route.
- **Dissent or statement given denial, 8%:** the stakes can attract a statement, but the record provides no particular Justice-specific signal and the ordinary denial forecast is silent. No hidden cert votes are predicted.
- **Big-case score, 0.70:** the possible cross-border effects on music, movies, books, and catalog valuation make the legal issue commercially important if decided. The single-song vehicle and specialized nature of the dispute limit broader public significance. This score is separate from the 32% grant forecast.

The greatest input limitation is the missing reply text, which may directly answer the opposition's split and vehicle objections. I have not inferred what it says. The petition's truncated later appendix and unavailable amicus texts also limit verification, but the provisioned merits dispute and cert arguments are sufficient for a non-blocked forecast.
