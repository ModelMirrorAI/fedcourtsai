# Forecast of the Court's action

The Court will most likely deny certiorari in BMG Rights Management (US) LLC v. Vetter. I assign a 32% probability to any grant, including a summary disposition. The likely obstacle is the absence of a square, developed circuit conflict on the geographic reach of statutory termination, reinforced by the separate renewal-interest issue. The commercial importance makes this a serious candidate for review, but does not make plenary intervention the most likely outcome.

The snapshot records one distribution, for the October 16, 2026 conference, and no CVSG. My modal procedural path is denial without another distribution, in an orders release following that conference, approximately late October 2026. I assign 45% to at least one additional distribution. Conditional on further distribution without a CVSG, one or two additional distributions are more likely than a long sequence; disposition would then most likely occur during autumn or early winter. These are forecasts, not statements of a confirmed release schedule.

I expect no CVSG, but assign a substantial 22% probability to an invitation. If the Court seeks the Solicitor General's views, it will most likely do so during initial consideration this autumn, seeking input on the Copyright Office's position and international copyright consequences. That branch would likely defer the petition decision for several months, potentially into spring or summer 2027.

If review is granted, the Court will most likely focus the question on whether termination under 17 U.S.C. § 304(c) of an assignment of worldwide rights also recaptures foreign exploitation rights. The distinct renewal rights acquired from the deceased coauthor's heirs need not be resolved through that question. The broader question presented encompasses both forms of reversion, but the focused termination dispute is the likelier subject of plenary review.

Conditional on a grant, I assign only 4% to disposition in the cert order itself. Plenary briefing and argument are much more likely: the record supplies no intervening controlling decision requiring a GVR, and the ownership-versus-territoriality dispute warrants fuller treatment than summary correction. If the Court grants plenary review, the likelier merits ground is a territorial reading of the statutory limitation preserving rights arising under foreign law, rather than a rule allowing U.S. termination automatically to recapture all foreign copyrights. This is conditional merits context, not a predicted judgment in this cert-stage cell.

Conditional on denial, I assign 8% to any noted dissent or statement respecting denial. The modal denial is unaccompanied by a separate writing; I do not forecast a named Justice or infer individual votes from silence.
