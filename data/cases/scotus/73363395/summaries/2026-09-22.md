## What happened

Arlevys Molina was struck and killed by a car driven by Orlando Baez Castillo, who was working as an Uber driver. Yesit Campo, representing Molina's estate, sued Uber Technologies and related companies (along with Castillo) for wrongful death in Florida state court. The trial court granted summary judgment for Uber, ending the case without a jury trial, on the theory that the evidence did not support holding Uber responsible. The petition describes conflicting testimony from Castillo about his phone use and Uber's own recordkeeping about whether he was logged into the driving app at the time of the crash. Florida's Third District Court of Appeal affirmed the trial court, and the Florida Supreme Court declined to take up the case further.

## What the Court is being asked

The petition asks the Court to decide whether Campo's case should have gone to a jury instead of being cut off by summary judgment. Campo argues that the evidence about Castillo's phone use and Uber's records was disputed enough that a jury should have been allowed to weigh it and possibly draw unfavorable conclusions against Uber, and that ending the case without a jury violated the right to a jury trial and fair legal process guaranteed by the federal Constitution.

## Where it stands

Uber has waived its right to respond to the petition, meaning it does not plan to file a brief opposing it unless the Court asks. The case has been distributed for the Court's conference on September 28, 2026, where the justices will decide whether to grant cert and hear the case.
