## In brief

The Court declined to hear a family's bid to revive a wrongful-death jury trial against Uber over a fatal pedestrian accident.

## What happened

Arlevys Molina was struck and killed by a driver working for Uber through its Rasier affiliates. Yesit Campo, representing Molina's estate, sued Uber and the driver for wrongful death and demanded a jury trial. A Florida trial court granted summary judgment for Uber and its affiliates, ending the case before it reached a jury. The Third District Court of Appeal affirmed that ruling, and the Florida Supreme Court declined to take up the case further. Campo then asked the United States Supreme Court to review the matter.

## What the Court is being asked

The petition asks whether cutting the case off before trial, rather than letting a jury weigh the evidence and draw its own conclusions from it, violated the right to a jury trial in civil cases and the guarantee that legal proceedings follow fair procedures.

## Where it stands

The petition was distributed for conference twice, after Uber waived its right to respond, and the Court denied the petition on October 5, 2026.

## What each outcome would mean

Campo, representing Molina's estate, asked the Court to take up the case. Denial means the Court will not review the matter, leaving the Florida courts' rulings in place: the summary judgment for Uber and its affiliates stands, and the case ends without a jury ever hearing it. The denial does not mean the Supreme Court agreed with the Florida courts' reasoning; it simply leaves that outcome undisturbed.
