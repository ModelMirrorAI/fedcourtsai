## In brief

The Court declined to hear a wrongful death case in which Molina's estate sought to revive claims against Uber dismissed without a jury trial.

## What happened

Arlevys Molina was struck and killed by a rideshare driver, Orlando Baez Castillo, who was working with Uber Technologies and related Rasier entities. Molina's estate, represented by Yesit Campo, sued Castillo and the Uber companies for wrongful death. A Florida trial court granted summary judgment for Uber and the Rasier entities, ending the case against them before trial. Florida's Third District Court of Appeal affirmed that ruling, and the Florida Supreme Court declined to take up the case further.

## What the Court is being asked

The estate asks the Court to decide whether ending its case against Uber and the Rasier companies by summary judgment, rather than letting a jury hear the evidence and draw its own conclusions from it, violated the estate's right to a jury trial and to fair legal process.

## Where it stands

The petition was distributed for conference twice, and the Court denied it on October 24, 2025 (though the docket's final entry records the denial as issued October 5, 2026). Uber waived its right to file a response before the case was decided.

## What each outcome would mean

Campo, representing Molina's estate, asked the Court to take up the case. A grant would have meant the Court would fully hear the case and decide whether the estate's jury-trial and due-process claims should proceed, without yet deciding who is right. The denial instead leaves the Florida courts' rulings in place: the summary judgment for Uber and the Rasier companies stands, and the estate's claims against them remain dismissed.
