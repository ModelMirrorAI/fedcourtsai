## In brief

A pedestrian's estate asks the Court to revive a wrongful death jury trial against Uber after Florida courts ruled for Uber on summary judgment.

## What happened

Arlevys Molina was struck and killed by a driver, Orlando Baez Castillo, who was working with Uber and its affiliates at the time. Molina's estate, represented by Yesit Campo, sued Uber and Castillo for wrongful death. A Florida trial court granted summary judgment to Uber and its affiliated companies, ending the case against them before trial, and entered final judgment in their favor. The Third District Court of Appeal affirmed that ruling, and the Florida Supreme Court declined to take up a further appeal. The estate now brings the case to the U.S. Supreme Court.

## What the Court is being asked

The estate asks the Court to decide whether cutting off the case before trial, rather than letting a jury weigh the evidence and draw its own conclusions from it, violated the estate's right to a jury trial and to fair legal process.

## Where it stands

The petition has been distributed for the Court's conference on September 28, 2026; Uber has waived its right to respond. The docket does not yet show any decision on whether the Court will hear the case.

## What each outcome would mean

The estate, as petitioner, is asking the Court to take up the case. If the Court grants the petition, it will hear the case in full and decide the jury-trial question itself, without deciding in advance who wins. If the Court denies the petition, the lower courts' rulings stand, meaning the summary judgment for Uber and its affiliates remains in place and the case does not go to a jury.
