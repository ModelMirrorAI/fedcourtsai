# Forecast: Campo v. Uber Technologies, No. 25-1292 (evt-petition-disposition)

**Disposition.** The Court will deny the petition on the order list following
the September 28, 2026 long conference (expected October 5, 2026), without
calling for a response and without any separate writing.

**Relists.** The docket shows one distribution (for the 9/28/2026 conference).
I expect no further distribution: the petition will be disposed of at its
first conference. The only realistic paths to a second distribution entry are
an administrative reschedule off the long conference or a call for a response
to Uber's waiver, neither of which this petition invites. Roughly a one-in-eight
chance of any further distribution entry, almost all of it the reschedule shape.

**CVSG.** None. No federal party, statute, or program is implicated; the case
is a Florida vicarious-liability dispute between private parties.

**Which question, if taken.** Neither question as written is grantable. Question
1 asks whether the Seventh Amendment required a jury trial in a Florida state
court; the Seventh Amendment has never been held to bind the States, and the
petition itself quotes Florida authority saying so. Question 2 recasts the same
grievance as due process, but a state court's grant of summary judgment on an
undisputed record raises no recognized due-process question. Were the Court to
take anything, it would have to reformulate the case into a Seventh Amendment
incorporation vehicle, and this record, in which no federal question was
addressed by the court below, is not that vehicle.

**Summary disposition versus plenary review.** Conditional on the very
unlikely grant, a summary route is somewhat more likely than plenary review,
because no version of these questions supports argument. But there is no
intervening decision to GVR against, so the conditional is not lopsided.

**Dissent from denial.** None expected. The jury-right framing is not one any
Justice has signaled interest in through a case of this shape, and the
petition presents no split or recurring question that would draw a statement
respecting denial.
