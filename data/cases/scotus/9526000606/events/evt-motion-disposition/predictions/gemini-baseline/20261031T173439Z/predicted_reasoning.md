## Conditional Merits and Stay-Standard Assessment

Under the traditional stay factors (*Nken v. Holder*, 556 U.S. 418, 434 (2009); *Hollingsworth v. Perry*, 558 U.S. 183, 190 (2010)), an applicant seeking a stay pending certiorari must demonstrate a reasonable probability that four Justices will vote to grant certiorari, a fair prospect that a majority of the Court will vote to reverse the judgment below, and a likelihood of irreparable harm absent a stay, with the balance of equities and public interest weighed alongside those factors. Even if the Court or Circuit Justice were to reach the substance of the application, the underlying Third Circuit decision (*Silva v. Attorney General*, No. 25-2933, slip op. at 3–5 (3d Cir. July 8, 2026)) presents insurmountable vehicle and merits obstacles:

- **Failure to Exhaust and BIA Waiver:** The applicants argued in the Third Circuit that the agency ignored evidence and failed to consider mixed motives in its nexus analysis, and also challenged the denial of CAT relief. Because the Government properly raised exhaustion (`Aguilar v. Att'y Gen.*, 107 F.4th 164, 169 (3d Cir. 2024)) and the applicants' counseled BIA brief had neither challenged the Immigration Judge's legal standard for nexus nor raised the CAT denial (`Sanchez v. Att'y Gen.*, 147 F.4th 348, 352 (3d Cir. 2025)), those claims were unexhausted and waived below.
- **Fact-Bound Substantial-Evidence Review:** To the extent any nexus argument was preserved, the Third Circuit held that substantial evidence supported the agency's finding that the record did not show why Silva's former husband was killed and showed that threats arose because Silva's son refused gang recruitment rather than on account of family membership. Such fact-specific substantial-evidence determinations do not warrant certiorari or emergency relief.
