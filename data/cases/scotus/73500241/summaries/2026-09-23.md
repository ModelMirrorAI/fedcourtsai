## What happened

In 2012, the Kansas Department of Revenue assessed a $460,000 tax against David Greene, Jr. and Marcia Greene over a substance the state treated as a controlled drug, and took roughly $62,500 in property. The Kansas Board of Tax Appeals later found the assessment invalid, and courts agreed in later rulings that no tax was owed. Even so, the state kept the property for years, finally returning it in 2023 after the Greenes and David Greene, III sued. Kansas courts dismissed their constitutional claim, the Kansas Court of Appeals affirmed, and the Kansas Supreme Court denied further review.

## What the Court is being asked

The Greenes ask the Court to decide what constitutional protection applies when the government keeps someone's property after the legal basis for taking it has been struck down. They argue this situation involves a right to fair process before property is taken or kept, while pointing out that federal appeals courts disagree on which constitutional provision governs such retained-property cases. They want the Court to resolve that disagreement and to say that keeping property after the underlying tax assessment was invalidated violates that right on its own.

## Where it stands

Kansas waived its right to respond to the petition, and the case has been distributed for the Court's conference on September 28, 2026, where the Justices will consider whether to grant certiorari, the Court's agreement to hear the case.
