## In brief

The Supreme Court declined to hear a Kansas family's claim that the state violated their constitutional rights by keeping seized property after the tax assessment behind it was overturned.

## What happened

Kansas tax officials assessed a $460,000 tax against David and Marcia Greene in 2012 and seized property worth $62,500. In 2014 a state tax board found the assessment invalid, and courts later confirmed no tax was owed, but the state did not return the property until 2023. The Greenes sued, arguing the continued retention of their property violated their constitutional rights. A Kansas trial court dismissed the claim, and the Kansas Court of Appeals affirmed, holding that taxation cannot amount to the kind of "taking" the Constitution restricts. The Kansas Supreme Court denied further review.

## What the Court is being asked

The petition asks the Court to decide whether a government's continued retention of property, after the legal basis for taking it has been invalidated, by itself violates constitutional protections against depriving someone of property without due process. The petitioners say federal appeals courts disagree on which constitutional provision governs this kind of claim, and that the Kansas courts' approach conflicts with that body of law.

## Where it stands

The Kansas Department of Revenue waived its right to respond to the petition. The case was distributed for the Court's September 28, 2026 conference, and on October 5, 2026 the Court denied the petition.

## What each outcome would mean

The Greenes, as petitioners, asked the Court to overturn the Kansas Court of Appeals' decision. By denying the petition, the Court leaves that decision in place, meaning the Kansas courts' ruling against the Greenes stands and their claim does not proceed further. The denial does not mean the Court agreed with the Kansas courts' reasoning; it simply leaves the lower court's judgment as the final word in this case.
