## In brief

The Court declined to hear a family's claim that Kansas violated their constitutional rights by keeping seized property after the tax assessment behind the seizure was invalidated.

## What happened

In 2012, the Kansas Department of Revenue took about $62,500 in property from David Greene, Jr., Marcia Greene, and David Greene, III based on a tax assessment. In 2014, the Kansas Board of Tax Appeals invalidated that assessment, and courts later confirmed no tax was owed. The state did not return the property until 2023. The Greenes sued, but a Kansas district court dismissed their constitutional claim, and the Kansas Court of Appeals affirmed, finding that the taking and retention did not violate the Constitution. The Kansas Supreme Court denied further review. The Greenes then asked the U.S. Supreme Court to take up their case.

## What the Court is being asked

The Greenes asked the Court to decide whether a state's continued retention of property, after the legal basis for first taking it has been struck down, by itself violates the constitutional guarantee that the government provide fair process and just compensation before depriving someone of property. They also pointed to disagreement among federal appeals courts over which constitutional provision governs this kind of claim.

## Where it stands

The Kansas Department of Revenue waived its right to respond to the petition. The Court distributed the case for its September 28, 2026 conference, and on October 5, 2026, it denied the petition.

## What each outcome would mean

The Greenes, who lost in the Kansas courts, asked the Supreme Court to take up their case. By denying the petition, the Court leaves the Kansas Court of Appeals's decision in place, meaning the ruling that the state's actions did not violate the Greenes' constitutional rights stands. The denial does not mean the Supreme Court agreed with that ruling; it simply leaves the lower court's judgment as the final word in this case.
