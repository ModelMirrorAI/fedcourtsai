## In brief

The Greenes ask the Court to hear their claim that Kansas violated their constitutional rights by keeping seized property after the tax assessment behind the seizure was invalidated.

## What happened

In 2012 the Kansas Department of Revenue assessed a tax against David and Marcia Greene over a substance later found not to be a controlled one, and took roughly $62,500 in property. State tax authorities and courts eventually ruled the assessment invalid, but the department kept the property for years, returning it only in 2023 after the Greenes sued. A Kansas trial court dismissed their constitutional claim over the retained property, and the Kansas Court of Appeals affirmed, reasoning that a tax assessment cannot count as a "taking" of property. The Kansas Supreme Court denied further review.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked whether a state violates a property owner's constitutional rights by continuing to hold property it seized under a legal assessment, once that assessment has been struck down, without giving the owner a process for getting it back. The petition argues that federal appeals courts disagree about which constitutional protection governs this kind of prolonged retention, and that the Kansas courts misapplied federal law by holding that a tax assessment can never count as a taking at all.

## Where it stands

The Kansas Department of Revenue waived its right to respond to the petition. The case was distributed for the Court's conference on September 28, 2026, and the docket shows no action taken on the petition since then.

## What each outcome would mean

The Greenes, as petitioners, are asking the Court to take up their case. If the Court grants the petition, it will hear full arguments and decide the constitutional question itself, without yet deciding who wins. If the Court denies the petition, the Kansas Court of Appeals' ruling against the Greenes stands, leaving them without the constitutional remedy they sought, and Kansas's position that the assessment did not amount to an unconstitutional taking remains in effect.
