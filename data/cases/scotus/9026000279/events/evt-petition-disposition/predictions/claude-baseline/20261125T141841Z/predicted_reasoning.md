# Forecast — Alvarez v. Guerrero, No. 26-279

The petition has been distributed once, for the Conference of December 4, 2026, and has never been relisted. The Court will deny certiorari.

- **Relists.** The single most likely course is a denial on the first order list after the December 4 conference. There is a substantial minority chance (about two in five) of at least one further distribution — a relist or a rescheduling, most plausibly because a Justice is writing separately or the Court calls for the record. If it is relisted, expect one to three further distributions before the order.
- **CVSG.** None. This is a state-prisoner habeas case with no federal interest.
- **Question presented.** If the Court acts favorably, it will not take the sleeping-lawyer question as written for plenary review. It would act narrowly on the AEDPA "clearly established law" point: whether the Fifth Circuit demanded factual parity inconsistent with Andrew v. White.
- **Summary disposition.** If there is a grant of any kind, a summary disposition is the likelier route (about three in five): a GVR or per curiam vacatur directing the Fifth Circuit to reconsider under Andrew v. White, rather than briefing and argument. A plenary grant on Cronic's application to a two-lawyer defense is the less likely form.
- **Separate writing.** If the Court denies, there is roughly a one-in-three chance of a dissent from, or statement respecting, the denial. The most likely author is Justice Sotomayor, possibly joined by Justice Jackson (who is the next most likely to write). No other Justice is expected to write on a denial. If the Court instead summarily vacates, a dissent by Justice Alito or Justice Thomas is possible.
- **Conditional merits view.** If the case were heard, the likely ground would be Strickland prejudice under AEDPA and the role of the engaged second lawyer, not a categorical Cronic rule for sleeping counsel.
