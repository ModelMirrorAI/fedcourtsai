We predict the Supreme Court will deny review, with only about a one-in-eleven (9%) chance of taking the case. Juan Carlos Alvarez, a Texas death-row prisoner, argues he was denied his constitutional right to a lawyer based on juror statements signed nine years after trial saying his lead lawyer repeatedly fell asleep while a courtroom rule kept his second lawyer from stepping in. Although experienced advocates and a dissenting appeals judge support him, strict federal habeas law—which requires federal courts to defer to reasonable state-court rulings—combined with strong evidence of guilt and an awake co-counsel makes Supreme Court intervention unlikely.
