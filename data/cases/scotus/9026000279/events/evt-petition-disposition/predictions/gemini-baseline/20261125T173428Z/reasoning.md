- **Substantial panel dissent by Judge Priscilla Richman:** Unlike routine unanimous Fifth Circuit habeas affirmances, Judge Richman authored a 42-page dissent (`Pet. App. 194a`).
- **`Andrew v. White` (2025) summary-vacatur hook:** Last Term in `Andrew v. White`, 604 U.S. 86 (2025) (per curiam), a 7–2 Court summarily vacated a Tenth Circuit capital AEDPA decision for treating a general constitutional principle as non-clearly-established under 28 U.S.C. § 2254(d)(1). Petitioner forcefully argues (`Pet. 11–14`, `Reply 2–4`) that Judge Jones's majority committed the parallel threshold error by holding that `United States v. Cronic`, 466 U.S. 648 (1984), does not clearly establish a violation in the "novel factual context" where one of two defense attorneys sleeps (`Pet. App. 13a, 18a–21a`).

### Factors pulling downward well below the 16.31% `cert_amicus_line`
- **Asymmetric AEDPA posture and `Woods v. Donald` / `Wright v. Van Patten`:** The `16.31%` `baseline + some` cohort is heavily driven by commercial, statutory, and government-adjacent splits; prisoner-side § 2254 capital petitions draw NACDL/former-prosecutor amici far more frequently than they draw four votes on the current Court. Doctrinally, `Alvarez` is much harder to reverse than `Andrew v. White`. In `Andrew`, the Tenth Circuit stopped at the threshold and never assessed reasonableness on the merits. Here, Judge Jones relied directly on `Wright v. Van Patten`, 552 U.S. 120, 125 (2008), and `Woods v. Donald`, 575 U.S. 312, 317 (2015) — Supreme Court *per curiam reversals* specifically holding that `Cronic`'s automatic prejudice presumption applies only to complete denial of counsel and is not clearly established under § 2254(d)(1) in novel partial-absence scenarios — **and** independently adjudicated `Strickland` prejudice (`Pet. App. 13a–17a`), concluding fairminded jurists could find no reasonable probability of a different outcome given overwhelming evidence of four gang-related killings and extensive defense cross-examination and mitigation proof.
- **Severe evidentiary and procedural vehicle flaws:** As the Fifth Circuit majority (`Pet. App. 7a–12a, 15a–18a`) and Texas's BIO (`Opp. 1–2, 9–12, 24–28`) emphasize:
  1. The entire sleeping allegation rests on two juror affidavits signed in **2008 — nearly nine years after the September 1999 trial**, submitted for the first time in a supplemental state habeas reply seven years into state post-conviction proceedings.
  2. Nothing in the 31-volume contemporaneous trial transcript corroborates the affidavits, even though one juror claimed the trial judge repeatedly called Reyes's name to wake him while the courtroom broke out in laughter (`Opp. 10–11`; compare `Pet. App. 266a`, showing only `"THE COURT: Mr. Reyes. / MR. REYES: Just briefly, Judge."`).
  3. Portions of the affidavits describe internal jury deliberations implicating Rule 606(b), and the TCCA's unexplained refusal to adopt findings #47–#73 ("relate to arguments rather than new claims," `Pet. App. 194a`) still leaves an unexplained merits denial entitled to `Harrington v. Richter`, 562 U.S. 86 (2011), deference on any reasonable factual or legal ground — including skepticism of tardy, uncorroborated affidavits or active co-counsel presence (`Denninger` sat awake beside Reyes throughout trial and could nudge, whisper, or confer with him).
- **Illusory post-AEDPA circuit split and zero en banc interest:** Three of Petitioner's five cited sleeping-counsel cases — `Javor v. United States`, 724 F.2d 831 (9th Cir. 1984), `Tippins v. Walker`, 77 F.3d 682 (2d Cir. 1996), and `Burdine v. Johnson`, 262 F.3d 336 (5th Cir. 2001) (en banc) — were **pre-AEDPA** de novo decisions (`Opp. 14–16`). Neither post-AEDPA decision (`Muniz v. Smith`, 647 F.3d 619 (6th Cir. 2011), which *denied* habeas relief, and `United States v. Ragin`, 820 F.3d 609 (4th Cir. 2016), a federal § 2255 appeal without active trial co-counsel) addressed an awake second-chair lawyer constrained by a one-lawyer-per-witness local custom. Indeed, Petitioner concedes (`Pet. 23`) that a more particularized split is unlikely to arise because the issue stems from local practice. Tellingly, despite Judge Richman's 42-page dissent, **no active Fifth Circuit judge** (not even Richman) requested an en banc poll (`Pet.
