Linton*, No. 25-297 (March 23, 2026), is a secondary possibility. Zorn is an already-issued general precedent, not a future decision for which this case must be held. Its relevance is the specificity of the immunity analysis, not factual identity with a fatal shooting.

The Court would most likely focus on the application-specific part of Question 2: whether existing precedent clearly prohibited the officer's conduct on the facts properly taken at summary judgment. Question 1's video-evidence and totality-of-circumstances issues would matter to that inquiry. I do not expect plenary review to settle whether only Supreme Court precedent can establish law, or to reconstruct immunity through Question 3's 1791 and statutory-stare-decisis theories.

If the Court instead denies, I predict **no accompanying dissent or statement**, assigning **12% conditional on denial** to the existence of such a writing. No particular Justice's vote or writing is forecast. A denial would leave the remand intact without endorsing either side's account of the shooting or resolving the constitutional merits.
