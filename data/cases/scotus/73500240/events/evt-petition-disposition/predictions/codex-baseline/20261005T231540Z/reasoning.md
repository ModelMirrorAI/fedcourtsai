## Why raise 5.1% to 28%?

**The record request is the strongest case-specific positive.** The September 22 request, subsequent PACER notation, and September 28 receipt are concrete actions beyond ordinary distribution. Because the petition makes the video and the officer's perception central, obtaining the record is consistent with serious examination of its proposed error-correction route. That is an inference, not an empirical record-request grant rate: the pack provides no such conditioned estimate. A request does not establish four votes, and I do not count record receipt as a second independent signal of the same magnitude.

**The lower-court division supplies a developed immunity dispute.** The petition's appended rehearing order records an 8–9 vote against en banc rehearing and separate dissents, while Judge Oldham dissented from the panel decision. Those are not an intercircuit conflict or a direct predictor of Supreme Court votes, but they sharpen the proposed legal error. The docket also records one joint amici filing by local-government and police organizations. I count that as one filing, not a series of independent endorsements. Sources: petition pp. 9–12 and App. 44a–47a; snapshot June 30 entry.

**The strongest review theory is narrower than the petition's broad questions.** The panel's clearly-established-law discussion principally invokes Lytle and Baker and describes the right at a fairly general level. Petitioner argues that neither decision gave specific notice in an armed flight following a dangerous pursuit, and that the video undercuts the supposed dispute over whether he perceived a gun. These arguments could support qualified-immunity error correction without a new rule governing the source of clearly established law. Sources: petition pp. 23–25, 31–35; App. 9a–13a.

**A verified recent general precedent makes that route concrete.** Through CourtListener MCP I read the per curiam opinion in *Zorn v. Linton*, No. 25-297, decided March 23, 2026, slip op. pp. 1–6, including footnote 3. It summarily reversed a denial of qualified immunity because the cited precedent did not specifically establish the unlawfulness of the conduct, while again assuming without deciding that controlling circuit precedent can establish law. Zorn supports the availability of narrow intervention and weighs against treating the broad source-of-law question as necessary. Its wristlock facts differ substantially from this fatal shooting, and it does not settle the disputed factual premises here. The BIO itself discusses Zorn and reports amici's alternative GVR request. Sources: Zorn, CourtListener opinion 11280281; BIO pp. 15–16. I give this limited, directional weight rather than transplanting its result to Granado.

## Why denial remains more likely

**The factual dispute is substantial and potentially dispositive.** The panel's appended opinion distinguishes the first shot from the following shots, identifies disputes over the officer's perception and whether Ramirez threatened the officers while fleeing, and describes the video, the other officer's account, and wound trajectories as supporting the nonmovant. Petitioner disputes that reading, emphasizing the pre-shot exclamation about a gun and the fuller post-shot exchange. I cannot resolve those competing video accounts from text. The Supreme Court might regard this as a fact-specific summary-judgment disagreement rather than a suitable corrective case. Sources: petition App. 6a–13a; petition pp. 23–26; BIO pp. 3–10.

**The legal vehicle is not as clean as the breadth of the questions suggests.** The claimed split over excessive-force elements rests largely on differently worded formulations and factor lists, without a clearly demonstrated outcome conflict on this record. The BIO asserts that the broad circuit-precedent question and the 1791/stare-decisis theories were not pressed or decided below. I have not independently reviewed the lower-court briefing to verify forfeiture, but the petition does not demonstrate a clean ruling below on those broad theories. The interlocutory remand is an additional prudential obstacle, not a jurisdictional bar to Supreme Court review; the original district-court judgment was final before the plaintiff's appeal. Sources: petition pp. 12–18, 33–38; BIO pp. 9–10, 14–17.

**Barnes does not automatically require correction here.** The parties disagree whether the panel improperly sliced the encounter into moments or properly recognized that the threat could change. The appended opinion expressly invokes Barnes and addresses preceding events. From those supplied materials, a GVR merely to apply Barnes is weaker than the distinct Zorn/specificity route. I did not retrieve Barnes separately, so this assessment rests on the parties' presentations and the appended opinion, not a fresh review of Barnes's full opinion.

Balancing the specific attention and plausible summary route against the fact-bound vehicle yields **P(any grant) = 0.28**, with denial the single most likely disposition. The adjustment is a judgment, not a fitted model or a measured subgroup rate. For coherence, one possible allocation is 18% summary reversal, 5% GVR, 5% plenary or partial grant, 71% denial, and 1% another non-grant termination. The conditional summary-route claim of 82% rounds 23/28; it is not an 82% unconditional prediction of intervention.
