# Forecast: Granado v. Ramirez, No. 25-1338 (cert stage, moment: distribution)

**State forecast from.** The docket shows **one distribution**, for the Conference of September 28, 2026 (the long conference). No CVSG. The brief in opposition was filed August 17 after two extensions; one amicus brief (Texas Association of Counties and eight law-enforcement associations) was filed in support of petitioner. On September 22 the Clerk entered **"Record Requested"**; the Fifth Circuit and district court records arrived September 23 and 28. The snapshot payload itself was generated September 28, so nothing about the conference's result is in view.

## Relists

Expect the petition to be **relisted at least once** past the September 28 conference (about 65%). A record request a week before conference is the Court pulling the body-camera video and the summary-judgment record for itself, and the two things that usually follow such a request, a per curiam summary reversal or a written dissent from denial, both take more than one conference to produce. The likeliest path is one to three relists through October and early November, then an order. The less likely alternative (about 35%) is a clean denial on the long-conference order list with no further distribution: the Court looked at the record, saw a genuine credibility dispute, and moved on.

## CVSG

**No CVSG** (about 2%). No federal party, no federal statute or program is implicated, and the Solicitor General has no stake in a Texas officer's individual-capacity qualified-immunity defense. The Court does not call for the views of the United States on fact-bound excessive-force petitions.

## Which question the Court would take

If the Court acts, it acts only on **Question 1**, and in a narrowed form: whether the Fifth Circuit erred in denying qualified immunity on this record, with the operative sub-questions being whether the panel's "did he have a gun?" inference is blatantly contradicted by the undisputed video and shout ("he's got a gun!") under *Scott v. Harris*, and whether *Garner*, *Lytle*, and *Baker* clearly established the specific rule applied, after *Mullenix* and *Zorn v. Linton*. **Question 2** (whether any court other than the Supreme Court can clearly establish law) will not be taken: it was not pressed or passed on below, and the Court assumed the opposite without deciding as recently as *Zorn* (March 2026), footnote 3. **Question 3** (the 1791 basis of immunity, statutory stare decisis for *Pierson*) is not presented by this case and will be ignored.

## Summary disposition versus plenary review

Conditional on any grant, the route is overwhelmingly **summary** (about 75%): a per curiam reversal in the *Mullenix* / *Kisela* / *Rivas-Villegas* / *Zorn* mold holding that no precedent clearly established that an officer violates the Fourth Amendment by shooting a suspect who emerged from a fleeing car gun in hand, struggled with an officer seconds earlier, and was still holding the gun as he ran, or alternatively a GVR for reconsideration in light of *Zorn v. Linton*, which the amici requested in the alternative. Plenary review is unlikely because the petition's doctrinal questions are either unpreserved or restatements of settled law, and the Court does not set fact-bound qualified-immunity cases for argument absent a clean legal question like the one in *Barnes v. Felix*.

## If denied: dissent from denial

Conditional on denial, expect a **written dissent from or statement respecting denial about 35% of the time**, most plausibly from Justice Thomas or Justice Alito, framed around the Fifth Circuit's repeated inversion of the qualified-immunity burden, the 9-8 en banc vote, and the dissents of Judges Oldham and Ho. The record request makes a writing likelier than it is for an ordinary denied petition; the modal denial is still silent.

## If granted: conditional merits forecast

If the Court summarily reverses, the ground would be that the panel defined clearly established law at too high a level of generality (the *Garner* / *Lytle* "fleeing felon who does not pose a sufficient threat" formulation that *Mullenix* already rejected from this circuit) and that the undisputed shout "he's got a gun!" plus the body-camera footage make the inference that Officer Granado did not perceive the weapon one no reasonable jury could draw. Expect a 6-3 lineup with Justices Sotomayor, Kagan, and Jackson dissenting, as in *Zorn*. If instead the Court GVRs in light of *Zorn*, expect it to be over a noted dissent from the same three Justices or without recorded dissent.
