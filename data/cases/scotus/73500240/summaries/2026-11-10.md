## In brief

A police officer asks the Court to revive his qualified immunity defense after an appeals court let a fatal shooting lawsuit proceed to trial.

## What happened

Officer Jonathan Granado shot and killed Estevan Ramirez, a fleeing passenger from a car that had evaded police, during a nighttime chase in 2021. Ramirez's mother, Juanita Ramirez, sued on behalf of his estate, claiming the shooting violated the Fourth Amendment. A federal district court granted Granado summary judgment based on qualified immunity, a legal protection shielding officials from suits unless they violated clearly established law. The Fifth Circuit reversed, holding that disputed facts—including whether Granado knew Ramirez was armed and whether Ramirez swung a gun toward officers while fleeing—must go to a jury. The full appeals court narrowly declined to rehear the case.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked to decide whether the shooting violated the Fourth Amendment's ban on unreasonable force, whether the appeals court applied the right legal standard for excessive-force claims and for when conduct counts as clearly established as unlawful, and whether only the Supreme Court itself, rather than lower courts, can establish law clearly enough to strip an officer of immunity.

## Where it stands

The petition has been distributed for conference multiple times, most recently for November 13, 2026, after earlier conferences in September and November produced no recorded action. The docket shows no decision yet on whether the Court will hear the case.

## What each outcome would mean

Granado, the officer, is asking the Court to reverse the Fifth Circuit's decision, which had ruled in favor of Ramirez's estate by allowing the lawsuit to proceed. If the Court grants review and eventually reverses, Granado would regain qualified immunity and the case would end in his favor. If it affirms, the lawsuit would continue toward trial. If the Court denies review, the Fifth Circuit's decision stands, and the case returns to the district court for further proceedings, including possibly a trial.
