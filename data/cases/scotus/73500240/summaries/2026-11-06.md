## In brief

A police officer asks the Court to revive his qualified immunity defense after an appeals court let a fatal shooting lawsuit proceed to trial.

## What happened

Officer Jonathan Granado shot and killed Estevan Ramirez, a passenger who fled on foot after a car chase. Ramirez's mother, Juanita Ramirez, sued, claiming the shooting violated the Fourth Amendment. A federal district court granted Granado summary judgment based on qualified immunity, a defense that shields officials from suit unless they violated clearly established law. The Fifth Circuit reversed, holding that disputed facts—including whether Granado perceived Ramirez was armed and whether Ramirez swung a gun toward officers while fleeing—must go to a jury. The full appeals court narrowly declined to rehear the case.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked whether the shooting violated the Fourth Amendment, given the disputed facts, and whether the appeals court properly applied the rules for excessive-force claims and qualified immunity. The petition also asks whether only the Supreme Court, as opposed to lower courts, can establish law clearly enough to strip an officer of immunity, and whether the Court should reconsider the historical basis for qualified immunity itself.
