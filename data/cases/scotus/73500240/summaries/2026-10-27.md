## In brief

A police officer asks the Court to revive qualified immunity protection for shooting a fleeing man, after an appeals court let the case go to a jury.

## What happened

Officer Jonathan Granado shot and killed Estevan Ramirez, a passenger who fled on foot after a car chase, firing six more shots as Ramirez ran away. Ramirez's mother, Juanita Ramirez, sued, claiming excessive force. A district court granted Granado summary judgment based on qualified immunity, a legal protection that shields officials from liability unless they violated clearly established law. The Fifth Circuit reversed, finding disputed facts—including whether Granado knew Ramirez was armed and whether Ramirez swung a gun toward officers—that only a jury could resolve. The full Fifth Circuit declined to rehear the case by one vote.

## What the Court is being asked

Granado asks the Court to decide whether his shooting was lawful given the disputed facts, whether lower appeals courts can even establish the "clearly established law" that qualified immunity depends on, or only the Supreme Court can, and whether the whole doctrine of qualified immunity should be reexamined based on historical legal immunities dating to 1791.

## Where it stands

The petition has been distributed for conference twice, most recently for November 6, 2026, after full briefing including a response and amicus brief. The docket shows no action yet on whether the Court will hear the case.

## What each outcome would mean

Granado, who lost below, is asking the Court to take up the case and reverse the Fifth Circuit's decision. If the Court grants review, it would hear full arguments and decide the legal questions; a grant alone would not decide who wins. If it denies review, the Fifth Circuit's ruling stands, and Ramirez's lawsuit returns to the lower courts for a jury to weigh the disputed facts about the shooting.
