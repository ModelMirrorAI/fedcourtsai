## In brief

## What happened

The National Trust for Historic Preservation sued to stop a National Park Service construction project, described in the filings as the "August Ballroom" project. A federal district court in Washington, D.C. issued a preliminary injunction on April 16, 2026, blocking the project. The Park Service asked the Court of Appeals for the D.C. Circuit for relief, and then, still blocked, asked the Chief Justice and the full Supreme Court to pause that injunction while the dispute continues. Several outside groups, including states, members of Congress, and advocacy organizations, filed briefs supporting one side or the other.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court was asked to pause a lower court's order blocking the Park Service's project while the government pursues further appeals, including a possible request that the Court take up the case fully. The question was not who is ultimately right about the project, but whether the injunction should stay in effect while that question is worked out.

## Where it stands

The Chief Justice first stayed the injunction temporarily on August 21, 2026, while the Court considered the application. On August 31, 2026, the full Court granted the stay, with the Chief Justice, Justice Sotomayor, Justice Kagan, and Justice Jackson dissenting. The stay lasts until the government's petition for review is decided, or, if none is filed in time, ends automatically.

## What each outcome would mean

The National Park Service, as applicant, asked the Court to pause the injunction that the National Trust had won in the lower court. By granting the stay, the Court allowed the Park Service's project to proceed for now, setting aside the Trust's win below while the case continues. Had the Court denied the stay, the injunction blocking the project would have remained in effect during further proceedings. The stay is temporary: it ends automatically if certiorari is denied, or when the Court's judgment issues if certiorari is granted.
