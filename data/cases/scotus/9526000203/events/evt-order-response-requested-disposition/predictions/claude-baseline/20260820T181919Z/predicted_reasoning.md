# Forecast — 26A203, National Park Service, et al. v. National Trust for Historic Preservation (stay application)

## What the record shows at prediction time

The application (a stay, submitted to the Chief Justice on August 13, 2026)
sits at the response-requested rung: the Chief Justice called for a response on
August 14, due at noon on August 18 — a four-day clock. The frozen context
shows no referral to the full Court and zero amicus briefs. The application
seeks a stay of a preliminary injunction entered by the District Court for the
District of Columbia (No. 1:25-cv-04316) against the ballroom-construction
project at the White House, which the D.C. Circuit affirmed per curiam on
August 7, 2026 (Nos. 26-5123/26-5134, Millett and Garcia in the majority, Rao
dissenting), staying its own ruling for fourteen days — to August 21 — expressly
so the government could seek Supreme Court review.

## The disposition

**The Court will grant the stay, unqualified — P(unqualified grant) = 0.80.**
The disposing entry will read as a stay of the district court's preliminary
injunction pending disposition of a petition for certiorari (and, if granted,
the Court's judgment). A plausible variant is that the Court construes the
application as a petition for certiorari before judgment and grants both; that
still resolves this event as a grant. The mixed partial-grant route — staying
the injunction only in part — is the main alternative to a clean grant and
would resolve as ungranted under the denial-first collapse; I put roughly 5%
there, roughly 10-12% on outright denial, and a residual sliver on
withdrawal/dismissal.

## Timing

The D.C. Circuit's self-stay expires and its mandate issues on August 21, so
expect immediate action: an administrative stay from the Chief Justice or the
Court on or before August 21, with the full disposition within one to three
weeks of the August 18 response — late August or the first half of September
2026.

## The escalation ladder from here

- **Further response request:** the rung has already fired (the August 14
  request is on the record), so the claim is vacuous for this cell; a second,
  later call for supplemental briefing is unlikely (~0.03).
- **Referral to the full Court:** not yet on the record. Expect it — a
  contested, nationally prominent application on which a response was called
  for is decided by the full Court, and the Chief Justice will refer it rather
  than act alone. P = 0.85, with most of the residual being a docket that never
  records a formal referral entry rather than a genuinely unreferred ruling.
- **Amicus briefs:** the frozen count is zero, but the docket's attorney roster
  already lists six non-party counsel of the kind who file amicus briefs
  (Members of Congress through the Constitutional Accountability Center, the
  Society for the Rule of Law, Campaign Legal Center/CREW, the State of
  Indiana, and two individuals), and several of the same groups filed amicus
  briefs in the D.C. Circuit. Expect multiple amicus filings on both sides
  before disposition. P(count rises past zero) = 0.93.

## Dissents and writings

Expect noted dissents from the grant: Justice Sotomayor most likely to write,
with Justice Jackson joining and Justice Kagan likely noting dissent without
opinion. A short explanatory majority opinion or a bare order are both live;
the Court has issued brief per curiam explanations on comparable
separation-of-powers stays.

## Conditional merits-shaped forecast

If the Court treats this as (or converts it into) a merits vehicle, the likely
ground is reviewability: presidential action is not "agency action" under the
APA (the Franklin v. Massachusetts line), and NHPA section 106 obligations do
not reach the President or the Executive Office of the President components
managing the White House complex — the track Judge Rao's 35-page dissent lays
out. A narrower equities-only stay rationale (irreparable separation-of-powers
harm from judicial supervision of the President's management of the White
House, likelihood of success on reviewability) is the most probable form of
any explanation accompanying the order.
