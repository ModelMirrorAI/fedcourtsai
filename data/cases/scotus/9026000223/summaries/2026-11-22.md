## In brief

Donald Trump asks the Court to revive his defamation lawsuit against CNN over its use of the phrase "Big Lie" linked to his 2020 election statements.

## What happened

Trump sued CNN for defamation after it repeatedly described his statements about the 2020 election as a "Big Lie," at times alongside references to Hitler and Goebbels. The district court dismissed the suit, ruling that the challenged statements were opinion, not verifiably false factual claims, and that Trump had not adequately alleged the statements were false. The court of appeals affirmed, reasoning that the phrase "Big Lie" was too ambiguous to be proven true or false and that CNN's characterization amounted to a subjective assessment rather than a factual accusation. Trump's request for rehearing was denied. He now asks the Supreme Court to take up the case.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked whether a publisher can be shielded from defamation liability by wrapping false factual claims inside a harsh label and striking imagery, or whether courts must still ask whether the overall publication conveys factual accusations a reasonable reader could understand as false, regardless of how inflammatory the language is.

## Where it stands

The petition was distributed for conference, and CNN was then asked to respond after initially waiving its right to do so. CNN has twice requested more time to file that response, most recently seeking an extension to late November 2026; the docket does not show the Court has acted on the petition itself.

## What each outcome would mean

Trump, who lost in the lower courts, is asking the Court to take up the case. If the Court grants review, it will hear full arguments and decide the legal question itself, without yet deciding who wins. If it denies review, the court of appeals' ruling affirming dismissal of Trump's lawsuit stays in place, and CNN's win stands. A denial would not mean the Court agreed with the lower court's reasoning, only that the case goes no further here.
