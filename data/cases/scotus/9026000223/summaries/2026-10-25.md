## In brief

Trump asks the Court to revive his defamation lawsuit against CNN over its use of the phrase "Big Lie" in coverage of his 2020 election challenges.

## What happened

Trump sued CNN for defamation, arguing that CNN's repeated use of the phrase "Big Lie," paired with comparisons to Hitler and Goebbels, falsely accused him of knowingly lying about the 2020 election. A federal district court dismissed the suit, ruling that CNN's statements were protected opinion rather than factual claims capable of being proven false, and separately found Trump had not adequately alleged CNN acted with the required malice. The court also denied Trump's bids for reconsideration and to amend his complaint. The Eleventh Circuit affirmed, agreeing the statements were not verifiable factual assertions.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked whether news organizations can be held liable for defamation when they use a harsh label, combined with provocative imagery, that a reasonable reader could understand to accuse someone of specific false facts—or whether using such a label and imagery instead keeps the statements protected as opinion, even when the label is tied to pointed factual claims.

## Where it stands

CNN waived its right to respond, but the Court later asked for a response anyway, and the petition was distributed for the October 9, 2026 conference. The docket does not record any action taken at that conference. CNN's deadline to respond has since been extended to November 25, 2026.

## What each outcome would mean

Trump, who lost in the lower courts, is asking the Court to take up his case. If the Court grants review, it would hear full arguments and decide the legal question itself, without yet deciding who wins. If it denies review, the Eleventh Circuit's decision affirming dismissal of Trump's lawsuit stays in place, and CNN's win stands. A denial would not mean the Court agrees the lower court was right—only that the case ends there.
