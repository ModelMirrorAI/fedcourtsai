## In brief

Donald Trump asks the Court to revive his defamation lawsuit against CNN over its "Big Lie" election coverage, after lower courts dismissed the case.

## What happened

Trump sued CNN for defamation in 2022, claiming the network falsely portrayed his statements about the 2020 election as a "Big Lie," a phrase he says CNN deliberately linked to Hitler and Nazi propaganda through images and commentary. A federal district court dismissed the suit, ruling that CNN's statements were opinion rather than factual claims capable of being proven false, and separately found Trump had not adequately alleged the network acted with actual malice. The Eleventh Circuit affirmed, and later denied rehearing. Trump then asked the Supreme Court to review the case.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked whether news organizations can avoid defamation liability for false factual accusations by wrapping them in an inflammatory label and imagery, or whether courts must look past such labels to decide if a reasonable reader would understand them to convey specific, checkable factual claims. Trump's petition says the appeals court's approach conflicts with how several other appeals courts handle the same question.

## Where it stands

The petition was distributed for the Court's October 9, 2026 conference, after which the Court asked CNN to respond, something CNN had initially waived. CNN has now been given until November 25, 2026, to file that response, so the petition remains pending and undecided.

## What each outcome would mean

Trump, as the petitioner, is asking the Court to take up the case. If the Court grants review, it would hear full arguments and decide the underlying legal question, which would decide nothing about who wins until it rules. If the Court denies review, the Eleventh Circuit's decision affirming dismissal of Trump's lawsuit stays in place, leaving CNN's win intact. A denial would not mean the Court agrees the lower court was right; it simply leaves that ruling standing.
