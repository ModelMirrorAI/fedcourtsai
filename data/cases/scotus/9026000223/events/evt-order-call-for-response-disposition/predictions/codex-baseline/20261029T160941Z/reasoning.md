## Why move from 5.11% to 12%?

The September 30 call after a waiver is affirmative judicial attention and makes immediate routine denial less persuasive than it was on arrival. The QP also frames a potentially consequential First Amendment issue: whether a pejorative label and inflammatory imagery protect accompanying, allegedly verifiable factual accusations. The petition invokes a multi-circuit conflict and the existing fact/opinion framework. These support an upward adjustment. There is no published response-request-conditioned rate, so the size of that adjustment is my judgment, not an empirical estimate.

Several features keep review unlikely. The appellate decision is unreported (petition p. 1). Its appended text applies falsity and verifiability principles and relies on other circuits' treatment of political labels; it does not clearly announce that every factual assertion becomes immune when attached to a label (Appendix A, pp. 3a–6a). The petition's claimed conflict may therefore be disagreement over application to these publications rather than incompatible rules. I treat its assertions that CNN lied and that a split exists as advocacy, not established facts.

There is also a vehicle tension: the petition emphasizes accusations of knowledge and deliberate deception, while the appellate opinion describes the argument as implying association with Hitler's genocidal acts. Petitioner asserts preservation (pp. 21–22), but the mismatch leaves uncertainty, not a finding of waiver. The district court also found actual malice inadequately pleaded, a ground the appellate court did not need to reach when it affirmed on falsity (Appendix A, p. 3a). Success on the QP would not by itself settle that separate requirement. This is not a petition expressly asking the Court to overrule New York Times v. Sullivan. The petition's account of Thomas's and Gorsuch's prior writings about that precedent supports possible individual interest, not an inference that four Justices will grant this narrower case.

## Other probabilities and stakes

The 98% distribution-increment forecast largely prices ordinary redistribution after the requested response, not substantive relisting after consideration; the moment's harness masks this claim. I put a new CVSG at 1% because this private state-law damages action offers no apparent need for the federal government's institutional views. Conditional on a grant, I assign 10% to disposition in the grant order: no identified intervening decision supplies a concrete GVR reason, and any doctrinal clarification is more plausibly plenary. Conditional on denial, I assign 16% to a noted dissent or statement, with Thomas the leading possible writer and Gorsuch a secondary one, based on the writings discussed in the petition at pp. 18–20. Order-writer numbers are unconditional per-Justice authorship marginals across all disposition routes, not joining probabilities or a categorical distribution. They make no assumption of statistical independence between Justices. Most probability remains on an unsigned denial.

The 0.86 significance score reflects potential effects on political reporting and a high-profile personal plaintiff, not odds of review. My largest uncertainties are the strength of the claimed split, preservation of the petition's specific factual-accusation theory, and whether the requested response reflects more than one Justice's preliminary interest. Generic denied corpus priors do not resolve those uncertainties. A CourtListener citation lookup was throttled and web attempts produced no usable content; I relied on the supplied appendix rather than pretending to have independent verification.
