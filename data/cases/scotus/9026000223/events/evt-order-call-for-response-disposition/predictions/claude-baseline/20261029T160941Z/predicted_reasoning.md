# Predicted course — Trump v. CNN, No. 26-223 (response-requested moment)

**Disposition: cert denied.** The Court called for a response on September 30,
2026 after CNN waived, pulling the petition from the October 9 conference. Once
CNN's brief in opposition is in (due October 30, 2026, absent an extension) and
the reply period runs, the petition will be redistributed and, most likely,
denied at its first post-response conference — late 2026 or early 2027.

**Distributions.** The docket shows one distribution (for the 10/9/2026
conference), overtaken by the call for a response. Expect it to be distributed
again once the response is in (near-certain), and decided at that next
conference without a further relist in the most likely path; one additional
relist is possible if a Justice is writing on the denial.

**CVSG.** None expected. Both parties are private litigants in a common-law
defamation suit (the President sues in his personal capacity, through private
counsel); there is no federal interest the Court would ask the Solicitor General
to address, and a CVSG here would be anomalous.

**If granted.** The Court would take the single question presented roughly as
written — whether a pejorative label ("Big Lie") with accompanying imagery can
convey provably false factual assertions actionable under *Milkovich* — and
would likely confine it to the fact/opinion line rather than reach *New York
Times v. Sullivan*'s actual-malice rule, which the petition invokes only as
context. Plenary review is the likelier route on a grant; a summary reversal of
an unpublished per curiam affirming a Rule 12(b)(6) dismissal is conceivable but
unlikely, and no intervening decision supports a GVR.

**Separate writing on denial.** A statement respecting or dissent from the denial
is plausible but not the most likely outcome. Justice Thomas has repeatedly
written on denials in public-figure defamation cases (McKee, Berisha, Coral
Ridge) and is the likeliest writer; Justice Gorsuch (Berisha) is a secondary
candidate. The question here is the *Milkovich* opinion doctrine rather than
actual malice, which makes a *Sullivan*-focused writing a less natural fit than
in those cases.
