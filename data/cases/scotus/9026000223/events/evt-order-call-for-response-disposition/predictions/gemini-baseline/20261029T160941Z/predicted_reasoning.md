# Predicted Court Reasoning and Procedural Trajectory: *Donald J. Trump v. Cable News Network, Inc.*, No. 26-223

## 1. Further Distributions and Relist Trajectory

The docket currently records one conference distribution (distributed on September 23, 2026 for the October 9, 2026 conference), followed by the Court's September 30, 2026 call for a response (due October 30, 2026) after respondent Cable News Network, Inc. (CNN) waived its right to respond. Because a response has been requested, the petition will be distributed at least once more to the conference after CNN files its Brief in Opposition and Petitioner has an opportunity to reply (`relist-increment` = 0.98). Once redistributed with the Brief in Opposition in hand, expect the Court to dispose of the petition at that conference (or after at most one additional relist if a Justice circulates a separate statement or dissent from denial) rather than engaging in multi-conference pre-grant relisting.

## 2. Call for the Views of the Solicitor General (CVSG)

The Court will not call for the views of the Solicitor General (`cvsg-increment` = 0.01). Although Petitioner is the sitting President litigating in his personal capacity, this is a private state-law diversity defamation action arising out of political commentary by a cable news network. No federal statute, regulatory scheme, or institutional executive-branch interest is implicated.

## 3. Question Presented and Conditional Merits Scope

If the Court were to grant certiorari, it would take the single Question Presented as framed in the petition—whether the First Amendment's opinion doctrine under *Milkovich v. Lorain Journal Co.*, 497 U.S. 1 (1990), shields a publisher from defamation liability when it pairs a pejorative label ("the Big Lie") with historical Nazi imagery to characterize a political figure's election challenges. Conditionally, if granted, the Court would address whether the Eleventh Circuit erred in treating both the comparison to Nazi propaganda techniques and the implied assertion of subjective dishonesty as non-verifiable opinion, while leaving the unreviewed actual-malice pleading question for remand.

## 4. Summary Disposition vs. Plenary Review

Conditional on a grant, plenary review with full merits briefing and oral argument is far likelier than a summary disposition in the cert order itself (`summary-disposition-route` = 0.15). No intervening Supreme Court precedent has altered the *Milkovich* framework since the Eleventh Circuit's November 2025 decision to warrant a GVR, and the Court would not summarily reverse an unpublished circuit affirmance in a high-profile First Amendment defamation dispute without merits briefing.

## 5. Separate Writings Respecting Denial

Upon denial of certiorari, there is a modest probability (`dissent-from-denial` = 0.16) that one or two Justices—most likely Justice Thomas (`order-writers`: 0.12), potentially joined by or alongside Justice Gorsuch (0.04) or Justice Alito (0.03)—will author a brief statement respecting the denial of certiorari or a dissent from denial reiterating longstanding criticisms of modern First Amendment defamation doctrine at the motion-to-dismiss stage, while the Court as a whole denies review without comment.
