## III. Case-Specific Evaluation of the Petition and BIO

### 1. Procedural Posture and Companion Petition No. 25-1246
Vasquez was convicted and sentenced to death in Nueces County, Texas in 1999 for the murder of his girlfriend's four-year-old daughter, Miranda Lopez. His initial state habeas application was denied in 2004, and his initial federal habeas petition was denied in 2008 (*Vasquez v. Quarterman*, No. 2:06-cv-111, 2008 WL 859149 (S.D. Tex. Mar. 28, 2008), *COA denied*, 304 F. App'x 334 (5th Cir. 2008), *cert. denied*, 556 U.S. 1224 (2009)).

After the Texas Court of Criminal Appeals dismissed Vasquez's fourth state habeas application in December 2022 (*Ex parte Vasquez*, No. WR-59,201-04, 2022 WL 17660560 (Tex. Crim. App. Dec. 14, 2022)), Vasquez moved the Fifth Circuit under 28 U.S.C. § 2244(b)(3)(A) for authorization to file a successive federal habeas petition raising (1) an ineffective-assistance-of-trial-counsel (IATC) claim regarding the victim's toxicology results, and (2) *Brady* and *Napue*/*Giglio* claims alleging that the State suppressed a toxicologist interview note and presented false testimony about cocaine levels (*In re Vasquez*, No. 23-40079). On June 20, 2023, the Fifth Circuit granted authorization *only* as to the IATC claim and denied authorization as to the *Brady* and *Napue*/*Giglio* claims (App. 93a–96a).

In the authorized successive proceeding (No. 2:23-cv-00043), the district court dismissed the IATC claim under § 2244(b)(2)(B) and refused to consider the unauthorized *Brady* and *Napue* claims (App. 40a–92a). On December 4, 2025, the Fifth Circuit affirmed (*Vasquez v. Guerrero*, No. 25-70005, 2025 WL 3488844 (5th Cir. Dec. 4, 2025), App. 15a–39a). Vasquez's petition for certiorari from that decision is currently pending in **No. 25-1246** (`scotus/73292081`, retrieved via `fedcourts query --court scotus --docket-number 25-1246`), which was redistributed on November 4, 2026, for the December 4, 2026 conference to be considered alongside this petition.

Meanwhile, on March 4, 2026, Vasquez filed a third-in-time federal habeas petition in the Southern District of Texas (No. 2:26-cv-00068) raising the same *Brady* and *Napue*/*Giglio* claims, explicitly telling the district court that he was filing "solely as a protective measure" so that he could argue on appeal and certiorari that those claims are not "second or successive" under *Panetti v. Quarterman* (App. 7a; BIO 11). The district court dismissed for lack of jurisdiction under § 2244(b)(3)(A) on March 18, 2026 (App. 2a–14a), and the Fifth Circuit denied a COA in a one-sentence order on June 17, 2026 (App. 1a).

### 2. Absence of a Circuit Split Supporting Petitioner
Petitioner concedes that the Fourth, Fifth, Sixth, Ninth, and Eleventh Circuits uniformly hold that second-in-time *Brady* and *Napue*/*Giglio* claims are subject to § 2244(b)'s gatekeeping rules (Pet. 25–27; BIO 13–14). Although Petitioner cites the Tenth Circuit's decision in *Douglas v. Workman*, 560 F.3d 1156 (10th Cir. 2009), *Douglas* explicitly recognized the general rule that *Brady* claims are subject to § 2244(b) and carved out only an extraordinary "suppression of the suppression" exception where the prosecutor affirmatively defrauded the prisoner and habeas counsel *during* the initial habeas proceeding (BIO 17–19). And while Justice Sotomayor authored statements in *Storey v. Lumpkin*, 142 S. Ct. 2576 (2022), and *Bernard v. United States*, 141 S. Ct. 504 (2020), the Court denied certiorari in both of those cases—which did not suffer from the acute procedural obstacles present here.

### 3. Five Independent Vehicle Defects Highlighted in the Record and BIO
The BIO (at 19–34) and the lower-court opinions in the appendix demonstrate why this case is an exceptionally poor vehicle:

1. **Circumvention of 28 U.S.C. § 2244(b)(3)(E) and Law of the Case:** Vasquez already submitted these exact *Brady* and *Napue*/*Giglio* claims to the Fifth Circuit in 2023 under § 2244(b)(3)(A), and the Fifth Circuit denied authorization (App. 95a–96a). Under 28 U.S.C. § 2244(b)(3)(E) (which, unlike § 2255(h) in *Bowe v. United States*, 607 U.S. ___ (2026), unquestionably governs state prisoners under § 2244), that denial is unreviewable on certiorari. Re-filing the rejected claims in the district court three years later to manufacture a vehicle implicates law-of-the-case, forfeiture, and jurisdictional bars before the Court could ever reach the *Panetti* question (BIO 20–21).
2. **Unreasoned One-Sentence COA Denial Below:** The Fifth Circuit's order under review (App. 1a) is a single sentence denying a COA without specifying whether it rested on the procedural ruling or the underlying merits prong of *Slack v. McDaniel*, 529 U.S. 473, 484 (2000) (BIO 22–23).
3. **Statute of Limitations and State Procedural Default:** Vasquez waited until March 2026 to file this petition—more than three years after the Texas Court of Criminal Appeals dismissed his fourth state habeas application in December 2022, and nearly four years after he reviewed the prosecutor's file in 2022—rendering the petition facially time-barred under 28 U.S.C. § 2244(d)(1) (since his 2023–2025 federal habeas litigation did not toll the limitations period under *Duncan v. Walker*, 533 U.S. 167 (2001)), as well as procedurally defaulted under Texas's independent and adequate abuse-of-the-writ bar (BIO 23–25).
4. **Adverse Fifth Circuit Finding on Notice / Suppression:** Vasquez's entire *Panetti* theory presupposes that the factual predicate of his claim was suppressed and unknowable during his first federal habeas proceeding. Yet in *Vasquez v. Guerrero*, 2025 WL 3488844, at *6 (App. 26a–27a), the Fifth Circuit held that the one-page 1998 Toxicology Report (App. 105a)—which on its face showed both "Negative" for the initial "Drugs of Abuse Screen" and "0.18 mg/L" cocaine / "0.42 mg/L" benzoylecgonine on the "Confirmed GC/MS" test—was disclosed to defense counsel before trial and admitted into evidence at trial, and that Dr. Backer testified at trial about the two-step screening and confirmation testing (BIO 27–30).
5. **Adverse State and Federal Rulings on Materiality:** Both the state habeas court (adopted by the Texas Court of Criminal Appeals, *Ex parte Vasquez*, 2021 WL 3746008) and the Fifth Circuit (App. 29a–31a) found that even without the cocaine evidence, the evidence of Vasquez's guilt and mens rea in beating four-year-old Miranda Lopez to death was overwhelming—including his admission to striking her, 20 to 30 areas of bruising across her head and body, fatal blunt-force brain trauma comparable to a high-speed car crash, and severe perineal injuries (BIO 30–34). While the Nueces County District Attorney signed statements in 2023 and 2025 supporting relief (App. 107a–110a), the Texas Court of Criminal Appeals rejected the state trial court's later recommendations and the Texas Attorney General vigorously opposes certiorari.

## IV. Rationale for Auxiliary Claims and Big-Case Score

- **`relist-increment` (`0.12`):** Because Companion Petition No. 25-1246 was redistributed to join No. 26-353 at the December 4, 2026 conference, both petitions are already synchronized for simultaneous disposition. There is a small (~12%) chance of at least one further distribution if a Justice's chambers holds the pair briefly to consider a statement respecting denial or to review the capital record, but an immediate denial after the December 4 conference is far more likely.
- **`cvsg-increment` (`0.002`):** Virtually zero on a state capital habeas petition with active opposition from the State's Attorney General.
- **`summary-disposition-route` (`0.20`):** Conditional on a grant, plenary review (80%) is much more likely than a summary order (20%), as there is no intervening Supreme Court precedent that would support a GVR and the circuit precedent below is uniform.
- **`dissent-from-denial` (`0.04`) and `order-writers`:** Justice Sotomayor (`0.030`) and Justice Jackson (`0.012`) are the most plausible authors of a statement respecting denial or dissent from denial given Justice Sotomayor's prior writings on this issue in *Storey* (2022) and *Bernard* (2020), though the severe vehicle defects make a separate writing unlikely.
- **`big_case_score` (`0.25`):** If decided on the merits, the question whether *Panetti* exempts newly discovered *Brady*/*Napue* claims from § 2244(b) would be significant for federal habeas practice nationwide, but the convoluted procedural posture makes the case unlikely to produce a clean doctrinal ruling even if taken.
