The upward considerations are substantial stakes, an identifiable recurring statutory question, and judicial criticism of the existing rule. The petition asks whether concealed-evidence Brady and false-testimony Napue/Giglio claims unavailable during an initial habeas application are nevertheless successive under section 2244(b). Its argument invokes Panetti and earlier Sotomayor writings in Storey and Bernard, and identifies appellate judges criticizing their circuits' own precedents. That gives the question more substance than a purely factual error-correction request. The capital posture also makes closer review or a statement respecting denial plausible.

The downward considerations dominate. **There is no demonstrated conflict between circuit holdings.** Petition pages 25–27 acknowledge adverse precedent in several circuits; disagreement within panels and separate writings is not an opposing circuit holding. Texas emphasizes precisely that distinction at opposition pages 15–17. I do not equate criticism of precedent with a mature circuit split.

**The vehicle is materially encumbered.** Appendix A, page 1a, confirms that the Fifth Circuit simply denied a certificate of appealability without explanation. Appendix B records dismissal of the new district-court petition for lack of the required authorization. Vasquez argues at petition pages 27–28 that this dismissal route cleanly presents the statutory issue and avoids section 2244(b)(3)(E)'s authorization-review bar. Texas responds at opposition pages 18–23 that authorization for these claims had already been denied in 2023, and invokes the statutory bar, law of the case, forfeiture, and abuse of the writ. I treat these as disputed objections, not adjudicated alternative holdings in the unexplained order. Even without resolving the jurisdictional dispute against Vasquez, their cumulative vehicle risk lowers the chance that the Court chooses this case to settle the broad question.

Finally, the petition's characterization of a State confession of error is not an undisputed concession. Opposition pages 30–33 contest the meaning of the toxicologist's notes, the relationship to testimony disclosed at trial, and suppression. The Court need not resolve those factual disputes to deny review; they make this less attractive than a clean statutory test case. The petition's policy argument could be right without securing four votes for this particular vehicle.

I therefore move from 5.15% to 2.5%, rather than to zero. That reflects genuine legal importance tempered by the absence of a split and independent procedural obstacles. My main uncertainty is whether the Justices see the dismissal route as an adequate way around the earlier authorization denial, or connect this petition procedurally to the other petition identified in the filings.
