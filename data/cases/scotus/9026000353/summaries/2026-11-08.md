## In brief

A death-row inmate asks the Court to decide whether his new claims about suppressed evidence count as a barred "successive" habeas filing.

## What happened

Richard Vasquez was convicted of capital murder and sentenced to death in Texas. Years later, he says he discovered that prosecutors had known before trial that a toxicology report used against him was internally inconsistent, and that testimony about cocaine in the victim's system was unreliable. He tried repeatedly, in state and federal courts, to raise claims based on this evidence. The Fifth Circuit denied him permission to pursue these particular claims, and after he filed a further federal petition without that permission, both the district court and the Fifth Circuit rejected it, with the Fifth Circuit denying him a certificate of appealability needed to proceed.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked to decide whether claims like Vasquez's, based on facts the inmate says he did not know about when he filed his first federal habeas petition, must meet the strict legal requirements that apply to repeat ("successive") habeas filings, or whether they should instead be treated as timely because they only became apparent later.

## Where it stands

The petition was distributed for the Court's December 4, 2026 conference, after the state's brief opposing review was filed in October. The docket shows no action since that distribution.

## What each outcome would mean

Vasquez, the inmate, is asking the Court to take up his case. If the Court grants review, it would hear full arguments and decide the legal question itself, without yet determining who wins. If it denies review, the Fifth Circuit's decision rejecting Vasquez's claims stands, and the state, represented by the prison system's director, keeps the benefit of that result. A denial would not mean the Court agrees the lower court was right, only that the case is not taken up.
