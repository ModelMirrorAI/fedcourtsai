## In brief

A death-row inmate asks the Court to decide whether his new claims about suppressed evidence count as a barred second habeas petition.

## What happened

Richard Vasquez was convicted of capital murder and sentenced to death in Texas. Years later, his lawyers say they discovered that prosecutors had known before trial that a toxicology report used to show cocaine in the victim's system was internally inconsistent and unreliable. Vasquez tried to raise claims based on this evidence, calling them suppression-of-evidence and false-testimony claims. The Fifth Circuit had previously ruled such claims, raised after an initial federal habeas petition, count as "second or successive" and need advance court permission. Vasquez filed a new federal petition without that permission, the district court dismissed it, and the Fifth Circuit denied him permission to appeal.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked whether a death-row inmate who did not know, when he filed his first federal habeas petition, that the state had withheld evidence or used false testimony can later raise claims about that withheld evidence and testimony without first getting a court of appeals' permission, or whether such claims must always be treated as a follow-on petition subject to strict limits on when follow-on petitions are allowed.

## Where it stands

The petition was distributed for a December 2026 conference, then rescheduled; the respondent filed an opposition brief and Vasquez filed a reply. The docket shows no action yet on whether the Court will hear the case.

## What each outcome would mean

Vasquez, the inmate, is asking the Court to take up his case. If the Court grants review, it would hear full arguments on whether his claims were wrongly blocked as successive; a grant would not itself decide who wins. If the Court denies review, the Fifth Circuit's decision stands, leaving in place the denial of permission for Vasquez to pursue these claims, and his conviction and sentence remain intact as far as this case is concerned.
