# Predicted Reasoning

## Procedural Forecast
The docket currently shows the petition distributed for its first conference with a full record (the upcoming September 28, 2026 conference), following the respondent's Brief in Opposition on July 22. While it sits at a second distribution overall due to the timing of the response request, it is at its first distribution for decision. 

A further relist is possible but relatively unlikely for a straightforward Munsingwear vacatur request. The Court usually grants the GVR or denies it without need for extensive relists. I forecast a low probability of further relists (0.20).

The Court will not call for the views of the Solicitor General (CVSG). The government is already the respondent in this habeas case and has fully briefed its position in opposition to the petition.

If the Court grants review, it will almost certainly do so via a summary disposition (a GVR - Grant, Vacate, Remand) to instruct the lower court to dismiss the case as moot pursuant to United States v. Munsingwear, Inc. The Court will not set the case down for plenary briefing and oral argument.

There is a small chance (0.05) that a Justice may note a dissent from the denial of certiorari, particularly if they believe the Fourth Circuit's application of Jones v. Hendrix to a Suspension Clause claim raises serious constitutional concerns that should not be evaded despite mootness, but a clean denial with no noted dissents is the most likely path if vacatur is refused.

## Substantive Scope
If the Court takes the case (via GVR), it will adopt the sole question presented regarding Munsingwear vacatur due to the petitioner having completed his sentence of supervised release, rendering the underlying challenge moot. It will not reach the merits of the underlying Suspension Clause or 28 U.S.C. § 2241 arguments.
