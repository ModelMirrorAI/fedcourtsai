## Why 38%, rather than the 17% anchor

The principal upward adjustment is the remedy sought. This is not an ordinary bid for plenary review: uncontested mootness creates a plausible summary-vacatur route despite the absence of a demonstrated square conflict on the precise saving-clause issue. Bell argues that his supervision ended on December 24, 2025, after judgment, rehearing, and the mandate, while he diligently pursued review. His effort to stay the mandate and the continuing effect of the published precedent strengthen that equitable presentation. These facts and arguments come from the petition, pages 3–11, and the provisioned question presented.

The requested response after waiver adds evidence of attention, but it is not four votes for relief. Nor do I count the summer briefing interval as repeated adverse-conference survival: the August distribution followed the July opposition. This limits the signal adjustment.

The opposition, pages 7–10, provides substantial reasons to retain denial as the modal outcome. It disputes independent certworthiness, emphasizes the jurisdictional dismissal, and argues that Bell helped cause mootness by requesting shortened supervision. The panel's disagreement over reasoning does not itself establish a conflict in outcomes between circuits. His earlier collateral-review history also makes this a less clean constitutional vehicle than the petition's introductory framing suggests. I treat the government's claimed categorical restrictions on vacatur as litigating positions, not settled prerequisites.

The reply, pages 2–10, materially improves Bell's answer: it distinguishes habeas from a direct criminal case, invokes vacatur of jurisdictional judgments, and stresses that early termination was sought before the adverse appellate decision for reasons independent of abandoning review. The government supported shorter supervision. These responses prevent me from treating voluntary conduct or the lack of a merits split as automatic bars. They do not eliminate the Court's equitable discretion. Azar v. Garza, 584 U.S. 726 (2018), verified through CourtListener, confirms that vacatur is circumstance-dependent rather than automatic. The 38% estimate is a judgmental synthesis, not a fitted rate for Munsingwear petitions; the pack supplies no matched subgroup for this posture.
