## In brief

Jason Bell asks the Court to erase a lower court's habeas ruling against him because his sentence ended before he could seek review, making the case moot.

## What happened

Bell was sentenced in 2003 as a "career offender," a designation he says relied on a rule later found unconstitutionally vague. After years of unsuccessful challenges, he filed a habeas petition arguing he had no other way to raise this constitutional claim. The district court dismissed it, and the Fourth Circuit affirmed, holding that recent Supreme Court precedent barred his claim and that this did not violate his constitutional right to seek habeas relief. Weeks after the appeals court's mandate issued, Bell finished his supervised release, completing his sentence entirely and, he says, mooting the case before he could ask the Supreme Court to review it.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked whether it should wipe out the appeals court's decision and send the case back with instructions to dismiss it as moot, because the case became unreviewable through no fault of Bell's, rather than leaving that decision in place as binding precedent.

## Where it stands

The petition has been distributed for conference more than once, with responses requested and filed and a reply from Bell submitted; the docket does not yet show a ruling on the petition.

## What each outcome would mean

Bell is asking the Court to act. If the Court grants the petition and orders vacatur, the Fourth Circuit's decision would be erased and the case sent back with instructions to dismiss it as moot, leaving no binding precedent from that ruling. If the Court denies the petition, the Fourth Circuit's decision affirming dismissal of Bell's habeas claim stands, including its rulings on the merits and on the constitutional question, and it would remain in effect as circuit precedent.
