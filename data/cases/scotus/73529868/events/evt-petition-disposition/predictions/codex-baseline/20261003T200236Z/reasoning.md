## Adjustment to 80% any grant

The primary legal source is Lopez-Campos v. Raycraft, consolidated Sixth
Circuit Nos. 25-1965/1969/1978/1982, May 11, 2026, CourtListener opinion
11324612. The majority and dissent identify a genuine disagreement over
whether section 1225(b)(2)(A) requires detention of noncitizens arrested in
the interior after an unlawful entry, rather than permitting bond under
section 1226(a). At pages 6 and 25-26, the opinions identify the Second and
Eleventh Circuits on one side and the Fifth and Eighth on the other. This is
not a split inferred merely from the parties' rhetoric. The published,
divided decision and national uniformity problem raise the estimate above
the federal prior. The snapshot additionally records the Solicitor General
as counsel and four amicus briefs, including Iowa and 22 other states.
Federal representation is already partly priced into the anchor; I do not
count it as a separate large multiplier.

The main discount concerns this vehicle, not the importance of the issue.
The majority also sustains due-process holdings, discussing individualized
bond hearings at pages 20-24; I do not assume the statutory question alone
necessarily disposes of every consolidated case. The opinion reports the
petitioners' release, which warrants caution about posture without proving
mootness. Most importantly, respondents' September 25 letter urges statutory
review in Buenrostro-Mendez or Rhoney if the Court takes the constitutional
question in Genalo v. D.C. That is advocacy for alternative vehicles, not an
order or proof of the government's full position. It makes a hold followed
by a GVR more plausible here than selecting this petition for plenary review.
The letter's report of dismissal in Genalo v. G.M. concerns another case and
predates this snapshot; it is not the outcome being predicted.
