# Forecast: what the Court will do with No. 25-1415, Putra v. Lopez-Campos

**State forecast from.** The docket shows **one distribution** (for the Conference of September 28, 2026) and no relist, no CVSG. The petitioner is the Solicitor General, so no CVSG can ever issue. Four amicus briefs support the petition (Iowa and 22 other States; America First Legal; America's Future; FAIR). Respondents' BIO, the reply, and two post-distribution letters from respondents' counsel (September 14 and 25) are on the docket. As of October 3 the docket carries no order on the petition.

**The decisive public fact.** On October 1, 2026 the Court granted the government's companion petition from the Second Circuit, *Rhoney v. Barbosa da Cunha*, No. 26-104, which presents only the statutory question (whether § 1225(b)(2)(A) mandates detention of noncitizens present without admission). The Solicitor General had asked the Court to do the opposite: grant *Putra* (which presents both the statutory question and a freestanding due-process question) and hold *Rhoney*. The Court did not act on *Putra* in the same order. A third petition, *Buenrostro-Mendez v. Blanche*, No. 26-43 (private petitioners from the Fifth Circuit, statutory question only), and the government's petition for certiorari before judgment on the constitutional question, *Genalo v. D.C.*, No. 26-379, are also pending.

**Forecast of the route.**

1. **Hold, not relist (most likely, ~83%).** This petition will be held pending *Rhoney* and will show no further docket activity for months. It will not be a live candidate at the October 9 conference. After *Rhoney* is decided (argument early 2027; decision expected by late June 2027) it will be **redistributed for a conference** and disposed of in the lead case's wake:
   - if the government prevails on the statute in *Rhoney*, the Sixth Circuit judgment will be **granted, vacated and remanded** for further consideration in light of *Rhoney* (the GVR is the modal route; a plenary grant limited to the due-process question is the minority alternative, more likely if the Court has also taken *Genalo v. D.C.*);
   - if the government loses in *Rhoney*, this petition will be **denied** without separate writing.
2. **Early plenary grant (~12%).** The Court relists this petition and grants it this fall, most plausibly paired with *Genalo v. D.C.* so that the due-process question is argued alongside *Rhoney*'s statutory question. If that happens the Court takes **Question 2 (due process)** and treats Question 1 as already before it in *Rhoney*.
3. **Early denial (~5%).** Denied on an autumn order list because the Court regards the released-respondent posture or the due-process overlay as a poor vehicle and the statutory question as fully covered by *Rhoney*.

**Relists.** Expect at least one further distribution (the redistribution that precedes disposition of a held case, or a relist preceding a fall grant). Only the early-denial branch leaves the count at one.

**CVSG.** None: the Solicitor General is the petitioner.

**Summary disposition.** Conditional on any grant, the cert-order route (GVR) is the more likely form, ~58%, because most of the grant mass sits in the government-wins-*Rhoney* branch where the Court sends the Sixth Circuit's alternative due-process holding back rather than reaching it.

**Dissent from denial.** Unlikely, ~6%. The dominant denial branch is a routine companion denial after the government has lost the merits in *Rhoney*; only the small early-denial branch invites a statement, and even there the Justices most sympathetic to the government would have *Rhoney* in hand.

**If decided on the merits (conditional only).** If the due-process question is argued, the government's strongest ground is that § 1225(b)(2)(A) detention pending removal proceedings is categorically reasonable under *Demore v. Kim* and *Jennings*; the respondents' is *Zadvydas*/*Mezei* protection for noncitizens who have "passed through our gates" and the Sixth Circuit's finding that the holding is independent of the statute.
