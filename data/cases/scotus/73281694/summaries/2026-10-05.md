## In brief

Norfolk Southern asked the Court to decide whether Pennsylvania's jurisdiction law violates the Constitution's commerce protections; the Court denied review.

## What happened

Robert Mallory sued Norfolk Southern in Pennsylvania state court, claiming workplace chemical exposure caused his cancer, even though he worked in Virginia and Ohio and the railroad is not headquartered in Pennsylvania. Pennsylvania courts had jurisdiction because Norfolk Southern registered to do business there. After this Court previously upheld that jurisdiction rule against a due-process challenge, the case returned to the trial court, where Norfolk Southern tried to raise a new constitutional argument about interstate commerce. The trial court ruled that Norfolk Southern had waited too long and waived that argument. Pennsylvania's appeals courts declined further review.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court was asked whether Pennsylvania's law, which lets its courts hear lawsuits against any company registered to do business in the state even when the dispute has no connection to Pennsylvania, improperly burdens trade between states.

## Where it stands

The petition was distributed for conference twice, after briefing that included a waiver of response, a later response requested and filed, and amicus briefs. On October 5, 2026, the Court denied the petition.

## What each outcome would mean

By denying the petition, the Court left in place the lower courts' rulings, meaning the trial court's finding that Norfolk Southern waited too long to raise its interstate-commerce argument stands, and that argument was never decided on its merits. Mallory's estate, which had opposed review, keeps the benefit of that outcome, and the underlying lawsuit can continue in the Pennsylvania trial court.
