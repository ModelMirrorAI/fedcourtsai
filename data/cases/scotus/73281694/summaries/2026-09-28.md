## In brief

Norfolk Southern asks the Court to decide whether Pennsylvania can require it to face this lawsuit based only on its business registration there.

## What happened

Robert Mallory sued Norfolk Southern in Pennsylvania state court, claiming workplace chemical exposure caused his cancer. Pennsylvania courts asserted jurisdiction because Norfolk Southern had registered to do business there, though the case has no other tie to the state. After this Court earlier upheld that registration-based jurisdiction against a due-process challenge, Norfolk Southern raised a separate argument that the arrangement improperly burdens interstate commerce. The trial court found Norfolk Southern had waited too long to raise this argument and treated it as forfeited; the Pennsylvania Superior Court and Supreme Court both declined further review without ruling on the substance. Norfolk Southern then petitioned this Court.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked whether a state can make a company answer lawsuits unconnected to that state simply because the company registered to do business there, or whether doing so improperly burdens trade among the states. Mallory's estate counters that no court below actually decided this question, since the trial court ruled only that Norfolk Southern had forfeited the argument by raising it too late.
