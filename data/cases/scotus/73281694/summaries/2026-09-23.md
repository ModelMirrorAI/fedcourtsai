## What happened

Robert Mallory sued Norfolk Southern Railway in Pennsylvania state court, claiming that his work for the railroad in Ohio and Virginia caused his cancer. The case had no connection to Pennsylvania except that Norfolk Southern had registered to do business there. In an earlier trip to the Supreme Court, the Court ruled that Pennsylvania's law making that registration a basis for suing companies did not violate due process, but left open whether it violates a different constitutional limit on state interference with interstate commerce. Back in the trial court, Norfolk Southern tried to raise that remaining argument, but the court found it had waited too long and treated the argument as waived. Pennsylvania's appeals courts declined to review that decision. Mallory has since died, and his estate has been substituted as the party pursuing the case. Norfolk Southern now asks the Supreme Court to take up the case again.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked whether a state can require an out-of-state company, simply because it registered to do business there, to face lawsuits in that state's courts over matters with no connection to the state at all, and whether doing so improperly burdens commerce between states. The estate responds that no court has actually ruled on this question yet, because the trial court only found that Norfolk Southern had missed its chance to raise the argument under state procedural rules, and that there is no final ruling for the Supreme Court to review.

## Where it stands
