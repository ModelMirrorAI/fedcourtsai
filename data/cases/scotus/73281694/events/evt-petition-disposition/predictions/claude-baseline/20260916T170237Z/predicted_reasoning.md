# Forecast: what the Court will do with No. 25-1208 (Norfolk Southern v. Mallory, second trip)

**Disposition.** The petition will most likely be **denied** (P(grant) = 0.25), on vehicle
grounds rather than for want of interest in the question. The Court will not reach the dormant
Commerce Clause question in this posture: the trial court denied preliminary objections in an
unexplained order after an earlier express waiver finding, no Pennsylvania appellate court
wrote a word, and finality under 28 U.S.C. § 1257 is genuinely contested. If the Court does
grant, it will be a plenary grant with argument in OT2026, not a summary disposition.

**Conference and relists.** The docket shows two distribution entries. The first (Apr 28 2026,
for the May 14 conference) never produced a conference vote: the Court called for a response
the next day after respondents had waived. The second (Jul 15 2026) places the petition on the
**September 28, 2026 long conference**, its first real consideration. From that state I expect
**one further distribution with probability 0.40**: this is exactly the profile the Court
relists once, either because a Justice is deciding whether to write on the denial or because
the vehicle question needs a second look. Conditional on a relist, I expect at most one or two,
not a long string, and I expect no hold: nothing pending on the merits docket controls it.
Orders on the long-conference list issue October 5, 2026 (a grant could come October 2).

**CVSG.** No CVSG has been called for and I expect **none (P = 0.07)**. The case is between
private parties, the federal interest (an interstate railroad, a FELA suit that cannot be
removed) is real but indirect, and the Court did not call for the Solicitor General's views
on the first Mallory petition either. If a CVSG did issue it would come from the September 28
conference and push disposition into 2027.

**Which question, if taken.** The Court would take the question as the petition frames it
(Justice Alito's formulation from 600 U.S. at 160), because the due process half was decided
in this very case in 2023 and the record carries no notice or in-state-presence complications.
It would not broaden it to registration schemes generally. The threshold questions the grant
would carry with it, and which the BIO makes unavoidable, are whether the trial court's order
rests on an adequate and independent state ground (waiver under Pa. R. Civ. P. 1032) and
whether the judgment is final under § 1257 via Mercantile National Bank v. Langdeau. A grant
order might well direct the parties to brief the jurisdictional questions alongside the QP.

**Summary disposition.** Very unlikely even given a grant (**P = 0.03 conditional on grant**).
There is no intervening decision to GVR in light of, and the merits question is one the Court
has never resolved, so a per curiam is not on the table.

**Writing on denial.** If the petition is denied, I expect a separate writing with **P = 0.30**:
most plausibly a statement respecting the denial from Justice Alito, whose 2023 concurrence
invited this exact challenge, noting that the question remains open and that a better vehicle
(a case with a reasoned merits decision below, such as the pending Tenth Circuit appeal in
American Food & Vending v. Goodyear) should present it. Justice Barrett, or one of the other
2023 dissenters, joining such a statement is possible but less likely than a solo writing.
The Court denied the sibling petition in Lynn v. BNSF (No. 25-1046) on May 4, 2026 with no
writing, which restrains this number.

**If granted, the likely ground** (conditional only): a majority would hold that Pennsylvania's
scheme, as applied to an out-of-state plaintiff suing an out-of-state defendant on out-of-state
conduct, imposes a burden on interstate commerce with no legitimate local interest to weigh
against it, on the Davis v. Farmers' Co-operative line and the Alito concurrence, rather than
on a discrimination theory. The five Justices who signalled receptiveness in 2023 (Alito plus
the four Barrett dissenters) are the natural majority for that holding.
