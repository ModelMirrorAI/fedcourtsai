## In brief

Broadcast Music asks the Court to review how closely appeals courts may scrutinize a lower court's music-licensing rate decision.

## What happened

Broadcast Music (BMI) licenses musical works to concert promoters, including members of the North American Concert Promoters Association (NACPA), under an antitrust consent decree that lets a federal district court set a "reasonable" fee when BMI and a licensee cannot agree. After BMI and NACPA failed to negotiate, the district court set a royalty rate and revenue base following a trial. The Second Circuit vacated that decision, holding the district court misapplied legal standards governing how to select and weigh comparison agreements and define the revenue base, and sent the case back for further proceedings. BMI's request for rehearing was denied.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked whether an appeals court must defer to a trial court's rate-setting determination and the judgments underlying it, or whether the appeals court may instead review those determinations fresh, without deferring to the trial court's findings.

## Where it stands

The petition has been distributed for conference more than once; after the first distribution the Court asked NACPA to respond, and the case was later distributed again following full briefing, including a brief in opposition and a reply. No action on the petition has yet been recorded.

## What each outcome would mean

BMI, which lost before the appeals court, is asking the Court to take up the case and reverse that ruling. If the Court grants review, it would hear the case and decide what standard of review applies, deciding nothing yet about who should ultimately win the rate dispute. If the Court denies review, the Second Circuit's decision stands, and the case returns to the district court to reset the rate under the appeals court's instructions, as NACPA urges.
