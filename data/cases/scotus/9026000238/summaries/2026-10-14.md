## In brief

A music licensing organization asks the Court to decide whether appeals courts must defer to trial judges' factual findings when setting music royalty rates.

## What happened

Broadcast Music, Inc. (BMI) licenses musical works to music users, including concert promoters, and operates under a court-supervised agreement allowing a judge to set rates when BMI and a licensee cannot agree. BMI and the North American Concert Promoters Association (NACPA) could not agree on new rates, so a district court held a trial and set a royalty rate and defined what revenue counts toward it. NACPA appealed, and the Second Circuit vacated that decision, holding that the rate-setting choices should be reviewed without deference to the trial court, not accepted unless clearly wrong. BMI's request for further review from the full appeals court was denied.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked whether appeals courts reviewing a trial judge's determination of a fair market rate for a music license, including the judge's choice of comparison agreements, must defer to the trial judge's findings unless clearly mistaken, or may instead review those determinations without such deference.

## Where it stands

The petition was distributed for the Court's conference on September 28, 2026. The Court then asked NACPA to respond, with that response due November 2, 2026; the docket shows no further action since.

## What each outcome would mean

BMI, which lost the rate it won at trial once NACPA appealed, is asking the Court to take up the case. If the Court grants review, it will hear full arguments and decide the proper standard of review, without yet deciding who wins. If it denies review, the Second Circuit's ruling stands, meaning the case returns to the district court for a new rate determination under the closer, less deferential review standard. Either way, the case would continue in the lower courts unless the Court later rules otherwise.
