## In brief

Broadcast Music asks the Court to decide whether appeals courts must defer to, rather than independently review, a trial court's music-licensing rate decision.

## What happened

Broadcast Music, Inc. (BMI) licenses musical works to concert promoters on behalf of songwriters and publishers, operating under an antitrust consent decree that lets a federal district court set rates when BMI and a licensee cannot agree. BMI and the North American Concert Promoters Association (NACPA) failed to agree on new rates, so a district court set a fee after trial. The Second Circuit vacated that decision, holding the district court misapplied legal standards governing how to pick and weigh comparable licensing agreements and define revenue, and sent the case back. BMI's request for rehearing was denied.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked whether an appeals court should independently decide, without deferring to the trial judge, how a lower court picked and weighed comparison licenses and defined revenue in setting a fair licensing rate, or whether it should accept the trial judge's findings unless they are clearly wrong.

## Where it stands

The petition has been fully briefed, with amicus briefs filed on both sides, and was distributed for the Court's conference of September 28, 2026; the docket shows no action recorded since then.

## What each outcome would mean

BMI, which lost at the Second Circuit, is asking the Court to reverse that decision. If the Court denies review, the Second Circuit's ruling stands, and the case returns to the district court to reset the rate using the appeals court's more independent review standard. If the Court grants review and later rules for BMI, appeals courts would have to defer more to trial judges' rate decisions, which could restore a result closer to the district court's original, higher rate. If it rules for NACPA, the Second Circuit's closer scrutiny of such rulings would be confirmed.
