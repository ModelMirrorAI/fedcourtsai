## In brief

Broadcast Music, Inc. asks the Court to decide what standard appeals courts should use when reviewing a judge's calculation of a reasonable music licensing fee.

## What happened

Broadcast Music, Inc. (BMI) licenses musical works to music users, including concert promoters, and operates under a court-supervised agreement that lets a judge set a "reasonable" fee when BMI and a licensee cannot agree. BMI and the North American Concert Promoters Association (NACPA) could not agree on new licensing rates, so a federal district court held a trial and set a rate and revenue base for NACPA to pay. NACPA appealed, and the United States Court of Appeals for the Second Circuit reviewed the judge's rate-setting decisions without deferring to them, found the rates unreasonable, and sent the case back to the lower court for further proceedings. BMI's request for rehearing was denied.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked whether an appeals court must defer heavily to a trial judge's calculation of a fair market rate and the underlying choices that go into it, such as which comparable licensing agreements to use, or whether the appeals court may instead review those choices fresh, with no deference to the trial judge's findings.

## Where it stands

The petition was distributed for conference, and the Court asked NACPA to respond after an amicus brief was filed; that response was due November 2, 2026, after an extension. The docket does not yet show further action.

## What each outcome would mean

BMI, which lost the favorable rate it won in the trial court, is asking the Supreme Court to overturn the Second Circuit's decision. If the Court denies review, the Second Circuit's ruling stands, and the case continues in the lower courts under the closer, less deferential review standard it applied, which favored NACPA. If the Court grants review, it would hear full arguments on what standard applies and decide the question, which would not by itself determine whether BMI's rates were reasonable.
