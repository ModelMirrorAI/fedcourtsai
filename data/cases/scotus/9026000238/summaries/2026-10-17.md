## In brief

A music licensing group asks the Court to decide what standard appeals courts should use when reviewing a judge's calculation of a reasonable music licensing fee.

## What happened

Broadcast Music, Inc. (BMI) licenses musical works to users, including concert promoters, and operates under a court-supervised consent decree that lets a judge set a fee when BMI and a licensee cannot agree. BMI and the North American Concert Promoters Association (NACPA) failed to agree on new rates, so a district court set a fee and revenue base after a trial. NACPA appealed, and the Second Circuit vacated that decision, holding that the district court's choice of comparison agreements and definition of revenue should be reviewed without deference to the trial judge, not merely checked for clear mistakes. The court sent the case back for further proceedings.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked to decide how closely appeals courts should scrutinize a trial judge's determination of a reasonable licensing fee and the underlying choices that go into it, such as which comparison agreements to rely on. BMI argues this should receive deferential review, as most other appeals courts do, rather than the fresh, independent review the Second Circuit applied. BMI also asks that, if the Court does not take up this question now, it hold the case until it decides a separate pending case involving a similar standard-of-review issue.

## Where it stands

BMI filed its petition on August 20, 2026. NACPA was not initially required to respond but was later asked to do so, with its response now due; several outside groups have filed briefs supporting BMI's petition. The petition has not yet been scheduled for a conference as this record shows.

## What each outcome would mean

BMI, which lost before the Second Circuit, is asking the Court to take up the case. If the Court grants review, it would hear full arguments and decide the proper standard for reviewing fee determinations, deciding nothing yet about who should win. If the Court denies review, the Second Circuit's ruling stands, and the case returns to the district court for a new fee determination under the stricter review standard NACPA favors. If the Court instead holds the petition pending its decision in another case addressing a similar standard-of-review question, no action is taken until that other case is resolved.
