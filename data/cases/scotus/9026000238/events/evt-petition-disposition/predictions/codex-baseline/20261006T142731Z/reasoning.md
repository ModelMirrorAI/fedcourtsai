I raise the 5.016% anchor to **18%** for three main reasons. First, the Court affirmatively requested a response after waiver, a concrete attention signal not captured by the frozen baseline band. Second, the question presents an intelligible and potentially recurring fact/law boundary, with a developed trial record and asserted disagreement across circuits. Third, ASCAP's recorded support and the petition's explanation of the concentrated consent-decree forum support importance beyond these parties. These are qualitative adjustments, not a fitted likelihood-ratio calculation.

Substantial discounts remain. The claimed nine-circuit split is not nine courts deciding the same royalty-consent-decree question: the petition relies on administrative copyright rates, takings valuations, patent licensing, tax, and other contexts (petition pp. 15–22). Those different legal frameworks may make the disagreement analogical rather than square. BMI acknowledges that the Second Circuit described the ultimate rate review as reasonableness review while separating out legal components (pp. 17–18). The genuine dispute may therefore be how a settled mixed-question framework applies to a particular record, not a clean conflict over the governing rule. The remand for further proceedings also leaves room for further development below; it is a practical vehicle discount, not a claimed jurisdictional bar. The missing opposition prevents testing these concerns against NACPA's actual arguments.

The petition's reliance on U.S. Bank National Association v. Village at Lakeridge, 583 U.S. 387 (2018), supplies a plausible doctrinal theory, but I do not equate potential merits error with certworthiness. Its requested RiseandShine hold (pp. 33–34) supplies a possible GVR mechanism. I have not verified that related case's current status and do not treat a future helpful ruling as established. That uncertainty supports a meaningful but minority 35% summary route conditional on grant.
