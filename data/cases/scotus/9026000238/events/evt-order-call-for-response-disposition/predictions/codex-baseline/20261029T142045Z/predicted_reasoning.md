# Forecast of the Court's action

The Court will most likely deny the petition after receiving the requested response and considering the completed certiorari briefing. The response request signals attention, not a commitment to review. The most likely order will contain no explanation and no separate writing.

The provisioned docket has **one distribution**, for the September 28, 2026 conference, followed by the September 10 response request with an October 13 deadline. It has **no CVSG** and **one docketed amicus brief**, from ASCAP. The petition will almost certainly be distributed at least once more after response briefing: P(distribution-count increase) = **0.98**. This is not a prediction that the Court will reconsider the case after an actual conference; the first distribution preceded the response request. On the most likely prompt-denial path, expect one additional distribution, with disposition in late 2026 or early 2027. A hold for the related standard-of-review case would extend the timeline into 2027 and need not produce repeated visible distributions.

The Court probably will not invite the Solicitor General's views: P(new CVSG) = **0.08**. The federal consent decree gives the government a relevant institutional interest, but the petition presents an appellate-review question rather than a request to restructure the decree. If an invitation issues, it is more likely after the opposition and reply are considered, in late 2026 or early 2027.

If the Court takes this case, the operative question will concern whether selecting and weighing comparable licensing agreements is a predominantly factual inquiry entitled to clear-error review, or a legal determination reviewed afresh on appeal. A narrower formulation focused on benchmarking under the BMI consent decree is more likely than a categorical rule governing every kind of fair-market valuation.

Conditional on any grant, a disposition in the certiorari order is more likely than separate plenary review: P(summary route | grant) = **0.65**. The leading such route is a hold followed by a grant, vacatur, and remand for reconsideration in light of **RiseandShine Corp. v. PepsiCo, Inc., No. 24-1016**. That case's already-granted standard-of-review question supplies a potential vehicle for guidance, although its trademark-strength issue is not identical to royalty benchmarking. An immediate summary reversal is less likely than that hold-and-remand route. If plenary review is granted instead, the likely ground would distinguish factual valuation judgments from independently reviewable legal constraints on the valuation methodology.

Conditional on denial, the probability of a noted dissent or statement respecting denial is **0.04**. No individual Justice is likely to write. The small independent probabilities in `order-writers` allow for a methodological objection, particularly from Justice Gorsuch, without predicting that a writing will occur. These are authorship forecasts, not inferred certiorari votes.
