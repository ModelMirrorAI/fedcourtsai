## Quantitative anchor

I checked the arithmetic against the corresponding `prefix_est_grant_rate` and `prefix_weighted_resolved` fields in `metrics/statpack.json`, avoiding rounding the rendered percentages. This is the private class's whole reachable baseline population, not a rate conditional on a response request. The 2026 row is excluded.

For context only, the modern-cert table has **1,282 grants/GVRs among 44,777 resolved petitions**, approximately **2.86%**. The Second Circuit cut is approximately **5.1% grant family among 2,070 resolved**, but mixes fee classes and signals and is not my scored-segment anchor. The paid-segment cuts describe terminal populations: zero relists has about **1.8%** grant family (**10,203 resolved**), one relist **13.7%** (**2,538**), two **41.2%** (**495**), and three or more **37.4%** (**484**). The CVSG cut is approximately **34.8%** grant family (**164 resolved**) versus **6.4%** without a CVSG (**13,556**). One terminal amicus entry corresponds to roughly **15.1%** grant family (**1,162 resolved**). These marginal cuts overlap, pool Terms, and condition on where petitions ended; they cannot be multiplied into a hazard from this petition's present state. In particular, redistribution after a response request is not equivalent to a substantive post-conference relist.

These figures describe the **committed pack available in this checkout**, not a newly refreshed corpus estimate. The pack files do not expose a build timestamp at their top level. `fedcourts corpus-info` could not supply corpus-wide freshness because the cell's service backend has no client-side connection. Thus the newest corpus-wide pull and stored-snapshot stamps are **unavailable here**; I make no claim that these counts reflect an independently verified October 29 corpus vintage. The companion's October 28 poll stamp is case-specific, not a substitute for corpus-wide freshness.

## Why P(any grant) is 0.24

The **24%** number is a substantial, judgmental upward adjustment from 5.11%, not a published response-requested rate; the pack has no such conditional table.

- **Response requested after waiver.** At least some institutional attention overcame the waiver. That is favorable selection relative to the broad baseline population, although the threshold for requesting a response is much lower than the threshold for granting review.
- **A serious but imperfect conflict.** The petition identifies comparator-selection cases in the Fourth and Ninth Circuits and a related Copyright Royalty Board line in the D.C. Circuit, alongside valuation cases elsewhere. Those examples lend substance to its Rule 52 and mixed-question argument. However, condemnation, patent licensing, agency review, and this consent-decree regime are not interchangeable; a nine-circuit split in exactly the same legal setting is not established merely by the petition's characterization.
- **The lower opinion tempers the claimed departure.** The appendix expressly preserves clear-error review for underlying facts and frames independent review as review of legal constraints on comparability, weighting, and the revenue base. It invokes precedents from 1990, 2003, and 2010 rather than announcing that all valuation findings are legal. That gives the respondent a plausible distinction between legal methodology and factual application. I derived that distinction from the opinion, not an imagined opposition.
- **Meaningful industry stakes and a plausible vehicle, but unresolved proceedings below.** The consent decrees concentrate these royalty disputes in the Second Circuit, so lack of an identical geographic split is not dispositive. The requested response and ASCAP filing reinforce that the issue matters beyond one contract. Against that, the judgment vacates and remands; further rate-setting proceedings may make immediate Supreme Court intervention less attractive. Several criticisms of the selected rate and revenue base also make the causal effect of a revised review standard less automatic.
- **A concrete alternative to plenary review.** The petition expressly seeks a hold and remand following RiseandShine, and the companion query confirms that the Court has already granted that different case. That raises the chance of an eventual grant-family result without requiring a second fully argued case. Trademark strength and consent-decree valuations remain sufficiently different that guidance might not warrant a remand here.

One approximate allocation consistent with my numbers is **15.6% summary-route grant**, **8.4% plenary grant**, **75% denial**, and **1% other termination**. The first two sum to 24%, and 15.6/24 = 65% is the stated conditional summary-route forecast. This is an elicited allocation, not an empirical model fit. Uncertainty is greatest over whether the Court regards RiseandShine as sufficiently related and whether the respondent can show that the supposed conflict is only methodological error correction.

The **0.08 CVSG** forecast reflects the government's consent-decree interest but discounts the need for an invitation on a general standard-of-review question. The **0.57 significance score** measures stakes if decided, not grant odds. A ruling could affect performance royalties and the allocation of authority between trial and appellate courts, while remaining technical and institutionally narrower than a major constitutional case. I do not adopt the petition's unverified numerical claims about industry participants as independent findings.
