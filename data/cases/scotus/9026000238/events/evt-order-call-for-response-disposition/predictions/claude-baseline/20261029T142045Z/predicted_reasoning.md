# Predicted course of the petition — Broadcast Music, Inc. v. North American Concert Promoters Ass'n, No. 26-238

**State forecast from.** One distribution so far: the petition went to the 9/28/2026 conference on the respondent's waiver, and the Court called for a response on 9/10/2026. It has not been relisted and there is no CVSG.

**Disposition.** The most likely outcome is a **denial** (about 78%). There is a real minority path to a grant in some form (about 22%). Roughly half of that is plenary review and half is a hold for *RiseandShine Corp. v. PepsiCo*, No. 24-1016, followed by a GVR.

**Further distributions.** Near certain. Once the brief in opposition is filed (the deadline was extended to November 2, 2026), the petition goes to a conference again, likely in late November or December 2026. I expect the Court to **hold** it for *RiseandShine* rather than deny it outright at that conference. *RiseandShine* was granted on 2026-06-29 on a closely parallel fact/law standard-of-review question arising from the same circuit. If so, the petition is not finally disposed of until after *RiseandShine* is decided, most likely in spring or June 2027.

**CVSG.** Unlikely. The BMI rate court operates under a Department of Justice antitrust consent decree, so the United States has an interest it could be invited to state. But the question presented is a generic standard-of-review question, and the Court can resolve it without the government's views.

**Which question, if granted.** QP 1 as written: whether a district court's fair-market-value determination and its parts, such as the selection of benchmark agreements, are reviewed de novo or for clear error. If argued, the Court would likely apply its *U.S. Bank v. Village at Lakeridge* framework for mixed questions.

**Summary route.** If relief comes, a GVR in light of *RiseandShine* is about as likely as plenary review. It would follow if *RiseandShine* holds trademark strength to be a factual question reviewed deferentially, or otherwise reasons broadly enough to cast doubt on the Second Circuit's de novo treatment here.

**Separate writing.** On a denial, I expect no dissent or statement respecting the denial. This is a commercial, technical petition of a kind that rarely draws one.
