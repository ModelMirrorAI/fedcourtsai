# Forecast: what the Court will do with No. 25-1287, N. R. v. Ellison

**Disposition.** The petition will be **denied**. The Court will not take up the equal-protection challenge to ICWA and MIFPA on this record, because the judgment under review does not decide that question: the Minnesota Supreme Court affirmed the denial of permissive intervention under the state best-interests standard and vacated the intermediate court's equal-protection discussion. The only federal question the state court actually resolved, whether weighing the foster parents' constitutional challenge in the intervention analysis violated the First Amendment or due process, is novel, fact-bound, uncontested by any conflicting authority, and was decided with alternative grounds the state court called "not the decisive factor."

**Relists.** The docket shows a single conference distribution: the petition was distributed on June 24, 2026 for the September 28, 2026 Long Conference, and after the requested response and reply arrived it was redistributed on September 9 for the same conference. No relist has occurred. I expect the petition to be **considered at the Long Conference and most likely denied on the following order list without a relist**; a single relist is the realistic alternative (roughly three-in-ten), driven either by a Justice weighing a separate writing or by the requested-response signal drawing a closer look. Two or more relists are unlikely absent a CVSG.

**CVSG.** The docket shows no call for the views of the Solicitor General. I expect **none**. The United States is not a party, but ICWA is a federal statute whose constitutionality is challenged, so a CVSG would be the natural route if the Court wanted to explore the question; the vehicle problems make an outright denial the likelier path. If the Court does call for the SG's views, it would do so off the Long Conference or the first relist, deferring disposition into 2027.

**Which question, if taken.** If the Court grants, it will more plausibly take **Question 2** (the First Amendment / due-process challenge to the intervention denial) or a reformulated version asking whether the state-law intervention ruling is an adequate and independent ground, rather than Question 1 as written. A grant limited to Question 1 (ICWA/MIFPA equal protection) is unlikely because the state high court never reached it and respondents' standing and redressability objections (the twins are enrolled members; relative preference exists under generic Minnesota law; placement with their grandmother is supported by every party) would sit unresolved in front of the merits.

**Summary disposition.** Petitioners ask in the alternative for a summary reversal on the First Amendment point. I do not expect it: the state court was unanimous, gave Mt. Healthy-style alternative grounds, and no intervening decision of this Court supplies a GVR hook. Conditional on any grant, the cert-order route (GVR or per curiam) is a small minority outcome (about one in eight); plenary review is the likelier form of a grant.

**Separate writing on denial.** A statement or dissent respecting denial is a live possibility but not the expectation (about one in five). The petition is built to draw Justice Thomas (who has written on ICWA's constitutional problems in Adoptive Couple and Brackeen) and Justice Alito (whose statements in Parents Protecting Our Children and Lee v. Poudre criticize lower courts for avoiding contentious constitutional questions through threshold doctrines). Against that, the poor vehicle and the children's settled placement give any Justice who wants the ICWA question decided a reason to wait for a cleaner case rather than write here.

**If granted (conditional).** The likely merits path would be a decision on whether the intervention ruling rests on adequate and independent state grounds and whether considering a litigant's good-faith constitutional argument as evidence of unfitness offends the First Amendment; the most probable relief would be a vacatur and remand for the intervention analysis to be redone, not a ruling on ICWA's constitutionality.
