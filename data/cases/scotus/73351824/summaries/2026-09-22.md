## What happened

Nathan and Kellie Reyelts were foster parents to twins born with serious medical needs who are eligible for membership in the Red Lake Nation. After more than a year of care, county officials moved the twins to a relative's home, citing the Indian Child Welfare Act (ICWA) and its Minnesota counterpart (MIFPA), which give placement preferences favoring Native families and tribes. The Reyeltses sought to intervene in the custody case, arguing these laws are unconstitutional. A Minnesota trial court denied intervention, saying their constitutional challenge showed poor judgment about the children's heritage. The Minnesota Court of Appeals had earlier allowed their standing but rejected the challenge on the merits; the Minnesota Supreme Court then affirmed the denial of intervention and declined to reach the constitutional question.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked whether these child-welfare laws, which apply different and less protective rules to Native children than to other children, unlawfully treat people differently based on race or ancestry. The Court is also asked whether it violates free-speech and petition rights for a court to deny someone the ability to participate in a custody case specifically because they made a good-faith argument that the governing law is unconstitutional.

## Where it stands
