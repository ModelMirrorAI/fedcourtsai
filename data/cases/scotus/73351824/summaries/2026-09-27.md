## In brief

Former foster parents ask the Court to hear their challenge to federal and state Indian child welfare laws and to their exclusion from a custody case after raising that challenge.

## What happened

Nathan and Kellie Reyelts, non-Indian foster parents, cared for twins eligible for membership in the Red Lake Nation after the twins were born with serious medical needs. When the county later moved to place the twins with a relative under the Indian Child Welfare Act and its Minnesota counterpart, the Reyeltses sought to intervene in the custody proceeding, arguing those laws are unconstitutional. Minnesota courts denied intervention, partly because they had raised that constitutional argument, and the Minnesota Supreme Court affirmed, declining to reach the constitutional question and setting aside the lower appellate court's ruling on it.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked whether these child welfare laws, which apply different, less protective rules to children eligible for tribal membership than to other children, unlawfully deny equal treatment to those children and to the adults seeking custody of them. It is also asked whether barring the Reyeltses from the case because they made that constitutional argument violated their free speech and petitioning rights.

## Where it stands

The petition has been distributed for conference twice, most recently for the conference of September 28, 2026, after the state filed a brief opposing review and the Reyeltses filed a reply. The docket shows no decision yet on whether the Court will hear the case.

## What each outcome would mean

The Reyeltses are asking the Court to take up their case. If the Court grants review, it would hear full arguments on the constitutional questions and decide them; a grant alone would not determine who wins. If the Court denies review, the Minnesota Supreme Court's decision stands, leaving in place both the exclusion of the Reyeltses from the custody proceeding and the unresolved constitutional challenge to the laws.
