# Forecast

The Court will most likely deny this petition, but the call is close: **46% probability of any grant**, including a grant, vacate, and remand. The principal competing route is a hold followed by a GVR associated with the related Nwauzor litigation, rather than an independent plenary grant in this case.

The supplied docket records **two distributions**, on September 30 and October 7, 2026, and **no CVSG in this case**. The October 6 rescheduling moved the scheduled conference from October 16 to November 6. Both conference dates are still in the future as of October 11: this is not evidence that the Justices considered the petition and then relisted it.

From that two-distribution baseline, I forecast **at least one additional distribution with probability 0.65**. One to three further distribution entries are the central forecast; a hold can also leave a long interval without repeated entries. The related petition, the disputed federal-contractor issue, and the opportunity to await a fuller ruling below will produce more deliberation than an ordinary first-conference denial. A November 2026 denial remains quite plausible, but the central timing expectation is action after November, with a companion-driven hold potentially extending into the first half of 2027. Timing is contextual, not a scored claim.

I forecast **no separate request for the Solicitor General's views** here: probability of a new CVSG is **0.10**. The supplied reply reports that the Solicitor General has already recommended review in Nwauzor and identified this dispute as related. The Court can use that related advocacy without formally requesting another brief. That is not a claim that this docket already has a CVSG.

**Conditional on any grant, the probability of disposition in the cert order is 0.70.** A GVR after clarification in the related case is substantially more likely within that category than an immediate summary reversal. If the Court instead orders plenary review, it will focus on whether state regulation directed at a contractor impermissibly controls federal detention operations or discriminates against them through the choice of comparators. It is more likely to narrow the petition's broadly framed question to those intergovernmental-immunity issues than to announce categorical immunity for every federal contractor or blanket preemption of state health regulation.

If plenary review occurs, the likely ground for disturbing the decision below is the distinction between permissible incidental burdens and impermissible control or discrimination against federal operations. That merits discussion is conditional, not a prediction that a majority opinion will accompany the expected denial.

**Conditional on denial, probability of a noted dissent or statement respecting denial is 0.16.** The modal denial will have no separate writing. I do not predict individual cert votes or an opinion author.
