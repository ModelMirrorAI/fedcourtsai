I raise the ultimate any-grant estimate to **0.46** for the following case-specific reasons:

- **Related federal support is the strongest positive signal.** The September 30 reply, printed pages 1-4 and 10-12, reports that the Solicitor General's September 15 brief urged review in Nwauzor, described a circuit conflict and exceptional importance, and twice identified this case as related. This is materially stronger than petitioner's assertion of a split alone, but it is still the reply's account of a brief I did not retrieve. It is not a formal recommendation to grant this particular petition or a CVSG on this docket. It creates a meaningful hold-and-GVR path in addition to independent plenary review.
- **There is a substantial doctrinal issue rather than only individualized error correction.** The QP and petition, printed pages 19-33, frame direct regulation and discriminatory treatment of federal operations performed through private contractors. The reply reports eight judges dissenting from denial of rehearing en banc. The retrieved United States v. Washington, 596 U.S. 832 (2022), opinion section III.A, confirms that discrimination against federal contractors can violate intergovernmental immunity, while mere increased federal costs do not establish the violation. That supports the seriousness of the dispute without deciding whether the regulations or comparators here cross the line.
- **The asserted conflict is contested.** The opposition, printed pages 1-3 and 18-25, argues that other circuits apply the same distinction between incidental economic burdens and actual interference with federal operations, and that contractor status is not categorical immunity. The reply, printed pages 2-7, argues instead that the Ninth Circuit materially dilutes protection when contractors perform federal functions. I treat this as a disputed doctrinal conflict, not an uncontested square split. The parties also disagree over the significance of Menocal and Hencely; I read their arguments without pretending to have independently retrieved those decisions.
- **Vehicle defects substantially restrain the estimate.** The opposition, printed pages 33-36, identifies an interlocutory ruling, a remanded discrimination inquiry, a subsequent inspection injunction and pending appeal, and a new ICE contract with different standards. The reply, printed pages 12-13, responds that the legal errors are already fixed and the new contract does not determine the constitutional questions. That response preserves a plausible review route, but a fuller forthcoming appeal and a related case with a different posture give the Court reasons not to grant this petition independently. These considerations keep denial narrowly ahead despite the substantial companion-case signal.

This is a judgmental update, not a fitted likelihood-ratio model. The largest uncertainty is whether the Court will use Nwauzor to dispose of this case, take both, or view the statutory and procedural differences as reasons to deny this petition. Counting GVRs as grants is essential: the 46% forecast is not a 46% chance of separate merits briefing and argument.
