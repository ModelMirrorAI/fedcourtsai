# Forecast — GEO Group v. Ferguson, No. 26-71

**Where things stand.** The docket shows two distribution entries. The first
(Sept 30, for the Oct 16 conference) was **rescheduled** on Oct 6 before the
Court ever considered it, and the petition was redistributed (Oct 7) for the
**Nov 6, 2026** conference. In effect this petition has never been discussed at
conference and has not been relisted after consideration. There is no CVSG on
this docket.

**The likeliest path is that it gets coordinated with Nwauzor (No. 25-828).**
In the companion petition, which involves the same facility and the same
Ninth Circuit intergovernmental-immunity doctrine, the Court called for the
SG's views. The United States answered on Sept 15, 2026, urging a grant and
calling this case a logical companion. My reading of the pre-conference
reschedule is that the Court is lining this petition up behind Nwauzor rather
than taking it up on its own.

- **Most likely: deny now or hold.** I expect the Court to grant Nwauzor
  (taking the contractor-intergovernmental-immunity question there) and either
  **hold** this petition until Nwauzor is decided or deny it outright. A
  plenary grant here is unlikely. The decision below is interlocutory: it
  vacated a preliminary injunction and remanded on the comparator question.
  A new preliminary-injunction appeal is already pending in the Ninth Circuit,
  and GEO's new ICE contract changes the factual landscape.
- **If held:** after a Nwauzor merits decision (likely spring or June 2027)
  that rejects the Ninth Circuit's approach, the likely disposition is a
  **GVR** in light of Nwauzor. If Nwauzor affirms, or is resolved narrowly on
  a wage-specific ground, expect a denial.
- **Further distribution:** more likely than not (about 55%) the petition
  appears on another conference list after Nov 6. That covers a further
  reschedule to sit with Nwauzor, or a post-hold redistribution. A clean
  grant or denial on Nov 6 would end it with no further distribution.
- **CVSG:** very unlikely (about 5%). The SG has already spoken to these
  doctrines in Nwauzor and referred to this case there.
- **Route if granted:** more likely summary (a GVR after Nwauzor) than
  plenary, at about 55/45. An outright plenary grant alongside Nwauzor, for
  consolidated argument, is the main alternative.
- **Question taken, if granted outright:** the QP as written, reframed around
  whether a state may regulate a federal contractor's performance of a
  federal function without facing intergovernmental-immunity
  (direct-regulation) scrutiny. The Ninth Circuit's discrimination-comparator
  holding would be left for remand.
- **Dissent from denial:** unlikely but possible (about 15%). Justice Thomas,
  Alito, or Gorsuch might note one, most plausibly if Nwauzor is granted while
  this petition is denied outright.
