## Reasons to depart upward

The provisioned question presented concerns state regulation of the details of a federal contractor's immigration-detention services while the State exempts its own detention facilities. The petition's reasons for review and September 30 reply argue that the Ninth Circuit's contractor distinction conflicts with other circuits, especially the Third Circuit's CoreCivic approach, and permits indirect interference with federal operations. The petition's appendix, App-38 onward, records Judge Bumatay's rehearing dissent joined by seven other judges. The published decision below matches the provisioned judgment date, appellate number 24-2815, parties, and challenged legislation; it is not merely a different GEO lawsuit. These features make the asserted federal issue substantial rather than routine error correction.

Most importantly, this is not an isolated petition. The provisioned petition and reply explicitly seek coordination with GEO Group v. Nwauzor, No. 25-828, involving the same contractor and detention facility. A corpus lookup and the Court's official companion docket confirmed that Nwauzor remains pending, received a CVSG on May 18, and received the invited brief on September 15. I read that official brief's recommendation: it urges a grant and identifies this petition as a possible companion, with an alternative hold tied to another case. This is direct federal support for review of the shared legal issue, not an SG recommendation filed in this petition. The new true relist makes coordinated consideration plausible. I raise the grant-family estimate substantially above the 34.8% anchor principally for that combination, and put more mass on a later companion-driven GVR than a separate argument.

## Reasons not to be more confident

The BIO's introduction, split discussion, and vehicle objections deserve substantial weight. Washington says the contract requires compliance with state and local health and safety standards; it distinguishes incidental regulation from measures controlling or prohibiting federal activity. It contends that CoreCivic actually approves the same standard and that the asserted split concerns applications rather than conflicting legal rules. The BIO also invokes Hencely and Menocal against categorical contractor immunity. GEO's reply responds that those decisions address different liability or immunity questions and do not authorize discriminatory control of federal functions. I do not treat the petition's description of a square split as an established fact.

The appellate judgment vacated preliminary relief and remanded the discrimination comparison rather than finally resolving the entire controversy. Washington argues that further proceedings are the better vehicle, and says a later discrimination appeal is pending. The decision-below summary confirms that civil detention facilities, not prisons, were selected as comparators. Those posture and comparator issues could justify denial here even if the Court takes Nwauzor. Its distinct minimum-wage controversy does not guarantee that its eventual holding will require vacatur in this health-and-safety-regulation case. The same-question file contains no matches above its threshold; that is not evidence against the expressly identified doctrinal companion.

My GVR forecast depends on a future relevant companion disposition; I identify no already-issued decision that presently compels a GVR. Denial remains a substantial 37% possibility. The further-distribution forecast is 72%, allowing for an immediate disposition at the next conference and for holds that may not promptly generate another entry. The 4% CVSG probability reflects that the government has already supplied its views on the related question elsewhere. Conditional on denial, I assign a 17% chance of a noted dissent or statement, not 17% unconditionally. Small independent order-writer probabilities also allow for writings on a grant-side summary order.
