## In brief

GEO Group asks the Court to decide whether Washington can impose health and safety rules on its privately run immigration detention facility.

## What happened

GEO Group operates a privately owned detention facility in Tacoma, Washington, under contract with federal immigration authorities. Washington passed a law applying health and safety standards, developed for other congregate care facilities, to private detention centers. GEO sued, arguing the law unconstitutionally regulates and discriminates against a federal contractor and is preempted by federal law. The district court agreed in part and blocked several provisions. The Ninth Circuit rejected GEO's direct-regulation and preemption arguments, but sent the discrimination claim back to the district court to apply a different comparison than the one the district court had used. GEO sought rehearing, which was denied, and then petitioned the Supreme Court.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked whether a state's rules governing how a federal contractor runs a facility holding federal detainees interfere with the federal government's authority, when the state exempts its own facilities from those same rules. The case touches on how much protection federal contractors receive from state regulation under the constitutional principle that states cannot control or discriminate against the federal government or those who work for it.

## Where it stands

The petition has been fully briefed, including a brief opposing review and a reply from GEO Group. It was distributed for the Court's October 16, 2026 conference, then rescheduled and redistributed for a conference on November 6, 2026. The docket shows no decision yet on whether the Court will hear the case.

## What each outcome would mean

GEO Group, which lost on the issues the Ninth Circuit decided against it, is asking the Court to take up the case. If the Court grants review, it would hear full arguments and decide the legal questions itself, deciding nothing yet about who prevails. If the Court denies review, the Ninth Circuit's decision stands, meaning Washington's law continues to apply and GEO's discrimination claim continues before the lower courts, which have already proceeded further while the petition was pending.
