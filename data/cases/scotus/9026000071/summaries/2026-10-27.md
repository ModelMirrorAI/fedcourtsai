## In brief

GEO Group asks the Court to hear its challenge to a Washington law regulating health and safety at its immigration detention facility, arguing the law unconstitutionally targets federal operations.

## What happened

GEO operates a privately run immigration detention facility in Tacoma under contract with federal immigration authorities. Washington extended health and safety standards, already applied to other facilities holding people involuntarily, to private detention facilities like GEO's. GEO sued, arguing the law improperly regulates and discriminates against the federal government and is preempted by federal law. The Ninth Circuit rejected GEO's direct-regulation and preemption arguments, but sent GEO's discrimination claim back to the district court to apply the correct comparison standard. GEO then asked the Supreme Court to review the Ninth Circuit's decision.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked whether a state may apply detailed health and safety rules to a privately run detention facility that houses federal detainees, when the state exempts its own detention facilities from the same rules, and whether such rules improperly interfere with federal authority over immigration detention even though they're directed at a private company rather than the federal government itself.

## Where it stands

The petition has been distributed for conference twice, most recently scheduled for November 6, 2026, after being rescheduled from an earlier conference; the docket shows no decision yet on whether the Court will hear the case.

## What each outcome would mean

GEO Group, which lost on two of its three arguments at the Ninth Circuit, is asking the Court to take up the case. If the Court grants review, it would hear full arguments and decide the legal questions itself; a grant would not by itself decide who wins. If the Court denies review, the Ninth Circuit's decision stands, meaning its rejection of GEO's direct-regulation and preemption arguments remains in place, and the case returns to the lower courts, where GEO's discrimination claim and related proceedings are already continuing.
