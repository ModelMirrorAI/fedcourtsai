## In brief

Randy and Evgenia Quaid ask the Court to revive privacy claims dismissed after courts found their claims barred by California's litigation privilege.

## What happened

Randy and Evgenia Quaid sued several people and entities, alleging that a sheriff's employee accessed their restricted criminal-history and jail records and routed that material through a prosecutor to private lawyers involved in related state civil litigation against them. The federal district court dismissed their claims without leave to amend, holding California's litigation privilege barred the claims because the records were ultimately used in court filings. The Ninth Circuit affirmed, finding the claims "all stem" from obtaining and disclosing the records in a judicial proceeding, and that amendment would be futile. The Quaids, representing themselves, now ask the Supreme Court to review that ruling.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked whether a federal court may treat a state law protecting courtroom communications as automatically blocking privacy claims aimed at an earlier, separate wrong—unauthorized access to and routing of restricted government records—just because that material later surfaced in a lawsuit, without separately examining whether the earlier conduct could support its own claim.

## Where it stands

The petition was filed and the respondents' response was due September 28, 2026. Some respondents waived their right to respond, while Fidelity National Title Insurance Company and others filed a brief opposing the petition. The docket shows no decision yet on whether the Court will hear the case.

## What each outcome would mean

The Quaids, as petitioners, are asking the Court to take up their case. If the Court grants the petition, it would hear full arguments and decide whether the lower courts wrongly treated the litigation privilege as an automatic bar, with no outcome guaranteed for either side. If the Court denies the petition, the Ninth Circuit's ruling stands, leaving the dismissal of the Quaids' claims in place and the case over.
