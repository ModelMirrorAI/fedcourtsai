## In brief

Randy and Evgenia Quaid ask the Court to review a Ninth Circuit ruling that barred their privacy lawsuit over law-enforcement records shared with private litigants.

## What happened

Randy and Evgenia Quaid sued several people and agencies, alleging that a Santa Barbara sheriff's employee accessed their criminal-history and arrest records and passed them to a prosecutor, who gave them to private lawyers for use in unrelated civil litigation against the Quaids. The Quaids say this happened without a subpoena, court order, or law-enforcement purpose. A federal district court dismissed their privacy claims without allowing them to amend, ruling that California's litigation privilege, which shields communications made in court proceedings, covered the conduct. The Ninth Circuit affirmed, and later denied rehearing.

## What the Court is being asked

The Quaids ask the Court to decide whether a federal court can treat a state rule protecting courtroom communications as automatically blocking any amended privacy claim, just because the disputed records eventually surfaced in a lawsuit, even when the claim is really about how the records were obtained and passed around beforehand, before any court filing occurred.

## Where it stands

The petition was filed and docketed on August 27, 2026. The respondents' response is due September 28, 2026, and the Court has not yet acted on the petition.

## What each outcome would mean

The Quaids are asking the Court to take up their case. If the Court grants review, it would hear full arguments and decide whether the lower courts wrongly blocked their privacy claims; a grant would not by itself decide who wins. If the Court denies review, the Ninth Circuit's ruling stays in place, meaning the case remains dismissed and the Quaids could not pursue amended privacy claims over the records' handling.
