## What happened

Randy Quaid and Evgenia Quaid sued a group of defendants, including a Santa Barbara County sheriff's employee, a prosecutor, private attorneys, and a title insurance company, over allegations that their criminal-history and arrest records, including mug shots and jail records, were pulled from a law-enforcement database and passed along to private lawyers for use in unrelated civil litigation against them. A federal district court dismissed their privacy claims without letting them amend, ruling that California's litigation privilege for statements made in court proceedings covered the conduct. The Ninth Circuit Court of Appeals agreed, finding the claims all grew out of records that were eventually disclosed in a lawsuit. The Quaids, representing themselves, asked for rehearing, which was denied.

## What the Court is being asked

The Quaids argue that courts should look separately at how information was obtained before it reached a courtroom, not just how it was later used. They say the alleged wrongdoing was the unauthorized pulling and sharing of protected government records, not anything said or filed in the lawsuit itself, and that this earlier conduct should not be shielded from a lawsuit just because the records eventually surfaced in litigation. The Court is asked whether a lower court can treat that kind of legal shield as an absolute bar to amending a complaint, simply because the improperly obtained material was later used in a case, without separately examining whether the earlier data access was itself an independent wrong.
