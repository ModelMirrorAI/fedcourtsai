## In brief

Randy and Evgenia Quaid ask the Court to review a Ninth Circuit ruling that their privacy claims over leaked criminal records were barred by California's litigation privilege.

## What happened

The Quaids sued several parties connected to a California property dispute, alleging that a sheriff's employee accessed their criminal history records and routed them, through a prosecutor, to private attorneys who used the records in related state court proceedings. The district court dismissed their privacy claims with prejudice, holding California's litigation privilege barred claims based on records obtained and disclosed in judicial proceedings, and separately dismissed other claims and defendants on other grounds, including declaring the Quaids vexatious litigants. The Ninth Circuit affirmed in an unpublished decision, and denied rehearing.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked whether a federal court may treat a state litigation privilege, which shields communications made during court proceedings, as automatically blocking a lawsuit over how evidence was first obtained, when the claim is that government records were improperly accessed and passed along before ever being filed in court, rather than about the filing itself.

## Where it stands

One group of respondents, Fidelity National Title Insurance Company and James Hepworth, filed a brief opposing review; other respondents waived their right to respond. The petitioners filed a reply, and the case was distributed for the Court's November 6, 2026 conference.

## What each outcome would mean

The Quaids, as petitioners, are asking the Court to take up their case. If the Court grants review, it would hear full arguments and decide whether the privilege rule was applied too broadly, without deciding yet who wins. If it denies review, the Ninth Circuit's decision affirming dismissal of the Quaids' claims stands, leaving those respondents' win in place. A grant would not itself decide the question; only a later merits ruling would.
