From that state, I expect zero further distributions and denial in an orders list shortly after that conference, most likely in November 2026. The probability of at least one further distribution is 4%; if another occurs, one additional distribution is more likely than a prolonged series. The timing is an unscored forecast, not a confirmed orders-list date.

No CVSG is recorded. I expect none to be requested; the probability of a new CVSG is 0.1%. The dispute concerns California litigation privilege and the adequacy of these pleadings, without an identified federal-government interest that would make Solicitor General participation useful.

If the Court grants review, I expect the question to be narrowed to whether a federal court may deny amendment as futile under California Civil Code section 47(b) without distinguishing independently wrongful acquisition of restricted records from later litigation communications. I do not expect review of the government-claims, service, property-title, vexatious-litigant, or fee rulings that the petition expressly excludes. This question-selection forecast is context only.

Petitioners' request for vacatur does not itself make a GVR likely. If review is granted and relief ultimately follows, the narrow issue would be an opportunity to amend, not a determination that the respondents are liable.

Conditional on denial, I assign 1% to any noted dissent or statement respecting denial. I expect a routine denial without separate writing and make no Justice-specific vote or authorship prediction. A denial would leave the judgment standing without resolving the parties' competing interpretations of California privilege law.
