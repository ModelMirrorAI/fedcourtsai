# Forecast: Quaid v. Granet, No. 26-249 (petition disposition)

**Denied at the November 6, 2026 conference.** The docket shows one distribution,
for the 11/6/2026 conference, and no relist. I expect no further distribution:
the Court will deny the petition at its first conference, with an order-list
entry around November 9–10, 2026.

- **Relist:** unlikely (about 3%). Nothing in the petition would draw a
  Justice's attention: it raises no federal question that is not a restatement
  of a state-law issue, and it cites no circuit split.
- **CVSG:** none. All parties are private, the dispute turns on California
  Civil Code § 47(b), and no federal interest is involved.
- **Which QP, if granted:** the single QP is whether a federal court may apply
  § 47(b) as a categorical bar to amendment of claims based on noncommunicative
  "antecedent" access to law-enforcement database material. A grant is very
  unlikely. If the Court did act, it would most likely be a summary disposition
  rather than plenary review: a per curiam or a GVR telling the Ninth Circuit to
  apply California's gravamen test (*Kimmel*, *Rusheen*). The Court almost never
  corrects error on state law, so even this route is remote.
- **Summary route vs plenary, given a grant:** I put a summary disposition at
  about 30%. Plenary review of a state-law privilege question has no evident
  federal hook, but summary correction of a misapplied state-law test would be
  unusual too.
- **Dissent from denial:** none expected.
