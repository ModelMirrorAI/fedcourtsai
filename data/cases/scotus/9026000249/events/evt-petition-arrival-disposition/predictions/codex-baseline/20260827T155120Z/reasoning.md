# Rationale

I assign a 0.25% probability of any grant and predict denial. The cell is a forward, paid-petition arrival forecast in OT2026, with a `baseline` band under `sal-v3`, zero distributions, and no CVSG at the frozen moment.

The leakage-safe arrival anchor is the weakest band's bracketed `reached` rate, not the terminal relist-zero rate. Pooling the `sal-v3` baseline risk sets across OT2017-OT2025 in the committed statpack gives an estimated grant-family rate of about 6.52% over 13,163 denial-reweighted resolved petitions. The modern Ninth Circuit cut is lower than that arrival anchor, with about 3.3% in the grant family (2.2% plenary grants plus 1.1% GVRs).

I adjust sharply downward from both figures. The provisioned snapshot identifies only a private appeal from the Ninth Circuit and supplies no petition, questions presented, or brief in opposition. Sanctioned CourtListener retrieval of the pre-petition lower-court record shows that the underlying action concerned private real-property proceedings; the district court dismissed nearly all claims with prejudice, declared the petitioners vexatious litigants, imposed a limited prefiling order, and awarded fees and costs. The consolidated appeal later proceeded pro se. That record looks highly case-specific and vehicle-heavy, and it discloses no federal party, circuit conflict, or recurring legal question that would ordinarily support certiorari. CourtListener listed the later Ninth Circuit memorandum but had no readable copy, and its opinion search returned no published opinion, so I could not test whether the court below created a broader doctrinal issue.

The 96% relist-increment number is an increment from the arrival record's zero distributions: it principally represents the likelihood of one ordinary initial conference distribution, not a forecast of a true post-conference relist. I expect no second distribution. The 0.05% CVSG probability reflects the absence of any apparent federal interest in this private dispute. Conditional on the very rare event of a grant, I put 70% on a cert-order disposition because a correctable procedural error or intervening authority is more plausible here than plenary review. Conditional on denial, I put only 0.2% on a noted dissent or statement because the available record reveals no issue likely to attract a separate writing.

The principal uncertainty is the missing petition/QP text and unavailable Ninth Circuit memorandum. If either document frames a clean, preserved federal question obscured by the docket history, this estimate is too low.
