# Forecast — Quaid, et ux. v. Granet, et al., No. 26-249

Forecast at docketing (August 27, 2026), before any distribution exists.

**Disposition: denied.** The Court will deny certiorari. The likeliest path:
the respondents (an attorney sued personally and the private buyers of the
petitioners' former home) waive a response by the September 28, 2026 due date,
the petition is distributed for a single conference in late October or
November 2026, and a one-line denial issues within a week of that conference —
denial by roughly December 2026, and almost certainly within OT2026.

**Relists: distributed once, then denied without relisting.** The petition
currently shows zero distributions; it will be distributed at least once
(nearly every properly filed paid petition is — the small residual is
withdrawal or procedural dismissal before conference), and I expect exactly
one distribution. Nothing here — a fact-bound pro se dispute arising from the
petitioners' own dismissed suits against their former Montecito home's buyers
and an opposing attorney — gives any chambers a reason to hold it over.

**No CVSG.** No federal party, no federal program, no question on which the
Solicitor General's views would be sought. A CVSG here would be extraordinary.

**No summary disposition.** No intervening decision of the Court bears on a
Ninth Circuit memorandum affirmance of a dismissal in a private property and
attorney-conduct dispute, so there is nothing to GVR against; the conditional
`summary-disposition-route` number reflects only that *if* the grant-family
tail event somehow occurred, a cert-order disposition would be at least as
likely as plenary review — not that either is expected.

**No separate writing.** Expect no dissent from denial and no statement
respecting denial from any Justice.

**If granted (counterfactual only):** the Court would not take any question
presented as written; a petition of this shape would reach plenary review only
via some procedural question invisible in the docket metadata, and I forecast
no such question surviving to the merits.
