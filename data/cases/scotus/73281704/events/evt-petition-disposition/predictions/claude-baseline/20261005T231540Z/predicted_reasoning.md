# Forecast: what the Court will do with No. 25-1199, Clark v. United States

**Bottom line.** The Court will not grant plenary review in this case on its own. Its fate is tied to the companion petition *Berry v. United States*, No. 25-7026, which presents the same first question (whether a circuit split alone can make a claim "debatable" for a certificate of appealability notwithstanding contrary home-circuit precedent). The modal outcome is a denial, most likely alongside a denial in *Berry*; the main path to a grant-side outcome here is a GVR after a *Berry* merits decision, not a grant of this petition.

**Relists.** The docket shows two distributions: the 9/28/2026 long conference and a relist to 10/9/2026. The relist moved in lockstep with *Berry*, which has now been distributed four times (6/18, 6/25, 9/28, 10/9) after a call for a response in April and two cert-stage amicus briefs. Expect this petition's distribution count to rise again more often than not (about 60%): if the Court grants *Berry* and holds *Clark*, as *Berry*'s counsel has asked, the hold itself produces no entries but the eventual disposition does, because a held petition is redistributed for the conference at which it is disposed of; if the Court instead is writing a dissent from denial, both cases will be relisted a few more times first. The one route that produces no further distribution is an outright denial of both petitions after the 10/9 conference, which is a real possibility (roughly a third).

**CVSG.** None. The United States is the respondent, so there is nobody to invite.

**Which question, if any question is taken.** If the Court takes the COA question, it will take it in *Berry*, whose COA denial expressly rested on *Hamilton v. Secretary* and on an underlying burden-of-proof split the government has conceded in another case. *Clark*'s own COA orders are one-line denials that do not say whether cognizability, procedural default, or the merits drove them, and the government disputes that any split exists on *Clark*'s underlying restitution/forfeiture question. The Court will not consolidate *Clark* with *Berry*; *Berry* opposes consolidation and the question is binary. QP2 (whether severe financial penalties can satisfy section 2255's custody requirement) will not be taken: the Court denied the related *Young v. United States*, No. 24-571, in April 2025 after calling for a response, and the government's survey of the circuits (no court has held restitution or forfeiture custodial; the Second Circuit has only reserved the question; the Sixth Circuit's cases are unreasoned and confined to ineffective-assistance claims) is more persuasive than the petition's 5-2 count.

**Summary disposition versus plenary review.** Conditional on any grant-side outcome here, a summary route is much the likelier form (about three in four): the realistic grant is a GVR for reconsideration in light of a petitioner-favorable *Berry* decision, entered when the held petition is finally disposed of in 2027. A plenary grant of *Clark* (consolidated with *Berry*) is possible but unlikely, and a summary reversal is not on the table.

**Dissent from denial.** If both petitions are denied, there is a meaningful chance (around one in three) of a dissent or statement from Justice Sotomayor, who has written repeatedly on COA administration (*McGee*, *Jordan v. Fisher*, *Johnson v. Vandergriff*, *Shockley v. Vandergriff*), likely joined by Justice Jackson and possibly Justice Kagan; it would more naturally be captioned in *Berry*, so the odds it appears on *Clark*'s own docket are lower. If *Clark* is denied after a hold, or denied outright while *Berry* is granted, expect no separate writing.

**If granted (conditional).** Were the Court to reach the COA question in a case including *Clark*, the likely holding is that an acknowledged circuit conflict on the dispositive issue satisfies *Slack*'s "debatable among jurists of reason" standard, so a COA must issue even where home-circuit precedent forecloses the claim. Nothing about the restitution/forfeiture custody question would be decided.
