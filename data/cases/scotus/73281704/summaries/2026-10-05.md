## In brief

Fred Davis Clark, Jr. asks the Court to revive his bid to challenge the restitution and forfeiture parts of his sentence through a federal prisoner's post-conviction motion.

## What happened

Clark was convicted of bank fraud and related crimes and sentenced to 40 years in prison plus about $179 million in restitution and $309 million in forfeiture. His prison term was later commuted, but the financial penalties stayed in place. After dropping his direct appeal, Clark filed a motion under a federal law that lets a prisoner ask the sentencing court to vacate or correct a sentence. The district court denied the motion, holding that this law does not cover noncustodial penalties like restitution and forfeiture, and it denied him permission to appeal. The Eleventh Circuit denied that permission too, and denied reconsideration. Clark now asks the Supreme Court to take up his case.

## What the Court is being asked

Clark raises two questions. First, whether an appeals court may deny permission to appeal a post-conviction ruling simply because its own prior decisions already foreclose the claim, even when other appeals courts have ruled the opposite way on the same issue. Second, whether the law allowing prisoners to challenge their sentences after conviction can reach financial penalties like restitution and forfeiture when those penalties are severe enough to restrict a person's freedom, even though the person is no longer in custody.

## Where it stands

The petition has been fully briefed, including the government's opposition and Clark's reply. It was distributed for the Court's conference on September 28, 2026, and then again for the conference on October 9, 2026; the docket does not show any action taken on it yet.

## What each outcome would mean

Clark is asking the Court to take up his case after losing below. If the Court grants review, it would hear the case and decide whether his financial-penalty claims can be raised this way and whether he should have been allowed to appeal; a grant would not by itself decide who wins. If the Court denies review, the lower court rulings stand, leaving in place the denial of Clark's challenge to his restitution and forfeiture obligations and the government's position that such claims are not available under this law.
