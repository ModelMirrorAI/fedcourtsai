## In brief

A man ordered to pay hundreds of millions in restitution and forfeiture asks the Court to revive his bid to challenge those penalties after his prison sentence was commuted.

## What happened

Fred Davis Clark, Jr. was convicted of bank fraud and related crimes and ordered to serve 40 years in prison, pay about $179 million in restitution, and forfeit roughly $309 million. His prison sentence was later commuted, but the financial penalties stayed in place. Clark dropped his direct appeal and instead asked a federal district court to vacate the financial penalties under the law governing motions to vacate a sentence. The district court ruled that this law does not cover purely financial penalties, following longstanding circuit precedent, and denied him permission to appeal. The appeals court agreed and denied reconsideration.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked two questions. First, whether a court of appeals must allow an appeal to proceed whenever other circuits disagree with its own precedent, even if its own circuit's rule is settled and controls the outcome. Second, whether someone can use this vacate-a-sentence law to challenge restitution or forfeiture orders—penalties that don't involve physical imprisonment—when the amount owed is so large that it effectively restrains the person's freedom.

## Where it stands

The petition has been fully briefed, including the government's opposition and Clark's reply. It was distributed for the Court's conference on September 28, 2026, and again for the conference on October 9, 2026; the docket does not yet show a decision.

## What each outcome would mean

Clark, who lost in the lower courts, is asking the Court to take up his case. If the Court grants review, it would hear full arguments and decide one or both questions, without yet deciding who wins. If the Court denies review, the lower courts' rulings stand, meaning Clark cannot pursue his challenge to the restitution and forfeiture orders, and the government's position prevails. Clark's reply brief also asks the Court to pair his case with a related petition, Berry v. United States, which raises a similar appealability question.
