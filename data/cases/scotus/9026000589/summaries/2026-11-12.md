## In brief

The federal government asks the Court to review whether a $16 billion court order forcing EPA to keep funding terminated climate grants can stand.

## What happened

EPA awarded billions of dollars in grants under a climate fund created by the Inflation Reduction Act. After a change in administration, EPA's new leadership raised concerns about conflicts of interest and oversight, and terminated grants to several nonprofit grantees. The grantees sued in federal district court, which blocked the terminations and ordered continued funding, including through Citibank, which held the money as the government's financial agent. A Court of Appeals panel initially sided with the government, but the full appeals court, split on various grounds, largely upheld the injunction. While the case was pending, Congress repealed the law authorizing the fund.

## What the Court is being asked

The government asks the Court to decide two things. First, whether a separate federal law that generally sends contract-like disputes with the government to a specialized claims court means these grantees should not have been allowed to sue in ordinary district court to fight their grant terminations. Second, whether the grantees can still rely on the now-repealed climate fund law to keep their court-ordered funding in place.

## Where it stands

The petition was filed on November 2, 2026, and a response is due December 4, 2026. The docket also shows a motion by one grantee to extend time, which the government opposed and the grantee defended in a reply; no ruling on cert has yet been recorded.

## What each outcome would mean

The federal government, as petitioner, is asking the Court to reverse the lower court's decision, which favored the grantees, including Climate United Fund and Coalition for Green Capital. If the Court grants review and later rules for the government, the grantees' lawsuit could be barred from district court and the funding order could fall. If it rules for the grantees, the funding order could stand. If the Court denies review, the appeals court's decision favoring the grantees remains in place.
