## In brief

The EPA asks the Court to decide whether grant recipients may sue in district court over EPA's termination of billions in climate grants.

## What happened

In 2024 the EPA awarded about $20 billion in grants under the Greenhouse Gas Reduction Fund, a climate program created by the Inflation Reduction Act. After a change in administration, EPA officials cited conflicts of interest and lack of oversight and terminated $16 billion of those grants, held in accounts at Citibank. The grantees sued in district court, which ordered EPA and Citibank to keep funding the grants. A panel of the D.C. Circuit reversed, but the full court of appeals, split in several directions, voted to uphold most of the injunction. Congress separately repealed the underlying grant program while the case was pending.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked whether a federal law that routes contract-like money claims against the government into a specialized claims court also bars grant recipients from suing in ordinary district court to stop the termination of their grants. It is also asked whether the recipients' claim that the termination violated the now-repealed law creating the grant program can support the court order blocking the terminations.

## Where it stands

The petition for review was filed on November 2, 2026, and a response is due by December 4, 2026. The Court has not yet acted on whether to hear the case.

## What each outcome would mean

The EPA, as petitioner, is asking the Court to reverse the lower court's decision upholding the injunction. If the Court grants review and later rules for the EPA, the grantees' lawsuit could be barred from district court, and the order requiring continued funding would likely fall. If it rules for the grantees, the district court injunction could stand, keeping EPA's terminations blocked. If the Court denies review, the court of appeals decision upholding most of the injunction remains in effect, and the grantees continue to have access to the disputed funds for now.
