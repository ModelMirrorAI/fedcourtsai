## In brief

EPA asks the Court to overturn an appeals court ruling that let grant recipients sue in district court over EPA's termination of $16 billion in climate grants.

## What happened

EPA awarded billions of dollars in Greenhouse Gas Reduction Fund grants to several nonprofits, including Climate United Fund and Coalition for Green Capital, with Citibank holding the money as the government's financial agent. After a change in administration, EPA terminated the grants, citing concerns about conflicts of interest and oversight. The grantees sued in district court, which blocked the terminations. After rounds of appeal, a divided full D.C. Circuit left the injunction mostly in place, with judges splitting on different grounds. EPA then asked the Supreme Court to step in.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked two questions. First, whether a federal law channeling contract-like claims against the government into a specialized claims court means these grantees' lawsuit should never have been allowed in regular district court at all. Second, whether the grantees can rely on the now-repealed law that created the grant fund to keep their court-ordered block on the terminations in place, given that Congress later repealed that law.

## Where it stands

The petition was filed and is awaiting a response from the respondents, now due December 15, 2026, after the Court granted a request to extend that deadline. The case has not yet been scheduled for the justices to consider at a conference.

## What each outcome would mean

EPA, joined by its officials, is asking the Court to take up the case and reverse the lower court. If the Court grants review, it would hear full arguments and decide whether the grantees' suit belongs in a different court and whether the repealed law can support the injunction; a grant alone would not decide who wins. If the Court denies review, the D.C. Circuit's decision keeping the injunction largely in place stands, leaving Climate United Fund, Coalition for Green Capital, and the other grantees able to continue pursuing their case in district court.
