## Why move above 73.4%

**A substantial and recurring jurisdictional question.** QP1 asks whether the Tucker Act prevents district-court challenges to EPA grant terminations. The petition, argument sections A and C, describes conflicting approaches to funding-cancellation suits and identifies the Fourth Circuit's *Solutions in Hometown Connections* as a contrary approach. These split descriptions are the petitioner's representations, not independently verified holdings on identical records. Even discounting their advocacy, the question is consequential beyond this single environmental program.

The independently retrieved primary opinion in *Department of Education v. California*, 604 U.S. 650, 651–652 (April 4, 2025; CourtListener opinion 11243445, opening chunk), supports the government's jurisdictional concern. On a stay application, the Court found the government's jurisdictional showing likely to succeed and distinguished orders enforcing contractual payment obligations from other agency review that might result in disbursements. That is a preliminary assessment, not a plenary resolution of this case's questions. The petition also relies on *NIH v. American Public Health Ass'n*, 606 U.S. 998 (2025); my citation and caption searches did not retrieve that opinion, so its treatment here rests on the petition, not an independently read decision. Recent emergency treatment of the general issue supports an eventual merits clarification without making cert automatic.

**The lower-court disagreement is concrete and mature enough for review.** The supplied `decision-below.txt` matches the August 4, 2026 en banc judgment described in the petition's appendix, including the same appeal numbers, parties, relief, and vote divisions. Six judges affirmed the bar on effectuating the March 2025 termination notices; the remaining injunction provisions survived an equally divided court. The six-judge coalition did not agree on all remedial and repeal issues. That fracture after en banc consideration is more meaningful than the generated metadata's unpublished label or a bare disagreement with a panel.

**Exceptional practical stakes.** According to the petition's statement and argument C, five grants worth more than $16 billion are implicated, and a continued administrative stay rests on respondents' consent while cert is sought. I use that as a representation in the petition, not a separately verified later stay order. The government has a concrete reason to seek resolution rather than wait indefinitely for final district-court proceedings. The 0.88 stakes score reflects the amount and nationwide funding-review implications, not the grant probability.

Together these considerations justify an upward adjustment of about nine percentage points from the class anchor, after the discounts below. This is a judgmental adjustment, not a fitted numerical likelihood ratio.

## Why not near certainty

**An awkward vehicle and substantial opposing arguments.** This is review of a preliminary injunction, not a final merits judgment. The money has already been placed in Citibank accounts, and respondents include both direct grantees and subgrantees lacking contracts with EPA. Judge Pillard's panel dissent in the supplied appendix argues that the claims enforce independent statutory and constitutional limits and protect already-disbursed property, rather than seek ordinary contractual performance. The retrieved *California* opinion itself preserves a distinction between those kinds of relief. The government's characterization does not eliminate these differences.

**Repeal complicates the scope of review.** QP2 asks whether the now-repealed Greenhouse Gas Reduction Fund statute can support the injunction. The actual en banc judgment records disagreement about whether repeal affects grants already obligated and disbursed, while agreeing on neither all remedies nor all grounds. Repeal therefore does not establish automatic mootness on this record. It creates both a reason to review the injunction and a potentially narrower, program-specific path that can make this an imperfect vehicle for the general Tucker Act question. The absence of a provisioned opposition limits my view of preservation, jurisdictional distinctions, and the best objections to cert.

**A different petition could lead.** The petition cites *Trump v. Thakur*, No. 26-210, in its discussion of claimants outside contractual privity. A limited corpus lookup showed that separate petition still pending, with one distribution for the November 13, 2026 conference, and no recorded grant or denial. I did not read its questions or briefs, so I do not claim that it presents an identical question or that this Court has linked the cases. It creates only a limited possibility of another vehicle leading and this petition being held and later GVR'd. The provisioned `same-question.json` contains no match above its 0.5 threshold, which is not proof that no relevant alternative exists. These facts leave a significant summary-route possibility without making it the modal route.
