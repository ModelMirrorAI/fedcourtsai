# Forecast of the Court's disposition

The Court will grant review, most likely for merits briefing and argument. The central question will be QP1: whether the Tucker Act channels these challenges to federal grant terminations out of district court and into the Court of Federal Claims. I expect review also to encompass QP2, the effect of the authorizing statute's repeal on the injunction, because that question bears on the independently asserted statutory basis for relief. The grant may narrow QP1 to the claims and remedies actually involved here rather than every challenge to government funding decisions.

The starting docket has **zero distributions and no CVSG**. I expect a first distribution after the December 4, 2026 response deadline and ordinary response/reply processing, probably around early 2027, followed by **one further distribution before action**. Two further distributions or a longer hold are the principal scheduling alternatives. The 0.99 `relist-increment` probability includes the first distribution from zero; it does not predict a 99% chance of a second conference. The Court will not call for the Solicitor General's views: the Solicitor General is already counsel for the petitioners. The probability of a new CVSG is 0.002.

Conditional on granting relief, the Court is more likely to order further merits proceedings than to resolve the case in the cert order: **70% versus 30%**. The jurisdictional issue recurs across grant programs, but the already-disbursed funds, subgrantees, and repeal dispute require attention beyond mechanically applying an earlier stay. The principal alternative is a hold followed by a GVR if another case supplies the controlling rule, or summary reversal under the existing grant-jurisdiction decisions. No already granted same-question lead case has been identified in the consulted record.

If the Court instead denies review, I expect a routine order without a dissent or statement; the conditional probability of any such notation or writing is 0.10. Thomas or Alito are the likelier dissenting writers in that denial branch. A summary disposition favoring the government instead makes a separate writing by Jackson, Kagan, or Sotomayor more plausible. The central prediction remains no separate writing on the cert order.

If granted and ultimately resolved on the merits, the likely focus is the boundary between independent public-law limits on agency action and enforcement of a contractual entitlement to grant funds. That is a conditional forecast, not a prediction that a denial would produce a majority opinion, and not an unconditional forecast of a merits victory for either side.
