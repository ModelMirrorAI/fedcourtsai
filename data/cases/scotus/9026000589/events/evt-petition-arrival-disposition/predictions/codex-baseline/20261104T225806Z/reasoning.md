I move about 12.6 percentage points above that anchor, rather than treating all federal petitions alike:

- **A recurring jurisdictional question with a concrete, exceptionally large dispute.** QP1 concerns whether the Tucker Act channels grant-termination challenges to the Court of Federal Claims. The petition alleges continuing disagreement after two Supreme Court stay decisions and identifies an opposing Fourth Circuit approach, including `Solutions in Hometown Connections v. Noem`. The dispute concerns approximately $16 billion for these respondents, not the entire $27 billion program. Sources: `questions-presented.txt`; petition pp. 2–5, 29–33. The circuit-conflict characterization is the government's submission, not an independently established identity of holdings.
- **The actual judgment confirms serious division.** The August 4, 2026 en banc judgment affirms the prohibition on effectuating EPA's termination notices with six votes and affirms the other portions by an equally divided court. Five judges would sustain the broader injunction, Judge Millett distinguishes ongoing administration after repeal, and four judges reject the relevant prospective relief after repeal. This is not simply an unfavorable panel decision the government declined to test en banc. Sources: `decision-below.txt`; petition appendix A, pp. 1a–4a. The direct judgment controls over the supplemental docket summary's limited post-judgment rehearing search; its failure to find a later rehearing petition does not negate the en banc adjudication already reflected in the judgment.
- **Existing Supreme Court intervention makes review plausible without deciding this petition for it.** I independently read the operative portion of `Department of Education v. California`, 604 U.S. 650 (2025), CourtListener opinion 10839607, chunk 0. Its April 4, 2025 stay order treated the government's contractual-forum argument as likely meritorious, while acknowledging that some APA relief with financial consequences remains within district-court jurisdiction. That directly supports the issue's salience, but a preliminary stay assessment is not a final merits holding governing every grant dispute. The petition's account of `NIH`, 606 U.S. 998, including Justice Barrett's distinction between grant termination and separate agency guidance, supplies additional context; unsuccessful MCP searches did not independently verify that account. Sources: California, pp. 1–3 and the beginning of Justice Kagan's dissent; petition pp. 15–17, 23–24.

I stop at 0.86 rather than above 0.95 because this is an interlocutory injunction involving unusual private-bank accounts, subgrantees without direct contractual privity, and a repealed funding statute. Those features permit distinctions from a standard demand for unpaid grant money. The en banc court's actual rationale treats already-disbursed funds differently from future administration, and much of its disposition supplies no majority rationale. The Court could prefer a cleaner vehicle, hold this petition, or conclude that further lower-court proceedings would sharpen the issue. Allegations of mismanagement and improper political influence in the petition are advocacy, not adjudicated facts supporting my forecast. Sources: `decision-below.txt`; petition pp. 6–12, 24–28, 31–33.
