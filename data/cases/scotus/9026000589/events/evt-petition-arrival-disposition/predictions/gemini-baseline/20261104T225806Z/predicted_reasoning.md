## 3. Questions Presented and Scope of Review
The Court will grant certiorari and take **both Questions Presented** as framed in the petition:
1. Whether the Tucker Act, 28 U.S.C. § 1491(a)(1), precludes respondents from challenging in district court the termination of grant agreements by the Environmental Protection Agency.
2. Whether respondents' claim that EPA's grant terminations violated the now-repealed statute authorizing the Greenhouse Gas Reduction Fund, 42 U.S.C. § 7434 (2024), can support the district court's injunction against those terminations.

Taking both questions gives the Court full flexibility to address both the threshold Tucker Act channeling rule (which divided the en banc D.C. Circuit 5–5 on the APA claims and 6–4 on the statutory claim) and the independent effect of Congress's repeal of Section 60103 of the Inflation Reduction Act in Section 60002 of the One Big Beautiful Bill Act, Pub. L. No. 119-21, 139 Stat. 154 (2025).

## 4. Plenary Review vs. Summary Disposition (`summary-disposition-route`: 0.08)
Conditional on a grant, **plenary review with full merits briefing and oral argument** is overwhelmingly the likeliest route (`92%` conditional probability), while a disposition in the cert order itself (such as a GVR or summary reversal) carries a small conditional probability (`0.08`). Unlike subsidiary cases held for a lead vehicle, this $16 billion en banc D.C. Circuit decision is itself the flagship vehicle presenting both the Tucker Act grant-termination issue and the OBBBA repeal issue. A GVR would occur only if the Court first decided the Tucker Act question in another case and elected to remand this case for reconsideration, or summarily vacated in light of the statutory repeal without oral argument.

## 5. Conditional Merits Outlook
If granted for plenary review, the Court is likely to vacate or reverse the D.C. Circuit's en banc judgment and direct dismissal or vacatur of the preliminary injunction, holding (consistent with its stay orders in *Department of Education v. California*, 604 U.S. 650 (2025), and *NIH v. American Public Health Ass'n*, 606 U.S. 998 (2025)) that claims seeking to enjoin the termination of federal grant agreements and compel continued access to grant funds are in essence contractual claims channeled to the Court of Federal Claims under the Tucker Act, and/or that prospective injunctive relief cannot be sustained on the basis of a repealed statutory appropriation (42 U.S.C. § 7434).

## 6. Separate Writings on the Cert Order (`dissent-from-denial`: 0.45; `order-writers`)
Because the Court is very likely to grant review via a standard unsigned grant order, the unconditional probability of any individual Justice authoring a separate statement on the cert order is low (`0.01` to `0.05` across the nine Justices). However, *conditional on the unlikely event that the Court denies certiorari*, there is a substantial probability (`0.45`) that one or more conservative Justices (most likely Justice Alito, Justice Thomas, or Justice Gorsuch) would author a dissent from the denial of certiorari, or that a statement would accompany a summary disposition, given the $16 billion fiscal stakes and the lower court's departure from *California* and *NIH*.
