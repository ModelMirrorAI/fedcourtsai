## 2. Case-Specific Signals and Adjustments

### Upward Factors (Pushing Above the 73.4% Federal Base Rate)
1. **Direct Continuity with Prior Supreme Court Emergency-Docket Stays (`California` and `NIH`):**
   In 2025, the Supreme Court twice stayed district-court preliminary injunctions that barred federal agencies from terminating grants and ordered continued payments, concluding that the government was likely to succeed in showing that the Tucker Act, 28 U.S.C. § 1491(a)(1), channels such claims to the Court of Federal Claims and precludes APA sovereign-immunity waivers under 5 U.S.C. § 702 (`Department of Education v. California`, 604 U.S. 650 (2025) (per curiam); `NIH v. American Public Health Ass'n`, 606 U.S. 998 (2025)). Having already found the Tucker Act issue certworthy as a predicate for those emergency stays, the Court has strong institutional reasons to resolve the question on the merits docket.
2. **Acknowledged Circuit Conflict and En Banc Fracture in the D.C. Circuit:**
   Following `California` and `NIH`, the courts of appeals have divided sharply over whether the Tucker Act bars district-court jurisdiction over grant-termination challenges framed as APA, statutory, or separation-of-powers claims. The First, Fourth, and Ninth Circuits (in cases such as `New York v. Trump`, 171 F.4th 1 (1st Cir. 2026); `Sustainability Institute v. Trump`, 165 F.4th 817 (4th Cir. 2026); `Solutions in Hometown Connections v. Noem`, 165 F.4th 835 (4th Cir. 2026); and `Thakur v. Trump`, 176 F.4th 1187 (9th Cir. 2026)) held that the Tucker Act bars such claims in district court, whereas the D.C. Circuit sitting en banc here (and the Ninth Circuit in `Pacito v. Trump`, 169 F.4th 895 (9th Cir. 2026)) sustained district-court preliminary injunctions. Moreover, the D.C. Circuit's en banc disposition here (`184 F.4th 679`) vacated a 2–1 panel opinion by Judge Rao (joined by Judge Katsas) and affirmed the preliminary injunction in an unreasoned per curiam judgment—6–4 on paragraph two of the injunction and 5–5 (by an equally divided court) on the remaining paragraphs and APA grounds.
3. **Massive Fiscal and Statutory Stakes ($16 Billion and the One Big Beautiful Bill Act Repeal):**
   This case involves over **$16 billion** in terminated EPA Greenhouse Gas Reduction Fund grants held at Citibank under Account Control Agreements, plus a second question presented of independent national importance: whether a preliminary injunction enforcing Section 60103 of the Inflation Reduction Act (42 U.S.C. § 7434) can stand after Congress expressly repealed that statute in Section 60002 of the One Big Beautiful Bill Act, Pub. L. No. 119-21, 139 Stat. 154 (2025). Notably, Chief Judge Srinivasan noted in the en banc judgment that he would have voted to affirm paragraph two *but for* Congress's repeal of Section 60103, underscoring the centrality and clean presentation of the repeal question. Furthermore, respondents consented to extend the D.C. Circuit's administrative stay pending disposition of this certiorari petition, preserving the status quo for plenary review.

### Downward / Moderating Factors and GVR Considerations
1. **Interlocutory Posture and Unusual Financial-Agent Structure:**
   The case arrives on review of a preliminary injunction rather than final judgment, and the Greenhouse Gas Reduction Fund grants use an unusual three-party financial-agent structure involving Citibank Account Control Agreements (which enabled respondents to argue that they hold a present property interest in deposited funds rather than a typical Treasury drawdown claim). However, both questions presented are pure legal issues (subject-matter jurisdiction under the Tucker Act and the legal effect of the statutory repeal in Pub. L. No. 119-21 § 60002), making the interlocutory posture no barrier to review.
2. **Other Pending Petitions Raising the Tucker Act Question (`Trump v. Thakur`, No. 26-210):**
   Corpus lookup shows that `Trump v. Thakur`, No. 26-210 (filed Aug. 19, 2026, distributed for the Nov. 13, 2026 conference), is also pending on the Court's docket. However, in `Thakur`, the Ninth Circuit *ruled for the government* on the Tucker Act issue (and the government petitioned only on a subsidiary class/scope question, or as a companion vehicle), whereas in `Zeldin v. Climate United Fund`, the en banc D.C. Circuit ruled *against* the government on a $16 billion injunction and also presents the statutory repeal question under the One Big Beautiful Bill Act. Consequently, `26-589` is far more likely to be granted for plenary review (`0.78`) than held for a GVR (`0.07`).

## 3. Conclusion and Probability Decomposition

Combining the 73.4% `federal` salience-band base rate with the extraordinary fiscal stakes ($16B), the 6–4 / 5–5 fractured en banc D.C. Circuit judgment invalidating EPA's grant terminations notwithstanding `California`, `NIH`, and Congress's repeal of 42 U.S.C. § 7434, we forecast a **85%** probability (`0.85`) that the petition will be granted (`granted` + `gvr` = `0.85`, `granted: true`).

We decompose the categorical probabilities across the cert-stage disposition space as follows:
- **`granted`**: `0.78` — Plenary review is the overwhelmingly likely form of grant given the $16 billion stakes, the D.C. Circuit en banc posture, and the two distinct questions presented (Tucker Act jurisdiction and OBBBA statutory repeal).
- **`gvr`**: `0.07` — A modest probability that the Court grants another Tucker Act vehicle first (or resolves the issue in a parallel appeal) and GVRs this case in light of that decision, or issues a summary disposition/GVR.
- **`denied`**: `0.13` — The Court declines review due to the interlocutory preliminary-injunction posture or case-specific complexities of the Citibank Account Control Agreements.
- **`dismissed`**: `0.02` — Small residual probability of settlement, mootness, or Rule 46 dismissal prior to disposition.
