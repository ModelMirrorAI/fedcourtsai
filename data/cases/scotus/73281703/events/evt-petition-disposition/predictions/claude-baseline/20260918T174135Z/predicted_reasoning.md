# Forecast: what the Court will do with No. 25-1197, Greer v. Benton School District

**Disposition.** The petition will most likely be **denied** at or shortly after the September 28, 2026 conference. P(any grant) = 0.18.

**Where the docket stands.** The snapshot shows **two distribution entries** (June 2 for the June 18 conference; August 26 for the September 28 conference), but the petition has **never actually been considered at a conference**: the Court called for a response on June 11, which pulled it from the June 18 list, and the August 26 entry is the ordinary redistribution once the brief in opposition (August 7) and reply (August 18) were in. So the forecast below runs from a state of *zero relists and one call for response*, not from "one relist".

**Relists.** The call for response shows at least one chambers took the petition seriously, and the Court's recent habit is to relist a petition at least once before granting it. I expect a **relist past the September 28 conference with probability ~0.30**: roughly the grant probability plus a further ~0.12 for a relist that ends in denial (a Justice considering a separate writing, or a vehicle check that fails). If it is relisted, one or two further distributions is the likely shape; a long relist string is not.

**CVSG.** No CVSG is on the docket. The United States is not a party, but section 504 is a federal statute the Departments of Justice and Education administer, and federal agencies are themselves section 504 defendants under the same provision (the D.C. Circuit's Redd case, on the petitioner's side of the split, was against a federal bureau). A CVSG is therefore a live but minority route: **~0.09**. If one issues, it would come off the September 28 or an October conference, and the petition would then carry into the spring.

**Which question the Court would take, if it grants.** Not the question as written. The reply concedes the QP carries "two superfluous words" ("for discrimination"), and the case is a retaliation claim by a non-disabled parent employed by a staffing agency. A grant would most plausibly come with a reformulated question, roughly: *whether 29 U.S.C. 794(d) imports Title I of the ADA's employer-employee requirement into section 504 claims arising from employment*. The Court would want the antecedent question (whether section 504 supports a private retaliation claim at all, which the Sixth Circuit has answered no) either bracketed by assumption or added as a second question; the possibility of having to add it is one reason the grant probability sits below where the split alone would put it.

**Summary disposition.** Conditional on a grant, a summary route is unlikely: **~0.04**. There is no intervening decision of this Court to GVR against, and a five-court split on a statutory question is resolved by argument, not per curiam.

**If denied.** Expect a **plain denial with no separate writing** (P(dissent or statement respecting denial | denial) ~0.05). This is a statutory split with no ideological valence and a vehicle a Justice can decline without comment; if anyone writes, it would be a short statement noting the split and inviting a cleaner vehicle rather than a dissent.

**If granted, the likely outcome (conditional only).** The petitioner's textual argument is the stronger one: section 504(a) protects any "otherwise qualified individual" in a federally funded program, and subsection (d) speaks of "standards" for deciding whether the section "has been violated", not of who may sue. A grant would more likely than not end in reversal of the Arkansas Supreme Court, with the retaliation-standing question reserved or resolved narrowly.
