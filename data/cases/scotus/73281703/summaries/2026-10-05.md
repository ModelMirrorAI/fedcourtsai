## In brief

The Supreme Court declined to hear a substitute teacher's bid to revive her disability-retaliation claim against a school district that stopped assigning her to its schools.

## What happened

Brandi Greer worked as a substitute teacher for Benton School District through a staffing agency, Kelly Services. After she filed an ethics complaint against her child's teacher, the District barred her from working at any of its schools. Greer sued the District under a federal disability-rights law, the Rehabilitation Act, claiming retaliation. A jury awarded her damages, and the trial court also issued an injunction in her favor. The Arkansas Supreme Court reversed, ruling that because Greer worked for a staffing agency rather than the District itself, she could not sue the District under that law. It also rejected her related state-law claims on separate grounds.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked whether a federal law barring disability discrimination by recipients of federal funding lets someone who works through an outside staffing agency, rather than directly for the funded organization, sue that organization for retaliation. Greer argues federal appeals courts are split on this question. The District answers that no real split covers her situation, that the case also turns on separate issues she has not challenged, and that the lower court's reading of the law was correct.

## Where it stands

The Court asked for and received a response from the District, along with a reply from Greer. The petition was distributed for a conference and, on October 5, 2026, the Court denied it.

## What each outcome would mean

Greer, who lost in the Arkansas Supreme Court, asked the Court to take up her case. By denying the petition, the Court leaves the Arkansas Supreme Court's decision in place: Greer's retaliation claim against Benton School District fails, along with the jury's damage award and the injunction that had barred the District from excluding her. The denial does not mean the Court agreed with the Arkansas court's reasoning; it simply leaves that ruling as the final word in this case.
