## In brief

The Court declined to hear a substitute teacher's bid to revive her disability-retaliation claim against a school district after Arkansas's top court ruled against her.

## What happened

Brandi Greer, a substitute teacher placed at a school by a staffing agency, filed an ethics complaint about her son's teacher. She says the Benton School District then barred her from working in the district, which she claims was retaliation related to her son's disability. She sued under a federal disability law, a civil-rights statute, and a constitutional claim. A trial court and jury ruled in her favor, awarding damages and an injunction. The Arkansas Supreme Court reversed, holding her claim failed because she was not the District's employee, and also ruled against her on separate state-law grounds.

## What the Court is being asked

Greer asks the Court to decide whether people who work for a federally funded program through an outside staffing agency, rather than being direct employees, can still sue that program for retaliation under the federal disability law. The District answers that the lower court never actually decided that broader question, and that other independent grounds support the ruling against Greer regardless.

## Where it stands

The petition was distributed for conference twice, with briefing completed in between, and the Court denied it.

## What each outcome would mean

Greer, who lost in the Arkansas Supreme Court, asked the Court to take up her case. Because the petition was denied, the Arkansas Supreme Court's decision stands: Greer's retaliation claim fails, and the damages award and injunction she won at trial remain reversed. The District's win below is final. Had the Court instead granted the petition, it would have agreed to hear and decide the underlying legal question, without yet determining which side was right.
