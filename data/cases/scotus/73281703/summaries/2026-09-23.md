## What happened

Brandi Greer worked as a substitute teacher in the Benton School District through a staffing agency, Kelly Services. After she filed an ethics complaint against her son's teacher over how the teacher treated her son, who has disabilities, the district barred her from substitute teaching there. Greer sued the district under Section 504 of the Rehabilitation Act, a federal law barring disability discrimination by programs that receive federal funding, along with related constitutional and state-law claims. A trial court ruled for Greer, and a jury awarded her damages. The Arkansas Supreme Court reversed, holding that Greer's retaliation claim failed because she was not the district's employee, and also rejected her other claims on separate grounds.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked whether a federal disability-discrimination law that protects people in programs receiving federal funding lets someone who works through an outside staffing agency, rather than being directly employed, bring a retaliation claim against the organization she worked for. Greer argues the law's protections are not limited to direct employees. The school district responds that the lower court never actually decided that broader question, that the case rests on other, unchallenged grounds, and that the courts of appeals are not divided on the specific issue this case presents.
