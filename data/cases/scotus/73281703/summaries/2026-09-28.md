## In brief

Greer asks the Court to decide if federal disability law lets contractor-employees sue school districts for retaliation, after Arkansas's top court rejected her claim.

## What happened

Brandi Greer, a substitute teacher employed by a staffing contractor, worked at a school her son attended. After she filed an ethics complaint against her son's teacher, the school district barred her from substitute teaching there. Greer sued the district and two officials, alleging retaliation under the Rehabilitation Act, plus constitutional and state civil-rights claims. A trial court ruled for Greer, and a jury awarded her damages. The Arkansas Supreme Court reversed, holding Greer could not sue under the Rehabilitation Act because she was not the district's employee, and separately rejecting her other claims on state-law grounds.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked whether a federal law barring disability discrimination by organizations that receive federal funding lets people who work as contractors, rather than direct employees, sue for retaliation the same way employees can. Lower courts disagree on this question. The school district responds that Arkansas's court never actually decided that broader question, since Greer's case involved retaliation rather than discrimination, and that other, independent grounds for its ruling make the case a poor one for resolving the issue.

## Where it stands

The petition has been distributed for a second time, for the Court's conference on September 28, 2026, after an earlier conference in June was followed by a request for the school district's response. The docket does not yet show any decision on the petition.

## What each outcome would mean

Greer, who lost in the Arkansas Supreme Court, is asking the Court to take up her case. If the Court grants the petition, it would hear the case and decide whether contractor-employees can bring retaliation claims under this law, without yet deciding who wins. If it denies the petition, the Arkansas Supreme Court's ruling against Greer stands, and the school district's win remains in place; denial would not mean the Court agrees the lower court was right, only that its decision remains final.
