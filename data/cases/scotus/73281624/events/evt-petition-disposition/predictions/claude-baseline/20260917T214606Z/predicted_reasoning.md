# Forecast: what the Court will do with No. 25-1130

**Disposition.** The petition will be **denied**, most likely on the order list following the September 28, 2026 conference or after a single relist. P(any grant) = 0.06.

**Relists.** The docket shows **two distributions**: one for the May 14, 2026 conference, interrupted when the Court called for a response on May 11, and one for the September 28, 2026 long conference after the brief in opposition arrived. That second conference has not yet been held. I forecast **no further distribution** as the modal outcome, with a **0.30** chance of at least one more: a call-for-response petition draws a relist noticeably more often than a cold petition, and the long conference produces a wave of relists, but the brief in opposition here is strong enough that the likeliest path is a clean denial on the first post-BIO conference. If it is relisted, expect one relist, not a string of them.

**CVSG.** No call for the views of the Solicitor General is on the docket and I expect **none** (0.05). No federal party is involved and the petition does not frame the issue in a way that invites the government's views. The residual probability reflects that the FTC has a long institutional interest in the state-action doctrine and once recommended a market-participant limit, so a Justice who wants to keep the petition alive has that lever available.

**Question presented, if taken.** If the Court took anything, it would not take the questions as written. It would reformulate around the first question, roughly: *whether a municipality that itself participates in a market and uses its ordinance power to exclude private rivals may claim Parker state-action immunity, or whether the clear-articulation requirement must be satisfied with respect to that self-awarded exclusivity.* The second question (error correction of the Fourth Circuit's application of Hallie and Phoebe Putney) and the third (a "heightened rigor" gloss not raised below) would be left behind, and the fourth issue on abandonment of the constitutional claims would not be touched.

**Summary disposition.** Very unlikely as a route (0.05 conditional on a grant). There is no intervening decision of this Court to GVR against, and the question is doctrinal, not a case for per curiam correction. Any grant would be for plenary review.

**Dissent from denial.** The call for a response after the City waived suggests at least one chambers took an interest, so a short statement respecting denial or a dissent from denial is somewhat more likely than on an ordinary paid petition, but still improbable: **0.10**. If one appears, the most natural author is Justice Gorsuch, who wrote on municipal Parker immunity on the Tenth Circuit, or Justice Thomas; it would flag the open market-participant question and criticize this petition's vehicle rather than call for reversal.

**Conditional merits note.** If the Court granted, the likeliest result would still be affirmance: the South Carolina statute expressly contemplates an exclusive on-beach equipment-rental right tied to lifeguard services, which is far closer to Town of Hallie than to Phoebe Putney's "permission to play." A grant here would more likely be a vehicle for saying something about the market-participant question than for correcting the Fourth Circuit.
