# Forecast

The Court will deny the petition, most likely in an orders release following the September 28, 2026 conference, without a noted dissent or statement respecting denial. This forecasts a disposition, not an endorsement of the Fourth Circuit's reasoning or a merits opinion.

The snapshot records two distributions: April 22 for the May 14 conference and July 29 for the September 28 conference. The intervening May 11 request for a response means these are not persuasive evidence of two completed, fully briefed conferences. I expect no further distribution beyond those two. The probability of at least one additional distribution is 24%; if further consideration occurs, one additional distribution is the likeliest increment rather than a prolonged sequence.

No CVSG is recorded. I expect none, assigning 5% to a new invitation. If one occurs, the likely occasion is the upcoming conference or an early additional consideration, reflecting the federal government's antitrust-enforcement interest rather than a federal party's participation.

The petition's strongest potential review question is QP 1: whether a municipality's own commercial participation limits state-action immunity when it excludes competitors. If review is granted, I expect that question to be framed together with the clear-articulation issue in QP 2: whether authorization of an exclusive private beach-services franchise sufficiently authorizes municipal self-exclusivity. I do not expect the Court to take the additional abandonment issue, or independently adopt QP 3's proposed heightened-rigor formulation without addressing its preservation problem.

Conditional on any grant, plenary briefing and argument are more likely than disposition in the cert order: 12% for the summary route, 88% for plenary review. The identified record supplies no intervening decision calling for a GVR, and recognizing a market-participant limitation would involve doctrinal development rather than an obvious summary correction. If the Court reaches the merits, the anticipated dispute is the boundary between clearly authorized displacement of competition and a municipality's expansion of that authorization for its own commercial benefit.

Conditional on denial, I assign 7% to any noted dissent or statement respecting denial and expect no separate writing. I make no individual Justice vote or authorship forecast.
