## Why below the reached-band rate

The strongest favorable signal is the Court's May 11 request for a response after the City's waiver. A Justice wanted opposition briefing rather than allowing an uncontested petition to proceed. The petition also raises a recognizable boundary question: the City allegedly uses regulatory power to reserve a commercial service to itself, while the expressly stated exclusivity provision addresses contracting with a private beach-safety company. That distinction, and the possibility of broader consequences for municipal enterprises, support a nontrivial grant chance (petition pp. 4–9).

But the two recorded distributions exaggerate the evidence of sustained conference interest. The response request preceded the first scheduled conference, and the next distribution followed the July 10 opposition. September 28 has not yet arrived. I do not treat this as a petition that survived two fully briefed conferences or treat summer elapsed time as a hold for another case. I retain the harness's elevated band while adjusting for what produced the count.

Substantively, the petition identifies no concrete conflicting appellate holding. The Fourth Circuit's reproduced opinion applies its existing Western Star decision and distinguishes mere general corporate authority from express authorization of an exclusive beach-services franchise (petition appendix pp. 8a–10a). Whether that rationale is ultimately correct is different from whether this vehicle warrants review. The City offers a substantial answer: statutory authority to displace competition exists, and the dispute concerns the identity of the authorized exclusive provider rather than permission to displace competition at all (opposition pp. 11–15). The statutory specificity makes a broad clarification less necessary than the petition's general anti-monopoly framing suggests.

The opposition also says the heightened-rigor theory was not raised below (pp. 15–17). I treat that as a respondent's preservation objection, not an independently adjudicated fact. The appendix itself confirms abandonment of the non-antitrust claims (p. 11a); that does not establish abandonment of the preserved core antitrust issue. The opposition's damages limitation is an additional practical constraint, not a conclusion that all useful prospective relief is unavailable. On balance, absence of a demonstrated split, a plausible precedent-based decision below, and uneven presentation outweigh the affirmative response request. My adjustment from 17.2% to 8% is judgmental, not an estimated regression coefficient.
