## What happened

Cherry Grove Beach Gear (CGBG) rents beach chairs and umbrellas near North Myrtle Beach, South Carolina, and once offered on-beach delivery and setup for an extra fee. The City later amended its ordinances to bar anyone but the City itself from setting up rented beach equipment on public beaches, reserving that business to itself to help fund lifeguard services. CGBG sued, claiming the City had illegally monopolized the beach-equipment setup business. The district court and, later, a unanimous panel of the Fourth Circuit ruled for the City, holding that South Carolina law clearly gave coastal cities authority to displace competition in this area, so the City was shielded from federal antitrust liability under a legal doctrine covering government action.

## What the Court is being asked

The case asks how clearly a state must authorize a city to eliminate competition before the city can claim immunity from federal antitrust law. CGBG argues the Fourth Circuit weakened that standard by treating the City's exclusive claim over beach-equipment setup as something state law merely made foreseeable, rather than something the state clearly and specifically chose. CGBG also asks the Court to recognize a new limit on that immunity when a city is itself competing commercially in the market it also regulates, and separately argues the appeals court wrongly treated some of its other claims as abandoned. The City responds that its reservation of the business to itself was a foreseeable and expressly authorized result of state law, and that no lower courts disagree on these points.

## Where it stands

The City waived its right to respond, but the Court asked for a response anyway; after an extension, the City filed a brief opposing review. The petition has been distributed for the Court's conference on September 28, 2026, where the Justices will decide whether to take the case.
