## In brief

The Supreme Court declined to hear a beach-equipment rental company's challenge to a city's claimed immunity from federal antitrust law over its beach rental monopoly.

## What happened

Cherry Grove Beach Gear rented beach chairs and umbrellas in North Myrtle Beach, including delivery and setup on the sand. The City, which also sells and sets up its own beach equipment to help fund lifeguard services, amended its ordinances to bar anyone but the City from delivering and setting up rented equipment on the beach. Cherry Grove sued, claiming the City had illegally monopolized that market. The lower courts ruled for the City, holding that South Carolina law clearly authorized the City to displace competition this way, so the City was immune from federal antitrust claims under a doctrine shielding government action from such lawsuits. The Fourth Circuit Court of Appeals affirmed.

## What the Court is being asked

The case asks whether state law must spell out, more clearly than it did here, that a city may exclude private competitors when the city itself is also selling the service, rather than just regulating others who sell it. It also asks whether the Court should recognize a new limit on government immunity for situations where a city competes directly in a market it also controls through its own rules.

## Where it stands

The petition was distributed for conference twice, and the Court denied it, ending the case at the Supreme Court.

## What each outcome would mean

Cherry Grove Beach Gear, the beach rental company, asked the Court to take up its case and overturn the Fourth Circuit's ruling for the City. Because the Court denied the petition, the Fourth Circuit's decision stands: the City of North Myrtle Beach keeps its exclusive right to provide on-beach rental setup and delivery, and Cherry Grove's antitrust claim does not go forward. The denial does not mean the Supreme Court agreed with the lower court's reasoning, only that it left that ruling in place.
