## In brief

The Supreme Court declined to review a beach-equipment rental company's antitrust challenge to a city's exclusive rental monopoly, leaving the city's win in place.

## What happened

Cherry Grove Beach Gear rented beach chairs and umbrellas in North Myrtle Beach, including delivery and setup on the sand. The City, which also sells and sets up its own beach equipment to help fund lifeguard services, passed an ordinance barring anyone but the City from doing on-beach setup and delivery. Cherry Grove sued, claiming the City illegally monopolized that market in violation of federal antitrust law. The district court and the Fourth Circuit Court of Appeals both ruled for the City, finding it immune from antitrust liability because South Carolina law clearly authorized this kind of exclusive arrangement.

## What the Court is being asked

The case asked the Court to decide whether a legal shield that protects local governments from federal antitrust claims, when they are implementing a state's clearly expressed policy, should still apply when a city is not just regulating a market but directly competing in it and uses its lawmaking power to shut out rivals. It also asked whether the lower court was too quick to find that state law clearly allowed the city's monopoly.

## Where it stands

The Court denied the petition on October 5, 2026, after considering it at two conferences.

## What each outcome would mean

Cherry Grove Beach Gear, the beach-rental company, asked the Court to take up its case after losing below. By denying review, the Court leaves the Fourth Circuit's ruling in place, meaning the City's exclusive control over on-beach equipment delivery and setup stands and Cherry Grove's antitrust claim fails. The denial does not mean the Court agreed the lower court was right; it simply leaves that decision as the final word in this case.
