## In brief

A beach equipment rental company asks the Court to revive its antitrust claim against a city that reserved beach chair and umbrella rentals to itself.

## What happened

Cherry Grove Beach Gear rented beach chairs and umbrellas, including delivery and setup, on beaches in North Myrtle Beach, South Carolina. After a dispute over whether city ordinances allowed on-beach delivery and setup, the city amended its code in 2022 to bar anyone but city employees from doing so. Cherry Grove sued, claiming the city was illegally monopolizing the rental market in violation of federal antitrust law. The district court and the Fourth Circuit both ruled for the city, holding that South Carolina law clearly authorized this kind of anticompetitive conduct, so the city is immune from antitrust liability under a doctrine that shields state-authorized restraints on competition.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked to decide whether the lower court applied the right standard for determining that a state clearly authorized a city to displace competition, whether that standard should be applied more strictly when a city is itself the business competing in the market, and whether the Court should recognize a limit on this immunity when a city acts as a market participant rather than as a regulator.

## Where it stands

The petition has been distributed for conference twice, most recently for the conference of September 28, 2026, after the city filed a brief opposing review. The docket does not show an outcome from either conference.

## What each outcome would mean

Cherry Grove Beach Gear asks the Court to take up the case. If the Court grants the petition, it would hear full arguments and decide the legal questions itself. If it denies the petition, the Fourth Circuit's decision favoring the city stands, and Cherry Grove's antitrust claim remains rejected. A denial would not mean the Court agrees the lower court was right; it would only leave that ruling in place.
