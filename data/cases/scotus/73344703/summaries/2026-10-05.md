## In brief

The Court declined to hear a pet owner's bid to revive emotional-distress and fraud claims against a veterinary clinic over her dog's death.

## What happened

Moneesha Kamani's dog died after being treated and repeatedly discharged by a Washington veterinary clinic, Animal Emergency and Specialty Center, and its veterinarian, Michael A. Stone. Kamani sued, alleging intentional infliction of emotional distress and fraud, pointing to altered medical records and a letter from the clinic threatening criminal and civil action if she posted negative reviews. A trial court dismissed these claims at summary judgment, meaning it found no factual dispute requiring a trial. The Washington Court of Appeals affirmed, and the Washington Supreme Court denied further review.

## What the Court is being asked

Kamani asks the Court to decide two things: first, whether courts may treat threats of criminal charges and lawsuits used to silence someone's social media posts as legally unimportant, despite free-speech protections and state laws shielding public commentary from retaliatory suits; and second, whether a court may dismiss a case before trial by brushing aside evidence in the record and applying legal requirements that do not otherwise exist.

## Where it stands

The petition was distributed for the Court's conference on September 28, 2026, and the Court denied it on October 5, 2026.

## What each outcome would mean

Kamani, who lost in the lower courts, asked the Court to take up her case. The denial means the Court will not review the matter, so the Washington Court of Appeals' decision affirming dismissal of her claims stands. This is not a ruling that the lower court's reasoning was correct; it simply leaves that decision in place. The clinic and Stone, who prevailed below, keep that result, and the case does not proceed further in the courts.
