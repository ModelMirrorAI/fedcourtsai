## Anchor and adjustment

**P(any grant) = 0.01.** The matching `sal-v4` table in `metrics/statpack.md` supplies the baseline band's bracketed **reached** rates. Pooling all displayed Terms strictly before the context's Term 2025, namely 2017–2024, yields approximately **5.12%**, weighted denominator **11,580**. This calculation weights each displayed reached rate by its displayed denominator; the percentages are rounded, so the resulting anchor is approximate. It excludes Terms 2025 and 2026. It is the private-petitioner risk set, not the terminal-baseline rate or a segment-wide rate. I retain the context's Term even though the scheduled conference falls later in the calendar.

I adjust materially downward because the provisioned petition presents an unusually weak federal-review vehicle relative to that risk set:

- **The petition predominantly seeks fact-specific error correction.** QP2 and petition pp. 14–32 dispute the handling of medical records, video, declarations, fraud, and intentional-infliction-of-emotional-distress elements. The petition calls the result a state/federal split but acknowledges that the lower court cited the governing standards correctly (pp. 16–17). Its examples mainly compare applications of tort rules to different factual records, rather than identify opposing holdings on one controlling federal question.
- **The federal speech question is not cleanly presented.** QP1 and pp. 7–14 concern a private veterinary business's alleged threats against online reviews. The petition itself acknowledges the difficulty of direct constitutional remedies against private actors, then argues that First Amendment protection should operate beyond governmental action. The state-action distinction in *Manhattan Community Access Corp. v. Halleck*, 587 U.S. 802 (2019), makes that proposed extension a substantial obstacle, not an already-established entitlement. The petition describes police involvement, but the supplied materials do not establish the government compulsion or other attributable governmental conduct needed to bridge that gap. This is a vehicle concern, not a finding that no federal theory could ever be preserved.
- **State-law and procedural issues dilute the federal question.** The petition seeks broader protection under Washington's UPEPA and revised tort elements, but its account says the anti-SLAPP mechanism is triggered by a lawsuit, whereas the challenged suppression here includes prelitigation threats (pp. 10–14). Its summary-judgment analogy does not establish that federal Rule 56 itself controls this state action. The petition also reports certification of the intentional-tort dismissal under state CR 54(b) (p. 16); without the opinion and full record I cannot establish federal finality or adequate-independent-state-ground issues either way. I treat these as uncertainty, not established jurisdictional defects.
- **No strong attention signal offsets those weaknesses.** One initial distribution, no visible CVSG, and no supplied opposing or amicus brief support neither a developed conflict nor exceptional Court interest. The opinion is described as unpublished in the petition's Opinions Below section, further limiting the apparent precedential stakes.

There is nevertheless a nonzero route to review. QP1 implicates recurring consumer-review suppression, and the alleged record manipulation and threats are serious if substantiated. The petition invokes *Tolan v. Cotton*, 572 U.S. 650 (2014), as an example of summary intervention over mishandled evidence. I verified that precedent through CourtListener: it concerned a federal Fourth Amendment/Section 1983 claim, and the Court summarily vacated and remanded. That differs materially from the state tort posture here. Justice Alito's concurrence, id. at 661, also specifically invokes Rule 10's reluctance to correct factual findings or misapplications of correctly stated rules. That concurrence is supporting context, not a holding that forecloses review. Together these considerations justify 1%, rather than either the full 5.12% risk-set anchor or certainty of denial.
