# Forecast: Kamani v. Stone, No. 25-1285 (evt-petition-disposition)

**Disposition.** The Court will deny the petition, most likely in the order list
following the September 28, 2026 long conference (i.e., on or about October 5,
2026). P(any grant, including GVR or summary reversal) = 0.01.

**Relists.** The docket shows exactly one distribution (July 1, 2026, for the
conference of September 28, 2026) and has never been relisted. I expect no
further distribution: the petition will be considered once and denied. The
residual chance of one more distribution (about 10%) comes almost entirely from
mechanical causes rather than interest — a reschedule off the long conference,
or a call for a response if a single Justice wants to see the respondents'
position before denial. A multi-relist trajectory is very unlikely.

**Call for a response.** No brief in opposition or waiver appears on the
docket, so the petition was distributed unopposed under Rule 15.5. I expect the
Court to deny without calling for a response; a call for a response is the one
plausible mechanism by which this petition would pick up a second distribution,
and I put it well under 10%.

**CVSG.** None. No federal party, no federal statute, no federal-program interest
is implicated; this is a state-law intentional-tort suit between private
parties. P(CVSG issues after prediction time) is effectively zero (0.003).

**Which question, if any.** If the Court took anything, it would not be either
question as written. QP 1 (whether private threats of criminal and civil
liability may be "discounted as a matter of law, notwithstanding the First
Amendment and state anti-SLAPP laws") has no state actor and the court below
did not decide a First Amendment question, so it would have to be reformulated
into something the record does not present. QP 2 (whether a court may grant
summary judgment "by disregarding record evidence") is a request for
error-correction of a state court's application of its own civil rule.

**Summary disposition versus plenary review.** Conditional on the very unlikely
grant, the summary route is somewhat more probable than plenary review
(P ≈ 0.55): the only cognizable theory the petition offers is a Tolan v.
Cotton-style summary reversal for resolving disputed facts against the
non-movant, and the petition itself asks for that. A GVR is not on the table —
no intervening decision of this Court bears on either question. Plenary review
of an unpublished state intermediate-court opinion in an emotional-distress
suit over a pet's death is the least likely grant shape.

**Dissent or statement respecting denial.** None expected (P ≈ 0.01). The
criminal-libel and anti-SLAPP framing is the kind of theme a Justice
occasionally notes, but this vehicle — private defendants, a cease-and-desist
letter, an unpublished opinion that did not reach the constitutional question —
gives no Justice a clean occasion to write.

**Merits, conditionally.** If the Court granted and reached the summary-judgment
question, the likely ground would be that the state court impermissibly weighed
contested evidence; but because a state court applying its own Rule 56 analogue
to state tort claims is not bound by federal summary-judgment doctrine, even
that path is doubtful, which is why the grant probability is as low as it is.
