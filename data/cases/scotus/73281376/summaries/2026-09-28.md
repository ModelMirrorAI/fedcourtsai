## In brief

Two North Carolina counties ask the Court to review a ruling that let a former detainee's lawsuit over inadequate medical care proceed against them.

## What happened

David Ray Gunter, who had a mechanical heart valve requiring daily blood-thinning medication, was detained for fifteen days in Davie County and Stokes County jails in North Carolina in 2012. Both counties had contracted with a private company, Southern Health Partners, to provide medical care to detainees. Gunter alleges he was denied his medication for days and underdosed the rest of the time, and later developed blood clots requiring surgery. He sued the counties and medical providers. A district court dismissed the claims against the counties, but the Fourth Circuit Court of Appeals reversed, allowing the case against the counties to proceed. Gunter has since died, and his estate's administrator, Juliana Swink, has been substituted as respondent.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked whether a local government that contracts out all medical care at its jail to a private company can be held responsible under federal civil rights law when that company's own customs or practices cause a detainee's injuries, or whether that would improperly hold the government liable simply for the company's actions.

## Where it stands

The case has been distributed for conference more than once, most recently for the conference of September 28, 2026, after full briefing including a response, a reply, and supporting briefs. The docket does not yet show any action taken on the petition.

## What each outcome would mean

Davie County and Stokes County, as petitioners, are asking the Court to take up and reverse the Fourth Circuit's ruling. If the Court grants review, it would hear full arguments and decide the legal question itself, without yet determining who wins. If the Court denies review, the Fourth Circuit's decision stands, and Swink's lawsuit against the counties continues in the lower courts alongside her separate claims against the medical provider, which are proceeding regardless of this petition's outcome.
