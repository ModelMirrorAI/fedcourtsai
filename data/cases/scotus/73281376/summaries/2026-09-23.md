## What happened

David Ray Gunter, a pretrial detainee with a mechanical heart valve, allegedly missed doses of his blood-thinning medication while held at jails in Davie County and Stokes County, North Carolina, which had each hired a private company, Southern Health Partners (SHP), to provide inmate medical care. He later suffered blood clots requiring surgery. His estate, now represented by Juliana Swink, sued the counties and SHP. A federal district court ruled for the counties, but the Fourth Circuit Court of Appeals reversed, holding that a jury could find the counties liable because they had delegated final authority over inmate medical care to SHP through their contracts. The counties then asked the Supreme Court to review that ruling.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked whether a local government that contracts out all inmate medical care to a private company can be held responsible under the federal civil-rights law for harm caused by that company's own customs and practices, or whether that would improperly make the government automatically liable just because it hired the company. The counties argue the appeals court's rule wrongly treats hiring a contractor as automatically making it a government policymaker. The estate answers that courts have long held governments cannot escape responsibility by delegating their duty to provide care, and says no lower courts actually disagree on this point.
