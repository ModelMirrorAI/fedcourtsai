## In brief

The Supreme Court declined to hear two counties' challenge to a ruling allowing a lawsuit over a detainee's medical care to proceed against them.

## What happened

David Ray Gunter, a pretrial detainee with a mechanical heart valve, missed doses of a blood-thinning medication while held at jails in Davie County and Stokes County, North Carolina, which had contracted with a private company, Southern Health Partners, to provide medical care. He later developed blood clots requiring surgery. His estate, now represented by Juliana Swink, sued the counties and the medical providers. A federal district court ruled for the defendants, but the Fourth Circuit Court of Appeals reversed, allowing claims against the counties and the medical providers to proceed. The counties asked the Supreme Court to review that ruling.

## What the Court is being asked

The counties asked the Court to decide whether a local government can be held responsible for injuries caused by a private company's practices when the government has contracted out jail medical care entirely, giving the company authority over medical decisions. They argued the appeals court's rule made them automatically liable just for hiring an outside provider. Swink's estate countered that every appeals court to consider the issue agrees a government cannot avoid responsibility by delegating its care obligations this way.

## Where it stands

The Court denied the petition on October 5, 2026, after considering it at more than one conference and receiving full briefing from both sides.

## What each outcome would mean

Davie County and Stokes County, the counties that petitioned, sought to have the Fourth Circuit's ruling reversed. By denying the petition, the Court left that ruling in place, meaning the lawsuit against the counties, along with claims against the medical providers, can continue in the lower courts. The denial does not mean the Supreme Court agreed with the Fourth Circuit's reasoning; it simply leaves that decision standing without the Court deciding the underlying legal question itself.
