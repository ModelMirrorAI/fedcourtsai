## Why 12%, below the 17.2% anchor

The request for a response after waivers is genuine evidence of attention, and the constitutional allocation of responsibility for outsourced jail healthcare is important and recurring. Petition pages 11–24 argue conflict with Monell and the final-policymaker framework; pages 25–32 allege disagreement among the Fourth, Seventh, and Eleventh Circuits versus the Sixth and Fifth. The petitioner also invokes a panel dissent. These features keep the probability materially above a routine unselected petition's odds.

The most important downward adjustment is that the asserted split is less clean than the petition presents. The BIO, pages 6–13, distinguishes delegation of final policymaking authority from mere employment of a medical provider. Its explanations of the Sixth Circuit cases are not independently verified here, but they identify concrete distinctions concerning evidence of delegation, the theory pleaded, and causation. I did independently inspect Robinson v. Midland County, Texas, 80 F.4th 704, 710 n.4 (5th Cir. 2023), through CourtListener opinion 9839933. Footnote 4 leaves the nondelegable-duty issue unresolved because the pleaded claim would fail even under that theory. That supports the BIO's narrower characterization and undermines the petition's description of a square contrary Fifth Circuit holding. I do not claim this single check establishes universal circuit agreement.

The provisioned majority opinion also resists the petition's most categorical framing. Appendix pages 23a–26a identify contracts delegating authority, discuss policy or custom and moving-force causation, and permit further proceedings rather than enter liability. That leaves an arguable problem about when operational discretion becomes policy, but it is not simply a holding that employing a tortfeasor creates liability for every constitutional wrong. A fact-dependent delegation dispute is a weaker review vehicle than a clear disagreement over the governing legal rule.

Vehicle concerns further reduce the odds. The BIO, pages 21–23, argues the county respondeat-superior framing first appeared at rehearing, and points out that claims against the medical providers proceed regardless. I cannot independently settle that preservation objection without the lower-court briefs, so I discount rather than treat it as dispositive. The reviewed majority opinion does confirm a remand for further proceedings. Counties may ultimately avoid liability on remaining issues, making intervention now less attractive than review after a developed final judgment. The asserted dissent raises the chance of scrutiny but its relevant text is beyond the extraction boundary.

Finally, two distributions do not necessarily mean two rounds of merits consideration: the response request predates the first scheduled conference, followed by extended briefing and summer redistribution. I preserve the frozen elevated anchor but temper the substantive signal rather than interpreting this as a strong serial-relist case. Balancing attention and recurring importance against the weaker split and vehicle concerns yields P(any grant) = 0.12 and a denial point prediction.
