The petition presents a classic Section 1983 *Monell* issue regarding whether a municipality can be held liable for the constitutional torts of an independent contractor healthcare provider on a "nondelegable duty" theory or by automatically treating the provider as a final policymaker. The petition identifies a deep circuit split, with the Fourth, Seventh, and Eleventh Circuits recognizing liability based on the mere delegation of healthcare duties, while the Fifth and Sixth Circuits reject such automatic liability. 

The Fourth Circuit opinion below clearly invoked the nondelegable duty theory, declaring that "providing medical care to those incarcerated in county jails is a nondelegable duty of the county, and thus, any independent contractor hired to perform that duty is an agent of the state as a matter of law." Judge Richardson dissented, explicitly flagging that this holding runs afoul of *Monell* by effectively imposing vicarious liability on the counties.
