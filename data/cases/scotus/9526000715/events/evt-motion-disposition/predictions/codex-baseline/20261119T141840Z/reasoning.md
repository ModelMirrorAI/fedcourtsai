I lower the estimate from 7% to 4% primarily because the path to reversal is narrow. The unpublished state decision relied on Odom's participation in later hearings and on *Baxter v. Palmigiano*, which permits some adverse inferences from silence in civil proceedings. Odom makes a serious distinction based on the same prosecutor's use of both proceedings and the limited immunity she received. To assess whether silence cost her custody, however, the Justices would have to examine the evidence underlying three state-law findings. A stay would also affect the child's interest in a timely placement decision. The principal uncertainty is what state action was imminent on November 16 and how narrowly Odom asked the Court to pause it. The public petition predates the application and cannot settle either point.
