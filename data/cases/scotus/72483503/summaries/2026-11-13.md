## In brief

Parker-Hannifin asks the Court to review whether a retirement-plan lawsuit alleging imprudent investment choices must point to a comparable fund as evidence.

## What happened

Five employees who participate in Parker-Hannifin's retirement savings plan sued the company and related plan committees, claiming they violated federal pension law by keeping a set of target-date retirement funds that underperformed other funds and by using higher-cost share classes when cheaper, identical versions were available. A district court dismissed the lawsuit, ruling the employees had not identified a comparable fund that could show the company acted imprudently. A divided panel of the Sixth Circuit reversed, holding that such a comparison is not always required and that, in any event, the employees had pointed to one. One judge dissented, saying the ruling conflicts with other appeals courts.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked whether an employee who claims a retirement plan manager imprudently kept a poor-performing investment must show that the investment used for comparison shared similar goals, risks, and strategies with the challenged fund, or whether looser comparisons can support such a claim.

## Where it stands

The petition was distributed for conference multiple times, and the Court invited the Solicitor General to weigh in; the government's brief, filed in December 2025, recommended granting review. Both sides then filed supplemental briefs, and the case was distributed again for a conference in January 2026, with no decision yet recorded.

## What each outcome would mean

Parker-Hannifin, the company and plan committees sued by the employees, is asking the Court to take up the case and reverse the lower court's decision. If the Court grants review and later rules for Parker-Hannifin, the employees' claim about the underperforming funds would likely be dismissed again, requiring more specific comparisons in future ERISA suits. If the Court rules for the employees instead, the Sixth Circuit's decision allowing the claim to proceed would stand. If the Court denies review, the Sixth Circuit's ruling permitting the lawsuit to continue remains in effect, and the case returns to the lower courts for further proceedings, including on a separate fee-related claim that was not part of this petition.
