The Supreme Court is slightly more likely than not, a little better than even, to agree to hear this case about what workers must allege to sue their employer over poorly performing retirement-plan investments. The lower court's own dissenting judge said the ruling conflicts with other appeals courts, and the Justices asked the federal government's top appellate lawyer, the Solicitor General, for advice, which they do only for petitions they take seriously. The case's early procedural stage could still lead the Court to decline.
