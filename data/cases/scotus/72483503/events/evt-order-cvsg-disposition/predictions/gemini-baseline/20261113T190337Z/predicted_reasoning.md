# Predicted Court Action — *Parker-Hannifin Corp. v. Johnson* (No. 24-1030)

## Disposition and Procedural Trajectory

The Court will **deny** the petition for a writ of certiorari after receiving the invited brief of the Solicitor General, with Justice Alito taking no part in the consideration or decision of the petition.

1. **Further Conference Distributions (`relist-increment`)**: The docket currently records **two** distributions (April 16, 2025 for the May 2, 2025 Conference, followed by a call for a response, and June 10, 2025 for the June 26, 2025 Conference, which produced the June 30, 2025 CVSG order). Barring a pre-distribution Rule 46 settlement dismissal, the petition will be **distributed at least once more** once the Solicitor General files the brief for the United States and the parties submit supplemental briefs. Expect **one post-CVSG distribution** (bringing the terminal distribution count to three) if the Court denies review in accordance with a vehicle-based denial recommendation, or **two post-CVSG distributions** (one post-brief conference plus one relist, bringing the terminal count to four) if the Solicitor General recommends review or if a Justice circulates a separate statement.
2. **Solicitor General Invitation (`cvsg-increment`)**: The docket already records a CVSG order entered on June 30, 2025; no additional CVSG order will issue.
3. **Why Denial Prevails Over Plenary Review**: Although the petition implicates serious tension between the Sixth Circuit's divided opinion below and decisions of the Eighth, Ninth, and Tenth Circuits over "meaningful benchmark" pleading under ERISA, three vehicle and institutional obstacles will lead the eight-member Court to pass on review here and await a cleaner vehicle:
   - **Eight-Justice Bench Without Justice Alito**: Justice Alito is recused, leaving eight participating Justices on a closely contested ERISA Rule 12(b)(6) plausibility question where a 4–4 deadlock is a realistic hazard and where cleaner nine-Justice vehicles arise regularly across the circuits.
   - **Alternative Fact-Bound Grounds Below**: The Sixth Circuit panel majority did not rest solely on its broad statement that a meaningful benchmark is unnecessary; it held in the alternative that the complaint pleaded a meaningful benchmark because the challenged Northern Trust Focus Funds were passive index-tracking target-date funds designed to meet industry-recognized benchmarks, reinforced by allegations of an abrupt $850 million portfolio turnover.
   - **Surviving Overlapping Claim on Remand**: Petitioners did not seek review of the Sixth Circuit's revival of respondents' share-class imprudence claim involving the very same Northern Trust Focus Funds, meaning discovery into petitioners' monitoring of those funds will proceed in the district court regardless of how this petition is resolved.

## Question Presented and Route Conditional on a Grant

- **Question Presented**: If the Court instead grants certiorari, it will take the petition's **single question presented as written**—whether pleading an imprudent-investment claim under ERISA based on performance comparisons requires allegations showing that the benchmark is a sound basis for comparison for that investment—without reformulation or addition.
- **Plenary Review vs. Summary Disposition (`summary-disposition-route`)**: Conditional on a grant, the Court will overwhelmingly set the case for **plenary briefing and oral argument** rather than disposing of it summarily in the cert order. No pending Supreme Court merits case addresses 29 U.S.C. § 1104(a)(1)(B) defined-contribution investment-benchmark pleading to anchor a grant, vacate, and remand (GVR), and resolving the methodological disagreement between the Sixth Circuit and the Eighth, Ninth, and Tenth Circuits would call for full merits treatment rather than a per curiam reversal.
- **Conditional Merits Ground**: If granted and argued without deadlocking 4–4, the Court would likely vacate and remand, holding under *Fifth Third Bancorp v. Dudenhoeffer*, 573 U.S. 409 (2014), and *Hughes v. Northwestern University*, 595 U.S. 170 (2022), that while ERISA does not require a comparator for every imprudence theory, a plaintiff who relies inferentially on relative investment underperformance to plead a flawed fiduciary process must allege facts showing that the chosen index or comparator shares materially similar investment objectives and risk characteristics, so that courts give due regard to a fiduciary's reasonable choice among conservative versus aggressive target-date strategies.

## Separate Writings on the Disposition Order (`dissent-from-denial`)

Conditional on a denial of certiorari, the Court will most likely issue an **unsigned one-line denial order with no separate writing** (noting only Justice Alito's non-participation). There is a modest (~9%) chance of a short separate writing—most plausibly a dissent from denial by Justice Thomas (potentially joined by Justice Gorsuch) or a statement respecting the denial of certiorari by Justice Kavanaugh highlighting the recurring circuit conflict over ERISA benchmark pleading while noting the vehicle constraints that counsel awaiting a future case.
