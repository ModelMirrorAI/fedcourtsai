# Forecast of the Court's treatment

The modal outcome is denial of the petition. I assign 46% to some grant-family disposition and 54% to no grant, including a small chance of dismissal or withdrawal. If denied, I expect an unexplained order, not a majority opinion endorsing the Sixth Circuit's reasoning.

## What happens after the recorded CVSG

The baseline contains **two distributions**, April 16 and June 10, 2025, and the **June 30, 2025 CVSG**. The first distribution was followed by a request for an opposition before the originally scheduled conference. I do not describe both distributions as completed conference consideration.

I expect the government to file the invited brief and the petition to return to conference. The probability of at least one additional distribution is **0.97**. My modal path is one additional distribution after the government's brief, with a second additional distribution a meaningful possibility. This is an increment from two, not a prediction of one or two distributions in total. From the baseline vantage, the expected waiting period is months rather than days; I expect resolution in the following Term after government briefing, not immediate disposal before the invitation has been answered.

No new CVSG is forecast: the Court has already requested the government's views. The `cvsg-increment` entry is zero and vacuous in this posture. I make no prediction about a recommendation supposedly contained in a brief that is not in the record.

## Question and route if review is granted

If the Court grants plenary review, I expect it to take the petition's single question substantially as framed: whether a complaint using comparative returns to allege imprudent ERISA investing must allege facts showing that its benchmark is a sound comparator. I do not expect review of the separate share-class claim or a general rule requiring a comparator in every conceivable fiduciary-duty suit.

Conditional on a grant, I assign **0.88 to plenary review** and **0.12 to a summary route**. No identified intervening controlling decision in the baseline makes a GVR the natural path. A grant would more likely seek a uniform pleading standard through briefing and argument than summarily decide this contested application of the standard. Any discussion of the eventual merits is conditional: if granted, the Court would likely distinguish return-based inferences from direct allegations of deficient fiduciary process rather than impose an invariably necessary comparator element on all ERISA claims.

## Separate writing

Conditional on denial, I assign **0.10** to at least one noted dissent or statement respecting denial. The modal denial has none. If a writing appears, Thomas or Gorsuch is a more plausible author than the other participating Justices, but no named author is the modal prediction. I expect Justice Alito to remain nonparticipating, consistently with the invitation order. The `order-writers` block gives independent unconditional marginals, includes every seated Justice, and does not predict a public tally of unannounced cert votes.
