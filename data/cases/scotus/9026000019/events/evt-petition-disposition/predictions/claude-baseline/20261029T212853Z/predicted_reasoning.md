# Predicted course — Nguyen v. Louisiana, No. 26-19

**Posture the forecast starts from.** The petition has been distributed once
(for the 28 September 2026 conference). Louisiana waived a response; the Court
then **requested a response** on 9 September 2026, and the Clerk extended the
deadline to 9 November 2026. No CVSG; no relist yet beyond the single
distribution.

**Disposition: denied.** I expect the Court to deny certiorari once the State's
response is in, most likely at a December 2026 or early January 2027
conference.

**Relisting.** The petition will almost certainly be **distributed again** once
the response is filed (or the time to file runs out) — the request for a
response forces a further distribution. Beyond that one redistribution, I
expect no further relist: a denial at the first conference after the
response is the likeliest path. Two or more additional distributions would
signal a Justice writing on the denial or serious grant interest.

**CVSG.** None. This is a state criminal prosecution with no federal party and
no federal regulatory interest the Court needs the Solicitor General's views
on; the federal firearm disability (18 U.S.C. § 922(g)(9)) is a collateral
consequence, not a federal claim in the case.

**Which question, if granted.** The Court would take the question presented
essentially as reformulated in general terms: whether the collateral loss of
firearm rights under federal law (and comparable state disabilities) renders
an offense punishable by no more than six months "serious" for Sixth Amendment
purposes under *Blanton v. City of North Las Vegas* — not the petition's
fact-laden list of penalties. A grant could also invite reconsideration of
the petty-offense exception itself.

**Summary disposition.** Unlikely. No intervening decision of this Court bears
on the question, so a GVR has no hook; a summary reversal would mean the Court
declaring a new rule about *Blanton* without briefing, which it does not do.
If granted, plenary review is far likelier.

**Separate writing on denial.** Possible but less likely than not. The most
plausible author is Justice Gorsuch, who has questioned the historical footing
of the petty-offense exception; Justice Thomas or Justice Jackson are distant
possibilities. A vehicle problem — the Louisiana appellate court affirmed the
conviction but remanded for resentencing, which invites a § 1257 finality
objection, and the pretrial ruling was treated as law of the case — makes a
writing less likely than it would be on a clean vehicle.
