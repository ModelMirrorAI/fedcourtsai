# Predicted Reasoning: *Allison Nguyen v. Louisiana* (No. 26-19)

The Supreme Court will **deny** the petition for a writ of certiorari after the State of Louisiana files its requested Brief in Opposition.

## Procedural Path: Further Distributions and CVSG

- **Current distribution state and further distributions (`relist-increment`):** The docket currently records **one distribution** (`Sep 15 2026`, for the Conference of October 9, 2026), followed on October 9, 2026 by a **Call for a Response** (`Response Requested`, due November 9, 2026). Because the Court requested a response after the initial distribution, the Clerk will redistribute the petition for a subsequent conference once Louisiana's Brief in Opposition is filed (or the response time expires). Accordingly, the petition is almost certain (`P = 0.97`) to be distributed at least once more, raising the terminal distribution count from 1 to at least 2. After that post-opposition conference, the most likely path is an immediate denial without further conference relists, though there is a modest chance (~15%) of one additional relist if a Justice circulates a separate statement or dissent from denial.
- **Call for the Views of the Solicitor General (`cvsg-increment`):** The Court will **not** call for the views of the Solicitor General (`P = 0.002`). Although the petition invokes the collateral consequence of lifetime federal firearm disarmament under 18 U.S.C. § 922(g)(9), this is a state criminal prosecution arising from the Louisiana courts over the scope of the Sixth Amendment right to a jury trial, a posture in which the Court virtually never invites the Solicitor General to file an amicus brief.

## Disposition Route and Scope of Review (If Granted)

- **Question presented if granted:** The petition presents a single question: whether a defendant has a Sixth Amendment right to a jury trial for a misdemeanor domestic violence offense carrying a maximum sentence of six months in jail and a $1,000 fine where conviction also triggers lifetime loss of Second Amendment firearm rights under 18 U.S.C. § 922(g)(9), a 10-year firearm prohibition under state law (La. R.S. 14:95.10), and permanent ineligibility for expungement (La. C.Cr.P. art. 977(C)(2)). If the Court were to grant review, it would take the single question presented, likely focusing on whether statutory firearm prohibitions that burden an individual's Second Amendment right to keep and bear arms rebut the *Blanton v. City of North Las Vegas*, 489 U.S. 538 (1989), presumption that offenses punishable by six months or less of incarceration are "petty."
- **Plenary review vs. summary disposition (`summary-disposition-route`):** Conditional on a grant, the Court would set the case for full merits briefing and oral argument rather than disposing of it summarily in the cert order (`P(summary disposition | grant) = 0.08`). No pending Supreme Court merits case presents the *Blanton* collateral-consequences issue to support a hold-and-GVR, and holding that firearm prohibitions transform a six-month misdemeanor into a "serious" offense requiring a jury trial would be a significant doctrinal departure from *Blanton* and lower-court consensus that the Court would not undertake by summary reversal.
- **Conditional merits ground:** If granted and decided on the merits, the central doctrinal question would be whether *Blanton*'s inquiry looks strictly to penalties imposed by the enacting state legislature (which would exclude 18 U.S.C. § 922(g)(9) and focus solely on Louisiana's 10-year firearm ban and expungement bar) and whether a 10-year suspension of state firearm possession rights is severe enough to overcome the six-month incarceration threshold.

## Separate Writings on Denial (`dissent-from-denial`)

- Conditional on denial, the likeliest outcome is an unsigned summary denial order without comment, but there is a non-trivial chance (`P = 0.06`) that a Justice—most plausibly Justice Thomas or Justice Gorsuch, potentially joined by Justice Alito—authors a dissent from denial or a statement respecting the denial of certiorari. The October 9, 2026 Call for Response in a solo-litigated state misdemeanor case without amicus support indicates that at least one Chambers flagged the tension between *Blanton*'s petty-offense presumption and permanent Second Amendment disarmament resulting from a non-jury bench trial.
