# Forecast of the Court's action

The Court will most likely deny this petition after receiving Louisiana's response, without a written explanation or separate statement. The probability of any grant, including a summary disposition, is 9%.

The snapshot records **one distribution**, for the September 28, 2026 conference, followed by a September 9 response request and an extension of Louisiana's response deadline to November 9. The Court will almost certainly distribute the petition again after the response and any reply: the probability of at least one additional distribution is 98%. The modal path is one further distribution and denial, probably in December 2026 or January 2027 if the current response deadline holds. That additional distribution would satisfy the increment claim even if it is only rescheduling after the response request, rather than a substantive relist after conference consideration. Several additional conferences are less likely.

No CVSG is recorded. The Court is unlikely to seek the Solicitor General's views; the probability of a new invitation is 1.5%. Although the petition invokes federal firearm consequences, the central issue is a criminal defendant's jury-trial entitlement in a state prosecution, which the Court can assess through the parties' briefing.

If the Court grants review, it will more likely order full briefing and argument than decide the case summarily. Conditional on any grant, the probability of a cert-order disposition is 10%. The question likely taken would be a focused version of the petition's sole question: whether the firearm disabilities accompanying a misdemeanor domestic-abuse conviction, considered together with a six-month maximum jail term and other statutory penalties, make the offense serious enough to require a jury under the Sixth Amendment. The Court would not need to decide a standalone challenge to the constitutionality of firearm prohibitions or revisit the factual finding of guilt.

If granted for plenary review, the likely analytical framework is Blanton's assessment of the aggregate statutory penalties, rather than a categorical rule that a six-month maximum always defeats the jury right. But the petition's procedural history makes denial more likely than an opinion resolving that issue. No provisioned intervening decision supplies a natural GVR ground, and the choice between competing applications of the additional-penalties test is not an obvious summary-reversal case.

Conditional on denial, the probability of any noted dissent or statement respecting denial is 8%; the modal forecast remains no writing. If a Justice does write, Thomas or Gorsuch is a somewhat more plausible author in my forecast than the other individual Justices, given the combination of jury rights and firearm consequences. Those are low-probability authorship judgments, not predictions of a recorded vote lineup.
