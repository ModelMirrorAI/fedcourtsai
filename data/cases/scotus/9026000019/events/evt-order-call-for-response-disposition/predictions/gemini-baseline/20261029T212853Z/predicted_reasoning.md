## Question presented and route if granted

- **Question presented if granted:** Only a single question presented appears in the petition—whether a defendant has a Sixth Amendment right to a jury trial in a misdemeanor domestic violence prosecution carrying up to six months in jail, a $1,000 fine, federal and state firearm prohibitions, and ineligibility for expungement. If the Court were to grant certiorari, it would take that single question, likely focusing on whether a state-enacted firearm disability implicates a fundamental constitutional right sufficient to overcome *Blanton*'s six-month petty-offense presumption.
- **Summary disposition route (`summary-disposition-route` = 0.08):** Conditional on a grant, the Court would set the case for plenary briefing and oral argument rather than disposing of it summarily. Resolving the tension between *Andersen* and decisions such as *State v. Acevedo*, 307 So. 3d 322 (La. App. 5 Cir. 2020), and *United States v. Chavez*, 204 F.3d 1305 (11th Cir. 2000), would require full merits consideration of *Blanton*'s collateral-consequences framework rather than a summary reversal or GVR.
