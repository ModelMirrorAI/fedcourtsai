# Forecast of the Court's next actions

The Court will most likely **deny certiorari** after receiving Louisiana's requested response and allowing the ordinary reply period. The probability of any grant-family disposition is **12%**.

The baseline shows **one distribution**, for the September 28, 2026 conference, and a September 9 response request. I expect **one further distribution** after the response process, with a decision on that next conference cycle or after one additional genuine relist. I assign **98%** to at least one further distribution. That forecast mainly describes the ordinary consequence of the response request, not a considered relist signal. From the provisioned vantage, the likely decision window is late October through December 2026; extensions or additional relists could move it later. This is a timing forecast, not a claim that later entries have been observed.

I expect **no CVSG**; the chance of a new invitation to the Solicitor General is **1.5%**. The Court will ordinarily obtain the state respondent's position rather than seek federal views on this state misdemeanor's jury-trial consequences.

**If review is granted**, the likely question is a narrowed version of the petition's sole question: whether the firearm restrictions accompanying a domestic-abuse misdemeanor punishable by no more than six months in jail overcome Blanton's presumption that the offense is petty for Sixth Amendment jury-trial purposes. The Court would be less likely to take an expansive question resting on an unqualified permanent loss of every firearm right or to decide the petitioner's guilt. If it reaches the merits, it would likely focus on the objective severity and source of the additional statutory penalties, not on the ordinary-language meaning of serious.

Conditional on any grant, I assign **25%** to a summary disposition in the cert order, versus **75%** to plenary review. A summary vacatur to reconsider the jury demand under Blanton is possible; an immediate merits reversal is less natural where statutory consequences and procedural preservation require development. No identified intervening precedent supplies a routine GVR trigger.

Conditional on denial, I assign **14%** to a noted dissent or statement respecting denial. **No separate writing** is the principal forecast. If there is one, Thomas or Gorsuch is the most plausible writer because of the firearms and jury-right dimensions. These are contextual authorship expectations, not a prediction of observable individual cert votes; no vote lineup is supplied.
