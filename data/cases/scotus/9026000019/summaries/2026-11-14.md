## In brief

A woman convicted of misdemeanor domestic abuse battery asks the Court to rule that she was entitled to a jury trial rather than a bench trial.

## What happened

Allison Nguyen was convicted after a bench trial of misdemeanor domestic abuse battery, stemming from an incident in which she struck her boyfriend with her phone. Louisiana law gives defendants a jury trial only for crimes punishable by more than six months imprisonment; her charge carried a maximum of six months, so the trial court denied her request for a jury. The Louisiana Fifth Circuit Court of Appeal and the Louisiana Supreme Court both left that ruling in place, though the appeals court sent the case back only to fix a sentencing detail unrelated to the jury issue.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked whether a crime that carries no more than six months in jail still counts as serious enough to require a jury trial, given that a conviction also strips the defendant of her right to possess a firearm for years, carries a fine, and cannot later be cleared from her record.

## Where it stands

The petition has been fully briefed: Louisiana waived its initial right to respond, the Court later asked for a response anyway, and both a brief in opposition and a reply have been filed. The case has not yet been scheduled for a conference as shown by the docket.

## What each outcome would mean

Nguyen is asking the Court to take up her case and overturn the ruling that denied her a jury trial. If the Court grants review, it would hear full arguments and decide whether her bench trial satisfied the Constitution. If it denies review, the Louisiana courts' rulings stand, leaving her conviction and bench-trial procedure in place. A denial would not mean the Court agrees with those rulings, only that it leaves them undisturbed.
