## In brief

A woman convicted of misdemeanor domestic abuse battery in Louisiana asks the Court to decide whether she was entitled to a jury trial.

## What happened

Allison Nguyen was charged with domestic abuse battery after striking her boyfriend with her phone at a gym. She asked for a jury trial, arguing the offense was serious because a conviction would cost her the right to own a firearm. The trial court denied a jury and convicted her in a bench trial; she was sentenced to six months in parish prison. The Louisiana Fifth Circuit Court of Appeal affirmed her conviction, though it sent the case back only to fix a sentencing detail unrelated to the jury issue. The Louisiana Supreme Court denied further review.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked whether a crime punishable by no more than six months in jail must still be tried before a jury, rather than a judge alone, when a conviction also carries consequences such as a long-term loss of firearm rights and the inability to clear the conviction from one's record.

## Where it stands

The petition has been fully briefed: Louisiana filed a brief opposing review after the Court asked for a response, and Nguyen filed a reply. The case is awaiting the Court's decision on whether to take it up.

## What each outcome would mean

Nguyen, who lost in the lower courts, is asking the Court to take up her case and rule that she was entitled to a jury trial. If the Court grants review, it would hear full arguments on the jury-trial question and decide it, deciding nothing yet about who is right. If it denies review, the Fifth Circuit's decision affirming her conviction without a jury trial stands, and Louisiana's position prevails.
