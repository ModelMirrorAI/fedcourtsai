## In brief

A woman convicted of misdemeanor domestic abuse battery asks the Court to decide whether she was entitled to a jury trial.

## What happened

Allison Nguyen was charged with domestic abuse battery after an incident at a gym involving her boyfriend. Louisiana law makes this offense punishable by up to six months in jail, and Nguyen requested a jury trial, arguing the consequences of conviction were severe enough to require one. The trial court denied the request and convicted her after a bench trial, sentencing her to six months. The Louisiana court of appeal affirmed, holding that because the maximum sentence is six months, no jury trial was required, and the Louisiana Supreme Court denied further review.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked whether a person charged with this kind of offense has a right to have a jury, rather than a judge alone, decide guilt or innocence, given that a conviction can mean months in jail, a fine, the loss of gun rights, and a record that can never be cleared.

## Where it stands

Louisiana waived its right to respond to the petition, but the Court later asked for a response, which is now due after an extension granted by the Court. The petition has not yet been scheduled for a conference.

## What each outcome would mean

Nguyen, who lost in the Louisiana courts, is asking the Supreme Court to take up her case. If the Court grants the petition, it would hear full arguments and decide whether she had a right to a jury trial, potentially leading to a new trial for her. If the Court denies the petition, the Louisiana courts' rulings stand, leaving her conviction and sentence in place without a decision from the Supreme Court on the jury-trial question.
