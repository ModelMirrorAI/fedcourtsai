## In brief

A doctor convicted of illegally prescribing opioids asks the Court to review how appeals courts decide whether a flawed jury instruction on his intent was harmless.

## What happened

Frank Parasmo, a New York physician, was convicted at trial on 32 counts of unlawfully distributing opioids. After his conviction, the Supreme Court ruled in another case that juries must find a doctor subjectively knew his prescriptions were unauthorized, not merely that they were objectively unreasonable. Parasmo argued the jury in his case had been wrongly instructed to use the objective standard. The Second Circuit agreed the instruction was flawed but, over a dissent, ruled the error was harmless because it found the government's evidence of guilt overwhelming, and affirmed his conviction.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked whether an appeals court may uphold a conviction despite a flawed jury instruction on a disputed element of the crime by weighing the strength of the government's evidence itself, or whether it must instead ask only whether the defendant's evidence could rationally have led a jury to acquit, without an appellate court weighing the evidence as a jury would.

## Where it stands

The petition was distributed for conference, and the government later waived its right to respond before the Court asked for a response anyway, with the response now due. Several amicus briefs supporting Parasmo have been filed, and the case awaits further action on whether the Court will hear it.

## What each outcome would mean

Parasmo is asking the Court to take up his case and reverse the Second Circuit's decision. If the Court grants review, it would hear full arguments and decide which legal standard applies, without yet deciding who wins. If it denies review, the Second Circuit's decision stands, leaving Parasmo's conviction and the harmless-error finding in place.
