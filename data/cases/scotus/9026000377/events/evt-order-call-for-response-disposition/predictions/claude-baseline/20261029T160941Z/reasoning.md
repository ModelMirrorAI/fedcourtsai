# Why 0.09

**Inputs read.** The snapshot `record/snapshots/2026-10-05.json`, the context
(`band: baseline`, sal-v4, `distribution_count: 1`, forward, cutoff
2026-10-06), and the provisioned `questions-presented.txt` and `petition.txt`
(44 pp., not truncated). I did not read the 152-page appendix in full. It holds
the Second Circuit opinions, and the petition quotes Judge Robinson's dissent
heavily. No brief in opposition exists yet, because the United States waived
its response.

**Anchor.** The petitioner is private (Frank Parasmo v. United States), so the
band and class floor is `baseline`. Its bracketed `reached` rate in the
statpack's sal-v4 segment table, over prior Terms 2017–2025, runs 4.0–5.9% (a
pooled figure of about 5%). The OT2026 row (1.8%, n=169) is the case's own
Term and still early, so I set it aside. The CA2 originating-circuit row has a
grant family of about 5% across all petitions.

**Adjustments up.**
- **The Court called for a response after the government waived.** This is
  the moment's defining signal: at least one chambers thought the petition
  deserved a full look. No committed table conditions on it. My judgement,
  from general knowledge of the Court's practice, is that grant rates after a
  call for response run several times the paid-docket rate but stay well
  under 20%.
- **The petition is strong on paper.** Elite counsel (Milbank). A divided
  panel, with a dissent (Robinson, J.) that itself describes a circuit split.
  The petition claims a 4–1 split over harmless error for *Ruan* instructional
  errors (*Kahn*, CA10; *Smithers*, CA4; *Qureshi*, CA5; *Duldulao*, CA11). It
  ties this to wider disagreement over applying *Neder* to contested elements
  and to a Sixth Amendment theme that appeals to several Justices. The jury's
  split verdict makes the vehicle cleaner.

**Adjustments down.**
- The Court rarely grants case-specific harmlessness disputes. The government
  will plausibly describe the "split" as different records under one *Neder*
  standard.
- The *Ruan* error cohort, trials held before June 2022, is shrinking. That
  weakens the issue's recurring importance.
- I have not seen the government's response. My number prices a credible
  but ordinary opposition.

Net, I put the grant family at 0.09. The `summary-disposition-route` estimate
(0.12, conditional on a grant) reflects plenary review as the expected route,
with a small chance of a confession of error followed by a GVR. The
`dissent-from-denial` estimate (0.07) reflects that criminal-procedure denials
sometimes draw writing from Gorsuch, Jackson or Sotomayor, but rarely on
harmless-error questions. The `cvsg-increment` estimate is near zero because
the United States is the respondent. The `relist-increment` estimate is near
certain, and the harness masks it on this moment.

**Where to discount me.** The effect of a call for response is my judgement,
not a published rate. I took the split's strength from the petition alone,
with no opposing brief to test it. Retrieval was one statpack read and two
corpus queries. Neither query returned a useful analogue, because the
citations filter is sparse.
