# Forecast of the Court's disposition

The Court will most likely **deny the petition**, leaving the Second
Circuit's judgment in place, without a written explanation. The request for a
response will lead to further consideration, but will not by itself produce
the four votes needed for review. The strongest obstacle to review will be
the characterization of this dispute as fact-specific harmless-error review
rather than a clean disagreement about the governing legal standard.

The supplied docket shows **one distribution**, for a conference not yet held
as of the October 5 snapshot, and **no CVSG**. I expect **at least one further
distribution** after the response deadline or the response's filing. The
central forecast is one additional distribution followed by denial, with
zero or one additional post-conference relist rather than a long sequence.
The probability of any further distribution is **98%**; that does not mean a
98% probability of a substantive relist after consideration at a conference.
Disposition is most likely in late 2026 or early 2027, allowing for response
extensions and ordinary scheduling. The Court will not separately call for
the Solicitor General's views: the United States is already the respondent.
The probability of a new CVSG is **0.2%**.

If the Court grants review, it will most likely take a narrowed form of the
petition's question: whether an appellate court may deem erroneous
instructions on a disputed subjective-intent element harmless when the
defendant introduced evidence capable of supporting a contrary finding. The
Ruan physician-prosecution setting will provide the concrete application;
the Court need not reconsider whether all omitted-element errors are
structural or overrule Neder. If granted, the likely corrective ground is
that appellate assessment of the prosecution's strength cannot replace the
required inquiry into whether a properly instructed jury could rationally
credit the defense on the disputed element.

**Conditional on a grant**, plenary review with briefing and argument is more
likely than a disposition in the certiorari order itself: **78% plenary, 22%
summary route**. A summary reversal or vacatur for reconsideration under
existing principles is the alternative, not a routine GVR based on an
identified new intervening decision.

**Conditional on denial**, the probability of a noted dissent or statement
respecting denial is **10%**. The central forecast is no separate writing;
if one appears, Sotomayor or Gorsuch is a more plausible author than most
other Justices, with Jackson another possibility. No public cert-vote lineup
or merits-opinion author is forecast.
