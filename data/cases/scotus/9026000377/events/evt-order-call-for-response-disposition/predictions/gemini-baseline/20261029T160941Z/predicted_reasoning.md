## Question Presented and Conditional Merits Ground

If the Court grants certiorari (`P = 0.11`), it will take the petition's single Question Presented as framed: whether, under *Neder v. United States*, 527 U.S. 1 (1999), and the Sixth Amendment, an appellate court may find an erroneous jury instruction on an element of the offense that the defendant plausibly contested at trial harmless based on the appellate court's assessment of the strength of the government's evidence.

Conditionally, if the Court grants plenary review, it would likely vacate the Second Circuit's judgment and clarify that under *Neder*, 527 U.S. at 19, and *Erlinger v. United States*, 602 U.S. 821, 842 (2024), when a jury instruction misdescribes the sole contested mens rea element of an offense—here, subjective knowledge or intent under *Ruan v. United States*, 597 U.S. 450 (2022)—and the defendant adduced affirmative trial evidence from which a rational jury could acquit, a reviewing court may not affirm the conviction merely by weighing the government's circumstantial evidence against the defense case.

## Summary Disposition vs. Plenary Review (`summary-disposition-route`)

Conditional on a grant, plenary review with full merits briefing and oral argument is the far likelier route (`85%`), with a smaller conditional probability (`P = 0.15`) of summary disposition in the certiorari order itself (such as a summary vacatur or reversal). No pending merits case on the OT2026 docket presents this *Neder* harmless-error question, so a hold-and-GVR for an intervening decision is unlikely, and the Solicitor General is unlikely to confess error given the Second Circuit majority's detailed reliance on patient-specific red flags.

## Separate Writings on Denial (`dissent-from-denial` and `order-writers`)

Conditional on the Court denying certiorari, there is an above-average probability (`P = 0.08`) that one or more Justices will author a dissent from the denial of certiorari or a statement respecting denial. The most likely authors are Justice Gorsuch (`P = 0.035`), who authored *Erlinger v. United States* and has repeatedly criticized expansive judicial factfinding under *Neder* as incompatible with the Sixth Amendment jury-trial guarantee; Justice Sotomayor (`P = 0.025`), Circuit Justice for the Second Circuit, who frequently writes separately when lower courts expand harmless-error doctrine in criminal appeals; and Justice Jackson (`P = 0.020`), who has shown sustained interest in Sixth Amendment and mens rea protections.
