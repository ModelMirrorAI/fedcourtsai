## Anchor and adjustment

The committed `metrics/statpack.md` supplies the appropriate `sal-v4` baseline risk set. Pooling **all nine displayed prior Terms, 2017-2025**, with exact `prefix_est_grant_rate` and `prefix_weighted_resolved` values from `metrics/statpack.json`, gives **644 grant-family cases / 12,883 weighted resolved cases = 4.9988%**. I exclude Term 2026. This is the bracketed reached-band rate, not the much lower rate for petitions that terminate in baseline. These are figures from the committed pack available to this run, not a fresh live-corpus measurement; I did not query or refresh the corpus.

The pack's modern-cert table supplies a low whole-docket cross-check, but the paid selected-segment risk set is the primary anchor. Its Second Circuit cut is not an independent multiplier. The paid relist cuts have grant-family shares of approximately 1.7%, 13.4%, 40.6%, and 36.8% for terminal relist buckets zero, one, two, and three-plus. The CVSG cut is approximately 34.8% for CVSG cases versus 6.2% without one. Those are terminal-state mixtures, not transition probabilities from today's state. I do not use either as a direct forecast of another distribution or confuse the government's requested response with an invitation for amicus views.

I raise the approximately 5% starting point to **24% P(any grant)** for three substantive reasons:

- **Affirmative attention:** the Court requested a response after a waiver. This is a stronger signal than an ordinary first distribution, although it does not establish that four Justices favor review. It is not captured by changing the frozen baseline band, which I leave alone.
- **A concrete, developed disagreement:** the petition identifies Ruan applications in the Fourth, Fifth, Tenth, and Eleventh Circuits, and the dissent below independently engages the purported conflict. Petition pp. 17-24 and appendix pp. 41a-44a provide more than a bare assertion of a split. The retrieved Smithers discussion, 92 F.4th 237, 251-52 (4th Cir. 2024), specifically treats evidence capable of supporting acquittal as defeating harmlessness despite powerful prosecution evidence. That confirms a meaningful point of tension, without independently proving every branch of the petition's claimed four-to-one split.
- **A recurring legal issue and an apparently preserved vehicle:** all members of the panel recognized an instructional defect, leaving harmlessness as the central disagreement. The petition's question concerns a general limit on appellate reassessment of contested elements, not just the correctness of a prescription. The developed dissent and the trial's mixed verdict make the jury-role issue concrete. These are judgmental upward adjustments, not estimated statistical likelihood ratios.

Important counterweights keep denial the clear favorite. The majority did not expressly reject the requirement that guilt be inevitable beyond a reasonable doubt under correct instructions. It invoked that standard and distinguished Kahn and Duldulao based on patient-specific evidence, communications, and medical records; see appendix pp. 11a-25a, especially footnote 2. That gives the Court a plausible reason to regard this as disagreement about evidence rather than incompatible legal rules. Smithers also involved direct testimony from the defendant about his beliefs, whereas the majority below emphasized that Parasmo did not testify. That distinction does not resolve the legal question, but it weakens any assertion of factually identical vehicles.

Neder itself, 527 U.S. 1, 18-19 (1999), permits harmless-error review and asks whether the record could rationally support a contrary finding. Its discussion gives the petitioner a serious argument, but also leaves room for a court to conclude that a nominally contested element could not rationally be resolved the other way. The government's forthcoming response could therefore narrow the asserted conflict substantially. The petition's broader survey also describes differences within circuits, complicating a clean geography-based split. I have neither a government concession nor a verified intervening decision calling for vacatur. The 24% estimate reflects those uncertainties rather than a prediction that the petitioner necessarily has the better merits argument.

## Other judgments

The 95% distribution-increment forecast follows the unusual combination of an existing distribution and a subsequently requested response. It mostly concerns the administrative return to conference after briefing; it is not borrowed from the terminal relist table. The 0.1% CVSG forecast reflects the Solicitor General's existing role as counsel for the respondent. Conditional on a grant, the 20% summary-route estimate allows for enforcement of existing precedent, while recognizing that the contested factual distinctions favor plenary treatment if the Court takes the case. The 10% conditional separate-writing estimate recognizes the dissent and jury-trial implications without assuming that a circuit dissent predicts a Supreme Court dissent.

The stakes score of 0.58 is separate from cert probability. Clarifying harmless-error limits could affect criminal appeals beyond physician prosecutions, but the immediate vehicle is an individual conviction and the Court could decide narrowly. My largest uncertainty is whether the response will reveal a clean legal conflict or reduce the petition to record-specific error correction.
