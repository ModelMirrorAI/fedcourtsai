## In brief

A Nevada city and two officers ask the Court to restore qualified immunity after a warrantless backyard search ended in a dog's shooting.

## What happened

North Las Vegas police lost sight of a fleeing domestic-violence suspect for at least eighteen minutes while setting up a perimeter. A K-9 unit then searched the neighborhood and alerted toward Genoa Jones and Cornell Tinsley's walled backyard. An officer entered without a warrant, and when the couple's dogs attacked the police dog, he shot and killed two of them. Jones and Tinsley sued the officers and the city. The district court granted the officers qualified immunity. The Ninth Circuit reversed on the warrantless-search claim, holding the chase was no longer a "hot pursuit" once the trail went cold for that long, while upholding immunity on the shooting itself and on the claims against the city. The full Ninth Circuit declined to rehear the case, over a dissent from eight judges.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked three things: whether officers who temporarily lose a fleeing suspect's trail but later believe they have picked it back up can still rely on the "hot pursuit" exception that lets police search without a warrant; whether, even if not, the law against this search was clear enough that the officers should have known it was illegal; and whether only this Court's own rulings, not lower-court decisions, can ever count as making a rule "clearly established" for these immunity purposes.

## Where it stands

The petition was distributed for conference, and the Court then asked the other side to respond; after an extension, that response was filed. The case has not yet been scheduled for a new conference in this record.

## What each outcome would mean

The city and officers, as petitioners, are asking the Court to take up the case and restore their immunity from suit. If the Court grants review, it would hear full arguments and decide the legal questions itself, without yet deciding who wins. If the Court denies review, the Ninth Circuit's ruling stands, and Jones and Tinsley's lawsuit over the warrantless search continues in the lower courts, while the officers keep immunity on the shooting and the city keeps its win on the broader claims against it.
