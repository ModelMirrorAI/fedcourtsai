## In brief

North Las Vegas and two officers ask the Court to revive qualified immunity after a lower court let a couple's Fourth Amendment lawsuit over a warrantless backyard search proceed.

## What happened

North Las Vegas police officers lost track of a domestic-violence suspect who fled over a backyard wall. About eighteen minutes later, with a perimeter set up, a police dog alerted toward a different yard, belonging to Genoa Jones and Cornell Tinsley. An officer entered without a warrant, and when the couple's dogs confronted the police dog, an officer shot and killed two of them. Jones and Tinsley sued the officers and the city. A federal district court ruled for the officers, finding qualified immunity, a defense that shields officials from suits unless they violated clearly established law. The Ninth Circuit reversed in part, holding the warrantless entry was not justified by hot pursuit of a fleeing suspect and that this was already clearly established law, though it upheld the officers on the dog-shooting claim and the city on a separate training claim. The full Ninth Circuit declined to rehear the case over several judges' dissent.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked whether officers who briefly lose a fleeing suspect's location, then believe they have relocated him through a perimeter and a trained dog, can still rely on the pursuit as an exception to the warrant requirement. It is also asked whether the officers' conduct violated rules that were already clearly spelled out, so that they can be sued despite qualified immunity, and more broadly whether only the Supreme Court's own rulings, rather than lower appeals courts' decisions, should be able to count as the clearly established law needed to defeat that immunity.

## Where it stands

The petition has been fully briefed, including a brief opposing review and a reply, and was distributed for the Court's conference on November 6, 2026. No decision on whether to hear the case has yet been recorded.

## What each outcome would mean

North Las Vegas and the officers are asking the Court to take up the case and reverse the Ninth Circuit. If the Court denies review, the Ninth Circuit's decision stands, and the couple's lawsuit over the warrantless search continues in the lower courts. If the Court grants review, it will decide the legal questions itself after further briefing and argument; a later ruling affirming would leave the lawsuit able to proceed, while reversing would end that claim in the officers' favor. A decision to send the case back without resolving it would leave the dispute unsettled for either side.
