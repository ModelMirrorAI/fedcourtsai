## In brief

North Las Vegas and two officers ask the Court to revive qualified immunity after a lawsuit over a warrantless backyard search that led to the shooting of two pet dogs.

## What happened

Police searching for a domestic-violence suspect who had fled lost track of him for at least eighteen minutes before a police dog alerted officers to a yard several houses away. An officer entered the yard without a warrant; the homeowners' dogs attacked the police dog, and an officer shot and killed two of them. The homeowners, Genoa Jones and Cornell Tinsley, sued the city and officers. The district court granted the officers qualified immunity. The Ninth Circuit reversed in part, holding that the "hot pursuit" exception to the warrant requirement did not apply because officers had lost the suspect's trail for too long, and that this was already clearly established law. Eight judges dissented from denial of rehearing en banc.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked whether officers who temporarily lose a fleeing suspect's trail but believe they later regained it through continued police work can still rely on the "hot pursuit" exception that allows searches without a warrant. The case also asks whether the officers were protected by qualified immunity, a doctrine shielding officials from suit unless they violated a rule of law that was already clearly settled, and whether only this Court's own rulings, rather than lower-court decisions, can count as settling such rules.

## Where it stands

The petition was distributed for conference after the respondents waived and then were asked to respond, and the response deadline was later extended. Briefing is now complete, and the case has been distributed for a conference in November 2026, with no action yet recorded since then.

## What each outcome would mean

North Las Vegas, the officers, and supporting city and police groups are asking the Court to take up the case and reverse the Ninth Circuit's decision denying them qualified immunity. If the Court grants review and ultimately reverses, the officers would regain qualified immunity and the lawsuit's search claim would be defeated. If it affirms, Jones and Tinsley's lawsuit over the warrantless search would continue in the lower courts. If the Court denies the petition, the Ninth Circuit's ruling against the officers on that claim stands, and the case returns to the district court for further proceedings on the search claim.
