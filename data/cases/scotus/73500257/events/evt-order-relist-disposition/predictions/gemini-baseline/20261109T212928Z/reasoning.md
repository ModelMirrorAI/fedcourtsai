### Factors Pulling Upward (Toward Summary Reversal or Grant)
1. **Eight-Judge En Banc Dissent Written as a Summary-Reversal Roadmap:** Judge Collins, joined by Judges Callahan, Bennett, R. Nelson, Lee, Bress, Bumatay, and Tung (App. 18a–36a), forcefully argues that the panel defined clearly established law at an impermissibly high level of generality in violation of *Kisela v. Hughes*, 584 U.S. 100 (2018), *Rivas-Villegas v. Cortesluna*, 595 U.S. 1 (2021), and *Stanton v. Sims*, 571 U.S. 3 (2013). Specifically, the panel relied solely on *United States v. Johnson*, 256 F.3d 895 (9th Cir. 2001) (en banc), to hold that the officers violated clearly established law when they climbed over a six-foot cinderblock wall into respondents' backyard after a K-9 alerted to human fear scent (apocrine) during a search for a fleeing domestic battery suspect. Judge Collins highlights that in *Johnson*, an officer lost a misdemeanor suspect in rural multi-acre woods, left the search to retrieve dropped pepper spray, waited 30 minutes with no new information, and entered a fenced yard on a "gut feeling"—whereas here, officers set up a suburban perimeter and relied on a K-9 alert.
2. **Panel's Post-Rehearing Edits and Misdemeanor Footnote:** In amending its opinion on March 6, 2026, the panel replaced *Johnson*'s word "permanently" ("the continuity of the chase was terminated permanently") with the bracketed word "[definitively]" (App. 9a, 34a), and added Footnote 2 stating that the only active warrant was for misdemeanor domestic battery (App. 9a n.2)—a point that sits in some tension with *Stanton v. Sims*, 571 U.S. at 9–10 (criticizing the Ninth Circuit for reading *Johnson* to categorically restrict hot pursuit for misdemeanors), and *Lange v. California*, 594 U.S. 295, 305 (2021).
3. **Recent OT2025 Summary Reversal Precedent (*Zorn v. Linton*):** Corpus and CourtListener retrieval confirms that earlier this year, in *Zorn v. Linton*, 607 U.S. ___ (Mar. 23, 2026) (per curiam) (No. 25-297), the Court summarily reversed the Second Circuit 6–3 after multiple relists where the panel had relied on a single factually distinguishable circuit precedent (*Kerman v. City of New York*) to deny qualified immunity.
4. **Call for Response and First Relist:** The Court called for a response on July 1, 2026, after respondents initially waived, and relisted the case after its first conference on November 6, 2026.

### Factors Pulling Downward (Toward Denial)
1. **Absence of a Genuine Circuit Split on Question 1:** Respondents' Brief in Opposition (authored by William Hubbard, Kelsi Brown Corkran, and Elizabeth Cruikshank at Georgetown Law's ICAP clinic) persuasively demonstrates (BIO 14–20) that every federal circuit applies the same totality-of-the-circumstances "immediate and continuous pursuit" standard under *Welsh v. Wisconsin*, 466 U.S. 740, 753 (1984), and *United States v. Santana*, 427 U.S. 38, 42–43 (1976). Indeed, petitioners' Cert Reply (at 6–7) concedes that hot-pursuit case law "consists of a patchwork of fact-specific lower court decisions." Cases finding hot pursuit after brief losses of sight (*United States v. Cruz*, 977 F.3d 998 (10th Cir. 2020); *United States v. Schmidt*, 403 F.3d 1009 (8th Cir. 2005); *Newman v. Underhill*, 134 F.4th 1025 (9th Cir. 2025)) involved suspects seen fleeing toward a specific building or block and located within minutes. Moreover, the Court already addressed Fourth Amendment home-entry exigency earlier in 2026 in *Case v. Montana*, 607 U.S. 107 (Jan. 14, 2026), leaving little appetite for plenary review of another fact-bound home-entry dispute.
2. **Severe Summary-Judgment Record Problems Undermining Summary Reversal:** While Judge Collins's dissent makes summary reversal tempting at first glance, the BIO (at 4–6, 9–14, 21–25, 30–31) exposes messy summary-judgment disputes that make a per curiam reversal hazardous under *Tolan v. Cotton*, 572 U.S. 650 (2014):
   - *No initial foot chase:* Officer Rose saw a man jump the back wall of the suspect's girlfriend's house, never climbed the wall or pursued on foot, drove two streets south, and never saw him again (App. 4a, 10a; BIO 9–10).
   - *Protracted and disputed timeline:* The 18-minute figure cited by petitioners is merely when Lt. Salkoff and K-9 Storm *arrived* at the scene (3:47 p.m. to 4:05 p.m.). How long the yard-to-yard search took before Salkoff climbed into respondents' yard (four houses east and one house south) is disputed: Officer Rose testified in his deposition that the search lasted "an hour" or "two, three hours," and the dog-shooting report was logged around 5:07 p.m.—over an hour after the initial flight (App. 5a n.1; BIO 5, 11).
   - *Unbounded perimeter and non-tracking dog:* In depositions, the officers could not identify the streets bounding the perimeter (BIO 4, 11–12), and K-9 Storm was **not** a tracking dog that followed a scent trail from the crime scene; Storm was trained only to detect airborne human fear odor (apocrine) from anyone in the area (App. 4a; BIO 5, 12–13). Thus, the dissent's premise that the officers "recovered the suspect's trail" is sharply contested.
   - *Direct conflict with Welsh v. Wisconsin:* Because *Welsh*, 466 U.S. at 753, itself held that a warrantless home entry is not a "hot pursuit" when "there was no immediate or continuous pursuit of the petitioner from the scene of a crime," respondents have a plausible argument that Supreme Court precedent—not merely *Johnson*—foreclosed treating a 20-to-80-minute neighborhood sweep as a hot pursuit.
3. **Fatal Vehicle Flaws on Question 2:** Petitioners' second question—asking whether *only* Supreme Court decisions can clearly establish law for qualified immunity—was **completely forfeited below**: petitioners never raised it in the district court, before the panel, or in their en banc petition (BIO 31–32; Cert. Reply 11). Furthermore, there is no circuit split on that question, and even in *Zorn v. Linton* (slip op. at 4 n.3), the Court again expressly declined to reach it ("We assume without deciding that 'controlling Circuit precedent' can clearly establish law for qualified-immunity purposes").
