# Forecast — City of North Las Vegas v. Jones, No. 25-1355 (cert, distribution moment)

**State forecast from.** The docket shows **one** distribution (for the September 28, 2026 conference, entered July 15). Respondents waived; the Court **requested a response** on July 21; the brief in opposition was filed October 5, 2026 after an extension. No CVSG. One amicus brief (a coalition of Nevada cities, police agencies, the Nevada Sheriffs' and Chiefs' Association, and the International Municipal Lawyers Association) supports the petition.

## Procedural forecast

- **Relists.** The petition will be **redistributed** — a second distribution is near-certain, for a conference in early-to-mid November 2026 (about two weeks after the October 5 BIO, with or without a reply). Whether it is then **relisted further** is the real question: I put roughly 40% on at least one relist past that redistribution. If the Court is drafting a per curiam summary reversal, expect **two to four** consecutive relists through November and December 2026 before an order; if it is headed for denial, expect disposition at the first or second conference after redistribution, with a possible single relist if a Justice is writing a dissent from denial.
- **CVSG.** No. No federal party, federal statute, or federal program is implicated; the Solicitor General has no stake in a city's qualified-immunity defense, and the Court does not ordinarily invite the SG into § 1983 error-correction petitions. Roughly 2%.
- **Timing.** An order on the petition by mid-December 2026 is the modal outcome; a summary reversal, if one comes, would most likely issue as a per curiam on an order list in December 2026 or January 2027.

## Disposition

**Denial is the most likely outcome** (about 83%). The petition presents no circuit split, is openly fact-bound on question 1 (whether an eighteen-minute gap plus a K-9 alert keeps a pursuit "hot"), and the BIO's record account — no chase at all, a one-to-three-hour neighborhood sweep, a dog not trained to track, a perimeter the officer himself called a total failure — is the kind of vehicle problem that makes the Court reluctant to intervene on summary-judgment facts viewed in respondents' favor.

**If the Court grants (about 17%), the likelier route is a summary disposition rather than plenary review** — I put the conditional at about 65%. The grant case is the familiar Ninth Circuit qualified-immunity error-correction pattern (Stanton v. Sims, Sheehan, Kisela, Rivas-Villegas): a published panel decision denying immunity on a single circuit precedent, an eight-judge dissent from denial of rehearing en banc saying the panel defined clearly established law at too high a level of generality, and a petition that expressly asks for Rule 16 summary reversal. The response request after the first distribution is consistent with at least one chambers taking that possibility seriously. The per curiam would rest on **question 2** (no clearly established law — United States v. Johnson involved a half-hour wait and a suspect loose in acres of woods, and lower courts have treated brief losses of a suspect as compatible with hot pursuit), and would not decide question 1's constitutional merits.

**Which question the Court would take, if plenary.** Question 1 reformulated as a Lange follow-on — whether and when a pursuit that begins immediately "goes cold" — is the only plenary-worthy question here, and the Chief Justice's Lange concurrence shows some appetite for it; but the disputed facts make this a poor vehicle for it, so plenary review is the minority grant route (about 35% of grants). **Question 3** (only this Court's decisions can clearly establish law) will **not** be granted: it is forfeited below, every circuit rejects it, and the Court has reserved it for a decade without taking it up.

**No GVR**: there is no intervening decision bearing on the hot-pursuit or clearly-established questions.

## Separate writings

**On a denial**, a dissent from or statement respecting denial is **unlikely but live** (about 12%). The candidates are Justice Alito or Justice Thomas, who have written when the Court declines to correct a perceived qualified-immunity error, or a short statement noting the question 3 issue remains open. **On a summary reversal**, expect a dissent from Justice Sotomayor, likely joined by Justice Jackson, objecting to summary reversal on disputed facts viewed in the light most favorable to the plaintiffs.
