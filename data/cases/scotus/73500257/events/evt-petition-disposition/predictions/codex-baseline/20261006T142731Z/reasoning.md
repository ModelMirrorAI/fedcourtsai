## Why 18%, rather than the anchor or a likely grant

Two concrete signals warrant a substantial upward adjustment. First, the Court requested a response after respondents waived one; that is affirmative attention rather than a routine voluntary opposition. Second, both briefs report eight judges dissenting from denial of rehearing en banc (petition pp. 9–11; opposition pp. 8–9). Petitioners frame the case as another overly general application of circuit precedent to deny qualified immunity, and identify a narrow summary-correction route (petition pp. 9–12, 20–22). Neither signal proves four votes for review, but together they make the unadjusted baseline too low.

The opposition substantially weakens the vehicle for plenary review. Petitioners describe continuous efforts to recover a suspect's trail after roughly eighteen minutes. Respondents contend that no immediate chase occurred, the asserted perimeter did not actually contain the suspect, and eighteen minutes measured the canine officer's arrival rather than the eventual backyard entry (opposition pp. 1–3, 9–14). On respondents' account, this was an extended neighborhood search, not an interrupted pursuit. I cannot resolve those factual disputes from advocacy alone; they make a clean categorical holding less likely and complicate summary reversal too.

The parties' disagreement also appears primarily about factual application, rather than a demonstrated conflict between circuits on a legal rule. The opposition identifies agreement that a pursuit must be immediate and continuous and explains that the Ninth Circuit itself permits some brief losses of visual contact (pp. 17–20). Petitioners' distinctions of Johnson nevertheless give the Court a plausible narrower qualified-immunity question. This is why I retain meaningful summary-reversal probability without treating the petition's assertion of obvious error as established.

Question three has much broader stakes but a particularly weak vehicle. Respondents say the Supreme-Court-precedent-only argument was never raised below, neither lower court decided it, and Supreme Court precedent independently supplies notice (opposition pp. 30–35). Those are respondents' preservation and alternative-ground arguments, not verified conclusions, but petitioners' broad policy request does not overcome them on this record. Moreover, the opposition reports that immunity for the dog-shooting seizure and judgment for the City survived below; the live controversy is narrower than the emotionally salient account of the dogs' deaths suggests (pp. 6–9).

My final 18% is a judgmental adjustment, not a fitted response-request multiplier. The largest uncertainty is how the Court reads the factual record and the particularity required of precedent. Denial remains the modal disposition. Conditional on intervention, narrow summary correction is more plausible than taking all three questions for argument, yielding a 75% conditional summary-route forecast.
