## 2. Disposition and Route (`disposition`: `0.25`; `summary-disposition-route`: `0.76`)

- **Primary Forecast — Certiorari Denied (`predicted_disposition: denied`, `0.75`):** After reviewing Respondents' Brief in Opposition, the Court is most likely to deny the petition for a writ of certiorari. Although Judge Collins's eight-judge dissent from the denial of rehearing en banc makes a strong case that the Ninth Circuit panel defined "clearly established" hot-pursuit law at too high a level of generality in relying solely on *United States v. Johnson*, 256 F.3d 895 (9th Cir. 2001) (en banc), the Brief in Opposition will highlight serious factual and vehicle obstacles on summary judgment:
  1. Viewing the record in the light most favorable to Plaintiffs, the officers did not merely lose visual contact for 18 minutes before recovering the trail; rather, Lieutenant Salkoff arrived after 18 minutes and then conducted a yard-by-yard search of up to a dozen residences (including warrantless entry into a neighbor's yard without a K-9 alert) that Officer Rose testified lasted "more than an hour and possibly for two or three hours" before the officers vaulted into Plaintiffs' walled backyard (App. 5a n.1, 56a).
  2. The panel merely *assumed without deciding* that the police dog Storm's alert to "apocrine" (human fear odor) from an elevated position down the street established probable cause to believe the suspect was inside Plaintiffs' locked curtilage (App. 12a). That unresolved predicate makes the case an awkward vehicle for either plenary review or summary reversal.
- **Conditional Route if Granted — Summary Reversal Over Plenary Review (`summary-disposition-route`: `0.76`):** Conditional on the Court granting review (`0.25`), the Court is roughly three times as likely to dispose of the case summarily in the cert order itself (`0.76` conditional probability, representing an ~18% unconditional probability of `summary-reversal` and ~1% of `gvr`) as it is to set the case for full merits briefing and oral argument (~6% unconditional probability). The petition is framed around classic qualified-immunity error correction under *Stanton v. Sims*, 571 U.S. 3 (2013) (per curiam), *Rivas-Villegas v. Cortesluna*, 595 U.S. 1 (2021) (per curiam), and *City of Tahlequah v. Bond*, 595 U.S. 9 (2021) (per curiam), pointing out that the panel cited the very passage of *Johnson* previously rebuked in *Stanton* and edited *Johnson*'s quote from "terminated permanently" to "terminated definitively" in its amended opinion.
- **Which Question Presented the Court Would Take (If Plenary Review Were Granted):** If the Court were to grant plenary review or summarily reverse, it would address **Question Presented 2** (whether the Ninth Circuit erred in denying qualified immunity by relying solely on the factually distinguishable decision in *United States v. Johnson* without Supreme Court precedent clearly establishing that a K-9 perimeter recovery falls outside hot pursuit), leaving **Question Presented 1** (the underlying Fourth Amendment merits question under *Lange v. California*, 594 U.S. 295 (2021)) undecided under *Pearson v. Callahan*, 555 U.S. 223 (2009).
