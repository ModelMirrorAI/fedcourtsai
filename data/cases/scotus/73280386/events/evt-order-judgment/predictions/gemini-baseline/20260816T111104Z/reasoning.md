I anchor on the historical disturbed rate for merits cases, which sits at ~69.7% for the 10-term period (2015-2024) strictly preceding this case's 2025 grant term. I adjust significantly upward for two reasons.

First, the core merits issue involves the due process limits on mandatory immigration detention under 8 U.S.C. 1226(c). In recent years, the conservative majority of the Supreme Court has consistently ruled in favor of the government in immigration detention cases, emphasizing statutory text over implied due process limits (e.g., *Jennings v. Rodriguez*, *Nielsen v. Preap*, *Johnson v. Arteaga-Martinez*, *Garland v. Gonzalez*). The Second Circuit's holding that due process requires individualized bond hearings with the burden of proof on the government by clear and convincing evidence is highly vulnerable to reversal under this precedent.

Second, the Court directed the parties to brief the question of mootness regarding respondent G.M. If the case is found moot, the standard practice under *United States v. Munsingwear* is to vacate the judgment below and remand with instructions to dismiss. A vacatur counts as disturbing the judgment.

Because the judgment is likely to be either reversed on the merits or vacated for mootness, the probability that the lower court's judgment is disturbed is extremely high. The primary uncertainty is the exact procedural route (reversal vs. vacatur for mootness), but both routes lead to a disturbed judgment.
