I expect the Court to reverse the Second Circuit's judgment. The Court is likely to hold that 8 U.S.C. 1226(c) constitutionally mandates the detention of criminal aliens without individualized bond hearings during their removal proceedings. The majority will likely conclude that the categorical mandate does not violate the Due Process Clause because the government has a compelling interest in preventing criminal aliens from absconding or committing further crimes, which justifies the deprivation of liberty without the individualized hearings the Second Circuit required. 

I expect a 6-3 split along ideological lines. Justice Alito or Justice Kavanaugh is a likely author for the majority. Justice Sotomayor is likely to write the principal dissent, joined by Justices Kagan and Jackson, arguing that prolonged detention without a hearing violates fundamental due process protections. 

There is a distinct possibility the Court finds respondent G.M.'s case moot, which would result in a *Munsingwear* vacatur rather than a merits reversal. However, given that there are two respondents (Black and G.M.) and the Court only flagged G.M. for mootness briefing, it is likely the Court will reach the merits in at least Black's case, resulting in a reversal. If G.M.'s case is moot, the Court will vacate the judgment with respect to G.M. In either scenario, the judgment of the court of appeals will be disturbed.
