# Forecast

The Court will reverse the Second Circuit, most likely by a 6–3 vote along the Court's current ideological division. The majority will conclude that detention mandated by 8 U.S.C. § 1226(c) remains constitutional while bona fide removal proceedings are pending and removal remains practically attainable. It will reject the Second Circuit's use of *Mathews v. Eldridge* to require a bond hearing based on the duration and individual burdens of detention alone.

The majority's rule will be categorical as to duration but will preserve a narrow as-applied limit for proceedings that have become a sham or for removal that is no longer realistically attainable. The Court will not resolve which party would bear the burden at a constitutionally required bond hearing: certiorari was granted only as to G.M.'s appeal, and the Second Circuit had remanded rather than deciding that remedial issue for him.

The added mootness question is a substantial alternative route. G.M. has been free since 2022, the government previously told the district court it had no current plan to detain him, and no bond hearing followed from the judgment. If the Court finds the threatened re-detention too speculative, it will vacate the Second Circuit's judgment and direct dismissal rather than affirm it. That route also disturbs the judgment below.

On the merits, Chief Justice Roberts and Justices Thomas, Alito, Gorsuch, Kavanaugh, and Barrett will form the majority. Justices Sotomayor, Kagan, and Jackson will dissent, emphasizing bodily liberty, the exceptional length of detention, and *Jennings*' preservation of constitutional challenges.
