# Forecast — Genalo v. Black (No. 25-886), merits judgment

**Judgment class: `reversed`.** The Court will disturb the Second Circuit's
judgment, most likely by reversing outright. It granted the government's
petition on June 15, 2026 — limited to G.M.'s case (CA2 No. 22-70) — to decide
whether due process ever makes 8 U.S.C. § 1226(c) detention "unreasonably
prolonged" so as to require a bond hearing, and whether such a hearing must put
a clear-and-convincing burden on the government. This Court has reversed every
court of appeals ruling extending bond-hearing rights to detained aliens since
2018: Jennings v. Rodriguez (2018, reversed CA9), Nielsen v. Preap (2019,
reversed CA9), Johnson v. Arteaga-Martinez (2022, reversed CA3), Garland v.
Aleman Gonzalez (2022, reversed CA9). Jennings expressly reserved this
constitutional question after ordering supplemental briefing on it, and two
CA2 judges (Nardini, Menashi) dissented from denial of rehearing en banc. The
Court does not grant a Solicitor General petition in this posture to affirm.

**The mootness question is the main route-splitter, not an escape to
affirmance.** The Court itself added the question "Whether G. M.'s case is now
moot" to the grant. G.M. has left the United States, and the respondents'
counsel pressed mootness before the grant (their suggestion of mootness was
rejected for filing under Rule 15.4). If the Court finds the case moot, the
disposition is a Munsingwear-style **vacatur** of the Second Circuit's judgment
— still a disturbance of the judgment below, as in New York State Rifle &
Pistol Assn. v. City of New York (2020). I put roughly a quarter of the
probability mass on that vacatur route, most of the rest on a merits reversal.
An affirmance (the Court constitutionalizing bond hearings for § 1226(c)
detainees) is very unlikely (~6%); a DIG is a small residual (~5%) if the
mootness/vehicle problems look worse after argument than the grant assumed.

**Vote lineup: 6–3.** Roberts, Thomas, Alito, Gorsuch, Kavanaugh, and Barrett
in the majority; Sotomayor, Kagan, and Jackson dissenting. This mirrors the
Jennings (5–3) and Preap (5–4) alignments carried forward to the current
bench. If the case instead resolves on mootness, the lineup would likely be
broader and could approach unanimity on the vacatur, with separate writings on
whether to reach the merits — but the 6–3 merits split is the modal forecast.
No recusal is apparent, so an equally divided affirmance is a negligible risk.
Likely majority author: a Justice from the Court's immigration-detention line —
Alito (Jennings, Preap) or Kavanaugh; this authorship guess is unscored
context.

**Ground and breadth** (the two semantic claims, stated in full in
`prediction.json`): the majority will hold, on the strength of Demore v. Kim,
that § 1226(c)'s mandatory detention during pending removal proceedings
comports with due process without an individualized bond hearing, rejecting
the Second Circuit's Mathews v. Eldridge balancing and its clear-and-convincing
burden; and it will do so categorically — a rule for the class of § 1226(c)
detainees, not a fact-bound holding about G.M.'s twenty-one months — reversing
without a remand for case-specific balancing. A narrower path the Court could
take instead is to reverse only on the burden/procedures question (QP2) while
leaving a duration trigger nominally open; I weight that lower because the
government's QP1 attacks the trigger itself and the Court granted both
questions.

**Timing** (unscored discipline): argument is set for October 13, 2026 — the
opening sitting of OT2026. A decision in a divided constitutional immigration
case typically lands late in the Term; expect the judgment between March and
June 2027, with June most likely. If the Court instead resolves it as moot, a
short per curiam or unsigned vacatur could come earlier, plausibly by
January–February 2027.
