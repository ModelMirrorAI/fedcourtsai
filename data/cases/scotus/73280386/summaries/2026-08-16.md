## What happened

Carol Williams Black and Keisy G.M., both longtime lawful permanent residents, were detained by immigration authorities under a law that requires holding certain noncitizens with qualifying criminal convictions while their removal cases proceed, without a bond hearing. Black was held seven months, G.M. twenty-one months. Both sued, arguing their prolonged detention without any hearing on flight risk or danger violated due process. The Second Circuit agreed, ruling that once such detention becomes unreasonably long, the government must give the person a bond hearing, and, in Black's case, must prove by clear and convincing evidence that continued detention is justified. The government asked the full appeals court to reconsider; it declined. The government then asked the Supreme Court to review the case, and the respondents argue the case is a poor candidate because neither man remains detained and one has left the country.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked whether the Constitution requires a hearing before an immigration judge once detention of this kind drags on too long, and if so, whether the government—rather than the detained person—must bear the burden of proving continued detention is justified, using a demanding evidentiary standard.
