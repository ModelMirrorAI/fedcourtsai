## Why 10%, below the elevated-band anchor

The positive evidence is genuine: the Court requested an opposition after waiver, the petition raises a recurring damages issue, and municipal counsel have organized amicus support. The petition describes a $25 million compensatory verdict and an initial prejudgment-interest award exceeding $7.6 million, while disclosing an agreement reducing the interest exposure and resolving the other aspects of the case. Those facts make the issue concrete without making the original amounts the remaining disputed recovery. Sources: petition, printed pp. 3-8; snapshot proceedings.

The strongest negative is the weakness of the claimed categorical split. The petition presents the Third, Fourth, and Tenth Circuits as opposed to other circuits, but its own account acknowledges that White v. Chafin upheld a discretionary denial. I independently retrieved that 2017 decision through CourtListener, which indexes it under White v. Wycoff, 862 F.3d 1065. The opinion confirms the abuse-of-discretion framework rather than a categorical ban. The Seventh Circuit's appendix discusses the same distinction. Sources: petition, pp. 9-12 and App. 9a-10a; BIO, pp. 11-14; CourtListener opinion 4184847.

The Fourth Circuit authority is less uniformly favorable to respondent than the BIO's framing might suggest. Gilliam v. Allen, 62 F.4th 829, 848-50 (4th Cir. 2023), emphasizes the jury's role and the danger of double recovery when damages already compensate for delay and include continuing injury. Its text nevertheless leaves room to consider interest when the jury has not fully compensated the plaintiffs. That makes a genuine difference in approach plausible, but it is narrower than the petition's across-the-board question. I checked the relevant passage directly through CourtListener opinion 9378265. The Third Circuit's Poleto language is characterized as dicta by the provisioned appellate opinion and BIO; I have not independently verified Poleto, and the petition's district-court applications of that language are a reason not to reduce the grant chance to the whole-docket floor.

Vehicle quality also cuts against review. The question asks about noneconomic damages broadly, while the BIO argues that availability depends on the particular federal cause of action and that a Section 1983-specific argument was not presented below. I treat that preservation objection as advocacy, not a separately established forfeiture finding. The appellate court also remanded to separate past and future damages, retaining a fact-sensitive path to adjusting the award. Sources: BIO, pp. 15-17; petition, pp. 7-8 and App. 9a-12a. A more focused future petition could present the institutional jury-versus-judge issue without the same mismatch between the question and the authorities.

Finally, the second distribution followed a response request before the first scheduled conference and subsequent briefing. It is weaker evidence of repeated merits consideration than an ordinary post-conference relist. I keep the harness's elevated classification intact but adjust the individual forecast downward for that trajectory. Balancing the request for a response and recurring issue against split and vehicle weaknesses yields 10%, rather than simply repeating 17.24%. This is a judgmental adjustment, not a fitted likelihood ratio.

## Other elicited beliefs and limitations

The 25% further-distribution probability leaves substantial room for closer examination after the requested BIO but puts most mass on disposal after the scheduled September conference. No further distribution is the modal count, and one is the modal positive count. The 1.5% CVSG probability reflects the absence of a demonstrated special federal-government interest in this municipal damages dispute, rather than any rule that federal law questions cannot draw a CVSG. Both are judgmental forward hazards; the terminal statpack cuts do not directly estimate them.

Conditional on any grant, I assign 6% to a cert-order disposition because the materials identify no intervening authority calling for a GVR and the contested legal question would benefit from plenary treatment. The 4% conditional denial-writing probability reflects a technical remedial dispute without a demonstrated campaign of separate writings in the materials consulted. Neither forecast assigns individual cert votes.

The stakes score of 0.40 is independent of grant likelihood. A decision could materially change civil-rights compensation and municipal interest exposure, with possible relevance to other federal causes of action. But it would not decide the underlying wrongful-conviction liability, and its reach is bounded by statutory context and the past/future damages distinction. My main limitations are the unavailable reply and amicus texts, the uncertain force of the Third Circuit practice, and the absence of a matched forward-hazard model for response-request redistributions.
