# Forecast: Pesavento v. Bolden, No. 25-1146 (evt-petition-disposition)

**Disposition.** The Court will deny the petition. P(any grant) = 0.09.

**Distributions and relists.** The docket shows two distributions: one for the
May 28, 2026 conference, pulled when the Court requested a response on May 20
after respondent had waived, and a redistribution on August 5 for the
September 28, 2026 long conference. The September 28 conference is therefore the
petition's *first* actual consideration; the second distribution is a
call-for-response redistribution, not a relist after conference. From that
state I expect the petition to be denied on the order list following the long
conference (October 5, 2026) without a further distribution. A single relist is
the plausible alternative, because the Court did think enough of the petition to
call for a response and because nearly every grant now passes through at least
one relist; I put the chance of at least one more distribution at 0.28, and the
chance of two or more at well under half of that.

**CVSG.** No call for the views of the Solicitor General. No federal party is
involved, the federal government's own prejudgment-interest exposure is governed
by statute rather than by the judge-made rule at issue, and the Court will not
need the SG to see the vehicle problems the BIO identifies. P = 0.04.

**Which question, if granted.** If the Court did grant, it would not take the
question as written, which asks whether prejudgment interest is unavailable "as
a matter of law" on noneconomic damages across all of federal law. It would
reformulate to the Section 1983 setting the case actually presents, roughly:
whether a district court may award prejudgment interest on a Section 1983 award
consisting entirely of past noneconomic damages. Plenary review would then turn
on the Court's context-specific prejudgment-interest framework (Rodgers, West
Virginia v. United States, City of Milwaukee) rather than on a categorical rule.

**Summary disposition.** Very unlikely. There is no intervening decision of this
Court to GVR against, and the Seventh Circuit opinion is a careful, unanimous,
published decision that follows the weight of circuit authority, so a per curiam
summary reversal is not a realistic route. Conditional on a grant, P(disposed of
in the cert order itself) = 0.04.

**Dissent or statement respecting denial.** None expected. The question is a
remedial one with no Justice's known agenda attached to it, and the strongest
argument for review (municipal fiscal exposure to large wrongful-conviction
verdicts) is a policy point rather than a doctrinal one. Conditional on denial,
P(some separate writing) = 0.05.
