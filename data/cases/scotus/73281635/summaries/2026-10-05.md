## In brief

The Court declined to hear Chicago police officers' and the City's bid to bar prejudgment interest on a wrongfully convicted man's noneconomic damages award.

## What happened

Eddie Bolden spent 22 years in prison before Illinois courts found his conviction unsound. He sued the City of Chicago and several police officers, and a jury awarded him $25 million for pain, suffering, and loss of a normal life. A federal district court later added $7.6 million in prejudgment interest, covering the years between his release and the judgment. The officers and the City appealed, and the Seventh Circuit agreed that such interest could be awarded on noneconomic damages, though it sent the case back to sort out how much of the award covered past, rather than future, harm.

## What the Court is being asked

The petitioners asked the Court to decide whether courts are ever allowed to add interest, covering the wait between an injury and a final judgment, to a damages award that compensates purely for pain, suffering, and similar intangible harm, rather than only to awards that compensate for lost money.

## Where it stands

The Court denied the petition on October 5, 2026, after considering it at two conferences; the case had earlier been distributed for a May 2026 conference and then redistributed after the Court requested a response from Bolden.

## What each outcome would mean

The officers and the City, as petitioners, asked the Court to overturn the Seventh Circuit's decision allowing interest on Bolden's award. Granting review would have let the Court decide whether such interest is categorically barred, with no outcome assured either side. By denying the petition, the Court leaves the Seventh Circuit's decision in place: Bolden can continue pursuing prejudgment interest on his noneconomic damages, with the case returning to the district court to apportion past from future damages, as the appeals court ordered.
