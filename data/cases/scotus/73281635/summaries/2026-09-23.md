## What happened

Eddie L. Bolden spent 22 years in prison before Illinois courts found he had not committed the crimes for which he was convicted. He sued the City of Chicago and several police officers, and a jury awarded him $25 million in compensatory damages for pain, suffering, and loss of a normal life. The trial court then added about $7.6 million in prejudgment interest, reasoning that Bolden waited years for judgment after his release. The city and officers appealed, arguing that this kind of interest cannot be added to damages for non-economic harm. The Seventh Circuit disagreed, following its own long-standing rule, though it sent the case back to sort out how much of the award covered past versus future harm.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked whether it is legally forbidden to add extra compensation for the passage of time to a damages award that covers pain, suffering, and similar non-economic injuries, as opposed to awards for lost wages or other measurable financial losses. The city argues such awards cannot represent a loss of "money" that was owed and delayed, so adding interest is unwarranted. Bolden answers that non-economic harm is still compensated with money owed from the time of injury, so delay in paying it can be compensated the same way as any other debt.
