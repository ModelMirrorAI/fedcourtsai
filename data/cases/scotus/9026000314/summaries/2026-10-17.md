## In brief

A driver and industry professional asks the Court to revive his challenge to federal headlight-safety rules that a lower court dismissed as untimely.

## What happened

Albert Ekladyous, representing himself, petitioned the D.C. Circuit for review of how the National Highway Traffic Safety Administration implemented a law requiring new vehicle headlight standards. He argued the agency never adopted the specific technical standard Congress wrote into the law, and that this failure was an ongoing violation he could still challenge. The D.C. Circuit dismissed his case, ruling that any challenge to the agency's 2022 rule was filed too late, and that he had not identified any other required action the agency failed to take. The full court denied rehearing.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked whether a claim that an agency never performed a specific duty Congress required can be dismissed as a late challenge to an existing rule, simply because the agency issued some rule on the topic. The case also asks whether a claim that an agency adopted a different technical standard than the one Congress specified can be reviewed as an ongoing failure to act, rather than treated as expired.

## Where it stands

The petition was distributed for the Court's November 6, 2026 conference, after the respondent agencies waived their right to respond. The docket shows no action since that distribution.

## What each outcome would mean

Ekladyous is asking the Court to hear his case. If the Court grants review, it would hear full arguments and decide whether his claim was wrongly dismissed as untimely, with no party yet favored. If the Court denies review, the D.C. Circuit's dismissal stands, leaving NHTSA's current headlight rule in place and closing Ekladyous's challenge without a ruling on whether the agency followed the standard Congress set.
