## In brief

A driver and industry professional asks the Court to revive his challenge to federal headlight safety rules that a lower court dismissed as untimely.

## What happened

Albert Ekladyous, representing himself, petitioned the D.C. Circuit for review of federal regulations on adaptive headlight systems, arguing that the National Highway Traffic Safety Administration never adopted a technical performance standard that Congress had written into law. The agency moved to dismiss, and the D.C. Circuit granted that motion. It held that any challenge to conduct other than the agency's 2022 final rule failed to identify a specific legal duty, and that any challenge to the rule itself came too late under a law requiring such challenges within 59 days. The full D.C. Circuit declined to rehear the case. Ekladyous then asked the Supreme Court to take up the matter.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked whether an appeals court may treat a claim that an agency never fulfilled an ongoing legal duty as if it were instead a late challenge to a rule the agency already issued. A related question is whether Ekladyous's specific claim — that the agency's rule departs from a technical standard Congress wrote into the statute — counts as the kind of ongoing-duty claim that can be reviewed no matter when it is brought, even though the agency has already issued a rule on the subject.

## Where it stands

The petition and supporting documents were filed, the government waived its right to respond, and the case was distributed for the Court's conference on November 6, 2026. No action on the petition has yet been recorded.

## What each outcome would mean

Ekladyous is asking the Court to take up his case. If the Court grants the petition, it will hear full arguments on whether his claim was wrongly treated as untimely, and the case would proceed to a decision on that question. If the Court denies the petition, the D.C. Circuit's dismissal stands, leaving the agency's current headlight rule in place without further judicial review of Ekladyous's claim that it departs from the standard Congress specified.
