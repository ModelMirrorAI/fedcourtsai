## In brief

The Court declined to hear a driver's bid to revive his challenge to federal headlight regulations after lower courts ruled it was filed too late.

## What happened

Albert Ekladyous, representing himself, asked the federal appeals court in Washington, D.C. to review headlight safety rules issued by the National Highway Traffic Safety Administration. He argued the agency never adopted a technical performance standard that a 2021 infrastructure law told it to use for new "adaptive driving beam" headlamps, which automatically dim for oncoming traffic. The appeals court dismissed his case, holding that any challenge to the 2022 rule itself came too late under a 59-day filing deadline, and that he had not identified any separate, ongoing agency failure. The full appeals court declined to rehear the case.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked whether an appeals court may treat a claim that an agency never carried out a specific, ongoing legal duty as if it were instead a late challenge to a rule the agency already issued. It is also asked whether a claim that the agency adopted a different technical standard than the one Congress specified counts as the kind of ongoing failure that can be reviewed regardless of filing deadlines that apply to completed rules.

## Where it stands

The petition was distributed for the Court's November 6, 2026 conference, and on November 9, 2026, the Court denied it.

## What each outcome would mean

Ekladyous, who lost in the lower court, asked the Court to take up his case. The denial means the Court will not review the question, so the appeals court's dismissal of his case stands. The agency's regulation remains in place, and Ekladyous's challenge to it is over. The denial does not mean the Supreme Court agreed with the lower court's reasoning; it simply leaves that ruling undisturbed.
