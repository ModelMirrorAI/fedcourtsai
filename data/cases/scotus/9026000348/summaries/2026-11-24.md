## In brief

The federal government asks the Court to review rulings that a prosecutor appointed to lead a U.S. Attorney's office after a vacancy arose was serving unlawfully.

## What happened

After the Senate-confirmed U.S. Attorney for the Northern District of New York left office, the Attorney General eventually designated John Sarcone III as First Assistant, and he then began serving as Acting U.S. Attorney. Sarcone obtained grand jury subpoenas against the Office of the New York State Attorney General, seeking records about its past lawsuits against Donald Trump and the National Rifle Association. New York's Attorney General's office moved to quash the subpoenas, arguing Sarcone's appointment was invalid. The district court agreed and disqualified Sarcone from the investigations; the Second Circuit affirmed, with one judge dissenting. The United States asked the Supreme Court to review that decision.

## What the Court is being asked

The case concerns a federal law governing temporary leadership of positions that normally require Senate confirmation. The Court is asked whether someone appointed as a top deputy only after a vacancy already began may automatically take over as the acting head of the office, or whether only a deputy already in place when the vacancy begins can do so. It is also asked whether a separate rule barring agencies from using general delegation powers to get around that law prevented the Attorney General from assigning this person all the duties of the vacant office by another route.

## Where it stands

The petition has been fully briefed, including a brief in opposition and the government's reply, and was distributed for the Court's December 4, 2026 conference. The government's reply asked that the petition be held pending the Court's disposition of a related petition, United States v. Jackson, involving the same legal questions from a different circuit.

## What each outcome would mean

The United States, as petitioner, is asking the Court to reverse the Second Circuit's decision, which favored the New York Attorney General's office. If the Court grants the petition, it would hear the case and decide these questions itself, without determining in advance who wins. If the Court denies the petition, the Second Circuit's ruling stands, leaving Sarcone's appointment invalid and his disqualification from the investigations in place. The government has also asked, as an alternative to either outcome, that the Court hold this petition until it resolves the similar Jackson case.
