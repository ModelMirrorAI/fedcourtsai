## What happened

The dispute is over who could lawfully lead the U.S. Attorney's Office for the Northern District of New York after the Senate-confirmed U.S. Attorney left office. John Sarcone III was later named First Assistant U.S. Attorney and also given a broad delegation of duties by the Attorney General; he then used the title "Acting U.S. Attorney" to obtain grand jury subpoenas seeking records from the Office of the New York State Attorney General related to its cases against the National Rifle Association and Donald Trump. New York's Attorney General's office asked a court to quash the subpoenas, arguing Sarcone had no valid authority. A district court agreed and disqualified Sarcone from the investigations; the Second Circuit Court of Appeals affirmed, over one judge's dissent. The federal government, as petitioner, then asked the Supreme Court to review the case.

## What the Court is being asked

The case turns on a federal law governing who may temporarily fill a vacant position that normally requires presidential appointment and Senate confirmation. The government argues that someone can become the acting head of an office even if they were named to a key deputy role after the vacancy began, not just someone already in that role beforehand. It also argues that an agency head may hand that person nearly all the office's duties without formally naming them the "acting" officer, and that doing so does not violate the law's requirement that this statute be the only route to acting authority. The lower courts ruled the opposite way on both points. Notably, the government's petition asks the Court to hold this case and decide it in light of another, related petition already pending.
