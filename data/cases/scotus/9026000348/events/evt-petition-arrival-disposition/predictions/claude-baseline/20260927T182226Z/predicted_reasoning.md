# Predicted reasoning — United States v. Office of the New York State Attorney General, No. 26-348

**What the Court will do.** This is a self-declared hold petition. The Solicitor General asks the Court not to grant it but to hold it for *United States v. Jackson*, No. 26-304 (CA9, filed Sept. 8, 2026), which presents the same two FVRA questions on a cleaner record, and then "dispose of it as appropriate." The Court will do exactly that: it will not act on this petition until *Jackson* is resolved. The disposition here is therefore derivative of two events in another docket — whether *Jackson* is granted, and, if it is, whether the government wins there.

- **If *Jackson* is granted and the government prevails on either question** (the post-vacancy first-assistant reading of 5 U.S.C. 3345(a)(1), or the delegation reading of 3347(a)), this petition will be **granted, the Second Circuit's judgment vacated, and the case remanded** (a GVR) in the clean-up order list after the *Jackson* decision — most likely late June or early October 2027. The GVR would also let the Second Circuit revisit mootness and the Rule 17 remedy, which the SG flags as this case's wrinkles.
- **If *Jackson* is granted and the government loses**, this petition will be **denied** in the same clean-up order list.
- **If *Jackson* is denied** (likely December 2026 or January 2027), this petition will be denied in the same or the next order list.
- A plenary grant of *this* petition, consolidated with *Jackson*, is unlikely (roughly one in twenty): the SG has told the Court that *Jackson* is the better vehicle because the subpoenas here are already unenforceable and mootness would have to be addressed first.

I put the lead case's grant around 70-75 percent: an SG petition, three circuits (CA2, CA3, CA9) invalidating a practice the Executive has used for 25 years, an acknowledged conflict with the Federal Circuit's *Arthrex* on the delegation question, and live disruption to U.S. Attorney offices in NDNY, D. Vt., D. Nev. and D.N.J. Against that, the appellate consensus is lopsided against the government — the Ninth Circuit panel was unanimous, with the opinion by Judge Miller, and the Second Circuit's was 2-1 — so the Court could let the rulings stand and leave the fix to nominations. Conditional on a grant, I put the government's chance of winning at least one question around 50 percent: the text of 3345(a)(1) plausibly bears the government's present-tense reading and there is long GAO-acquiesced practice, but *SW General* shows this Court reads the FVRA's anti-circumvention structure strictly, and the delegation theory would let an agency head hollow out the Act.

**Distributions (relist claim).** The docket shows **zero distributions**; the record is the petition entry of Sept. 15, 2026 with the response due Oct. 15, 2026. The New York AG will either waive or file a brief in opposition (a BIO arguing mootness and agreeing that the case should not be the vehicle is the likelier shape). The petition will then be distributed once for a conference in November or December 2026 and left undecided while *Jackson* is pending, then redistributed after *Jackson* is decided. So at least one further distribution is near-certain, and two or more are likely; only a Rule 46 dismissal before the first distribution (e.g., if Mr. Sarcone leaves and the government abandons the case) would prevent it.

**CVSG.** None. The United States is the petitioner; the Court does not call for the views of a party.

**Which question, if taken.** If the Court takes anything from this docket it will be through the GVR route, resolving nothing on the merits here. Were it, against expectation, to grant plenary review alongside *Jackson*, it would take both QPs as written and would likely have to address mootness (capable-of-repetition and the standing disqualification order) as a threshold matter.

**Summary route.** Conditional on a grant, the summary route (a GVR in light of *Jackson*) is the overwhelmingly likely form — about nine in ten — because the SG has asked for exactly that and the vehicle defects cut against plenary review here.

**Dissent from denial.** Conditional on a denial, a noted dissent or statement is unlikely (about one in ten). A denial would come either after the Court has already decided the question in *Jackson*, when a separate writing on the companion would be unusual, or as a companion to a *Jackson* denial, where any writing would attach to the lead docket rather than this one.
