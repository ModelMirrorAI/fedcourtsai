# Forecast of the Court's Action — No. 26-348, *United States v. Office of the New York State Attorney General*

## Conference Trajectory, Relists, and Hold Mechanics

This petition currently sits at a single conference distribution (`distribution_count = 1`), having been distributed on November 17, 2026, alongside its lead companion petition, *United States v. Jackson*, No. 26-304, for the Conference of December 4, 2026. Expect the petition to be **distributed at least once more** (`P = 0.84`), most likely accumulating **two to three total conference distributions** (one to two further distributions beyond the current snapshot) before terminal resolution:

1. **Initial December 2026 relist:** Consistent with the Court's ordinary practice before granting plenary review on a pair of related petitions, the Court is likely to relist both *Jackson* (No. 26-304) and this petition from the December 4, 2026 conference to the December 11, 2026 (or January 8, 2027) conference while chambers examine the competing vehicle arguments over whether to grant *Jackson* alone or consolidate both cases.
2. **Hold pending *Jackson* and post-decision redistribution:** Once the Court grants certiorari in *Jackson*, it will follow the Solicitor General's request and hold No. 26-348 in abeyance pending the merits disposition of *Jackson*. After *Jackson* is argued in spring 2027 and decided in May or June 2027, the Clerk will redistribute No. 26-348 for the post-decision cleanup conference so the Court can enter its terminal order.

## Call for the Views of the Solicitor General (CVSG)

No CVSG will issue (`P = 0.001`). The United States is the petitioner, represented by the Solicitor General, whose views are already before the Court in the petition and reply brief.

## Summary Disposition Route vs. Plenary Review

Conditional on the Court granting this petition in any form, **disposing of the case in the certiorari order itself via a grant, vacate, and remand (GVR) is substantially likelier than setting No. 26-348 for plenary briefing and argument** (`P = 0.786` conditional on a grant; unconditional `gvr` probability `0.44` vs. plenary `granted` probability `0.12`).

The Court will respect the Solicitor General's request to hold this petition behind *Jackson* rather than adopting respondent's alternative proposal to grant and consolidate both cases. Consolidating No. 26-348 would burden the merits proceeding with three case-specific complications absent from *Jackson*: (1) the grand jury subpoenas served on the New York State Attorney General's Office have already expired, leaving live jurisdiction over the subpoena-quashal ruling dependent on the capable-of-repetition-yet-evading-review exception; (2) John Sarcone's 210-day acting service window under 5 U.S.C. § 3346(a)(1) expired no later than February 2026 with no Senate nomination pending for the Northern District of New York; and (3) the Second Circuit found both that the government forfeited any independent challenge to the disqualification remedy and that Sarcone signed the August 2025 subpoenas solely under the title "Acting U.S. Attorney" without invoking his separate Special Attorney or First Assistant delegations.

A minority plenary-grant pathway (`~21%` of grants) remains live because *Jackson* carries its own threshold disputes — criminal collateral-order appellate jurisdiction under *Flanagan v. United States* and a three-month gap between the expiration of Sigal Chattah's initial 210-day period and George Kelesis's nomination under 5 U.S.C. § 3346(a)(2) — whereas an order quashing grand jury subpoenas in a standalone miscellaneous proceeding is indisputably final and Sarcone issued those subpoenas within the initial 210-day window. Most likely, however, the Court grants *Jackson* alone, holds No. 26-348, reverses or vacates the Ninth Circuit in *Jackson* on at least one of the two Federal Vacancies Reform Act questions, and then enters a standard *Lawrence v. Chater* GVR in No. 26-348 remanding to the Second Circuit for further consideration in light of *Jackson* (leaving respondent's case-specific forfeiture and title-invocation arguments for the Second Circuit on remand). Conversely, if the Court affirms the Ninth Circuit across the board in *Jackson* — or denies review in *Jackson* at the outset — it will deny certiorari in No. 26-348 (`0.42` unconditional denial probability). A per curiam summary reversal without merits briefing is exceedingly unlikely given thorough, published opinions against the government across three circuits.

## Questions Presented and Conditional Merits Scope

If the Court were instead to grant plenary review in No. 26-348 (either consolidated with *Jackson* or as a substitute vehicle), it would take **both Question Presented 1 and Question Presented 2 as framed in the petition** without narrowing or reformulating them, because both questions were squarely decided by the Second Circuit majority and dissenting opinions and together govern whether Sarcone could lawfully oversee the investigations either as an acting officer under 5 U.S.C. § 3345(a)(1) or as a Special Attorney exercising delegated non-exclusive duties under 28 U.S.C. §§ 509, 510, and 515 alongside 5 U.S.C. §§ 3347(a) and 3348(b)(2).

Conditionally, if No. 26-348 (or *Jackson* ahead of a GVR here) reaches a merits decision, a majority of the Court is modestly likelier than not to disturb the judgment below on textualist grounds: either holding under § 3345(a)(1) that "the first assistant to the office of such officer" encompasses post-vacancy first assistants because Congress included express pre-vacancy incumbency requirements in §§ 3345(a)(3)(A) and 3345(b)(1)(A) while omitting one from § 3345(a)(1), or holding on Question 2 (in alignment with *Arthrex, Inc. v. Smith & Nephew, Inc.*) that §§ 3347(a) and 3348 bar only the unconfirmed performance of exclusive, non-delegable PAS duties rather than the Attorney General's statutory delegation of non-exclusive litigation duties under 28 U.S.C. §§ 510 and 515.

## Separate Writings on the Disposition Order

Conditional on a denial of certiorari, a noted dissent or statement respecting the denial is relatively unlikely (`P = 0.09`). Nearly half of the denial probability mass arises after a full merits decision in *Jackson* affirming the Ninth Circuit; on a post-merits cleanup order list, Justices who dissented on the merits in *Jackson* almost never author a redundant separate writing on held companion denials. If, however, the Court denies certiorari in both *Jackson* and No. 26-348 at the threshold stage, there is a meaningful chance that Justice Alito or Justice Thomas (potentially joined by Justice Gorsuch or Justice Kavanaugh) authors a dissent from or statement respecting the denial emphasizing the invalidation of 25 years of Executive Branch practice across multiple U.S. Attorney's Offices. Conversely, on a post-*Jackson* GVR order, there is a small chance Justice Jackson or Justice Sotomayor dissents on the ground that the Second Circuit's forfeiture and subpoena-signature holdings independently support the judgment below.
