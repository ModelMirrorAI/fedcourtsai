## Why I Adjust Downward from `0.731` / `0.689` to `0.56` (`gvr` Plurality)

Anchoring directly on `0.731` (or `0.689`) would be a structural mistake for a **hold petition**. A lead Solicitor General petition seeking plenary review needs to clear only **one** hurdle: convincing four Justices to grant certiorari. A hold petition typically must clear **two** hurdles to resolve in the grant family (`granted` or `gvr`): (1) the Court must grant review in the lead vehicle (*Jackson*, No. 26-304), **and** (2) either the Court consolidates the companion petition for plenary review or the United States must prevail on the merits in *Jackson* so that the held petition receives a GVR rather than a post-decision denial. Corpus priors of Solicitor General hold petitions cited in `cert-reply.txt` (at 2) illustrate the second-stage denial channel directly: when the United States lost *United States v. Hemani* in June 2026 and *United States v. Davis* in June 2019, its held companion petitions — *United States v. Daniels* (`24-1248`), *United States v. Sam* (`24-1249`), and *United States v. Salas* (`18-428`) — all resolved as `denied`. Conversely, when a lead vehicle disturbs the reasoning below (as in *NRC v. Fasken Land and Minerals*, `23-1341`), the held petition resolves as `gvr`.

I decompose the joint outcome space across three nodes:

1. **Node 1 — Does the Court grant certiorari on the FVRA questions (`0.78` yes vs. `0.22` threshold denial of both petitions)?**
   *Jackson* is a strong Solicitor General plenary candidate: three Courts of Appeals (Second, Third, and Ninth) have invalidated high-profile Acting U.S. Attorney and Special Attorney designations across multiple districts, upending a 25-year bipartisan Executive Branch, OLC (2001), and GAO (2001) understanding of 5 U.S.C. § 3345(a)(1) on Question 1, and openly splitting with the Federal Circuit's published decision in *Arthrex, Inc. v. Smith & Nephew, Inc.*, 35 F.4th 1328 (Fed. Cir. 2022), on Question 2 (5 U.S.C. §§ 3347(a), 3348(b)(2)). Judge Park's thorough Second Circuit dissent underscores the textualist appeal of review. I retain a `0.22` probability of outright cert denial in both cases because: (a) the circuits are 0–3 against the government on Question 1 (and DOJ declined to seek certiorari from the Third Circuit's December 2025 decision in *United States v. Giraud*); (b) *NLRB v. SW General, Inc.*, 580 U.S. 288 (2017), enforced FVRA limits strictly notwithstanding longstanding executive practice; (c) *Jackson* carries disputed criminal collateral-order jurisdiction (*Flanagan*) and § 3346(a)(2) tolling-gap issues; and (d) neither docket drew cert-stage amicus briefs.

2. **Node 2 — Given review is granted (`0.78` mass), plenary grant/consolidation (`0.12`) vs. hold (`0.66`)?**
   When the United States is petitioner in both cases, the Court usually honors the Solicitor General's vehicle selection (`~85%` conditional rate). As `cert-reply.txt` (at 3–5) details, No. 26-348 has serious vehicle flaws of its own: expired grand jury subpoenas, expiration of Sarcone's 210-day FVRA window without any pending N.D.N.Y. nomination, a Second Circuit forfeiture holding on the disqualification remedy, and an alternative ground that Sarcone signed the subpoenas solely as "Acting U.S. Attorney." Still, because *Jackson*'s criminal interlocutory appeal raises a real *Flanagan* jurisdictional risk whereas No. 26-348 arises from a final order quashing grand jury subpoenas in a standalone miscellaneous docket (`25 Misc. 19`), and both respondents urged consolidation if cert is granted, I assign `0.12` unconditional probability (`0.11` initial consolidation/vehicle selection plus `0.01` post-hold elevation if *Jackson* hits a jurisdictional/mootness snag) to plenary `granted`.

3. **Node 3 — Given No. 26-348 is held for *Jackson* (`0.66` mass), how does the hold resolve?**
   - **United States wins *Jackson* on QP 1 and/or QP 2 -> `gvr` (`0.44` total):** On the current Court, Judge Park's textualist negative-implication argument on § 3345(a)(1) ("first assistant to the office," contrasted with express 90-day pre-vacancy incumbency limits in §§ 3345(a)(3)(A) and 3345(b)(1)(A)) and the statutory delegation structure on QP 2 (28 U.S.C. §§ 509, 510, 515(a) + 5 U.S.C. § 3348(b)(2) + *Arthrex*) give the government roughly a two-thirds conditional chance (`~66%`) of prevailing on at least one question in *Jackson*. Chater* GVR roughly four-fifths of the time (`0.09` GVR) so the Second Circuit can reassess ongoing disqualification and its alternative subpoena-title remarks, and a denial roughly one-fifth of the time (`0.02` denied) where respondent convinces the Court that forfeiture and the subpoena signature independently sustain the judgment.
