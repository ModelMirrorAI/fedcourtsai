A ruling in *Jackson* could unsettle either of the Second Circuit’s grounds. Section 3345(a)(1) assigns acting service to the first assistant “to the office.” Judge Park’s dissent reads that language to include someone who becomes first assistant during the vacancy. On the second question, he reads § 3347 to regulate acting appointments while permitting delegation of duties that other officials may lawfully perform. Either reading could change the analysis of Sarcone’s authority when he obtained the subpoenas alone. The scale of the effect on U.S. Attorneys’ offices and the divided panel support review of the common questions. Against that, the Second, Third and Ninth Circuits agree on succession. New York makes a substantial case that the Federal Circuit’s *Arthrex* decision concerned delegation of one patent rehearing function and left open whether a single person can receive an entire office’s delegable powers. A denial in *Jackson*, or a decision sustaining both restrictions, would make denial here likely. A hold itself settles nothing. Two held government petitions cited in the reply, *Sam* and *Daniels*, ended in denials. (Questions presented, petition 9–12, opposition 25–28, decision below and reply 2.)
