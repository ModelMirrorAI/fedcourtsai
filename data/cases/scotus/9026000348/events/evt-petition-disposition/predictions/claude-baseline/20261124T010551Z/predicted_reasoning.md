# Forecast: United States v. Office of the New York State Attorney General, No. 26-348

**State forecast from.** The docket shows one distribution (for the Conference of December 4, 2026), no relist, no CVSG, no amicus briefs. The government's companion petition on the identical questions, United States v. Jackson, No. 26-304 (CA9), is distributed for the same conference.

**What the Court does at the December 4 conference.** The Court will not act on this petition on its own. It travels with Jackson. I expect Jackson to be relisted once and then granted (about four in five), and this petition to be **held** for Jackson rather than granted and consolidated: the Solicitor General asks only for a hold, the two cases present identical pure questions of statutory interpretation, and this case carries a grand-jury mootness wrinkle the Court has no reason to take on. A grant-and-consolidate, which respondent asks for in the alternative, is the minority outcome (roughly 15% conditional on a Jackson grant).

**Further distributions.** Yes, more likely than not (about 0.77). A held petition is redistributed after the lead case is decided; if instead the Court considers consolidating, the petition would be relisted alongside Jackson. Only a same-day denial of both petitions on the first conference leaves the count at one.

**CVSG.** None. The United States is the petitioner.

**Final disposition.** This petition is most likely resolved in the cert order following the Jackson decision, near the end of OT2026 (June–July 2027):
- If the government prevails in Jackson on either question (about even odds), the Court grants, vacates and remands here (GVR).
- If the Court affirms in Jackson, or denies Jackson outright, this petition is denied. That combined path is slightly the more likely one, so the single most probable terminal label is **denied**, with P(any grant) = 0.47.

**Summary route.** Conditional on any grant, the grant is far more likely a GVR in light of Jackson than plenary review (about 0.74).

**Question presented, if taken for plenary review.** Both QPs as the government wrote them, consolidated with Jackson; the Court might add a mootness question given the discharged grand jury and the lapse of Mr. Sarcone's acting tenure.

**Separate writings.** If denied after a Jackson affirmance, no writing on this order. A noted dissent or statement is plausible only if the Court denies both petitions at the cert stage (Justices Thomas, Alito, Gorsuch or Kavanaugh being the likeliest to note they would grant); overall about 0.08 conditional on denial.

**If decided on the merits (in Jackson).** A close case. The likeliest government win is on the delegation question (Section 3347(a) does not bar delegating delegable, non-exclusive duties), which carries an acknowledged conflict with the Federal Circuit; the first-assistant question is the harder one for the government after NLRB v. SW General.
