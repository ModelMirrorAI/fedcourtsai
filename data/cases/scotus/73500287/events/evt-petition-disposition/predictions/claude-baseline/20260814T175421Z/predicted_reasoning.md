# Forecast: Endure Industries, Inc. v. Vizient, Inc. (No. 25-1385)

**State I am forecasting from.** The docket shows two distributions as frozen in
my cell's context, but only one is the petition's: the May 26, 2026 distribution
(conference of 6/11/2026) was of the sealing motion 25M86, and the petition
itself was distributed on June 24, 2026 for the long conference of **September
28, 2026**, which has not yet been held. Respondent Vizient waived its response
on June 22, 2026. One amicus brief (Elizabeth H. Rader, a solo practitioner) was
filed July 16 and distributed. No CVSG, no request for a response.

**Disposition.** The petition will be **denied**, most likely in the order list
following the September 28 long conference or within one or two conferences
after it. P(any grant) = 0.07.

**Response and relists.** The live procedural question is whether the Court
calls for a response to the waived BIO — the necessary first step on any grant
path here, since the Court does not grant against a waiver. I put the chance of
a call for a response at roughly 25–30%: the petition is paid, competently
drafted, presses an express refusal by the Fifth Circuit to follow the D.C.
Circuit, and has drawn one (lightweight) amicus — the profile that gets a CFR —
but the split it asserts is soft (see below). Combining a CFR (which forces a
redistribution) with the smaller chance of a plain relist, I put P(at least one
more distribution beyond the two my context froze) at **0.32**. If no response
is requested at or before the long conference, expect a straight denial without
relisting.

**CVSG.** Unlikely — 0.05. Antitrust market definition is the classic CVSG
subject and the Solicitor General has views on the 2023 Merger Guidelines'
practical-indicia approach, so the tail is fatter than the ~1% docket-wide rate;
but a private treble-damages suit at summary judgment, with a waived response
and no circuit dissent, will more likely die at the long conference before any
invitation issues.

**Which question, if taken.** QP1 — whether evidence that some customers can
shift purchases out of a proposed submarket conclusively defeats it despite
other practical-indicia evidence — as written or lightly reformulated around
"whether cross-shopping evidence is dispositive of market definition." QP2 (the
intermediary-controlled-channel market) would be left behind or folded in; it is
the weaker, more Kodak/Newcal-dependent question.

**Summary disposition.** No. There is no intervening decision of this Court for
a GVR to ride on, and a fact-heavy summary-judgment affirmance is not
summary-reversal material.

**Separate writings on denial.** Expect none. The likeliest tail is a short
statement or dissent from denial by Justice Gorsuch or Justice Kavanaugh —
both have engaged with market-definition doctrine (Kavanaugh wrote separately
in Whole Foods as a circuit judge, arguing Brown Shoe's indicia should be
retired) — but statements in private antitrust denials are rare; I put any
separate writing at under 10%.

**If granted** (conditional): the likely ground would be a holding that Brown
Shoe's practical indicia remain part of the market-definition inquiry and that
marginal-customer substitution evidence is probative but not conclusive at
summary judgment — a vacate-and-remand rather than an endorsement of Endure's
proposed submarkets, which the petition itself does not ask the Court to bless.
