# Forecast — Endure Industries, Inc. v. Vizient, Inc. (No. 25-1385)

**Bottom line: the Court denies certiorari, most likely without relisting and
without separate writing, at or shortly after the September 28, 2026
conference.**

The docket as I see it: the petition was filed May 13, 2026, respondents
waived their response on June 22, and the petition was distributed once — for
the long conference of September 28, 2026, which has not yet been held. No
conference has considered the petition yet, no response has been called for,
and no CVSG is on the docket. The frozen context records two distributions,
but one of those is the May 26 distribution of the sealing motion (25M86),
not of the petition itself; the petition sits at its first distribution,
never relisted. That zero-conference, waived-response state is what I am
forecasting from.

**Relists.** The most likely single path is a straight denial on the
long-conference order list. The live alternative is a call for a response:
the QPs are colorably split-framed and the Court will not grant against a
waiver without first directing Vizient to respond, so any Justice's interest
surfaces first as a CFR, which redistributes the petition. I put the chance
of at least one further distribution — a CFR-and-redistribution or a relist
out of the September 28 conference — at about 30%. If a CFR comes, expect it
at or just before that conference, with a denial or (less likely) a grant
following in late 2026 or early 2027 after the BIO and one or two relists.

**CVSG.** Unlikely (~4%). This is a private Sherman Act suit; the federal
government is not a party and no statutory scheme it administers is
construed. The petition's reliance on the 2023 Merger Guidelines gives the
SG a stake in market-definition doctrine, and antitrust CVSGs do happen, but
the Court rarely invites the SG where it has not even called for a response.

**Which question, if taken.** QP1 — whether marginal cross-shopping evidence
conclusively defeats a proposed submarket supported by other Brown Shoe
indicia — is the granted question, likely reformulated to ask directly
whether Brown Shoe's practical-indicia framework survives as an independent
basis for market definition. QP2 (the intermediary-controlled channel
market) is a fact-dressed application of QP1 and would be left behind. Note
the grant risk runs both ways doctrinally: then-Judge Kavanaugh's Whole
Foods dissent argued Brown Shoe's indicia should no longer govern, so a
grant could as easily be to cut back Brown Shoe as to enforce it.

**Summary disposition.** Very unlikely conditional on a grant (~3%). There
is no intervening decision of this Court to GVR against, and a fact-heavy
summary-judgment affirmance in a private antitrust case is not a
summary-reversal shape. A grant here means plenary review.

**Dissent from denial.** Expect none (~5% conditional on denial). Market
definition is a live academic controversy but not one that has drawn
denial-stage writings; if anyone wrote, Justice Kavanaugh (who has engaged
Brown Shoe's status directly) or Justice Gorsuch (who writes on antitrust)
would be the candidates, but a silent denial is the strong default.
