# Forecast: Endure Industries, Inc. v. Vizient, Inc. (No. 25-1385)

**Disposition.** The Court will deny the petition at or shortly after the
September 28, 2026 conference, without calling for a response. P(any grant)
= 0.015.

**Relists.** The docket shows **one** distribution (June 24, 2026, for the
September 28 conference) and no relist; the respondent waived its response on
June 22. I forecast **no further distribution** (P(at least one more) = 0.10).
The only likely routes to a second distribution are a call for a response in
the ten days remaining before the conference, which would pull the petition
off the September 28 list and redistribute it once the brief in opposition
arrives, or a one-conference relist to hold for a pending case or to let a
Justice write. Neither is probable on this record.

**CVSG.** None (P = 0.01). No federal party or program is implicated; the
dispute is a private supplier's monopolization claim against a group
purchasing organization, and the Court would call for a response from the
respondent long before it invited the Solicitor General.

**Which question, if any.** If the Court took the case it would take Question
1 as written, the general question whether marginal cross-shopping evidence
can be dispositive against a proposed submarket supported by Brown Shoe
practical indicia. It would leave Question 2 behind: the Fifth Circuit
treated the "Vizient DMS Market" as a disfavored single-brand market that
Endure widened on appeal, and that question is too entangled with the record
to be a clean vehicle.

**Summary disposition.** Conditional on a grant, plenary review is the likely
route rather than a GVR or summary reversal (P(cert-order disposition | grant)
= 0.15). I know of no intervening decision of this Court on market definition
that a GVR could send the case back "in light of," and the petition does not
ask for one.

**Dissent from denial.** None expected (P = 0.02). No Justice has recently
signalled interest in revisiting Brown Shoe's submarket indicia in a private
case; the one Justice on record in this area, then-Judge Kavanaugh's Whole
Foods dissent, argued for abandoning the indicia rather than for the
petitioner's broader use of them.
