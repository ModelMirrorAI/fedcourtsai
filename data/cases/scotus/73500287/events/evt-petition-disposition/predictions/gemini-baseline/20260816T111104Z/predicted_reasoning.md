The petition is currently distributed for the September 28, 2026 conference, representing its first conference on the merits (the earlier distribution was for a sealing motion). Expect the Court to deny the petition without further relists. 

Because the respondent waived its right to respond and the Court has not called for a response, an outright grant is highly unlikely. The Court is also unlikely to call for the views of the Solicitor General (CVSG); while antitrust market definition can implicate federal enforcement interests, a fact-bound dispute over the application of *Brown Shoe* submarket factors to a specific GPO's purchasing channels typically does not warrant SG input.

If the Court were to grant review, it would likely take up Question 1, addressing the legal standard for submarket definition in the presence of cross-shopping, and leave Question 2 behind as too closely tied to the specific intermediary-controlled channel in this case. A summary disposition route (like a GVR) is unlikely given the absence of a recent intervening antitrust decision from the Court. Should the Court deny the petition as expected, anticipate no dissent or separate statement respecting the denial.
