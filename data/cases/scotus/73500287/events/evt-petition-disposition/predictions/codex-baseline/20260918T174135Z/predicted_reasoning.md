# Forecast of the Court's action

The Court will most likely **deny certiorari**, without an explanatory opinion, following the September 28, 2026 conference, with the disposing order most likely in late September or early October. The probability of any grant is **3.5%**. This is a forecast of petition disposition, not a claim that the Fifth Circuit's antitrust analysis is correct.

The record contains **one petition distribution and no relist**. The separate sealing-motion distribution does not count. I expect **zero additional petition distributions** before disposition. I assign **15%** to at least one further distribution; if that branch occurs, one or two additional distributions are the most likely extension, potentially after a call for a response. A months-long hold is not the central forecast.

I expect **no call for the Solicitor General's views**; the probability of a new CVSG is **1.5%**. Were one issued, the first few consideration cycles after the September conference are the plausible window, and disposition would then likely be delayed substantially. There is no CVSG already on this record.

If the Court grants review, the most likely question is a narrowed version of **QP 1**: whether evidence of purchases outside a proposed submarket categorically defeats a customer- or channel-defined submarket despite evidence of distinct competitive constraints. QP 2's intermediary-controlled market would likely be considered as the application of that question rather than as a freestanding rule validating any market drawn around an intermediary's customers. This conditional question forecast does not predict a merits result.

**Plenary review is more likely than a summary order on the grant branch.** I assign **8% conditional on a grant** to disposition in the cert order itself. No intervening controlling decision is identified in the supplied materials that would make a GVR the natural route; resolving the asserted doctrinal conflict would ordinarily call for fuller briefing and argument.

On the denial branch, I expect **no noted dissent or statement respecting denial**, assigning **2.5% conditional on denial** to the existence of any such writing. I do not forecast a named author or infer how individual Justices would vote. The likely denial reflects a judgment that this record and the asserted conflict do not warrant plenary review, not an announced resolution of the market-definition questions.
