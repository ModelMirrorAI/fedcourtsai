# Forecast of the Court's disposition

The Court will likely deny the petition after its first petition conference without increasing the harness-frozen distribution count beyond 2. The record's other counted distribution concerns the separate sealing motion, not an earlier conference on the petition. A request for a response is possible after respondents' waiver, but the likelier course is no response request, no further distribution, and no CVSG.

The petition frames an important antitrust market-definition issue, but the Court is unlikely to use this case to resolve it. The Fifth Circuit treated the core-customer theory as forfeited and not presented below, and it identified evidentiary and framing defects in the proposed markets. Those independent vehicle problems allow denial without resolving whether the Fifth Circuit's emphasis on cross-shopping conflicts with *Whole Foods* or *Newcal*.

If review is granted, the Court would most likely take a narrowed version of Question 1: whether evidence of some customer substitution categorically forecloses a proposed submarket when other record evidence supports a commercially distinct core customer group. It would likely leave Question 2's intermediary-controlled-channel formulation aside because of the disputed definition and record-specific lock-in problems.

A plenary grant would be much more likely than a summary disposition. No intervening decision supplies a natural GVR basis, and the claimed conflict over the governing market-definition standard would require briefing and argument. If certiorari is denied, no Justice is likely to publish a dissent from denial or a statement respecting the denial.
