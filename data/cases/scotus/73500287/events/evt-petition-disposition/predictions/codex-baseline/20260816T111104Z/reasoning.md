# Rationale

I assign a 4% probability of any grant and predict denial. The provisioned record is a forward-mode, paid cert petition from the Fifth Circuit. It includes the complete 29-page public petition and extracted questions presented, but no brief in opposition because respondents waived their right to respond. One individual amicus brief appears on the docket, and no CVSG appears.

The committed statpack's applicable `sal-v3` risk-set anchor for an `elevated` petition is 20.27%: 693 grant-family dispositions among 3,419 weighted resolved petitions across Terms 2017–2024, strictly before this petition's Term 2025. I do not treat that elevated figure as substantively representative here. The frozen `distribution_count` is 2, but the snapshot shows one distribution of the separate motion to seal and one first distribution of the cert petition. It shows no petition relist. The count therefore appears to have promoted the petition into the elevated band based on a procedural-motion conference. I retain the harness-frozen band and count because they are authoritative scoring inputs, disclose the mismatch in `flags.json`, and adjust toward the prior-Term paid baseline risk-set rate of 6.67%. That leakage-safe comparator covers the whole paid segment at its weakest band, including petitions that later strengthen, and does not assume this petition will end without a relist. The broader statpack cuts are consistent with caution: the modern-cert grant-family rate is 2.93%, the Fifth Circuit cut is 3.87%, and the no-CVSG paid scored-segment cut is 6.19%.

The petition presents a potentially important antitrust question about whether cross-shopping by marginal customers can defeat a targeted-customer or channel submarket despite other evidence of commercial realities. Its best grant feature is the Fifth Circuit's published treatment of cross-shopping and its express discussion of the D.C. Circuit's *Whole Foods* approach. The question could recur in markets organized through purchasing intermediaries.

The vehicle problems outweigh that feature. The pre-decision Fifth Circuit opinion retrieved through CourtListener says the core-customer theory did not appear in the operative complaint, was not decided below, and was forfeited on appeal. It also describes the second proposed market as having shifted on appeal and faults Endure for not tying specific record evidence to lock-in or switching costs. Those preservation and record-specific grounds give the Court poor control over the broad questions presented and make the asserted circuit conflict less clean than the petition suggests. The absence of a BIO leaves the provisioned merits presentation one-sided, and the public petition's sealed-record redactions limit an independent vehicle assessment. Respondents' waiver and the lack of institutional amici or a government interest signal further reduce my estimate to 4%.

The frozen count is 2, so the 5% relist-increment claim is the probability of a later distribution that raises that count beyond 2, not a claim about whether the petition has already been relisted. A CVSG is possible because the questions concern antitrust enforcement doctrine, but it is unlikely in this private, procedurally compromised case; I assign 1%. Conditional on a grant, the petition seeks clarification of a legal standard and presents no intervening authority that would naturally support a GVR, so I assign only 4% to a cert-order summary route. A denial is very unlikely to draw a separate statement or dissent, also 1%.

My principal uncertainty is whether the Court will call for a response despite the waiver. Such a request would be a meaningful sign of interest and could lead to a genuine relist, but neither appears in the frozen record.
