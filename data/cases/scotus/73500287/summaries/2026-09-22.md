## What happened

Endure Industries sells disposable medical supplies to hospitals. It sued Vizient, a large group purchasing organization that negotiates supply contracts for hospitals, claiming Vizient's contracting and rebate practices violated antitrust law by locking hospitals into buying through Vizient's system. To win, Endure had to define the relevant market its claims were based on. The district court ruled against Endure, holding that its proposed markets failed as a matter of law because some hospitals still bought supplies outside Vizient's system. The Fifth Circuit affirmed, treating that outside buying as decisive and declining to follow an approach used by other appeals courts that gives more weight to evidence about a distinct group of customers facing special constraints.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked to decide how much weight evidence of some customers shopping elsewhere should get when someone tries to define a narrower market within a bigger one. Specifically, the case asks whether such evidence automatically defeats a proposed narrower market, or whether courts must also weigh other evidence showing that a particular group of customers faces distinct pricing, contract terms, and switching costs. It also asks whether a narrower market can be built around sales that happen to run through one purchasing organization's channel, even though multiple suppliers compete within that channel.
