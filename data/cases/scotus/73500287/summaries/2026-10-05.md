## In brief

The Supreme Court declined to hear Endure Industries' bid to revive its antitrust suit against Vizient over how courts define a relevant market.

## What happened

Endure, a medical-supply seller, sued Vizient, a group purchasing organization, claiming Vizient's contracting and rebate practices violated antitrust law. Endure argued the relevant market should be defined narrowly around hospitals buying through Vizient's purchasing channel. The district court granted summary judgment to Vizient, ruling Endure's proposed markets failed as a matter of law because some hospitals bought supplies outside that channel. The Fifth Circuit affirmed, treating evidence of this outside purchasing as decisive and declining to adopt a different approach used by other appeals courts.

## What the Court is being asked

The case asks whether a court can reject a proposed narrower market just because some customers can and do shop elsewhere, even when other evidence shows a distinct group of customers faces unique competitive constraints within that channel. It also asks whether such a market can be defined around sales made through a particular purchasing channel, rather than limited to one seller's branded products.

## Where it stands

The petition was distributed for conference, the respondent waived its right to respond, and an amicus brief was filed. The Court denied the petition on October 5, 2026.

## What each outcome would mean

Endure, which lost in the lower courts, asked the Court to take up the case. The denial leaves the Fifth Circuit's ruling in place, meaning Endure's antitrust claims against Vizient remain rejected because the proposed markets were found legally deficient. The denial does not mean the Supreme Court agreed with the Fifth Circuit's reasoning; it simply leaves that decision as the final word in this case, without resolving the broader disagreement among appeals courts over how to weigh such evidence.
