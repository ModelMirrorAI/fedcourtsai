## In brief

The Supreme Court declined to hear Endure Industries' bid to revive its antitrust lawsuit against Vizient over how courts define a relevant market.

## What happened

Endure Industries, a medical-supply seller, sued Vizient and related entities, a large group purchasing organization, claiming antitrust violations over how Vizient structured contracts for hospitals buying disposable medical supplies. Endure argued two specific submarkets existed that Vizient dominated unfairly. The district court granted summary judgment to Vizient, ruling Endure's proposed markets failed as a matter of law because some customers bought supplies outside those channels. The Fifth Circuit affirmed, rejecting an approach other appeals courts use that weighs evidence of a core customer group's unique constraints even when some customers can shop elsewhere.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court was asked whether a proposed slice of a market must be thrown out simply because some customers can shift purchases elsewhere, even when other evidence shows a distinct group of customers faces unique competitive pressures. It was also asked whether such a market can be defined around sales made through a particular purchasing arrangement, where multiple suppliers compete rather than just one.

## Where it stands

The petition was distributed for conference, the respondent waived its right to respond, and an amicus brief was filed. The Court denied the petition.

## What each outcome would mean

Endure asked the Court to take up its case. By denying the petition, the Court leaves the Fifth Circuit's ruling in place, meaning Vizient's win on summary judgment stands and Endure's lawsuit remains dismissed. The denial does not mean the Court agreed with the Fifth Circuit's reasoning on market definition; it simply leaves that lower court decision as the final word in this case, without resolving the disagreement among appeals courts that Endure's petition described.
