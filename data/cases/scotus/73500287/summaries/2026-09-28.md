## In brief

A medical supply seller asks the Court to revisit how courts should define antitrust markets when some customers can buy through other channels.

## What happened

Endure Industries sued Vizient, a large group purchasing organization, and related entities, claiming they used their market power to block Endure from selling disposable medical supplies to hospitals. Endure proposed two ways of defining the relevant market, tied to hospitals that buy through Vizient's purchasing arrangements. The district court granted summary judgment to Vizient, ruling that Endure's proposed markets failed as a matter of law because some hospitals bought supplies outside those channels. The Fifth Circuit affirmed, agreeing that evidence of this cross-shopping defeated Endure's market definitions, and declined to follow an approach used by the D.C. Circuit that treats such evidence as relevant but not automatically decisive.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked whether a court must reject a proposed market definition simply because some customers can also buy elsewhere, even when other evidence shows a distinct group of customers faces unique competitive constraints. The Court is also asked whether a market can be defined around sales made through a particular purchasing channel controlled by an intermediary, where multiple suppliers compete within that channel rather than just one seller.

## Where it stands

The petition was distributed for the Court's September 28, 2026 conference after Vizient waived its right to respond and an amicus brief was filed supporting Endure. The docket does not yet show any action from that conference.

## What each outcome would mean

Endure asked the Court to take up the case after losing in the lower courts. If the Court grants review, it would hear full arguments and decide how courts should weigh cross-shopping evidence against evidence of distinct customer groups, without yet deciding who wins. If the Court denies review, the Fifth Circuit's ruling against Endure stays in place, meaning Endure's antitrust claims against Vizient would remain rejected on market-definition grounds. A denial would not mean the Court agrees the Fifth Circuit was right.
