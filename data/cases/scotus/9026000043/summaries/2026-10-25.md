## In brief

Immigration detainees ask the Court to decide a statutory question about their detention, after the Fifth Circuit ruled against them.

## What happened

Victor Buenrostro-Mendez and other petitioners, represented by the ACLU and the Northwest Immigrant Rights Project, were held by immigration authorities and are challenging aspects of their detention. Their case reached the United States Court of Appeals for the Fifth Circuit, which issued a decision against them on February 6, 2026, and denied rehearing on April 9, 2026. They then asked the Supreme Court to review that decision. The respondents are Martin Frink, a warden, and federal officials including the Attorney General, represented by the Solicitor General.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked to decide a question about how a federal statute governing immigration detention should be interpreted or applied. A September 25 letter from petitioners' counsel notes that this case presents a statutory issue distinct from constitutional questions raised in related cases, and urges that if the Court takes up a related matter, it should grant review only on the statutory issue raised here and in another related case.

## Where it stands

The petition has been fully briefed, including a waiver of response from the warden, a brief from federal respondents, and a reply from petitioners. It was distributed for the Court's September 28, 2026 conference, rescheduled once, then distributed again for the same conference date; the docket shows no decision yet on whether the Court will hear the case.

## What each outcome would mean

Buenrostro-Mendez and the other detainees are asking the Court to take up their case. If the Court grants review, it will hear full arguments and decide the statutory question itself, without yet deciding who wins. If the Court denies review, the Fifth Circuit's decision against the petitioners stays in place, and the case ends there. A denial would not mean the Court agrees with the lower court's reasoning, only that the decision stands.
