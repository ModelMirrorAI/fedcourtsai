# Forecast: what the Court will do with No. 26-43, Buenrostro-Mendez v. Blanche

**State forecast from.** The docket shows one distribution (for the September 28, 2026 long conference, rescheduled once and redistributed for the same conference), no CVSG, a brief from the federal respondents, a reply, and two petitioner letters (September 14 and September 25, the latter captioned "re DC"). The snapshot was generated October 2, 2026, before the October 5 order list.

**The companion grant controls the path.** On October 1, 2026 the Court granted the government's petition in *Rhoney v. Barbosa da Cunha*, No. 26-104 (CA2), which presents the same question from the other side of the split and is litigated by the same counsel on both sides. This petition was not granted alongside it. The most likely treatment is therefore a **hold**: the petition sits without a docket entry until *Rhoney* is decided (expected by late June 2027), is then redistributed for a conference, and is disposed of in light of the merits decision.

- **Relists.** At least one further distribution is very likely (about 0.88): either a relist on the October 5 order list, or the redistribution that precedes the disposition of a held petition after *Rhoney* is decided. The alternative is an outright denial on October 5 with no further distribution, which I put near 0.10.
- **CVSG.** None. The Solicitor General already represents the federal respondents; a call for the government's views cannot issue.
- **Disposition.** Roughly 0.47 that the petition is granted in some form. The dominant grant route is a **GVR** in light of *Rhoney* if the Court rules for the noncitizens there (the Fifth Circuit ruled for the government, so a favorable merits decision would vacate its judgment). If the Court rules for the government in *Rhoney*, expect a quiet denial of this petition. A plenary grant with consolidation is unlikely (about 0.07) since the Court declined to take both cases on October 1, though it becomes the replacement vehicle if *Rhoney* falls away (a possibility the Court has just lived through in *Genalo v. Black*, dismissed per stipulation in September 2026).
- **Summary disposition route, conditional on a grant.** About 0.85 that any grant is a GVR rather than plenary review.
- **Which question, if taken plenarily.** The single statutory question as framed by the petition: whether a noncitizen present without admission and arrested in the interior is detained under section 1225(b)(2)(A) or section 1226(a). The Court would not take any Fifth-Circuit-specific habeas-scope issue.
- **Dissent from denial.** Unlikely (about 0.08). A denial here would almost certainly follow a government win in *Rhoney*, and held petitions denied after a merits decision rarely draw a separate writing. If one appears, it would be a statement by Justice Sotomayor or Justice Jackson about the detention consequences, not a dissent from the Court's refusal to hear the case.
- **Timing.** No disposition of this petition before the *Rhoney* decision, so the expected resolution window is June 2027, with the redistribution appearing in the days before the final conferences of the Term.

If the Court were to grant plenary review, it would consolidate with *Rhoney* and the merits forecast would be that of the lead case: the Court is closely divided on the textual question, with the deeming provision in section 1225(a)(1) favoring the government and the "seeking admission" language, thirty years of administrative practice, and *Jennings*'s description of the scheme favoring the noncitizens.
