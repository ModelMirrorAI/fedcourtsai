## In brief

Southern Company asks the Court to review a ruling that pension plans must use reasonable assumptions when calculating certain retirement benefits under federal law.

## What happened

Two retirees, William Drummond and Richard Odom, sued Southern Company Services, its pension plan, and the plan's benefits committee, claiming the plan used outdated assumptions about how long retirees and spouses would live when converting their pensions into joint-and-survivor annuities, a payment option covering both a retiree and a surviving spouse. They argued this shortchanged them under a federal pension law. A district court dismissed the lawsuit, ruling the law does not require any particular assumptions. The Eleventh Circuit reversed, holding the law requires reasonable, realistic assumptions, allowing the retirees' claims to proceed.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked whether a federal pension law's requirement that two retirement payout options be of equal actuarial value also requires the assumptions used in that calculation, such as how long people are expected to live, to be reasonable, or whether plans may use any assumptions they choose as long as they are written down in advance.

## Where it stands

The petition was distributed for the Court's November 6, 2026 conference. After that conference, the Court asked the retirees to file a response, due November 18, 2026, even though they had previously waived their right to respond.

## What each outcome would mean

Southern Company is asking the Court to take up the case. If the Court grants the petition, it would hear the case and decide whether the law requires reasonable actuarial assumptions, which would resolve the dispute on the merits. If the Court denies the petition, the Eleventh Circuit's ruling favoring the retirees would stand, and the lawsuit would continue in the lower courts. The petition also asks the Court to instead hold this case pending its decision on two similar petitions raising the same issue, which would delay any action here until those cases are resolved.
