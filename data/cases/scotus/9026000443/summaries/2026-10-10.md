## In brief

Southern Company asks the Court to review whether a federal pension law requires retirement plans to use "reasonable" actuarial assumptions when calculating survivor benefits.

## What happened

William Drummond and Richard Odom, participants in the Southern Company Pension Plan, sued Southern Company Services, the Plan, and its benefits committee, claiming the Plan used outdated actuarial assumptions when converting single-life annuities into joint-and-survivor annuities for married participants, violating a federal pension law. A federal district court dismissed the claims, ruling the law does not require any particular assumptions. The Eleventh Circuit reversed, holding the law's requirement that the two annuity options be of equal actuarial value also requires the assumptions used to be reasonable. Southern Company then asked the Supreme Court to review that decision.

## What the Court is being asked

The dispute is about a federal pension law requiring that two retirement payout options offered to married workers be of equal financial value. The Court is asked whether that equal-value requirement only means the math must work out evenly under whatever assumptions a plan uses, or whether it also requires the plan's underlying assumptions, such as estimates of how long people will live and future interest rates, to be reasonable and up to date.

## Where it stands

Drummond and Odom waived their right to respond to the petition, and the petition was distributed for the Court's conference on November 6, 2026. No action on it has been recorded since.

## What each outcome would mean

Southern Company, the plan sponsor, asked the Court to take the case after losing in the Eleventh Circuit. If the Court grants the petition, it would hear the case and decide what the pension law's equal-value requirement means, with no outcome assumed in advance. If the Court denies the petition, the Eleventh Circuit's ruling stands, leaving in place its holding that plans must use reasonable actuarial assumptions, allowing Drummond and Odom's claims to continue. The petition also asks the Court to instead hold this case until it rules on two related petitions raising the same question.
