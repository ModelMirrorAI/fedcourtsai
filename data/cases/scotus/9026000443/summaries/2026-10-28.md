## In brief

Southern Company asks the Court to review a ruling that pension plans must use reasonable assumptions when calculating certain retirement benefits under federal law.

## What happened

Two retirees, William Drummond and Richard Odom, sued Southern Company Services, its pension plan, and the plan's benefits committee, claiming the plan used outdated assumptions about life expectancy and interest rates to calculate their retirement payments, reducing what they received. A federal district court dismissed their lawsuit, ruling the relevant pension law does not require plans to use reasonable or current assumptions. The Eleventh Circuit reversed, holding that the law's requirement that two forms of retirement benefit be of equal ("actuarially equivalent") value means plans must use assumptions a reasonable actuary would consider sound. Southern Company then asked the Supreme Court to review that decision.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked whether a federal pension law provision requiring two types of retirement benefit calculations to be of equal value also requires the assumptions used in that calculation to be reasonable, or whether plans may use any assumptions they choose, however outdated, as long as they are written into the plan documents in advance.

## Where it stands

Southern Company filed its petition on September 29, 2026. Drummond waived his right to respond, but the Court later requested a response, due November 18, 2026; the case has not yet been scheduled for the Court to decide whether to hear it.

## What each outcome would mean

Southern Company is asking the Court to take up the case and reverse the Eleventh Circuit's ruling, which favored Drummond and Odom. If the Court denies review, the Eleventh Circuit's decision stands, and the retirees' lawsuit continues in the lower courts under the reasonableness requirement that court adopted. If the Court grants review, it will hear full arguments and decide the legal question itself; a grant alone would not determine which side wins. The petition also asks the Court to hold this case pending related petitions raising the same issue in other cases.
