## In brief

A pension plan sponsor asks the Court to review a ruling that its method for calculating retirement annuities must use reasonable assumptions about life expectancy and interest rates.

## What happened

Two retirees, William Drummond and Richard Odom, sued their former employer, Southern Company Services, its pension plan, and the plan's benefits committee, claiming the plan shortchanged their monthly retirement payments. They argued that converting their standard retirement benefit into a joint payment covering a spouse used mortality and interest-rate assumptions so outdated that the payments fell short of what federal pension law requires. A district court dismissed the lawsuit, ruling the law imposes no reasonableness requirement. The Eleventh Circuit reversed, holding that the law requires reasonable, realistic assumptions, and let the retirees' claims proceed.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked whether a federal pension law's requirement that two retirement payment options be of equal value also requires the assumptions used in that calculation to be reasonable and current, or whether plans may use any assumptions they choose as long as they are written into the plan documents, regardless of how outdated.

## Where it stands

The petition was distributed for the Court's November 6, 2026 conference. On October 19, 2026, after that distribution, the Court asked the retirees to file a response, due November 18, 2026; the respondents had earlier waived their right to respond.

## What each outcome would mean

Southern Company is asking the Court to reverse the Eleventh Circuit's decision, which had sided with the retirees. If the Court grants review, it would hear the case fully and decide the question itself, resolving it for both sides. If it denies review, the Eleventh Circuit's ruling stands, and the retirees' lawsuit continues in the lower courts under that court's reasonableness requirement. The petition also asks the Court to hold this case pending two related petitions raising the same question.
