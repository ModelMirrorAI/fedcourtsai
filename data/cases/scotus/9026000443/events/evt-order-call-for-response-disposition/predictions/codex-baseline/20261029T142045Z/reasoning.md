I read the provisioned `2026-10-19.json` snapshot, the complete petition and questions presented, the document manifest, and relevant portions of the separate appendix, including the Eleventh Circuit's reasoning and disposition. The mode is forward; the baseline has a date cut at October 20, 2026. I did not retrieve this petition's current docket, its outcome, or another cell's predictions.

The October 19 request for a response is real evidence of attention after the respondent waived a response. It is not a grant signal equivalent to a merits vote, a CVSG, or a genuine relist. No brief in opposition is provisioned, consistently with the response deadline still lying ahead of this moment; I do not infer its arguments. The petition is untruncated and text-available. Neither missing future opposition nor the appendix's disclosed cap is treated as evidence favoring petitioners.

## Reasons for moving above the anchor

The Court has affirmatively requested an answer despite a waiver. The petition presents a focused statutory question affecting pension-benefit calculations across multiple plans, and identifies two earlier petitions on the same question: FedEx, No. 26-360, and Bakery, No. 26-387. Its introduction and conclusion expressly seek a hold, followed by consistent treatment, with plenary review as an alternative. Those features create a more credible route to eventual grant-family relief than an ordinary baseline petition. They support increasing 5.1% to 13%, but that increase is judgment rather than an empirically measured response-request multiplier.

The related-petition route also explains the 75% summary-route probability conditional on a grant. It is predominantly a possible future GVR following a lead case, not an expectation of immediate summary reversal. The relationship comes from the petition itself: the snapshot has no Vide links and no related snapshots were staged. I have not verified either related petition's later procedural status and assume no grant in either.

## Reasons denial remains dominant

The petition describes the Sixth and Eleventh Circuits as reaching the same conclusion. A dissent in the Sixth Circuit and disagreements among district judges do not establish a conflict between courts of appeals on this precise question. This lowers the urgency of selecting a merits vehicle. The Eleventh Circuit reversed a pleading-stage dismissal and remanded; no final factual determination of the reasonableness of the challenged assumptions is before the Court. The case also contains distinct nonforfeiture, survivor-coverage-charge, and fiduciary claims beyond the single presented question.

I do not adopt the petition's characterization that the court below relied on statutory purpose alone. The appendix opinion discusses the technical meaning of actuarial equivalence, professional norms, statutory context, and Treasury regulations, and emphasizes a range of permissible reasonable assumptions rather than one mandatory current table. It leaves the actual adequacy of these assumptions for later proceedings. That is a substantive answer to the petition's textual objection, even if the Supreme Court could ultimately prefer the petitioners' reading. Likewise, the recent M&K decision is invoked by both the petition and the opinion below; its existence does not by itself establish a controlling conflict requiring summary relief.

A routine technical denial is unlikely to generate separate writings, hence the 3% conditional denial-writing probability and low unconditional order-writer numbers.
