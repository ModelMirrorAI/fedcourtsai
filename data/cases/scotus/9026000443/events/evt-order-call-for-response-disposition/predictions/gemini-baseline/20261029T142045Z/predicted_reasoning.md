## Question Presented and Conditional Grant Route

- **Question Presented if taken:** If the Court grants review (either by consolidating No. 26-443 with *FedEx*/*Kellogg* or taking it as a vehicle), it would take the single question presented as written: whether 29 U.S.C. § 1055(d)(1)(B)'s requirement that a qualified joint-and-survivor annuity be the "actuarial equivalent" of a single-life annuity requires that the underlying actuarial assumptions be "reasonable," or requires only mathematical equivalence under the plan's stated assumptions.
- **Summary disposition vs. plenary review (`summary-disposition-route` = 0.72 conditional on grant):** Because Petitioners explicitly ask the Court to hold No. 26-443 pending *FedEx* (No. 26-360) and *Kellogg* (No. 26-387), the dominant grant pathway for *this* docket is a post-hold **grant, vacate, and remand (GVR)** in the cert order itself if the Court grants review in *FedEx* or *Kellogg* and reverses or vacates the Sixth Circuit's interpretation of § 1055(d)(1)(B). Standalone or consolidated plenary review in No. 26-443 is a smaller share of the conditional grant probability because *FedEx* and *Kellogg* were filed first and do not carry the vehicle complication that lead plaintiff Drummond abandoned Count I below.
- **Conditional merits ground:** If granted for plenary review (or via the lead Sixth Circuit petitions), the dispute would turn on whether "actuarial equivalent" in 29 U.S.C. § 1055(d)(1)(B) incorporates professional actuarial standards requiring reasonable assumptions—consistent with the Court's discussion of Actuarial Standards of Practice in *M&K Employee Solutions, LLC v. Trustees of the IAM National Pension Fund*, 608 U.S. 264 (2026), and 29 U.S.C. § 1002(27)'s definition of "present value"—or whether *Russello v. United States*, 464 U.S. 16 (1983), forecloses a reasonableness requirement because other ERISA provisions expressly specify "reasonable" actuarial assumptions.
