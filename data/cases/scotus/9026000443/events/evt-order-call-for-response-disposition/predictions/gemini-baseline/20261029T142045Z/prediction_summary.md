The Supreme Court is very likely to deny Southern Company Services' petition for review, with only about a one-in-fourteen (7%) chance of granting it (including a potential grant, vacate, and remand—an order sending the case back to the lower court—if the Court takes up and reverses a pair of earlier companion cases from the Sixth Circuit). The case asks whether the federal pension law (ERISA) requires employers to use reasonable life-expectancy and interest-rate assumptions when converting a retiree's single-person pension into a joint pension that also covers a surviving spouse. Although the Court asked the retirees to file a response after they initially waived one, both federal appeals courts to decide this question (the Sixth and Eleventh Circuits) agreed that reasonable assumptions are required, leaving no disagreement among the lower courts for the Supreme Court to resolve now.
