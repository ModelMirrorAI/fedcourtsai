### Downward Factors (pulling back from ~11% to 7.0%)
1. **Absence of a Circuit Split (2–0 Agreement Between the Sixth and Eleventh Circuits):** As the Eleventh Circuit noted (Pet.App. 20a) and the petition concedes (Pet. 3, 6, 13–14), the Sixth and Eleventh Circuits are the **only** two federal courts of appeals to have decided whether 29 U.S.C. § 1055(d)(1)(B)'s "actuarial equivalent" requirement entails reasonable actuarial assumptions, and **both reached the exact same conclusion**. Moreover, the Eleventh Circuit panel—Judges Rosenbaum, Grant, and Brasher—was unanimous, and denied rehearing without a noted dissent. It is unusual for the Court to grant certiorari on a statutory ERISA question where every circuit to reach the issue agrees.
2. **Recent Supreme Court Precedent Pointing the Same Way:** In *M&K Employee Solutions, LLC v. Trustees of the IAM National Pension Fund*, 608 U.S. 264 (May 21, 2026), the Court itself relied on the Actuarial Standards of Practice (ASOPs) as the specialist reference point for ERISA actuarial provisions and described actuarial assumptions as predictive judgments rather than arbitrary inputs frozen in time (cited at Pet.App. 27a, 31a n.20). Combined with the Treasury Department's 1977 regulation (26 C.F.R. § 1.401(a)-11(b)(2)) and 29 U.S.C. § 1002(27)'s definition of "present value" ("adjusted to reflect anticipated events"), the Court is unlikely to feel an urgent need to intervene before any circuit adopts Petitioners' reading.
3. **Interlocutory Rule 12(b)(6) Posture and Vehicle Imperfections:** The Eleventh Circuit reversed a Rule 12(b)(6) dismissal and remanded for further proceedings, explicitly noting that a developed factual and expert record may show that the Plan's actuarial assumptions fall within the "zone" of reasonable actuarial practice (Pet.App. 31a–32a, 34a, 68a). Furthermore, lead plaintiff Drummond abandoned Count I (§ 1055(d)) after discovering that the 1951 GAM table gave him a higher JSA payout (Pet. 10–11), and Counts II–III in the Eleventh Circuit involved additional issues (nonforfeiture under § 1053(a) and QPSA charges under § 1055(i)) not presented in the single Question Presented.
4. **Explicit Hold Posture:** Petitioners repeatedly ask the Court to **hold** this petition pending *FedEx* (No. 26-360) and *Kellogg* (No. 26-387) and to GVR if the Court grants and reverses in those cases (Pet. 2, 7, 13, 15, 16). Thus, for No. 26-443 to result in a `granted` outcome (including `gvr`), the Court must most likely (a) grant certiorari in *FedEx*/*Kellogg* despite the absence of a circuit split (~10–11% chance) AND (b) reverse or vacate the Sixth Circuit on the merits (~50–55% conditional chance), or else include No. 26-443 in a consolidated grant (~1–2% chance). That places `P(grant)` for No. 26-443 at **0.07**.
