# Predicted course: Southern Company Services v. Drummond, No. 26-443

**Posture the forecast starts from.** Distributed once (for the 11/6/2026
conference); respondents waived; the Court called for a response on
2026-10-19, the same day it called for responses in the two petitions this
one asks to be held for — *FedEx Corp. v. Watt*, No. 26-360, and *BCTGM
Pension Committee v. Reichert*, No. 26-387, both from the Sixth Circuit's
split decision in *Reichert v. Kellogg Co.* No CVSG.

**Expected course.** The response will come in (after an extension, likely
mid-December 2026) and the petition will be redistributed, almost surely
alongside the two Sixth Circuit petitions. I expect the three to travel as a
group: the Court acts on the lead Sixth Circuit vehicle(s) and treats this
Eleventh Circuit petition as a hold, which is what the petitioners themselves
ask for.

- **Most likely single outcome: denial** (about three in four overall). Either
  the Court denies the lead petitions — the two circuits that have ruled agree
  with each other, so there is no square circuit split — and denies this one
  with them, or it grants a lead case, affirms the "reasonable assumptions"
  reading, and then denies this held petition.
- **Grant-family path (about one in four):** the Court grants a Sixth Circuit
  petition (more likely than this one as the lead, since it was filed first
  and carries a dissent below), holds 26-443, and — if it rejects the implied
  reasonableness requirement — GVRs this case in light of that decision. A
  plenary grant here (consolidation, or this as the alternative vehicle) is
  the smaller share of the grant family.
- **Relists:** at least one further distribution is near-certain; if the
  Court grants a companion, this petition will sit through many conferences
  as a hold until the merits decision (likely June 2027 or later).
- **CVSG:** possible but unlikely here (~15%); if the Court wants the
  Government's view it will more likely invite it in a lead Sixth Circuit
  case and hold this one.
- **Summary route, conditional on a grant:** more likely than plenary review
  for this docket specifically (~60%), because a grant-family result here most
  plausibly arrives as a GVR after a companion is decided.
- **If granted (as lead or alongside):** the Court would take the question as
  written — whether § 1055(d)(1)(B)'s "actuarial equivalent" implies that the
  underlying actuarial assumptions be reasonable — with no reformulation.
- **Separate writing on a denial:** not expected. A dissent from denial is
  most conceivable only if the companions are denied outright, from a
  textualist Justice (Thomas, Alito or Gorsuch).
