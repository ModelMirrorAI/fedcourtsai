# Forecast: Southern Company Services, Inc. v. Drummond, No. 26-443

**Disposition: denied, P(any grant) = 0.10.** The grant mass is almost
entirely a *hold-then-GVR* path that runs through the two Sixth Circuit
petitions this one asks to be held behind, *FedEx Corp. v. Watt*, No. 26-360,
and *Bakery, Confectionery, Tobacco Workers & Grain Millers Pension Committee
v. Reichert*, No. 26-387. Those petitions will most likely be denied, and this
one will be denied with them.

**Relists.** The docket shows **one** distribution (for the November 6, 2026
conference), filed September 29, response waived October 2, distributed
October 7. I expect the Court to treat this as a companion petition and act on
it only when it acts on Nos. 26-360 and 26-387. Both lead petitions have
respondent waivers (September 24 and 25) and will reach a conference in the
same late-October / November window, so the modal path is one more
distribution of this docket at most: either the three are denied together on
the November 9 order list with no further distribution, or this one sits a
conference or two and is redistributed once before a joint denial. If the Court
calls for a response in the lead cases, which is the only realistic road to a
grant given that every respondent waived, this petition is held for months and
redistributed once at the end. I put P(at least one further distribution)
at 0.60, and expect the count to end at one or two, not three or more.

**CVSG.** No CVSG on the docket. A call for the Solicitor General's views is
plausible in the *lead* cases, since the Sixth and Eleventh Circuits leaned on
IRS and Treasury interpretations of the parallel Code provisions, but any such
order would issue in No. 26-360 or 26-387, not here. P(CVSG on this docket)
= 0.04.

**Which question, if taken.** The Court would not take this petition as the
vehicle. If it takes the issue at all it takes the Sixth Circuit cases and
decides the question as the petitions frame it: whether section 1055(d)'s
"actuarial equivalent" requirement carries an implicit requirement that the
plan's mortality and interest assumptions be reasonable. This docket's
disposition would then follow the merits result.

**Summary route versus plenary review.** Conditional on any grant here, the
route is a GVR in light of a reversal in the Sixth Circuit cases with
probability about 0.80. The residual is a consolidation grant alongside the
lead petitions, which I think unlikely because the Court would not need an
Eleventh Circuit vehicle for a question the Sixth Circuit cases present on
materially identical reasoning and posture.

**Dissent from denial.** Conditional on denial, I expect no separate writing
attached to this docket number: P = 0.05. If a Justice writes on the
actuarial-equivalence denial at all, the statement would attach to the Sixth
Circuit lead petition, and only occasionally are companion dockets listed in
such a statement.

**If granted on the merits (conditional only).** Should the Court grant the
lead cases, the most likely ground for reversal is the textual one the
petitions press: Congress imposed express reasonableness constraints on
actuarial assumptions elsewhere in ERISA and omitted one in section 1055(d).
I put reversal at a little better than even in that branch, which is why the
GVR share of this docket's grant mass is large but the grant mass itself is
small.
