## What moves the number

- **A concrete companion route raises the grant-family chance.** The QP and petition pages 2–7 and 13–16 identify FedEx v. Watt, No. 26-360, and the Bakery pension committee's Reichert petition, No. 26-387, as presenting the same issue. The requested principal relief is to hold this case and then dispose of it consistently with those proceedings. A later GVR counts as a grant for this forecast even if this docket never receives plenary argument. These companion facts come only from the provisioned petition; their current status was not checked.
- **The demonstrated appellate alignment limits the case for immediate intervention.** The petition describes the Sixth and Eleventh Circuits as reaching the same answer. A divided Sixth Circuit panel and disagreement among judges are not evidence of an opposing circuit holding. The supplied materials therefore do not establish a square circuit conflict. The petition's textualist critique and assertions of nationwide importance are reasons to consider review, not proof that review is likely.
- **The issue is recurring and legally focused.** The QP concerns a statutory constraint on actuarial assumptions, with consequences for survivor-benefit calculations and repeated ERISA litigation. The petition, pages 14–15, describes uncertainty for plan administrators and pleading-stage litigation costs. Those considerations justify an upward adjustment, while the claimed scale of affected assets is not treated as a measured amount at stake in this case.
- **This is not an obviously superior lead vehicle.** According to petition pages 9–13, the appellate decision reversed dismissal of a complaint; unresolved factual questions and several derivative counts remain. The petition says Odom carries the relevant Section 1055(d) claim while Drummond abandoned that claim, and it distinguishes their actuarial assumptions. Those details add complexity to an otherwise clean legal question and favor a companion route over selecting this case for plenary review. I do not treat the interlocutory posture as a jurisdictional bar.
- **The docket has not yet shown special interest.** One distribution after a waiver supplies neither an actual relist nor a request for federal views. A waiver is not dispositive evidence against review, but it supplies no affirmative scrutiny signal. No Court-requested response is recorded. I do not treat petitioners' request for a hold as a hold ordered by the Court.

## Other probabilities and stakes

The **32% redistribution** estimate reflects the companion issue and potential request for briefing, balanced against the still-modal routine denial. It is a judgment about a future additional distribution, not an empirically measured hazard. The **5% CVSG** estimate allows for federal pension-policy interests but remains low because the record contains no invitation and the Court could address the issue through a lead petition instead.

The **80% summary-route probability is conditional on a grant**. Combined with 12% any-grant probability, it implies roughly 9.6% unconditional mass on a cert-order disposition and 2.4% on a grant opening plenary proceedings. Most of the former is a possible companion-driven GVR, not an immediate summary reversal. The petition's reliance on general textualist principles does not establish a controlling intervening decision requiring summary relief. The **2.5% separate-writing probability is conditional on denial**, reflecting the absence of a demonstrated sharp circuit conflict or unusually strong record of judicial attention. No per-Justice cert votes are asserted.

The **0.58 big-case score** measures significance if decided, not review likelihood: benefit calculations for married retirees and surviving spouses, pension administration, and recurring employer liability are meaningful national stakes, while the specific statutory question is technical and narrower than a broad constitutional dispute.

The largest uncertainty is whether a companion draws sustained Court interest. I deliberately did not resolve that uncertainty by looking up later proceedings. The absent BIO and appendix also limit my ability to assess vehicle objections and the lower courts' reasoning independently.
