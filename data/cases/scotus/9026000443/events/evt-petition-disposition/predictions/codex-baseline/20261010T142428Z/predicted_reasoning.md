# Forecast of the Court's disposition

The Court will deny the petition. The central question is whether ERISA's actuarial-equivalence requirement for joint-and-survivor annuities requires reasonable underlying actuarial assumptions. The most likely order will leave that question unresolved by the Supreme Court rather than announce a merits rule.

The baseline records **one distribution**, entered October 7, 2026, for the November 6, 2026 conference, and no CVSG. The modal trajectory is **no further distribution** and an ordinary denial after that conference. I assign **32%** to at least one additional distribution. Within that alternative, one or two additional distributions are more likely than a lengthy sequence, although a hold for a companion merits case could last much longer. A hold need not itself generate a new distribution entry; this claim forecasts an actual increment in that count.

The Court will not call for the Solicitor General's views; the probability of a new CVSG is **5%**. If one issues, I expect it during the initial rounds of consideration rather than after a long run of repeated conferences.

If review is granted, the route will most likely be a **GVR following resolution of a companion petition**, rather than plenary review of this case or an immediate summary reversal. The probability of disposition in the cert order, conditional on any grant, is **80%**. That forecast depends on a companion first producing a relevant ruling; I do not predict that such a ruling already exists or that a hold itself counts as a grant. The petition identifies FedEx v. Watt and the Bakery pension committee's petition in Reichert as earlier-filed vehicles and principally requests a hold for them.

If this case instead receives plenary review, the Court will take the single statutory question substantially as presented: whether Section 1055(d)'s actuarial-equivalence requirement constrains the reasonableness of the assumptions used to calculate survivor annuities. It will not use the petition to resolve every pleaded fiduciary-duty and related ERISA claim independently. Conditional on a petitioner-favorable merits disposition, the likely rationale would reject an additional reasonableness obligation as unsupported by the statutory term; that is not a forecast of an opinion accompanying the predicted denial.

If review is denied, there will be **no noted dissent or statement respecting denial**. I assign **2.5%**, conditional on denial, to any such separate expression, without forecasting an individual Justice's otherwise unobservable cert vote.
