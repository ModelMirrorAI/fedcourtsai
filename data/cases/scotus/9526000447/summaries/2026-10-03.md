## In brief

A trucking company asked the Supreme Court to pause a state court's document-production order while it sought review; the Court denied that request.

## What happened

Plaintiffs sued Swift Transportation over a fatal New Mexico highway crash, alleging a Swift trailer was involved. After disputes over Swift's discovery compliance, the trial court appointed an examiner, previously an expert witness for the plaintiffs, and authorized him to demand "all documents and information" he wanted from Swift, including passwords. Swift argued this violated the Fourth Amendment's requirement that searches be specifically described, and sought relief from the New Mexico Supreme Court, which denied its petition on August 20. With a contempt hearing approaching, Swift asked the U.S. Supreme Court to temporarily block enforcement of the examination order while it prepared a certiorari petition.

## What the Court is being asked

Swift asked a single justice to pause the state court's order while Swift pursues further review, arguing that letting the order take effect, including the threat of contempt for noncompliance, would cause harm that could not later be undone. The underlying question Swift wants the full Court to eventually decide is whether an order letting a court-appointed examiner demand any company records he wants, without describing them specifically, follows the rule that searches and seizures must be clearly limited in scope.

## Where it stands

Justice Gorsuch, acting as the circuit justice for this region, denied Swift's emergency application for a stay on October 2, two days after it was filed.

## What each outcome would mean

Swift, the trucking company, asked for the stay. Denial, which is what happened, means the trial court's examination order remains in effect and enforceable, including the show-cause hearing over Swift's compliance, while Swift's case continues in the lower courts and any future certiorari petition is considered separately. The denial does not decide the underlying constitutional question Swift raised; it only means the order was not paused while that question is pursued further.
