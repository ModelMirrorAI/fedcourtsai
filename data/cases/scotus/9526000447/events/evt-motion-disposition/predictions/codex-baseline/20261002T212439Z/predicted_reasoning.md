# Forecast: denial of the stay application

I predict denial of Swift's application to stay the examination order and its enforcement pending a certiorari petition. The probability that the disposing entry records an unqualified grant is **0.06**. This is not the probability of receiving any temporary accommodation: an administrative pause does not establish that the substantive application ultimately succeeds, and a mixed grant-and-denial disposition counts as denied under this event's contract.

The baseline records only the September 30, 2026 submission to Justice Gorsuch. It records no Supreme Court response request, no referral to the full Court, and zero amicus entries. From that state I forecast:

- **Response requested: 0.20.** The concrete disclosure concern could prompt an answer, but denial without requesting one is more likely.
- **Referral to the full Court: 0.25.** Gorsuch is more likely to dispose of the application himself than refer it. Submission to him is not itself a full-Court referral.
- **Amicus count increases above zero: 0.04.** I expect no further amicus entries, including submitted briefs, given the short timetable and case-specific dispute.

The modal path is a short denial without an explanatory opinion, without a new response request or full-Court referral. I expect disposition promptly, most likely by October 12, 2026, and approximately within two weeks of the September 30 submission. The application identifies an October 1 contempt hearing and an intended October 12 certiorari filing; I do not forecast that the now-past hearing will occur in the future or assert what actually happened there. Timing is a contextual forecast, not a scored claim.

My predicted obstacle to relief is the showing needed for Supreme Court review and reversal, not a categorical absence of Fourth Amendment protection for businesses. Swift presents the examiner's authority as an unrestricted search. Respondents' state-court opposition instead describes a supervised inquiry into missing or belatedly disclosed accident-related records, with opportunities to contest the order. I expect the Court to leave this discovery dispute to the available state procedures rather than intervene on the emergency record. If the Court does request a response, the scope of the examiner's authority and the immediacy of compelled disclosure will be the important issues. A denial would not itself endorse the respondents' constitutional theory or supply a merits holding.
