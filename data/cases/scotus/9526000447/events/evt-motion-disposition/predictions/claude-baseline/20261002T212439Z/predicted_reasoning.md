# Forecast: what the Court will do with application 26A447

**Disposition.** The application will be **denied**, and the denial will be unqualified: no stay of the examination order, no stay of the contempt proceedings, and no administrative stay that survives to the disposing entry. Swift is a private civil litigant asking the Court to halt a state trial court's discovery-examiner order mid-case, after the Supreme Court of New Mexico declined superintending control without opinion. That is the posture in which the Court almost never intervenes, and nothing on this record changes it. The dispositive weaknesses the Court will see are (1) the 28 U.S.C. § 1257 finality problem — the "judgment" Swift points to is a one-line denial of an extraordinary writ aimed at an interlocutory discovery order, and the adequate ordinary remedy (appeal from any contempt adjudication) is one the application itself concedes exists; (2) the absence of any conflict among courts on whether a court-appointed examiner under a Rule 706 analogue is a Fourth Amendment "search" needing particularity; and (3) the fact that the relief requested would have the Court supervise a state court's management of a discovery sanction. A "reasonable probability" of certiorari is not shown, so the stay standard fails at its first step without the Court needing to reach irreparable harm.

**Response called for.** Probably **not**. Justice Gorsuch, as Circuit Justice for the Tenth Circuit, is more likely to deny without a response than to call for one. The application is professionally drafted and pitched at his own Fourth Amendment property-based writing, which is the main reason a response is a live possibility (about one in four), but a Circuit Justice who sees a finality and vehicle problem does not need the plaintiffs' views to deny.

**Referral to the full Court.** Roughly even odds, leaning against. Single-Justice denials of private civil-litigant stay applications are routine, but a constitutional claim framed this carefully is the kind a Circuit Justice often refers so the denial carries the Court's name. If referred, expect "application referred to the Court and denied" with no noted dissent.

**Amicus filings.** The record shows zero amicus entries, and I expect it to stay at zero. A trucking-industry amicus is conceivable but unlikely in the few days this application will live.

**Timing and shape.** Disposition within about one to two weeks of submission: by roughly October 10 if denied by Justice Gorsuch alone, by about October 17 if a response is called for first. The October 1 show-cause hearing was set for the day after submission, so any administrative-stay request was likely overtaken before action; the disposing entry will be a flat denial. A noted dissent from the denial is unlikely (under 10%).

**Conditional merits content.** If the Court did grant a stay, it would be because it saw the eventual petition as a vehicle for extending *Jones*'s property-based framework to compulsory process against businesses, confining the *Marshall v. Barlow's* / *Burger* "closely regulated industry" language. I do not expect it to reach any of this.
