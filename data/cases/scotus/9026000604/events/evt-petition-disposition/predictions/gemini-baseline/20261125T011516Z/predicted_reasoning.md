The petition in *Daniel Samuel Eta v. United States* (No. 26-604) has been **distributed once so far** (on November 24, 2026, for the Conference of December 11, 2026, following the Solicitor General's November 17 waiver of a response), with **no CVSG** on the docket. The Court will **deny certiorari**, most likely directly off the December 11, 2026 conference without any further distribution.

- **Further distributions / relists:** Expect **zero further distributions** (~82% likelihood), resulting in a clean denial on the December 14, 2026 order list. Although *Anibowei v. Blanche*, No. 26-208 (raising whether border cell-phone searches require a warrant, with a response requested September 29, 2026) is currently pending, the Seventh Circuit's independent `Davis` good-faith holding on a February 2018 manual border search gives the Court little reason to hold *Eta* behind *Anibowei*. If the petition is distributed again (~18%), the likeliest route is a hold pending *Anibowei* followed by **one additional distribution** once *Anibowei* is resolved (or, less likely, a Call for Response followed by one post-opposition distribution and one conference relist).
- **CVSG:** The Court will **not** call for the views of the Solicitor General (`cvsg-increment` = 0.001). The United States is already the respondent party represented by the Solicitor General; any request for the government's position would take the form of a routine Call for Response (CFR), not a CVSG invitation.
- **Question presented if taken:** If the Court were to grant plenary review, it would take the petition's single Question Presented narrowed to its purpose-nexus prong — whether a warrantless border search of a traveler's cell phone requires a justification tied to detecting contraband, enforcing customs laws, or determining admissibility rather than assisting a domestic criminal investigation — or consolidate the case alongside *Anibowei v. Blanche*, No. 26-208, rather than taking up a standalone categorical warrant rule that every circuit to address the issue has rejected.
- **Summary disposition vs. plenary review:** Conditional on any grant occurring, **summary disposition via a post-hold GVR** (`60%` of grants) is likelier than setting *Eta* for plenary briefing and oral argument (`40%`). Because *Anibowei* is a civil expungement suit unencumbered by the exclusionary rule's good-faith exception, the Court is substantially likelier to use *Anibowei* for plenary review (if it grants the issue at all) and GVR *Eta* in light of a merits decision in *Anibowei* than to argue *Eta* on the merits. A summary merits reversal on the cert papers is virtually ruled out. If granted for plenary review, the Court would likely address whether *Riley v. California*, 573 U.S. 373 (2014), prohibits untethering warrantless border searches of cell phones from traditional customs, contraband, and admissibility purposes, while confronting whether good-faith reliance on pre-2019 border-search precedent independently sustains the judgment below.
- **Dissent from or statement respecting denial:** Should the Court deny certiorari, expect **no separate writing** (~97.5% conditional on denial). Any Justice inclined to write separately on warrantless border searches of electronic devices — most plausibly **Justice Gorsuch** or **Justice Sotomayor** — will almost certainly reserve separate writing for *Anibowei*, No. 26-208, rather than writing on a government-waived criminal suppression appeal insulated by an alternative good-faith holding.
