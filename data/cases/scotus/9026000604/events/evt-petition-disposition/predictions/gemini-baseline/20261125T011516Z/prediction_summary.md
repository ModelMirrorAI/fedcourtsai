The Supreme Court is overwhelmingly likely to deny review in *Daniel Samuel Eta v. United States*, with less than a one-in-twenty chance (4.5%) of taking up the case. Eta asks whether border officers violated the Fourth Amendment by scrolling through his cell phones at an airport without a search warrant to help the FBI investigate fraud, rather than to look for smuggled contraband. Although that constitutional question is important, the government waived its response, another pending civil lawsuit (*Anibowei v. Blanche*) raises the issue more cleanly, and the lower court ruled that Eta's conviction would stand anyway under the "good-faith exception," a rule allowing evidence when officers reasonably relied on existing law at the time of a 2018 search.
