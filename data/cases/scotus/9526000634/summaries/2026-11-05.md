## In brief

The Supreme Court denied Jesse Guardado's request to delay his execution and declined to hear his challenge to Florida's lethal-injection procedures.

## What happened

Jesse Guardado, a Florida death-row prisoner, sued prison officials claiming Florida's lethal-injection protocol was being administered unreliably, citing drug logs showing recordkeeping errors, altered expiration dates, and possible use of unauthorized or improperly dosed drugs. He filed suit after completing clemency proceedings but before a death warrant was signed; the state then signed his warrant, and the district court held an expedited evidentiary hearing before ruling against him. The Eleventh Circuit affirmed, calling his challenge speculative and faulting him for not raising it sooner. Guardado asked the Supreme Court to pause his execution while it considered his appeal.

## What the Court is being asked

The case asks whether the lower courts were wrong to let the timing of the execution warrant control how quickly Guardado's evidence could be gathered and reviewed, and whether the evidence he did gather showed a real risk that the state's execution procedure would cause severe, unconstitutional suffering. A second question concerns whether he was wrongly denied access to unredacted drug records needed to support his claim.

## Where it stands

The Supreme Court received the stay application, the state's response, and Guardado's reply, then denied the stay of execution and denied the petition for review on November 5, 2026.

## What each outcome would mean

Guardado, the death-row prisoner, asked the Court to pause his execution and take up his case; Florida prison officials opposed both requests. By denying the stay, the Court allowed Florida to proceed with the execution without further review. By denying the petition, the Court left the Eleventh Circuit's ruling in place, meaning Guardado's challenge to the execution protocol did not succeed and the lower courts' findings against him stand.
