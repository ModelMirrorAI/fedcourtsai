## 2. Case-Specific Adjustments to `P(grant) = 0.02`

Within the substantive interim docket, prisoner applications for a stay of execution of a death sentence filed days before a scheduled execution—especially challenging state lethal-injection administration after an evidentiary hearing—have a substantially lower grant rate than the ~8–12% overall substantive application pool (which includes solicitor general and state emergency stay applications). Looking at resolved OT2026 capital stay applications in the corpus (`26A524`, *William Reaves v. Several specific features of Guardado's record in `application.txt` and the lower-court orders reinforced adjusting `probability` (`interim-disposition`) down to **0.02**:

1. **Adverse Factual Findings After an Evidentiary Hearing:** Guardado filed a 42 U.S.C. § 1983 method-of-execution complaint in the Middle District of Florida on September 28, 2026, alleging maladministration of Florida's three-drug etomidate protocol (missing or post-dated etomidate log entries in the 2025 executions of Michael Bell, Thomas Gudinas, and Edward Zakrzewski; variations in whole-blood postmortem etomidate toxicology levels; pharmacy log entries showing a `1/31/2025` expiration date altered with Wite-Out to `1/31/2027` across six executions; discrepant vial counts of potassium acetate and rocuronium bromide; and removal of injectable lidocaine not listed in the written protocol). However, District Judge Marcia Morales Howard held an evidentiary hearing on October 22, 2026, heard live testimony from FDOC Bureau Chief of Pharmacy Services Dr. Marcano, FDLE execution monitors, medical examiner Dr. Webb, and Guardado's experts (Drs. Almgren, Zivot, and Antognini), and inspected unredacted drug purchase receipts and lot numbers *in camera*. The District Court found as fact that etomidate was administered in the prior executions (confirmed by postmortem blood toxicology), that the etomidate stock was unexpired (expiring January 2027) and the log discrepancies were clerical errors by a pharmacy employee, and that the drugs set aside for Guardado are unexpired. Under *Glossip v. Gross*, 576 U.S. 863, 881–82 (2015), the Supreme Court gives heavy deference to concurrent District Court and Court of Appeals fact-finding on method-of-execution risk.
2. **Unanimous Eleventh Circuit Denial and *Glossip* Prong-Two Omission:** On November 2, 2026, a unanimous Eleventh Circuit panel (Chief Judge William Pryor, Judge Jill Pryor, and Judge Robert Luck) denied a stay in No. 26-14027. Critically, in addition to affirming the District Court's Prong-One factual findings under clear-error review, the Eleventh Circuit held that Guardado's stay motion failed even to mention—let alone establish—a feasible, readily implemented alternative method of execution as required by the second prong of *Baze* / *Glossip* / *Bucklew*. Provisioned `application.txt` in this Court similarly focuses entirely on protocol maladministration (Prong One) and equitable diligence under Fla. Stat. § 922.052 (*Abdool v. Bondi*), without developing *Glossip* Prong Two. That omission makes a grant of certiorari or a stay exceedingly unlikely even for Justices troubled by Florida's pharmacy practices.
3. **Why `P(grant)` Is Not Zero (0.02):** Guardado has two non-trivial equitable and procedural points that keep the probability at 2% rather than <0.5%: (a) unlike typical last-minute capital § 1983 suits filed *after* a death warrant is signed, Guardado filed his complaint *before* the Governor signed his warrant (and shortly after his clemency proceedings concluded in August 2026), yet the subsequent signing of the warrant compressed discovery to 22 pages of heavily redacted, Wite-Out-altered drug logs and an *in camera* review his counsel could not inspect; and (b) Judge Jill Pryor wrote a pointed concurrence in the Eleventh Circuit calling FDOC's undocumented use of Wite-Out on execution drug logs during active litigation "completely inappropriate." While Judge Pryor still voted to deny the stay under the clear-error standard, her concurrence gives the application slightly higher salience than a routine capital stay request.
