## Legal Standard and Conditional Assessment

To obtain a stay of execution pending certiorari under *Barefoot v. Estelle*, 463 U.S. 880 (1983), *Hill v. McDonough*, 547 U.S. 573 (2006), and *Nken v. Holder*, 556 U.S. 418 (2009), Guardado must establish a reasonable probability that four Justices will vote to grant certiorari, a significant possibility of reversal of the Eleventh Circuit's decision, and a likelihood of irreparable harm, with equitable factors (including diligence) also weighing in the balance.

The Court will deny the stay because the lower courts' concurrent rejection of Guardado's 42 U.S.C. § 1983 Eighth Amendment method-of-execution challenge rests on fact-bound credibility and evidentiary determinations after an evidentiary hearing and on an independent doctrinal defect under *Glossip v. Gross*, 576 U.S. 863 (2015), and *Bucklew v. Precythe*, 587 U.S. 119 (2019):

- **Deference to Concurrent Lower-Court Fact-Finding on Prong One:** Following an October 22, 2026 evidentiary hearing and an *in camera* review of unredacted pharmacy purchase receipts and lot numbers, the District Court (Judge Marcia Morales Howard) found as a matter of fact that etomidate was administered in recent Florida executions, that the log discrepancies and Wite-Out alterations reflected recordkeeping errors rather than the use of expired or sub-potent etomidate, and that the etomidate stock designated for Guardado's execution expires in January 2027. The Eleventh Circuit panel (Chief Judge William Pryor, Judge Jill Pryor, and Judge Robert Luck) unanimously held on November 2, 2026 that those factual findings were not clearly erroneous. The Supreme Court rarely grants certiorari or stays an execution to re-weigh District Court factual findings affirmed under clear-error review.
- **Failure to Argue a Feasible Alternative Method on Appeal (Prong Two):** Under *Baze*, *Glossip*, and *Bucklew*, an Eighth Amendment method-of-execution claimant must plead and prove a feasible, readily implemented alternative method that significantly reduces a substantial risk of severe pain. As the Eleventh Circuit majority noted, Guardado's stay presentation on appeal (and his stay application in this Court) omitted argument on the required second prong, creating an independent barrier to likelihood of success on the merits.
- **Equitable Delay Dispute:** While Guardado argues that he filed his § 1983 action on September 28, 2026—shortly after his clemency proceedings concluded in August 2026 under Fla. Stat. § 922.052 (*Abdool v. Bondi*, 141 So. 3d 529 (Fla. 2014)) and a week before the Governor signed his death warrant—the Eleventh Circuit majority's alternative reliance on delay is secondary to the merits holdings on both *Glossip* prongs, which all three panel judges agreed foreclosed a stay.

If the Court were unexpectedly to grant a stay of execution, it would do so pending disposition of the linked petition for a writ of certiorari in No. 26-6051 to examine whether truncated discovery and *in camera* reliance on altered pharmacy logs in a pre-warrant § 1983 suit improperly foreclosed Guardado's ability to prove systemic maladministration under the Eighth Amendment. On the expected denial, most orders in this posture issue without separate writings, though there is a modest chance (~6–8%) that Justice Sotomayor or Justice Jackson authors a brief dissent or statement highlighting the pharmacy recordkeeping and Wite-Out concerns emphasized in Judge Jill Pryor's concurrence below.
