# Forecast of the Court's action

The Court will most likely **deny the stay of execution**, leaving the requested suspension of Florida's execution authority ungranted. The probability of substantive relief in whole or in part is **6%, conditional on a nonmoot disposition**. The expected order will be brief and will not announce a new constitutional rule. The likely obstacle is the applicant's failure to establish a sufficiently strong basis for emergency intervention after adverse factual assessments below, notwithstanding the gravity of the alleged protocol failures and restricted discovery. This forecasts the action, not a holding that every challenged practice is constitutional.

The baseline is the November 3 submission to Justice Thomas: **no response request, no full-Court referral, and zero amicus entries** have yet been recorded. From that state:

- A further, expressly recorded **request for a response has a 40% probability**. A state response may instead be filed without a recorded request; that would not satisfy the response-request increment.
- **Referral to the full Court has a 95% probability**. The capital posture makes collective review the expected route, including on a denial.
- **At least one additional amicus-count entry has an 8% probability**. The central prediction is no increase from zero. A qualifying submitted brief counts without awaiting acceptance.

Disposition is expected by **November 5, 2026, before the scheduled 6 p.m. execution**, most likely on November 4 or November 5. That is a forecast about the application, not a prediction that the execution necessarily occurs. A temporary administrative pause, if issued, would not by itself establish that the substantive stay has been granted.

No separate writing is the modal prediction. If an explanation is published, Sotomayor is the likeliest author, focusing on meaningful review of systemic execution-procedure allegations; Jackson is the next most plausible. The independent probabilities of authorship are Sotomayor 18%, Jackson 7%, Kagan 2%, Thomas 0.8%, Alito and Gorsuch 0.5% each, and Roberts, Kavanaugh, and Barrett 0.3% each. These probabilities concern writing on the disposing order, not merely joining a writing or casting an unreported vote.
