I reduce the 5.15% anchor substantially because the documents reveal a weak review vehicle even within the baseline population:

1. **The operative dispute is Texas tort law, not a contested federal rule.** The petition describes removal on diversity grounds and asks whether the plaintiff's unrelated banking relationship changes the duty owed in transactions involving third-party accounts. Its reasons for review explicitly disclaim both a private federal regulatory cause of action and federal policy dictating state tort law (petition pp. 4, 6–8). National-bank status and federal monitoring requirements therefore do not themselves supply the missing federal decisional conflict. This is a discretionary-review assessment, not an assertion that the Supreme Court lacks jurisdiction over a federal appellate judgment applying state law.
2. **No developed conflict is demonstrated.** The petition's authorities are the cases the panel applied; it identifies neither a conflicting circuit holding on the same governing law nor a state high-court conflict nor a contrary Supreme Court holding. Its principal argument is that fraud is widespread and the panel's rule is unjustifiably categorical. Those concerns can matter, but do not establish a conflict warranting this Court's intervention.
3. **The appendix materially weakens the categorical-rule framing.** The unpublished, unanimous panel opinion treats the customer's other transactions and post-fraud refund communications as insufficient to establish foreseeable injury in these transactions (appendix, appellate opinion pp. 2–4). The magistrate judge separately explains that the amended pleading did not specify the nature of the longstanding customer relationship or concrete facts showing what the bank should have anticipated before the losses (recommendation pp. 8–10). Thus review would involve the sufficiency of these allegations as well as the abstract duty question. I do not treat alleged sham accounts or warning signs as adjudicated facts.
4. **The asserted federal route was disavowed below as well.** The recommendation records abandonment of a direct banking-statute claim and rejects reliance on those statutes to cure the Texas-law duty deficiency (recommendation pp. 16–17). The contract, fiduciary-duty, and consumer-protection theories failed separately. Those separate failures do not logically prevent revival of a negligence claim, but they do not furnish a stronger federal question either.
5. **The procedural record adds no affirmative selection signal.** There has been no relist, no invited government participation, no recorded amicus support, and no response request in the supplied record. I do not penalize a genuinely missing opposition as if I had read it. The record simply supplies no counterweight to the substantial substantive vehicle problems.

The resulting **0.4%** is a judgmental adjustment, not an estimated rate for a measured state-law/pro-se subgroup. I retain a small nonzero chance because the petition seeks a broadly consequential clarification and an individual Justice could see a larger pleading or banking-duty issue. The petitioner appears for himself, but the petition also identifies Supreme Court bar membership; I do not equate self-representation with a lack of legal qualification. My decisive considerations are the question and record, not that status.
