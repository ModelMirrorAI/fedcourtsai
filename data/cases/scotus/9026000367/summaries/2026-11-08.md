## In brief

A man who lost money to scammers asks the Court to revive his lawsuit against Bank of America over how courts decided the bank owed him no duty.

## What happened

Venky Venkatraman alleges he was tricked by scammers into depositing $94,345 in money orders and cashier's checks into Bank of America accounts controlled by third parties in January 2023. Bank of America returned about $26,000 but not the rest. He sued in Texas state court; the bank moved the case to federal court. The district court dismissed his claims, allowed one amendment, then dismissed the amended complaint with prejudice, finding the bank owed him no legal duty and that his fraud, contract, and consumer-protection claims were inadequately pleaded. The Fifth Circuit affirmed, holding that banks owe no duty to prevent third parties from using their own accounts for fraud, regardless of Venkatraman's long customer history with the bank or its later efforts to help him recover funds.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked whether a bank can be automatically cleared of any duty-of-care question simply because fraud proceeds passed through accounts titled to other people, even when the person harmed is the bank's own longtime customer and points to the bank's control over those accounts, possible warning signs, and its later partial repayment.

## Where it stands

The petition was distributed for the Court's December 4, 2026 conference, where the justices will decide whether to take the case.

## What each outcome would mean

Venkatraman, who lost in the lower courts, is asking the Court to take up his case. If the Court grants the petition, it would agree to fully hear the case and decide the legal question; granting review does not decide who is right. If the Court denies the petition, the Fifth Circuit's ruling affirming dismissal of Venkatraman's claims with prejudice stays in place, and Bank of America's win below stands, ending his lawsuit.
