## In brief

A former dock clerk asks the Court to review lower-court rulings that dismissed his lawsuit claiming his employer and union fired him in retaliation for reporting workplace discrimination.

## What happened

Efrain Areizaga, a former union clerk, says he was fired by TForce Freight the day after filing a grievance about a supervisor's treatment of a coworker. He sued TForce Freight, United Parcel Service, and his union, representing himself. The district court dismissed most claims as untimely and, after a summary judgment motion, ruled that his remaining retaliation claim failed because he had not shown a reasonable belief that the conduct he reported was sex discrimination under federal civil rights law. The court of appeals affirmed without issuing its own opinion, and later denied rehearing.

## What the Court is being asked

Areizaga's petition raises several questions: whether the lower courts were wrong to dismiss his case despite factual disputes he says should have gone to a jury, whether the courts should have ruled on additional retaliation claims he raised, whether the district court improperly granted the employer more time to respond to the lawsuit without letting him object first, and whether the courts applied the wrong time limit for filing one of his claims.

## Where it stands

The petition was filed in October 2026. The union, International Brotherhood of Teamsters Local 745, waived its right to respond. The case was distributed for the Court's conference on December 11, 2026, and the docket shows no action since.

## What each outcome would mean

Areizaga, as petitioner, is asking the Court to take up his case and reverse the lower courts' rulings against him. If the Court grants review, it would hear full arguments and decide the legal questions itself, without yet determining who wins. If the Court denies review, the lower courts' dismissal of Areizaga's claims stands, and the case ends in favor of TForce Freight, UPS, and the union.
