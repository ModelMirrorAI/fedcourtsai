# Forecast: Hameed v. DEA, No. 26-561

The petition has been distributed once, for the conference of December 4, 2026, after the Solicitor General waived a response. The Court will deny certiorari on the order list following that conference (expected December 7, 2026), without calling for a response.

- **Further distributions:** none. The petition is decided at its first conference. The only realistic path to another distribution is a Justice calling for a response from the government, which I put at roughly one in twenty-five; a true relist after that is less likely still.
- **CVSG:** none. The United States is already the respondent, so there is no occasion to invite the Solicitor General's views.
- **Question presented:** if the Court took the case, it would take the single question as written — whether 21 U.S.C. § 824(c)(3)'s "shall determine" is satisfied by a Diversion Control Division letter the agency called preliminary. There is nothing to narrow or reformulate.
- **Summary route:** conditional on any grant, plenary review is somewhat likelier than a cert-order disposition, but a GVR is a real share of that small mass: the plausible grant story here is the government confessing some error after a called-for response, or a vacatur for the D.C. Circuit to explain its two-sentence holding. No pending merits case bears on § 824(c)(3), so a hold-and-GVR is not in view.
- **Separate writing:** none. Should the Court deny, no Justice notes a dissent or writes a statement.

If granted, the likely ground would be a narrow statutory one — that the determination must be made by, or adopted in, the final agency decision — resolved for the petitioner with a remand to the agency.
