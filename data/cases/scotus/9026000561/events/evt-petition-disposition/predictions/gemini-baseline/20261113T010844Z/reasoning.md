## 2. Case-Specific Adjustments (1.6% → 0.8%)

### Downward factors (decisive)
1. **Complete absence of a circuit split (conceded by petitioner):** Petitioner openly acknowledges (Pet. 15, 18) that no circuit split exists on 21 U.S.C. § 824(c)(3) and that no published appellate opinion has squarely construed what "shall determine" requires when DEA rejects a corrective action plan (CAP). Our CourtListener search across all federal appellate opinions mentioning § 824(c) and corrective action plans confirmed this: the only published circuit decision touching the CAP framework in detail, *Ashraf v. DEA*, 153 F.4th 1161 (11th Cir. Sept. 3, 2025), recites § 824(c) as background (with Judge Tjoflat concurring to note that a CAP does not substitute for a hearing request) and involves no conflict with the D.C. Circuit.
2. **Unpublished, non-precedential three-sentence disposition below:** A unanimous, cross-ideological D.C. Circuit panel (Judges Millett, Wilkins, and Katsas) decided the case without oral argument under D.C. Cir. Rule 34(j) in an unpublished per curiam judgment (App. 1a–4a) and denied panel rehearing without dissent on August 18, 2026 (App. 5a). Contrary to petitioner's contention that the ruling "sets the floor" for national venue in the D.C. Circuit (Pet. 15), an unpublished judgment under D.C. Cir. Rule 36(e)(2) does not bind future D.C. Circuit panels. Moreover, Judge Katsas's participation on both the merits panel and the rehearing denial is a strong signal that a leading administrative-law textualist saw no *Chenery* or statutory defect warranting even a published opinion.
3. **Substantive statutory weakness of petitioner's "preliminary vs. final" dilemma:** Petitioner's core argument is a neat rhetorical trap—that Assistant Administrator O'Malley's May 5, 2022 letter called itself a "limited, preliminary determination" while Administrator Cole's Final Order ignored the CAP, leaving no "final" § 824(c)(3) determination anywhere. Reading the full text of § 824(c)(3) alongside the May 5, 2022 letter (App. 35a–36a) and Order to Show Cause (App. 43a–45a) shows why the panel rejected this in three sentences. Section 824(c)(3) requires DEA, upon receiving a CAP on or before the appearance date, to "determine whether denial, revocation, or suspension proceedings should be discontinued, or deferred for the purposes of modification, amendment, or clarification to such plan." That is by nature a *threshold, pre-hearing gatekeeping decision* about whether to halt enforcement proceedings—and Assistant Administrator O'Malley explicitly made it in writing ("I am denying the request to discontinue or defer administrative proceedings. Furthermore, I have determined there is no potential modification of your PCAP that could or would alter my decision in this regard," App. 35a). Labeling that refusal "preliminary" to the "final Agency adjudication" simply prevents premature interlocutory appeals under 21 U.S.C. § 877 while preserving the registrant's right (which O'Malley's letter expressly noted, App. 36a) to place remedial evidence before the Administrator on the merits.
4. **Severe vehicle and harmless-error problems (5 U.S.C. § 706):** Even if a Justice questioned whether the Administrator must personally ratify a Division-level CAP denial in the Final Order, this case is an unattractive vehicle with an independent, dispositive ground supporting revocation. The undisputed Maryland Board record established that Dr. Hameed habitually combined oxycodone, over-prescribed eszopiclone, and alcohol for months and performed interventional pain procedures on patients while intoxicated—including performing an unconsented radiofrequency ablation instead of the cervical facet block ordered (App. 8a–12a). Crucially, the Administrator revoked his registration because Dr. Hameed **failed the threshold requirement of unequivocal acceptance of responsibility**, repeatedly minimizing his impairment and drug abuse in his sworn DEA submissions (App. 30a–33a), while still expressly considering his rehabilitation and Maryland Physician Health Program compliance in Footnote 14 (App. 32a–33a n.14). Because lack of unequivocal acceptance of responsibility independently forecloses entrustment under settled DEA precedent regardless of future monitoring proposals, any technical omission in referencing the 2022 CAP would be harmless on remand.
