# Predicted Court Action — *Haroon I. Hameed v. Drug Enforcement Administration, et al.* (No. 26-561)

- **Predicted disposition:** Certiorari **denied** (`P(any grant) = 0.008`).
- **Further distributions (`relist-increment = 0.045`):** The docket currently records **one** distribution (distributed on November 12, 2026, for the Conference of December 4, 2026) following the Solicitor General's November 6, 2026 waiver of a response, and has never been relisted. Expect **zero** further distributions: the Court will deny the petition off the December 4, 2026 Conference order list without calling for a response or relisting. In the unlikely event (~4.5%) that a chambers flags the petition's 21 U.S.C. § 824(c)(3) textual argument, the next step would be a Call for a Response from the Solicitor General followed by one redistribution—and likely one post-response relist if seriously considered—after the brief in opposition arrives.
- **Call for the Views of the Solicitor General (`cvsg-increment = 0.001`):** Expect **no CVSG**. Respondent Drug Enforcement Administration and its Administrator are federal parties already represented by the Solicitor General, who filed a waiver on November 6, 2026. If the Court wants the federal government's position, it will enter a routine Call for a Response rather than an invitation to file an amicus brief.
- **Question presented if granted (context-only):** The petition presents a single question presented—whether the Attorney General's duty under 21 U.S.C. § 824(c)(3) to "determine whether denial, revocation, or suspension proceedings should be discontinued, or deferred" upon review of a registrant's corrective action plan is satisfied by a Diversion Control Division official's letter labeled a "limited, preliminary determination" when the Administrator's final revocation order neither adopts nor addresses that determination. If the Court were to grant plenary review, it would take that single statutory question substantially as written.
- **Plenary review vs. summary disposition (`summary-disposition-route = 0.35` conditional on grant):** Conditional on review being granted at all, plenary briefing and argument (`0.65`) is more likely than a summary disposition in the cert order (`0.35`). No intervening Supreme Court precedent postdates the D.C. Circuit's July 16, 2026 judgment, and summary reversal on an issue of first impression with no circuit precedent is exceedingly unlikely; however, roughly one-third of the tiny grant-family tail consists of a narrow GVR scenario in which, following a Call for a Response, the Solicitor General declines to defend the Final Order's omission of DEA's customary ratification sentence regarding the corrective action plan and suggests a remand.
- **Separate writings on the order (`dissent-from-denial = 0.006` conditional on denial):** Conditional on denial, expect **no** dissent from denial or statement respecting denial (~99.4% clean denial). Because the Court almost never publishes a separate writing respecting denial without first calling for a response, any separate writing is gated by a Call for a Response; in that small tail, Justice Gorsuch (`0.003`) or Justice Thomas (`0.002`) is the likeliest author to comment on agency adherence to mandatory pre-deprivation procedural statutes or summary circuit review.
- **Conditional merits outlook (if granted):** If granted for plenary review, the Court would likely confront two threshold obstacles for petitioner—first, that § 824(c)(3) governs an interlocutory gatekeeping decision whether to halt show-cause proceedings (which Assistant Administrator O'Malley expressly denied in writing on May 5, 2022, calling it "preliminary" only relative to the final revocation order under 21 U.S.C. § 877), and second, harmless error under 5 U.S.C. § 706 because the Administrator revoked registration on the independent ground that petitioner failed to unequivocally accept responsibility for practicing while impaired.
