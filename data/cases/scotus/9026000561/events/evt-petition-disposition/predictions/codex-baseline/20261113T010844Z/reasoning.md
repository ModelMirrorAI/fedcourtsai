## Legal strengths and vehicle weaknesses

The strongest point is concrete: section 824(c)(3) requires a determination about discontinuing or deferring proceedings; the Division described its decision as preliminary, and the final order mentioned the plan's earlier rejection without a separate plan analysis. The petition frames a recurring statutory procedural protection, not merely disagreement with the revocation sanction. The operative statute, charging language, and Division letter appear in Appendix G, p. 52a; Appendix E, pp. 44a–45a; and Appendix D, pp. 35a–36a. This provides a reason for a nonzero grant probability and moderate regulatory stakes.

But the actual Division letter expressly denies both discontinuance and deferral after reviewing the plan and rejects possible modifications. A plausible defense is that this satisfies the procedural determination even if it does not end the entire registration adjudication. That is my reading of a potential defense, not a position attributed to an unprovided opposition. The statute reproduced in the appendix does not expressly command a second determination in the Administrator's final order. The charging document says the Administrator may consider the plan, rather than promising a second review. The asserted mismatch between the letter's label and statutory function consequently looks more debatable and record-dependent than a clear violation of settled Supreme Court law.

The final order also relies on failure to unequivocally accept responsibility and discusses rehabilitation, creating a potential prejudice/remedy complication even if petitioner wins the procedural point (Appendix C, pp. 29a–33a). I do not treat the forfeited agency-delay claim as forfeiture of the question presented: Appendix A, p. 3a, resolves the corrective-plan issue separately on its merits. Nor do I assume that the hearing waiver itself eliminates the statutory claim.

I checked the published Eleventh Circuit opinion in *Sualeh Ashraf v. DEA*, No. 23-10340 (September 3, 2025), CourtListener opinion 11132123, using its corrective-plan excerpts. It describes substantive Administrator consideration of that registrant's plan and a hearing-waiver issue, not a contrary holding requiring a second final-order determination under section 824(c)(3). It therefore supplies background, not an established split. The provisioned `same-question.json` lists no match above its threshold; that is a limited text-matching result, not proof that no similar litigation exists.
