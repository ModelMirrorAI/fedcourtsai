## In brief

A physician whose DEA registration was revoked asks the Court to review whether the agency ever made the legally required decision on his proposed corrective action plan.

## What happened

Haroon Hameed, a pain physician, faced a DEA proceeding to revoke his controlled-substance registration based on findings that he abused drugs and alcohol while treating patients. He waived a hearing, accepted responsibility, and proposed a monitoring plan as his corrective action plan, asking DEA to defer proceedings while discussing changes to it. A DEA division official's letter denied the request while calling itself a "limited, preliminary determination," saying the agency's Administrator would later issue the final decision. More than three years later, the Administrator's final order revoked Hameed's registration, mentioning the plan only once. The D.C. Circuit denied his petition for review in an unpublished decision, and later denied rehearing.

## What the Court is being asked

The case concerns a law requiring the Attorney General to actually decide whether a revocation proceeding should be stopped or paused so a registrant's proposed corrective plan can be revised. The Court is asked whether that requirement was satisfied here, given that the only response to the plan came from a lower official in a letter the agency itself called preliminary, and the final order never took up the plan itself.

## Where it stands

The federal government, representing the Drug Enforcement Administration, waived its right to respond to the petition. The case was distributed for the Court's December 4, 2026 conference, where the justices will decide whether to take it up.

## What each outcome would mean

Hameed, who lost below, is asking the Court to take up his case. If the Court grants review, it would hear full arguments on whether the agency satisfied the law's requirement to decide on his corrective action plan, without yet deciding who is right. If the Court denies review, the D.C. Circuit's decision upholding the revocation of Hameed's registration would stand, and the case would end there.
