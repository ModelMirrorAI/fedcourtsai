# Predicted reasoning — Nexstar Media Inc. v. NLRB, No. 26-435 (arrival moment)

**Disposition: denied, in a routine order with no separate writing.** P(any grant) = 0.03.

## Procedural trajectory

- **Distribution.** The record shows zero distributions, which is the arrival moment's definition. The petition will be distributed at least once (P ≈ 0.97): nothing about this docket points to a pre-distribution withdrawal or dismissal. The NLRB does not settle a test-of-certification enforcement case, and the petitioner has nothing to gain from withdrawing.
- **Government response.** The NLRB is a federal respondent represented at this stage by the Solicitor General. Expect a **waiver** of the right to respond within the November 2, 2026 window, after which the petition is distributed for a December 2026 or early January 2027 conference. A call for a response is possible but less likely than not (roughly one in four): the petition dresses a substantial-evidence loss as a Loper Bright question, and a clerk reading the summary order's own "we review the NLRB's legal conclusions de novo" line will see that.
- **Relists.** One conference and out. If a response is requested the timeline slides into January or February 2027 but the probability of a relist stays low; a single relist is the most likely form a grant-side signal would take, and I do not expect one.
- **CVSG.** None (P ≈ 0.01). The United States is the respondent, so the Solicitor General files for a party and is never invited.

## If the Court acts

- **Route.** Conditional on any grant, a **GVR** "for further consideration in light of Loper Bright" is the likely form (P ≈ 0.75 conditional), which is also what the petition itself primarily asks for, with plenary review as the alternative. A GVR here would be unusual because Loper Bright is two years old and the panel recited de novo review for legal conclusions, so an intervening-decision GVR of the Hospital Menonita shape does not fit; the Court would have to treat the panel's "considerable deference" sentence as a Loper Bright error in its own right.
- **Question taken.** If plenary review were granted, the Court would reformulate the petition's two questions into one: whether a court of appeals reviewing an NLRB order may give the Board's legal conclusions deference after Loper Bright. It would not take QP 1 as written. The claimed split with Multimedia KSDK is about the supervisory status of particular producers on a particular record, a fact-bound question the Court has no reason to resolve.
- **Merits, conditionally.** If it reached the question, the Court would hold that no deference is owed to the Board's legal conclusions, consistent with the Fifth, Sixth, Tenth, D.C., and now Second Circuit decisions the petition cites. That unanimity is itself a reason the Court will not take the case.

## On denial

- **No dissent or statement respecting denial** (P ≈ 0.04). A statement by Justice Gorsuch or Justice Thomas urging lower courts to take Loper Bright seriously is conceivable, but this unpublished, substantial-evidence summary order is not the vehicle a Justice would pick to make that point, especially with the Second Circuit having already corrected course in Siren Retail Corp. v. NLRB (Sept. 2, 2026).
