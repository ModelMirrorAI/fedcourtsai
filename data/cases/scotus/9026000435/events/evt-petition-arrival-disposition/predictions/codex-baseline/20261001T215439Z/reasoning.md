I raise that anchor to **12%** because the attached order actually repeats a legal-deference formulation that gives the petition a concrete Loper Bright argument. Appendix A, page 3a, states de novo review but then accords considerable deference to expertise-based legal conclusions. Pages 5a–8a use the Board's Oakwood definitions of assignment, independent judgment, and responsible direction. This is more than an unsupported allegation that the panel merely reached the wrong result.

Several features restrain the increase:

- **Law versus proof:** Appendix A, pages 6a–8a, identifies missing examples of discretionary assignment, actions taken at higher management's direction, a single available worker, and no evidence of accountability for subordinates. These are substantial factual obstacles even if statutory meaning receives independent review. Loper Bright itself distinguishes independent legal interpretation from deferential review of factfinding and policymaking, 603 U.S. 369, 392 (2024), checked through CourtListener opinion 11066629.
- **The alleged split is not categorical:** Multimedia KSDK, 303 F.3d 896, 899–900 (8th Cir. 2002), checked through CourtListener lead opinion 9817149, rejected a categorical exclusion of professional or technical judgment under Kentucky River. It does not establish that every station's producers are supervisors regardless of their actual duties. The supplied order rests on the degree and proof of discretion instead. That distinction weakens, without eliminating, the claimed conflict.
- **Limited prospective disagreement:** The petition itself reports that Siren Retail subsequently adopted independent review in the Second Circuit, petition pages 13–14. I treat that account as advocacy, not independently verified authority. If accurate, it reduces the need for a new plenary rule, although it strengthens the argument for correcting this particular judgment.
- **Remand is plausible, not automatic:** The petition invokes Hospital Menonita as a Loper Bright GVR analogy, pages 14–15. I have not independently verified its procedural account. Loper Bright already predated this June 2026 judgment, so the analogy is weaker than a routine remand following a newly announced controlling decision. The summary-order vehicle and possible independent evidentiary grounds also counsel against a high grant estimate.
