## Why the legal issue raises the number only modestly

**A genuine legal-review concern:** The petition's second question challenges the panel's treatment of the Board's legal conclusions after Loper Bright. Appendix A, page 3a, does retain language giving considerable deference to legal conclusions based on agency expertise. That is a concrete textual hook, not simply an employer disagreeing with a factual finding. The petition expressly seeks a GVR and invokes Hospital Menonita as an analogy (petition pages 14–17). I treat that analogy as advocacy in the provisioned petition, not as an independently verified match. It makes a short remand more plausible than plenary review and justifies a modest increase over the 5.111% anchor.

**But the appendix is materially less favorable than the petition's framing:** On that same page 3a the panel expressly says it reviews legal conclusions de novo. Its operative analysis then identifies case-specific evidentiary deficiencies: no demonstrated independent assignment judgment for the news producers, digital producers seeking or implementing superiors' decisions, an obvious sole available assignee for creative-services tasks, and no evidence of accountability for other employees' performance (pages 5a–8a). These reasons could sustain the result without deference to an agency's reading of ambiguous law. Loper Bright itself distinguishes independent legal interpretation from deferential review of agency factfinding; I checked that distinction in the majority opinion through CourtListener, opinion 10452863, opinion-of-the-Court page 14. Thus, a problematic boilerplate sentence need not have controlled the judgment.

**The alleged circuit split is not clean:** The first question treats Multimedia KSDK as establishing supervisory status for television producers. The retrieved en banc opinion, 303 F.3d 896 (8th Cir. 2002), instead attacks the Board's then-used categorical exclusion of judgment grounded in professional or technical skill, following Kentucky River. Its majority also recognizes substantial-evidence review of supervisory-status findings. It does not establish that every producer with that job title is necessarily a supervisor. Nexstar's appended decision turns on proof of authority and accountability under a later framework. The old case supplies an arguable analogy, not an established present-day conflict on materially identical records.

**Limited need for a new Supreme Court rule:** The petition itself reports that the Second Circuit subsequently recognized independent legal review in Siren Retail (petition pages 13–14). I use that only as the petition's supplied account, without retrieving that litigation. Even accepting it, the proposition weakens the case for a persistent circuit-level doctrinal split while leaving a possible individual error-correction argument. The order below is an unpublished summary order. Loper Bright also preceded that order, making an automatic intervening-decision GVR less obvious than where a lower judgment predates the new authority.

**Procedural attention is still limited:** The record shows neither a response request nor a relist, and the government waived its response. A waiver is not a merits concession. A call for response would be the natural intermediate step if the Court were seriously considering relief. I place most mass on routine denial, while leaving meaningful room for that call and subsequent consideration.

The net result is 7%, not an elevated-band grant probability. Within that grant mass, the likely route is a GVR to let the panel separate legal interpretation from fact review; the independent chance of a summary route is 0.07 × 0.80 = 5.6%. Plenary or other nonsummary grant routes together account for the remaining 1.4%. The 80% structured route claim is conditional on a grant, not an 80% overall chance of relief.
