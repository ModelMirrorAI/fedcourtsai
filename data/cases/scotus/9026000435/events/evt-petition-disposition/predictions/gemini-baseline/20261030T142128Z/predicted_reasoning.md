## Conditional Route and Scope if Granted
- **Summary disposition vs. plenary review (`summary-disposition-route: 0.65`):** Conditional on the unlikely event that the Court calls for a response and grants certiorari, a summary disposition—specifically a grant, vacate, and remand (GVR) directing the Second Circuit to omit its pre-*Loper Bright* boilerplate ("considerable deference" to the Board's legal conclusions)—is substantially likelier than setting a non-precedential summary order down for full merits briefing and oral argument (65% conditional probability of summary disposition vs. 35% plenary review).
- **Questions presented if granted:** If the Court were to grant plenary review, it would take only the second question presented (whether *Loper Bright* requires non-deferential *de novo* review of the Board's legal conclusions and mixed law-and-fact applications under 29 U.S.C. § 152(11)) and decline the first question's fact-bound comparison with *Multimedia KSDK, Inc. v. NLRB*, 303 F.3d 896 (8th Cir. 2002). If granted on the merits, the Court would likely clarify that while pure legal interpretations of Section 2(11)'s terms ("assign," "responsibly to direct," and "independent judgment") are reviewed *de novo* under *Loper Bright*, the Board's factual findings regarding specific employees' workplace authority remain subject to substantial-evidence review under 29 U.S.C. § 160(e)–(f).
