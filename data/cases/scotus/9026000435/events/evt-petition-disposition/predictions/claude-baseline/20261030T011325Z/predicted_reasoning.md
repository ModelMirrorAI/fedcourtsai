# Forecast — Nexstar Media Inc. (WROC-TV) v. NLRB, No. 26-435

**Disposition: denied.** The docket shows one distribution (for the conference of
November 13, 2026), no relist, no CVSG, and a waiver of response by the federal
respondent. I expect the petition to be denied at that conference without a
response being requested.

**Further relists.** Most likely none (about 85%). The live path to another
distribution is the Court calling for a response from the Solicitor General.
That is plausible here because the Second Circuit's summary order, decided two
years after *Loper Bright*, describes the Board's legal conclusions as owed
"considerable deference". If the Court does call for a response, the petition
would be redistributed once the response is in, probably in early 2027, and
then most likely denied or GVR'd at that conference.

**CVSG.** None. The United States, through the NLRB, is the respondent, so the
Court would call for a response rather than invite the Solicitor General's views.

**If granted, which question.** Not plenary review of QP 1, the asserted split
with the Eighth Circuit's *Multimedia KSDK* (2002) on television producers. That
is a fact-intensive supervisory-status question, and the conflict is a
twenty-four-year-old decision against an unpublished order. Any grant would
take QP 2 in its narrow form: the panel applied a deference standard that
*Loper Bright* displaced.

**Summary route.** Conditional on any grant, a GVR in light of *Loper Bright*
(or in light of the Second Circuit's own published *Siren Retail*) is the
likelier route, at roughly 70%. Plenary review is improbable. A per curiam
summary reversal on the merits of supervisory status is very unlikely.

**Dissent from denial.** Not expected (about 2%). If any Justice writes, Justice
Gorsuch or Justice Thomas is the likeliest, on the persistence of deference to
the Board after *Loper Bright*.
