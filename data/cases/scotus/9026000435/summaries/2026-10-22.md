## In brief

A television station asks the Court to review whether an appeals court gave improper deference to a labor board ruling against it.

## What happened

The National Labor Relations Board certified a union to represent certain employees at Nexstar's television station in Rochester, New York, including producers Nexstar says are supervisors and therefore exempt from union representation. When Nexstar refused to bargain, the Board found this an unfair labor practice and ordered Nexstar to bargain. The Second Circuit enforced the Board's order, applying what it called considerable deference to the Board's legal conclusions. Nexstar now asks the Supreme Court to review that decision.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked whether the appeals court was wrong to defer to the labor board's legal conclusions about which employees count as supervisors, instead of deciding that legal question independently, and whether the appeals court's approach conflicts with another circuit's decision on the same issue involving television producers.

## Where it stands

The petition was filed on September 28, 2026, and a response from the Board is due November 2, 2026. The Court has not yet acted on the petition.

## What each outcome would mean

Nexstar, which lost before the Board and the Second Circuit, is asking the Court to take up the case. If the Court grants the petition, it would hear the case and decide the legal question itself; a grant does not decide who is right. If the Court denies the petition, the Second Circuit's decision stands, leaving in place the order requiring Nexstar to bargain with the union. Nexstar has also suggested the Court could instead vacate the lower court's decision and send the case back for reconsideration without a full hearing.
