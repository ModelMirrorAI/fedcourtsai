## In brief

The Supreme Court declined to hear a television station's challenge to a labor board order requiring it to bargain with a union representing its news producers.

## What happened

Nexstar Media, which operates television station WROC-TV, refused to bargain with a union after federal labor officials certified it as the bargaining representative for several producer positions. Nexstar argued these producers were supervisors, not regular employees, and therefore outside the union's reach. The labor board rejected that argument and ordered Nexstar to bargain. The board then asked a federal appeals court to enforce its order. The appeals court agreed with the board, applying deference to the board's legal conclusions, and ordered Nexstar to comply.

## What the Court is being asked

Nexstar asked the Court to decide two things: whether the appeals court's deferential approach to the labor board conflicts with a different appeals court's decision on the same supervisor question, and whether the appeals court was wrong to defer to the board's legal conclusions rather than reviewing them independently, as Nexstar says a recent Supreme Court ruling requires.

## Where it stands

The federal government, representing the labor board, waived its right to respond to the petition. The petition was distributed for the Court's November 13, 2026 conference, and on November 16, 2026, the Court denied the petition.

## What each outcome would mean

Nexstar, as petitioner, asked the Court to overturn the appeals court's ruling in favor of the labor board and the union. By denying the petition, the Court leaves the appeals court's decision in place: Nexstar must recognize and bargain with the union representing its news producers, digital content producers, and creative services producers. The denial does not mean the Court agreed with the appeals court's reasoning; it simply leaves that ruling standing without deciding the legal question Nexstar raised.
