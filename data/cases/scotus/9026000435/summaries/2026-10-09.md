Nexstar Media asks the Court to review whether a federal appeals court wrongly deferred to the labor board's legal conclusions instead of reviewing them independently.

## What happened

A union sought to represent certain Nexstar employees, including news and content producers, at a television station. Nexstar argued these producers were supervisors who do not get collective-bargaining rights, but the National Labor Relations Board certified the union anyway and later found Nexstar violated labor law by refusing to bargain. Nexstar challenged that finding before the Second Circuit, which sided with the Board, applying a standard of deference to the Board's legal conclusions. Nexstar says this conflicts with an Eighth Circuit ruling on similar producers and with a recent Supreme Court decision ending judicial deference to agency legal interpretations.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked whether the appeals court should have independently reviewed the labor board's legal conclusions about who counts as a supervisor, rather than deferring to the board's expertise, and whether the appeals court's approach conflicts with another circuit's ruling on the same issue.

## Where it stands

Nexstar filed its petition on September 28, 2026, and the Board's response is due November 2, 2026. The Court has not yet acted on the petition.

## What each outcome would mean

Nexstar, which lost before the Second Circuit, is asking the Court to take up the case. If the Court grants the petition, it would hear the case and decide the legal question itself, deciding nothing in advance about who is right. If the Court denies the petition, the Second Circuit's ruling enforcing the Board's order against Nexstar stands, leaving Nexstar bound to recognize and bargain with the union.
