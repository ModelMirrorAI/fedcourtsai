## What happened

Ryan Hauber sued his former employer, Honkamp Krueger & Co., alleging disability and medical-leave discrimination. After his lawyer withdrew, Hauber represented himself and filed a motion asking the court to accommodate his PTSD. While that motion sat undecided, the district court dismissed his case with prejudice as a sanction for discovery violations, calling his noncompliance willful, and the accommodations motion was dismissed as moot along with everything else pending. The Eighth Circuit affirmed, and rehearing was denied. The district court later denied Hauber's motions to reopen the judgment.

## What the Court is being asked

Hauber asked the Court to decide whether it violates basic fairness for a federal court to end a self-represented disabled litigant's case as a discovery sanction while his supported request for help participating in the case remains unresolved.

## Where it stands

The petition was distributed for conference twice, in September and again in November 2026, with a supplemental brief and a motion to delay consideration filed in between. The Court denied the petition on November 9, 2026.

Hauber, representing himself, asked the Court to take up his case and reverse the lower court's decision against him. By denying the petition, the Court leaves the Eighth Circuit's ruling in place: Hauber's lawsuit against Honkamp Krueger remains dismissed with prejudice, and the question he raised about resolving accommodation requests before imposing case-ending sanctions goes undecided by the Court.
