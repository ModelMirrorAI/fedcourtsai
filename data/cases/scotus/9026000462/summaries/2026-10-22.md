## In brief

A former plaintiff asks the Court to review whether his case was wrongly dismissed as a discovery sanction while his accommodations request sat unresolved.

## What happened

Ryan Hauber sued his former employer, Honkamp Krueger & Co., P.C., for disability and medical-leave violations. After his lawyer withdrew, Hauber represented himself and sought accommodations related to PTSD. A magistrate judge later found he had willfully violated discovery orders concerning his medical records, and recommended dismissal as a sanction. The district court adopted that recommendation, dismissed the case with prejudice, and denied all other pending motions, including Hauber's renewed accommodations request, as moot. The Eighth Circuit affirmed, and later denied rehearing. The district court subsequently denied Hauber's motions for relief from judgment, while acknowledging it had never explicitly ruled on his accommodations motion.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked whether it violates a litigant's right to a fair hearing for a federal court to end a disabled, self-represented person's case as a discovery punishment before ruling on that person's pending, medically supported request for help participating in the case.

## Where it stands

The petition was distributed for conference, and the respondent later filed a waiver of its right to respond. The case has since been distributed again for a later conference, and the docket shows no decision yet on whether the Court will hear it.

## What each outcome would mean

Hauber, who lost in the lower courts, is asking the Court to take up his case. If the Court grants review, it would hear the case fully and decide the constitutional question, without yet deciding who wins. If it denies review, the Eighth Circuit's decision affirming dismissal of Hauber's lawsuit stays in place, and Honkamp's win below stands. A denial would not mean the Court agrees the dismissal was proper, only that the lower court's ruling remains final.
