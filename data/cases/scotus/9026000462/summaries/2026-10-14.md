## In brief

A former employee asks the Court to review whether his lawsuit could be dismissed as a discovery sanction while his disability accommodation request sat undecided.

## What happened

Ryan Hauber sued his former employer, Honkamp Krueger & Co., P.C., alleging disability and medical-leave violations. Proceeding without a lawyer, Hauber repeatedly sought accommodations for PTSD while also facing court orders to disclose medical records. After finding he willfully violated discovery orders, the district court dismissed his case with prejudice as a sanction and declared his then-pending accommodations motion moot. The Eighth Circuit affirmed, and rehearing was denied. The district court later denied Hauber's motions for relief from judgment, though it acknowledged it had never explicitly ruled on his accommodations request.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked whether it violates a litigant's right to a fair hearing for a federal court to end a case as a punishment for discovery violations while a pending, medically supported request for accommodations—filed by a disabled, self-represented litigant—has not yet been decided.

## Where it stands

The petition was distributed for the Court's September 28, 2026 conference, and the docket shows no action recorded since. The respondent has waived its right to file a response, and the Court granted a motion to file part of the petition's appendix under seal.

## What each outcome would mean

Hauber, the petitioner, asks the Court to take up his case. If the Court grants the petition, it would hear full arguments and decide the constitutional question itself, deciding nothing in advance for either side. If it denies the petition, the Eighth Circuit's decision affirming the dismissal of Hauber's lawsuit stands, leaving Honkamp's win in place; denial would not mean the Court agrees the dismissal was proper, only that it leaves that ruling undisturbed.
