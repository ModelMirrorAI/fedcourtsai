## In brief

A self-represented former employee asks the Court to review whether a court dismissed his disability discrimination lawsuit before deciding his pending accommodations request.

## What happened

Ryan Hauber sued his former employer, Honkamp Krueger & Co., P.C., alleging disability and medical-leave discrimination. After his lawyer withdrew, Hauber represented himself and filed a motion asking for accommodations based on PTSD, supported by a doctor's declaration. Before ruling on that motion, the district court dismissed his case with prejudice as a sanction for discovery violations, finding he had withheld medical records in bad faith, and it denied the pending accommodations motion as moot. The Eighth Circuit affirmed without addressing the accommodations issue, and later post-judgment motions were also denied.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked whether it violates a litigant's right to a fair hearing for a federal court to end a disabled, self-represented person's case as a punishment for discovery problems while his pending, medically supported request for help participating in the case remains undecided.

## Where it stands

The petition was distributed for conference, then distributed again for a later conference after the respondent waived its right to respond. Hauber has since filed a supplemental brief pointing to other recent decisions and newly filed petitions, along with a motion asking the Court to consider his case alongside two of those petitions at the same conference; the docket does not yet show the Court's action on the petition itself.

## What each outcome would mean

Hauber, who lost in the lower courts, is asking the Court to take up his case and reverse that outcome. If the Court grants review, it would hear the case fully and decide the constitutional question; granting does not itself decide who wins. If it denies review, the Eighth Circuit's decision affirming dismissal of Hauber's lawsuit stands, and the case remains closed. Either way, no outcome is determined until the Court acts.
