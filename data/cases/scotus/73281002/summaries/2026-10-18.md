## In brief

The Republican National Committee asks the Court to overturn a ruling that struck down Pennsylvania's requirement that mail-in ballots be discarded if voters fail to date their return envelopes.

## What happened

Bette Eakin and other Pennsylvania voters and groups sued the state's county election boards after Eakin's mail-in ballot was rejected in 2022 because she had not dated her return envelope. They argued the dating rule violates the Constitution. The Republican National Committee, National Republican Congressional Committee, and Republican Party of Pennsylvania intervened to defend the requirement. A federal district court ruled the requirement unconstitutional and ordered county boards to stop discarding undated or misdated ballots. The United States Court of Appeals for the Third Circuit affirmed that ruling, and it later declined to rehear the case as a full court, over several judges' dissents. The Republican National Committee and Pennsylvania (in a related case) then asked the Supreme Court to review the decision.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked whether a voting rule that imposes only ordinary burdens on voters can still be challenged successfully under the Constitution, whether rules governing mail voting should receive a more lenient level of judicial review when in-person voting remains available, and, if a stricter balancing test applies, whether that test treats a minimally burdensome rule more leniently and whether a rule's burden should be measured by how hard it is to comply or by what happens to voters who don't comply.

## Where it stands

The petition has been distributed for conference more than once, and the Court has asked the Solicitor General to submit a brief giving the federal government's views on whether the case should be heard. No decision on whether to grant or deny review has been recorded.

## What each outcome would mean

The Republican National Committee and its co-petitioners are asking the Court to take up the case. If the Court grants review, it would hear full arguments and decide the constitutional questions itself, without yet deciding who wins. If the Court denies review, the Third Circuit's decision striking down the dating requirement stands, meaning Pennsylvania counties would continue to count mail ballots that are missing dates or have incorrect dates, as the lower courts ordered. A grant does not indicate the requirement will ultimately be upheld or struck down; a denial would not mean the Court agrees with the lower court's reasoning, only that the ruling remains in place.
