## In brief

The Republican National Committee and Pennsylvania ask the Court to review a ruling that struck down a state requirement that mail voters date their ballot envelopes.

## What happened

Pennsylvania requires mail-in and absentee voters to write a date on the outer return envelope. Voters denied this, and county election officials, sued, arguing the requirement violates the Constitution's protections for the right to vote by causing valid ballots to be thrown out over minor dating errors. A federal district court agreed and struck the requirement down. The Third Circuit Court of Appeals affirmed, applying a balancing test that weighs voting burdens against state interests, and the full appeals court declined to rehear the case by a 7-6 vote. The Republican National Committee and Pennsylvania, defending the requirement, separately petitioned the Supreme Court.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked whether a voting rule that imposes only ordinary burdens on voters can be found unconstitutional at all, whether rules governing mail voting should be judged by a more lenient standard when in-person voting remains available, and, if the stricter balancing test does apply, whether courts should measure a rule's burden by how hard it is to comply with or by what happens to voters who do not comply.

## Where it stands

The case has been fully briefed, including amicus filings and a reply, and was distributed for conference twice, most recently for June 25, 2026. The docket shows the Court then granted a motion to file an amicus brief and invited the Solicitor General to file a brief expressing the federal government's views; no further action is recorded.

## What each outcome would mean

The Republican National Committee and Pennsylvania, joined by other Republican party organizations, are asking the Court to take up the case and reverse the Third Circuit. If the Court grants review, it would hear full briefing and argument and decide the constitutional question itself, which could uphold the date requirement, strike it down again, or send the case back without resolving it. If the Court denies review, the Third Circuit's decision striking down the date requirement stays in place, a result some respondents, including election officials and voter and party groups who sued, support, while others take no position.
