## What happened

Pennsylvania requires voters who cast mail-in ballots to write a date on the return envelope. Bette Eakin and other voters and organizations sued, arguing this dating requirement violates the U.S. Constitution because it causes thousands of otherwise valid ballots to be thrown out for minor errors, without serving any real purpose. A federal district court agreed and blocked enforcement of the requirement; the Third Circuit Court of Appeals affirmed, and the full Third Circuit declined further review by a 7-6 vote. The Republican National Committee and allied Republican groups, who had intervened to defend the requirement, ask the Supreme Court to take up the case. Pennsylvania's Commonwealth government filed a related, separately docketed petition. Many of the 67 county election boards named as parties have taken no position or waived any response.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked to decide how demanding a legal test should apply to election rules like this one. Specifically, the questions include whether a voting rule that imposes only ordinary, everyday burdens on voters can violate the Constitution at all; whether rules about mail voting should receive a more lenient level of review when voters can still vote in person; and, if closer scrutiny does apply, how courts should weigh a rule's burden against the state's reasons for it, and whether that burden should be measured by how hard the rule is to follow or by what happens to voters who fail to follow it.

## Where it stands

The petition was distributed for conference twice, and the Court asked for a response, which respondents filed along with several briefs opposing and one supporting review. After further briefing, including a reply from the petitioners, the case was distributed for another conference. The Court has since invited the Solicitor General to file a brief giving the federal government's views, so a decision on whether to hear the case is still pending.
