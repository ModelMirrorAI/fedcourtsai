## In brief

The Republican National Committee asks the Court to overturn a ruling that struck down Pennsylvania's requirement that mail-in ballot envelopes be dated.

## What happened

Bette Eakin and other voters sued Pennsylvania's county election boards after her mail-in ballot was rejected in 2022 for lacking a date on the return envelope. The Republican National Committee and allied Republican groups intervened to defend the requirement. A federal district court ruled that discarding undated or misdated ballots violates the Constitution, and the U.S. Court of Appeals for the Third Circuit affirmed, finding the requirement burdens voters without sufficiently serving the state's interests in fraud prevention, solemnity, or election administration. The Pennsylvania Attorney General, who had intervened on appeal to defend the requirement, separately petitioned the Court as well. The Third Circuit denied rehearing by a divided vote.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked whether a voting rule that imposes only ordinary burdens on voters is constitutional, whether a rule governing mail ballots should receive relaxed judicial review when in-person voting remains available, and, if closer review applies, how courts should weigh a mail-voting rule's burden against the state's reasons for it.

## Where it stands

The petition has been fully briefed, with responses, a reply, and amicus briefs filed. The Court invited the Solicitor General to file a brief expressing the views of the United States, and the case has not yet been scheduled for a decision on whether to grant review.

## What each outcome would mean

The Republican National Committee is asking the Court to reverse the Third Circuit's decision, which Eakin and the other voters who sued had won. If the Court declines to hear the case, the Third Circuit's ruling stands, and Pennsylvania election officials remain barred from discarding mail ballots solely for missing or incorrect dates on the return envelope. If the Court takes up the case, it would decide the legal standard for reviewing such voting rules and could ultimately uphold the date requirement, leave the lower court's reasoning undisturbed, or send the case back for further review without resolving who prevails.
