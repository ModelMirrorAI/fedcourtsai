# Predicted reasoning — RNC v. Eakin, No. 25-962 (CVSG moment)

**Bottom line.** The Court grants review, more likely than not (P = 0.55 for
any grant). The likeliest single path is a plenary grant, consolidated with
the Commonwealth of Pennsylvania's companion petition (No. 25-967), on the
Anderson-Burdick questions. The main alternative to a plenary grant is not a
clean denial but a mootness exit: if the Pennsylvania Supreme Court strikes the
date requirement under the state constitution in *Baxter* before the Court
acts, the Court most likely grants, vacates the Third Circuit's judgment under
*Munsingwear*, and remands with instructions to dismiss.

## Relists

The docket shows **two distributions** (for the 4/17/2026 and 6/25/2026
conferences); the CVSG issued after the second. The petition will be
distributed at least once more, near-certainly: once the Solicitor General
files, the Clerk redistributes for the next available conference. Expect the SG
brief around December 2026 and redistribution for a January 2027 conference,
with one to two further conferences before the order. A hold for *Baxter*, if
the Court chooses one, adds relists rather than removing them.

## CVSG

Already on the docket (June 29, 2026). No second invitation.

## Which question the Court would take

If it grants, the Court takes QP 3 as the working question — whether a
minimally burdensome, non-discriminatory voting rule gets rational-basis
review under Anderson-Burdick and whether burden is measured by the cost of
compliance rather than the consequence of noncompliance — and reaches QP 1's
"usual burdens of voting" threshold as part of that analysis. QP 2 (McDonald
and mail voting as a privilege) is the question the Court is least likely to
make the holding turn on: a rule that mail-voting regulations get only rational
basis whenever in-person voting exists is broader than the Court needs, and the
Commonwealth's companion petition frames the case around the balancing
framework. Expect the Court to grant on the petitions as framed rather than
rewrite the questions, and to consolidate 25-962 and 25-967 for one hour of
argument.

## Summary disposition versus plenary review

Conditional on a grant, roughly 30% that the disposition rides in the cert
order rather than going to argument — nearly all of that mass is the
*Munsingwear* scenario (state-law mootness via *Baxter*). A merits GVR in light
of *Center for Coalfield Justice* is unlikely: the Third Circuit already had that
decision before it when it denied rehearing, and *Coalfield Justice* touches
notice-and-cure only in counties that pre-canvass. No summary reversal: the
Court does not summarily reverse a published, en-banc-contested circuit
decision on a contested constitutional standard.

## If the Court denies

Roughly even odds of a dissent from denial or a statement respecting denial.
The likeliest author is Justice Alito, who wrote about this very date
requirement in *Ritter v. Migliori* (2022) and has criticized lower courts'
invalidation of Pennsylvania mail-ballot rules; Justices Thomas and Gorsuch are
the likeliest joiners. If the denial is a mootness denial after *Baxter*, a
statement noting that the Third Circuit's opinion should carry no precedential
weight is more likely than a dissent.

## Conditional merits shape

If granted and argued, the Court is more likely than not to reverse: a majority
would hold that a neutral rule whose compliance cost is trivial imposes at most
the usual burdens of voting and survives Anderson-Burdick without the State
having to prove its interests with evidence. The likelier ground is the
cost-of-compliance measure of burden (QP 3) rather than a categorical
McDonald holding (QP 2).

## Timing

SG brief December 2026; redistribution January 2027; order in January or
February 2027, or later if the Court holds for *Baxter*. A grant in January
would allow April 2027 argument in OT2026; a later grant pushes argument to
OT2027.
