# Predicted reasoning — RNC v. Eakin (25-962), CVSG-moment cert forecast

**State forecast from:** the docket shows two distributions (Conferences of
4/17/2026 and 6/25/2026), a response requested 4/1/2026, and the CVSG entered
6/29/2026. The petition is now parked awaiting the Solicitor General's brief.

**Relists.** The petition will be distributed at least once more — near
certainty. A CVSG'd petition is redistributed once the SG's brief arrives, and
this one will be: expect the SG to file the invited brief in the
November 2026–January 2027 window (a late-June invitation ordinarily returns a
brief in time for a same-Term grant), followed by one to two further
distributions in January–February 2027 before the Court acts. Total further
distributions: most likely 2, plausibly 1–3.

**CVSG.** Already on the docket (6/29/2026); no further call for the views of
the United States will issue. When the brief comes, I expect the SG to support
the petitioners and recommend a grant: the United States' current enforcement
posture aligns with the state parties defending the date requirement, and the
Third Circuit's Anderson-Burdick holding implicates federal election-law
interests the SG will want corrected rather than left standing.

**Disposition and route.** Most likely outcome: **plenary grant, around
January–February 2027**, argued in the spring of 2027 or early OT2027. If the
Court takes the case it will grant on the framework questions — QP 1 and QP 3
(whether a nondiscriminatory, minimally burdensome voting rule gets
rational-basis review under/instead of Anderson-Burdick, and whether burden is
measured by compliance cost or noncompliance consequence) — likely consolidated
or vided with No. 25-967. QP 2 (rational basis whenever in-person voting is
available) is broader than the Court needs and I expect it left behind or
folded into a reformulated single question. A summary disposition is unlikely:
there is no intervening decision to GVR against, and the 7-6 en banc division
makes this a full-dress vehicle, not error correction.

**The contingency that changes the route:** the Pennsylvania Supreme Court's
pending decision in *Baxter v. Philadelphia Board of Elections* (argued
September 2025, still undecided as of today). If it strikes the date
requirement under the state constitution's Free and Equal Elections Clause
before the Court acts, the federal question becomes academic. In that branch I
expect the Court not to simply deny: petitioners would seek — and would likely
get — a Munsingwear vacatur of the Third Circuit's judgment, wiping the
adverse Anderson-Burdick precedent (the Court took exactly that route with the
Third Circuit's materiality-clause ruling in *Ritter v. Migliori*).

**If denied outright** (against my expectation), expect a dissent from denial
or statement respecting denial — Justice Alito is the most likely author,
joined by Justices Thomas and Gorsuch, echoing their engagement with this same
Pennsylvania date requirement in the *Ritter* stay litigation.
