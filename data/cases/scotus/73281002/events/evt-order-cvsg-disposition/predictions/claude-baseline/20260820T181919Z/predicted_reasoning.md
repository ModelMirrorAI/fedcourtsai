# Forecast: RNC v. Eakin (25-962), disposition after CVSG

**Bottom line: the Court grants certiorari, most likely in the first half of
OT2026, and sets the case for argument (probably consolidated with
Pennsylvania v. Eakin, No. 25-967).**

## What happens from here

- **Relists.** The docket shows two distributions (conferences of 4/17/2026 and
  6/25/2026), after which the Court called for the views of the Solicitor
  General rather than acting. The petition will be **distributed at least once
  more** — near-certainty (~0.96): once the SG files, the case is redistributed
  as a matter of course, and only a pre-filing dismissal or withdrawal (rare,
  and nothing on this record suggests one) prevents it. Expect two to three
  further distributions after the SG brief arrives.
- **Timing.** The CVSG issued June 29, 2026 — the end-of-Term batch. Invitation
  briefs from a June CVSG typically arrive in December (to permit same-Term
  argument) or by the spring "clean-up" filing wave. Expect the SG brief
  around **December 2026**, redistribution in **January 2027**, and a
  disposition between **January and June 2027**.
- **No further CVSG** can occur; the invitation is already on the docket (the
  claim is vacuous here).
- **The SG's recommendation.** The United States will most likely support the
  petitioners: the current administration is aligned with the RNC's position on
  ballot-integrity rules, and even a neutral SG would acknowledge the recurring
  circuit conflicts over how Anderson-Burdick applies to nondiscriminatory
  voting regulations. A grant recommendation makes the grant substantially
  more likely; a deny recommendation (perhaps on vehicle grounds — the
  stipulations below and the parallel Pennsylvania state-constitutional
  litigation over the same date requirement) would likely produce a denial with
  separate writing.
- **Which question presented.** If the Court grants, it most plausibly takes
  **QP 3** (how Anderson-Burdick measures burden and when rational-basis review
  applies to minimally burdensome rules), possibly together with QP 2's
  McDonald question about mail voting specifically. QP 1 as written ("whether a
  non-discriminatory rule imposing the usual burdens of voting is
  constitutional") is closer to a merits slogan than a certiorari question and
  would likely be reformulated or absorbed into QP 3. (Registered
  context-only.)
- **Summary route.** Conditional on a grant, a summary disposition (~0.06) is
  unlikely: there is no intervening decision of this Court to GVR in light of,
  and the whole point of the CVSG was to inform plenary consideration of the
  framework question. Expect briefing and argument.
- **If denied.** After a 7–6 en banc denial below, a CVSG, and a federal
  court's invalidation of a state election statute, a denial would very likely
  draw a **dissent from, or statement respecting, denial** (~0.4 conditional on
  denial) — Justices Thomas and Alito have repeatedly written around
  Pennsylvania mail-ballot litigation (e.g., the 2020 election cases and Ritter
  v. Migliori), and Justice Alito handled the extension application here.

## Why a grant is more likely than not

The Third Circuit struck down a state election statute under a framework whose
operation the petition credibly shows is splitting the circuits three ways; the
en banc court divided 7–6; the Commonwealth of Pennsylvania itself filed a
companion petition (25-967, vided with this one); and the Court has already
escalated twice on its own motion — requesting a response after the respondents
uniformly waived, then calling for the SG's views after two conferences. Every
observable signal of the Court's own attention is present. The main
counterweights are vehicle-shaped, not importance-shaped: record stipulations
that the date requirement serves no current anti-fraud purpose, and parallel
Pennsylvania state-court litigation that may independently bar enforcement.
Those could persuade the SG or the Court to wait for a cleaner vehicle, which
is the main denial scenario.
