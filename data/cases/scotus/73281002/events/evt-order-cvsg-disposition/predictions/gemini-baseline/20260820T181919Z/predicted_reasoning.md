The Court is likely to grant plenary review to resolve the deep circuit split regarding the application of the Anderson-Burdick framework to minimally burdensome election rules, particularly mail-in ballot date requirements. If granted, the Court will likely take the questions presented as written, focusing on whether a non-discriminatory rule imposing usual voting burdens requires more than rational-basis review. A summary disposition is unlikely, as the Court typically prefers full briefing and argument to clarify the Anderson-Burdick balancing test. Should the Court deny the petition, expect a dissent from denial by conservative Justices (e.g., Alito, Thomas, or Gorsuch) emphasizing the need to respect state legislative judgments in election administration.
