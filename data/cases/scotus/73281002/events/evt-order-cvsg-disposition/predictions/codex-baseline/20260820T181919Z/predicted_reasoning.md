# Forecast

The Court will probably grant plenary review, most likely after the Solicitor General files the invited brief. The petition had been distributed for two conferences as of the June 30 snapshot. Because a CVSG issued on June 29, it will almost certainly be distributed at least once more after the government's brief arrives; I expect one or two additional distributions before disposition. A second CVSG is not expected.

If review is granted, the Court is more likely to reformulate the petition's first and third questions into a narrower question about how Anderson-Burdick applies to a concededly minimal, nondiscriminatory ballot-casting burden than to accept all three questions verbatim. The acknowledged disagreement among circuits about rational-basis review and about measuring the burden by compliance costs rather than the consequence of noncompliance makes plenary review more likely than a summary route.

A summary disposition remains a meaningful alternative. The Commonwealth's companion petition requests a GVR based on an intervening Pennsylvania Supreme Court decision, while respondents argue that the development does not undermine the Third Circuit's analysis. Conditional on a grant, I assign a 25% chance to a cert-order disposition, principally a GVR, and a 75% chance to plenary review.

If the Court denies review, a noted dissent or statement respecting denial is possible but not likely. The 7-6 denial of rehearing and the election-law stakes could attract a separate writing, most plausibly from one or more of Justices Thomas, Alito, or Gorsuch, but the CVSG process may give the Court a less fractured basis for disposition.

The most likely timing is after the Solicitor General's brief and renewed distribution during the ensuing Term, rather than an immediate order.
