# Rationale

I assign P(any grant) = 0.55 and select a plenary grant as the modal disposition.

The quantitative anchor is the committed statpack's sal-v3 paid-segment table, matching the frozen `high` band and salience version in `record/context.json`. Pooling the bracketed `reached` rates for OT2017 through OT2024 (strictly before this OT2025 petition) gives about 40.9% over a weighted resolved denominator of 1,074. The broader paid CVSG cut is somewhat lower: 29.4% plenary grants plus 5.5% GVRs, or 34.9% in the grant family, among 163 resolved petitions. I treated those as anchors rather than answers because the high-band pool captures the petition's frozen risk set while the CVSG cut confirms the strength of this specific procedural signal.

I adjusted upward for case-specific certworthiness. The June 30 snapshot shows a paid petition from the Third Circuit, four amicus filings or attempted filings before distribution, a requested response, two conference distributions, a 7-6 denial of rehearing en banc, and the June 29 CVSG. The petition presents a facially clean federal question after the Third Circuit invalidated a state election statute, and it identifies disagreements among the circuits over whether mail-voting rules receive rational-basis review, whether minimal burdens do, and how the burden is measured. The questions-presented text and petition emphasize the panel's own recognition of disagreement and the low compliance cost. The number of state amici and the companion Commonwealth petition reinforce the institutional stakes. These considerations support both the 0.55 grant probability and the 0.86 significance score.

I did not raise the forecast further because the opposition materials expose serious vehicle and merits risks. They characterize the dispute as fact-bound: Pennsylvania election officials disclaimed any current administrative or fraud-prevention use for the handwritten date, while thousands of otherwise timely ballots have been rejected. They also argue that the supposed circuit conflicts arise from materially different laws and records. Most importantly, parallel Pennsylvania constitutional litigation could independently eliminate the date requirement and moot the federal dispute. The Commonwealth's alternative request for a GVR based on an intervening state decision creates a real summary route, but respondents argue that the Third Circuit already considered that development and that it would not alter the result. Those points leave denial nearly as plausible as a grant and justify only moderate confidence.

The 0.97 relist-increment probability follows from the CVSG posture: absent an unusual withdrawal, dismissal, or intervening mootness, the petition must return to distribution after the Solicitor General's brief. The `cvsg-increment` claim is already vacuous because the frozen context records the June 29 invitation; 0.01 represents the negligible chance of another invitation rather than restating the existing CVSG. The 0.25 conditional summary-route probability is above an ordinary plenary-grant case because a GVR is expressly requested, but plenary review remains more likely if the Court grants. The 0.18 conditional probability of a dissent or statement on denial reflects the ideological and institutional salience, tempered by the usual absence of separate writings from cert denials.

The snapshot was a truncated, point-in-time record with a June 30 cutoff. The provisioned petition, questions presented, and compiled opposition text were complete according to `documents.json`; I used their pre-cutoff filing content. Supplemental CourtListener searches were limited to general, pre-cutoff Anderson-Burdick and absentee-ballot context and revealed no case-specific disposition or other outcome information.
