# Predicted reasoning — 26A560, Mullin v. Friends of the Ruidosa Church (response-filed moment)

**Disposition.** The Court will **grant the stay** of the Western District of
Texas's October 2 preliminary injunction. The injunction blocks the Secretary's
three 2026 IIRIRA § 102 waivers for the Big Bend Sector and halts border-wall
construction there. The grant will most likely come in a short unsigned order
issued once the application has been referred to the full Court. Expect it
within roughly one to two weeks of the October 29 response, after the
government files its reply.

Why: IIRIRA § 102(c)(2)(A) lets district courts hear only claims "alleging a
violation of the Constitution." The injunction's sole merits basis is a
major-questions holding, and respondents can only fit that through the
jurisdictional bar by recasting the major-questions doctrine as a
constitutional rule tied to nondelegation. Dalton v. Specter, which holds that
an ultra vires claim is not a constitutional claim, sits directly against that
theory. The equities also echo Trump v. Sierra Club (2019), where the Court
stayed an injunction against wall construction that rested on aesthetic and
recreational harms. The government's emergency applications have been granted
at a high rate in recent Terms. A district-court injunction that is novel, on
a first-of-its-kind theory, and was initially unreasoned (the opinion came
twelve days later) is the profile the Court most often stays.

**Ways it does not read as an unqualified grant.** The application also asks
the Court to treat it as a petition for certiorari and summarily reverse. The
likeliest course is to grant the stay and leave or deny the summary-reversal
request. A separate denial line in the same entry could make the order read as
mixed. A less likely course is a partial stay, for example limited to segments
where construction had already begun or excluding land inside the national
park. Outright denial is the remaining path. It would rest on the injunction's
interlocutory posture, on the lack of any court of appeals (review lies only by
certiorari) and on respondents' argument that the government delayed.

**Ladder.** A response was already requested (Justice Alito, Oct 22) and has
been filed. No further request is expected. **Referral to the full Court:**
very likely. A Solicitor General stay of a nationwide-significance policy is
routinely referred, and the disposing order will recite it. **Amici:** one
brief is on the docket so far (FAIR, supporting the applicants). One or more
further amicus filings before disposition is roughly a coin flip: Texas or
other states and immigration-enforcement groups for the applicants, and
environmental or tribal groups for the respondents. The short window caps it.

**Writings.** If the stay is granted, expect a noted dissent from at least one
of Justices Sotomayor, Kagan and Jackson. A written dissent is plausible from
Sotomayor or Jackson. (Jackson rejected a waiver challenge as a district judge
in CBD v. McAleenan, which makes a dissent from her on the jurisdictional point
less certain.) A concurrence on whether the major-questions doctrine is
constitutional is possible from Gorsuch or Kavanaugh. If the stay is denied,
expect a written dissent from Alito, Thomas or Gorsuch.

**Conditional merits.** If the Court addresses the merits, the likely ground is
the jurisdictional bar: a major-questions argument is a claim of statutory
authority, not a constitutional claim under § 102(c)(2)(A). The Court would
likely not decide whether Big Bend is an "area of high illegal entry."
