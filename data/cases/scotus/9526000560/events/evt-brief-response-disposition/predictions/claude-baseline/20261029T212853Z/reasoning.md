# Reasoning — P(unqualified grant) = 0.74

**Inputs read.** Snapshot `record/snapshots/2026-10-29.json` (the 26A560
docket through Oct 29). `record/context.json`: forward mode, `band: null` (the
normal interim case, so no cert band is used), response requested, not
referred, one amicus. `record/documents/application.txt` (39 pp., not
truncated): the SG's application for a stay plus a request for certiorari and
summary reversal. `record/documents/application-response.txt` (50 pp.): the
respondents' opposition. I read the introductions, tables of contents and key
argument sections of both, not every page.

**Anchor.** The statpack's "The interim docket (applications)" section
publishes per-Term substantive counts. Pooling application-Terms 2016–2025
(the ten strictly before OT2026) gives 160 granted out of 1366 resolved, about
**11.7%**. That clears the floor of 50. Every pooled Term shows `unparsed` at
0, except 2016 (15) and 2017 (1). The section's caption describes this as the
scored base rate (the current caption, not the older descriptive-only one).

**Adjustment, and why it is large.** The pooled population is mostly private
and prisoner applications. This one has the strongest features an application
can have:
- **Applicant is the Solicitor General**, seeking to stay a single district
  judge's injunction against a federal program. SG stay applications have been
  granted in full or in large part at a rate far above 50% in recent Terms.
  That figure is my general knowledge, not a committed table. No statpack cut
  conditions on the applicant, so this is judgment.
- **A response was requested** quickly (Oct 22 for Oct 29), which is the
  affirmative-attention rung.
- **The merits look favorable to the applicant.** IIRIRA § 102(c)(2)(A)
  expressly limits district-court review to constitutional claims. The
  injunction rests only on the major-questions doctrine. Respondents' answer
  (opposition §I.A) is that the major-questions doctrine is "constitutional at
  its core", an as-applied nondelegation rule. That is creative, but Dalton v.
  Specter is a serious obstacle. On the equities, Trump v. Sierra Club (2019)
  is close precedent.
- **The district court's order was initially unreasoned.** It issued Oct 2
  with no explanation of which claim succeeded, and the opinion followed
  Oct 14. The Court has been receptive to stay requests in that posture.

**Why not higher.** (1) The scored quantity is an *unqualified* grant, and a
mixed order reads as denied. The application bundles a request for summary
reversal that the Court is likely not to grant, and a partial stay
(geographic or temporal) is a real option. Either could produce text that
reads as mixed. (2) Respondents make specific factual arguments on harm: that
the government paused construction itself, that illegal entries in Big Bend
are low, and that the national-park landscape suffers irreversible harm. These
could attract Barrett or Roberts. (3) Review lies only by certiorari, with no
court-of-appeals ruling, which some Justices may see as a reason for caution.
I put P(stay granted in some form) near 0.82 and P(an order that reads as a
clean grant) near 0.74.

**Ladder claims.** Response-requested has already fired, so the harness will
mask that claim; I state 0.05 for a further request. Referral 0.85: almost
every contested SG stay is referred to the full Court, and the residual covers
the chance that the docket text does not record a referral the resolver can
detect. Amicus increment 0.45: one brief so far (FAIR, filed the same day as
the response). The window to disposition is probably one to two weeks, and
states or advocacy groups could file, but the docket has drawn little amicus
activity so far.

**Uncertainty and discounts.** The SG grant rate I rely on is remembered, not
committed. I did not verify current-Term SG outcomes. The corpus query I ran
returned recent, mostly extension, applications and did not help. I did not
use CourtListener or web search. The Court's live docket for this case past my
snapshot was not consulted.
