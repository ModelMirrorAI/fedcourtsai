# Forecast of the application's disposition

The Court will most likely grant the government's application to stay the Western District of Texas injunction pending the filing and disposition of a timely certiorari petition. The probability of an unqualified grant is **80%**. This forecasts interim relief, not a final judgment approving all construction or rejecting every constitutional challenge. A mixed grant-and-denial order counts as ungranted for this forecast.

The application will likely be referred to the full Court before disposition: **95%** probability of referral beyond the present record, which shows submission to Justice Alito but no referral. A short order granting a stay is the leading route; immediate plenary merits review is not required to give the government that relief. The alternative request to treat the application as a petition and summarily reverse is less likely than a stay preserving further litigation.

If the Court explains a grant, its most likely ground is that the injunction rests on an insufficiently supported merits premise. The statute expressly authorizes waivers for expeditious border-barrier construction and confines judicial review of waiver decisions to constitutional claims. The Court is likely to distinguish a genuinely constitutional challenge from an argument that the Secretary exceeded the statutory meaning of an area of high illegal entry. The government's jurisdictional objection provides a narrower route than resolving the full constitutional validity of the waiver scheme. An explanation could also credit the government's border-security and construction-disruption interests without resolving the ultimate factual dispute about the Big Bend region.

The strongest route to denial is the combination of the injunction's preliminary posture and respondents' record of irreversible physical harm. If that route prevails, the Court would leave the injunction in place while the district court completes its consideration of the merits, rather than necessarily endorse its major-questions analysis.

Justice Alito already requested a response on October 22, and respondents filed it on October 29. No initial response request remains to occur; the **response-requested-increment probability is 0%**, with this already-fired rung expected to be treated as vacuous. This does not predict whether a supplemental filing might be requested. The frozen record counts **one amicus submission**, including FAIR's submitted brief. There is a **25%** chance that the count rises beyond one before resolution; acceptance of the existing submission alone would not be an increment. No additional amicus entry is the modal forecast.

The leading timing window is the next several days to roughly two weeks after October 29, 2026, potentially following a government reply. Timing is an unscored forecast. A reasoned dissent is plausible but not certain. Jackson and Sotomayor are the most likely individual writers; an unexplained order, including an order with noted votes but no separate opinion, remains quite possible. The per-Justice writing probabilities concern authorship, not merely joining someone else's writing.
