This is the **forward, interim, response-filed** moment for application 26A560. The context records response_requested=true, referred_to_court=false, and amicus_briefs=1. Its null salience band is normal for an interim application and supplies no cert-stage anchor.

I did not retrieve this application's outcome, subsequent history, or live docket, and I do not know its disposition. General-authority retrieval is itemized in retrieval.md.

## Published anchor

I used the committed metrics/statpack.md interim-applications section and the corresponding metrics/statpack.json counts. For application Term 2026, the prescribed prior ten-Term window is **2016 through 2025**. Those rows contain **160 substantive grants among 1,366 substantive resolutions**, giving **11.713%**, comfortably above the 50-resolution floor. I exclude Term 2026, extensions, and the pack-wide rate. The pool contains **16 unparsed applications**, concentrated in 2016 and 2017; parse coverage and machine-matchable resolution selection limit representativeness. These are counts from the committed pack, not a claim about a freshly queried live corpus.

This is an unconditional application baseline, not an empirical success rate for federal-government applicants with requested responses. Published escalation counts include pending matters and terminal observations, so I did not turn them into a conditional response-filed rate. Selection itself favors applications high on the escalation ladder. The large upward adjustment is case-specific judgment, not a measured lift or evidence of forecasting skill.

## Why substantially above that anchor

The government's strongest argument is jurisdictional. Application pages 15–19 argue that IIRIRA section 102(c) allows constitutional challenges but excludes statutory ones, and that respondents' major-questions theory ultimately contests the Secretary's statutory authority. I checked Dalton v. Specter, 511 U.S. 462 (1994), through CourtListener; its distinction between statutory overreach and a constitutional violation supports that argument, though it does not decide this case automatically. The REAL ID Act's waiver and review provisions were also checked in the official public-law text.

Independently, application pages 19–23 emphasize express delegation to waive legal requirements for construction, the agency's border-security mission, and repeated prior use of that power. That is a substantial answer to treating this as an agency's discovery of an entirely new kind of regulatory authority. The dispute over how high entry must be may look to the Court like ordinary statutory interpretation rather than an independently enforceable constitutional prohibition.

The requested stay also has a direct review route. The application explains that review of these district-court decisions proceeds to this Court rather than through the court of appeals. Thus the lack of a circuit ruling is not the ordinary failure to exhaust an available appellate path. Application pages 24–31 also assert continuing security harms and nonrecoverable contractor suspension costs. I treat the amounts and operational descriptions as the government's allegations, not independently established facts. Taken together, these points make a stay materially more likely than the generic interim baseline.

## Why not near certainty

The opposition is substantive, not a formal response without countervailing evidence. Its pages 14–17 argue that major questions safeguards constitutional separation of powers and distinguish Dalton; pages 19–23 argue that the waiver power remains bounded by areas of high illegal entry. I put more weight on the government's answer, but the distinction between constitutional and statutory claims is the principal legal uncertainty, and other pleaded constitutional claims need not fail merely because the injunction's current rationale is vulnerable.

Opposition pages 23–37 provide a serious equities case: alleged irreparable environmental, property, cultural, and livelihood injuries; relatively low apprehension levels; existing enforcement methods; a voluntary construction pause; and the government's failure to present evidence at the injunction hearing. Those points weaken the claim that all delay is an emergency and favor preserving the status quo while summary-judgment proceedings continue. Pages 37–40 also make interlocutory posture and unresolved claims concrete objections to summary reversal. I have not read the separately linked evidentiary appendices or independently verified the factual disputes, which is a limitation rather than grounds to presume either advocate's version true.

I reserve 20% for ungranted outcomes, predominantly denial, including the possibility of a mixed partial order that the resolver collapses to denial. The 80% number is a calibrated judgment, not the output of a fitted conditional model. The response request is evidence of attention, not a commitment to grant.

## Additional forecasts and significance

Referral at 0.95 reflects the scope of the requested relief and the expected involvement of the full bench, not a referral already present in the record. The response-requested increment is 0.00 because its triggering act already happened and the claim is vacuous here. Amicus increment at 0.25 reflects the possibility of another submission during a short remaining window; FAIR's existing submitted brief already counts. These incremental probabilities have no published conditioned baseline.

The order-writer probabilities are independent authorship forecasts, not a vote lineup or a distribution summing to one. They allow both no separate writing and writings by more than one Justice. Jackson and Sotomayor receive the highest probabilities because an objection to disturbing a fact-developed protective injunction is a plausible source of a separate opinion; that is a subjective forecast, not a retrieved result.

The **0.79 big-case score** reflects extensive border construction, potentially irreversible damage to a distinctive region, and the institutional significance of waiver authority and review limits. It measures stakes separately from the probability that the government wins interim relief.
