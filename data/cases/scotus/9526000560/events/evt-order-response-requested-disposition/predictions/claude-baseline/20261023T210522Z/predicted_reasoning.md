# Forecast — 26A560, Mullin v. Friends of the Ruidosa Church (stay application)

**Disposition: the full Court grants the stay of the W.D. Tex. preliminary injunction (P ≈ 0.74 of an unqualified grant).**

- **Record state at my vantage.** Application submitted to Justice Alito on Oct 21, 2026; Justice Alito called for a response on Oct 22, due Oct 29. No referral to the full Court, no amicus entries.
- **Response requested (increment).** Already fired; a further call is not a meaningful possibility. ~0.05.
- **Referral to the full Court.** Likely. A Solicitor General application against a nationwide-impact injunction, coupled with a request to treat the application as a cert petition and summarily reverse, is the kind of matter a Circuit Justice refers rather than decides alone. ~0.85.
- **Amicus filings.** Moderately likely before disposition: border states (Texas and allies) supporting the government and environmental or religious-heritage groups supporting respondents are plausible filers within the week-plus window. ~0.55.
- **Timing.** Disposition shortly after the response and any reply — most likely in the first half of November 2026, by unsigned order.
- **Form.** Most likely a short order granting the stay pending the timely filing and disposition of a cert petition, with a noted dissent from two or three Justices (Sotomayor, Kagan, Jackson most likely). The Court probably declines the invitation to treat the application as a petition and summarily reverse; if it does take that route, it could grant cert and set briefing instead, which would be a less clean "grant" reading. A partial stay (e.g., carving out Big Bend National Park's ~17-mile project area) is the main way this resolves as something other than an unqualified grant.
- **Conditional on the merits.** If the Court explains itself, the likely ground is IIRIRA § 102(c)(2)(A)'s bar on non-constitutional claims: a major-questions argument is a claim that the Secretary exceeded statutory authority (Dalton v. Specter), not a constitutional claim, so the district court likely lacked jurisdiction.
