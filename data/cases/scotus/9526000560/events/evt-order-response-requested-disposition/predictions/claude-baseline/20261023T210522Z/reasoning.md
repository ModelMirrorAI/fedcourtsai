# Why 0.74

**Inputs.** Snapshot `2026-10-23.json` (two entries: application submitted Oct 21; response requested by Justice Alito Oct 22, due Oct 29), `record/context.json` (forward mode, `band: null`, response_requested true, not referred, 0 amicus), and `record/documents/application.txt` (39 pages, not truncated, not OCR). No response has been filed or provisioned, so I have only the government's side.

**Anchor.** The statpack's interim-docket section, pooled over application-Terms strictly before 2026 (the Term's ten-year window holds parsed rows only for 2023–2025): 51 granted / 547 resolved substantive applications ≈ **9.3%**, which clears the floor of 50. That pool is dominated by private and prisoner applications. It is not conditioned on the applicant or on the escalation ladder. The pack's caption calls this the scored baseline, and I read it that way.

**Adjustments up (large).**
- The applicant is the Solicitor General. Recent Terms show SG stay applications granted in full or in large part at a rate far above the docket's. That is my general knowledge of the Court's practice, not a committed figure.
- The merits argument is strong. IIRIRA § 102(c)(2)(A) limits review to claims "alleging a violation of the Constitution". The district court's holding that the major-questions doctrine is a constitutional claim cuts against Dalton v. Specter and against the Court's own description of the doctrine as statutory interpretation. Every prior challenge to a § 102(c) waiver has failed (the application collects them, including a D.D.C. decision by now-Justice Jackson).
- Review lies only by certiorari, so the fair-prospect-of-cert prong is close to automatic: no court of appeals sits in between, and a novel district-court invalidation of a waiver is cert-worthy.
- On the equities, Trump v. Sierra Club (2019) stayed an injunction against border-wall construction on a similar balance of security interests against aesthetic and recreational ones.
- The district court issued the injunction without reasons for 12 days, a procedural irregularity under Rules 52 and 65 that the Court tends to note.

**Adjustments down.**
- The resolver reads a mixed order as denied, and I do not know whether the Court would grant only partial relief, e.g. leave the injunction in place for the Big Bend National Park project area while staying the rest. That partial-relief branch is my largest discount, about 0.10–0.12.
- The government also asks the Court to grant cert and summarily reverse. If the Court instead grants cert before judgment with expedited briefing and defers or reshapes the stay, the disposing entry may not read as a plain grant.
- Respondents' brief is not yet filed. Evidence of irreparable environmental harm, or a record that the Big Bend Sector does not meet "high illegal entry", could matter more than the application lets on.
- There is a small chance of withdrawal or mootness if the district court rules on summary judgment first.

Net: about 0.74. Calling for a response is the routine first step on an SG application and adds little beyond the applicant's identity.

**Ladder claims.** Response-requested has already fired, so the claim is vacuous; I state 0.05 for form. Referral 0.85: SG emergency applications are almost always decided by the full Court, and the docket usually records the referral at or near disposition. The main risk is that no separate referral entry is ever docketed. Amicus 0.55: this is a salient border and environmental fight with a week-plus window, but amici on emergency applications are not guaranteed.

**Where to discount me.** The SG-specific grant rate is from memory, not from the committed pack. I have read only one side's brief. I did no live retrieval (see `retrieval.md`).
