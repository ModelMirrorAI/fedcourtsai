- **Expected Vote Lineup**: **6–3 to grant the stay** (`Roberts, C.J., Thomas, Alito, Gorsuch, Kavanaugh, and Barrett, JJ.` voting to `grant`; `Sotomayor, Kagan, and Jackson, JJ.` voting to `deny` and noting their dissent).
- **Order Format**: The Court is most likely to issue a standard shadow-docket stay order (either an unsigned order or a brief *per curiam* opinion similar to *National Park Service v. National Trust for Historic Preservation*, No. 26A203, or *Department of Homeland Security v. The Court is unlikely to construe the application as a petition for a writ of certiorari and grant plenary review immediately at the stay stage, preferring to let the government file its certiorari petition within the 60-day statutory window under IIRIRA § 102(c)(2)(C).
- **Substantive Grounds (Conditional on Explanation)**:
  - **Primary Ground — Jurisdictional Preclusion Under IIRIRA § 102(c)(2)(A)**: If the majority or a concurring opinion explains the basis for the stay, it will rest primarily on Congress's express command in IIRIRA § 102(c)(2)(A) (8 U.S.C. § 1103 note) that a claim arising from the Secretary's § 102(c)(1) waiver decisions "may only be brought alleging a violation of the Constitution of the United States" and that "[t]he court shall not have jurisdiction to hear any claim not specified in this subparagraph." Consistent with *Mullin v. Doe*, No. 25-1083 (U.S. June 25, 2026), *Patel v. Garland*, 596 U.S. 328 (2022), and *Dalton v. Specter*, 511 U.S. 462 (1994), the majority will conclude that the government has a strong likelihood of showing that § 102(c)(2)(A) forecloses non-constitutional *ultra vires* statutory challenges to predicate determinations under § 102(a) and (b).
  - **Secondary Ground — Statutory Authority, Major Questions Doctrine, and Overbreadth**: Even reaching the statutory merits, the majority will conclude that IIRIRA § 102(a) and (c)(1), reinforced by Congress's $46.55 billion border-barrier appropriation in the One Big Beautiful Bill Act (Pub. L. No. 119-21, § 90001), expressly authorize the Secretary to install physical barriers and roads and waive legal requirements, distinguishing *Learning Resources, Inc. v. Trump*, No. 24-1287 (U.S. Feb. 20, 2026). Additionally, the majority will view the 300-mile sector-wide injunction as overbroad under *Trump v. CASA, Inc.*, 606 U.S. 831 (2025), and will find that halting $4.5 billion in border-barrier contracts at a cost of $173,000 per day inflicts irreparable harm on the government under *Winter v. Natural Resources Defense Council, Inc.*, 555 U.S. 7 (2008).
  - **Dissenting Rationale**: The dissenting Justices will emphasize that the government forfeited its substantive statutory arguments under § 102(a)–(b) in opposing the preliminary injunction in the district court, that Big Bend has the lowest border-crossing numbers of any southwest sector, and that blasting and bulldozing in Big Bend National Park and around the historic adobe Ruidosa Church will inflict permanent, irreversible environmental and cultural destruction before appellate review can occur.
