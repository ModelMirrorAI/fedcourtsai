## Conditional Merits and Vote Lineup

- **Expected Vote Split**: 6–3 to grant the stay (Chief Justice Roberts and Justices Thomas, Alito, Gorsuch, Kavanaugh, and Barrett voting to grant; Justices Sotomayor, Kagan, and Jackson voting to deny and likely noting their dissents on the order).
- **Conditional Rationale (if explained or reached)**:
  - **Exclusive Supreme Court Review (`IIRIRA § 102(c)(2)(C)`)**: Because Congress stripped the courts of appeals of appellate jurisdiction over challenges to IIRIRA § 102(c) waivers and channeled review of any interlocutory or final district court order exclusively to the Supreme Court via certiorari, there is a high likelihood that the Court would grant review if the injunction remained in place.
  - **Likelihood of Success on the Merits (`IIRIRA § 102(c)(2)(A)` and Major Questions Doctrine)**: Under IIRIRA § 102(c)(2)(A), a claim challenging a waiver determination "may only be brought alleging a violation of the Constitution." The district court bypassed Plaintiffs' Article I nondelegation claim and instead treated the major questions doctrine as an independent constitutional cause of action to invalidate the Secretary's determination that the Big Bend Sector is an "area[] of high illegal entry" requiring expeditious barrier construction to achieve "operational control" (`IIRIRA § 102(a), (b)(1)`). A majority of the Court will view the major questions doctrine as a canon of statutory interpretation rather than a standalone constitutional claim capable of overcoming § 102(c)(2)(A)'s jurisdictional bar, and will further conclude that § 102(a)–(c) and the 2006/2008 amendments expressly vest the Secretary with broad discretion to install barriers and waive legal requirements along the southwest border.
  - **Overbreadth and Equities (`Trump v. CASA, Inc.`)**: Even apart from the threshold statutory/constitutional merits, the district court's blanket injunction halting construction across all 510 miles of the Big Bend Sector runs afoul of *Trump v. CASA, Inc.*, 606 U.S. 831 (2025), because it extends far beyond the discrete parcels and recreational/historical sites where Plaintiffs established concrete injury, and under *Trump v. Sierra Club*, 588 U.S. 930 (2019), and *National Park Service v. National Trust for Historic Preservation*, No. 26A203 (Aug. 31, 2026), the balance of equities favors allowing congressionally funded federal construction contracts to proceed during litigation.
