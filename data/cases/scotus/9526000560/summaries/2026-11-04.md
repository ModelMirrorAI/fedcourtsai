## In brief

The federal government asks the Court to pause an injunction blocking border wall construction in Texas's Big Bend region while litigation continues.

## What happened

The Secretary of Homeland Security issued waivers in 2026 exempting border-wall construction in the Big Bend Sector from various environmental and other laws, citing a federal statute allowing such waivers in areas of high illegal entry. Friends of the Ruidosa Church and other groups sued, arguing the waivers were unlawful. A federal district court in Texas agreed, preliminarily enjoining construction after finding the challengers were likely to succeed on a claim that the waivers exceeded the Secretary's authority. The government asked the district court to pause its own injunction pending Supreme Court review; the district court refused. The government then brought this application directly to the Supreme Court, the only avenue for review under the statute's special rules.

## What the Court is being asked

The government asks the Court to pause the injunction while the case continues, arguing the challengers' claim is really about statutory authority, not the Constitution, so the district court had no power to hear it, and that the waivers were lawful anyway. It separately asks the Court to treat this filing as a petition and immediately decide the case in the government's favor. The challengers respond that the claim does raise a constitutional separation-of-powers question the district court could properly hear, and that the waivers exceeded the Secretary's authority.

## Where it stands

The application has been fully briefed: the government filed its request, the challengers filed an opposing response, an outside group filed a supporting brief, and the government filed a reply. The docket shows no order yet from the Court or the assigned Justice.

## What each outcome would mean

The federal government, as applicant, is asking the Court to pause the injunction that favored Friends of the Ruidosa Church and the other challengers in the lower court. Granting the stay would let border-wall construction resume in the Big Bend Sector while the case continues, pausing the lower court's block. Denying it would leave the injunction in place, continuing to stop construction under the challenged waivers. Either way the order would be temporary, and the underlying lawsuit would continue in the lower courts. The government separately asked the Court to immediately decide the case in its favor without full briefing or argument; granting that request would reverse the lower court outright, while declining it would leave the case to proceed through ordinary review.
