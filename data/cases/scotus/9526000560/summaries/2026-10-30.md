## In brief

The federal government asks the Court to lift an injunction blocking border wall construction waivers in Texas's Big Bend region while the underlying lawsuit continues.

## What happened

The Secretary of Homeland Security issued waivers under a 1996 immigration law allowing border barrier construction in the Big Bend Sector without complying with various environmental and other laws. Friends of the Ruidosa Church and other groups sued, arguing the waivers were unlawful. A federal district court in Texas issued a preliminary injunction blocking construction, finding the challengers were likely to succeed on a claim that the waivers improperly exceeded the Secretary's authority without clear congressional authorization. The court also denied the government's request to pause that injunction while the government seeks Supreme Court review.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked whether to pause the lower court's injunction while litigation continues, and separately whether to treat this request as a request to hear the case immediately and reverse the lower court without full briefing or argument. Underlying these requests is a dispute over whether a court may hear a claim that the Secretary exceeded his statutory waiver power, and whether the Big Bend region qualifies as an area where that power may be used.

## Where it stands

The government filed an emergency application for a stay, the Court asked the challengers to respond, and they filed their opposition along with an amicus brief and other filings. The application remains pending; the docket does not show any ruling on it yet.

## What each outcome would mean

The government, as applicant, is asking the Court to pause the injunction and, alternatively, to take up and summarily decide the case now. Granting the stay would let border wall construction resume in the Big Bend Sector while the lawsuit continues in the lower court; denying it would leave construction blocked during that time. Either way, the stay decision itself would not resolve the underlying lawsuit. If the Court also grants the request to treat this as a petition for certiorari and summarily reverse, it would decide the case now in the government's favor without full briefing; if it declines that request, the case would continue through normal proceedings below.
