## In brief

The Supreme Court stayed a district court injunction that had blocked border wall construction waivers in the Big Bend region of Texas, pending any further appeal.

## What happened

The Secretary of Homeland Security issued waivers under a 1996 immigration law allowing him to bypass environmental and other legal requirements to speed border barrier construction in the Big Bend Sector. Friends of the Ruidosa Church and other groups sued, arguing the waivers were unlawful. A Texas federal district court agreed, finding the challengers were likely to succeed on their claim that the waivers violated a doctrine requiring clear congressional authorization for major decisions, and it blocked construction. The government asked the Supreme Court to pause that injunction while it pursued review, and also asked the Court to treat the request itself as a petition for review and immediately overturn the lower court.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked whether the lower court had authority to hear this challenge at all, given a law limiting such challenges to constitutional claims, and whether the Secretary's waivers were properly authorized in an area the lower court found had comparatively low rates of illegal border crossings.

## Where it stands

The Court received the stay application, asked for and received a response and reply, and on November 9, 2026 granted the stay. The construction injunction is paused while the government's anticipated petition for review proceeds, with Justice Jackson noting she would have denied the stay.

## What each outcome would mean

The government asked the Court to pause the injunction. By granting the stay, the Court allowed border wall construction in the Big Bend Sector to resume while the government pursues further review, pausing the lower court's block on the waivers. If the government's certiorari petition is later denied, the stay ends automatically, and construction could again face the injunction. If certiorari is granted, the stay continues until the Court decides the case.
