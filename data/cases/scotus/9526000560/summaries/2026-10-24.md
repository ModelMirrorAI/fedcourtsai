## In brief

The government asks the Court to pause a district court order blocking border wall construction in Texas's Big Bend Sector pending further review.

## What happened

The Secretary of Homeland Security issued three waivers in 2026 allowing border barrier and road construction across roughly 300 miles of the Big Bend Sector, suspending certain environmental and other legal requirements as federal law permits. Friends of the Ruidosa Church, the Center for Biological Diversity, and an individual river guide sued, arguing the waivers exceeded the Secretary's authority. A federal district court in Texas agreed, issuing a preliminary injunction halting construction in the sector. The court later denied the government's request to pause that injunction while the government seeks Supreme Court review, though it acknowledged the government would suffer some harm from the delay.

## What the Court is being asked

The government asks the Court to pause the lower court's injunction while review continues, arguing the district court had no authority to hear this kind of challenge to the waivers and, even if it did, wrongly read the law to limit the Secretary's waiver power. The government separately asks the Court to treat this request as a full appeal, take the case immediately, and reverse the lower court without further briefing or argument.

## Where it stands

The government filed this application on October 21, 2026. A justice has asked the respondents to reply by October 29, 2026; the Court has not yet ruled on the request.

## What each outcome would mean

The government is asking the Court to act. If the Court grants the stay, construction could resume while the case continues, and the lower court's injunction would not apply during that time. If the Court denies the stay, the injunction stays in effect, blocking construction in the Big Bend Sector for now. Either way, this is a temporary decision; it does not resolve the underlying lawsuit, which continues in the lower courts unless the Court also acts on the government's separate request to take up and decide the case immediately.
