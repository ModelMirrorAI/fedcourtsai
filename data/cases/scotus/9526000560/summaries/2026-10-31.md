## In brief

The federal government asks the Court to pause a district court order blocking border wall construction in Texas's Big Bend region while the underlying lawsuit continues.

## What happened

The Secretary of Homeland Security issued three waivers in 2026 suspending environmental and other legal requirements to speed border barrier construction in the Big Bend Sector, under a federal law letting the Secretary waive legal requirements he deems necessary for expeditious construction. Friends of the Ruidosa Church, an environmental group, and others sued, arguing the waivers exceeded the Secretary's authority. After a hearing, a federal district court in Texas issued a preliminary injunction blocking construction, finding the challengers likely to succeed on their claim that the waivers improperly covered an area without sufficiently high illegal entry. The court later denied the government's request to pause that injunction while it seeks Supreme Court review.

## What the Court is being asked

The government asks the Court to pause the district court's injunction while it pursues review, and separately asks the Court to treat this request as a full appeal, take up the case immediately, and reverse the lower court without further briefing or argument. The underlying dispute concerns whether a legal rule requiring clear congressional permission before an agency exercises sweeping new power applies to limit the Secretary's waiver authority, and whether the courts even have power to hear that kind of challenge given a provision limiting lawsuits over these waivers to constitutional claims.

## Where it stands

## What each outcome would mean

The federal government, through the Secretary of Homeland Security, is asking the Court to pause the lower court's injunction and, ultimately, to reverse it. Granting a pause would let border wall construction resume in the Big Bend Sector while the case continues; denying it would leave the construction halt in place. Either way, this would be temporary and would not finally decide the case. If the Court also grants the request to treat this as a full appeal and summarily reverses, that would end the case in the government's favor without further proceedings; declining to do so would leave the question for ordinary litigation, including a pending summary judgment ruling in the district court.
