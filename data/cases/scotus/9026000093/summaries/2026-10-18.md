## In brief

A home-distilling hobbyist asks the Court to strike down the federal ban on distilling spirits at home as beyond Congress's power.

## What happened

John Ream wants to distill whiskey at home but federal law bans producing distilled spirits in a dwelling house, backed by criminal penalties. Ream sued before taking that step, arguing the ban exceeds Congress's power. A district court found he lacked standing to sue before being prosecuted. The Sixth Circuit disagreed on standing but ruled against him on the merits, holding the ban a valid, necessary and proper tool for collecting the federal excise tax on spirits. That decision conflicts with a Fifth Circuit ruling in a similar case, McNutt, which struck the same ban down. One Sixth Circuit judge dissented, saying Ream still lacked standing.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked whether the home-distilling ban falls outside Congress's enumerated powers, including its power to tax and its power to regulate interstate commerce. Ream also asks the Court to reconsider or narrow a past ruling that gave Congress broad authority to regulate local, noncommercial activity when regulating it helps enforce rules on an interstate market. The federal government agrees review is warranted but argues the Court should instead take up its own companion case, McNutt, as a better vehicle, holding this case until that one is resolved, and wants the Court to leave aside the commerce-power and precedent-reconsideration questions.

## Where it stands

The petition has been distributed for three conferences, most recently one dated after the reply brief was filed, and the docket shows no decision yet on whether the Court will hear the case.

## What each outcome would mean

Ream asks the Court to hear his case. If the Court grants review, it would hear the case on the merits and decide the constitutional questions; a grant does not decide who wins. If the Court denies review, the Sixth Circuit's ruling upholding the ban stands, at least for this case, leaving federal law on home distilling to differ between circuits. The Court could also grant the government's separate McNutt petition and hold or send back Ream's case for further handling in light of that ruling, without resolving Ream's claims now.
