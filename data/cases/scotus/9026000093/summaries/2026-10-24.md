## In brief

John Ream asks the Court to strike down the federal ban on distilling spirits at home, arguing Congress lacked power to enact it.

## What happened

John Ream wants to distill whiskey at home but says a federal law banning distilling in a dwelling house stops him. He sued the Treasury Department, arguing the ban exceeds Congress's power. A district court dismissed his case, finding he lacked standing because he had not taken concrete steps toward distilling or faced a specific threat of prosecution. The Sixth Circuit reversed on standing but ruled against him on the merits, holding the ban is a valid way to help collect taxes on distilled spirits. One judge dissented on standing. A different appeals court, the Fifth Circuit, had reached the opposite conclusion in a similar case called McNutt.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked whether a long-standing federal ban on producing spirits in a home goes beyond the powers the Constitution gives Congress, including its power to tax and to regulate interstate commerce. A related question is whether the Court should overturn or narrow a past decision, Gonzales v. Raich, that gave Congress broad authority to regulate local, noncommercial activity. The government agrees review is warranted but wants the Court to take up a different case, McNutt, covering only the tax-related question, and to hold Ream's case until that one is decided.

## Where it stands

The petition has been distributed for conference more than once, most recently for October 16, 2026, and the docket shows no action on it since that conference.

## What each outcome would mean

Ream, who lost on the merits below, is asking the Court to take up his case and reverse that ruling. If the Court grants review, it would hear the case in full and decide the constitutional question itself, without yet deciding who wins. If it denies review, the Sixth Circuit's decision upholding the ban stands, leaving Ream unable to distill at home. The government has suggested the Court instead grant review in the related McNutt case and hold Ream's petition pending that outcome, which would delay any decision on Ream's case specifically.
