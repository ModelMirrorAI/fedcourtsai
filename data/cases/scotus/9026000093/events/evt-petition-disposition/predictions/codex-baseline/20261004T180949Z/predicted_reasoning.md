# Forecast of the Court's action

The Court will most likely hold Ream while it considers the government's companion petition in McNutt, then grant, vacate, and remand Ream in light of the companion decision. The predicted final disposition is GVR. The probability of any grant-family disposition is 60%; this is not a 60% probability of plenary review or overruling Raich.

## Procedure and timing

The record already shows two distributions, with a rescheduling between them, and no CVSG. It does not establish that the Court has considered the petition at two conferences. From that starting point, the probability of at least one additional distribution is 85%. Expect one or two further distribution entries around coordination or eventual disposition, rather than a long uninterrupted series of weekly relists.

The modal path is no final disposition at the October 9, 2026 conference, followed by a hold extending into 2027 if McNutt receives plenary review. On that path, late spring or summer 2027 is the likeliest final-disposition window. An October or November 2026 grant taking both cases remains a less likely alternative. Timing is a forecast, not a separately scored claim.

There will probably be no CVSG: the probability of a new invitation is 0.5%. The Solicitor General already represents the federal respondents and has supplied the government's position, so another invitation is unlikely to advance the Court's consideration.

## Question and route

If the Court grants plenary review here, it will most likely focus on whether the home-distilling prohibition is necessary and proper to execution of the taxing power, narrowing the first question rather than taking a free-standing invitation to overrule Raich. The companion petition is the likelier lead vehicle for that issue. The Court will probably leave the commerce-power question for another procedural setting rather than reach an alternative rationale not decided below and no longer advanced by the government at this stage.

Conditional on a grant-family outcome, the probability of disposition in the cert order itself is 80%. A companion-based GVR is much more likely than a summary reversal deciding the constitutional issue without argument. If McNutt sustains the prohibition, denial of Ream is the leading alternative; if it undermines the Sixth Circuit's taxing rationale, a remand is the leading consequence. A hold itself is neither route's final resolution.

Conditional on denial, the probability of any noted dissent or statement respecting denial is 18%. The modal denial has no separate writing. If there is a writing, concern about leaving the broader commerce-power challenge unresolved is the likely theme; no individual Justice's vote or authorship is forecast.
