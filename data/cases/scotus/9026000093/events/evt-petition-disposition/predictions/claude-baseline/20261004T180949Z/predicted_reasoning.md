# Forecast: what the Court will do with No. 26-93, Ream v. Department of the Treasury

**State forecast from.** The snapshot shows two distribution entries: distributed for the September 28, 2026 conference, rescheduled on September 8 before that conference met, and redistributed for the October 9, 2026 conference. No conference has yet considered the petition, so there has been no true relist. No CVSG is on the docket, and none can be: the Solicitor General is counsel for the respondents. Eleven amicus briefs support the petition, including one from Ohio and fourteen other states.

**The petition's fate is tied to No. 26-204, Department of Justice v. McNutt.** The government filed its own petition from the Fifth Circuit's contrary decision on August 14, 2026, the same day it responded here, and asked the Court to grant McNutt and hold Ream. Both petitions are distributed for the October 9 conference. The reschedule on September 8 was almost certainly to align Ream with McNutt's briefing.

**Most likely path: McNutt granted, Ream held, Ream then disposed of in light of McNutt.** I expect the Court to grant certiorari in McNutt at or shortly after the October 9 conference, probably after one relist, and to hold Ream rather than grant it. The government has unquestioned standing concerns about Ream (the district court and the panel dissenter found no standing), McNutt carries only the question the courts of appeals actually decided, and Ream's counsel already represents the McNutt respondents, so the challengers' side is fully presented without a second grant. I put roughly 70% on a hold, roughly 25% on the Court granting both petitions and consolidating them (limited in substance to the taxing-power question), and about 5% on an outright denial of Ream at the conference.

**Relists.** Further distributions are very likely (about 83%). A held petition is redistributed once McNutt is decided, and even a grant now would ordinarily follow one relist under the Court's current practice.

**CVSG.** None. The United States is a party.

**Which question the Court would take.** If the Court grants Ream, it takes question 1 as the government framed it: whether the home-distilling location restriction is necessary and proper to Congress's taxing power. It will not grant on question 2 (overruling or confining Raich); no court of appeals addressed the Commerce Clause theory and the government has abandoned it.

**Summary disposition.** Conditional on any grant, roughly half the probability is a GVR rather than plenary review: if Ream is held and the Court affirms the Fifth Circuit in McNutt, the Sixth Circuit judgment upholding the prohibition is vacated and remanded in light of McNutt. The other half is a consolidated plenary grant alongside McNutt. My merits view for McNutt is that the government is more likely than not to prevail (roughly 2 to 1), relying on Felsenheld, Comstock, and Dewitt's own distinction of distilling regulations as plainly adapted to collecting the tax, so a held Ream ends more often in a denial than in a GVR.

**Dissent from denial.** If Ream is denied after the government wins McNutt, I expect no separate writing most of the time. The exception is a statement by Justice Thomas respecting the Raich question, which he has written on before in a denial posture; I put that at under one in five.

**Timing.** The hold scenario means the petition will not be disposed of until McNutt is decided, most likely by late June 2027. The grant-both scenario resolves in October or November 2026.
