# Prediction Reasoning

This case involves a petition for certiorari by a private party (John Ream) against the federal government (Department of the Treasury). The petitioner challenges the federal prohibition on home distilling (26 U.S.C. § 5601(a)(6)), arguing it exceeds Congress's enumerated powers and asking the Court to overrule or clarify *Gonzales v. Raich*.

## Signals and Context
- **Band**: "elevated" (frozen at prediction). According to the statpack, the risk-set base rate for the elevated band is historically around 14-18%.
- **Amicus Briefs**: The case has generated extraordinary interest, with 11 amicus briefs filed at the cert stage, representing a broad ideological spectrum and prominent think tanks (e.g., Cato Institute, Goldwater Institute, Pacific Legal Foundation). This is a strong indicator of high salience and cert-worthiness.
- **Circuit Split**: The petition asserts a direct circuit split, stating that the Sixth Circuit upheld the prohibition "breaking with a recent decision of the Fifth Circuit holding precisely the opposite." 
- **Federal Respondent**: The federal government won below, but the presence of a circuit split on the constitutionality of a federal statute makes this a prime candidate for Supreme Court review to ensure uniform application of federal law.

## Probabilities
- **Disposition**: Given the combination of a direct circuit split on a federal statute's constitutionality and immense amicus interest (11 briefs), the likelihood of a grant is extremely high, well above the baseline for the "elevated" band. I estimate the probability of grant at 0.75.
- **Relist Increment**: Cases of this magnitude and complexity are typically relisted at least once as the Justices finalize their views and prepare dissent-from-denial or concurrence opinions, or simply to take a closer look. I assign a high probability (0.85) to a relist.
- **CVSG Increment**: The federal government (Department of the Treasury) is already a party and has filed a response. The Court does not call for the views of the Solicitor General when the SG is already participating. Therefore, the probability of a CVSG is 0.0.

## Big Case Rationale
A challenge to *Gonzales v. Raich* regarding the scope of the Commerce Clause, combined with a circuit split on home distilling, marks this as a high-stakes, highly newsworthy case. Score: 0.85.- **Dissent from Denial**: If the Court were to deny cert despite the circuit split and strong amicus interest, there is a reasonable likelihood that one or more conservative justices (e.g., Thomas, Gorsuch) would pen a dissent from denial (0.15), given the constitutional significance of the commerce clause challenge.
- **Summary Disposition Route**: A summary reversal or GVR is unlikely because this case presents a substantive constitutional question that would benefit from plenary review, though there's a small chance if the Court feels the lower court egregiously misapplied existing precedent (0.10).
