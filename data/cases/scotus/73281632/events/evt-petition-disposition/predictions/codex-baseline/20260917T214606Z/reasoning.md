## Why below the anchor

The petition's strongest argument is that CFPB's actual funding scheme had a numerical cap, leaving room to distinguish FHFA's scheme. Its historical appropriation examples and its contention that general agency expenses impose insufficient qualitative restraint are genuine cert arguments, not merely an assertion that the outcome below was wrong. Its proposed holding could have significant institutional consequences. The response request after waiver is also affirmative attention, which keeps my estimate above a routine low-interest petition.

Against that, petition pp. 16–38 does not establish an inter-circuit conflict on the federal question. The government's opposition, pp. 3–7, argues that both constitutional theories are applications of recent controlling decisions rather than unresolved conflicts. The majority's source-and-purpose reasoning in CFPB v. Community Financial Services Ass'n, 601 U.S. 416, 426–427, 441 (2024), confirmed through CourtListener opinion 11066682, makes the proposed numerical-cap requirement difficult to extract from that decision. FCC v. Consumers' Research, 606 U.S. 656, 680–681 (2025), confirmed through opinion 11243407, rejects the necessity of numeric limits where qualitative constraints suffice. The latter does not automatically establish that every agency's constraints suffice; it does make a grant to revisit the general numerical-cap proposition less likely. I give greater weight to those majority rationales than to the petition's reliance on dissenting views.

The BIO also identifies three vehicle risks, pp. 8–9: standing, claim preclusion from earlier quiet-title litigation, and preservation of the separate Section 4516(f)(2) theory. These are the respondents' arguments, not established defects. The Ninth Circuit expressly upheld standing, including traceability under Collins and redressability (petition appendix pp. 7a–10a), and the petition disputes the preservation objection (pp. 28–30). Nonetheless, those additional disputes reduce the appeal of this case as a clean vehicle for revisiting funding doctrine. I therefore lower the band anchor to 7%, while leaving meaningful upside for interest in distinguishing FHFA or in the nondelegation question.
