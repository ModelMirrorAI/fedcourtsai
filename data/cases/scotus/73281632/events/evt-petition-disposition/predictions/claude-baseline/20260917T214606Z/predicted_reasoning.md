# Forecast: Daisey Trust v. FHFA, No. 25-1144 (evt-petition-disposition)

**Disposition.** The Court will deny certiorari, most likely on the order list following the September 28, 2026 long conference or, if relisted, within one or two conferences after it. P(any grant) = 0.07.

**Relists.** The docket shows two distributions: the first (for the May 14 conference) was superseded when the Court called for a response on May 5, and the second (July 22) set the fully briefed petition for the September 28 long conference. So this petition is effectively at its first substantive conference and has never been relisted. I expect it to be denied without relist more often than not; the chance of at least one further distribution is about 0.30, driven by the possibility that a Justice who dissented in *CFPB v. CFSA* or *FCC v. Consumers' Research* takes a second look or drafts a statement. If it relists, expect one relist, not a string.

**CVSG.** None. The Solicitor General is already counsel of record for the respondents and has filed the brief in opposition, so there is no federal view left to call for. P ≈ 0.01.

**Question presented, if taken.** If the Court took anything it would be QP 1 alone, whether the Appropriations Clause requires a cap or other ascertainable limit beyond source and purpose. QP 2 (the "shall not be construed to be appropriated money" clause) was not developed below and the panel did not address it; QP 3 (nondelegation) runs into *Consumers' Research*'s express rejection of a numeric-cap requirement. A grant would be plenary, not summary.

**Summary disposition.** Very unlikely conditional on a grant (about 0.05). No intervening decision bears on the question, so there is nothing to GVR in light of, and a per curiam reversal of a unanimous published panel applying two recent precedents faithfully is not a plausible route.

**Dissent or statement respecting denial.** Conditional on denial, about 0.10 that any Justice notes a dissent or files a statement. The most plausible author would be Justice Gorsuch or Justice Alito on the Appropriations Clause theme, but the vehicle (HOA-sale investor plaintiffs, contested standing, a claim-preclusion defense, an unpreserved QP) gives little reason to write.

**If granted, conditionally.** The likely outcome would be affirmance on the ground that *CFSA* requires only an identified source and purpose, with the nondelegation claim resolved under *Consumers' Research*'s qualitative-standard holding. The Court would probably not reach QP 2.
