## In brief

The Supreme Court declined to review a property investors' challenge to the Federal Housing Finance Agency's funding structure, leaving a Ninth Circuit ruling against them in place.

## What happened

Daisey Trust and other investors bought homes at foreclosure sales, later finding the properties still carried mortgage liens held by Fannie Mae or Freddie Mac, which the Federal Housing Finance Agency (FHFA) oversees as conservator. Facing foreclosure, they sued FHFA, arguing its funding method—collecting assessments from Fannie Mae and Freddie Mac without any congressional cap—was unconstitutional. A federal district court dismissed the suit, and the Ninth Circuit Court of Appeals affirmed, finding FHFA's funding structure consistent with a recent Supreme Court decision upholding a similarly structured federal agency.

## What the Court is being asked

The investors asked the Court to decide whether an agency's funding counts as a lawful appropriation of government money when Congress names where the money comes from and what it can be used for, but sets no maximum amount the agency can collect or spend. They also asked whether Congress can validly declare that such funds do not count as government money for constitutional purposes, and whether letting the agency's director decide what counts as "reasonable" costs gives the executive branch too much unchecked power to tax and spend.

## Where it stands

The petition was distributed for conference twice, and the government, after initially waiving its right to respond, filed a brief opposing review once the Court requested one. The Court denied the petition on October 5, 2026.

## What each outcome would mean

Daisey Trust and the other investors asked the Court to take up their case. With the petition denied, the Ninth Circuit's ruling stands, meaning FHFA's funding structure remains in place and the investors' constitutional challenge fails, leaving them without a basis to block foreclosure on that ground. A grant would have meant the Court would have fully heard and decided the funding question itself, without presuming either side's position correct.
