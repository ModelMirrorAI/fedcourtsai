## In brief

The Court declined to hear property investors' challenge to the funding structure of the Federal Housing Finance Agency, which they said let the agency foreclose unlawfully.

## What happened

Daisey Trust and related entities bought properties at foreclosure sales; the properties were still subject to mortgage liens held by Fannie Mae and Freddie Mac, which the Federal Housing Finance Agency (FHFA) oversees as conservator. Facing foreclosure, they sued FHFA, arguing its funding—assessments on Fannie Mae and Freddie Mac with no spending cap—violates the constitutional requirement that government spending be appropriated by law, and improperly gives the agency open-ended power that Congress should have limited. A federal district court dismissed the suit, and the Ninth Circuit affirmed, finding the funding scheme constitutional.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked whether an agency's funding scheme must include a cap or other measurable limit on how much it can raise and spend to satisfy the rule that government money be spent only as authorized by law, whether Congress can declare that an agency's funds do not count as government money for that purpose, and whether the law authorizing FHFA's funding gives the agency clear enough limits on its discretion to collect and spend money.

## Where it stands

The petition was distributed for two conferences, with the Court requesting a response from FHFA after the first distribution. The Court denied the petition.

## What each outcome would mean

Daisey Trust and the other investment entities asked the Court to take up their case and reverse the Ninth Circuit's ruling. By denying review, the Court leaves the Ninth Circuit's decision in place, meaning the lower court's ruling that FHFA's funding structure is constitutional stands, and FHFA's funding and foreclosure authority over these properties remains intact. Had the Court granted the petition, it would have heard full arguments and decided the funding questions itself, without that happening now.
