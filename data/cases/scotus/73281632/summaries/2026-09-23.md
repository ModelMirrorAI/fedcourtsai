## What happened

Daisey Trust, Cape Jasmine Court Trust, and Saticoy Bay LLC bought homes at foreclosure sales, but the properties were also subject to mortgage liens held by Fannie Mae and Freddie Mac, which the Federal Housing Finance Agency (FHFA) oversees as conservator. Facing foreclosure by FHFA, the trusts sued, arguing that FHFA's funding structure—assessments it collects from Fannie Mae and Freddie Mac rather than through the normal congressional budget process—was unconstitutional. A federal district court dismissed the suit, and the Ninth Circuit Court of Appeals agreed, ruling that the funding law was constitutional.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked whether a federal agency's funding scheme is invalid because Congress did not set any dollar cap, sum, or other measurable limit on how much money the agency can collect and spend, even though the law does say where the money comes from and what it can be used for. A related question is whether Congress can validly declare, by statute, that the funds the agency collects do not count as government money subject to that constitutional rule. The case also asks whether the law gives the agency's director clear enough guidance to satisfy the separate rule that Congress cannot hand over its lawmaking power to an agency without setting boundaries.

## Where it stands

The trusts asked the Court to take up the case, and FHFA opposed, arguing there is no conflict with other courts and that procedural issues—like whether the trusts have the right to sue and whether their claims are barred by an earlier lost lawsuit—could prevent the Court from even reaching the questions. After both sides filed briefs, including a reply from the trusts, the case was distributed for the Court's conference on September 28, 2026, where the Justices will decide whether to hear it.
