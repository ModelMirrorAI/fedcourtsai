## In brief

Investment trusts ask the Court to hear their claim that the Federal Housing Finance Agency's funding structure is unconstitutional.

## What happened

Daisey Trust and related entities bought properties at foreclosure sales that were encumbered by mortgage liens held by Fannie Mae and Freddie Mac, which the Federal Housing Finance Agency (FHFA) regulates and controls as conservator. Facing foreclosure, they sued FHFA, arguing its funding method—collecting assessments from Fannie Mae and Freddie Mac without any congressional cap on the amount—is unconstitutional. A federal district court dismissed the suit, and the United States Court of Appeals for the Ninth Circuit affirmed, holding the funding scheme satisfies constitutional requirements because it identifies a source and purpose for the funds and gives the FHFA director adequate guidance.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked whether a federal agency's funding arrangement is constitutionally valid even though Congress set no dollar limit, cap, or other measurable ceiling on how much the agency can collect and spend, and whether Congress may validly declare that such funds do not count as government money subject to the ordinary constitutional rules for spending public funds. The Court is also asked whether the law gives the agency's director clear enough boundaries on spending decisions, consistent with rules limiting how much authority Congress can hand to executive agencies.

## Where it stands

The petition has been distributed for conference twice, most recently for the conference of September 28, 2026, after full briefing including the government's opposition and the trust's reply. The docket shows no order yet on whether the Court will hear the case.

## What each outcome would mean

Daisey Trust and the other petitioners are asking the Court to take up the case. If the Court grants review, it would hear full arguments and decide the funding and delegation questions itself, deciding nothing in advance about who is right. If the Court denies review, the Ninth Circuit's ruling upholding FHFA's funding structure stays in place, and the agency's foreclosure authority over petitioners' properties continues unaffected, without that denial being a ruling on the merits.
