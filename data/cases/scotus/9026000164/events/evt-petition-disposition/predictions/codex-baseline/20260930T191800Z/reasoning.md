## Why 16%, rather than the anchor alone

**The petition identifies a substantial procedural question.** The QP asks about notice and an opportunity to participate, not a constitutional entitlement to win custody. Petition pp. 21-27 distinguishes procedural liberty interests from substantive parental rights, and pp. 30-34 identifies competing approaches to nonparent caregivers and biological kinship. The allegation that a permanent adoption was entered without notice to existing caregivers is serious. The petition describes a 4-3 state supreme court decision. Seven amicus filings and the Court's request for an opposition after waiver are additional reasons not to treat this as a routine low-salience petition. These observations support a meaningful grant probability and the relatively high stakes score.

**The opposition presents specific obstacles to reaching that question.** BIO pp. 16-20 says the broader nonparent-caretaker theory was waived, quoting the state court's footnote 27 at App. 48a. It argues that this is an adequate and independent state ground and, independently, a discretionary reason to deny review. Petition pp. 26-27 answers in substance that procedural and substantive claims were both presented and that the distinction was emphasized on rehearing. I do not resolve that conflict in respondents' favor as an established jurisdictional fact. Nevertheless, a preservation dispute about the very theory offered for review materially reduces vehicle quality.

BIO pp. 21-25 also disputes whether the cited decisions establish a square conflict, rather than different results under different custody arrangements. Its pp. 25-28 stresses the statute of repose, the timing of actual notice, contested kinship and guardianship, and petitioners' foreign residence when the adoption was entered. Petition p. 25 n.5 itself acknowledges that the trial judge declined to find a biological relationship and says that finding was appealed. Thus I do not adopt either party's characterization of kinship, fraud, or ultimate custody entitlement as an independently established fact. Success on the abstract liberty-interest question might still leave substantial issues unresolved.

**The distribution sequence is weaker than a clean deliberative relist.** The August distribution preceded a requested response that arrived September 16; the next distribution names October 16. This could reflect completion and rescheduling of briefing rather than sustained post-conference interest. The pack expressly warns that distribution counts are an upper bound on true relists. I retain the harness's elevated band while discounting any additional inference that the Court has already repeatedly deliberated.

Taken together, the serious procedural question, amicus attention, and requested opposition approximately offset the preservation, factual, and timing obstacles. My final **16%** is slightly below the **16.89%** reached-band anchor. The adjustment is judgmental, not a fitted likelihood ratio. Denial is the modal outcome, but the record supports appreciably more than a negligible chance of review.

## Other probabilities and limitations

The **38%** additional-distribution estimate allows for further examination of preservation and the humanitarian and governmental interests while leaving no further distribution as the modal path. The **15%** CVSG estimate reflects a real federal interest but not a federal question on which government participation is necessarily required. Neither is a measured conditional hazard from the statpack. The **12%** summary-route estimate is conditional on a grant: this contested vehicle is less suited to immediate correction than to briefing and argument. The **20%** separate-writing estimate is conditional on denial and reflects the sharp disagreement and seriousness of the alleged notice failure, not a forecast of private cert votes.

The petition is marked **truncated**. Its argument and QP are present, but its appendix ends before the crucial App. 48a passage; the BIO is complete. Consequently I relied on the BIO's quotation and the petition's counterargument rather than independently inspecting that entire state-court passage. No reply is provisioned. This asymmetry especially limits confidence in the waiver analysis and is flagged durably.

General-law web searches and an attempted official rules PDF retrieval returned no usable content. I used no external search result as evidence and did not retrieve this petition's outcome, subsequent history, or decision coverage. I have no known outcome for this petition. No CourtListener MCP or corpus-query lookup was made. This prediction rests on the provisioned pre-decision materials and committed aggregate context, not a resolution discovered elsewhere.
