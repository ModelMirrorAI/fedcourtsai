# Forecast of the Court's action

The Court will most likely deny the petition. My probability of any grant, including a GVR or summary reversal, is **16%**.

The snapshot records **two distributions**: August 12 for the September 28, 2026 conference, and September 30 for the October 16 conference. A response was requested between those entries and the opposition was filed September 16. I do not treat the second distribution as proof of a second substantive consideration by the Justices.

The modal path is denial following the October 16 conference, without another distribution. I assign **38%** to at least one additional distribution beyond the recorded two. If the case continues, one or two additional distributions during October or November are more likely than an extended series. My central disposition window is October-November 2026; a call for the Solicitor General's views would instead push the decision out by months.

No CVSG appears on the record. I forecast no invitation, but assign **15%** to a new CVSG. The United States' involvement in the child's transfer and the international dimensions make an invitation plausible, even though the question presented concerns procedural rights in state adoption proceedings. Any invitation is most likely at the October conference or a shortly following conference.

If review is granted, I expect the Court to take the petition's core question: whether an established nonparent caregiving relationship can create a liberty interest requiring notice and an opportunity to be heard before an adoption permanently ends that relationship. It is more likely to focus on that procedural protection than to recognize parental-equivalent substantive custody rights. A grant would not itself determine who should have custody or establish the disputed biological relationship.

Conditional on a grant, **12%** is assigned to disposition in the cert order itself, and **88%** to a route requiring further merits proceedings. The disputed preservation issue and the unsettled threshold for a nonparent liberty interest make plenary review more plausible than summary reversal. I do not forecast a particular intervening decision as the basis for a GVR.

Conditional on denial, I assign **20%** to a noted dissent or statement respecting denial. The central forecast is a denial without a separate writing; I do not forecast a particular Justice. Such a denial would leave the judgment below intact without supplying a Supreme Court merits rationale.
