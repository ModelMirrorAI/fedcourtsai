# Forecast: what the Court will do with No. 26-164, *A.A. v. Mast*

**Bottom line.** The petition is more likely denied than granted, but it is a
genuine contender: P(any grant) 0.29. The likeliest grant form is plenary
review on the question presented as written; a hold followed by a GVR in light
of *Genalo v. Black* (No. 25-886, argued this Term) is the secondary grant
route.

## Relists

The docket shows **two distributions**: August 12 for the September 28
conference, and September 30 for the October 16 conference. The first was
superseded when the Court requested a response on August 17 (respondents had
waived), so October 16 is the first conference at which the petition will
actually be considered. Forecast from that state: **P(at least one further
distribution) 0.60.** A grant would almost certainly follow a relist rather
than issue at first consideration; a hold pending *Genalo* would produce a run
of redistributions; and a denial with a separate writing usually costs a relist
or two. The path to no further distribution is a clean denial on October 16,
which for a petition this prominent is plausible but not the modal outcome.
If relisted, expect one to three further distributions, with a decision by
late November or early December 2026 unless the petition is held.

## Call for the views of the Solicitor General

No CVSG is on the docket. **P(CVSG issues) 0.15.** The federal interest is real
and unusual for a state adoption case: the State and Defense Departments
arranged the child's placement with petitioners in Afghanistan, the United
States filed statements of interest and lower-court amicus briefs calling the
adoption a fraud on the court, and then withdrew its Virginia Supreme Court
amicus brief in March 2025 without disavowing it. The Court might want the
current Solicitor General's position on the record. Against that, the question
presented is a Fourteenth Amendment procedural-due-process question against a
state statute of repose, the kind of family-law question on which the Court
rarely invites the government, and the government's withdrawal signals it does
not want to be in the case. If a CVSG issues, it would come out of the October
16 or a November conference, with the brief filed in spring 2027 and a
decision on the petition by June 2027.

## Which question the Court would take

If it grants plenary review, the Court takes the question presented as written
(whether, or under what circumstances, a child's nonparent caretakers have a
cognizable liberty interest entitling them to procedural due process before an
adoption order permanently removes the child from their care). The respondents'
counterstatement adds a threshold question about waiver and an adequate and
independent state ground; the Court would not add it to the grant but would
have to satisfy itself on it, and a grant limited to the federal question
presented is the most likely form. Respondents' extraterritoriality argument
(that foreign nationals abroad hold no Fourteenth Amendment rights) would be
briefed as an alternative ground for affirmance rather than granted as a
question.

## Summary disposition versus plenary review

**P(disposition in the cert order | grant) 0.25.** A per curiam summary
reversal is unlikely: the record is contested, the trial court found petitioners
did not prove kinship and rejected the trafficking narrative, and the legal
question has divided lower courts for fifty years. The realistic cert-order
route is a hold followed by a GVR in light of *Genalo v. Black*, which the
petition itself cites for the distinction between substantive and procedural
due process claims. That depends on *Genalo* saying something about which
liberty interests trigger procedural protection, which is uncertain. If the
Court reaches the merits after argument, it would more likely vacate than
affirm, but that is conditional on a plenary grant and is not forecast here as
an unconditional claim.

## Dissent or statement from a denial

**P(some Justice writes on a denial | denial) 0.30.** The facts are
extraordinary and sympathetic to petitioners, the state supreme court split
4-3, seven amicus briefs support the petition, and the federal government's
own earlier filings called the adoption fraudulent. A statement respecting the
denial that stresses the waiver footnote and the fact-bound record, or a
dissent arguing the due-process question is squarely presented, is the shape
to expect. The likeliest authors are Justice Sotomayor or Justice Jackson, with
Justice Gorsuch the plausible cross-ideological voice given the rule-of-law and
foreign-sovereign framing; authorship is context only and is not scored.
