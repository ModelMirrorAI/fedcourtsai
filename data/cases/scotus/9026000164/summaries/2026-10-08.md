## In brief

A couple who raised a child in Afghanistan ask the Court to revive their challenge to a Virginia adoption that took the child from them.

## What happened

A.A. and F.A. say they cared for a child, orphaned in a 2019 U.S. military operation in Afghanistan, after Afghan authorities identified them as her relatives. The petition says that around the same time, Joshua and Stephanie Mast obtained adoption orders from a Virginia court without telling petitioners or the U.S. government, and later took the child after she reached the United States. A.A. and F.A. sued to vacate the adoption. A circuit court and the Virginia Court of Appeals ruled the adoption void, but the Virginia Supreme Court, 4-3, reversed and dismissed the challenge, holding Virginia's six-month deadline for attacking adoption orders applied and that petitioners had no constitutional right to notice because they were not the child's parents.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked whether, and under what circumstances, someone who has cared for a child but is not a biological or legal parent has a constitutionally protected interest requiring that they be given notice and a chance to be heard before a court permanently places the child with someone else through adoption.

## Where it stands

The petition was distributed for conference twice, after the respondents waived and then were asked to respond, and the respondents have filed a brief opposing review; both sides have now filed their briefs, including a reply from the petitioners, and the case awaits the Court's decision on whether to hear it.

## What each outcome would mean

A.A. and F.A., who lost below, are asking the Court to take up the case. If the Court grants review, it would hear the case in full and decide the due-process question; granting does not itself decide who wins. If the Court denies review, the Virginia Supreme Court's decision dismissing the challenge stands, leaving the adoption in place and the child with the Masts. The respondents argue, among other things, that petitioners waived the specific argument now pressed and that the case is a poor vehicle for deciding the question, positions the Court would not need to resolve if it denies review.
