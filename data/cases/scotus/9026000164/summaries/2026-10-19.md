## In brief

The Supreme Court declined to review a Virginia custody dispute over a child adopted by the Masts after an Afghan battlefield rescue, leaving the adoption intact.

## What happened

A.A. and F.A. say they were raising a young girl in Afghanistan, with the Afghan government's blessing, when they learned that Joshua and Stephanie Mast had secured a Virginia court order adopting her. After the family fled to the United States in 2021, the Masts took custody of the child under that order. A.A. and F.A. sued in Virginia to undo the adoption, citing fraud and lack of notice. Virginia trial and appellate courts largely sided with them, but the Supreme Court of Virginia reversed, ruling their challenge was barred by a state deadline for contesting adoptions and that they had no constitutional right to notice because they were not the child's legal parents.

## What the Court is being asked

The question is whether, and under what circumstances, someone who has been caring for a child but is not a legal parent has a constitutional right to be notified and heard before a court finalizes an adoption that permanently removes the child from their care.

## Where it stands

The petition was distributed for conference twice, and the respondents filed a brief opposing review while the petitioners filed a reply; on October 19, 2026, the Court denied the petition.

## What each outcome would mean

A.A. and F.A., as petitioners, asked the Court to take up their case. The denial means the Court will not hear it, leaving in place the Supreme Court of Virginia's ruling that upheld the adoption and rejected the petitioners' constitutional claim. The Masts' custody of the child, secured through that adoption order, remains undisturbed, and A.A. and F.A. have no further avenue in this case to challenge it before the Supreme Court.
