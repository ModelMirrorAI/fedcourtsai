## In brief

A.A. and F.A. ask the Court to revive their constitutional challenge to a Virginia adoption order that gave their family member to the Masts, who raised her instead.

## What happened

A.A. and F.A. say Afghan authorities placed an orphaned child with their family in Afghanistan, but a Virginia court, without notifying them, had already granted Joshua and Stephanie Mast an adoption order for the same child. After the child reached the United States, the Masts took custody. A.A. and F.A. sued in Virginia to undo the adoption, citing fraud and lack of notice. A trial court and the Virginia Court of Appeals sided with them, but the Supreme Court of Virginia reversed, ruling 4-3 that Virginia's six-month deadline for challenging adoptions barred most of their claims and that they lacked a constitutional right to notice because they were not the child's legal or biological parents. The Masts respond that A.A. and F.A. waited too long, waived their arguments, and that the Masts rescued and lawfully adopted the child.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked whether, and under what circumstances, people who have cared for a child but are not her legal parents have a constitutional right to be notified and heard before a court permanently places that child with someone else through adoption.

## Where it stands

The petition was distributed for conference twice, most recently for October 16, 2026, after the Masts filed their opposition and A.A. and F.A. filed a reply; the docket does not yet show an outcome.

## What each outcome would mean

A.A. and F.A., as petitioners, are asking the Court to take up the case. If the Court grants review, it would hear full arguments and decide the notice question itself, without yet deciding who wins. If the Court denies review, the Virginia Supreme Court's ruling stands, leaving the adoption order intact and the child in the Masts' custody. A denial would not mean the Court agrees with the Virginia court's reasoning, only that the case ends there for now.
