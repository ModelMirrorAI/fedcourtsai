## In brief

The Supreme Court vacated a lower court order blocking Alabama's 2023 congressional map and sent the case back for reconsideration after its ruling in a similar Louisiana case.

## What happened

Alabama's 2021 congressional map was found likely to dilute Black voters' voting power, so Alabama passed a new 2023 map that kept certain regions, including the Gulf Coast, together in single districts. Voters led by Marcus Caster sued, arguing the new map still violated the Voting Rights Act by not creating a second district where Black voters could elect their preferred candidates. After a trial, a federal district court permanently blocked the 2023 map, finding it violated the Voting Rights Act and was enacted with discriminatory intent. Alabama appealed and asked the Supreme Court to take the case before the appeals court ruled.

## What the Court is being asked

The case asks whether a state's redistricting plan can be struck down for not creating an additional district where a racial group can elect its preferred candidates, when doing so would require splitting up a region the state says it has legitimate, non-racial reasons to keep together. It also asks whether requiring a state to draw such a district can itself violate the Constitution's equal protection guarantees, and whether private voters may sue to enforce the relevant voting-rights law at all.

## Where it stands

The Supreme Court granted Alabama's petition, vacated the district court's judgment, and sent the case back to the appeals court with instructions to return it to the district court for further review in light of the Court's recent decision in a Louisiana redistricting case. Justice Sotomayor, joined by Justices Kagan and Jackson, dissented. A related emergency request by Alabama for a stay was denied as unnecessary once the Court acted.

## What each outcome would mean

Alabama, which asked the Court to intervene, sought to overturn the lower court's ruling blocking its map. By vacating that ruling and sending the case back, the Court set aside the injunction against Alabama's 2023 map without deciding whether the map is lawful. The district court must now reconsider the dispute using the legal standards from the Court's recent Louisiana ruling. Caster and the other voters, who won the injunction below, no longer have that ruling in place while the case is reconsidered, and the map's ultimate legality remains undecided.
