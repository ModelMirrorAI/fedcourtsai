## What happened

Black voters challenged Alabama's 2023 congressional redistricting map, arguing it diluted their voting strength by keeping only one of Alabama's seven districts as a district where Black voters could elect their preferred candidates. This followed an earlier round of litigation in which the Supreme Court upheld an injunction against a similar 2021 map. After an 11-day trial, the district court permanently blocked the 2023 map, ruling it violated the Voting Rights Act's protections against vote dilution and finding it was enacted with discriminatory intent. Alabama appealed to the Eleventh Circuit and, before that court ruled, asked the Supreme Court to take the case directly.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked whether Alabama's map improperly weakened Black voters' ability to elect their preferred candidates, and separately, whether the legal test courts use to decide such vote-dilution claims is itself a valid exercise of Congress's power, or whether applying it the way the lower court did goes too far.

## Where it stands

The Court granted the petition, vacated the district court's judgment, and sent the case back to the Eleventh Circuit with instructions to return it to the district court for further review in light of a newer decision, Louisiana v. Callais. Justice Sotomayor, joined by Justices Kagan and Jackson, dissented, and a related emergency stay application was denied as moot.
