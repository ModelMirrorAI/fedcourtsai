A **summary disposition by GVR**—granting certiorari, vacating the Ninth Circuit's judgment, and remanding for further consideration in light of *Department of Homeland Security v. D.V.D.*, No. 26-426—is far likelier than setting the case for plenary briefing and oral argument. The Solicitor General explicitly asks the Court to hold this petition pending *D.V.D.*, where the Court has already stayed the district court's judgment and directed briefing on threshold district-court jurisdiction (Question 1), and where the First Circuit expressly relied on the Ninth Circuit's decision here in construing 8 U.S.C. § 1252(g). A decision in *D.V.D.* enforcing § 1252(g) (or related § 1252 channeling limits) against legal-authority challenges to third-country execution of removal orders, or repudiating the Ninth Circuit's premise that § 1252(g) reaches only discretionary actions, will trigger a routine unsigned post-decision GVR order here.

If *D.V.D.* instead resolves on a threshold standing or programmatic-APA ground that leaves individual-execution jurisdiction under § 1252(g) undecided without supporting a GVR, and the Court grants **plenary review** in this case to resolve the 5–1 circuit split over individual execution claims, the Court will take the petition's **single Question Presented as written**: whether 8 U.S.C. § 1252(g) bars an alien with a valid final order of removal from bringing a claim in federal district court that the government lacked legal authority in executing that order. Conditional on plenary review, the Court would likely reverse on textualist grounds, holding that § 1252(g)'s bar on claims "arising from the decision or action ... to ... execute removal orders" contains no exception for challenges framed as attacks on legal authority.

Should the Court **deny** certiorari—most plausibly on a routine post-*D.V.D.* mop-up order list if a majority in *D.V.D.* rejects the government's reading of § 1252(g) on grounds that sustain the Ninth Circuit's rule, or less plausibly if the Court passes over this interlocutory FTCA remand on vehicle grounds—expect **no separate writing** on the denial order, with only a modest chance that Justice Thomas or Justice Alito (possibly joined by Justice Gorsuch) notes a dissent or authors a brief dissent from denial if the circuit split remains open.
