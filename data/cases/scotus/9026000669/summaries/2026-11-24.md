## In brief

The federal government asks the Court to hold a case about whether a jurisdiction-limiting immigration law blocks a deported man's lawsuit, pending its ruling in a related case.

## What happened

Jorge Felix Ibarra-Perez, a Cuban national, was granted protection from removal to Cuba but was then sent by immigration officials to Mexico, a country not named in his removal order, despite telling officials he feared returning there. He sued the United States for damages, arguing officials lacked legal authority to remove him to Mexico without first letting him raise that fear. A district court dismissed the suit, ruling a federal law stripped it of jurisdiction. The Ninth Circuit reversed, reading that law narrowly; the full appeals court declined to rehear the case, over several judges' objections. The government now asks the Supreme Court to review that ruling.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked whether a law that blocks courts from hearing claims arising from the government's decisions to start, decide, or carry out deportation cases also blocks a claim that the government lacked legal authority to carry out a deportation in the way it did.

## Where it stands

The government filed its petition asking the Court to take up the case, but asks that it be held until the Court decides a related case already granted review, where the same legal question arises; the deadline for Ibarra-Perez's response is December 23, 2026.

## What each outcome would mean

The United States, as petitioner, asks the Court to take up the case and reverse the Ninth Circuit's decision, which allowed Ibarra-Perez's suit to proceed. If the Court grants review, it would hear the case in full and decide the legal question, without yet deciding who wins. If it denies review, the Ninth Circuit's decision favoring Ibarra-Perez stands, and his suit continues in the lower courts. The government has asked that the petition instead be held pending its related case.
