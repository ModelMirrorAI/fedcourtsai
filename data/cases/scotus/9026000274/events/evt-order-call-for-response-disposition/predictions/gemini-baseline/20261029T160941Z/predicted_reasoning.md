## Scope of Review and Route if Granted

- **Question presented if granted (context-only):** If the Court were to grant review, it would consolidate or reformulate Questions Presented I and II into a single question asking whether simultaneous convictions under 18 U.S.C. § 2251(a) (production of child pornography) and 18 U.S.C. § 2422(b) (coercion and enticement of a minor) for the same conduct violate the Double Jeopardy Clause when § 2251(a) serves as the predicate "sexual activity for which any person can be charged with a criminal offense" under § 2422(b).
- **Summary disposition vs. plenary review (`summary-disposition-route`: 0.25 conditional on grant):** Conditional on a grant, plenary review (0.75) is substantially likelier than a summary disposition in the cert order itself (0.25). The First Circuit decided this case on May 6, 2026—nearly four months *after* *Barrett v. United States*, 607 U.S. 128 (Jan. 14, 2026)—and expressly cited and applied *Barrett* in its *Blockburger* analysis (Pet. App. 38a–39a). There is therefore no overlooked intervening Supreme Court decision warranting a routine GVR, and a GVR would occur only in the unlikely event that the Solicitor General confesses a charging or jury-instruction defect in the Brief in Opposition.
- **Conditional merits ground if granted:** If the Court granted plenary review, a majority would likely affirm the First Circuit—or dismiss the writ as improvidently granted once the plain-error posture is briefed—holding that even if the target criminal offense under 18 U.S.C. § 2422(b) is treated as an element under *Richardson v. United States*, 526 U.S. 813 (1999), and *Whalen v. United States*, 445 U.S. 684 (1980), §§ 2251(a) and 2422(b) still satisfy *Blockburger v. United States*, 284 U.S. 299 (1932), because § 2251(a) requires actual production (or interstate materials used to produce) a visual depiction whereas § 2422(b) requires only persuasion, inducement, enticement, or coercion using a facility of interstate commerce with the requisite intent, and need not result in a completed visual depiction.

- **Dissent or statement respecting denial (`dissent-from-denial`: 0.02):** The Court will almost certainly issue a summary denial order with no separate writing. While Justice Gorsuch (concurring in *Barrett*) and Justice Jackson (author of *Barrett*) have recently written on *Blockburger* and cumulative federal criminal punishments, and Justice Sotomayor occasionally writes respecting criminal-procedure denials, petitioner failed to preserve his Double Jeopardy objection in the district court, subjecting the claim to plain-error review under Federal Rule of Criminal Procedure 52(b).
