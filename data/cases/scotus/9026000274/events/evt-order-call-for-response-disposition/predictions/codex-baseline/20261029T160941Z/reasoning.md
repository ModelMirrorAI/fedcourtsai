## Why move from 5.1% to 10%?

The response request is meaningful positive evidence that at least one Justice wanted the government's explanation before dismissal. It moves this petition above an ordinary baseline petition, but it is a low-threshold screening act, not the stronger indication supplied by repeated post-conference relists or a recommendation to grant. I make an upward judgmental adjustment, not a claim that the pack measures a response-request multiplier.

The petition also raises a recurring federal statutory question rather than simply contesting the sufficiency of its evidence. Its core claim is that the separately charged production offense becomes part of the enticement offense when used as the latter's predicate. The petition's description of disagreement about predicate elements and jury unanimity makes the issue more substantial than a routine request for error correction. The incorporated published First Circuit opinion actually reaches the statutory comparison, which helps distinguish this from a wholly unreviewed issue.

Three constraints keep the probability low. First, **Appendix A at 36a expressly says the double-jeopardy objection was not raised at trial and is reviewed for plain error**. Its production/enticement discussion at 47a–49a finds no error at the first step, but the unpreserved posture still creates a potential obstacle to relief even if the statutory answer changes. I do not accept the petition's clean-vehicle characterization without that qualification. Its second question's independent preservation and role in the judgment are also less clear from the supplied materials.

Second, the opinion identifies the Sixth, Tenth, and Eleventh Circuits as agreeing with its result on this actual statutory pairing. The contrary Seventh Circuit authority described in the petition concerns predicate elements and unanimity. A disagreement about instructions or indictments is not necessarily a square disagreement authorizing opposite cumulative-conviction results on these facts. The petition's proposed doctrinal bridge is plausible, but its claim of a direct split is advocacy, not an independently established four-to-one split on the exact question. No en banc rehearing was sought, according to the petition.

Third, the original sentences run concurrently, and the lower court separately remedied a receipt/possession overlap. That does not erase the legal injury associated with extra convictions, but it makes the remaining question less likely to alter the aggregate prison term and less urgent as a vehicle. The Court's Barrett decision is invoked throughout the petition, yet the supplied appellate opinion itself cites it: this is not a straightforward case in which an intervening decision obviously requires a GVR. The government's waiver also provides no affirmative government support for review.

These considerations support a modest increase above the 5.1% anchor, not the much larger probability one might assign a square, preserved split with government support. **10%** is my calibrated judgment under those limitations, not a fitted conditional estimate.
