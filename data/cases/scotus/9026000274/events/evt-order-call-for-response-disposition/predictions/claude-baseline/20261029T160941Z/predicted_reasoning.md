# Forecast: Ortiz-Colón v. United States, No. 26-274

**Disposition: denied.** P(grant) ≈ 5%.

- **Where the docket stands.** It was distributed once (for the 10/9/2026 conference) after the United States waived a response. On 1 October 2026 the Court called for a response, due 2 November 2026. No relist and no CVSG appear on the record.
- **Further distribution.** This is near-certain. Once the government's brief in opposition (or an extension of its deadline) is in, the petition will be redistributed, most likely for a conference in late November or December 2026. I expect a single further distribution followed by a denial. A second relist after that is unlikely (about 10%).
- **CVSG.** None. The United States is the respondent, so the Court will hear from the Solicitor General directly through the requested response.
- **Expected response.** I expect the government to oppose. Its likely arguments: the circuit split is lopsided, with the Seventh Circuit's older position the outlier; the Ninth Circuit's internal disagreement is not a clean split; and the question has little practical effect where sentences run concurrently. The record does not yet show any of this.
- **If granted.** The Court would most likely take QP I (the Double Jeopardy/Blockburger question) and read QP II (element vs. means of the incorporated "criminal offense" in § 2422(b)) as subsumed in it, or reformulate the two as one question. Plenary review is far likelier than a summary disposition, because no intervening decision calls for a GVR.
- **Separate writing on denial.** Unlikely. If one appears, a statement from Justice Gorsuch on double-jeopardy stacking is the most plausible.
