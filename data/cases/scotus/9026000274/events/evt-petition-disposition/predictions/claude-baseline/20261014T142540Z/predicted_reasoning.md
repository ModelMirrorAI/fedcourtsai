# Predicted reasoning — Ortiz-Colón v. United States (No. 26-274)

**State forecast from.** The docket shows one distribution (for the 10/9/2026
conference, entered Sep 23), no relist, and no CVSG. The United States had waived
its response on Sep 18; on Oct 1 the Court **requested a response** (due Nov 2,
2026), which takes the petition off the 10/9 conference.

- **Further distribution: yes (very likely).** Once the Solicitor General's brief
  in opposition is filed (I expect a 30-day extension request into early
  December, which is routine), the petition will be redistributed for a
  conference in December 2026 or January 2027. That redistribution is the
  increment. A further relist past that one is unlikely.
- **CVSG: no.** The United States is the respondent and has been ordered to
  respond; the Court never invites the SG's views in a case where the SG is
  already a party.
- **Disposition: denied**, most likely on the order list after the
  post-response conference. Expect the SG's BIO to argue that the four
  circuits that reached the double-jeopardy question (CA6 *Hart*, CA10
  *Isabella*, CA11 *Sanchez*, CA1 below) agree, that CA7's *Mannava* concerns
  jury unanimity rather than *Blockburger*, and that the case is a poor vehicle
  because the issue matters only to concurrent-sentence structure.
- **If granted:** the Court would most likely take QP I (the double-jeopardy
  question) and reach QP II (element vs. means of the incorporated
  "criminal offense") only as a step toward it. A **summary disposition**
  (a GVR) is a real but minority path. It would most likely follow if the SG
  confesses error on the double-jeopardy count, or if the Court reads the
  recent *Barrett* double-jeopardy decision as calling the panel's
  *Blockburger* analysis into question. Plenary review is still the more likely
  route if the Court acts at all.
- **Dissent from denial:** not expected. If any Justice writes, Justice Gorsuch
  is the likeliest, given his separate writing in *Barrett* on "the same
  offence."
