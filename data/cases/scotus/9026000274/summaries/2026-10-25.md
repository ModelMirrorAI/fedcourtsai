## In brief

A man convicted of child pornography and related crimes asks the Court to decide whether his convictions under two overlapping federal laws violate double jeopardy.

## What happened

Francisco Ortiz-Colón was convicted by a jury in Puerto Rico of producing child pornography and separately of coercing and enticing minors, based on the same underlying acts involving nine minors. He was sentenced to 360 months in prison. On appeal, he argued, among other things, that convicting and punishing him under both laws for the same conduct violated the Constitution's protection against being punished twice for the same offense. The First Circuit affirmed, joining several other appeals courts in holding that the two laws involve different legal elements and do not trigger that protection.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked whether a person can be convicted and punished separately under two different federal laws when one law's violation is also used as the basis for satisfying an element of the other law, arising from the same conduct. A related question asks whether that incorporated requirement counts as a formal element of the second offense, which would matter for double jeopardy purposes, or merely describes one way the offense can be committed. Ortiz-Colón's petition says federal appeals courts disagree on these questions.

## What each outcome would mean

Ortiz-Colón, who lost in the lower court, is asking the Court to take up his case. If the Court grants the petition, it will hear full arguments and decide whether his convictions violate double jeopardy protections, resolving disagreement among federal appeals courts on the question. If the Court denies the petition, the First Circuit's decision upholding his convictions and sentence stands, and the case ends without the Supreme Court deciding the underlying legal question.
