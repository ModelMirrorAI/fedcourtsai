I adjust strongly downward to P(any grant) = 0.18 because this is a contingent GVR request whose identified legal catalyst, as described in the later opposition, did not deliver the premise the government sought. Federal petitions seeking plenary review of an important conflict are not interchangeable with a derivative petition after an apparently unfavorable lead decision. A materially relevant intervening opinion can still justify reconsideration even when the respondent argues it changes nothing, and the opinion does not resolve the separate felon-disqualification statute. Those considerations preserve substantial GVR probability rather than driving the forecast close to zero. My assessment is judgmental, not a fitted adjustment or a measured Hemani-companion base rate.

The main uncertainty is that the opposition's interpretation of Hemani could understate its effect on the Fifth Circuit's reasoning. CourtListener search independently located Hemani, docket 24-1234, filed June 18, 2026, but its opinion-text lookup reported no text available. Web attempts supplied no usable content. Thus I verified metadata, not the opinion's reasoning. I neither know nor sought Hembree's Supreme Court outcome.
