# Forecast: United States v. Hembree, No. 25-1219 (evt-petition-disposition)

**Disposition.** The Court will deny the petition, most likely on the order list following the September 28, 2026 long conference, for which the petition was distributed on July 15, 2026.

**Relists.** The docket shows one distribution (for the 9/28/2026 conference) and has never been relisted. I expect no further distribution: the petition arrives at conference with its stated basis already resolved, so there is nothing to hold it for. A single relist or reschedule remains possible (the Court sometimes carries a Solicitor General petition one conference before denying it), but it is the less likely path.

**CVSG.** None. The United States is the petitioner; the Court does not call for the views of the Solicitor General on the Solicitor General's own petition.

**Which question, if taken.** The petition does not ask for plenary review. It asks the Court to hold the case for *United States v. Hemani*, No. 24-1234, and then grant, vacate, and remand. Hemani was decided June 18, 2026, against the government (7 Justices in the majority, 2 concurring in the judgment; §922(g)(3) held unconstitutional as applied to a habitual marijuana user absent proof of intoxication or dangerousness, with an express statement that the Court was not addressing §922(g)(1)). So the requested GVR no longer has an intervening decision favoring the petitioner to rest on. If the Court nonetheless grants, the overwhelmingly likely form is a GVR rather than plenary review, either an unexpected GVR "for further consideration in light of Hemani" or, more plausibly, a hold pending some other §922(g)(1) vehicle the Court might grant this Term followed by a GVR if the government prevails there. Plenary review in this case, with the government's own question presented as written, is very unlikely: the Solicitor General did not request it, and the Court has denied every §922(g)(1) as-applied petition presented to it through June 2026, from both defendants (Diaz, Duarte, Vincent) and the government (Doucet, Cockerham, Mitchell).

**Summary disposition route (conditional on a grant).** If granted, a summary disposition (GVR) is the likelier route than plenary review, roughly three-to-two.

**Dissent or statement respecting denial.** None expected. The three government petitions from the Fifth Circuit denied this year on the same issue (Doucet on April 27, Cockerham on June 8, Mitchell on June 29, 2026) drew no noted dissent or statement, and neither did the defendant-side denials the opposition cites. A short statement by Justice Thomas or Justice Alito about the state of §922(g)(1) law is conceivable but unlikely.

**Timing.** Denial on the first order list after the long conference (on or about October 5, 2026, the Monday following the 9/28 conference and the start of the Term), or one conference later if relisted.
