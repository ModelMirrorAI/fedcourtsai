## What happened

Charles Hembree pleaded guilty to possessing a firearm as a convicted felon, based on a prior state conviction for simple possession of methamphetamine. He preserved the right to argue that the federal felon-in-possession law violates his Second Amendment rights given that his only prior conviction was for simple drug possession, not a violent or trafficking offense. The trial court rejected that argument, but the Fifth Circuit reversed, ruling that the government had not shown a historical tradition supporting permanent disarmament based on a simple-possession conviction, and it overturned Hembree's conviction. The federal government, representing the United States, then asked the Supreme Court to step in.

## What the Court is being asked

The government does not ask the Court to decide whether the appeals court's reasoning was wrong. Instead, it asks the Court to hold this case until it decides a related case about a different federal gun law covering drug users, and then send Hembree's case back to the lower court for reconsideration in light of that ruling. Hembree responds that the other case does not address permanent disarmament for felony convictions and expressly declined to reach the issue in his case, so sending his case back would serve no purpose.
