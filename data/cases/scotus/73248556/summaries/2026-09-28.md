## In brief

The government asks the Court to hold, and potentially send back for reconsideration, a ruling that Charles Hembree could not lose his gun rights over a simple drug-possession conviction.

## What happened

Charles Hembree has a 2018 felony conviction for possessing methamphetamine. In 2022 he was charged with illegally possessing a firearm as a felon. He argued this violated his Second Amendment right to bear arms, since his only disqualifying conviction was for simple drug possession rather than a violent or dangerous crime. The district court rejected that argument, and Hembree pleaded guilty while preserving his right to appeal. The Fifth Circuit reversed, ruling that the government had not shown a historical tradition supporting permanent disarmament based on simple drug possession, and it threw out his conviction.

## What the Court is being asked

The government does not dispute the Fifth Circuit's reasoning directly. Instead, it asks the Court to hold this case until it decides a related case about a different firearms law, and then, if appropriate, send Hembree's case back to the lower court for reconsideration in light of that decision. Hembree opposes this, arguing the other case would not affect his situation because it addressed a different statute and expressly did not decide the question raised here.

## Where it stands

The petition has been fully briefed, including Hembree's brief opposing review, and was distributed for the Court's conference on September 28, 2026. The docket shows no action from that conference yet.

## What each outcome would mean

The government, as petitioner, asks the Court to hold the case and potentially send it back for another look. If the Court grants that request, Hembree's win in the Fifth Circuit would be set aside and the case returned for reconsideration, deciding nothing yet for either side. If the Court denies the petition, the Fifth Circuit's ruling reversing Hembree's conviction stands, and his conviction remains overturned.
