# Forecast: what the Court will do with No. 25-1413, Bauer v. Marks

**Where the docket stands.** The petition was distributed twice: for the
September 28, 2026 long conference and, after a relist, for the October 9
conference. Between the two the Court called for the record (Record
Requested August 31; the Eighth Circuit box and the district court's
electronic record arrived September 9 and 16). No CVSG, no amicus brief on
this docket. Petitioner is a Minneapolis police officer sued in his
individual capacity; this is the case's second visit, after a May 2025 GVR in
light of Barnes v. Felix.

**Relists.** Expect at least one further distribution past the two shown
(about 70%). A record request before the first conference followed by a
relist is the shape of a chambers writing something, and both a per curiam
and a dissent from denial take several conferences to circulate. The
alternative is a clean denial on the order list after October 9. I expect
two to four more distributions rather than one.

**CVSG.** None. Two private parties, a Section 1983 excessive-force claim,
no federal statute or program at stake; the Solicitor General has no
interest to be asked about.

**Which question, if any.** The Court would not take the question presented
as written for plenary review. If it acts, it acts on the clearly-established
prong only, in the Kisela / Rivas-Villegas / Zorn v. Linton mould: accept the
Eighth Circuit's statement of the facts and hold that no precedent placed the
unlawfulness of a single less-lethal round, fired within seconds of the
plaintiff striking an officer and grabbing at his baton during a riot,
"beyond debate." It would not reach whether the panel disobeyed the Barnes
remand, and it would not decide whether a 40 mm round to the face is deadly
force.

**Route.** Conditional on any grant, a summary disposition is far the
likelier route (about 80%): a per curiam reversal on the clearly-established
prong, with no intervening decision to support a GVR. A plenary grant on a
fact-bound interlocutory qualified-immunity appeal, with no circuit split,
is the residual.

**If denied.** A denial is the single most likely outcome, but not a quiet
one: given the record call and relist I put the chance of a noted dissent
from denial, or a statement respecting it, at roughly 45%. The likeliest
author is Justice Alito or Justice Thomas, joined by Justice Gorsuch, on the
theme that the panel defined the right at a high level of generality after
the Court had already vacated once. A statement from the other side
defending the denial on Johnson v. Jones grounds is possible if a dissent
circulates.

**Timing.** If the Court denies without writing, the order comes on the
list following the October 9 conference. If a per curiam or dissent is in
the works, expect the disposition between late October and mid-December
2026, after further relists.
