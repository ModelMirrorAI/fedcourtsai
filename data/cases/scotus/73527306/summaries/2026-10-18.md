## In brief

A Minneapolis police officer asks the Court to review a ruling that he is not entitled to qualified immunity for shooting a man in the eye with a "less-lethal" launcher.

## What happened

In May 2020, Minneapolis officer Benjamin Bauer shot Ethan Marks in the eye at close range with a 40-millimeter projectile during an encounter near a riot, causing severe injuries; Marks later died, and his mother, Anne Marks, was substituted as plaintiff. Marks sued, claiming excessive force. The district court denied Bauer qualified immunity, finding disputed facts about the threat Marks posed. The Eighth Circuit affirmed. The Supreme Court earlier vacated that ruling and sent the case back for reconsideration under its decision in Barnes v. Felix, which addressed how courts should weigh the full sequence of events in force cases. On remand, the Eighth Circuit again ruled against Bauer, saying it had already applied the correct approach.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked to decide whether it was already clearly established that an officer's use of a less-lethal projectile against Marks, given everything that happened during the encounter, amounted to an unreasonable use of force. Bauer argues the appeals court still focused too narrowly on the instant force was used rather than the full situation, while Marks's side argues the lower court properly weighed the whole encounter and that disputed facts should go to a jury rather than be resolved by the Supreme Court.

## Where it stands

The petition has been distributed for conference three times, most recently for the October 16, 2026 conference, after full briefing including a response and reply. The docket does not yet show any decision on the petition.

## What each outcome would mean

Bauer, the officer, asked the Court to take up the case after losing twice before the Eighth Circuit. If the Court grants review, it would hear the case fully and decide whether the force used was clearly unlawful, without yet deciding who wins. If it denies review, the Eighth Circuit's decision stands, meaning Marks's lawsuit continues and Bauer does not receive qualified immunity at this stage, though the case could still proceed to a jury on the underlying facts.
