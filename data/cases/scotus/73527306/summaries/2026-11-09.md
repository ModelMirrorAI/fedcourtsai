## In brief

A Minneapolis police officer asks the Court to revive his qualified immunity defense after lower courts let an excessive-force lawsuit over a protest shooting proceed to trial.

## What happened

In 2020, during unrest following George Floyd's death, Officer Benjamin Bauer shot Ethan Marks in the eye with a "less-lethal" projectile launcher, causing severe injuries; Marks later died, and his mother, Anne Marks, continued the suit. Marks sued for excessive force. The district court denied Bauer qualified immunity, finding disputed facts about the threat Marks posed. The Eighth Circuit affirmed. The Supreme Court earlier vacated that ruling and sent the case back for reconsideration under a newer decision on how courts should weigh the full sequence of events, not just the instant force was used. On remand, the Eighth Circuit again ruled against Bauer, saying it had already applied that approach. Bauer again asks the Supreme Court to step in.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked whether, considering everything leading up to the shooting and not just the instant it happened, it was already clearly settled that using this kind of projectile on someone in Marks's position was an unreasonable use of force — the standard that determines whether an officer can be sued personally despite qualified immunity.

## Where it stands

The petition has been distributed for several conferences, most recently November 13, 2026, and the docket shows no action on it as of the snapshot date.

## What each outcome would mean

Bauer, the officer, is asking the Court to reverse the Eighth Circuit's decision, which left in place the denial of qualified immunity and allowed Marks's lawsuit to continue. If the Court grants review, it would hear full arguments and decide the legal question itself, deciding nothing yet about who wins. If it denies review, the Eighth Circuit's ruling stands, and the case returns to the lower courts for trial on Marks's excessive-force claim.
