## In brief

A Minneapolis police officer asks the Court to review a ruling that he is not entitled to qualified immunity for shooting a man in the eye with a less-lethal projectile.

## What happened

Ethan Marks was shot in the eye by Officer Benjamin Bauer during a 2020 protest, causing severe injuries; Marks later died, and his mother, Anne Marks, continued the lawsuit. Marks sued for excessive force, and the district court denied Bauer qualified immunity, finding disputed facts about whether the shooting was reasonable. The Eighth Circuit affirmed. The Supreme Court earlier vacated that ruling and sent the case back for reconsideration under its decision in Barnes v. Felix, which addressed how courts should weigh the circumstances surrounding a use of force. On remand, the Eighth Circuit again ruled against Bauer, saying it had not used the approach Barnes rejected. Bauer again asks the Supreme Court to step in.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked whether it was already clearly established, at the time of the shooting, that using a less-lethal projectile this way was an unreasonable use of force, such that Bauer should have been denied legal protection from suit. Marks's side argues the lower court properly applied the Supreme Court's guidance and that disputed facts should go to a jury rather than be decided by the Court on the petition papers.

## Where it stands

The petition has been distributed for conference twice, most recently for October 9, 2026, after full briefing including a response and reply. The docket shows no action yet on whether the Court will hear the case.

## What each outcome would mean

Bauer, the officer, is asking the Court to take up the case. If the Court grants review, it would hear full arguments and decide the legal question itself, without yet deciding who wins. If the Court denies review, the Eighth Circuit's decision stands, meaning the case returns to the lower courts and Marks's lawsuit against Bauer continues, with the question of qualified immunity resolved against Bauer for now. A denial would not mean the Court agrees with the lower court's reasoning, only that the decision remains in place.
