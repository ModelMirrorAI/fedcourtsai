## In brief

A Minneapolis police officer asks the Court to review a ruling that denied him immunity from a lawsuit over his shooting of a protester in the eye.

## What happened

During unrest following George Floyd's death in May 2020, Minneapolis officer Benjamin Bauer shot Ethan Marks in the eye at close range with a "less-lethal" launcher after Marks scuffled with another officer, Officer Pobuda. Marks, who later died (his mother Anne Marks now carries on the suit), sued Bauer for excessive force. The district court and the Eighth Circuit both ruled that disputed facts prevented giving Bauer qualified immunity, a legal protection that shields officials from suits unless they violated clearly established law. The Supreme Court earlier sent the case back for reconsideration under a new ruling, but the Eighth Circuit reaffirmed its decision.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked to decide whether the law was already clear enough, at the time of the shooting, that using a less-lethal projectile this way counted as an unreasonable use of force, such that Bauer should not get immunity from the lawsuit. Bauer's petition argues the appeals court kept using an improper method focused only on the instant force was used rather than the full situation; Marks's side answers that the lower court properly weighed everything and that the dispute is really just about facts a jury should resolve.

## Where it stands

The petition has been distributed for three scheduled conferences, most recently October 16, 2026, and the docket shows no decision yet on whether the Court will hear the case.

## What each outcome would mean

Bauer, the officer, is asking the Court to take up the case and reverse the ruling against him. If the Court grants review, it would hear full arguments and decide the legal question itself, deciding nothing in advance for either side. If the Court denies review, the Eighth Circuit's decision stands, meaning Bauer does not get qualified immunity and Marks's lawsuit over the shooting continues in the lower courts.
