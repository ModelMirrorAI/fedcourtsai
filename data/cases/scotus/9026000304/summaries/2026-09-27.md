## What happened

Sigal Chattah served as interim U.S. Attorney for the District of Nevada under a 120-day appointment. When that term expired, the Attorney General named her First Assistant U.S. Attorney and also delegated her authority to handle federal prosecutions there. Defendants in several Nevada criminal cases, including Devonte Devon Jackson, argued that Chattah could not lawfully lead the office this way. A district court agreed and disqualified her from supervising their cases, though it declined to dismiss the indictments themselves. The Ninth Circuit affirmed, holding that a law governing temporary officeholders lets only the person who was already the first assistant when a vacancy began step into the top job, and that the Attorney General cannot hand someone all of a vacant office's powers by delegation without following that law's procedures. The United States, represented by the Solicitor General, is now asking the Supreme Court to review that ruling.

## What the Court is being asked

The case turns on how officials may temporarily run federal agencies when a Senate-confirmed position is empty. One question is whether someone who is named the office's top deputy only after the vacancy already exists can still automatically take over as the acting head, or whether only the deputy who held that role when the vacancy began qualifies. The other question is whether the rule limiting how acting officials may be chosen also blocks an agency head from giving someone, through internal delegation, all of the vacant office's powers that could otherwise be assigned to others.
