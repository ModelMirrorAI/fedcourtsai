## In brief

The federal government asks the Court to overturn rulings that disqualified an acting U.S. Attorney whose appointment, challenged defendants argued, violated federal vacancy-filling rules.

## What happened

After Nevada's U.S. Attorney resigned, the Attorney General eventually named Sigal Chattah "First Assistant" and she began serving as acting U.S. Attorney, later also receiving a broad delegation of the office's duties. Several criminal defendants argued her service was unlawful under the Federal Vacancies Reform Act. A district court agreed and disqualified her from supervising their prosecutions, though it declined to dismiss the indictments. The Ninth Circuit affirmed, joining similar rulings from other circuits, while dismissing the defendants' separate appeal for lack of jurisdiction.

## What the Court is being asked

The case asks whether a law governing temporary leadership of federal agencies allows someone named "first assistant" only after a vacancy begins to automatically take over as acting officer, and separately, whether that same law stops an agency head from handing all of a vacant office's delegable duties to one person outside the law's usual procedures for naming an acting official.

## Where it stands

The petition was distributed for the Court's December 4, 2026 conference after full briefing, including a reply from the government; the docket shows no action since that distribution.

## What each outcome would mean

The United States, which lost below, asks the Court to overturn the Ninth Circuit's rulings. If the Court grants review and later rules for the government, acting officials like Chattah could serve this way, and the disqualification would be lifted. If it rules for the defendants, the lower court's disqualification stands. A denial of review would leave the Ninth Circuit's decision disqualifying Chattah in place without the Court deciding the legal questions. A vacate-and-remand would set that decision aside without resolving the issue for either side.
