## In brief

The federal government asks the Court to review a ruling that disqualified a Nevada prosecutor from supervising criminal cases because of how she became acting U.S. Attorney.

## What happened

Sigal Chattah served briefly as interim U.S. Attorney for Nevada, then resigned and was named First Assistant U.S. Attorney after the office was already vacant, which the government says made her Acting U.S. Attorney under a federal law governing vacancies. She also received a separate delegation of the U.S. Attorney's powers from the Attorney General. Defendants in several criminal cases argued she could not lawfully supervise their prosecutions. The district court agreed and disqualified her; the Ninth Circuit affirmed, holding that only someone who was already the first assistant when the vacancy began can become acting officer, and that the delegation improperly made her a "de facto" Acting U.S. Attorney.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked to decide two things: whether a person appointed as an agency's second-in-command only after the top position becomes vacant can still temporarily take over that top position under the vacancies law's default rule, and separately, whether the law's requirement that it be the only way to temporarily fill such a position blocks an agency head from delegating all of that position's transferable duties to someone who is not formally the acting officer.
