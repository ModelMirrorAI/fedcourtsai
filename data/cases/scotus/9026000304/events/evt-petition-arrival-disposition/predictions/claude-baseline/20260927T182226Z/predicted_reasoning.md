# Forecast: what the Court will do with No. 26-304, United States v. Jackson

**Disposition.** The Court will grant certiorari and set the case for briefing and argument. The petition is the Solicitor General's, it presents an acknowledged conflict between the Federal Circuit (Arthrex, 2022) and the Second, Third, and Ninth Circuits on the second question presented, and all three of those circuits have in the last year rejected two executive-branch vacancy practices that the Office of Legal Counsel and the Government Accountability Office have endorsed since 2001. The Court routinely takes a Solicitor General petition when a court of appeals has invalidated a settled executive practice (NLRB v. SW General is the template), and here the ruling directly governs who may lead five U.S. Attorney's Offices in the Ninth Circuit and nine more elsewhere. Expect a grant on both questions presented as written; the two are intertwined (the delegation question is the government's fallback if the first-assistant question is lost) and the petition asks for both.

**Timing and distribution.** The docket shows no distribution and no conference. Respondents' response is due October 8, 2026. Expect respondents, represented by the Federal Public Defender for the District of Nevada, to file a brief in opposition rather than waive, likely after one extension, so the petition should be distributed for a conference in December 2026 or early January 2027. A grant in time for argument in the February through April 2027 sittings is the likeliest path. If respondents waive, a grant could come as early as the late-October or November conferences.

**Relists.** The petition will be distributed at least once. Beyond that, expect at most one relist before the grant: Solicitor General petitions on a live conflict are usually granted at the first or second conference, and a relist here would reflect a Justice preparing a separate statement or the Court checking whether the pending Kelesis nomination has moved, not doubt about the question.

**CVSG.** None. The United States is the petitioner, so there is no Solicitor General whose views could be called for.

**Summary disposition.** Very unlikely. No intervening decision of this Court bears on the questions, and the government asks for plenary review of an entrenched conflict, so a GVR or a summary reversal is not a live route. If the Court grants, it will be for briefing and argument.

**If denied.** The realistic denial path is not disagreement about certworthiness but mootness or vehicle: Senate confirmation of a U.S. Attorney for Nevada before the Court acts, which would end Ms. Chattah's acting service and drain the disqualification orders of practical effect, or a preference to wait for the government's promised hold petition from the Second Circuit case. A denial on that footing would probably come without a separate writing. If the Court instead denies on the merits shadow, a statement or dissent respecting denial from one of Justices Thomas, Alito, or Gorsuch is plausible but not the expected case.

**If granted, conditionally.** The Court would take both questions as framed. The likeliest outcome is a reversal at least on the first question, holding that section 3345(a)(1) reaches a first assistant designated after the vacancy arose, with the delegation question either decided the same way or left unreached. A division along the lines of the recent removal and appointment cases is more probable than a lopsided one.
