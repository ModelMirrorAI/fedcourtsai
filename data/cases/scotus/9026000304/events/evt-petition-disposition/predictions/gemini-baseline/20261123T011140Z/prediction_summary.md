The Supreme Court is likely — about a three-in-four chance — to grant certiorari, meaning it agrees to hear full arguments and decide *United States v. Jackson*. The Solicitor General, the federal government's top Supreme Court lawyer, asked the Justices to step in after three federal appeals courts ruled that the administration unlawfully installed temporary U.S. Attorneys without Senate confirmation under the Federal Vacancies Reform Act, the federal law governing temporary replacements. Because the government rarely loses major executive-power disputes across multiple circuits without Supreme Court review, full review is the likeliest outcome.
