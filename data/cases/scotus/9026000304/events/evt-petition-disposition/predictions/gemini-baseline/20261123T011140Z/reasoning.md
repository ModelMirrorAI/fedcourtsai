## Case-Specific Adjustments from the Anchor (`0.689` / `0.731` → `0.76`)

### Upward Factors

1. **Designated Lead Solicitor General Vehicle on a Government-Wide Appointments & Delegation Dispute:**
   Three courts of appeals — the Third Circuit in *United States v. Giraud*, 160 F.4th 390 (2025), the Ninth Circuit below (*United States v. Garcia*, 188 F.4th 468 (Aug. 17, 2026)), and the Second Circuit in *In re Grand Jury Subpoenas to the Office of the New York State Attorney General*, 188 F.4th 595 (Aug. 27, 2026) — have invalidated the Executive Branch's post-2001 OLC and GAO reading of the Federal Vacancies Reform Act of 1998 (FVRA), 5 U.S.C. §§ 3345(a)(1) and 3347(a), disqualifying the officials leading U.S. Attorneys' Offices in multiple districts. Both the Ninth and Second Circuits stayed their mandates pending this Court's cert disposition (`Pet. App. 80a`; `26-348 Pet. App. 95a`).
   Crucially, `same-question.json` and the `supremecourt.gov` docket for companion petition No. `26-348` (*United States v. Office of the New York State Attorney General*, also distributed November 17, 2026 for the December 4, 2026 conference) confirm that the Solicitor General explicitly designated *Jackson* (`26-304`) as the **lead merits vehicle** and asked the Court to **hold** `26-348` pending *Jackson* because `26-348` carries a grand-jury-expiration mootness complication. Because the raw `federal` band pool (`73.1%` overall, `68.9%` zero-amicus) mixes lead merits petitions with defensive/protective SG hold petitions (which are denied whenever the lead case is denied), being the SG's chosen plenary vehicle warrants an upward adjustment above the `none`-arm mean. Even if the Court adopted NYOAG's alternative request in `26-348` to grant both petitions and consolidate them (`BIO 15 n.1`), `26-304` is granted on *either* plenary path.
2. **Published Circuit Dissent and Inter-Circuit Doctrinal Tension on QP2:**
   In the companion Second Circuit decision (`188 F.4th 595`), Judge Park dissented at length on both questions presented, endorsing the government's textual reading of § 3345(a)(1) ("the first assistant to the office" includes post-vacancy designees) and § 3347(a)/§ 3348 (exclusivity restricts "acting" status and exclusive non-delegable functions, leaving the Attorney General's vesting-and-delegation authority under 28 U.S.C. §§ 509, 510, 515 intact). On QP2, there is also real methodological tension with the Federal Circuit's recurring framework in *Arthrex, Inc. v. Smith & Nephew, Inc.*, 35 F.4th 1328, 1335–39 (Fed. Cir. 2022), cert. denied, 143 S. Ct. 2493 (2023), which treated the FVRA as reaching only non-delegable duties. Most importantly, the Supreme Court regularly grants SG petitions challenging circuit decisions that restrict Executive Branch structural authority even without a square split — *SW General*, 580 U.S. 288 (2017), the Court's sole prior FVRA merits case, was granted out of the D.C. Circuit with no split. Under the Rule of Four, securing at least four votes to review circuit rulings that foreclose a 25-year OLC-endorsed vacancy/delegation mechanism across presidential transitions is highly plausible.
3. **Clean Interlocutory Vehicle & Manageable Mootness Horizon:**
   Respondents' three vehicle objections in the BIO (`BIO 20–24`) are unlikely to deter a grant:
   - *Collateral-order jurisdiction:* Respondents did not contest appellate jurisdiction below, and Ninth Circuit precedent (*United States v. Williams*, 68 F.4th 564, 570–71 (9th Cir. 2023)), alongside the Third Circuit in *Giraud* (`160 F.4th at 396 n.2`) and every other circuit to address the issue, holds that an order disqualifying U.S. Attorney leadership in a criminal prosecution is immediately appealable under 28 U.S.C. § 1291 because Double Jeopardy bars post-acquittal government appeal.
   - *FVRA time-limit / "springing" § 3346(a)(2) objection:* President Trump's February 11, 2026 nomination of George Kelesis to be U.S. Attorney for the District of Nevada remains pending in the Senate Judiciary Committee after nearly ten months of inaction (with two Nevada Democratic Senators). Every circuit to address § 3346(a)(2) (*Rush v. Kijakazi*, 65 F.4th 114 (4th Cir. 2023); *Dahle v. Kijakazi*, 62 F.4th 424 (8th Cir. 2023)) has held that a pending nomination triggers an independent acting-service period even after the initial 210/300-day window expires; and when the 119th Congress adjourns sine die on January 3, 2027 and returns the nomination, § 3346(b)(1) provides an additional 210 days (through early August 2027, past the end of OT2026). Moreover, QP2 (delegation under 28 U.S.C. §§ 509, 510, 515) is unaffected by § 3346's time limits altogether, and any unaddressed time-limit defense goes to remand merits rather than Article III mootness (*Chafin v. Chafin*, 568 U.S. 165, 174 (2013)).

### Downward / Moderating Factors (Why `P(grant)` Is `0.76`, Not `0.90+`)

1. **Unbroken 3–0 Circuit Alignment and *SW General* Headwinds on the Merits:**
   Every court of appeals to reach the administration's 2025 post-28 U.S.C. § 546(d) maneuver (CA3, CA9, CA2 — including opinions authored or joined by Trump-appointed Judges Bibas, Phipps, and Miller) has rejected both government theories on forceful textual, structural, and historical grounds closely tracking Chief Justice Roberts's 6–2 opinion in *SW General*, 580 U.S. 288. Conservative textualists who view the FVRA as Congress's deliberate constitutional guardrail against end-runs around Senate confirmation (and Justice Thomas, who concurred in *SW General* to warn that broader acting designations threaten the Appointments Clause) may be skeptical of the government's merits position, reducing the appetite for a "rescue" grant and raising the risk of a "defensive denial" where skeptics prefer letting the circuit consensus stand (or awaiting pending CA4 and CADC decisions in *Comey*/*James* and *Abramowitz v. Lake*) rather than granting only to affirm against the Executive Branch.
2. **Modest Practical Remedy Below:**
   Because neither the district court nor the Ninth Circuit dismissed the underlying criminal indictments — requiring only that supervision rest with career AUSAs rather than Ms. Chattah (`Pet. App. 8a, 34a`; `BIO 17–19`), and because three of the initially cited districts resolved their leadership posture by November 2026 (`Cert. Reply 9 n.*`) — Justices inclined toward further percolation can deny review without halting federal criminal prosecutions in Nevada.
