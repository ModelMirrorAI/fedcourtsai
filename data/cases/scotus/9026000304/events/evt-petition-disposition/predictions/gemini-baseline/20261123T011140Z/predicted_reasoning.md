# Predicted Court Action — *United States v. Devonte Devon Jackson, et al.* (No. 26-304)

## Conference Trajectory, Relists, and CVSG

The petition has been distributed once so far (`distribution_count = 1`), on November 17, 2026, for the Conference of December 4, 2026, alongside companion petition *United States v. Office of the New York State Attorney General*, No. 26-348.

- **Relists:** Expect **one further relist** (bringing `distribution_count` to `2`, for the December 11, 2026 conference) before the Court issues its grant order, following the Court's standard regular-conference relist practice so chambers can vet respondents' collateral-order and FVRA § 3346 mootness objections and resolve whether to hold or consolidate No. 26-348.
- **CVSG:** **No CVSG** will issue — the United States, represented by the Solicitor General, is already the petitioner.

## Disposition, Scope of Grant, and Companion Handling

- **Disposition Route:** The Court will **grant certiorari for plenary briefing and oral argument** during OT2026 (likely for the March or April 2027 argument sitting), rather than disposing of the petition summarily in the cert order. A summary reversal of three published court-of-appeals decisions on an intricate Federal Vacancies Reform Act question will not occur, and a summary GVR would arise only in the unlikely event that intervening Senate confirmation of U.S. Attorney nominee George Kelesis moots the controversy under *United States v. Munsingwear, Inc.*
- **Questions Presented Taken:** Expect an **unmodified grant on both Questions Presented** as framed in the petition — covering both eligibility of post-vacancy first assistants to serve as acting officers under 5 U.S.C. § 3345(a)(1) (QP1) and delegation of non-exclusive U.S. Attorney duties to a non-acting official under 5 U.S.C. § 3347(a) and 28 U.S.C. §§ 509, 510, and 515 (QP2) — without adding a threshold jurisdictional question on collateral-order appealability. The Court will hold companion petition No. 26-348 pending its merits disposition of *Jackson* rather than consolidating the two cases.
- **Conditional Merits Direction:** If granted, the merits battleground will center on whether the statutory phrase "the first assistant to the office of such officer" in 5 U.S.C. § 3345(a)(1) carries a temporal incumbency limitation under *NLRB v. SW General, Inc.*, 580 U.S. 288 (2017), and whether 5 U.S.C. § 3347(a)–(b)'s exclusivity bar forecloses delegating *all* delegable duties of a vacant PAS office to a single unconfirmed official under 28 U.S.C. §§ 509 and 510.

## Separate Writings at the Cert Stage

- On the predicted plenary grant, **no Justice will author a separate opinion**.
- Should the Court instead deny certiorari, there is roughly a one-in-four conditional chance of a noted dissent or statement respecting the denial, most plausibly authored by Justice Alito, Justice Thomas, Justice Gorsuch, or Justice Kavanaugh addressing Executive Branch vacancy-filling authority during transitions.
