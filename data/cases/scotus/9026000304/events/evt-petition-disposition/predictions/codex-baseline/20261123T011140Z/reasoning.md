The district court left the indictments intact but disqualified Chattah from supervising the prosecutions. The Ninth Circuit upheld that disqualification on both statutory grounds. The Second and Third Circuits agree about first assistants appointed after a vacancy. The stronger conflict concerns delegation. The Ninth Circuit expressly declined to follow the Federal Circuit’s broad reading of the vacancies statute in *Arthrex*. Respondents make a fair distinction because *Arthrex* adjudicated a single patent rehearing decision. But the Commissioner there had received all the Director’s nonexclusive functions, and an official exercising those powers in another circuit now faces a materially different rule. The reply identifies eleven U.S. Attorneys’ offices affected by the decisions. The availability of other prosecutors limits the immediate harm to individual cases, but the same statute governs appointments across agencies during presidential transitions. See the questions presented, Petition 10–14, 29–33 and App. 31a–32a, Opposition 12–19, and Reply 7–10.

The choice of case matters. `record/same-question.json` identified No. 26-348. Its docket confirms that the New York grand-jury subpoena case is set for the same December 4 conference. In its November 16 reply there, the government expressly asks the Court to hold New York for Jackson. The New York official’s acting-service period had expired and the grand jury had been discharged. Those facts make Nevada a better way to reach both questions. The Jackson respondents suggest consolidation if review is granted (Opposition 15 n.1). Either course would entail a grant here.

The main discount is the possibility that the Court could never reach both questions. The government appealed an order disqualifying one prosecutor before the criminal cases ended. Its cited circuit authority concerns disqualification of an entire office, though the government’s inability to appeal an acquittal gives it a substantial argument for immediate review here. Also, confirmation of the pending Nevada nominee could end the dispute over future supervision. The narrower timing objection is less persuasive because the government cites appellate authority allowing acting service to resume while a nomination is pending, and the delegation issue survives that objection. These are reasons a Justice might wait for another case even while accepting the importance of the questions. See Opposition 20–24 and Reply 10–12.
