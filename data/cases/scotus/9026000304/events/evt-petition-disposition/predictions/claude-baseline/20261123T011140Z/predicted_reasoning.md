# Forecast — what the Court will do with No. 26-304

- **State forecast from:** one distribution (conference of Dec. 4, 2026); no relist, no CVSG, no amici.
- **Disposition:** The Court grants certiorari (about 70%). The most likely form is a grant in this case together with No. 26-348 (the Second Circuit companion), consolidated for one hour of argument, or a grant in this case with 26-348 held. Less likely: grant 26-348 only and hold this petition.
- **Relist:** The petition is relisted once after Dec. 4 and granted at the Dec. 11 conference or the first January conference, in time for argument in April 2027. One further distribution expected; two at most.
- **CVSG:** None. The United States is the petitioner.
- **Questions presented:** If granted, the Court takes both questions as the government wrote them: (1) whether a first assistant designated after the vacancy arose can be the acting officer under 5 U.S.C. 3345(a)(1), and (2) whether 5 U.S.C. 3347(a) bars delegating all delegable duties of the vacant office. It may add a question on appellate jurisdiction under the collateral-order doctrine or on mootness, which respondents raised.
- **Route:** Plenary review with briefing and argument. No summary reversal; a GVR happens only if this petition is held behind 26-348 and the government then prevails.
- **If denied:** Most likely a bare denial. There is perhaps a one-in-five chance that one or more Justices (most plausibly from among Thomas, Alito and Gorsuch) note that they would grant.
- **Conditional on a grant (merits-shaped, not committed):** the likely contest is textual, present-tense and office-centric wording of § 3345(a)(1) against the structure of the three-option scheme and § 3347's exclusivity clause, with the delegation question the closer one.
