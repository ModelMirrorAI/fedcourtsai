## In brief

A man asks the Court to review Colorado courts' handling of a civil protection order that required him to give up firearms without, he says, adequate notice or a hearing.

## What happened

Samuel Collin Robinson and Katherine Lyman Freeman share a long-running Colorado family-court case. In 2025, Freeman sought a civil protection order against Robinson concerning contact with their son at church. A magistrate issued a temporary protection order the same day, including a firearms relinquishment requirement, which Robinson says was entered without notifying him or holding the required hearing. A later hearing made the order permanent. Robinson's challenges to the district court and the Colorado Court of Appeals were rejected, the latter on mootness and then jurisdictional grounds, and the Colorado Supreme Court denied his petition without explanation.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked to decide whether state magistrates and the firearms-relinquishment statutes they relied on satisfy constitutional due process requirements of notice and a chance to be heard before a person can be ordered to give up firearms, and whether a recent Supreme Court ruling on disarming people found to pose a credible safety threat applies when no such threat was found. The petition also asks whether rulings based on earlier, allegedly flawed findings can validly support a later permanent order.

## Where it stands

Robinson filed his petition for a writ of certiorari on May 22, 2026, and the response is due November 23, 2026. The docket shows no further action since filing.

## What each outcome would mean

Robinson, as petitioner, is asking the Court to take up and reverse the result reached by the Colorado courts. If the Court grants the petition, it would hear the case in full and decide the due process and firearms-relinquishment questions, without yet determining who wins. If it denies the petition, the Colorado Supreme Court's decision not to intervene stands, leaving the permanent protection order and its firearms relinquishment requirement in place as issued by the lower courts.
