# Reasoning — New Mexico v. Neal, No. 26-37 (P(grant) = 0.14)

**Inputs read.** Snapshot `record/snapshots/2026-11-06.json`; `record/context.json` (forward mode, band `state`, sal-v7, distribution_count 1, no CVSG, `cert_anchor.rate` 0.233); `record/documents/questions-presented.txt` and `petition.txt` (116 pp., truncated at the appendix; the body through the vehicle section is intact); `record/same-question.json` (empty list — no other pending or decided petition scored above threshold). No BIO exists yet: it is due November 25, 2026.

**Docket posture.** State petition from the New Mexico Supreme Court (S-1-SC-40407, decided Apr. 6, 2026). Respondent waived; the petition was distributed for the 9/28/2026 conference; the Court then requested a response (Aug. 26), so the petition was pulled before that conference. Respondent has taken two Clerk extensions; the BIO is now due Nov. 25. So one distribution so far, and it never produced a decision.

**Anchor.** The frozen `cert_anchor` — the `state` band's bracketed reached rate pooled over the ten prior Terms — is 23.3%. Per-Term reached rates for `state` range roughly 14%–39% (statpack sal-v7 table).

**Adjustments.**
- *Up:* The Court called for a response, which shows at least one chambers took the petition seriously. The petitioner is a State asking the Court to correct a state supreme court on a federal constitutional rule, and it pleads a three-way split (CA2/9/11 abstract elements; CA6/8, Tex. CCA, Conn. as-charged elements; NM trial-record approach). The Court has left the Blockburger-operation question open since the fractured *Dixon* opinions.
- *Down (heavily):* (1) **Adequate and independent state ground.** The NMSC cited both the Fifth Amendment and N.M. Const. art. II § 15. The petition's own *Michigan v. Long* argument concedes the issue is live, and the respondent will certainly press it. (2) **Multiple punishment in a single trial turns on legislative intent** (*Missouri v. Hunter*), and the meaning of New Mexico's statutes is a state-law question this Court must accept from the NMSC. A respondent can plausibly recast NM's "unitary conduct" and modified-Blockburger approach as a state method for reading state legislative intent, not a misreading of the federal Clause. That is the strongest vehicle objection, and I expect the BIO to lead with it. (3) The case is fact-bound: it turns on how the jury may have relied on the sexual-offense alternative for kidnapping. And the State partly conceded the subsumption point below. (4) Response-requested petitions are still mostly denied. The relist cut shows grant-family rates of only ~13.5% for petitions that end at one relist.
- The state band's reached rate already includes the stronger state petitions (AEDPA summary-reversal candidates and the like). This one is a direct-review criminal case with a significant jurisdictional overhang, so I place it below the band mean.

Net: 0.14. A grant is plausible but less likely than the anchor suggests.

**Increments.** Relist: once the BIO is filed (or its time runs out), the petition will almost certainly be distributed again. The only off-ramps are withdrawal or settlement, which are improbable for a State prosecution, so 0.96. CVSG: the United States is not a party and this is a state criminal case. CVSGs in such cases are very rare, and the Court can read federal practice from the circuit split, so 0.03. Summary route given a grant: 0.15. A per curiam summary reversal correcting the NMSC is conceivable, but a three-way split question is normally set for argument, and no intervening decision invites a GVR. Dissent from denial given a denial: 0.06. Gorsuch, Thomas, or Alito might write on Blockburger or on state-court double jeopardy, but criminal-defendant-favorable state rulings rarely draw dissents from denial.

**Uncertainty / discount.** I have not seen the BIO, which will shape the AISG framing. The petition text is truncated in the appendix, so I relied on the petition's own description of the NMSC opinion, not the opinion itself. No MCP/web retrieval was performed.
