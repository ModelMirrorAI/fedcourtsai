## 2. Disposition and Route Forecast

- **Denial of Certiorari (`disposition: 0.08`, `predicted_disposition: "denied"`)**: Although the August 26, 2026 Call for a Response reflects initial interest from at least one chambers, the Court will deny certiorari once Respondent's counsel (Derek C. Reinbold of Kellogg, Hansen, Todd, Figel & Frederick, P.L.L.C.) files the Brief in Opposition. The Brief in Opposition will demonstrate that the New Mexico Supreme Court's vacatur of Respondent's second-degree criminal sexual penetration (CSP II) conviction is insulated by multiple state-law and vehicle barriers:
  1. The New Mexico Supreme Court expressly grounded its double-description framework in both the Fifth Amendment and Article II, Section 15 of the New Mexico Constitution (App. 9a, 40a);
  2. Under *Missouri v. Hunter*, 459 U.S. 359, 366–68 (1983), cumulative-punishment claims in a single state trial turn on state legislative intent, and the New Mexico Supreme Court authoritatively construed the text, structure, and 2003 amendment history of NMSA 1978, § 30-4-1(B) alongside New Mexico's state-law rule of lenity to conclude that the New Mexico Legislature did not authorize cumulative punishments when a lone sexual offense elevates kidnapping from second to first degree (App. 22a–26a); and
  3. The State conceded at oral argument below that the elements of CSP II are subsumed by first-degree kidnapping if the jury relied on the `"sexual offense"` alternative of § 30-4-1(B) (App. 18a–19a), making the outcome turn on New Mexico's state-law *Foster* general-verdict presumption rather than on a departure from *Blockburger v. United States*, 284 U.S. 299 (1932), or *Whalen v. United States*, 445 U.S. 684 (1980).
- **Question Presented If Granted (Context-Only)**: If the Court were to grant certiorari, it would take the single Question Presented in the petition—whether a court conducting a *Blockburger* same-offense inquiry in a compound-statute case is limited to statutory elements and charging instruments or may examine trial evidence, closing arguments, and jury instructions.
- **Summary Disposition vs. Plenary Review Conditional on Grant (`summary-disposition-route: 0.12`)**: Conditional on the Court granting the petition, plenary briefing and oral argument (`0.88`) is substantially likelier than a summary reversal or GVR in the cert order itself (`0.12`). No intervening Supreme Court decision provides a basis for a GVR, and the Petition frames a three-way doctrinal conflict among federal circuits and state high courts that would not lend itself to per curiam resolution, especially given the state-law statutory-construction and *Michigan v. Long* entanglement below.
- **Separate Writings on Denial (`dissent-from-denial: 0.04`)**: Conditional on denial, the Court is very likely (`0.96`) to issue a clean denial order with no separate opinion. There is a small (`0.04`) probability that one or more Justices who prompted the Call for a Response—most plausibly Justice Alito (`0.015`), Justice Thomas (`0.015`), or Justice Gorsuch (`0.008`, the Tenth Circuit Justice)—file a brief dissent from or statement respecting the denial of certiorari criticizing the New Mexico Supreme Court's reliance on trial evidence under the Fifth Amendment while acknowledging the state-law vehicle complications.

## 3. Conditional Merits Outlook (If Granted)

If the Court were to grant plenary review and reach the federal constitutional merits past the *Michigan v. Long* and *Missouri v. Hunter* barriers, it would likely reaffirm that under *Blockburger* and *Whalen*, the federal Double Jeopardy Clause compares statutory elements—using the charging instrument or jury instructions only to identify which statutory alternative of a divisible compound offense is at issue—rather than conducting a free-ranging inquiry into whether trial evidence was "unitary." Even on that holding, however, the Court would likely vacate and remand rather than reverse outright, leaving the New Mexico Supreme Court free to reach the same result under Article II, Section 15 of the New Mexico Constitution, New Mexico's statutory rule of lenity, or its state-law *Foster* general-verdict rule.
