# Forecast of the Court's action

The Court will most likely deny New Mexico's petition. The central obstacle will be the difficulty of separating the claimed federal same-offense methodological conflict from the state court's construction of its own punishment scheme and the prosecution's alternative trial theories. A denial will announce no merits rationale and will not endorse the decision below.

The docket currently records exactly one distribution and no CVSG. I expect at least one additional distribution, with 98% probability, after the requested response is filed or its time expires. The most likely path is one redistribution followed by denial, rather than a long series of substantive relists. This increment primarily reflects renewed consideration after a response request, not a strong affirmative grant signal. With the opposition presently due November 25, 2026, the most likely disposition window is January–February 2027, assuming no further extensions.

The Court will probably not invite the Solicitor General's views; I assign 2.5% to a new CVSG. The federal constitutional issue can be addressed without a distinct federal regulatory-interest submission. If an invitation occurs, it will follow consideration of the opposition rather than precede the currently outstanding response.

If the Court grants review, it will most likely focus on whether the Fifth Amendment permits looking beyond statutory elements and charging instruments to trial evidence, argument, and instructions in this multiple-punishment setting. That is narrower than definitively choosing among all three approaches for every double-jeopardy context. Any merits-ground prediction is conditional on a grant: the likely inquiry would emphasize legislative authorization and distinguish federal constitutional requirements from state statutory interpretation.

Conditional on any grant, plenary briefing and argument is the likelier route, at 85%; a disposition in the cert order has 15% probability. A summary reversal is more plausible than a routine intervening-precedent GVR because no such intervening decision is identified in the record. The Court will most likely deny without an accompanying dissent or statement; the probability of at least one such notation or writing, conditional on denial, is 10%. No individual Justice is predicted to write as the modal outcome; the low independent probabilities in prediction.json allow for exceptional separate writings.
