## 2. Disposition and Conditional Route

- **Denial of certiorari (`disposition: 0.12`, `predicted_disposition: "denied"`):** Once Respondent's Supreme Court counsel files the Brief in Opposition, the Court will deny certiorari. The Brief in Opposition will show that *State v. Neal* is an unsuitable vehicle to resolve the alleged split over *Blockburger v. United States*, 284 U.S. 299 (1932), for three reasons:
  1. In a single-trial cumulative-punishment case from state court, the Fifth Amendment Double Jeopardy Clause does no more than prevent a sentencing court from prescribing greater punishment than the state legislature intended (*Missouri v. Hunter*, 459 U.S. 359, 366–68 (1983); *Ohio v. Johnson*, 467 U.S. 493, 499 (1984); reaffirmed in *Barrett v. United States*, 607 U.S. ___, slip op. at 8–10 (Jan. 14, 2026)), and the New Mexico Supreme Court explicitly applied its *Swafford* / modified-*Blockburger* framework as a canon for discerning New Mexico legislative intent while also citing N.M. Const. art. II, § 15 and state stare decisis principles.
  2. New Mexico's first-degree kidnapping statute (`N.M. Stat. Ann. § 30-4-1`) is a compound statute that elevates kidnapping to a first-degree felony unless the defendant frees the victim without inflicting "physical injury or a sexual offense upon the victim," and the general verdict did not specify which statutory alternative the jury found—meaning the vacatur of the second-degree criminal sexual penetration (`CSP II`) count turned on a general-verdict ambiguity over alternative statutory elements rather than a pure conflict between abstract statutory elements and trial evidence.
  3. The New Mexico Supreme Court ruled in the State's favor by reinstating the aggravated battery conviction (Count 3) and noted that the double jeopardy overlap on Count 2 was avoidable under existing state law had the prosecution structured its jury instructions differently.
- **Question Presented if granted (context-only):** If the Court were to grant certiorari, it would take the single Question Presented in the petition—whether *Blockburger*'s same-offense inquiry in a concurrent prosecution is limited to abstract statutory elements, looks to the indictment, or permits examination of trial evidence, argument, and jury instructions—while also confronting whether New Mexico's decision rests on adequate and independent state-law grounds or state legislative-intent determinations under *Missouri v. Hunter*.
- **Conditional summary disposition route (`summary-disposition-route: 0.12`):** Conditional on a grant, the Court would most likely set the case for plenary briefing and oral argument (`0.88` conditional share) rather than summary reversal or GVR (`0.12` conditional share), because New Mexico asks the Court to resolve an entrenched multi-jurisdictional methodological split over *Blockburger* and *United States v. Dixon*, 509 U.S. 688 (1993), rather than correct a simple misapplication of settled law.
- **Conditional dissent from denial (`dissent-from-denial: 0.06`):** Conditional on a denial of certiorari, the overwhelmingly likely outcome (`0.94`) is an unsigned denial order with no separate writing. There is a modest (`0.06`) chance that one or more Justices—most plausibly Justice Gorsuch (who concurred in part in *Barrett v. United States*, 607 U.S. ___ (Jan. 14, 2026), highlighting doctrinal confusion surrounding *Blockburger* in concurrent prosecutions), or Justice Thomas or Justice Alito—files a brief statement respecting or dissenting from the denial of certiorari noting the lower-court division over modified-*Blockburger* tests while acknowledging the state-law vehicle obstacles here.
