## In brief

New Mexico asks the Court to review how courts decide whether two crimes count as the same offense for double jeopardy purposes.

## What happened

Samuel Neal was convicted of kidnapping, criminal sexual penetration, and aggravated battery after a sexual assault. He argued the convictions punished him twice for the same conduct. New Mexico's Court of Appeals vacated two of his three convictions on double jeopardy grounds. The New Mexico Supreme Court partly disagreed, reinstating the aggravated battery conviction but agreeing that the kidnapping and sexual-penetration convictions were the same offense, based on its review of the evidence, arguments, and jury instructions at trial rather than only the statutes' abstract elements. New Mexico, as petitioner, now asks the United States Supreme Court to review that decision, arguing it conflicts with how federal appeals courts and other state courts decide such questions.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked what information a court may look at when deciding whether a defendant's two convictions are really punishment for a single offense. One approach looks only at the crimes' statutory elements in the abstract. Another also considers how the charges were written in the indictment. New Mexico's approach goes further, examining the evidence, arguments, and jury instructions actually used at trial. The petition asks the Court to say which approach the Constitution requires.

## Where it stands

Neal waived his right to respond, but the Court requested a response, which has twice been granted extensions, most recently to November 25, 2026. The petition has not yet been distributed for a conference since that response was requested.

## What each outcome would mean

New Mexico, as petitioner, is asking the Court to take up the case and reject the New Mexico Supreme Court's approach. If the Court grants review, it would hear full arguments and decide which method courts must use, with no outcome yet determined. If the Court denies review, the New Mexico Supreme Court's decision stands, leaving Neal's sexual-penetration conviction vacated and his kidnapping and aggravated-battery convictions intact, under New Mexico's existing approach.
