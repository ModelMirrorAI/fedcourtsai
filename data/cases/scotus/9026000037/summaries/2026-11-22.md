## In brief

New Mexico asks the Court to review a ruling that barred a second punishment for a man convicted of kidnapping and sexual assault in the same trial.

## What happened

Samuel Neal was convicted of first-degree kidnapping, criminal sexual penetration (CSP), and aggravated battery after a New Mexico trial. The jury could find first-degree kidnapping based on either physical injury or a sexual offense, and it returned a general verdict without saying which. The New Mexico Supreme Court held that, because the only sexual offense presented was the same one underlying the CSP charge, the state's own law did not allow punishing Neal separately for both kidnapping and CSP. It reinstated his aggravated-battery conviction but vacated the CSP conviction. New Mexico now asks the Supreme Court to review that ruling.

## What the Court is being asked

The case concerns how courts decide whether two crimes count as the "same offense" for double jeopardy purposes, so that a person cannot be punished twice for one act. New Mexico argues the Court should clarify whether courts may look only at a crime's legal elements in the abstract, at how the charges were written, or also at the evidence and arguments used at trial, as New Mexico's courts did here. Neal responds that the state court's decision rested on its own interpretation of New Mexico's kidnapping law, not on a disputed point of federal constitutional law, so there is no federal question for the Court to resolve.

## Where it stands

The petition has been fully briefed, including New Mexico's petition, Neal's brief in opposition, and earlier distribution for a conference; the docket does not yet show any further action by the Court on whether to grant or deny review.

## What each outcome would mean

New Mexico, which lost the double-punishment ruling below, is asking the Court to take up the case. If the Court grants review, it would hear full arguments and decide the legal question itself, without deciding in advance who wins. If it denies review, the New Mexico Supreme Court's decision stays in place, meaning Neal's CSP conviction remains vacated and only his kidnapping and aggravated-battery convictions stand. A denial would not mean the Court agrees with the New Mexico court's reasoning, only that the case is not taken up.
