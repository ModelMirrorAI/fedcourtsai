## In brief

Securities firms ask the Court to overturn a jury verdict and financial penalty, arguing their private regulatory filings were not securities fraud.

## What happened

The Securities and Exchange Commission sued Spartan Securities Group, Island Capital Management, and two of their officers, alleging they helped fraudsters take shell companies public. A jury rejected 13 of the SEC's 14 claims but found the defendants liable on one count, involving statements made to a private regulator, FINRA, and to a stock clearinghouse, DTC, rather than to investors directly. The trial court ordered Island to pay over $150,000 in disgorgement, meaning repayment of gains tied to the misconduct, to the U.S. Treasury, plus penalties. The Eleventh Circuit affirmed, and later denied rehearing.

## What the Court is being asked

The petitioners ask the Court to decide two things: first, whether a private statement made only to a regulator or clearinghouse, never seen by any investor, counts as fraud connected to the purchase or sale of a security under the relevant securities-fraud rule; and second, whether courts may order a company to give up its gains to the government when no specific investor was identified as harmed, rather than limiting such orders to traditional principles requiring a link to actual victims.

## Where it stands

The federal government, representing the SEC, waived its right to respond to the petition. The case was distributed for the Court's November 6, 2026 conference, but the Court instead asked for a response, due November 23, 2026, and the docket shows no further action since.

## What each outcome would mean

Spartan Securities Group and the other firms are asking the Court to reverse the Eleventh Circuit's ruling, which upheld the jury verdict and disgorgement order in the SEC's favor. If the Court grants the petition, it would hear full arguments on whether the lower court applied the right legal standards. If it denies the petition, the Eleventh Circuit's decision stands, leaving the liability finding and disgorgement order against the petitioners in place. A denial would not mean the Court agrees with the lower court's reasoning, only that the case ends there.
