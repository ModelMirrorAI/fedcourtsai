## 2. Disposition and Scope of Review If Granted

- **Disposition (`predicted_disposition: "denied"`, `disposition: 0.11`):** Once the Solicitor General files the Brief in Opposition, the Court will deny the petition for a writ of certiorari.
- **Question Presented Selection (Conditional on Grant):** If the Court were to grant certiorari, it would limit review to **Question Presented 2**—whether 15 U.S.C. § 78u(d)(7) authorizes federal courts to order SEC disgorgement paid to the U.S. Treasury when there are no identified harmed investors—and decline to take Question Presented 1 (which challenges the sufficiency of the evidence and the "in connection with" standard under Section 10(b) and Rule 10b-5(b) for FINRA Form 211 and DTC submissions). If the Court were to grant review on Question Presented 2, the majority would likely address whether § 78u(d)(7) incorporates the equitable victim-benefit constraints of *Liu v. SEC*, 591 U.S. 71 (2020), or instead permits Treasury deposits where distributing ill-gotten transfer-agent fees to dispersed microcap investors is stipulated to be infeasible.
- **Summary Disposition vs. Plenary Review (`summary-disposition-route: 0.12`):** Conditional on a grant, plenary review is substantially likelier than a summary disposition. The Court decided *Sripetch v. SEC*, 608 U.S. ___, 146 S. Ct. 1403 (June 4, 2026), after the Eleventh Circuit's panel opinion (January 16, 2026) and rehearing denial (April 30, 2026), but *Sripetch* affirmed the SEC's disgorgement award and expressly declined to resolve whether § 78u(d)(7) departs from *Liu*'s equitable boundaries. Because *Sripetch* does not cast doubt on the Eleventh Circuit's judgment, a GVR in light of *Sripetch* is unlikely (`0.12` conditional on grant), though not impossible if the Court wished the Eleventh Circuit to reconsider its categorical statutory holding under *Sripetch*'s assumption that § 78u(d)(7) incorporates traditional equity.

## 3. Separate Writings on the Disposition Order

- **Dissent or Statement Respecting Denial (`dissent-from-denial: 0.08`):** Should the Court deny certiorari as predicted, we expect no separate writing in the base case (`92%`), but there is a meaningful (`8%`) probability of a statement respecting the denial of certiorari or a dissent from denial—most likely authored by **Justice Thomas** (`0.055`), potentially joined by or written alongside **Justice Gorsuch** (`0.025`) or **Justice Alito** (`0.015`). In *Sripetch*, Justice Thomas concurred separately (citing ICAN's amicus brief) to emphasize that the Courts of Appeals are divided on whether § 78u(d)(7) creates a legal remedy (*SEC v. Hallam*, 42 F.4th 316 (5th Cir. 2022) vs. *SEC v. Ahmed*, 72 F.4th 379 (2d Cir. 2023)) and to criticize the SEC's practice of depositing disgorgement into the Treasury. Because Petitioners here stipulated to the infeasibility of investor distribution and did not preserve a Seventh Amendment challenge to disgorgement, a Justice interested in the § 78u(d)(7) Treasury question is much more likely to concur in or note a brief statement respecting the denial (observing the vehicle defects and awaiting a cleaner case) than to force a plenary grant.
