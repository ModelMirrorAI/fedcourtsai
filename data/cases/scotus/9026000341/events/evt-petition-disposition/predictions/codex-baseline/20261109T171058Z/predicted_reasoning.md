# Forecast of the Court's treatment

The Court will deny the petition, most likely without an explanatory writing. The denial will not decide the merits of either question presented.

The snapshot records **one distribution**, for November 6, 2026, and **no CVSG**. The response requested on October 23 is due November 23, 2026. The Court will ordinarily await that response cycle and distribute the petition again: **96% probability of at least one additional distribution**. The modal forecast is **one further distribution**, followed by disposition in December 2026 or early 2027 if no substantial extension intervenes. A further distribution in this posture does not necessarily mean the Court has already considered and substantively relisted the case. If review is ultimately granted, one or two additional relists after the response-stage distribution are plausible.

The Court will **not call for the Solicitor General's views as amicus**: **0.2% probability of a new CVSG**. The Solicitor General is counsel for the respondent and has already been asked for a party response; that is a different procedure.

**If review is granted**, the Court will most likely take a narrowed version of **Question 2**: whether Section 78u(d)(7) permits disgorgement payable to the Treasury when distribution to investors is infeasible, and what investor-benefit or equitable limitations survive Liu and Sripetch. It is less likely to take the broader Question 1 premise that statements to FINRA or DTC cannot satisfy the transaction nexus unless an investor actually received them. The lower court's findings about market access, market-making and potential access to the forms complicate that categorical question.

**Conditional on any grant, plenary review is more likely than summary treatment: 75% versus 25%.** The principal summary path is a grant, vacatur and remand for the Eleventh Circuit to reconsider the disgorgement question in light of Sripetch. That decision postdates the lower judgment, but leaves the central Treasury issue unresolved, so it does not compel that route. An outright summary reversal is less likely than a GVR.

**Conditional on denial, there is a 10% probability of a noted dissent or statement respecting denial.** The forecast is no such writing. If a Justice does write, Thomas or Gorsuch is more likely than the others, addressing the statutory or equitable limits left open after Sripetch rather than announcing a holding that the denial does not produce. No majority opinion or cert-stage vote lineup is forecast.
