## In brief

A man whose civil rights lawsuit over his criminal prosecution was dismissed before trial asks the Court to revive it.

## What happened

Charles Kakembo Sessanga was prosecuted in Texas for a child sexual abuse charge and was acquitted by a jury; the criminal case was later expunged. He then sued the City of DeSoto's police department, several officers, and a prosecutor, representing himself, claiming they pursued the prosecution without probable cause and malicious intent. A federal magistrate judge recommended dismissing the case before any defendant was served, and the district court agreed, dismissing it with prejudice (meaning it cannot be refiled) and denying leave to amend. The Fifth Circuit Court of Appeals affirmed, holding that a grand jury's indictment generally shields officers from liability unless the plaintiff shows the process was tainted, and that the prosecutor was immune from suit.

## What the Court is being asked

Sessanga asks the Court to decide whether courts can dismiss a civil-rights lawsuit filed by someone representing himself, at an early screening stage and before any fact-finding, for not including specific details that only the defendants would have access to. He also asks whether the rule shielding officers from liability when a grand jury indicts someone should still apply if the plaintiff claims officers withheld evidence or misled the prosecutor, and whether a prosecutor's offer of a lighter plea deal can count as evidence that the case lacked a solid basis.

## Where it stands

## What each outcome would mean

Sessanga, who lost in the lower courts, is asking the Court to take up his case. If the Court grants the petition, it would hear full arguments and decide the legal questions he raises, without yet determining who wins. If the Court denies the petition, the Fifth Circuit's decision affirming dismissal of Sessanga's lawsuit stays in place, and the case remains closed.
