# Forecast

The Court will deny this petition rather than set another presidential-removal case for plenary review. The most likely procedural endpoint is an order following the scheduled October 16, 2026 conference, without another distribution beyond the two already recorded. The probability of at least one additional distribution is 0.30; if further consideration occurs, one or two additional distributions are more likely than an extended hold.

The Court will not call for the Solicitor General's views. No CVSG is recorded, and the Solicitor General has already presented the government's position in the requested opposition. The probability of a new CVSG is 0.005.

The likely reason to decline another case is that the intervening Slaughter decision, as described in the opposition, substantially answers the removal and remedial questions in the government's favor. The Court will not treat the NLRB's separation of prosecution from adjudication as sufficient reason to revisit that framework through this petition. This forecasts the selection decision, not a merits holding implicit in denial.

If review is granted, disposition in the cert order is much likelier than full briefing and argument: P(summary route | grant) = 0.85. A grant followed by summary affirmance is the leading grant-side alternative, with a GVR for application of Slaughter also plausible. Summary affirmance is not summary reversal, and a grant need not represent a victory for Wilcox. A plenary grant remains a smaller possibility.

If the Court takes the case for plenary review, it will focus on QP 1's agency-specific question: whether the NLRB's bifurcated structure permits protected tenure for Board members under the post-Slaughter framework. QP 2's proposed severance of agency functions would be a remedial subsidiary issue rather than an independent reason for review. Conditionally on a merits decision, the likelier ground is that the Board still exercises executive authority despite its separation from the General Counsel, and that severance targets the removal restriction rather than the agency's statutory functions.

Conditional on denial, the most likely order contains no noted dissent or statement. I nevertheless assign 0.35 to at least one such recorded writing or notation because the adjudicatory-independence question could prompt an objection. I do not predict individual cert votes or an opinion author.
