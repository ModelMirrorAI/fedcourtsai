# Forecast: Wilcox v. Trump, No. 26-5 (evt-petition-disposition)

**Disposition.** The Court will deny the petition. P(any grant) = 0.08.

**Relists.** The docket shows two distributions: August 5 for the September 28 conference, and September 30 for the October 16 conference after the Court called for a response and the Solicitor General filed the brief in opposition. The second entry is a redistribution after a requested response, not a relist from a conference, so this petition has never actually been considered and carried over. From here I expect it to be decided at or shortly after the October 16 conference. I put the chance of at least one further distribution at 0.35: the ordinary path is a denial straight off the October 16 list, and the main route to a relist is a Justice writing separately about the denial, which takes a conference cycle.

**CVSG.** None. The Solicitor General is counsel of record for the respondents and has already filed the opposition, so there is no one to call for the views of. P = 0.01.

**Which question the Court would take, if any.** The live question is not the petition's QP 1 as written, which Slaughter has answered for agencies that adjudicate and enforce. It is whether a predominantly adjudicatory multimember body falls inside the "different set of questions" the Slaughter majority reserved for non-Article III courts. If the Court granted plenary review it would reformulate around that carve-out: whether the NLRB, given its Taft-Hartley bifurcation from the General Counsel, is closer to the Tax Court than to the FTC. QP 2 (severing residual executive functions) would not be taken; Slaughter reaffirmed that the remedy for an invalid removal restriction is to sever the restriction, not agency powers.

**Summary disposition versus plenary review.** Conditional on any grant, a summary route is the likelier one, P = 0.7. Petitioner's lead ask is a GVR in light of Slaughter, and the government's fallback ask is grant and summary affirmance; a plenary grant would require four Justices who want to define the adjudicatory carve-out now, in a case whose result they already signalled when they stayed the district court's judgment and described the Board as wielding "considerable executive power." A GVR is unlikely on its own terms because the judgment below already matches Slaughter, so there is nothing for the D.C. Circuit to reconsider. The realistic summary form, if any, is a grant with a short per curiam affirmance, and that is rare enough that the whole grant family stays under a tenth.

**Dissent or statement from denial.** Conditional on denial, P = 0.25 that some Justice notes a dissent or files a statement respecting the denial. The three Slaughter dissenters wrote that "how bodies like the MSPB or NLRB, which are mostly adjudicatory" should be treated is an open question, and Justice Kagan dissented from the 2025 Wilcox stay. That makes a short statement noting the question remains open plausible. Against it, the same three Justices let Harris v. Bessent, the MSPB companion from the same D.C. Circuit opinion, go without the petition being held or GVR'd the day after Slaughter, and a denial here is consistent with the precedent they lost on, which usually produces silence rather than a writing. If anyone writes, expect Justice Sotomayor or Justice Kagan, joined by the other two; that "from whom" half is context only.

**If granted (conditional).** On a plenary grant the Court would most likely affirm the D.C. Circuit, holding that an agency that adjudicates unfair-labor-practice cases and issues binding orders enforceable in court exercises executive power under Slaughter regardless of the General Counsel bifurcation, and that the NLRB is not a non-Article III court.
