## In brief

The Supreme Court declined to hear Gwynne Wilcox's challenge to her removal from the National Labor Relations Board.

## What happened

Gwynne Wilcox was removed by President Trump from her position on the National Labor Relations Board before her term ended, without the cause the law governing the Board normally requires. She sued, and a district court ruled in her favor, ordering her reinstated. The government appealed, and the Court of Appeals for the D.C. Circuit reversed, holding that the law protecting Board members from removal without cause violates the separation of powers. The full D.C. Circuit declined to rehear the case. Wilcox then asked the Supreme Court to take up the matter.

## What the Court is being asked

The case asks whether Congress may limit the President's power to remove members of the National Labor Relations Board to cases of neglect of duty or wrongdoing, or whether the President may remove them at will. It also raises a backup question: if that protection is unconstitutional, whether the right fix is to strip the Board of some of its powers rather than erase the removal protection entirely.

## Where it stands

The petition was distributed for conference more than once, and the government filed a brief opposing it after the Court asked for a response. The Supreme Court denied the petition.

## What each outcome would mean

Wilcox, who lost below, asked the Court to take up her case. By denying the petition, the Court leaves the D.C. Circuit's ruling in place: the removal protection for NLRB members is unenforceable, and Wilcox's removal stands. The denial does not itself decide whether the D.C. Circuit was right; it simply leaves that court's judgment as the final word in this case. The docket shows the denial came after the Court's decision in a related case, Trump v. Slaughter, which addressed similar removal-protection questions for a different agency.
