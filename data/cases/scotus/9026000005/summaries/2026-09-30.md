## In brief

A former National Labor Relations Board member asks the Court to review a ruling that her removal by the President did not violate the Constitution.

## What happened

President Trump removed Gwynne A. Wilcox from the National Labor Relations Board before her term ended, without citing neglect of duty or malfeasance, the grounds the law governing Board removals requires. Wilcox sued, and the district court ruled for her, finding the removal protection valid under existing Supreme Court precedent. The government appealed, and the Court of Appeals for the District of Columbia Circuit reversed, holding that the law's limit on removing Board members violates the separation of powers. The full appeals court declined to rehear the case. While this case was pending, the Supreme Court separately overruled the precedent on which Wilcox had relied, in a case about the Federal Trade Commission.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked whether Congress may limit the President's power to remove Board members to cases of neglect of duty or wrongdoing, and, if that limit is invalid, whether the right fix is to strike only the Board's executive-type powers rather than the removal protection itself.

## Where it stands

The petition has been distributed for a conference in October 2026, after the government filed a brief opposing review; the docket does not yet show any action taken on it.

## What each outcome would mean

Wilcox, who lost below, is asking the Court to take up her case. If the Court denies review, the appeals court's ruling against her stands, and the removal protection for Board members is not enforced. If the Court grants review, it would hear full arguments and decide the removal question itself, without yet deciding who wins. The government has also suggested the Court could grant review and summarily affirm the lower ruling without full briefing, which would likewise leave that ruling in place.
