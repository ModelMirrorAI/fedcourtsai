## In brief

A former National Labor Relations Board member asks the Court to review a ruling that her removal by the President was constitutional.

## What happened

President Trump removed Gwynne Wilcox from the National Labor Relations Board before her term ended, without citing any of the reasons a federal law says are required for removing a board member. Wilcox sued, and a district court ruled in her favor, holding that a 1935 Supreme Court precedent protected the board's independence. The government appealed, and the D.C. Circuit reversed, holding that the board's powers are substantial enough that the President must be able to remove its members freely. While this case was pending, the Supreme Court separately decided another case, Trump v. Slaughter, which overruled the 1935 precedent and held that the President may remove members of the Federal Trade Commission at will. Wilcox then asked the Supreme Court to take her case too.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked whether a law limiting the President's power to remove board members to cases of neglect of duty or misconduct is constitutional, and if parts of the board's powers are found to be executive in nature, whether the right fix is to strip away those specific powers rather than invalidate the removal protection entirely.

## Where it stands

The petition has been distributed for conference twice, most recently in October 2026, after the government filed a brief opposing review. The docket does not show any ruling yet on whether the Court will hear the case.

## What each outcome would mean

Wilcox, who lost below, is asking the Court to take up her case. If the Court grants review, it would hear full arguments and decide the removal question itself, deciding nothing in advance. If it denies review, the D.C. Circuit's ruling against Wilcox stands, meaning her removal was lawful. The government has also suggested the Court could grant the petition but summarily affirm the lower court's decision without full briefing, which would likewise leave that ruling in place for Wilcox.
