## In brief

The Court declined to hear a federal rice inspector's bid to revive her lawsuit against the rice mill where a forklift injured her.

## What happened

Rachel Goodley, a federal grain inspector, was injured when a forklift operated by a Supreme Rice employee ran over her foot while she inspected rice at Supreme Rice's mill. She sued Supreme Rice and its insurer in Louisiana state court for her injuries; the case was moved to federal court. The district court ruled that Louisiana's workers' compensation law gave Supreme Rice immunity from the lawsuit, because Supreme Rice had contracted with her employer, a federal inspection agency, to fulfill its obligation to have the rice inspected under a sales contract. The court of appeals agreed and upheld that decision.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked whether a state can treat a federal inspector, sent to a private business to perform official government inspections, as if she were that business's own employee for purposes of a state law that would block her from suing the business over an on-the-job injury. Goodley argues the inspection work was not something Supreme Rice could ever have been obligated to perform itself, since federal law required an independent inspection, so the legal rule should not have applied to shield Supreme Rice from her lawsuit.

## Where it stands

The petition was distributed for the Court's October 9, 2026 conference, and on October 13, 2026 the Court denied the petition.

## What each outcome would mean

Goodley asked the Court to take up her case. With the petition denied, the court of appeals' ruling stands, meaning Supreme Rice and its insurer remain shielded from her tort lawsuit under Louisiana's workers' compensation law, and Goodley's case does not proceed further. The denial does not mean the Supreme Court agreed the lower court was correct; it simply leaves that decision in place without reviewing it.
