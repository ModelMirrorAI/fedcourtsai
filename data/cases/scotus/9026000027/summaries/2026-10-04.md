## In brief

Rachel Goodley, a federal rice inspector hurt in a workplace accident, asks the Court to revive her lawsuit against the rice mill where she was injured.

## What happened

Rachel Goodley, a federal grain inspector, says she was injured when a forklift ran over her foot while she was inspecting rice at a mill operated by Supreme Rice, L.L.C. Goodley sued Supreme Rice and its insurer in Louisiana state court; the companies moved the case to federal court and argued that Louisiana's workers' compensation law shields them from her tort claims because, under a "two-contract" rule, Goodley counted as Supreme Rice's statutory employee. The district court agreed and granted summary judgment against Goodley. The Fifth Circuit affirmed in an unpublished opinion, and Goodley, representing herself, asks the Supreme Court to take the case.

## What the Court is being asked

The case turns on whether a company that hires a federal inspector to certify its products can use a state workers' compensation rule to block the inspector's injury lawsuit, treating the inspector as the company's own employee for legal purposes. Goodley argues an employee of a federal regulatory body should never count as a private company's statutory employee. Supreme Rice responds that the question is a routine application of settled Louisiana law to specific facts, not an issue warranting the Court's review.

## Where it stands

The petition has been distributed for the Court's conference of October 9, 2026, after full briefing, including an extension of time the Court granted for Supreme Rice's response.

## What each outcome would mean

Goodley is asking the Court to take up her case. If the Court grants the petition, it will hear full arguments and decide whether Louisiana's rule was properly applied to bar her tort claims, deciding nothing about who wins until it rules. If the Court denies the petition, the Fifth Circuit's decision stands, leaving in place the summary judgment against Goodley and the dismissal of her tort claims against Supreme Rice and its insurer. A denial would not mean the Supreme Court agrees the lower courts were right, only that their decision remains final.
