# Forecast: Goodley v. Supreme Rice, L.L.C. (No. 26-27)

**Disposition.** The Court will deny the petition on the order list following the October 9, 2026 conference. This is a paid, pro se petition from an unpublished Fifth Circuit opinion applying Louisiana's "two-contract" statutory-employer defense to a federal grain inspector's tort suit. The panel expressly confined its holding to "this specific arrangement," the questions presented are state-law questions dressed in federal clothing, and the one genuinely federal argument (FECA conflict / Supremacy Clause) was held waived below. Nothing on this record moves the Court.

**Relists.** The docket shows exactly one distribution (September 23, 2026, for the October 9 conference) and has never been relisted. I expect it to be decided at that first conference with no further distribution. A single reschedule or hold-over is the only plausible way the count rises, and nothing on the docket invites one.

**CVSG.** No call for the views of the Solicitor General. The petitioner is a federal employee and the case touches a federal inspection program, but the United States is not a party, the federal-law questions were not preserved, and no Justice will spend a CVSG on a pro se petition raising a Louisiana workers' compensation defense.

**Which question, if any.** If the Court took anything it would be a reformulated version of QP 2 — whether a State's statutory-employer immunity may be applied to a federal employee performing official regulatory duties — not QP 1, which is a pure state-law question about the scope of a rice mill's "principal trade and business." But QP 2 was raised for the first time in a motion for reconsideration and on appeal, the panel declined to reach it, and no conflict among the courts of appeals or state high courts is identified. So the Court will not take either question.

**Summary disposition versus plenary review.** Conditional on the very unlikely event of a grant, a GVR is not the natural route: no intervening decision of this Court bears on the two-contract defense or on FECA's interaction with state statutory-employer immunity. A summary reversal of a fact-bound unpublished state-law ruling is equally hard to imagine. So the conditional probability of a cert-order disposition sits somewhat below the ordinary cert-order share of grants, but not at zero, because the only grant I can picture is one that sends the preemption question back for a first look rather than one that sets a pro se Louisiana workers' compensation case for argument.

**Dissent from denial.** None. No Justice will write respecting the denial of a pro se petition of this kind.

**Timing.** Denial on the order list of October 14, 2026 (the Monday after the October 9 conference is a federal holiday), or at latest the following order list if the case is rescheduled.
