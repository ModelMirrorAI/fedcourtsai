## Why reduce 5.02% to 1%?

**The strongest affirmative argument is the federal character of the inspector's work.** QP 2 asks whether a state may treat a federal employee performing regulatory duties as the statutory employee of the regulated private entity. The petition, printed pp. 7-10, argues that independent federal inspection differs fundamentally from commercial subcontracting and emphasizes lack of mill control over the inspector. Its discussion at pp. 24-28 also contests the lower court's treatment of alternative inspection providers and invokes the incompatibility it sees between federal employment and state compensation immunity. This is a real candidate federal issue, with potential relevance beyond this claimant; I do not assign zero probability or dismiss it solely because the petitioner appears without separate counsel in the snapshot.

**The actual vehicle appears much narrower than that formulation.** The opposition, pp. 1-4 and 6-9, describes an unpublished Fifth Circuit decision applying Louisiana's two-contract defense to a particular export-sales and inspection arrangement. It reports that the panel expressly limited its holding and emphasized the voluntary character of this rice-inspection program. The petition's own characterization of the holding as narrow and factually novel does not establish an appellate conflict. The opposition distinguishes an adverse Virginia district-court example by state law and regulatory regime; that is not, on this record, a demonstrated square conflict among courts of appeals.

**Preservation is the strongest negative factor.** According to the opposition, pp. 2 and 7, the FECA-conflict theory was first presented on reconsideration, and the Supremacy Clause argument was first presented on appeal; the panel declined to reach those belated arguments, while also discussing the asserted FECA conflict. The petition's discussion of its motion for new trial at pp. 26-28 does not demonstrate that these federal theories were timely preserved. Even if the substantive issue could interest the Court, these obstacles make this petition an unattractive vehicle for resolving it. I attribute the panel account to the briefs: the appendix/lower-court opinion itself was not among the provisioned text documents and was not independently retrieved.

**Most remaining argument seeks correction of this state-law application.** QP 1 and much of the petition concern the trade-or-business relationship, the meaning of the contracts, and the application of summary judgment. The opposition answers that the two-contract defense is analytically distinct from the alternative trade-or-business inquiry and invokes Rule 10's distinction between cert-worthy questions and case-specific error correction (opposition pp. 2-4, 8-9). I treat these as competing advocacy, not as a finding that respondents necessarily deserve to win the underlying tort dispute.

Together, the narrow unpublished decision, unestablished split, and concrete preservation objections justify a substantial reduction from the 5.02% private risk-set anchor. **1% is my judgmental probability of any grant, including a GVR or summary reversal**, not a fitted estimate or the terminal baseline rate disguised as a forward one. It retains room for an unexpectedly persuasive federal-interest reading or a vehicle fact the supplied filings do not settle. Denial is the categorical prediction; the 99% complement includes small possibilities of other nongrant exits rather than purporting to be an exact denial probability.
