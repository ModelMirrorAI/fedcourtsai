# Forecast: what the Court will do with No. 25-1300, Doe v. Kennedy

**Disposition.** The Court will deny the petition. The denial will issue on the
order list that follows the September 28, 2026 conference, which is the Long
Conference opening October Term 2026, so the expected date is the first order
list of the Term on or about Monday, October 5, 2026. No separate writing will
accompany the denial.

**Relists.** The docket shows exactly one distribution, for the conference of
September 28, 2026. The petition will not be relisted: the government has
waived its response, no question of law is presented, and nothing in the
petition would prompt a Justice to hold it over. The only path to a second
distribution entry is administrative, such as a reschedule out of the Long
Conference's very large slate or a request for a response, and I put the
combined chance of any further distribution near 7 percent.

**Call for a response.** The Court will not request a response from the
Solicitor General. Response requests follow a Justice seeing a colorable
question, and the questions presented here are accusations of collusion and
fraud framed as questions, not legal issues on which a response would help.

**CVSG.** None. The respondent is the Secretary of Health and Human Services,
represented by the Solicitor General, so the government's views arrive as the
respondent's position and there is no third-party federal interest to invite.
The chance of a CVSG is effectively zero.

**Which question presented, if any were taken.** Neither as written. Both
questions assert that the lower courts' judgments were the product of
attorney-judge collusion, health fraud, and unlawful access to military and
veterans records; neither states a rule of law the Court could adopt or
reject. The only legal ruling actually under review is the Federal Circuit's
December 9, 2025 affirmance resting on the Court of Federal Claims' holding
that the petitioner's motion for review of the special master's damages
decision was untimely. A grant would require the Court itself to reformulate
the case as a question about the Vaccine Act's review deadline, and nothing in
the petition asks for or develops that question.

**Summary disposition versus plenary review.** Conditional on any grant, the
route would almost certainly be a summary one, because plenary review of these
questions is not a realistic outcome; but there is no intervening decision of
this Court bearing on Vaccine Act review deadlines or special-master
procedure, so no vehicle for a GVR exists either. That conditional is what the
summary-route claim states; unconditionally, neither route is expected.

**Dissent from denial.** None expected. The case presents no issue any Justice
has flagged in prior writings, the petitioner is pro se, the award below was
not rejected by the petitioner herself, and the government did not even file
an opposition. The probability that any Justice notes a dissent from or
statement respecting the denial is well under 1 percent.

**If granted (conditional only).** If the Court were to act at all, the
realistic form would be a GVR in light of some later decision on the
timeliness of motions for review under the Vaccine Act, which does not exist
today; the Court would not reach the collusion or records-access allegations,
which were not adjudicated as legal claims below.
