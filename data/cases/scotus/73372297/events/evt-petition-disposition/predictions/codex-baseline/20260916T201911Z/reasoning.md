## Why substantially below the anchor

1. **The requested intervention is predominantly case-specific.** The two QPs ask whether this judgment can stand given alleged misconduct and unauthorized use of protected records. The petition's statement and reasons for granting seek correction of evidentiary treatment, damages calculations, attorney conduct, and the handling of this claimant's proceeding. They do not develop a specific inter-court conflict or a clean competing interpretation requiring resolution. I treat the accusations as allegations, not findings, and do not adopt the petition's medical-causation assertions as independent facts.
2. **The petition itself reveals a procedural vehicle problem.** In its section headed “The Appeal,” it says lower courts relied on an allegedly late request for review/reconsideration rather than reaching the merits. Its explanation emphasizes personal circumstances and alleged misconduct rather than isolating a controlling legal conflict over the deadline. Without the appendix, I cannot establish the deadline's exact basis, jurisdictional character, or exceptions. This uncertainty prevents treating a procedural bar as conclusively established, but still weighs against plenary review of the damages issues.
3. **The applicable review criteria do not favor ordinary error correction.** I checked Supreme Court Rule 10 in the Court's official 2026 rules, printed pages 5–6. The rule emphasizes compelling reasons, including important conflicts or exceptional departures from ordinary judicial proceedings, and disfavors factual-error or settled-rule misapplication claims. The allegations could in principle invoke the supervisory category, but their seriousness alone does not demonstrate that the record establishes such a departure. The petition's reliance on asserted violations of established rules is not itself a demonstrated conflict warranting certiorari. Source retrieval is recorded in `retrieval.md`.
4. **The observed attention is minimal.** One initial distribution and a respondent's waiver provide no affirmative signal comparable to a response request, repeated distributions, or an invitation for the government's views. The waiver is not a concession. The petition's references to the Secretary's general criticism of the compensation program are advocacy about wider policy, not evidence that the government supports relief in this case; the observed government act is the waiver.

The move from 5.12% to 0.4% is a judgmental adjustment for this combination, not a fitted model or an empirically measured pro se penalty. I retain nonzero probability because the absent lower-court materials could disclose a clearer procedural problem than the petition's presentation establishes. I do not lower the probability merely because the litigant is self-represented or describes serious hardship.
