## In brief

The Court declined to hear a vaccine-injury claimant's bid to overturn lower court rulings on her compensation case.

## What happened

Jane Doe, representing herself, filed a claim under the federal vaccine injury compensation program, arguing that vaccines she received caused and then worsened her multiple sclerosis. The United States Court of Federal Claims awarded her damages but, according to her petition, did not include everything she believes she is owed, and she says earlier attorneys and a special master mishandled her case, including missing deadlines and excluding evidence. She appealed to the United States Court of Appeals for the Federal Circuit, which ruled against her, upholding the lower court's handling of her case. She then asked the Supreme Court to review that decision.

## What the Court is being asked

The petition asks the Court to decide whether a judgment can stand when the petitioner says the proceedings below were marred by misconduct, mishandling of evidence, and missed deadlines she attributes to circumstances beyond her control, and whether a ruling can stand that she says relied on records obtained in violation of privacy laws.

## Where it stands

The Secretary of Health and Human Services waived the right to respond to the petition. The case was distributed for the Court's conference on September 28, 2026, and the petition was denied on October 5, 2026.

## What each outcome would mean

Jane Doe asked the Court to take up her case. The denial means the Court will not review the Federal Circuit's decision, so that court's ruling against her stands, leaving the lower courts' handling of her compensation claim and damages award in place. The denial does not mean the Supreme Court found the lower courts correct on the merits of her claims; it simply leaves those decisions as the final word in her case.
