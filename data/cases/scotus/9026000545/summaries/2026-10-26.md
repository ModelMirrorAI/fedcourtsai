## In brief

A former transit authority employee asks the Court to revive her discrimination and retaliation lawsuit after lower courts ruled against her.

## What happened

Diana Lugo worked as a customer service representative for the Birmingham Jefferson County Transit Authority, scheduling rides for disabled passengers. After receiving discipline for performance problems, she was placed on a performance improvement plan and then fired. Representing herself, Lugo sued, claiming discrimination based on race and national origin and retaliation for complaints and advocacy on behalf of disabled riders. The district court granted summary judgment to the Transit Authority on all claims, finding insufficient evidence of discrimination or retaliation. The Eleventh Circuit affirmed, and the district court and appeals court each denied Lugo's requests for reconsideration and rehearing.

## What the Court is being asked

Lugo's petition raises three questions. First, whether the appeals court used too strict a standard for comparing her treatment to coworkers, making it harder to prove discrimination in that circuit than elsewhere. Second, whether the courts below improperly decided factual disputes that should have gone to a jury, rather than simply checking whether a jury could reasonably side with her. Third, whether speaking up on behalf of disabled customers counts as a protected activity under disability-rights law, a question other appeals courts answer differently.

## Where it stands

The petition was filed on October 21, 2026, and docketed on October 26, 2026. The Transit Authority's response is due November 25, 2026. The Court has not yet acted on the petition.

## What each outcome would mean

Lugo, who lost in the lower courts, is asking the Court to take up her case. If the Court grants the petition, it will hear the case and decide these questions, which would decide nothing yet about who prevails. If the Court denies the petition, the Eleventh Circuit's decision stands, leaving in place the summary judgment victory for the Birmingham Jefferson County Transit Authority and ending Lugo's lawsuit.
