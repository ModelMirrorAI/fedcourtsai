## In brief

A former transit authority employee asks the Court to review the dismissal of her workplace discrimination and retaliation lawsuit.

## What happened

Diana Lugo worked as a customer service representative for the Birmingham Jefferson County Transit Authority, scheduling rides for disabled passengers. After she was disciplined several times and eventually fired, she sued, representing herself, claiming race and national-origin discrimination and retaliation under federal civil rights and disability laws. She argued she was punished for complaining about mistreatment and for advocating for disabled riders, not for poor performance. The district court found insufficient evidence to let a jury decide her claims and granted summary judgment to the transit authority, meaning it ruled against her without a trial. The court also denied her request to reconsider that ruling. The Eleventh Circuit Court of Appeals affirmed, and later denied rehearing.

## What the Court is being asked

Lugo's petition raises three issues: whether the appeals court used too strict a test for comparing her treatment to coworkers of other backgrounds, in a way that conflicts with other appeals courts; whether the appeals court wrongly avoided weighing conflicting evidence that she says should have gone to a jury rather than being decided by a judge; and whether advocating on behalf of disabled customers counts as protected activity under disability-rights retaliation law, a question she says other appeals courts answer differently than the Eleventh Circuit.

## Where it stands

The case is a petition for certiorari, asking the Supreme Court to take up the case. The petition was filed in October 2026, and the transit authority's response is due in late November 2026. The docket does not yet show any further action.

## What each outcome would mean

Lugo, who lost in the lower courts, is asking the Supreme Court to take up her case and reverse the rulings against her. If the Court grants the petition, it would agree to hear the case and decide these questions itself, without yet deciding who is right. If it denies the petition, the Eleventh Circuit's decision affirming summary judgment for the transit authority stands, and Lugo's lawsuit remains dismissed. A denial would not mean the Supreme Court agrees with the lower courts, only that the case will not be reviewed further.
