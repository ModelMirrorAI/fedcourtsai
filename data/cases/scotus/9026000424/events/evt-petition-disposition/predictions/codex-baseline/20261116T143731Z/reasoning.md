This is the cert-stage distribution event, in forward mode, using the case-level snapshot `record/snapshots/2026-11-16.json` and its conditioning in `record/context.json`. The petition raises two questions: deprivation of a nonparty owner's property without a meaningful hearing or appeal, and the authority of an FTC receivership to reform contracts and dispose of that property under Section 13(b).

I exclude Term 2026 and do not substitute the substantially lower terminal-baseline rates. The modern-cert disposition table provides a broader cross-check: 1,282 grant-family outcomes among 45,129 resolved petitions, approximately 2.84%, but the frozen band is the appropriate starting population.

The frozen amicus reference is materially higher: **16.33%**, based on 2,315 resolved petitions in the baseline-reached, some-amicus arm. That is evidence to weigh, not ignore. But this split uses the brief count at disposition, and some petitions gained support only after attracting additional Court attention. Here the one brief comes from purchasers directly connected to this receivership; its existence demonstrates an affected constituency, not an established inter-circuit conflict. Its text was not provisioned and I did not retrieve it, so I make no claim about its arguments or position. I give the filing positive weight, but not the full reference-rate uplift.

## Why the vehicle moves me below the band anchor

The strongest affirmative point is the petition's account of a procedural trap: the district court rejected attempted participation for lack of party status, while the court of appeals refused review partly for lack of participation. If that accurately describes the available avenues and deprivation, the first question has genuine constitutional force. AMG Capital Management, LLC v. FTC, 593 U.S. 67 (2021), also supplies a substantial statutory limit: Section 13(b) does not itself authorize equitable monetary relief such as restitution or disgorgement. I verified that holding through the primary opinion, CourtListener opinion 4679977, rather than treating counsel's characterization as sufficient.

Nevertheless, the judgment below is an unpublished, three-page application of intervention and nonparty-appeal doctrine, not a merits holding approving the challenged property sale. It separately affirms the refusal to permit intervention under abuse-of-discretion review and dismisses the remaining appeal for lack of nonparty appellate standing (`decision-below.txt`, pp. 1–3). The QPs do not isolate a developed conflict over either procedural ground. The petition identifies no concrete circuit split; its central pitch is incompatibility with general Supreme Court precedents (`petition.txt`, pp. 24–33). Sky Cable, LLC v. DIRECTV, Inc., 886 F.3d 375, 384 (4th Cir. 2018), the precedent the panel applied, itself recognizes an exception for interested nonparties who participated in the relevant proceedings. Its primary text was checked through CourtListener opinion 4259045. Thus this record does not establish a new categorical rule barring all nonparty appeals.

More importantly, the petition itself recounts a November 2024 order directing Boddie to a separate complaint procedure and stating that a timely complaint meeting the requirements would be opened as a new case (`petition.txt`, p. 17). That substantially complicates the assertion that every forum was foreclosed. I cannot determine whether that alternative could have fully protected her rights, but its existence makes the first QP's clean no-hearing premise disputable. The second question additionally requires getting past these procedural rulings before reaching the receivership's statutory basis, the precise ownership interest, and the effect of earlier remedial orders. AMG predates the challenged appellate judgment by almost five years; it is not a newly intervening decision supplying a routine GVR route. These problems outweigh the modest amicus and constitutional-interest uplift and bring my final grant-family probability to 2%.

## Other probabilities and limitations

I assign 12% to a further distribution, usually just one additional conference if it occurs, rather than treating the existing distribution as a relist. A possible request for a response or closer examination of the alleged procedural trap supports that tail. A new CVSG is only 0.3%: this is not a demonstrated unsettled national policy conflict, and ordinary response procedures would be a more natural way to seek the government's position. Conditional on a grant, I put 45% on disposal in the cert order: correction of a procedural error could produce a summary disposition, but the record disputes and absence of a newly intervening controlling decision leave plenary consideration slightly more likely within that small grant branch. The implied unconditional summary-route probability is 0.9%, not 45%.

Conditional on denial, I put 1.5% on a noted dissent or statement. The independent, unconditional order-writer probabilities are correspondingly very small; Thomas and Gorsuch receive the largest amounts because a property-rights or remedial-power concern supplies the most plausible separate-writing theme. This is weak issue-fit judgment, not a forecast that either Justice will actually write. The significance score, 0.38, reflects the real consequences for owners and possible broader receivership implications, independently of grant likelihood.

The manifest marks the staged filings as nonempty, untruncated, and not OCR-derived. Nevertheless, the nine-page appendix includes the contents list, the appellate opinion, and the February 2026 letter order, then says further material is available in the Clerk's Office. I have not independently read the underlying sale, redress, and FTC letter documents listed there; their contents here largely come from petitioner's descriptions. No BIO, cert reply, invited SG brief, or same-question/related-docket record was provisioned. Their absence is a limit on this information set, not proof of concessions or of absent related litigation.

The corpus citation query returned no matching AMG row and expressly warned of sparse citation coverage. A broader structured prior query returned heterogeneous recent resolutions, not a matched sample; I did not derive odds from those five results. General official-site web attempts produced no readable output, so none supplied evidence. The CourtListener primary-opinion tools and corpus service worked. I did not retrieve this petition's disposition, its subsequent history, or another person's forecast, and I have no known prior knowledge of its outcome.
