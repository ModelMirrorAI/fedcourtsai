# Forecast of the Court's action

The Court will deny the petition, most likely in a routine orders list following the December 4, 2026 conference, during December 2026. The present record contains **one distribution and no completed relist**. The modal path is denial without another distribution. There is a **12% chance of at least one additional distribution**; if that occurs, one further distribution is more likely than several, with a decision correspondingly delayed into a later conference cycle.

The Court will not call for the Solicitor General's views. The probability of a new CVSG is **0.3%**. Any additional governmental input is more likely to come through an ordinary requested response than an invitation to provide views. Nothing in the current snapshot records either step.

The petition will most likely end without an explanation or separate writing. Conditional on denial, the chance of a noted dissent or statement respecting denial is **1.5%**. A rare writing would more plausibly focus on the asserted inability of a property owner to obtain a hearing than endorse the petition's whole account of the receivership. Thomas or Gorsuch is the most plausible author within that small writing tail; no author is expected on the modal path.

If the Court grants review, it will more likely focus on a narrowed version of the first question: whether an owner allegedly bound by a receivership disposition can be denied intervention and appellate review after attempts to object were rejected. It is less likely to take the second question's broader challenge to the entire FTC redress regime without first resolving that procedural obstacle. If plenary review occurs, the scope and adequacy of the separate-complaint procedure will be central to the due-process question.

Conditional on any grant, **45%** goes to a disposition in the cert order itself and **55%** to plenary review. A summary correction of the intervention/nonparty-review ruling is more plausible than a merits ruling summarily invalidating the redress plan. A conventional GVR based on a newly intervening precedent is not the expected route: the AMG decision invoked by the petition already predates the appellate judgment. All merits discussion here is conditional; a denial will produce no majority opinion deciding the property's ownership or the legality of the receivership.
