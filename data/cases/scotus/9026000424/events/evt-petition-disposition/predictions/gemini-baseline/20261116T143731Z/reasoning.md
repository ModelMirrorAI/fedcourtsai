The petition arises from the long-running Federal Trade Commission enforcement receivership in *In re Sanctuary Belize Litigation* (*FTC v. Sanctuary Belize Enterprise Ltd.*), No. 1:18-cv-03309-PX (D. Md.), involving a massive Belizean real-estate development fraud orchestrated by Andris Pukke, Peter Baker, and John Usher. See *FTC v. Pukke* (*Pukke I*), 53 F.4th 80 (4th Cir. 2022), *cert. denied*, 144 S. Ct. 73 (2023); *FTC v. Pukke* (*Pukke II*), No. 23-1742 (4th Cir. Dec. 12, 2024); *FTC v. Yu Lin*, 66 F.4th 164 (4th Cir. 2023). Petitioner Beckie Boddie, proceeding *pro se* in her capacity as "Successor Trustee" of "The Boerum Family Trust" — described in the petition as "an irrevocable trust governed by Nevada law" (`petition.txt` at 34) holding pre-receivership installment lot-purchase rights at Sanctuary Belize / Kanantik — was never a party to the District of Maryland action.

The underlying district-court and appellate record establishes the following sequence:
1. **Consumer Redress Plan & Next-Phase Survey Orders (2022–2023):** Following trial and judgment, the district court approved a Consumer Redress Plan effective September 12, 2022 (`ECF Nos. 1117-1, 1373`) and, on June 14, 2023, entered the *Order Implementing Next Phase of Consumer Redress* (`ECF No. 1446`, adopting `ECF No. 1437-1`), giving eligible lot purchasers a 90-day election among three options (immediate cash/third-party-financed closing prior to an institutional sale, deferral until post-sale terms offered by a bulk buyer, or waiver of lot acquisition while retaining pro rata cash distribution rights).
2. **Returned Nonparty Submissions and Explicit Separate-Action Guidance (Oct.–Nov. 2024):** In October 2024, Boddie submitted a nonparty petition (`ECF No. 1540`), which was returned because she was not a party (`ECF No. 1541`). On November 19, 2024, denying her motion for reconsideration (`ECF No. 1543`), Judge Peter J. Messitte entered a Memorandum Order (`ECF No. 1547`, quoted at `petition.txt` p. 17) explicitly instructing her:
   > *"The Court DENIES the Motion and DIRECTS you to refer to the Receiver's email regarding Kanantik Survey Option #1 sent on or about August 21, 2024. That email informs claimants, such as yourself, how to file a separate complaint with the Court. If a complaint is timely and meets all other requirements, the Court will accept it and open a new case. As the Receiver's letter notes, you may wish to hire an attorney if you intend to file a complaint."*
   Boddie neither appealed `ECF No. 1547`, nor filed a separate civil complaint, nor retained counsel for the irrevocable trust, nor filed a formal motion to intervene under Federal Rule of Civil Procedure 24.
3. **Final Receivership Property Sale and Post-Sale Redress Distributions (May 2025 – Jan. 2026):** After Judge Messitte's passing and reassignment to Judge Paula Xinis, Boddie sent a March 2025 letter to Chief Judge George L. Russell III objecting to payoff interest and acceleration calculations, which was returned un-docketed on nonparty grounds (`ECF Nos. 1574, 1574-1`). By contrast, when represented third-party bidder Bob Yari filed a formal Rule 24 motion to intervene (`ECF No. 1563`), Judge Xinis held a hearing and issued a 9-page Memorandum Opinion denying intervention on the merits (`ECF No. 1587`, May 13, 2025). That same day, May 13, 2025, Judge Xinis entered the final Sale Order (`ECF No. 1588`, App. D) approving the $20.5 million bulk sale of the Sanctuary Belize and Kanantik assets to First Belizean Investment Market Ltd. (`FBIM`) and terminating pre-receivership Memorandum of Sale contracts (`ECF No. 1588` ¶ 8). Boddie did not move to intervene before the sale or appeal the May 13, 2025 Sale Order. Eight months later, on January 12, 2026, Judge Xinis approved consumer redress distributions (`ECF No. 1603`).
4. **January 2026 Nonparty Rule 60(b)(4) Motion and Fourth Circuit Affirmance/Dismissal (Jan.–Apr. 2026):** Two days after the distribution order, on January 14, 2026, Boddie filed a *pro se* motion in `No. 1:18-cv-03309-PX` seeking relief from "void orders" under Fed. R. Civ. P. 60(b)(4) (`ECF No. 1605`). On February 3, 2026, Judge Xinis entered a two-page Letter Order (`ECF No. 1612`, App. B, 4a–5a) striking `ECF No. 1605` because Boddie was not a party to the litigation and referring her back to `ECF No. 1547`. On April 9, 2026, a unanimous Fourth Circuit panel (Judges Wilkinson, King, and Thacker — the same panel that authored *Pukke I* and *Pukke II*) issued a three-page unpublished per curiam opinion in *In re: Beckie Boddie*, No. 26-1130 (`decision-below.txt`, App. A, 1a–3a), affirming in part and dismissing in part:
   - Construing Boddie's Rule 60(b)(4) motion as a request to intervene in the district-court proceedings, the Fourth Circuit discerned "no abuse of discretion in the court's refusal to allow Boddie to intervene" (citing *Gould v. Alleco, Inc.*, 883 F.2d 281, 284 (4th Cir. 1989)).
   - As to Boddie's attempt to challenge the underlying redress plan orders directly, the Fourth Circuit dismissed the appeal for lack of nonparty appellate standing because she "did not actively participate in the district court proceedings" (citing *Sky Cable, LLC v. DIRECTV, Inc.*, 886 F.3d 375, 384 (4th Cir. 2018); *Doe v. Pub. Citizen*, 749 F.3d 246, 259 (4th Cir. 2014)). No rehearing petition was filed (`lower-court-docket.txt`).

In this Court, Boddie presents two questions (`questions-presented.txt`): (1) whether the Fifth Amendment Due Process Clause is violated when a court in an FTC enforcement action disposes of a nonparty owner's real property without notice or a hearing and forecloses appellate review on nonparty grounds; and (2) whether Section 13(b) of the FTC Act, 15 U.S.C. § 53(b), after *AMG Capital Management, LLC v. FTC*, 593 U.S. 67 (2021), empowers a district court to enter a redress plan order that rewrites a nonparty owner's contracts and disposes of the nonparty's real property.

## 3. Case-Specific Analysis: Severe Vehicle, Jurisdictional, and Merits Barriers

### A. *Pro Se* Representation of an Irrevocable Trust and Defective Respondent Caption
1. **Unrepresented Irrevocable Trust Defect (28 U.S.C. § 1654):** Petitioner files *pro se* (`snapshot_contact_scrub.sides = ["Petitioner"]`) as "Successor Trustee" of "The Boerum Family Trust," which she affirmatively describes as **"an irrevocable trust governed by Nevada law"** holding the Sanctuary Belize / Kanantik lot interest (`petition.txt` at i, iv, 4, 34, 36). Under settled federal practice construing 28 U.S.C. § 1654, a non-attorney trustee cannot represent a trust *pro se* in federal court because a trustee acts in a fiduciary/representative capacity rather than prosecuting solely her own personal cause. See, e.g., *Rowland v. California Men's Colony*, 506 U.S. 194, 201–02 (1993); *C.E. Pope Equity Trust v. United States*, 818 F.2d 696, 697–98 (9th Cir. 1987). Granting plenary review would immediately confront the Court with an unauthorized *pro se* representative filing on behalf of an artificial trust entity.
2. **Wrong Respondent Named on Certiorari:** Although styled as a Rule 12 Petition for a Writ of Certiorari to the Fourth Circuit (not an extraordinary writ of mandamus under Rule 20), Petitioner named **"United States District Court for the District of Maryland"** as the sole Respondent and expressly stated that neither the **Federal Trade Commission** nor **Successor Receiver Marc-Philip Ferzan** — the actual adverse parties whose orders and asset sale she attacks — is a party to the petition (`petition.txt` at ii–iv). Adjudicating the validity of the FTC Redress Plan or the Receiver's closed $20.5 million sale to `FBIM` without the FTC, the Receiver, or the third-party purchaser before the Court is procedurally untenable.

### B. Collapse of QP 1's Factual Premise ("No Forum Available") and Multiple Procedural Bars
Petitioner's first Question Presented rests entirely on the assertion that the district court and Fourth Circuit trapped her in a "bound-but-unheard" Catch-22 leaving "no forum ... in which the Fifth Amendment claim may be heard" (`petition.txt` at i, 24, 27–29, citing *Mullane v. Central Hanover Bank & Trust Co.*, 339 U.S. 306 (1950), *United States v. James Daniel Good Real Property*, 510 U.S. 43 (1993), and *Devlin v. Scardelletti*, 536 U.S. 1 (2002)). That premise fails on the face of Petitioner's own appendix and statement of the case:
1. **Express Separate-Complaint Procedure Declined (`ECF No. 1547`):** Six months *before* the May 13, 2025 Sale Order, Judge Messitte's November 19, 2024 Memorandum Order (`ECF No. 1547`, quoted verbatim at `petition.txt` p. 17) specifically pointed Petitioner to the Receiver's August 21, 2024 instructions explaining *"how to file a separate complaint with the Court,"* assured her that *"[i]f a complaint is timely and meets all other requirements, the Court will accept it and open a new case,"* and noted she *"may wish to hire an attorney"* to do so. Petitioner never hired counsel or filed that separate civil action.
2. **Untimely Post-Sale, Post-Distribution Filing (`ECF No. 1605`):** Nor did Petitioner file a timely Rule 24 motion to intervene before the May 13, 2025 Sale Order (`ECF No. 1588`) — unlike third-party bidder Bob Yari, whose counselled Rule 24 motion (`ECF No. 1563`) received a full hearing and a 9-page opinion (`ECF No. 1587`). Instead, Petitioner waited until **January 14, 2026** — eight years into the receivership, more than two-and-a-half years after the June 2023 Redress Order (`ECF No. 1446`), eight months after the May 2025 Sale Order (`ECF No. 1588`), and two days after final consumer redress distributions were approved (`ECF No. 1603`) — to file a nonparty Rule 60(b)(4) motion. Rule 60(b) by its plain text applies only to *"a party or its legal representative,"* and even generously construed by the Fourth Circuit as a late-stage Rule 24 intervention request, affirming the denial for untimeliness/no abuse of discretion (*Gould*, 883 F.2d at 284; cf. *NAACP v. New York*, 413 U.S. 345, 365 (1973)) is a routine, factbound application of settled law with zero circuit conflict.
3. **Independent Jurisdictional Untimeliness as to Underlying Redress and Sale Orders:** Even if *Devlin v. Scardelletti*, 536 U.S. 1 (2002), were extended beyond unnamed class members who timely object at a Rule 23 fairness hearing and timely appeal within the statutory window, Petitioner's February 2026 notice of appeal came **years** after the September 2022 and June 2023 Redress Orders (`ECF Nos. 1373, 1446`) and **nearly nine months** after the May 13, 2025 Sale Order (`ECF No. 1588`) — far outside the 60-day jurisdictional civil appeal deadline of 28 U.S.C. § 2107(b) and Fed. R. App. P. 4(a)(1)(B). See *Bowles v. Russell*, 551 U.S. 205, 214 (2007). Thus, the Fourth Circuit lacked appellate jurisdiction to review those underlying orders directly regardless of nonparty standing.

### C. QP 2 (*AMG Capital* / FTC Act § 13(b)) Was Unreached Below and Misstates the Receivership's Legal Basis
1. **Unreached Merits:** Neither Judge Xinis's two-sentence substantive ruling in `ECF No. 1612` nor the Fourth Circuit's three-page unpublished per curiam opinion (`App. A, 1a–3a`) said a word about FTC Act § 13(b), *AMG Capital*, 28 U.S.C. § 959(b), 11 U.S.C. § 541(d), or 18 U.S.C. § 983(d). As "a court of review, not of first view" (*Cutter v. Wilkinson*, 544 U.S. 709, 718 n.7 (2005)), this Court does not grant certiorari to decide statutory remedies questions where two threshold procedural bars prevented any lower court from reaching them.
2. **Independent $120.2 Million Contempt Judgment Already Affirmed (*Pukke I* & *Pukke II*):** Even on the merits, QP 2's premise — that the Sanctuary Belize receivership and liquidation rest solely on Section 13(b) in defiance of *AMG Capital* — was squarely rejected by the Fourth Circuit in **two published opinions** in this very litigation. In *Pukke I*, 53 F.4th at 105–09, *cert. denied*, 144 S. Ct. 73 (2023), and *Pukke II*, No. 23-1742, slip op. at 7–10 (4th Cir. Dec. 12, 2024), the Fourth Circuit explained that while it vacated the standalone Section 13(b) equitable monetary judgment after *AMG Capital*, (a) the **$120.2 million compensatory civil contempt sanction** for violating the 2005 *AmeriDebt* injunction independently supports consumer redress collection, and (b) the receivership and asset freeze independently stand as ancillary equitable relief effectuating the permanent injunctions under Section 13(b). Having already denied certiorari to the represented principal defendants on that exact post-*AMG* challenge in *Pukke v. FTC*, 144 S. Ct. 73 (2023), the Court has zero reason to revisit it on a jurisdictionally defective *pro se* collateral appeal after the Belizean land sale has already closed.

### D. Docket Signals: Solicitor General Non-Response vs. Single Co-Purchaser Amicus Brief
- **Solicitor General Silence (`distribution_count = 1`):** The Solicitor General allowed the October 28, 2026 response deadline to expire without filing a Brief in Opposition, sending the petition directly to the December 4, 2026 conference under Rule 15.5. That reflects DOJ's routine assessment that a *pro se* nonparty appeal from a 3-page unpublished procedural affirmance/dismissal poses negligible grant risk.
- **Single Lot-Purchaser Amicus Brief:** The sole factor counseling a modest upward adjustment from an ultra-low (~0.1%–0.2%) *pro se* floor is the timely October 28, 2026 amicus brief filed by licensed D.C. counsel (`Michael Joseph Showalter`) on behalf of fellow *Sanctuary Belize Lot Purchasers*. Unlike an institutional repeat-player amicus flagging a clean national circuit conflict, this brief comes from co-claimants in the same receivership proceeding. At most, having counselled amicus papers marginally raises the chance (~2.5%) that a chambers law clerk flags the *AMG* / property-rights framing for a **Call for a Response (`CFR`)** out of caution; once a Brief in Opposition lays out `ECF No. 1547`, the closed May 2025 sale, § 2107(b) untimeliness, *pro se* trust representation, and *Pukke I/II*'s contempt holding, any transient interest would evaporate.
