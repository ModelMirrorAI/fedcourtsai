In *Beckie Boddie v. United States District Court for the District of Maryland* (No. 26-424), a certiorari-stage petition distributed for the Supreme Court's December 4, 2026 conference, the predicted disposition is **denied**, with a **0.5% probability of a grant** (99.5% probability against review). That sits far below both the **5.2% historical grant-family base rate** for paid petitions entering at the Court's baseline tier and the **16.3% reference rate** for paid baseline petitions supported by at least one amicus brief.

Severe procedural and vehicle defects drive the forecast to 0.5%. Proceeding *pro se* as trustee of an irrevocable Nevada family trust—a representative capacity generally requiring licensed counsel in federal court—Petitioner challenges consumer-redress and asset-sale orders in the Federal Trade Commission's *Sanctuary Belize* fraud receivership while naming only the federal district court, rather than the FTC or the court-appointed receiver, as respondent. Although she contends that lower-court rulings left no forum for her Fifth Amendment due-process claim, the petition itself quotes a November 2024 district-court order instructing her six months before the May 2025 bulk sale how to file a timely separate civil complaint with counsel. Instead, she waited until January 2026—eight months after the $20.5 million sale closed—to file a nonparty Rule 60(b)(4) motion, producing a three-page unpublished Fourth Circuit per curiam decision affirming the denial of untimely intervention and dismissing the remaining appeal for lack of nonparty appellate standing. Her second question under *AMG Capital Management, LLC v. FTC* and Section 13(b) was unreached below and ignores the independent $120.2 million civil-contempt judgment previously affirmed by the Fourth Circuit.

The Solicitor General filed no opposition, reinforcing the likelihood of a routine denial without separate writings. A timely amicus brief from fellow Sanctuary Belize lot purchasers creates only a modest (~3.5%) chance of a call for a response or relist; even conditional on review, a summary vacatur and remand (60%) is likelier than plenary argument.
