# Forecast: what the Court will do with No. 26-424, Boddie v. United States District Court for the District of Maryland

**Disposition: denied**, on the first order list after the petition is
considered at conference, most likely in December 2026 or January 2027.

**Distributions.** The docket shows zero distributions; this is the arrival
moment, so nothing about conference exists yet. The Solicitor General, as
counsel for the respondent court, will waive the right to respond within a few
weeks of docketing (the response is nominally due October 28, 2026). The
petition will then be distributed once for a conference roughly a month later.
I expect exactly one distribution and no relist: the petition will be denied at
its first conference.

**CVSG.** None. The Solicitor General already represents the respondent, so
there is no independent federal view to call for, and nothing about a pro se
mandamus petition invites one.

**Question the Court would take, if any.** None as framed. The petition asks the
Court to review the Fourth Circuit's unpublished per curiam order of April 9,
2026 denying a writ of mandamus (In re Beckie Boddie, No. 26-1130) directed at
the district court presiding over In re Sanctuary Belize Litigation, where the
petitioner filed as a self-described victim and third party. Mandamus review of
a district court's handling of a receivership claimant's correspondence raises
no question of federal law on which courts of appeals divide, and there is no
plausible narrowed or reformulated question that would.

**Summary disposition.** Not the likely route, because no grant of any kind is
likely. Conditional on the Court granting anything, the only realistic form is
a summary order (a GVR) rather than plenary review, since no argued question
exists.

**Dissent or statement respecting denial.** None expected. No Justice writes
separately on a pro se mandamus denial of this kind.
