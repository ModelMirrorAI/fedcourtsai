## In brief

A property owner asks the Court to review a lower court's refusal to let her challenge a receivership sale affecting her property.

## What happened

Beckie Boddie, trustee of a family trust that owned a lot at the Sanctuary Belize development, was never named as a defendant in a Federal Trade Commission enforcement lawsuit against the project's developers in federal district court in Maryland. She says court orders in that case nonetheless bound her property, changed the financial terms attached to it, and eventually approved its sale to a third party. When she tried to object in the district court, her filings were returned or struck because she was not a party. The Fourth Circuit affirmed the denial of her bid to intervene and dismissed the rest of her appeal, holding she lacked standing to appeal as a nonparty who had not actively participated below.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked whether it violates constitutional due process rights for a court to dispose of a nonparty's real property without notice or a hearing, and then bar that person from appealing because she was never made a party. It is also asked whether the law letting the FTC seek injunctions in such cases allows a court to rewrite a nonparty's private contracts and dispose of her property.

## Where it stands

The petition was filed on July 6, 2026, and the respondent's response is due October 28, 2026. The docket shows no further action since filing.

## What each outcome would mean

Boddie, the petitioner, is asking the Court to take up her case. If the Court grants the petition, it would hear full arguments and decide the due process and statutory questions, without yet deciding who wins. If it denies the petition, the Fourth Circuit's decision dismissing her appeal and leaving the lower court's orders in place would stand, and she would have no further avenue in this case to challenge the property disposition.
