## In brief

Beckie Boddie asks the Court to review a ruling that she could not challenge orders affecting trust property because she was never a party.

## What happened

A federal enforcement action by the Federal Trade Commission against operators of the Sanctuary Belize and Kanantik developments led a district court to appoint a receiver and approve a redress plan and eventual property sale affecting lot owners. Boddie, acting as trustee for a family trust that holds an interest in one of the lots, was never named as a party. She repeatedly tried to file motions objecting to how the redress plan and sale affected the trust's property; the district court struck or returned these filings, saying she was not a party and could not seek relief. She appealed, and the Fourth Circuit affirmed the denial of her request to intervene and dismissed the rest of her appeal, holding she lacked standing to appeal as a nonparty who had not actively participated below.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked whether it violates constitutional due process protections for a federal court to bind and ultimately sell a nonparty's real property in an enforcement action without giving that owner notice or a hearing, and then to deny any appeal on the ground that the owner was never a party. The Court is also asked whether the law letting the FTC seek only injunctions in such cases allows a court to go further and rewrite a nonparty's private contracts and dispose of that nonparty's property.

## Where it stands

The petition was filed and docketed, with a response due in late October 2026. An amicus brief supporting the petition was later filed by a group describing itself as Sanctuary Belize lot purchasers. The docket does not show that the Court has acted on the petition.

## What each outcome would mean

Boddie is asking the Court to take up her case. If the Court grants the petition, it will hear full briefing and argument and decide whether the lower courts' handling of her claims violated due process and exceeded the FTC Act's authority; a grant would not itself decide who wins. If the Court denies the petition, the Fourth Circuit's dismissal of Boddie's appeal stands, leaving in place the rulings that treated her as unable to challenge the orders affecting the trust's property because she was not a party below.
