## In brief

Beckie Boddie asks the Court to review a lower-court ruling that she could not challenge orders affecting a property interest because she was not a party to the underlying case.

## What happened

The case grows out of a Federal Trade Commission enforcement action against companies selling real estate in Belize, which led a federal district court to appoint a receiver and approve a redress plan for consumers who had bought lots. Boddie, who holds an interest in a lot through a family trust, was never named as a party in that case. She tried to challenge court orders covering the property, including an order approving its sale, but the district court repeatedly struck or returned her filings, saying she was not a party and could not seek relief. The Fourth Circuit affirmed the denial of her request to intervene and dismissed the rest of her appeal, holding that nonparties generally cannot appeal unless they actively participated below, which it found she had not done.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked whether it violates constitutional fair-process guarantees for a court to dispose of a nonparty's property without notice or a hearing, and then bar that person from appealing because they were never made a party. The petition also asks whether the law letting the FTC seek injunctions in enforcement cases allows a court to rewrite a nonparty's private contracts and order the sale of that nonparty's property.

## Where it stands

The petition was filed and docketed, with a response from the government originally due in late October 2026. The docket shows a supporting brief filed by a group called Sanctuary Belize Lot Purchasers, but no order from the Court granting or denying the petition.

## What each outcome would mean

Boddie, the petitioner, is asking the Court to take up her case and reverse the Fourth Circuit's dismissal. If the Court grants the petition, it would hear full briefing and argument and decide whether the lower courts' treatment of her as a nonparty violated her rights. If it denies the petition, the Fourth Circuit's dismissal stands, leaving in place the rulings that Boddie could not intervene or appeal, and the underlying property and redress orders remain undisturbed.
