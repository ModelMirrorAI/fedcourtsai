## In brief

A property owner asks the Court to review a federal enforcement case's handling of her property interest after lower courts found she was not a party.

## What happened

Beckie Boddie, as successor trustee of a family trust that owns lots at properties called Sanctuary Belize and Kanantik, says the trust owns real estate caught up in a Federal Trade Commission enforcement case against other defendants. She was never named as a party in that case. Over several years, the district court entered orders governing a "redress plan" for consumers and eventually approved selling the properties, including, Boddie says, the trust's lot. When Boddie tried to object or seek relief in the district court, her filings were returned or struck because she was not a party. The Fourth Circuit treated her filing as a request to intervene, agreed she could not intervene, and dismissed the rest of her appeal because she had not participated in the district court case.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked whether it violates constitutional protections against taking property without fair procedures for a federal court to dispose of a non-party owner's real property without notice or a hearing, and then to block any appeal on the ground that the owner was never a party. The Court is also asked whether the law letting the FTC seek only injunctions lets a court instead issue an order that rewrites a non-party owner's private contracts and disposes of that owner's property.

## Where it stands

The petition was filed and docketed on September 28, 2026, with the respondent's response due October 28, 2026. The Court has not yet acted on it.

## What each outcome would mean

Boddie, the petitioner, is asking the Court to take up her case. If the Court grants the petition, it would hear the case fully and decide whether the procedures used to bind and sell her trust's property violated constitutional protections and exceeded the FTC's authority; a grant would not itself decide who wins. If the Court denies the petition, the Fourth Circuit's decision stands, leaving in place the rulings that Boddie was not entitled to intervene or appeal, and the property sale and redress orders remain as entered.
