## In brief

A trust's successor trustee asks the Court to review whether courts can bind and sell her trust's property in an FTC enforcement case without making her a party.

## What happened

Beckie Boddie, successor trustee of a family trust that owns a lot in the Sanctuary Belize development, was never named as a party in the underlying FTC enforcement action against the development's operators. A federal district court entered orders establishing a redress plan, later changed its financial terms, and approved selling the property to a third party, ending Boddie's original purchase contract. When Boddie tried to challenge these orders, the district court repeatedly struck her filings because she was not a party. The Fourth Circuit affirmed denial of her bid to intervene and dismissed the rest of her appeal, finding she lacked standing to appeal as a nonparty.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked whether it violates constitutional due-process protections for a federal court to dispose of a nonparty property owner's real estate without notice or a hearing, while also blocking any appeal on the ground that the owner was never a party. The Court is also asked whether the law letting the agency seek injunctions in such cases allows a court to rewrite a nonparty's private contracts and dispose of that nonparty's property.

## Where it stands

The petition was distributed for the Court's conference of December 4, 2026. The docket shows no action on it since that distribution.

## What each outcome would mean

Boddie is asking the Court to take up and reverse the result below. If the Court grants the petition, it would hear the case fully and decide whether the property disposition and the bar on her appeal violated due process; granting does not itself decide who is right. If the Court denies the petition, the Fourth Circuit's dismissal of Boddie's appeal stands, leaving the property sale and redress-plan orders in place without further review by the Court.
