## In brief

Beckie Boddie asks the Court to review a lower-court ruling that she could not challenge, as a nonparty, a redress plan affecting property she says is hers.

## What happened

This case arises from a long-running Federal Trade Commission enforcement action over the Sanctuary Belize real estate development, which led a federal district court to appoint a receiver and establish a redress plan for consumers. Boddie, trustee of a family trust that owns lots in the development, was never named as a party to that case. She tried repeatedly to file motions objecting to orders affecting her property, but the district court struck or returned her filings, saying she was not a party and could not seek relief. The Fourth Circuit affirmed the denial of her bid to intervene and dismissed the rest of her appeal, holding she lacked standing to appeal as a nonparty who had not participated below.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked whether it violates constitutional property-protection guarantees for a federal court to dispose of a nonparty owner's real property without notice or a hearing, while also blocking that owner from appealing on the ground that she was never a party. The petition also asks whether the law letting the agency seek only injunctions allows a court to rewrite a nonparty's private contracts and dispose of her property.

## Where it stands

The petition for certiorari was filed and docketed, with a response from the government due October 28, 2026. The docket shows no further action since filing.

## What each outcome would mean

Boddie, the petitioner, is asking the Court to hear her case. If the Court grants the petition, it would hear full arguments and decide whether her property rights were violated and whether the law was properly applied; granting review decides nothing by itself about who prevails. If the Court denies the petition, the Fourth Circuit's ruling stands, leaving in place the conclusion that Boddie could not appeal the orders affecting her property because she was not a party to the case below.
