## In brief

RiseandShine sued PepsiCo over the "RISE" trademark, and the Supreme Court is deciding whether a mark's inherent strength is a factual or legal question.

## What happened

RiseandShine, which sells canned nitro cold-brew coffee under "RISE" marks, sued PepsiCo in 2021 claiming its "MTN DEW RISE ENERGY" drink infringed RiseandShine's trademark. A district court granted RiseandShine a preliminary injunction, but the Second Circuit vacated it, holding that RiseandShine's mark was inherently weak as a matter of law given its close association with coffee. On remand, the district court granted PepsiCo summary judgment, and the Second Circuit affirmed, again treating the mark's "inherent strength" as a legal question for judges rather than a factual question for juries.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked whether, in deciding if a trademark infringement is likely to confuse consumers, the question of how distinctive or strong a trademark inherently is should be treated as a factual matter normally decided by a jury, or as a legal matter decided by judges. RiseandShine says every other appeals court treats this as a factual question while only the Second Circuit treats it as legal; PepsiCo says the inquiry has an inescapable legal component tied to defining the scope of trademark protection.

## Where it stands

The Court granted certiorari, received merits briefing and multiple amicus briefs including one from the federal government supporting vacatur, and heard the case after it was set for argument on January 12, 2027. The case awaits the Court's decision following briefing completed in November 2026.

## What each outcome would mean

RiseandShine, the side seeking to overturn the lower court's ruling, argues the Second Circuit wrongly treated trademark strength as a legal question, which led courts to rule against it without letting a jury weigh the evidence. If the Court rules for RiseandShine, finding this a factual question, the case would likely be sent back for further proceedings where a jury could reconsider the mark's strength. If the Court sides with PepsiCo and affirms, the Second Circuit's judgment granting PepsiCo summary judgment would stand, meaning RiseandShine's infringement claims against PepsiCo would remain rejected. A vacatur, which the federal government's brief supported, would set aside the lower court's decision and send the case back without resolving the issue for either side.
