## In brief

Rise Brewing asks the Court to decide whether a trademark's inherent strength is a factual question for juries rather than a legal one for judges.

## What happened

Rise Brewing sells canned coffee under the RISE mark and sued PepsiCo, claiming its MTN DEW RISE ENERGY drink infringed that mark. A district court initially granted Rise Brewing a preliminary injunction, but the Second Circuit vacated it, holding that the RISE mark's "inherent strength"—part of the test for likely confusion between marks—was weak as a matter of law. On remand, the district court granted PepsiCo summary judgment, and the Second Circuit affirmed in an unpublished order, again treating inherent strength as a legal question. Rise Brewing says twelve other circuits treat that question as factual.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked whether, in deciding if a trademark's use by someone else is likely to confuse consumers, a court evaluating how distinctive the trademark inherently is must treat that as a factual issue for a jury, rather than a legal issue a judge can decide alone.

## Where it stands

The Court granted certiorari on June 29, 2026, after inviting and receiving the views of the federal government, which recommended denying review. The case has since been briefed on the merits and is awaiting oral argument or decision.

## What each outcome would mean

Rise Brewing, which lost in the lower courts, is asking the Court to reverse the Second Circuit's decision. If the Court affirms, PepsiCo's win below stands, and courts may continue to treat a mark's inherent strength as a legal question in the Second Circuit while other circuits treat it as factual. If the Court reverses, the case would likely return to the lower courts with instructions to treat inherent strength as a factual question, potentially allowing a jury to reconsider Rise Brewing's infringement claim. If the Court vacates, it would set aside the Second Circuit's decision and send the case back without deciding which side is right.
