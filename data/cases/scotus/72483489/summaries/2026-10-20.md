## In brief

RiseandShine's trademark suit against PepsiCo reached the Supreme Court over whether a trademark's inherent strength is decided by a judge or a jury.

## What happened

RiseandShine, which sells canned coffee under the "RISE" mark, sued PepsiCo in 2021 after PepsiCo launched an energy drink called MTN DEW RISE ENERGY. A district court granted RiseandShine a preliminary injunction, but the Second Circuit vacated it, ruling that RiseandShine's mark was inherently weak as a matter of law. On remand, the district court granted summary judgment to PepsiCo, and the Second Circuit affirmed, again treating the mark's inherent strength as a legal question for judges rather than a factual question for juries. RiseandShine asked the Supreme Court to review that ruling.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked whether, in deciding if a trademark infringement is likely to confuse consumers, a trademark's inherent strength (how distinctive it is) should be treated as a factual question for a jury to decide, or as a legal question judges can decide themselves, as the Second Circuit held and as most other appeals courts do not.

## Where it stands

The Court granted the petition on June 29, 2026, after the Solicitor General, invited to weigh in, recommended denying review. The case has since been briefed on the merits, with the petitioner's brief filed September 8, 2026, and respondent's brief due November 4, 2026; the case has not yet been argued or decided.

## What each outcome would mean

RiseandShine petitioned for review after losing in the lower courts, which ruled for PepsiCo. If the Court affirms, PepsiCo's win stands, and a trademark's inherent strength will continue to be treated as a legal question for judges, at least in the Second Circuit. If the Court reverses, it would side with RiseandShine's view that juries should decide inherent strength, likely sending the case back for further proceedings under that standard. If the Court vacates, it would set aside the Second Circuit's decision and send the case back without deciding the underlying dispute for either side.
