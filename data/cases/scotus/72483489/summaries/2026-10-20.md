## In brief

RiseandShine asks the Court to decide whether a trademark's inherent strength is a factual question for juries, after losing its infringement suit against PepsiCo.

## What happened

RiseandShine, which sells canned coffee under the "RISE" mark, sued PepsiCo for trademark infringement after PepsiCo launched "MTN DEW RISE ENERGY." A district court granted RiseandShine a preliminary injunction, but the Second Circuit vacated it, holding that the RISE mark's inherent strength was weak "as a matter of law." On remand, the district court granted PepsiCo summary judgment, and the Second Circuit affirmed, again treating the mark's inherent strength as a legal question rather than a factual one. RiseandShine says twelve other circuits treat this question as factual, to be decided by a jury.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked whether, in deciding if a trademark infringement is likely to confuse consumers, the strength of the senior company's mark should be treated as a factual question for a jury to decide, rather than a legal question decided by a judge.

## Where it stands

The Court granted certiorari after inviting and receiving the views of the federal government, which argued the question involves mostly factual work but recommended against review. The case has been fully briefed and awaits oral argument and decision.

## What each outcome would mean

RiseandShine, which lost in the lower courts, is asking the Court to reverse the Second Circuit's decision. If the Court rules that trademark strength is a factual question, RiseandShine's case could return to the lower courts, where a jury might reconsider the strength of its mark and whether PepsiCo's product caused confusion. If the Court rules that the question is legal, or otherwise affirms, PepsiCo's win below would stand, leaving the summary judgment against RiseandShine in place. The Court could also vacate the decision and send the case back without resolving the question for either side.
