## In brief

Rise Brewing's trademark dispute with PepsiCo over the "Rise" name reached the Supreme Court, which agreed to hear the case and is now awaiting merits briefing.

## What happened

Rise Brewing, which sells canned nitro-brewed coffee under "Rise" trademarks, sued PepsiCo, claiming PepsiCo's "Mtn Dew Rise Energy" drink infringed its marks. A district court initially granted Rise Brewing a preliminary injunction, but the Second Circuit vacated it, finding the "Rise" mark inherently weak and the two products' packaging dissimilar. After further proceedings, the district court granted PepsiCo summary judgment, and the Second Circuit affirmed in an unpublished order. Rise Brewing asked the Supreme Court to review how courts decide a trademark's strength in weighing likelihood of confusion.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked whether a trademark's strength, meaning how distinctive it is, should be decided by a jury as a factual matter, or whether a judge may decide it as a legal question. Rise Brewing says most appeals courts treat this as a factual issue while the appeals court here alone treats it as legal; PepsiCo disputes that any such division among courts actually exists.

## Where it stands

The Court granted the petition on June 29, 2026, after asking the Solicitor General for the government's views and receiving supplemental briefing from both sides and an amicus. The case now awaits merits briefing, with deadlines extended to August 31, 2026, for Rise Brewing's brief and October 19, 2026, for PepsiCo's.

## What each outcome would mean

Rise Brewing, which lost below, asked the Court to take the case. If the Court rules for Rise Brewing, it would hold that trademark strength is a factual question, potentially reviving its infringement claim and sending the case back for further proceedings. If the Court rules for PepsiCo, the current approach treating strength as a legal question would stand, and PepsiCo's win below would likely remain in place. If the Court vacates the lower court's decision, it would send the case back without deciding who is right.
