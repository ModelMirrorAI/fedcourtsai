## In brief

Rise Brewing asked the Court to decide whether a trademark's strength, in assessing confusion with another mark, is a factual or legal question; the Court granted review.

## What happened

Rise Brewing, maker of canned coffee drinks sold under "RISE" marks, sued PepsiCo, claiming its "MTN DEW RISE ENERGY" energy drink infringed those marks. Rise Brewing won a preliminary injunction, but the Second Circuit vacated it, finding Rise Brewing's mark conceptually weak as a matter of law. On remand, the district court granted PepsiCo summary judgment, and the Second Circuit affirmed in an unpublished order, again treating the mark's strength as a legal question rather than a factual one for a jury. Rise Brewing asked the Supreme Court to review that approach.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked whether a trademark's strength - how distinctive it is, which affects whether consumers would confuse it with another company's mark - should be treated as a factual question, normally decided by a jury, or as a legal question a judge can decide, including on summary judgment without a trial.

## Where it stands

The Court invited the Solicitor General to weigh in, received that brief, and granted the petition in June 2026. The case was briefed on the merits over the summer, with the government and several groups filing briefs, and awaits argument and decision.

## What each outcome would mean

Rise Brewing, which lost in the lower courts, asked the Court to take up the case. If the Court rules that trademark strength is a factual question, Rise Brewing's case would likely return to the lower courts for further proceedings, potentially before a jury. If the Court rules that courts may treat it as a legal question, PepsiCo's win below would stand. The Court could also send the case back without deciding the question either way, leaving it for further proceedings.
