## In brief

RiseandShine asks the Court to decide whether a trademark's inherent strength is a factual or legal question in trademark infringement cases.

## What happened

RiseandShine, which sells canned coffee under "RISE" marks, sued PepsiCo for trademark infringement over PepsiCo's "MTN DEW RISE ENERGY" drink. A district court granted RiseandShine a preliminary injunction, finding the RISE mark's strength favored RiseandShine. The Second Circuit vacated that injunction, holding that the mark's "inherent strength"—one part of how courts assess a mark's strength—was weak as a matter of law given its close association with coffee. On remand, the district court, treating that ruling as binding, granted PepsiCo summary judgment. The Second Circuit affirmed, again stating that inherent strength is a legal question for courts, not juries, to decide.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked whether a trademark's inherent strength—how strongly a mark's own meaning or character points to a particular source, independent of how well-known it has become in the marketplace—should be treated as a factual matter normally decided by a jury, or as a legal matter decided by a judge, when courts assess whether a competitor's use of a similar mark is likely to confuse consumers.

## Where it stands

The Court granted certiorari on June 29, 2026, after inviting and receiving the views of the Solicitor General, who recommended denying review. The case has been fully briefed and argued; it was set for argument on January 12, 2027, and the record has been received from the court of appeals, so the case now awaits decision.

## What each outcome would mean

RiseandShine, which lost below, is asking the Court to reverse the Second Circuit's judgment. If the Court rules that inherent strength is a factual question, it would set aside the Second Circuit's approach and could send the case back for further proceedings, potentially allowing a jury to weigh in on the mark's strength, though PepsiCo argues other unchallenged factors would still support judgment for PepsiCo. If the Court affirms, the Second Circuit's treatment of inherent strength as a legal question—and its judgment for PepsiCo—would stand. The Court could also vacate and remand without resolving the question for either side.
