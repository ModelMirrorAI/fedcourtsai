## In brief

Rise Brewing asks the Court to decide whether a trademark's inherent strength is a factual question for juries, after the Second Circuit treated it as a legal one.

## What happened

Rise Brewing, which sells canned coffee under the "RISE" mark, sued PepsiCo over its "MTN DEW RISE ENERGY" energy drink, claiming trademark infringement. A district court initially granted Rise Brewing a preliminary injunction, but the Second Circuit vacated it, holding that Rise Brewing's mark was inherently weak as a matter of law. On remand, the district court granted PepsiCo summary judgment, and the Second Circuit affirmed in an unpublished order, again treating the mark's inherent strength as a legal question rather than a factual one. Rise Brewing says twelve other circuits treat this question as factual.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked whether, in deciding if a trademark infringer's use of a similar mark is likely to confuse consumers, a trademark's inherent strength—how distinctive it is—should be decided by a jury as a factual matter, or whether a judge may decide it as a matter of law.

## Where it stands

The Court granted the petition, and the case has been briefed and argued; it was set for argument on January 12, 2027, and now awaits decision. The Solicitor General, invited to weigh in, told the Court the Second Circuit erred but recommended against taking the case.

## What each outcome would mean

Rise Brewing, which lost in the lower courts, is asking the Court to reverse the Second Circuit's decision. If the Court reverses, it would hold that trademark strength is a factual question, likely sending the case back for further proceedings where a jury could consider the issue. If the Court affirms, PepsiCo's win below would stand, and the Second Circuit's approach of treating this question as legal would continue. If the Court vacates, it would set the lower court's decision aside and return the case without deciding the issue for either side.
