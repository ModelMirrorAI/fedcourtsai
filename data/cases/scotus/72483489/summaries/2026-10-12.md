## In brief

RiseandShine asks the Court to decide whether a trademark's inherent strength is a factual question for a jury or a legal question for a judge.

## What happened

RiseandShine, which sells canned coffee under the "RISE" mark, sued PepsiCo over its "MTN DEW RISE ENERGY" energy drink, claiming trademark infringement. A district court granted RiseandShine a preliminary injunction, but the Second Circuit vacated it, holding that RiseandShine's mark was inherently weak as a matter of law. On remand, the district court granted PepsiCo summary judgment, and the Second Circuit affirmed, again treating the mark's inherent strength as a legal question. RiseandShine says twelve other circuits treat that question as factual.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked whether, in deciding if a trademark infringement is likely to confuse consumers, a dispute over how strong and distinctive a mark is should be decided by a judge as a matter of law or submitted to a jury as a question of fact.

## Where it stands

The Court granted the petition on June 29, 2026, after inviting and receiving the views of the Solicitor General, who recommended denial. The case was argued on the merits, with briefing completed by both sides and several amici, and the case now awaits decision.

## What each outcome would mean

RiseandShine petitioned for review after losing below. If the Court affirms, PepsiCo's win in the lower courts stands, and the mark's inherent strength continues to be treated as a legal question in the Second Circuit, leaving RiseandShine's infringement claim rejected. If the Court reverses, it would hold that inherent strength is a factual question, likely sending the case back for further proceedings where a jury could consider the issue. If the Court vacates, it would set the Second Circuit's decision aside and send the case back without deciding the question for either side.
