# Forecast of the Court's judgment and reasoning

## Judgment and lineup

The Court will **vacate the Second Circuit's judgment and remand**, with all nine participating Justices joining the judgment and the Court's reasoning. The expected lineup is Roberts, Thomas, Alito, Sotomayor, Kagan, Gorsuch, Kavanaugh, Barrett, and Jackson in the majority. The forecast probability that the judgment is disturbed by vacatur, reversal, or a mixed judgment is **76%**; vacatur is the modal particular judgment, not the entirety of that probability.

Sotomayor is the leading candidate to deliver the opinion, extending the rationale of her opinion in *Hana Financial*. Kagan is the next candidate because the law/fact framework of her *Lakeridge* opinion supplies another natural route. No separate writing is the modal call. If there is one, Thomas is the leading candidate, stressing the boundary between legal standards and factual applications. Alito is a close alternative, particularly on whether the existing record already justifies judgment for PepsiCo. These possibilities are represented by independent writing probabilities, not by a predicted dissent in the modal lineup.

## Majority ground

Applying *Hana Financial*'s consumer-perception rationale, the majority will hold that a particular trademark's inherent strength is a **predominantly factual mixed question**, rejecting the Second Circuit's treatment of it as a pure question of law. The legal spectrum and the instructions defining strength remain for judges; how a particular designation functions in the minds of the relevant consumers ordinarily calls for factual assessment.

The opinion will reject PepsiCo's attempt to make that assessment equivalent to construing a patent's legal boundaries. It will not need to accept Rise's stronger suggestion that every aspect of inherent strength is exhausted by assigning the mark to one distinctiveness category. Nor will it rest its holding on a new constitutional rule about jury trials.

## Breadth and remedy

The Court will announce a rule for **inherent-strength determinations generally**, not a special rule about RISE or caffeinated beverages. It will preserve summary judgment when no reasonable factfinder could disagree and leave unresolved the classification of the ultimate likelihood-of-confusion determination. This is a categorical allocation rule for the subfactor, not a requirement that all trademark cases go to juries.

On remedy, the Court will leave PepsiCo's other summary-judgment grounds for the lower court to reconsider. Its opinion will not hold that RISE is necessarily a strong mark, that the two products are confusingly similar, or that Rise must receive a trial. The Second Circuit's reliance on the supposed legal weakness of RISE will make remand preferable to deciding factual sufficiency in the first instance.

The principal alternative is an affirmance that corrects the legal characterization but treats the error as immaterial in this record. A DIG is a small secondary possibility because the disputed subfactor sits inside a larger, partly unchallenged multifactor judgment. No recusal appears in the baseline, so an equally divided disposition is only a remote contingency.
