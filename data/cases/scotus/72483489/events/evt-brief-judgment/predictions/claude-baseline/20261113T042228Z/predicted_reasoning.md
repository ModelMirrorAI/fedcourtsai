# Forecast: RiseandShine Corp. v. PepsiCo, Inc., No. 24-1016 (judgment)

**Judgment class.** The Court will **vacate** the Second Circuit's judgment and remand. It will hold that the Second Circuit erred in treating a trademark's inherent strength as a pure question of law, and it will send the case back rather than decide whether summary judgment for PepsiCo survives on other grounds. A plain "reversed and remanded" is the next most likely label (the petitioner asks for it); an affirmance is the main undisturbed outcome; a DIG is a live but small tail.

**Vote lineup.** Unanimous or nearly so on the judgment: 9–0, with at most one or two Justices writing separately. I expect no dissent on the core holding. If there is separate writing, the likeliest sources are Thomas or Gorsuch (on the jury's role / Seventh Amendment history, or on whether the whole likelihood-of-confusion inquiry is factual), or Jackson or Barrett (on how to classify mixed questions).

**Ground.** The majority will reason that inherent strength asks where a mark sits on the distinctiveness spectrum as ordinary consumers perceive it, and that such consumer-perception questions are for the factfinder under *Hana Financial v. Hana Bank* (2015), reinforced by *Booking.com* (2020) and the *U.S. Bank v. Village at Lakeridge* test for mixed questions. It will reject PepsiCo's argument that inherent strength is a distinct, policy-laden "scope of protection" question akin to patent claim construction, though it may acknowledge that the governing legal framework (the Abercrombie categories) is for the court.

**Breadth.** Narrow. The opinion will confine itself to the inherent-strength sub-factor, stress that summary judgment remains available when no reasonable factfinder could find for the non-movant, decline to resolve whether the ultimate likelihood-of-confusion determination is legal or factual (the petitioner did not seek review of that holding), and leave PepsiCo's alternative grounds — dissimilarity of the marks and the other Polaroid factors — to the Second Circuit on remand.

**Procedural exits.** A DIG is conceivable because the Solicitor General at the cert stage called the Second Circuit's rule ambiguous and the vehicle poor, and because PepsiCo now argues the real dispute is what "inherent strength" includes; I put it under 5%. No recusal is indicated, so an equally divided Court is not live.

**Who writes.** Argument is set for January 12, 2027. The opinion is the kind of short, low-conflict intellectual-property opinion that most often goes to Kagan, Sotomayor (author of *Hana Financial*), Jackson or Thomas; I spread the author distribution accordingly and expect few separate writings. Decision likely by spring 2027, well before the end of June.
