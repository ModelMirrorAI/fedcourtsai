# Forecast

The Court will vacate the Second Circuit's judgment and remand. It will hold that a mark's inherent or conceptual strength is a predominantly factual mixed question because it turns on the source-identifying impression the mark makes on ordinary consumers. The Second Circuit therefore could not treat the weakness of RISE as a pure legal conclusion subject to conclusive de novo determination.

The opinion will be narrow. It will preserve summary judgment where the evidence permits only one reasonable finding and will not establish a uniform likelihood-of-confusion test, decide how acquired strength should be weighted in reverse-confusion cases, or itself decide whether PepsiCo infringed. The lower courts will remain free on remand to consider the dissimilarity of the marks, RiseandShine's prior descriptions of RISE as weak, third-party uses, survey evidence, and the other Polaroid factors under the corrected fact-law framework.

All nine Justices will join the judgment. The consumer-perception principle from *Hana Financial* supplies a nonideological basis for agreement, while the narrow Rule 56 limitation answers the concern that labeling inherent strength fact-sensitive necessarily requires a trial in every case. No procedural exit or equal division will occur.
