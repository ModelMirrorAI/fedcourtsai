# Forecast: what the Court will do with the judgment below

**Judgment class: `vacated`.** The Court will hold that trademark strength —
conceptual strength included — is a question of fact in the likelihood-of-confusion
analysis under 15 U.S.C. § 1114, and will vacate the Second Circuit's judgment and
remand for that court to reconsider the summary-judgment affirmance under the
correct characterization. It will not itself decide whether summary judgment for
PepsiCo survives clear-error/factual treatment of the RISE marks' strength; that
application goes back down. A straight `reversed` is the main alternative label if
the opinion concludes the strength dispute is genuine on this record, but the
vacate-and-remand form is the Court's usual disposition when the error below is a
mischaracterization of the standard (Teva v. Sandoz, Highmark, Dewberry), so I
put vacated well ahead of reversed.

**Vote lineup: unanimous, 9–0.** The question is a clean characterization issue on
which twelve circuits already agree, the Solicitor General told the Court the
Second Circuit "mischaracterized the inquiry," and the Court's modern trademark
decisions run near-unanimous (Hana Financial 9–0, Booking.com 8–1, Jack Daniel's
9–0, Abitron 9–0, Dewberry 9–0). I forecast all nine Justices in the majority,
with the most plausible fracture being a concurrence (not a dissent) emphasizing
that summary judgment remains freely available where no reasonable jury could find
a mark strong — a point PepsiCo's BIO presses and the Court will likely
accommodate in dicta rather than lose a vote over. An outright dissent defending
the Second Circuit's law-treatment is unlikely from any Justice; if one appears,
Alito (the Booking.com dissenter's genericness-skeptic seat is closest in spirit)
is the least-unlikely candidate, but I do not predict it.

**Ground.** Hana Financial supplies the rule: an inquiry that "relies upon an
ordinary consumer's understanding of the impression that a mark conveys falls
comfortably within the ken of a jury." Booking.com's consumer-meaning framing of a
mark's character reinforces it. The Court will reject the BIO's decomposition —
that only "classification" is disputed while a further "meaning of the word" step
is legal — as re-labeling the same consumer-perception assessment. Expect the
opinion to analogize to the fact-treatment of tacking (Hana) and to Fed. R. Civ.
P. 52(a)'s allocation, and to say expressly that this does not make summary
judgment unavailable where the factual record permits only one answer.

**Breadth.** Categorical, trans-substantive: strength is factual in every § 1114
confusion case, in every circuit — not a case-specific ruling about the RISE
marks. The reverse-confusion wrinkle the BIO raises (how strength cuts when the
junior user swamps the senior) will be noted and left open, not resolved.

**Procedural exits.** A DIG is live but unlikely (~4–5%): the Solicitor General
recommended denial on vehicle grounds — outcome-independence, unpreserved
Booking.com/Hana arguments — and the Court granted anyway after both sides filed
supplemental briefs addressing exactly those objections, so the vehicle problems
were priced in at the grant. An affirmance on the alternative ground that the
strength dispute is immaterial on this record (~15%) is the more serious
undisturbed path than a holding that the Second Circuit's law-treatment is
correct, which twelve circuits and the United States reject. An equally divided
affirmance is negligible absent a recusal, and none is apparent.

**Timing.** Merits briefing was just extended (petitioner's brief due August 31,
2026; respondent's October 19, 2026), so argument lands in the winter sitting
(likely December 2026 or January 2027) and the decision by the end of OT2026, i.e.
by late June 2027. Unscored, but it disciplines the rest: a spring argument slot
leaves room for a full signed opinion, and the case's low doctrinal heat makes a
quick, short, lopsided opinion the modal shape.
