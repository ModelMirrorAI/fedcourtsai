The Court will vacate or reverse the Second Circuit's decision and hold that trademark strength is a question of fact for the jury (or fact-finder). A 12-1 circuit split currently exists, with only the Second Circuit treating it as a question of law. The Court's jurisprudence strongly emphasizes that issues of consumer confusion and mark distinctiveness are intensely factual and should not be treated as purely legal questions. Consequently, the Court will likely establish a categorical procedural rule applying to all likelihood-of-confusion cases under 15 U.S.C. 1114, aligning the Second Circuit with the rest of the country. Expect a unanimous or near-unanimous opinion, with no significant dissent on the core question of fact versus law.
