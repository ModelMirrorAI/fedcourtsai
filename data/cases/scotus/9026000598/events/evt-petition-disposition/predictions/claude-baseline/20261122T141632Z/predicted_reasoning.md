# Forecast: No. 26-598, Meidinger v. Commissioner of Internal Revenue

The Court will deny the petition, most likely on the order list following the December 4, 2026 conference, without comment.

- **Relists.** The docket shows one distribution (for the conference of 12/4/2026) and no relist. I expect no further distribution: the petition will be denied at its first conference. A further distribution would arise only if a Justice called for a response from the Solicitor General, who has waived; I put that at about 3%.
- **CVSG.** None. The United States is the respondent, so the Court would call for a response rather than invite the Solicitor General's views; a CVSG is effectively excluded.
- **Question presented.** If the Court took anything, it would be the first question only, reformulated as whether the Tax Court has jurisdiction under 26 U.S.C. § 7623(b)(4) to review a whistleblower award denial when the IRS referred the claim to an operating division but took no action against a taxpayer. Questions 2 to 4 (APA record review, agency inaction, evidentiary support for jurisdictional dismissals) would be left behind.
- **Summary disposition.** No intervening decision of the Court bears on the question, so a GVR has no present hook. Conditional on the unlikely event of a grant, a cert-order disposition (for instance a GVR after legislation or a change in the government's position) is about as likely as plenary review.
- **Separate writing.** None expected on denial. No Justice has signalled interest in the § 7623(b)(4) jurisdictional line.
