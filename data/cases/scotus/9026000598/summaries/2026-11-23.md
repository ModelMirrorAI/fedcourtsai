## In brief

A taxpayer whose IRS whistleblower claim was rejected asks the Court to revive his case after lower courts found no authority to review it.

## What happened

Roy Meidinger filed a whistleblower claim with the IRS, alleging that healthcare providers improperly avoid taxes on discounts given to insurers. The IRS Whistleblower Office rejected his claim, saying it decided not to pursue his information. The Tax Court dismissed his case for lack of jurisdiction, relying on a prior D.C. Circuit decision holding that threshold rejections of whistleblower claims cannot be reviewed by courts. The D.C. Circuit affirmed, consolidating Meidinger's appeal with a similar case, and later denied rehearing, including rehearing by the full court.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked whether a final IRS decision turning away a whistleblower's claim, on the ground that the agency collected no money from the targeted taxpayer, can still be reviewed by the Tax Court. It is also asked whether the Tax Court must examine the agency's underlying records before dismissing such a claim for lack of authority, and whether an agency's choice not to act can block a whistleblower's statutory right to appeal.

## Where it stands

The respondent waived the right to file a response. The petition was distributed for the Court's December 4, 2026 conference, and the docket shows no action on it since.

## What each outcome would mean

Meidinger, who lost in the lower courts, is asking the Court to take up his case. If the Court grants the petition, it would hear the case and decide whether his claim can be reviewed, deciding nothing yet about who is right. If it denies the petition, the D.C. Circuit's ruling stands, leaving Meidinger's whistleblower claim dismissed and upholding the rule that such agency rejections are not reviewable by the Tax Court in these circumstances.
