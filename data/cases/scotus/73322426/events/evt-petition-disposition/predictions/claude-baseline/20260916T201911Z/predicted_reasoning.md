# Forecast: Johnson v. Montana, No. 25-1273 (petition for certiorari)

**Bottom line.** The petition will most likely be denied, but it is a live
candidate for a grant, not a routine denial: I put the chance of any grant at
about 15%. If granted, the case would be set for plenary briefing and argument.

## Distributions and relists

The docket shows **one distribution** so far: on June 24, 2026 the petition was
distributed for the Conference of September 28, 2026. Montana had waived a
response; on July 6 the Court **requested a response**, which took the petition
off that conference until the brief in opposition (filed September 4) and the
reply (submitted September 15) were in. From that state I forecast:

- **A further distribution is near-certain.** With the reply in, the petition
  will be redistributed — for the September 28 long conference if the Clerk
  processes it in time, otherwise for the October 9 or October 16 conference.
- **At least one relist after first consideration is more likely than not**
  (roughly 55%). A call for a response means at least one chamber wanted to
  look closely; the reply is credible on preservation, and the Court will
  probably take a second conference before acting. I do not expect a long
  string of relists: two to three total distributions, with a disposition by
  early November 2026.

## Call for the views of the Solicitor General

No CVSG is on the docket and I do not expect one (about 4%). This is a state
prosecution with no federal party. The federal government does have a stake in
the standard for remote testimony in federal trials, which is why a CVSG is not
impossible, but the Court has historically resolved the Confrontation Clause
standard without inviting the SG at the petition stage in state cases.

## Which question the Court would take

If it grants, the Court would take the question presented essentially **as
written** — which standard governs a prosecution witness's testimony by two-way
video — and would most likely frame it as whether Maryland v. Craig's
necessity-plus-reliability test or Crawford v. Washington's
unavailability-plus-prior-cross-examination rule applies to an available adult
witness. It would leave the harmless-error question to the Montana courts on
remand, as the reply invites.

## Summary disposition

A summary disposition is unlikely conditional on a grant (about 5%). There is no
intervening decision to GVR in light of — Pitts v. Mississippi (2025) applied
Craig rather than unsettling it — and the Court would not overrule or cabin a
36-year-old precedent in a per curiam order. Any grant is a plenary grant.

## If the Court denies

If the petition is denied, I expect the order to be silent about 85% of the
time. The residual 15% is a **statement respecting the denial or a dissent from
denial** — the Wrotten v. New York (2010) pattern, where Justice Sotomayor
flagged the same question as important and open but agreed the vehicle was
poor. The most likely authors of such a writing are Justice Gorsuch, whose
Confrontation Clause originalism makes Craig's reliability balancing a natural
target, and Justice Sotomayor, given Wrotten; Justice Thomas is a possible
joiner. A separate writing would most likely cite the preservation and
harmless-error problems as the reason to pass on this vehicle while noting the
question deserves resolution in a cleaner case.

## If granted (conditional)

If the Court grants and reaches the merits, the likeliest outcome is a
vacatur: a holding that Craig does not extend to an available adult witness
testifying by two-way video for reasons of convenience, with remand for
harmless-error review. A holding that Crawford wholesale displaces Craig is
less likely than a narrower limit on Craig to its child-witness, necessity
facts.
