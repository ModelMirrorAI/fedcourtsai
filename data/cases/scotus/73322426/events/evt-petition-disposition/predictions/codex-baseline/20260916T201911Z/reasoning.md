## Why 25%, rather than the anchor or a likely grant

The main upward adjustment is substantive. The petition, pp. 8–18, identifies a concrete disagreement between courts applying Craig to adult video testimony and the Michigan and Missouri high courts limiting Craig and applying Crawford. Montana's opposition, pp. 13–15, disputes the split's importance but acknowledges those two contrary state decisions. The split is more substantial than a mere disagreement among individual judges. The question concerns repeatable trial procedure, and the petition presents a final state judgment with a developed federal issue. The Court's requested response adds a separate, modest positive signal; it is not itself a relist or a CVSG.

Counterweights remain substantial. The opposition, pp. 5–10, contests preservation, argues invited error, and identifies the defendant's and an officer's testimony as grounds for harmlessness. The reply, pp. 1–6, gives meaningful answers: the federal confrontation claim was raised throughout; the state supreme court addressed the governing standard; no forfeiture holding supplied an independent ground; and harmlessness was not decided below. I therefore do not treat these objections as established jurisdictional defects. But the parties' dispute about whether the legal ruling would affect the conviction makes the vehicle less automatic than the petition portrays. I did not comprehensively adjudicate the appended trial transcript, and discount confidence accordingly.

Pitts v. Mississippi, No. 24-1159 (November 24, 2025), independently read through CourtListener, is relevant in both directions. Its per curiam opinion enforced case-specific necessity under Coy and Craig, then left harmlessness for remand. That supports continued attention to physical confrontation and shows harmless-error questions do not necessarily prevent review. But it applied Craig rather than displacing it. Importantly, Pitts involved a **physical courtroom screen shielding a child**, not adult two-way video; the reply's description as one-way video is not the opinion's factual account. Pitts does not decide Johnson's question and predates the judgment below, so I do not infer an automatic intervening-decision GVR.
