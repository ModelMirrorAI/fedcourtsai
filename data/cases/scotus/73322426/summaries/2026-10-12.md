## In brief

A man convicted in Montana asks the Court to decide what legal test governs whether a witness may testify by two-way video instead of in person at a criminal trial.

## What happened

Stephen Joseph Johnson was convicted in Montana of criminal mischief, burglary, and bail jumping after a car crash and a confrontation in a homeowner's garage. At trial, the homeowner, Eugene Maw, testified against Johnson by two-way video rather than in person, over Johnson's objection, because Maw lived far away and had caretaking responsibilities. The Montana Supreme Court affirmed the conviction, holding that a 1990 Supreme Court case setting a two-part public-policy-and-reliability test, rather than a later 2004 case requiring that a witness be unavailable, governs whether such video testimony violates the right to confront witnesses. Johnson now asks the Supreme Court to review that ruling.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked which legal test applies when the prosecution wants a witness to testify over live video instead of appearing physically in court: a test asking whether video testimony serves an important public policy and is otherwise reliable, or a stricter test requiring that the witness be unavailable and that the defendant already had a chance to cross-examine them.

## Where it stands

The petition has been distributed for conference twice, most recently for the conference of October 9, 2026, after Montana filed a brief opposing review and Johnson filed a reply. The docket does not yet show any ruling on the petition.

## What each outcome would mean

Johnson, who lost below, is asking the Court to take up his case. If the Court grants review, it would hear full arguments and decide which standard applies, without yet deciding who wins; the case could then return to the Montana courts to apply that standard. If the Court denies review, the Montana Supreme Court's decision affirming Johnson's conviction stands, and the video-testimony standard it applied remains in place for Montana courts.
