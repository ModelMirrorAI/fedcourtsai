## 2. Downward Adjustments from the `0.350`–`0.385` Baseline

Four case-specific considerations push `P(grant)` meaningfully below the ~35–38.5% CVSG baseline:

1. **Unopposed foreign-sovereign-assets posture dilutes the CVSG signal.** Neither the Taliban nor DAB has ever appeared, so the Court had no Brief in Opposition defending a Second Circuit judgment governing $3.5 billion in central-bank reserves at the Federal Reserve Bank of New York (FRBNY). Calling for the Solicitor General's views was virtually an institutional prerequisite before taking any non-denial action, rather than a pure signal that four Justices already lean toward granting review over adversarial opposition.
2. **High likelihood of an SG recommendation to deny.** In historical CVSG practice, the ~36% grant rate blends ~75–85% grant odds when the SG recommends a grant with ~15–20% grant odds when the SG recommends denial. Here, the Department of Justice actively participated in SDNY and the Second Circuit *defending* Afghanistan's foreign-state status and opposing attachment/turnover of the remaining $3.5B in DAB reserves. Although an Administration transition intervened in January 2025 (and Iowa plus 30 States filed an amicus brief supporting petitioners in companion *Havlish*, No. 26-292), the Second Circuit's decision is a major Article II precedent affirming conclusive presidential recognition authority under *Zivotofsky v. Kerry*, 576 U.S. 1 (2015), while preserving Executive Branch diplomatic and IEEPA/OFAC leverage over $3.5B in blocked reserves. Institutional incentives strongly favor SG D. John Sauer recommending denial (~80%+ probability on No. 26-294).
3. **Absence of a circuit split and mismatch between the QP's deference framing and the opinion below.** Petitioners' Question Presented asks whether courts must defer to State Department websites and DOJ filings in interpreting the FSIA. Yet on Step 1 ("foreign state"), no circuit has ever held that a nation formally recognized by the President ceases to be a "foreign state" under the FSIA (*Ungar* and *Klinghoffer* involved unrecognized Palestine/PLO; *OI European Group* involved Venezuela where FSIA § 1610 exceptions applied and the Third Circuit honored Executive recognition of the Guaidó government). Notably, even Judge Menashi—one of the five Second Circuit en banc dissenters—agreed with the panel majority and Judge Nardini's concurrence that *Zivotofsky* forecloses judicial derecognition of Afghanistan (`170 F.4th` at 108–13). On Step 2 ("agency or instrumentality" under 28 U.S.C. § 1603(b)(2)), the panel *explicitly agreed* with petitioners in Footnote 97 (`152 F.4th` at 356 n.97) that courts cannot defer to the Executive, resting its holding instead on its own statutory reading of Afghanistan's 2003 Bank Law—an unusual, sui generis factual posture that an SG brief will highlight as poor cert fodder. Moreover, because *Owens* appeals a New York CPLR prejudgment attachment order, respondents/amicus or the SG can flag lurking state-law corporate-separateness hurdles under NY CPLR § 6202 even apart from the FSIA.
4. **Asymmetric vehicle competition with *Havlish* (No. 26-292) on TRIA § 201(a).** Unlike the 9/11 judgment creditors in *Havlish* and *Ashton*, the *Owens*/*Aliganga* plaintiffs lacked a judgment when they moved for attachment in March 2022 and therefore **do not raise Terrorism Risk Insurance Act (TRIA) § 201(a)** (`152 F.4th` at 351–57). The Second Circuit agreed that TRIA § 201(a) overrides FSIA central-bank immunity altogether, ruling against *Havlish*/*Ashton* solely on an unbriefed holding that TRIA instrumentality status is frozen on the August 15, 2021 blocking date. If four Justices (or the SG) wish to allow terrorism victims to reach the $3.5B FRBNY funds without disturbing *Zivotofsky* recognition or weakening FSIA central-bank immunity globally, granting *Havlish* (No. 26-292) limited to the narrow TRIA § 201(a) timing question is a far cleaner off-ramp. Under the cell rules, if *Owens* is held for a TRIA-only grant in *Havlish* and subsequently denied, `evt-order-cvsg-disposition` resolves as `denied`.

## 3. Countervailing Upward Forces Preventing a Lower Probability

Three substantial factors keep `P(grant)` at `0.25` rather than dropping toward 10–15%:

1. **$3.5 billion stakes and five en banc dissenters on § 1603(b)(2).** Unlike *Owens*'s Step 1 statehood argument, Step 2—whether a central bank captured and run by an unrecognized terrorist group remains an "organ" or state-owned instrumentality of the recognized State of Afghanistan under 28 U.S.C. § 1603(b)(2)—united **all five** Second Circuit en banc dissenters (Judge Sullivan, joined by Chief Judge Livingston and Judges Bianco and Park, and Judge Menashi separately), who argued the panel disregarded *Bartlett v. Baasiri*, 81 F.4th 28 (2d Cir. 2023), and relied on an inoperative 2003 Afghan statute. Combined with elite Supreme Court counsel (Matthew D. McGill of King & Spalding) and this Court's consistent appetite for major FSIA statutory disputes (*Halkbank*, *Hungary v. Simon*, *CC/Devas*, and *Exxon Mobil Corp. v. Corporación Cimex*, No. 24-699, granted after a CVSG and reversed June 23, 2026), the chance of a grant even over SG opposition is materially higher than in an ordinary CVSG.
2. **Justice Kagan's non-recusal makes *Owens* the sole 9-Justice vehicle on the FSIA question.** Crucially, `26-292.json` and `26-293.json` show that Justice Kagan is recused in both *Havlish* and *Ashton* due to prior government employment in the 9/11 MDL, whereas **all nine Justices participated** in *Owens* (`26-294`, filed as an independent 2022 action). If four Justices want to reach the FSIA question—or avoid the risk of a 4–4 tie in an 8-Justice *Havlish* merits proceeding—the Court has strong institutional reason to grant *Owens* either alongside *Havlish* or as the FSIA vehicle.
3. **Modest GVR tail (~2%).** If an intervening Executive Branch order/licensing shift alters the status of the blocked DAB funds while the CVSG is pending, or if the Court reaches the FSIA issue in a companion case while holding *Owens*, a GVR (`granted = 1`) adds ~2 percentage points on top of a ~23% plenary-grant probability (`0.23 + 0.02 = 0.25`).
