The docket currently records two conference distributions (October 21, 2026, for the November 6 conference, and November 9, 2026, for the November 13 conference) followed by the November 16, 2026 order inviting the Solicitor General to file a brief expressing the views of the United States across this petition and its two companions (*Havlish*, No. 26-292, and *Ashton*, No. 26-293). Because a CVSG order is already on the docket, no further initial CVSG order will issue.

Once the Solicitor General files the invited brief—likely in spring 2027 (ahead of the late-May cutoff) or late summer 2027 (for the Long Conference)—and petitioners file their supplemental brief in response, the petition is virtually certain to be distributed at least once more for conference unless withdrawn beforehand under Rule 46. One or two post-CVSG distributions are most likely, as the Court will consider *Owens* alongside *Havlish* (No. 26-292) and *Ashton* (No. 26-293) and weigh vehicle selection across the three dockets.

Following receipt of the Solicitor General's brief, the likeliest outcome is a denial of certiorari. The Solicitor General is expected to recommend denial, defending the Second Circuit's holding that continued Executive recognition of the State of Afghanistan establishes foreign-state status under the Foreign Sovereign Immunities Act (FSIA) and urging preservation of Executive control over the $3.5 billion in blocked Da Afghanistan Bank (DAB) reserves at the Federal Reserve Bank of New York. Even if the Court grants review solely on the narrower Terrorism Risk Insurance Act (TRIA) § 201(a) blocking-date question presented in *Havlish* (No. 26-292) and *Ashton* (No. 26-293), *Owens* raises no TRIA claim and would either be denied immediately or held pending *Havlish* and denied once *Havlish* is resolved on TRIA grounds.

If the Court instead grants certiorari in *Owens*—either consolidated with *Havlish* or as the lead FSIA vehicle because Justice Kagan is not recused in No. 26-294—the Court would likely narrow or reformulate the question presented to focus on whether DAB currently qualifies as an "agency or instrumentality of a foreign state" under 28 U.S.C. § 1603(b)(2) when an unrecognized regime controls it, rather than questioning presidential recognition of Afghanistan under *Zivotofsky v. Kerry*. Conditional on a grant, plenary briefing and argument (requiring Court-appointed amicus curiae to defend the judgment below given respondents' non-appearance) is far likelier than a summary disposition; a GVR would occur only if an intervening Executive Branch action or a merits ruling on the FSIA ground in a companion docket alters the baseline.

Conditional on a denial of certiorari, there is a meaningful but minority chance (~14%) of a separate writing—most likely a dissent from denial by Justice Thomas or Justice Gorsuch questioning the Second Circuit's § 1603(b)(2) instrumentality analysis, or a brief statement respecting denial noting that disposition of the blocked reserves rests with Congress and the Executive Branch.
