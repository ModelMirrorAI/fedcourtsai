The Supreme Court will probably decline to hear this case, though the chance it takes it is meaningful: a little over one in four. Victims of the 1998 embassy bombings want to seize frozen Afghan central bank money to satisfy claims against the Taliban; a lower court said the funds are protected by foreign sovereign immunity (a rule shielding other countries' property from U.S. lawsuits). The Court asked the federal government's top appellate lawyer for advice, which signals interest, but the government likely supports the ruling below.
