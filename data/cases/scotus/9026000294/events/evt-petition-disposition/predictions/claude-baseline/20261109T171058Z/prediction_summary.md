Victims of terrorist attacks who hold court judgments against the Taliban want to collect them from frozen Afghan central-bank money. The appeals court held the money is protected by foreign sovereign immunity, the legal rule that shields foreign governments from most U.S. lawsuits, relying on State Department statements. We predict the Supreme Court will probably refuse to hear the case, but the chance it takes it is real: about three in ten. The Court has already held the petition over from one weekly conference to the next, and it may ask the federal government for its views first.
