## 2. Disposition Route and Question Presented Selection

- **Disposition (`disposition`: 0.44)**: The single slightly more probable outcome over the full life of the petition is that the Court ultimately **denies certiorari** (probability 0.56 of no grant, vs. 0.44 of a grant), either after the November 13 Conference or following a CVSG in which the Solicitor General urges denial to protect Executive foreign-affairs flexibility and central-bank reserve immunity. However, a **plenary grant** (consolidated with *Havlish*, No. 26-292, and *Ashton*, No. 26-293) is a close contender at 44%.
- **Summary disposition (`summary-disposition-route`: 0.03 conditional on grant)**: Conditional on a grant of certiorari, a summary disposition (GVR or summary reversal) is very unlikely (3%). The Second Circuit issued comprehensive majority, concurring, and dissenting opinions at both the panel stage (152 F.4th 339) and the en banc stage (170 F.4th 100), and no intervening Supreme Court decision has altered the governing FSIA or Terrorism Risk Insurance Act ("TRIA") framework since March 2026. If the Court grants review, it will almost certainly set the consolidated cases for full merits briefing and oral argument.
- **Questions Presented if Granted**: If certiorari is granted alongside *Havlish* (No. 26-292) and *Ashton* (No. 26-293), the Court is most likely to grant review on **Question Presented 2** of the *Owens* petition—whether DAB currently qualifies as an "agency or instrumentality of a foreign state" under 28 U.S.C. § 1603(b)(2) when the recognized State of Afghanistan no longer exists as a functioning government and DAB is completely controlled by the unrecognized Taliban regime (the issue on which all five Second Circuit en banc dissenters—Chief Judge Livingston and Judges Sullivan, Bianco, Park, and Menashi—agreed)—together with the TRIA § 201(a) timing question presented in *Havlish* and *Ashton*. The Court is less likely to disturb the Second Circuit's holding on Question Presented 1 that the President's formal recognition of a foreign state is conclusive under *Zivotofsky v. Kerry*, 576 U.S. 1 (2015), though if it grants the petition without limiting the questions, it would likely affirm on the recognition point while focusing its statutory scrutiny on § 1603(b)(2) and TRIA § 201(a).
