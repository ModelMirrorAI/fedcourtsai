## 2. Adjustments from the 24.81% Elevated-Band Anchor

Starting from the **24.81%** `elevated` band anchor, I adjust upward to **`probability: 0.44`** (44% chance of a grant; 56% chance of denial/dismissal) for the following reasons:

### A. Strong Upward Adjustments (+25 to +28 percentage points)
1. **Five-Judge En Banc Dissent on Statutory Text and *Loper Bright* / *Altmann* Principles**: In *Havlish v. Taliban; Aliganga v. Taliban*, 170 F.4th 100 (2d Cir. 2026), five active Second Circuit judges (Chief Judge Livingston and Judges Sullivan, Bianco, Park, and Menashi) dissented from the denial of rehearing en banc. Even Judge Menashi—who agreed with the panel majority that the President's diplomatic recognition of the State of Afghanistan is conclusive under *Zivotofsky v. Kerry*, 576 U.S. 1 (2015)—joined the other four dissenters in concluding that the panel gravely erred by holding (1) that Da Afghanistan Bank ("DAB") remains an "agency or instrumentality" of the recognized State of Afghanistan under 28 U.S.C. § 1603(b)(2) based on a 2003 Afghan law and an extra-record German dissertation despite the Taliban's total de facto takeover of DAB, and (2) that under TRIA § 201(a) an entity's status as an agency or instrumentality of a terrorist party is frozen on the date the assets were blocked rather than assessed at the time of execution/turnover.
2. **Massive Financial and Public Importance (`big_case_score: 0.78`)**: Approximately $3.5 billion in frozen FRBNY central-bank reserves and the ability of hundreds of U.S. terrorism victims (both the 1998 U.S. Embassy bombing victims in No. 26-294 and the 9/11 judgment creditors in Nos. 26-292 and 26-293) to enforce judgments against the Taliban are at stake.
3. **True Post-Conference Relist Across Three Coordinated Vide Petitions**: All three vide petitions (Nos. 26-292, 26-293, and 26-294), filed by top Supreme Court practitioners (Matthew D. McGill in No. 26-294; Stuart Singer and Ian Gershengorn in No. 26-292; Samuel Issacharoff in No. 26-293) and supported by 31 States as amici in No. 26-292, were considered at the November 6, 2026 Conference and relisted together on November 9, 2026 for the November 13, 2026 Conference. Unlike a pre-conference reschedule, this is a genuine conference relist indicating active judicial engagement.

### B. Countervailing Downward / Restraining Factors (-6 to -9 percentage points)
1. **Absence of a Direct Circuit Split and Sui Generis Factual Context**: As both the panel majority (Judges Cabranes and Calabresi, joined in their en banc statement by Judges Nardini and Lohier) and Judge Menashi noted, no court of appeals has ever applied *Klinghoffer* to strip FSIA statehood from a state formally recognized by the President. And the Third Circuit's decision in *OI European Group B.V. v. Bolivarian Republic of Venezuela*, 73 F.4th 157 (3d Cir. 2023), concerned the *Bancec* alter-ego doctrine rather than threshold § 1603(b)(2) ownership or TRIA's blocking-date timing. The posture of post-August 2021 Afghanistan—where the United States continues to recognize the State of Afghanistan while refusing to recognize any government of Afghanistan—is factually unique.
2. **Executive Foreign-Affairs Sensitivity and Potential for Denial or CVSG**: The $3.5 billion in DAB reserves has been the subject of intense Executive Branch policy judgment (including Executive Order 14,064 and DOJ's district-court statement of interest opposing turnover of DAB's central-bank reserves). Even after *Loper Bright*, several Justices are historically reluctant to override central-bank execution immunity under 28 U.S.C. § 1611(b)(1) or complicate presidential diplomacy over sovereign reserves without the Executive's support.
3. **Vehicle Comparison Between No. 26-294 (*Owens*) and Nos. 26-292/26-293 (*Havlish*/*Ashton*)**: In *Havlish* and *Ashton*, petitioners are *judgment creditors* invoking TRIA § 201(a), which everyone on the Second Circuit agreed abrogates FSIA §§ 1609 and 1611(b)(1) immunity if DAB qualifies as an agency or instrumentality of a terrorist party at the relevant time. In *Owens* (No. 26-294), petitioners sought a *prejudgment* attachment under New York law and therefore could not rely on TRIA § 201(a) below; to prevail, they must defeat FSIA attachment immunity directly (including § 1611(b)(1) central-bank immunity). While the Court would very likely grant all three vide petitions together if it grants review, there is a small risk that the Court either denies all three or grants only the TRIA petitions (Nos. 26-292 and 26-293) and holds No. 26-294.

Netting these considerations yields **`probability: 0.44`**, **`granted: 0`**, and **`predicted_disposition: "denied"`**.
