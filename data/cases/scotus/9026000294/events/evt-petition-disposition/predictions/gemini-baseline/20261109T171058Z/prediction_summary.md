The Supreme Court is slightly more likely to deny review than to grant it, with about a two-in-five (44%) chance of granting the petition. Victims of the 1998 U.S. Embassy bombings seek to attach $3.5 billion in frozen Afghan central-bank reserves to satisfy claims against the Taliban, arguing that under the Foreign Sovereign Immunities Act (the federal law governing when foreign governments can be sued in U.S. courts) the central bank is now controlled by the Taliban rather than a recognized foreign state. Although a sharp five-judge dissent in the lower court and a recent conference relist make this a close call, the lack of a direct conflict among federal appeals courts and the diplomatic sensitivity of seizing foreign central-bank reserves tilt the odds narrowly toward denial.
