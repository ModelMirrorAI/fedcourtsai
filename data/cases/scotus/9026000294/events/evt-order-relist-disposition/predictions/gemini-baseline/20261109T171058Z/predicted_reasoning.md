## 2. Disposition and Scope of Review If Granted

- **Overall Disposition (`disposition`: 0.14, `predicted_disposition`: `denied`):** The Court will likely deny the petition. Even if four Justices wished to review the Second Circuit's judgment insulating DAB's $3.5 billion FRBNY account from terrorism victims, the companion *Havlish* (No. 26-292) and *Ashton* (No. 26-293) petitions present a far cleaner, final-judgment statutory vehicle under Section 201(a) of the Terrorism Risk Insurance Act ("TRIA"), which overrides Foreign Sovereign Immunities Act ("FSIA") immunity without requiring the Court to disturb baseline foreign central bank immunity (`28 U.S.C. §§ 1609, 1611(b)(1)`) or executive recognition doctrine under *Zivotofsky ex rel. Zivotofsky v. Kerry*, 576 U.S. 1 (2015).
- **Questions Presented If Granted (Context-Only):** If the Court were to grant certiorari in No. 26-294 (most likely consolidated or paired alongside Nos. 26-292 and 26-293 after a CVSG), it would take only **Question Presented 1**—and likely reformulate or narrow it to whether an entity remains an "agency or instrumentality of a foreign state" under 28 U.S.C. § 1603(b)(2) when an unrecognized regime exercises *de facto* control over it—while leaving **Question Presented 2** (challenging *sua sponte* application of FSIA attachment immunity under 28 U.S.C. §§ 1609 and 1611(b)(1)) behind, given the unbroken circuit consensus and unanimity among all Second Circuit judges on that issue. If granted on the merits, the Court would likely affirm the Second Circuit as to the conclusive effect of Presidential recognition of the State of Afghanistan under § 1603(a) and *Zivotofsky*, while focusing any closer scrutiny on § 1603(b)(2)'s statutory ownership requirement under *Dole Food Co. v. Patrickson*, 538 U.S. 468 (2003).
- **Summary Disposition Route Conditional on Grant (`summary-disposition-route`: 0.05):** Conditional on a grant of certiorari, plenary briefing and oral argument would be overwhelming (`0.95`). No intervening Supreme Court decision calls for a grant, vacate, and remand ("GVR"), and the Court would not summarily reverse a published Second Circuit decision protecting $3.5 billion in blocked central bank reserves without merits briefing and participation by the United States or an appointed amicus.
