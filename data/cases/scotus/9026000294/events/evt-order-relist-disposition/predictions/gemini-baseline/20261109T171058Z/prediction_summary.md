The Supreme Court is likely to deny review in this case, with about a one-in-seven chance (14%) of granting the petition. Victims of the 1998 United States embassy bombings seek to seize $3.5 billion in frozen Afghan central bank funds held at the Federal Reserve Bank of New York to satisfy claims against the Taliban, arguing that the central bank is no longer protected by foreign sovereign immunity (the legal rule shielding foreign governments and their central banks from lawsuits and asset seizures in United States courts). Although five appellate judges dissented below and the financial stakes are massive, no opposing party has filed a response, there is no disagreement among federal appeals courts, and two companion petitions by September 11 victims offer a cleaner legal path to the same funds.
