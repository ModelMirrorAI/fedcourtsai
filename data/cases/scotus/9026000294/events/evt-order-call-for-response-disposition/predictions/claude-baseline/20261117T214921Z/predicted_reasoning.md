# Forecast: Owens v. Taliban, No. 26-294 (response-requested moment)

**State forecast from.** The docket shows two distributions (conferences of 11/6 and 11/13/2026), a CVSG on Nov. 16, 2026, and a called-for response on Nov. 17 (due Dec. 17, 2026). The same three steps were taken the same days in the companion petitions from the same Second Circuit judgment, Havlish (26-292) and Ashton (26-293).

**Further distributions.** Near certain. The petition will be redistributed once the Solicitor General's invited brief is in. I expect that brief in May 2027 (in time for a June conference) or, less likely, December 2027; one redistribution after it, with perhaps one relist if the Court is assembling a grant across the three petitions.

**CVSG.** Already issued; no second one.

**Disposition.** More likely denied than granted (about 36% for any grant). The Court will act on the three petitions together and will most likely follow the Solicitor General. I expect the SG to defend the judgment below — the Second Circuit's rule makes Executive recognition conclusive of "foreign state" status, which is the Executive's institutional position and the position the United States took in the district court — and to recommend denial, in which case the Court denies all three. If the SG instead recommends review (plausible given the terrorism-victim equities and the five-judge en banc dissent), the Court grants.

**Question presented, if granted.** The Court would take the FSIA question in substance — whether Executive recognition is conclusive of "foreign state" status under 28 U.S.C. § 1603 — probably in the SG's or the Havlish petition's formulation rather than this petition's "websites and litigation filings" framing. The Court could grant one petition (most likely Havlish, the lead case below) and hold this one, or grant and consolidate; either way this docket would resolve on the grant side.

**Summary disposition.** Unlikely as the route to a grant (about 8% conditional on a grant); the only realistic path is this petition being held for a granted companion and then GVR'd. No intervening decision is in view and a per curiam reversal on a recognition-power question is improbable.

**Separate writing.** If denied, most likely no noted dissent or statement (about 12% that there is one); the likeliest writers would be Gorsuch, Thomas or Alito, on the judiciary's duty to construe the FSIA independently.
