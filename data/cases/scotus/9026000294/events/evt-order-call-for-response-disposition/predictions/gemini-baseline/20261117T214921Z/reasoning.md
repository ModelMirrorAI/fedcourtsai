## 2. Record & Lower-Court Findings Driving Adjustments

Because `decision-below.txt` was not staged in `record/documents/` (`lower-court-docket.txt` matched member docket No. 23-354, whereas the Second Circuit docketed its combined opinions on lead consolidated docket No. 23-258), I retrieved and read both full Second Circuit decisions via CourtListener MCP: the panel opinion and partial dissent in *Havlish v. Taliban; Aliganga v. Taliban*, 152 F.4th 339 (2d Cir. Aug. 26, 2025) (`opinion_id: 11127439`), and the 8–5 order denying rehearing en banc with concurrences and dissents, 170 F.4th 100 (2d Cir. Mar. 19, 2026) (`opinion_id: 11277807`), alongside the official Supreme Court docket headers for companion petitions No. 26-292 (*Havlish*) and No. 26-293 (*Ashton*). Reading the full opinions below materially sharpens both the upward and downward case-specific factors relative to the petition's framing alone:

### Upward Factors (Supporting Substantial Grant Odds)
1. **Active Court engagement across all three companion dockets:** After distributing Nos. 26-292, 26-293, and 26-294 twice (for the November 6 and November 13, 2026 Conferences), the Court not only invited the Solicitor General on November 16, 2026, but followed up on November 17, 2026 with `Response Requested` (due December 17, 2026) on all three dockets. Because the Taliban defaulted throughout and the Federal Reserve Bank of New York (FRBNY, a neutral garnishee-appellee on the Second Circuit caption, explaining `"The Taliban, et al."`) filed no opposition, calling for a response satisfies the Court's customary procedural prerequisite for a grant while awaiting the Solicitor General's brief.
2. **Deep 8–5 Second Circuit en banc fracture on FSIA § 1603(b) instrumentality status:** Five active judges dissented from the denial of rehearing en banc—Judge Sullivan (joined by Chief Judge Livingston and Judges Bianco and Park) and Judge Menashi separately. Crucially, while Judge Menashi rejected petitioners' threshold argument on state recognition, **all five en banc dissenters agreed** that the panel erred under **28 U.S.C. § 1603(b)** (covered in Part I.B of *Owens*'s petition) by relying on an inoperative 2003 Afghan banking statute and an extra-record 2007 German dissertation to hold that Da Afghanistan Bank (DAB) remains an "agency or instrumentality" of the recognized Republic of Afghanistan despite its total capture by the unrecognized Taliban.
3. **Full 9-Justice bench in No. 26-294 vs. Kagan recusal in Nos. 26-292 and 26-293:** The staged companion snapshots (`record/related/26-292.json` and `26-293.json`) reveal a critical vehicle advantage for *Owens*: **Justice Kagan is recused** in both *Havlish* (No. 26-292) and *Ashton* (No. 26-293) under 28 U.S.C. § 455(b)(3) and Canon 3B(2)(e) (prior government employment), leaving an 8-Justice Court at risk of a 4–4 affirmance in those dockets, whereas **Justice Kagan is not recused in No. 26-294**. Combined with elite Supreme Court counsel (Matthew D. McGill of King & Spalding), $3.5 billion in stakes, a 31-State amicus brief in companion No. 26-292, and the Roberts Court's consistent interest in FSIA textualism (*Turkiye Halk Bankasi*, *Republic of Hungary v. Simon*, *Exxon Mobil v. Corporacion Cimex*), No. 26-294 is an indispensable vehicle if the Court wishes to reach FSIA immunity with a full bench.

### Downward Factors (Pushing Below the 35.0% CVSG / High-Band Anchor)
1. **Absence of a genuine inter-circuit split on recognized foreign states:** Petitioners frame a circuit split with the First, Second, Seventh, and D.C. Circuits (*Ungar*, *Klinghoffer*, *Rubin*, *Weinstein*), yet every out-of-circuit statehood decision they invoke involved an *unrecognized* entity (such as Palestine/PLO) where no presidential recognition existed. No circuit has ever held that federal courts may declare a country *formally recognized by the President* to have lost "foreign state" status under the FSIA. Nine of thirteen active Second Circuit judges—including en banc dissenter Judge Menashi and concurring Judges Nardini and Lohier—agreed that *Zivotofsky v. Kerry*, 576 U.S. 1, 11, 14 (2015), forecloses judicial withdrawal of formal diplomatic recognition.
2. **High probability (~80%) that the Solicitor General recommends denial:** Below, the United States participated via 28 U.S.C. § 517 Statements of Interest and amicus briefing to defend FSIA execution immunity for the $3.5 billion DAB reserves at FRBNY, and the State Department continued recognizing Afghanistan as an independent state into 2025–2026 (`152 F.4th at 352 n.67`). Across administrations, the Executive Branch strongly defends both Article II recognition exclusivity and foreign central-bank reserve immunity under 28 U.S.C. §§ 1609/1611(b)(1) to preserve New York's status as custodian of sovereign reserves and retain diplomatic leverage over frozen funds. Conditional on an SG recommendation to deny in a sensitive foreign-relations / sovereign-reserve dispute without a square circuit split, the grant probability drops substantially (~23%), whereas the ~15% chance of an SG grant/partial-grant recommendation covering FSIA (at ~78% grant yield) and ~5% chance of an SG split recommendation favoring only TRIA in *Havlish* (~25% spillover grant/GVR yield for *Owens*) combine to `0.80 × 0.23 + 0.15 × 0.78 + 0.05 × 0.25 = 0.3135`.
3. **Companion-vehicle decoupling and downstream obstacles:** Unlike *Havlish* (No. 26-292) and *Ashton* (No. 26-293), *Owens* (No. 26-294—which joins pre-judgment *Aliganga* plaintiffs to whom TRIA § 201(a) cannot apply) **omits the TRIA § 201(a) blocking-date question** from its Question Presented. Because TRIA § 201(a) offers a clean textualist ground that avoids *Zivotofsky* and leaves non-terrorism central-bank immunity intact, there is a nontrivial risk that if four Justices vote to grant review despite an SG opposition, they grant *Havlish* on TRIA alone and deny *Owens* (or deny *Owens* after *Havlish*). Moreover, even if petitioners prevailed on §§ 1603(b)/1609 in *Owens*, the panel majority left unresolved both FSIA § 1611(b)(1) central-bank immunity and whether New York attachment law permits seizing DAB's assets for the Taliban's debts absent TRIA.

Netting these forces moves my forecast moderately below the `0.350` anchor to **`P(grant) = 0.31`** (`granted = 0`, `predicted_disposition = "denied"`).
