The Supreme Court will likely deny review, with roughly a three-in-ten chance (31%) of taking the case. Victims of the 1998 East Africa embassy bombings seek $3.5 billion in frozen Afghan central-bank reserves to satisfy judgments against the Taliban, arguing lower courts improperly deferred to the Executive Branch on foreign sovereign immunity (the legal shield protecting foreign governments' property from lawsuits). Although the Court invited the Solicitor General (the federal government's top appellate lawyer) to weigh in and requested a response, no circuit conflict exists and the government will likely urge denial to protect presidential diplomatic authority and central-bank reserves.
