## In brief

Terrorism victims ask the Court to review a ruling letting courts treat State Department statements as binding when deciding whether a Taliban-controlled bank is immune from seizure.

## What happened

Petitioners are victims, or relatives of victims, of the 1998 al-Qaeda bombings of U.S. embassies in Kenya and Tanzania, who hold a roughly $5.2 billion default judgment against the Taliban. They sought to attach about $3.5 billion in blocked funds held in the name of Da Afghanistan Bank (DAB), Afghanistan's central bank, now controlled by the Taliban. A district court denied confirmation of the attachment, deferring to a Justice Department letter describing DAB as Afghanistan's central bank. The Second Circuit affirmed, over a dissent, holding it was bound by a State Department website and the Justice Department's letter to treat Afghanistan as a "foreign state" entitled to immunity under a federal immunity statute. Rehearing en banc was denied, with five judges dissenting.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked whether courts deciding if an entity qualifies for sovereign immunity must independently interpret the immunity statute themselves, or whether they must instead defer to statements from the State Department's website and Justice Department litigation filings.

## Where it stands

The petition has been distributed for two conferences, and the Court has invited the Solicitor General to submit a brief giving the federal government's views before deciding whether to hear the case; that brief is due in mid-December 2026.

## What each outcome would mean

The terrorism victims, as petitioners, are asking the Court to overturn the Second Circuit's ruling. If the Court grants review and ultimately reverses, courts would have to independently interpret the immunity statute rather than defer to the executive branch's statements, potentially letting the victims pursue the bank's funds. If it affirms, the funds would remain immune from attachment. If the Court denies review, the Second Circuit's decision favoring immunity stands. A grant without a merits decision yet, or a vacate-and-remand, would leave the immunity question undecided for now.
