## In brief

Terrorism victims ask the Court to decide whether courts, not the Executive Branch, must independently determine whether an entity qualifies as a foreign state entitled to immunity from lawsuits.

## What happened

Petitioners are victims, or relatives of victims, of al-Qaeda's 1998 embassy bombings in Kenya and Tanzania, attacks the Taliban allegedly supported. They won a nearly $5.2 billion default judgment against the Taliban and sought to collect it by attaching billions in blocked funds held by Da Afghanistan Bank, which they say the Taliban now controls. The district court denied attachment, relying on a Department of Justice letter describing the bank as Afghanistan's central bank. The Second Circuit affirmed, over a dissent, holding it was bound by that letter and a State Department website to treat Afghanistan as a foreign state. Rehearing en banc was denied, with five judges dissenting.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked whether a law governing when foreign governments and their agencies are shielded from lawsuits and asset seizures requires courts to work out for themselves whether an entity counts as a foreign state, or whether courts must instead accept the Executive Branch's own statements, including an informal website listing and a brief litigation letter, as conclusively deciding that question.

## Where it stands

The petition was distributed for conferences on November 6 and November 13, 2026. The docket shows no action on it since those conferences.

## What each outcome would mean

The Owens and Aliganga petitioners are asking the Court to reverse the Second Circuit's ruling, which favored the Taliban and Da Afghanistan Bank by keeping disputed funds immune from attachment. If the Court grants review, it would hear full briefing and argument and decide whether courts must independently interpret the immunity law rather than defer to Executive statements. If it denies review, the Second Circuit's decision stands, leaving the funds immune and the petitioners unable to collect their judgment through this route.
