## In brief

Terrorism victims ask the Court to decide whether courts, not the State Department, must interpret who counts as a foreign state entitled to immunity from lawsuits.

## What happened

Petitioners are victims, or relatives of victims, of the 1998 al-Qaeda bombings of U.S. embassies in Kenya and Tanzania, who hold a nearly $5.2 billion default judgment against the Taliban. They sought to attach $3.5 billion in blocked funds held in the name of Afghanistan's former central bank, Da Afghanistan Bank, now controlled by the Taliban. The district court denied confirmation of the attachment, deferring to a Justice Department letter describing the bank as Afghanistan's central bank. A divided Second Circuit panel affirmed, holding it was bound by a State Department website and the Justice Department letter to treat Afghanistan as a foreign state. The full appeals court denied rehearing en banc over five judges' dissents.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked whether a law governing when foreign governments and their agencies are immune from U.S. lawsuits requires judges to work out for themselves whether an entity counts as a foreign state, or instead requires judges to accept the executive branch's own statements, such as a government website or a brief filed in litigation, as conclusive on that question.

## Where it stands

The petition has been distributed for two conferences, most recently November 13, 2026. On November 16, 2026, the Court invited the Solicitor General to file a brief expressing the federal government's views on whether the case should be heard.

## What each outcome would mean

James Owens and the other petitioners, who lost below, are asking the Court to reverse the Second Circuit's decision. If the Court grants the petition, it will hear the case and decide whether courts must independently interpret who qualifies as a foreign state, without deciding yet who wins. If it denies the petition, the Second Circuit's ruling stands, leaving the Taliban-controlled bank's funds shielded from attachment. Because the Court has asked the Solicitor General to weigh in, any grant or denial will likely wait until after that brief is filed.
