## In brief

An objector asks the Court to decide whether judges may award class-action representative plaintiffs extra payments for serving in that role.

## What happened

This case grew out of a class action accusing the federal government of overcharging PACER users for court-record access. The case settled for $125 million, and the district court approved the settlement, attorney's fees, and $10,000 "incentive awards" to each of the three nonprofit organizations that served as named plaintiffs. Eric Alan Isaacson, a class member who objected to the settlement below, appealed. The Federal Circuit affirmed, rejecting his argument that Supreme Court precedent bars such incentive awards. Isaacson now petitions for review of that ruling.

## What the Court is being asked

The question is whether judges, without any specific authorization from a statute or procedural rule, may approve payments to class representatives from a settlement fund as a reward for their service in bringing the lawsuit, or whether older Supreme Court rulings about common funds forbid this practice. The petition says one appeals court bars such payments while five others allow them.

## Where it stands

The petition was filed and the federal government, named as a respondent, waived its right to respond. Another respondent group has asked for more time to file its opposition brief. The docket shows no further action since that request.

## What each outcome would mean

Isaacson, the objector, is asking the Court to take up the case. If the Court grants review, it would hear full arguments and decide whether these incentive awards are lawful, deciding nothing yet about who is right. If the Court denies review, the Federal Circuit's decision upholding the settlement and the $10,000 awards to the named plaintiffs would stand, though this would not mean the Court agrees with that ruling.
