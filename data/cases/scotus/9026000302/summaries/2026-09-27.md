## What happened

This case grew out of a class action accusing the federal government of overcharging the public for PACER court-record access fees. Three nonprofit groups served as representative plaintiffs and settled the case for $125 million. The district court approved the settlement, along with attorney's fees and a $10,000 "incentive award" to each of the three representative plaintiffs, meant to recognize their service leading the case. Eric Alan Isaacson, a lawyer and class member, objected, arguing such awards are barred by old Supreme Court common-fund rulings. The Federal Circuit rejected his objections and affirmed the awards, joining most other appeals courts, while one circuit, the Eleventh, has held such awards unlawful.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked to resolve whether judges overseeing class-action settlements may approve extra payments to representative plaintiffs, beyond their ordinary share of the settlement, as a reward for serving in that role, when no statute or procedural rule specifically permits it. Isaacson argues that longstanding Supreme Court precedent forbids compensating representative plaintiffs for their personal service in a case, while most appeals courts have concluded that this old rule no longer applies to modern class actions.
