## In brief

An objecting class member asks the Court to decide whether courts may award class-action representatives bonus payments from settlement funds.

## What happened

This case grew out of a class action by three nonprofit organizations against the federal government over PACER court-record fees, which settled for $125 million. The district court approved the settlement, along with attorney's fees and $10,000 "incentive awards" to each of the three named plaintiffs for serving as class representatives. Eric Alan Isaacson, a class member who objected to the settlement, appealed, arguing that such incentive awards are barred by old Supreme Court common-fund rulings. The Federal Circuit affirmed, joining most other circuits in allowing incentive awards, though the Eleventh Circuit has held they are unlawful.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked whether it is lawful, under old rulings limiting what representative plaintiffs may be paid from a shared settlement fund, for courts to approve extra payments rewarding people for having served as class representatives, even though no statute or procedural rule specifically permits such payments.

## Where it stands

The petition was filed September 3, 2026. The federal government, named as a respondent, waived its right to file a response on September 23, 2026. The docket shows no further action since then.

## What each outcome would mean

Isaacson, the objector, asks the Court to take up the case. If the Court grants review, it would hear full arguments and decide whether these incentive awards are lawful, deciding nothing yet on its own. If the Court denies review, the Federal Circuit's ruling upholding the $10,000 awards to the three named plaintiffs, and the underlying settlement, stays in place, though the denial would not mean the Court agrees with that outcome.
