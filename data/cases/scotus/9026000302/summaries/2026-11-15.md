## In brief

A class-action objector asks the Court to rule that judges may not award payments to class representatives for their work leading a lawsuit.

## What happened

Three nonprofit organizations sued the federal government over PACER court-record fees and eventually settled for $125 million. The settlement included $10,000 payments to each of the three organizations for their work leading the case. Eric Alan Isaacson, a class member who objected, argued these payments were unlawful. The district court approved the settlement and payments, and the Federal Circuit affirmed, joining several other circuits that allow such payments while disagreeing with the Eleventh Circuit, which has barred them.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked whether judges overseeing class-action settlements may approve payments to the people who served as lead plaintiffs, rewarding them for their work running the case, when no statute or formal court rule specifically authorizes such payments. Isaacson argues old Supreme Court precedent forbids this; the organizations and lower courts say that precedent does not apply to modern class actions.

## Where it stands

The petition has been fully briefed: the respondents filed a brief opposing review on November 12, 2026, after receiving two extensions of time. The docket shows no further action since then, and the case has not been scheduled for a conference in this record.

## What each outcome would mean

Isaacson, the objector, is asking the Court to reverse the Federal Circuit's decision upholding the payments. If the Court grants review and ultimately rules for Isaacson, such payments could be barred or restricted in class actions nationwide. If it rules for the organizations, or simply denies review, the Federal Circuit's decision approving the settlement and the $10,000 payments would stand, leaving the current circuit split unresolved either way review is denied.
