## In brief

A class-action objector asks the Court to decide whether judges may award class representatives extra "incentive" payments from settlement funds.

## What happened

Three nonprofit organizations sued the federal government over excessive PACER court-records fees, eventually settling for $125 million. The district court approved the settlement, including $10,000 "incentive awards" to each of the three named plaintiffs for serving as class representatives. Eric Alan Isaacson, a class member who objected below, argued these payments were barred by old Supreme Court precedent limiting what representative plaintiffs may personally collect from a common fund. The Federal Circuit affirmed the settlement, the attorney's fees, and the incentive awards, joining most other circuits in allowing such payments while one circuit, the Eleventh, forbids them.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked whether judges overseeing class-action settlements may approve extra payments to representative plaintiffs as a reward for serving in that role, or whether older rules governing shared recovery funds prevent such payments unless a statute or formal rule specifically allows them.

## Where it stands

Isaacson filed his petition on September 3, 2026. One group of respondents, the federal government, waived its right to respond. The other respondents, the nonprofit plaintiffs, received an extension and must respond by November 9, 2026. The Court has not yet acted on the petition.

## What each outcome would mean

Isaacson, the objector, is asking the Court to take up the case. If the Court grants review, it will hear full arguments and decide whether such incentive payments are lawful, without presuming either side is right. If it denies review, the Federal Circuit's decision stands, leaving in place the $10,000 awards to the three named plaintiffs and the settlement as approved, while the disagreement among other appeals courts over this question would remain unresolved.
