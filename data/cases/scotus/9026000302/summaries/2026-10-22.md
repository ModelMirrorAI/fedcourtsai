## In brief

An objecting class member asks the Court to decide whether courts may award class-action representatives extra "incentive" payments from settlement funds.

## What happened

Three nonprofit organizations sued the federal government over PACER court-record fees and reached a $125 million settlement. Eric Alan Isaacson, a class member who objected below, challenged the settlement's structure, a nearly $24 million attorney's fee award, and $10,000 "incentive awards" paid to each named plaintiff for serving as class representatives. The district court approved the settlement and awards over his objections. The Federal Circuit affirmed, rejecting Isaacson's argument that two 19th-century Supreme Court decisions bar such incentive payments, and sided with most other circuits that have allowed them. Isaacson then petitioned the Supreme Court for review.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked whether judges may approve extra payments to representative plaintiffs, drawn from a class-action settlement fund, to reward them for serving in that role, given old Supreme Court rulings that the petition says forbid paying litigants for their own services in a case, even though no statute or procedural rule specifically authorizes such payments.

## Where it stands

The petition was filed and the government respondent waived its right to respond. The other respondents, the nonprofit organizations, requested and were granted more time to respond, with their response now due November 9, 2026. The Court has not yet acted on the petition itself.

## What each outcome would mean

Isaacson, the objecting class member, is asking the Court to take up the case and rule that incentive awards like these are unlawful. If the Court grants review, it would hear the case and decide the question itself, deciding nothing yet about who is right. If it denies review, the Federal Circuit's decision stands, leaving in place the settlement and the $10,000 incentive awards to the three named plaintiffs, National Veterans Legal Services Program, National Consumer Law Center, and Alliance for Justice. A denial would not mean the Court agrees with the Federal Circuit, only that its ruling remains undisturbed.
