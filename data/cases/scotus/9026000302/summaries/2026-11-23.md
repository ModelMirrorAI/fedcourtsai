## In brief

A class-action objector asks the Court to review whether courts may award money to class representatives for their service leading a lawsuit.

## What happened

Three nonprofit groups sued the federal government over PACER court-record fees, reaching a $125 million settlement that included $10,000 payments to each group for leading the litigation. Eric Alan Isaacson, a class member who objected, argued these payments are barred by old Supreme Court common-fund rulings. The district court approved the settlement and payments; the Federal Circuit affirmed, joining most other circuits in holding that those rulings do not forbid such payments. Isaacson now asks the Supreme Court to take the case.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked whether lower courts may give money from a class-action settlement fund to the representative plaintiffs as payment for their work running the case, when no law or court rule specifically allows it, given old Supreme Court decisions some read as forbidding payments to representatives for their personal efforts.

## Where it stands

The petition was distributed and the respondents' response was extended twice before they filed a brief opposing review. The docket shows no further action since that brief was filed.

## What each outcome would mean

Isaacson, the objector, is asking the Court to take up the case and reverse the Federal Circuit's decision upholding the payments. If the Court grants review, it would hear full arguments and decide whether such payments are lawful. If it denies review, the Federal Circuit's ruling stands, leaving the $10,000 payments to the three nonprofit class representatives in place and leaving intact the settlement the lower courts approved. A denial would not mean the Court agrees the payments are lawful, only that the lower court's decision remains undisturbed.
