# Forecast: what the Court will do with No. 26-302 (Isaacson v. NVLSP)

**Disposition.** The petition will most likely be **denied** (P(any grant) = 0.20). The
question presented — whether *Greenough* and *Pettus* still bar incentive awards to
class representatives — is a genuine, acknowledged 5–1 circuit split (CA1, CA2, CA7,
CA9, CAFC against CA11), and the Court is looking at it right now: the same
petitioner's companion petition from the Second Circuit, *Isaacson v. Moses*,
No. 25-1411, drew a call for a response in August 2026 and a Chamber of Commerce
amicus in support. If the Court takes the question this Term, it will take it
through **25-1411**, the cleaner private-party vehicle with the Second Circuit's
fully reasoned "superseded by Rule 23" holding; this Federal Circuit petition, a
Little Tucker Act class against the United States decided in a short published
order, is the follow-on. So the grant scenarios for 26-302 are (a) a consolidated
grant alongside 25-1411, or (b) a hold for 25-1411 followed by a GVR if the Court
reverses the Second Circuit. Denial follows either from the Court passing on the
question again — as it did in *Johnson v. Dickenson*, No. 22-389, denied April 2023
after four conferences — or from a hold that ends in denial once 25-1411 is affirmed.

**Distributions and relists.** The docket shows **zero distributions** (arrival
moment: the petition was docketed 2026-09-08; the federal respondent waived on
2026-09-23; the class respondents' response is due 2026-10-08). It will be
distributed at least once (P = 0.97) — a first conference in late October or
November 2026 if the class plaintiffs also waive, later if they file a BIO. Expect
it to travel with 25-1411: either both reach the same conference or 26-302 is held
behind it, so a multi-conference trajectory (relist or reschedule) is likely and a
long hold is plausible if the companion is granted.

**CVSG.** None. The United States is already a respondent and has waived its
response; the Court does not invite the Solicitor General's views in a case where
the government is a party (P = 0.01).

**Which question, if taken.** The QP as written — it is a single clean question
about whether two of the Court's own precedents remain good law. A reformulation
is unnecessary. The Court would not take the Federal Circuit's Little Tucker Act
jurisdiction or fee-award holdings, which the petition does not present.

**Summary disposition versus plenary review.** Conditional on any grant of 26-302,
a summary route (a GVR after a merits decision in 25-1411) is about as likely as a
consolidated plenary grant: P(summary route | grant) = 0.45. There is no
already-decided intervening case to GVR in light of today, so a GVR would come only
at the end of a hold.

**Dissent from denial.** If denied, P(any dissent or statement respecting denial)
= 0.08. The split is the kind that draws a short statement noting the conflict
while pointing to vehicle problems (a serial objector, small awards, an unusual
Federal Circuit posture), most plausibly from Justice Thomas or Justice Gorsuch;
but the Court has denied this question before with no separate writing, and a
denial that follows an affirmance in 25-1411 would carry none.

**If granted (conditional only).** The likely ground would be the text and
history of the common-fund doctrine: whether Rule 23's silence and a century of
lower-court practice can displace *Greenough*'s statement that payments to the
representative for personal services are "illegally made." A textualist majority
is more likely than not to side with the Eleventh Circuit, which is why the hold-and-GVR
route carries real weight for this petition.
