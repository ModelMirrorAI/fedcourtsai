## In brief

Two litigants declared vexatious ask the Court to review California's refusal to let them challenge a decades-old car-warranty judgment's accuracy.

## What happened

Alvin E. Williams and Judith M. Brown-Williams sued Bentley Motors and a dealership over a vehicle warranty dispute beginning around 2005; a jury found for them in 2007, and a judgment followed in 2009. In 2015, in separate litigation, a California court declared them vexatious litigants, requiring court permission before filing new suits. Over the following years they repeatedly sought to vacate that designation and to get a court to clarify or correct the 2009 judgment, which they say contains factual errors. In August 2026, a Los Angeles trial court denied their applications to lift the vexatious-litigant restrictions and ruled their request to clarify the judgment was not properly before it. The California Court of Appeal and California Supreme Court both declined to disturb that ruling.

## What the Court is being asked

The petitioners ask the Court to decide whether it violates constitutional guarantees of fair process for a state to leave someone, who is subject to litigation restrictions for being a repeat filer, with no court willing to actually consider their claim that a judgment against them is inaccurate or improperly entered. They also ask whether a state can use those filing restrictions to block consideration of a request to correct or clarify a judgment in a case that was already pending before the restrictions were ever imposed.

## Where it stands

The petition for a writ of certiorari was filed on November 9, 2026, and the respondents' response is due December 17, 2026. The Court has not yet acted on the petition.

## What each outcome would mean

Williams and Brown-Williams, as petitioners, are asking the Court to take up their case and overturn the rulings below. If the Court grants the petition, it would hear full arguments on whether the procedures they faced violated due process, and would then decide the question rather than resolve who is right. If the Court denies the petition, the lower courts' rulings stand, leaving in place the denial of their requests to lift the vexatious-litigant restrictions and to obtain judicial review of the judgment's accuracy.
