# Predicted reasoning — Trump v. Thakur, No. 26-210 (forecast at docketing)

## What the Court will do with this petition

**The Court grants certiorari and sets the case for plenary briefing and
argument.** This is a Solicitor General petition from a Ninth Circuit decision
(May 26, 2026) affirming a classwide preliminary injunction against the
government's DEI-related grant terminations, presenting a single clean First
Amendment question the SG frames as a direct conflict with *Rust v. Sullivan*
and *NEA v. Finley*. The government rarely petitions, petitions only on
questions it has selected for institutional importance, and wins cert at rates
an order of magnitude above the docket's. I expect a grant in the ordinary
course — conference in the late fall of OT2026, a grant by roughly December
2026–January 2027, and argument in the spring of 2027.

## Distribution and relists

The docket shows **zero distributions** — the petition was docketed August 19,
2026, with the brief in opposition due September 18, 2026. I am forecasting
from that zero-distribution state. The petition will almost certainly be
distributed for conference at least once (only a pre-conference dismissal or
withdrawal prevents it, and the government will not withdraw its own flagship
petition): P(at least one distribution) ≈ 0.97. Respondents — a certified
class of UC researchers represented by capable counsel — will likely take an
extension, pushing first distribution to a November or December conference.
Expect one to two relists beyond the first distribution: the Court frequently
relists once even on strong SG petitions, and the lurking Tucker Act
jurisdictional question (which divided the Court in the NIH grant-termination
stay litigation in August 2025) may prompt a second look, but the petition
will not linger the way a CVSG-track case does.

## CVSG

**No CVSG — effectively impossible.** The United States is the petitioner; the
Solicitor General's views are already before the Court by definition. P ≈ 0.01.

## Which question the Court would take

The QP as written: "Whether the First Amendment prohibits the government from
considering the viewpoints furthered by academic research projects when
deciding whether to grant or terminate funding for those projects through
competitive grant programs." It is a single, well-framed question and the sole
ground of the Ninth Circuit's affirmance; there is no second QP to shed. The
realistic reformulation risk is the Court adding or reaching the Tucker Act
preclusion question the government deliberately did not press below — some
Justices flagged that channeling issue in the 2025 grant-termination stay
cases — but the likelier course is taking the First Amendment question as
presented. (Context-only; not scored.)

## Summary disposition vs. plenary review

**Plenary review is the strong favorite conditional on a grant** (conditional
P(cert-order disposition) ≈ 0.08). There is no intervening decision of this
Court to GVR in light of — the petition's own theory is that existing
precedent (*Rust*, *Finley*, *USAID*) already resolves the question, which is
an argument for reversal after argument, not for a GVR. A summary reversal is
conceivable given the Court's recent willingness to summarily correct the
Ninth Circuit, but a First Amendment question of this breadth, governing an
estimated $200 billion in annual competitive grantmaking and a nationwide
wave of parallel litigation, is the kind the Court resolves with a full
opinion after argument.

## If the Court denies

A denial would most plausibly be posture-based — awaiting a final judgment
rather than reviewing a preliminary injunction, or awaiting percolation in the
First, Second, and D.C. Circuit appeals the petition catalogues. Conditional
on a denial, I put a **noted dissent from or statement respecting denial at
roughly 30%** — Justices Thomas and Alito are the likeliest writers, given
their demonstrated sympathy for the government's *Rust*/*Finley* reading in
the 2025–26 shadow-docket grant cases; but a purely vehicular denial (with the
question preserved for a later, final-judgment vehicle) could well pass in
silence.

## If granted (conditional merits outlook)

If granted, the likely ground of decision is the subsidy/penalty line of
*Rust*–*Finley*–*USAID*: a majority sympathetic to broad Executive discretion
over competitive grantmaking likely reverses or vacates, holding that
selective funding — including termination tied to a program's furthered
viewpoint — is not viewpoint discrimination forbidden by the First Amendment,
possibly with a remand on the arbitrary-and-capricious (APA) theory the Ninth
Circuit did not reach as an independent ground. This is conditional context
only; the arrival-stage event resolves on the petition's disposition, not the
merits.
