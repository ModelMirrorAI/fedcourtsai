# Rationale

I assign a 0.78 probability to any grant and predict a plenary grant. The committed statpack's sal-v3 federal band is the correct arrival-time anchor because the United States is the petitioner and the context freezes `band: federal`, `distribution_count: 0`, and Term 2026. Pooling the federal-band risk sets over the nine displayed prior Terms gives 143 grant-family dispositions among 201 resolved petitions, or 71.1%. This is much more informative than either the whole paid-petition rate or the relist-zero cut, which describes petitions that ended without progressing rather than the risk faced at arrival.

I adjust upward from 71.1% because the Solicitor General presents one legal question arising from a published Ninth Circuit decision, the lower court's analysis squarely engages and distinguishes Supreme Court precedent, and the asserted consequences extend across agencies and recurring challenges to federal grant policy. The petition also identifies substantial live funding and an injunction with ongoing operational effects. I stop at 0.78 because there is no developed circuit split, the case arrives from a preliminary-injunction posture, and jurisdictional, remedial, class, or mootness developments could make this vehicle less attractive even though the petition argues that the First Amendment issue itself is clean.

The 0.99 distribution-increment probability is from the arrival state of zero distributions: a paid federal petition of this importance should reach at least one conference, although a settlement, withdrawal, or other pre-conference disposition is possible. The CVSG probability is 0.001 because the Solicitor General is already the petitioner's counsel, making a later request for the federal government's views functionally inapposite. Conditional on a grant, I put 0.24 on a cert-order disposition: the petition frames the Ninth Circuit's decision as directly contrary to *Rust* and *Finley*, but the doctrinal distinction and broad prospective consequences favor plenary treatment. Conditional on denial, I put 0.18 on some noted dissent or statement; the case is unusually prominent, but separate writings from denial remain exceptional.

The provisioned 155-page petition was marked `truncated: true`; its extracted text nevertheless included the complete question presented, petition argument, and Ninth Circuit opinion before ending during the appended district-court order. No brief in opposition existed in the provisioned documents at this arrival moment, so vehicle objections and the respondents' framing remain the largest evidence gap. I used the provisioned snapshot, questions presented, petition text, context, and committed statpack. I did not use CourtListener MCP, a corpus query, or web search.
