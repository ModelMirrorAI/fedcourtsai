# Forecast

The Court will grant the petition and set the case for plenary briefing and argument. The petition presents a single, nationally consequential First Amendment question: whether the government may consider the viewpoints advanced by competitively funded academic projects when awarding or terminating those grants. The United States attacks a precedential Ninth Circuit ruling that affirmed classwide preliminary relief and expressly distinguished *Rust* and *Finley*. The combination of a federal petitioner, a clean legal issue, recurring litigation, and substantial effects across federal grant programs makes review likely.

The arrival snapshot shows zero distributions. The petition will be distributed at least once, most likely for a late-2026 conference, and may be held for one additional conference before disposition. No CVSG will issue: the Solicitor General already represents the petitioners and has supplied the federal government's views in the petition.

If the Court grants, it will take the question presented substantially as written rather than narrow it to a particular agency or grant program. Plenary review is more likely than a disposition in the certiorari order because resolving the Ninth Circuit's facial-versus-as-applied distinction and defining the boundary between government-selected speech and impermissible viewpoint discrimination warrants a reasoned opinion. A summary reversal remains a minority possibility if the Court views *Rust* and *Finley* as directly controlling; a GVR is unlikely absent an intervening decision.

If review is denied, no separate writing is the likeliest outcome. A dissent from denial or statement respecting denial is possible given the national stakes and the government's claim that the Ninth Circuit openly departed from precedent, with Justices Thomas or Alito the most plausible authors.

Conditioned on plenary review, the Court is likely to hold that competitive grantmaking permits the government to favor projects advancing its chosen programmatic priorities, while leaving open limits where funding is used to regulate recipients' speech outside the funded project.
