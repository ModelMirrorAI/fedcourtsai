# Forecast of the Court's Action: *Trump v. Thakur*, No. 26-210

## 1. Conference Trajectory and Relists (`relist-increment` = 0.72)

The docket currently records a single distribution (`distribution_count = 1`), made on October 28, 2026, for the Conference of November 13, 2026. The petition will likely be **relisted at least once** (most likely 1 to 2 additional distributions, resolving in late November or December 2026). Under the Court's standard practice, petitions seriously considered for plenary review—and particularly Solicitor General petitions—are almost always relisted at least once after their first conference for vehicle and record checks, and here the Justices will also want to monitor the status of the November 17, 2026 summary judgment hearing in the Northern District of California.

## 2. Call for the Views of the Solicitor General (`cvsg-increment` = 0.001)

The Court **will not** issue a CVSG. The Solicitor General is counsel of record for the federal petitioners (*Donald J. Trump, President of the United States, et al.*) and has already stated the United States' position in the petition and reply brief.

## 3. Disposition and Question Presented (`disposition` = 0.56)

The likeliest outcome is a **plenary grant of certiorari** (`granted`, P = 0.56). If the Court grants review, it will take the **single Question Presented in the petition as written**, without reformulation or addition: whether the First Amendment prohibits the government from considering the viewpoints furthered by academic research projects when deciding whether to grant or terminate funding for those projects through competitive grant programs.

If granted, the Court would set the case for full merits briefing and oral argument in spring 2027, where the likely focus would be whether competitive academic research grantmaking at NSF and NEH is governed by the government-as-patron framework of *National Endowment for the Arts v. Finley*, 524 U.S. 569 (1998), and *Rust v. Sullivan*, 500 U.S. 173 (1991), rather than the limited-public-forum / neutral-subsidy framework of *Rosenberger v. Rector & Visitors of University of Virginia*, 515 U.S. 819 (1995).

## 4. Summary Disposition Route (`summary-disposition-route` = 0.02)

Conditional on a grant, a summary disposition in the cert order itself is **very unlikely** (P = 0.02 conditional on grant). The Court will not summarily reverse a published circuit opinion on a novel, high-stakes First Amendment question involving competitive federal research grants without merits briefing and oral argument. The small conditional probability (2%) reflects the narrow possibility that the Court holds the petition and later issues a GVR (or *Munsingwear* vacatur) if final summary judgment or settlement proceedings in the district court moot the preliminary injunction before merits review.

## 5. Separate Writings on Denial (`dissent-from-denial` = 0.18)

Should the Court deny certiorari (P = 0.44 for non-grant, primarily driven by the interlocutory preliminary-injunction posture, the imminent district court summary judgment hearing on November 17, 2026, and the absence of a completed circuit split while *American Council of Learned Societies v. McDonald* remains pending in the Second Circuit), there is a **moderate likelihood (P = 0.18 conditional on denial)** of a dissent from the denial of certiorari or a statement respecting the denial. If such a separate writing issues, it is most likely to be authored by **Justice Thomas** or **Justice Alito** (potentially joined by Justice Gorsuch), faulting the Ninth Circuit's extension of *Rosenberger* to competitive federal grant programs under *Finley* or emphasizing that the denial of interlocutory review does not signify agreement with the decision below.
