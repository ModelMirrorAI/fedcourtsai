## In brief

The Trump administration asks the Court to review a ruling that blocked its termination of university research grants tied to diversity, equity and inclusion topics.

## What happened

Several University of California researchers sued after federal agencies canceled their research grants, saying the cancellations followed executive orders targeting diversity, equity and inclusion programs. A district court ruled for the researchers, provisionally certifying classes and ordering agencies to reinstate the grants tied to those orders. The Ninth Circuit largely affirmed, holding the terminations likely amounted to unconstitutional viewpoint discrimination, though it reversed the injunction for a separate group whose grants were canceled through generic form letters. The government now asks the Supreme Court to take the case.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked whether the government may take into account the viewpoint promoted by a research project when deciding whether to award or cancel a competitive research grant, or whether doing so amounts to unconstitutional discrimination against disfavored viewpoints.

## Where it stands

The petition for certiorari was filed on August 18, 2026, and a response is due September 18, 2026. The Court has not yet acted on the petition.

## What each outcome would mean

The Trump administration, as petitioner, is asking the Court to take up the case. If the Court grants the petition, it will hear full arguments and decide the First Amendment question itself, deciding nothing yet about who is right. If the Court denies the petition, the Ninth Circuit's ruling stands, leaving in place the preliminary injunction that requires the government to keep funding the DEI-related grants held by Neeta Thakur and the other researchers while the underlying lawsuit continues.
