## What happened

Several federal agencies terminated research grants held by University of California researchers after executive orders directed agencies to end funding connected to diversity, equity, and inclusion (DEI) work. Neeta Thakur and other researchers sued, arguing the terminations were unconstitutional and improperly explained. A federal district court in California preliminarily ordered the grants reinstated for two groups of researchers. The Ninth Circuit affirmed as to researchers whose grants were cut for DEI-related reasons, agreeing that the terminations likely amounted to unconstitutional viewpoint discrimination, while ruling against a second group on other grounds. The government, represented by the Solicitor General on behalf of President Trump and various agencies, is now asking the Supreme Court to review the DEI-related ruling.

## What the Court is being asked

The government wants the Court to decide whether the government may take into account the viewpoint promoted by a research project when deciding whether to fund or continue funding it under a competitive grant program. The government argues that choosing which projects to fund based on their subject matter or outlook is a normal part of running selective grant programs and does not amount to punishing speech. The researchers contend that cutting off already-awarded grants specifically because of their perceived viewpoint on DEI crosses a line the First Amendment does not allow.

## Where it stands

The case is a newly filed petition asking the Supreme Court to take up the case; the respondents' response is due September 18, 2026, and the Court has not yet acted on the petition.
