## In brief

The federal government asks the Court to review a ruling that blocked it from ending university research grants based on their perceived viewpoint.

## What happened

Several University of California researchers sued after federal agencies canceled their research grants, citing executive orders targeting programs on diversity, equity and inclusion and similar topics. A district court found the terminations likely violated the First Amendment and ordered the grants reinstated for one group of researchers, while finding a separate group's claims belonged in a different court. The Ninth Circuit largely agreed, holding that the agencies likely engaged in unconstitutional viewpoint discrimination by cutting off already-awarded grants because of the perceived views expressed in the funded research. The government then asked the Supreme Court to review that First Amendment ruling.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked whether the government violates free speech protections when it factors in the viewpoint expressed by academic research in deciding whether to fund or stop funding that research through competitive grant programs, or whether such funding decisions are allowed even when based partly on viewpoint.

## Where it stands

The petition has been fully briefed, with the researchers' opposition and the government's reply filed in October 2026. The case was distributed for the Court's conference on November 13, 2026, and the docket shows no action on it since.

## What each outcome would mean

The government, as petitioner, is asking the Court to reverse the Ninth Circuit's ruling, which favored the researchers. If the Court grants review, it would hear full arguments and decide the constitutional question itself, deciding nothing yet about who wins. If it denies review, the Ninth Circuit's decision favoring the researchers stands, and the preliminary order requiring the agencies to reinstate the grants remains in place while the case continues below.
