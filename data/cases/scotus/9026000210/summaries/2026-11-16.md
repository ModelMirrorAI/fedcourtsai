## In brief

The Court declined to hear the Trump administration's bid to overturn a ruling that blocked it from cutting research grants to University of California scientists over their work's perceived viewpoint.

## What happened

Several federal agencies terminated research grants to University of California faculty after President Trump issued executive orders targeting programs related to diversity, equity, inclusion, and similar topics. Researchers sued, arguing the terminations were unconstitutional viewpoint discrimination and, separately, were arbitrary under administrative law. A federal district court issued a preliminary injunction requiring reinstatement of the grants, and the Ninth Circuit largely affirmed, agreeing the researchers were likely to succeed on their First Amendment claim, while ruling that a related claim belonged in a different court. The federal government then asked the Supreme Court to take the case.

## What the Court is being asked

The question is whether the First Amendment stops the government from taking into account the viewpoint expressed in academic research when deciding whether to award or cut off competitive research grants. The government argues it may stop funding projects it no longer considers in the public interest, even based on viewpoint, just as it may choose what to fund in the first place. The researchers argue that once a grant is awarded, cutting it off specifically because of the perceived viewpoint of the research violates the First Amendment's bar on viewpoint discrimination.

## Where it stands

The petition was distributed for the Court's November 13, 2026 conference, and the Court denied it on November 16, 2026.
