## In brief

The government asks the Court to review a ruling that blocked it from cutting university research grants it linked to diversity, equity and inclusion viewpoints.

## What happened

Several University of California researchers sued after federal agencies terminated their research grants, citing executive orders targeting diversity, equity and inclusion initiatives. A district court preliminarily ordered the grants reinstated, ruling the terminations likely amounted to unconstitutional viewpoint discrimination against one group of grantees, and certified classes of affected researchers. The Ninth Circuit affirmed that ruling for grants terminated under the DEI-related orders, though it sided with the government on a separate group of grants terminated by generic form letters, finding those claims likely belonged in a different court. The government, led by President Trump and numerous federal agencies, then asked the Supreme Court to take up the case.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked whether the government may take into account the viewpoints promoted by academic research when deciding whether to award or cancel competitive research grants, or whether doing so amounts to unconstitutional discrimination against disfavored viewpoints.

## Where it stands

The petition was filed in August 2026. The researchers' deadline to respond was extended, at their request, to October 19, 2026. The docket shows no further action since.

## What each outcome would mean

The federal government asked the Court to take the case after losing on this issue at the Ninth Circuit. If the Court grants review, it will hear full arguments and decide the constitutional question itself, resolving nothing yet about who is right. If it denies review, the Ninth Circuit's ruling stands, leaving in place the order requiring the government to keep funding the researchers' grants while the broader case continues below.
