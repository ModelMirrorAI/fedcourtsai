# Expected disposition

The Court will most likely deny the petition. The order will probably provide no explanation and will not decide the statutory location-data or penalty-cap questions. The denial forecast does not assert that the Court endorses the FCC's reasoning or the judgment below.

# Procedure from this moment

There have already been two distributions and one actual relist. The modal path is no further distribution, followed by disposition after the November 13, 2026 conference or shortly thereafter. I assign a 38% probability of at least one further distribution. If another is needed, one or two additional distributions are more likely than a long hold. The existing first relist is not part of that increment.

The Court will not request the Solicitor General's views: the federal respondents have already filed a substantive opposition. The probability of a new CVSG is 0.5%.

# If review is granted

The likely route is a grant, vacatur, and remand for the D.C. Circuit to reconsider the effect of AT&T and the government's concessions on the carriers' payment and jury-trial arguments. Conditional on any grant, summary disposition is 88% likely; a GVR is substantially more likely than a summary merits reversal. Such an order would not itself require a refund or decide that the FCC affirmatively misled the carriers.

The first question presented supplies the likely basis for relief: whether reconsideration is warranted in light of AT&T and the government's concessions. The Court would likely leave the precise effect and any preservation questions to the lower court. If it instead grants plenary review, the penalty-cap question would require resolving the forfeiture obstacle; the location-data question would concern the statutory boundary between call-location information and device-location information. Neither alternative is the modal route.

# Writings

Conditional on denial, an unexplained order without a noted dissent or statement is most likely; the probability of at least one such separate response is 18%. Thomas is the leading possible writer, with Gorsuch a secondary possibility, on the concern that the carriers should have an opportunity to litigate whether they were induced to pay under a mistaken account of their rights. These are possibilities subordinate to the forecast of no writing, not a predicted cert vote lineup.
