# Predicted Court Reasoning & Procedural Forecast: *Sprint Corp., et al. v. FCC, et al.* (No. 25-1422)

- **Further Relists (`relist-increment`: 0.22):** The petition has been distributed twice so far (initially distributed on October 21, 2026 for the November 6, 2026 Conference, and relisted on November 9, 2026 for the November 13, 2026 Conference). Expect the Court to dispose of the petition at the November 13, 2026 Conference without a further relist; there is a modest (~22%) chance of one or two additional relists if a Justice circulates a brief statement or dissent from denial (most likely Justice Thomas, who dissented alone in *FCC v. AT&T, Inc.*, 608 U.S. 531 (2026)) or if chambers take an extra conference to verify whether a GVR under Footnote 5 of *AT&T* is warranted.
- **Call for the Views of the Solicitor General (`cvsg-increment`: 0.005):** The Court will not issue a CVSG. The Federal Communications Commission and the United States are already the respondents, and the Solicitor General has already filed a comprehensive Brief in Opposition on their behalf.
- **Disposition (`disposition`: 0.14; `predicted_disposition`: `denied`):** The Court will deny the petition for a writ of certiorari.
  - On Question 1 (requesting a GVR in light of *FCC v. AT&T, Inc.*, 608 U.S. 531 (2026), to litigate whether the FCC's forfeiture orders misled petitioners into paying rather than awaiting a 47 U.S.C. § 504(a) collection suit), the Court already confronted the identical refund/involuntary-waiver argument in Footnote 5 of *AT&T* and nonetheless affirmed the Second Circuit's denial of Verizon's petition for review outright. Moreover, unlike in *AT&T*, petitioners here never raised an involuntary-waiver or refund claim before the Commission or the D.C. Circuit, and D.C. Circuit precedent already recognized that a § 504(a) trial *de novo* encompasses both factual and legal challenges.
  - On Question 2 (whether customer location data is CPNI under 47 U.S.C. § 222(h)(1)(A)), the First, Second, and D.C. Circuits are in 3–0 agreement that location data falls within § 222(h)(1)(A), and petitioners' primary voice-versus-data argument was held forfeited below under 47 U.S.C. § 405(a).
  - On Question 3 (whether the number of "continuing violations" under 47 U.S.C. § 503(b) was reviewed with improper deference under *Loper Bright Enterprises v. Raimondo*, 603 U.S. 369 (2024)), there is no circuit conflict and the D.C. Circuit independently construed § 503(b) before reviewing the individualized penalty assessment for reasonableness.
- **Summary Disposition vs. Plenary Review Conditional on Grant (`summary-disposition-route`: 0.71):** Conditional on the Court granting certiorari, the likelier route (71%) is a summary grant, vacatur, and remand (GVR) on Question 1 in light of *FCC v. AT&T, Inc.*, 608 U.S. 531, 544 n.5 (2026), leaving Questions 2 and 3 untouched. If instead the Court were to grant plenary review (29% conditional share of grants), it would likely take Questions 2 and 3 (or Question 2 alone) on the statutory scope of CPNI under 47 U.S.C. § 222(h)(1)(A) and affirm or reverse depending on whether it excuses the § 405(a) forfeiture bar.
- **Separate Writings on Denial (`dissent-from-denial`: 0.12):** Conditional on a denial of certiorari, the modal outcome (88%) is an unsigned summary denial order with no separate writing. There is an ~12% probability of a separate statement or dissent from the denial of certiorari—most plausibly authored by Justice Thomas (reiterating his dissent in *AT&T* that carriers who paid under protest before *AT&T* clarified the non-binding nature of § 503(b)(4) forfeiture orders should be permitted to seek relief), potentially joined by or accompanied by a separate writing from Justice Gorsuch or Justice Alito.
