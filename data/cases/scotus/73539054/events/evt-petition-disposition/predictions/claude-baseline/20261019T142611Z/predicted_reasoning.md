# Forecast — Sprint Corp. (T-Mobile) v. FCC, No. 25-1422

**Disposition: denied** (P(any grant) ≈ 0.04).

- **Distributions so far: one** (DISTRIBUTED for the conference of Nov. 6, 2026); never relisted. I expect the
  petition to be **denied at that first conference** without being relisted. A relist is the main alternative path
  (≈15%). That would most likely be a single relist while a Justice considers a separate writing on the GVR
  question, not a run-up to plenary review.
- **No CVSG.** The federal government is the respondent and has already filed a brief in opposition through the
  Solicitor General, so a call for its views cannot meaningfully happen.
- **If granted, the route would most likely be a GVR in light of FCC v. AT&T (QP 1)**. The petition asks for that
  first, and it is the cheapest form of relief. Plenary review of QP 2 (whether "location" CPNI under § 222 covers
  device location beyond call location) is the next most plausible grant. QP 3 (whether counting "continuing
  violations" under § 503(b) is a question for courts after Loper Bright) has a vehicle problem: the D.C. Circuit
  held that the statutory argument was forfeited. Conditional on any grant, I put a cert-order disposition (GVR) and
  briefing-and-argument at about even.
- **Why denial:** the Court refused a remand in AT&T itself, including on Verizon's rehearing petition (denied Aug.
  2026), and the "misled into paying" argument was not raised below. On CPNI there is no circuit conflict, because
  the Second Circuit read the definition the same way in Verizon. The carriers have also ended the location-data
  programs at issue, so the question has little prospective weight.
- **Separate writing:** a dissent from denial or a statement respecting denial is possible but unlikely (≈10%). The
  likeliest author is Justice Thomas, who dissented in AT&T, possibly joined by Justice Gorsuch, on the jury-trial
  and forfeiture-order concerns behind the GVR request.
