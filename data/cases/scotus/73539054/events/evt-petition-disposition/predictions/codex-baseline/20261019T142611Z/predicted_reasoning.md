# Forecast

The Court will most likely deny the petition without an explanatory opinion or separate writing. The record shows one distribution, entered October 14, 2026, for the November 6, 2026 conference, and no CVSG. I expect disposition in November or December 2026, ordinarily without another distribution. The probability of at least one additional distribution is 0.28; if further consideration occurs, one or two additional distributions are the most likely course. Timing is a forecast, not a scored claim.

The Court is unlikely to solicit additional views from the Solicitor General: the federal respondents have already supplied a substantive opposition. P(a new CVSG) is 0.005.

The principal alternative to denial is a grant, vacatur, and remand for consideration of the effect of FCC v. AT&T and the government's concessions on these petitioners' payment and waiver of jury-trial rights. P(any grant) is 0.18. Conditional on a grant, the probability of disposition in the cert order itself is 0.83, overwhelmingly a GVR rather than summary reversal. A GVR would leave the refund and misleading-order issues for the D.C. Circuit, not establish that petitioners must recover their payments.

The most likely barrier to that course is that the D.C. Circuit already treated a de novo jury proceeding as available before compulsory payment. The intervening decision therefore does not obviously undermine its judgment. The government's preservation objections and the treatment of Verizon's request for a similar remand further favor denial, notwithstanding petitioners' distinction between rehearing after plenary review and a first cert petition.

If review is granted through the summary route, the Court will most likely act on question 1, without separately resolving the CPNI definition or penalty-cap questions. If the Court instead sets the case for argument, question 3, concerning independent judicial interpretation of the statutory penalty unit, is the stronger candidate for focused review. The scope of device-location CPNI presents important regulatory consequences but not a clean conflicting-outcomes split in this record. Any merits ruling remains conditional on review.

Conditional on denial, P(a noted dissent or statement respecting denial) is 0.07. I expect no such writing and make no individual Justice prediction.
