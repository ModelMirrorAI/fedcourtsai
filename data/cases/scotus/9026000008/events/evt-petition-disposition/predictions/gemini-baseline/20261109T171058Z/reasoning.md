## Case-Specific Adjustments from the 24.81% `elevated` Reached Anchor

### Factors Pushing Upward (Supporting Grant or GVR)
1. **Vigorous Fourth Circuit Dissent by Judge Wilkinson on Question 2**:
   Judge Wilkinson's 11-page dissent forcefully argues that the panel majority's reading of *Garcetti v. Ceballos*, 547 U.S. 410 (2006)—treating a teacher's manner of addressing students in the classroom as categorical government speech immune from First Amendment scrutiny—eviscerates *Tinker* and *Pickering* and creates a one-way ratchet whereby states can compel or forbid ideological speech by teachers on noncurricular matters.
2. **Heavy Amicus Participation and Repeat-Player Advocates**:
   Eight amicus briefs support certiorari, including a 20-State brief led by West Virginia and South Carolina, a Coalition for Jewish Values brief authored by Eugene Volokh, and a Goldwater/Cato Institute brief. Petitioner is represented by Claybrook LLC and Alliance Defending Freedom (John Bursch, James Campbell), while Respondents are represented by WilmerHale (Alan Schoenfeld). Moreover, Respondent MCPS is the very same school board whose LGBTQ storybook policy the Court held likely unconstitutional in *Mahmoud v. Taylor*, 606 U.S. 522 (2025).
3. **First Relist on November 9, 2026**:
   While dozens of petitions from the November 6, 2026 Conference were denied on the November 9 order list, *Polk* was relisted for the November 13, 2026 Conference, confirming active chamber attention.
4. **The Intervening *Mirabelli v. Bonta* Decision and Respondents' GVR Invitation**:
   Five weeks after the Fourth Circuit issued its January 28, 2026 opinion in *Polk*, this Court issued its per curiam decision in *Mirabelli v. Bonta*, 607 U.S. 492 (Mar. 2, 2026), holding that California policies prohibiting schools from disclosing a student's gender transition to parents without the student's consent likely violate parents' Free Exercise rights under *Mahmoud*/*Yoder* and substantive due process rights under *Pierce*/*Parham*. Because the Fourth Circuit in *Polk* relied in part on the premise that MCPS's parental-secrecy rule was "lawful" and served a legitimate interest in student safety (Pet. App. 24a, 30a), Respondents themselves explicitly suggested in their Brief in Opposition (BIO 13 n.2, 28) that if the Court does not deny the petition outright, it could GVR in light of *Mirabelli* and MCPS's subsequent July 2026 policy revisions.

### Factors Pushing Downward (Weighing Strongly Toward Denial)
1. **Direct Vote Signal from *Mirabelli v. Bonta* (Mar. 2, 2026) Against Teacher Claims**:
   Crucially, in *Mirabelli v. Bonta*, the district court had certified subclasses of both **parents** and **teachers** ("The teachers object to their compelled participation in the implementation of the State's policies," 607 U.S. at 492). Yet the Supreme Court granted the application to vacate the Ninth Circuit's stay **only "as to the parents"** and ordered that the application **"is otherwise denied"** as to the teachers, with only **Justice Thomas and Justice Alito** noting that they would have granted the application in full (and Justice Barrett, joined by Chief Justice Roberts and Justice Kavanaugh, writing a concurrence focused exclusively on the parents' claims). That March 2026 vote demonstrates that at least four of the six conservative Justices (Roberts, Gorsuch, Kavanaugh, Barrett) deliberately declined to extend interim constitutional relief to public-school teachers challenging gender-identity pronoun and parental-secrecy policies. Similarly, in April 2026, the Court denied certiorari in *Foote v. Ludlow School Committee*, No. 25-77, after 13 distributions.
2. **Severe Vehicle Defects and Post-Judgment Policy Changes**:
   - **July 2026 Policy Revision Eliminating the Parental-Secrecy Rule**: In response to *Mirabelli* and April 2026 Maryland State Department of Education guidance, MCPS revised its Guidelines in July 2026 to remove the requirement that staff conceal a student's gender expression from parents upon parental request ("Staff are not required to conceal this information from parents/guardians," BIO 10, 12). That revision guts the parental-concealment half of Question Presented 1 going forward.
   - **Reinstatement and Accommodation Offer**: MCPS reactivated Polk on the substitute-teacher roster in early 2026 without requiring her to click the training compliance box and offered her an accommodation to address students by their first or last names rather than pronouns (BIO 10, 12; Reply 10–11). Although Petitioner replies that her backpay/nominal damages claim prevents mootness and that the written Guidelines still require staff to respect gender-identity pronouns, these post-judgment developments make the case a messy, fact-bound vehicle for prospective constitutional pronouncements.
   - **Abstract Pre-Enforcement Record and Abandoned Title VII Claim**: Polk worked only 14 days in 2021–2022 as a substitute teacher, never encountered a transgender student in any classroom, was never asked to use preferred pronouns, and was never asked to withhold information from a parent. Worse still, she failed to exhaust her administrative remedies on her **Title VII religious-accommodation claim** (Count I) and abandoned it on appeal—depriving the Court of the natural statutory ground (*Groff v. DeJoy*, 600 U.S. 447 (2023)) that would avoid reaching broad constitutional questions about public-employee speech and *Employment Division v. Smith*.
3. **Unanimous Rejection of Question Presented 1 Below and Lack of a Direct K–12 Federal Circuit Split on Question Presented 2**:
   Even Judge Wilkinson agreed that Polk's Free Exercise claim fails under *Smith* ("That is, as they say, elementary," Pet. App. 37a). And on the Free Speech claim, every federal court of appeals to address K–12 public school teachers (*Polk* in the 4th Cir., *Wood v. Florida Department of Education*, 142 F.4th 1286 (11th Cir. 2025), and analogous decisions in the 7th and 9th Circuits) has applied *Garcetti* to in-class pronoun policies, while *Meriwether v. Hartop*, 992 F.3d 492 (6th Cir. 2021), explicitly relied on university academic-freedom principles and *Vlaming v. West Point School Board*, 895 S.E.2d 705 (Va. 2023), rested ultimately on the Virginia Constitution.
