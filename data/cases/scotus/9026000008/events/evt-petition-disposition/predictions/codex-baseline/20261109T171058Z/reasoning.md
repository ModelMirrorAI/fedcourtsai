# Reasons to take the petition seriously

The questions-presented file and petition, printed pp. 3–11 and 21–33, identify two distinct issues: whether Smith governs these asserted religious burdens, and whether a public school can compel the challenged teacher speech under Garcetti. The petition includes the Fourth Circuit opinion in its appendix. That opinion matches the provisioned January 28, 2026 judgment and docket 25-1136. Judge Wilkinson's dissent concerns **speech**, not the panel's unanimous rejection of the free-exercise claim; it should not be counted as a dissent supporting both questions. His concern about noncurricular compelled expression makes the second question a more plausible plenary vehicle than a generalized request to reconsider Smith.

The reply, printed pp. 2–9, does meaningfully answer the opposition. It argues that the free-exercise claim was preserved even if its Barnette argument was refined; distinguishes ideological affirmation from curricular instruction; and identifies conflicting approaches to pronoun use and noncurricular speech. The number of amici and the potential reach across school systems support significant stakes. These factors move my estimate upward from the mechanical band anchor before the vehicle adjustments below.

# Why denial remains more likely

**The supposed clean split is less clean on inspection.** The BIO, printed pp. 19–23, distinguishes Meriwether's university setting from elementary-school teaching and explains that district-court disagreement is not itself a circuit split. It also identifies the independent Virginia constitutional ground in Vlaming. I checked the latter decision through CourtListener, opinion 9909805, especially its discussion around printed pp. 53–60. Vlaming rejects a sweeping official-duties defense to a teacher's compelled-speech allegations, but expressly grounds its holding in Article I, Section 12 of the Virginia Constitution. This is genuine doctrinal tension, not a verified square federal appellate conflict. The reply's pp. 7–8 characterization does not eliminate that distinction. The separate Bates neutrality conflict remains contested by the parties, not independently established here.

**Postjudgment developments make this a less useful vehicle.** The BIO, printed pp. 10–14 and 23–24, reports reinstatement, permission to avoid pronouns by using names, and revision of the parent-communication policy after Mirabelli. The reply, printed pp. 10–12, disputes the adequacy of those changes, including parental-request and principal-consultation conditions. I do not assume the new policy has eliminated every burden. Nevertheless, a case about a now-accommodated substitute and materially revised rules is less attractive for a definitive national rule than a live disciplinary dispute under an unchanged policy. The parties do **not** present mootness as an established jurisdictional fact: the BIO says the developments may not moot the case, and the reply emphasizes that concession. Denial for vehicle reasons is more likely than dismissal on a proven absence of a live controversy.

**The case is interlocutory and includes preservation questions.** The petition, printed pp. 8–10, and BIO, printed pp. 7–9, explain that the appeal arose from denial of a preliminary injunction, with pendent review of dismissal of the constitutional claims while a Title VII claim continued. The parties dispute preservation of the Barnette-based religious-exercise argument. The Fourth Circuit did not reach whether Pickering supplies the proper standard for compelled speech once Garcetti is inapplicable. Those issues need not foreclose review, but they give the Court reasons to await a cleaner record.

**Mirabelli supports a limited remand more readily than automatic plenary review.** Both filings describe that intervening decision as granting relief with respect to parents, not deciding these teachers' claims. The BIO's printed p. 13 footnote 2 expressly acknowledges remand as appropriate if the Court thinks the free-exercise challenge should be reconsidered in its light. This preserves a material GVR possibility, but not a prediction that Mirabelli necessarily controls or that a summary merits reversal is straightforward. The external web attempts returned no usable material, so my account of that decision rests on the provisioned filings, not an independently verified new opinion.
