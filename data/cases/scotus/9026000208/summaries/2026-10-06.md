## In brief

George Anibowei asks the Court to decide whether border agents need a warrant to search the digital contents of travelers' cell phones.

## What happened

George Anibowei, an immigration attorney and U.S. citizen, says border agents searched his cell phone without a warrant five times as he reentered the country, once copying all its data, including confidential client communications. He sued to challenge Customs and Border Protection and Immigration and Customs Enforcement policies allowing warrantless phone searches. The district court dismissed his claims, holding itself bound by Fifth Circuit precedent rejecting a warrant requirement for border phone searches. The Fifth Circuit affirmed, agreeing that its prior decisions foreclosed his argument, and later denied rehearing before the full court.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked whether government border agents may examine everything stored on a traveler's phone without first getting a warrant, or whether the privacy interests in digital data require one, as the Court has required in other contexts involving searches of cell phone contents.

## Where it stands

The petition, along with numerous amicus briefs supporting Anibowei, was distributed for the Court's October 9, 2026 conference. Shortly afterward, the Court requested a response from the federal respondents, due October 29, 2026, even though they had earlier waived their right to respond.

## What each outcome would mean

Anibowei, who lost in the lower courts, is asking the Court to take up his case. If the Court grants review, it would hear full arguments and decide whether warrantless border searches of cell phone contents violate the Fourth Amendment, without yet deciding who wins. If it denies review, the Fifth Circuit's ruling allowing such warrantless searches stands, though that denial would not mean the Court agrees with that result.
