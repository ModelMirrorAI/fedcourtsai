# Predicted reasoning — Anibowei v. Blanche, No. 26-208

**Call: certiorari denied (P(grant) ≈ 0.12).**

**Where the docket stands.** The petition was distributed once, for the
10/9/2026 conference. The Court then called for a response on 9/29/2026 (due
10/29/2026), after the federal respondents had waived on 9/14. No CVSG is on
the docket. Ten amicus briefs were filed in support of the petitioner.

- **Further distributions.** Close to certain (≈0.95). Once the Solicitor
  General's brief in opposition is filed (or the time to file it runs out),
  the petition will be distributed again, probably for a November or December
  conference. One further distribution followed by a denial is the modal
  path. A relist after that conference would be the signal for a grant.
- **CVSG.** None. The federal government is the respondent, so its views
  will already be in the brief in opposition.
- **The opposition's likely content (context, not a forecast of its text).**
  The government will probably not deny the split. Its own 2021 petition in
  *United States v. Cano* described the split as entrenched. It is more
  likely to argue vehicle problems: a civil APA challenge to the CBP/ICE
  directives as a whole, rather than one concrete search; questions about
  standing for prospective relief; and an APA adequate-remedy (§ 704)
  alternative ground that the district court adopted and the Fifth Circuit
  declined to reach. It may also argue that the split rarely matters in
  practice because of good-faith reliance in criminal cases. Here, though,
  that point cuts toward taking a civil vehicle.
- **If granted.** The Court would take the single QP as written, likely with
  attention to the manual versus forensic distinction. Plenary review would
  follow. A GVR or summary disposition is very unlikely, because no
  intervening decision bears directly on the question (the 2026 *Chatrie*
  decision is related doctrine, not a controlling one).
- **Separate writing on a denial.** Possible but not likely (≈0.15). The
  likeliest writers are Justice Gorsuch (property and digital Fourth Amendment
  themes, his *Carpenter* writing) and Justice Sotomayor, with Justice Jackson
  next. A statement respecting denial that flags the question for a better
  vehicle (for example, a criminal case that has a warrant-requirement holding
  on its facts) is the most plausible form.
