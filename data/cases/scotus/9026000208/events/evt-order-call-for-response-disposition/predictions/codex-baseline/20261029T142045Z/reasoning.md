# Rationale

## Forecast and information set

I assign **25% to any grant of review**, including a summary grant, and predict denial. This is a cert-stage, response-requested cell, not a prediction about whether the Court will request a response: that request already occurred on September 29, 2026.

I used the provisioned **2026-09-30.json** snapshot and its matching context. The context identifies forward mode, a September 30 date cutoff, sal-v4 baseline band, one distribution, no CVSG, and docket-number Term 2026. The single distribution was entered September 23 for the October 9 conference; a scheduled conference is not proof that the petition was considered there. Ten distinct amicus-brief entries appear before the call for response. The requested response was due October 29. No response text is provisioned, and I do not infer its arguments or whether it has subsequently been filed.

I read the questions presented, the petition's factual, procedural, split and vehicle discussions, and the appendix's panel opinion and relevant district-court discussion. The petition is not truncated or OCR-derived. The appendix is marked truncated, so the later reproduced record and policies should not be treated as exhaustively available. The document manifest lists no brief in opposition, reply, or related-docket snapshot. A post-cutoff retrieval date on an August filing does not make the filing itself post-cutoff evidence. I deliberately did not fetch this petition's current docket, disposition, later filings, or other cells' forecasts. I have no known outcome for this event, and encountered none.

## Statistical anchor

The committed statpack's **sal-v4 baseline bracketed reached rates**, not its terminal-baseline rates, supply the anchor. Pooling every displayed Term strictly before 2026, OT2017 through OT2025, gives **660 estimated grant-family outcomes / 12,913 weighted resolved petitions = 5.1111%**. I computed this from `metrics/statpack.json` using each baseline segment's `prefix_est_grant_rate` and `prefix_weighted_resolved`; the Markdown table describes the population and window. The petitioner is a private individual. Federal respondents do not turn him into a federal petitioner, and I retain the harness's baseline band rather than reclassifying it because of the amici.

The whole modern-cert table is a low-single-digit grant-family population, but is less appropriate than this conditioned anchor. The paid segment's terminal zero-relist bucket is only a cross-check: **10,203 weighted resolved**, with approximately 1.8% grants plus GVRs. It conditions on where petitions ended and is not the hazard faced by a petition awaiting the government's requested response. The CVSG cut has **164 weighted resolved** and about 34.8% grants plus GVRs, but this case has no CVSG; a call for a response is not that signal. The two-or-more-amici bucket has **1,449 weighted resolved**, with about 22.9% grants plus GVRs. Ten amici put this petition well within that bucket, but the published cut is terminal and marginal, not a calibrated ten-amici-plus-response-request rate. I do not multiply these correlated cuts.

These are figures in the committed pack available to this run, not a claim to freshly recomputed corpus statistics. `corpus-info` failed because the cell's service backend exposes no client-side connection, so the newest corpus pull/snapshot vintage could not be established. The case baseline's own stated vintage is September 30, 2026; the manifest dates the petition and appendix retrievals October 5 and October 11. The historical query rows' pull stamps were withheld under the retrieval clock. That limits freshness claims, not the ability to use the supplied pack as the instructed anchor.

## Why above the anchor

- **A substantial issue and unusually broad interest.** The petition asks whether border agents may search the digital contents of a traveler's phone without a warrant. Its ten docketed amici include legal-profession, civil-liberties and press-related organizations. That is substantially more organized interest than an ordinary baseline petition, though I have not read the amicus briefs and do not attribute particular arguments to them.
- **A genuine but narrower conflict than the headline.** The petition, pp. 9-11, identifies disagreement about forensic searches, permissible investigative purposes and required suspicion. It describes *United States v. Cano*, 934 F.3d 1002 (2019), as imposing a contraband-focused limit and a reasonable-suspicion requirement for forensic searches. On the petition's account this is meaningful national nonuniformity, but I could not independently retrieve the underlying appellate opinions. It is not a clean split between circuits requiring warrants for all phone searches and circuits never requiring them: the petition itself acknowledges agreement that manual searches can be routine, including its discussion of *Belmonte Cardozo*. The broad question presented therefore somewhat outruns the narrowest live disagreements.
- **An improved procedural vehicle.** The appendix panel opinion, pp. 1a-4a, affirms on circuit precedent governing phone searches, rather than the district court's alternative adequate-remedy ground. This is a final civil policy challenge rather than a criminal suppression case, reducing good-faith/exclusionary-rule obstacles. The petition's account of repeated searches and retained client data makes the stakes concrete. I do not assume that all possible standing, policy-replacement or remedial objections have thereby disappeared.
- **The Court affirmatively sought adversarial briefing.** A call following a federal waiver is real evidence that the petition attracted attention. It does not show that four Justices favor review. There is no published call-for-response-conditioned rate, so moving from roughly 5% to 25% is a judgmental adjustment for this bundle of features, not a claimed empirical estimate of that signal's effect.

## Why denial remains more likely

The decision below is unpublished and applies existing precedents rather than announcing a new doctrinal departure. The petition's desired general warrant requirement is broader than the demonstrated disagreements about investigative scope and forensic suspicion. Its own discussion of *Riley v. California*, 573 U.S. 373 (2014), supplies a strong analogy, but Riley addressed searches incident to arrest, not border searches; extending it would require substantive adjudication rather than an obvious ministerial correction.

Historical corpus lookups of two related-issue petitions show that *Merchant v. Mayorkas*, No. 20-1505, and *United States v. Cano*, No. 20-1043, were both denied on June 28, 2021. These are issue-context priors, not a random sample or this petition's outcome. They caution against treating the longstanding conflict alone as sufficient for review. The current petition's own earlier interlocutory cert denial, disclosed in the provisioned petition, is also not the present event's outcome and does not settle this final-judgment petition.

Together those considerations keep my grant estimate below one in three despite the strong attention signals. Most of the 25% grant mass is plenary review; the conditional summary-route estimate is 8% because no supplied intervening decision provides a natural GVR hook and the extension of existing law is contested.

## Additional claims and limitations

The 98% further-distribution probability refers to an additional distribution entry after the snapshot's one, usually to put the petition back on a conference list after the response. It does not mean 98% confidence in a substantive post-conference relist; the harness masks this increment at this moment. A separate CVSG is very unlikely, 1%, because the United States is already responding as a party. Conditional on denial, I put a statement or dissent at 12%, mostly because digital privacy is significant, while expecting a silent denial. The low per-Justice writer probabilities are unconditional, independent forecasts, not a categorical author assignment; Sotomayor, Gorsuch and Jackson receive the largest but still small values. I make no cert vote-count prediction.

The 0.82 big-case score describes potential nationwide digital-privacy stakes, not grant likelihood. A high-stakes case can still be denied. My main uncertainties are the government's unseen vehicle arguments and whether enough Justices see the different circuit rules as a question this petition cleanly presents. The CourtListener MCP lookup for *Aigbekaen* was throttled; I used no direct REST fallback. All five general web calls returned no visible usable result text. I therefore rely on the provisioned petition for its characterization of Cano and Riley, not on a claimed independent reading of either opinion. The appendix's reproduced decision below is available and supplies the more reliable check on this case's actual posture.
