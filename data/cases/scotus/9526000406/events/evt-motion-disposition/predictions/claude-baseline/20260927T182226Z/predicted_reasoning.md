# Forecast: DHS v. D.V.D., No. 26A406 (application for a stay)

**Vantage.** The provisioned snapshot ends at the entry that opened this event: the
government's stay application, submitted to Justice Jackson on September 24, 2026.
At that entry the record shows no response requested, no referral to the full
Court, and no amicus entries. The claims below are increments from that state.

## Disposition

**The Court will grant the stay in full**, staying the district court's February 25,
2026 judgment (the classwide declarations and the universal vacatur of the DHS
third-country-removal guidance) pending the filing and disposition of a petition for
certiorari. P(unqualified grant) = 0.74. The order will most likely issue as a short
unsigned order from the full Court, with a noted dissent from the three Justices who
dissented from the June 23, 2025 stay in the same case (Sotomayor, Kagan, Jackson),
Justice Sotomayor writing. A qualified order (a stay of the vacatur but not of the
class declarations, or relief carved around the four named plaintiffs, whom
§ 1252(f)(1) permits individual relief for) is the main alternative to a clean grant;
outright denial is the second.

Conditional content: if the Court grants, the ground it will signal is the one its
June 2025 order already implied, that the government is likely to prevail on the
jurisdictional channeling and § 1252(f)(1) arguments, and that the district court's
relabelling of the same relief as declaratory judgments plus vacatur does not escape
the bar the Court found likely to apply at the preliminary-injunction stage. I do not
expect the Court to treat the application as a petition for certiorari and grant
review in the same order; the ordinary cert timeline from the First Circuit's
September 18 judgment is available and the government has not asked for that.

## The escalation ladder

- **Response requested.** Yes. Justice Jackson called for a response later on
  September 24, due 4 p.m. September 28. This rung has already fired relative to the
  provisioned baseline, so the increment resolves true (P = 0.97, the residual being
  resolution-mechanics uncertainty, not doubt about the fact).
- **Referral to the full Court.** Yes (P = 0.93). Solicitor General applications with
  a response called for are referred rather than decided in chambers as a matter of
  course; the prior application in this case was referred on the day it was decided.
  The referral will most likely appear on the docket the day the Court rules, as it
  did in 24A1153, rather than as an early standalone entry. The government's request
  that the administrative-stay question be referred separately will probably not
  produce a separate referral entry: Justice Jackson took no action on the
  administrative stay, and the Court is more likely to resolve the whole application
  than to act on the administrative request in isolation.
- **Amicus entries.** The count will rise from zero (P = 0.88). The prior application
  in this case drew three amicus briefs (two supporting respondents, one supporting
  the government) within the response window. Expect two to six entries here, most
  filed around the September 28 response deadline, with a reasonable chance of a
  brief from the immigration-judge or refugee-studies organizations that appeared
  last time and from at least one organization supporting the government.

## Timing

Response due September 28; a government reply within a day or two. The June 2025
stay in this case issued nineteen days after the response deadline. I expect the
disposition between October 5 and October 20, 2026, with the second week of October
most likely. Earlier action (an administrative stay by the full Court before the
response arrives) is possible but less likely than the Court simply waiting for the
response: Justice Jackson took no action on the administrative-stay request and set a
four-day schedule, and the full Court did not enter an administrative stay in the
prior round either.
