# Forecast: stay granted

The Court will grant the government's application to stay the district court's February 25, 2026 order pending the timely filing and disposition of a certiorari petition and any resulting Supreme Court proceedings. The probability of an unqualified grant is **86%**. A disposition granting only part of the relief and expressly denying the remainder belongs to the ungranted side of this forecast under the interim resolver's denial-first rule. An administrative pause alone is not my forecast of the ultimate substantive disposition.

## Procedure from the arrival record

The baseline contains only the September 24 submission to Justice Jackson. No response has been requested, no referral to the full Court is recorded, and the amicus count is zero.

- **Response requested: 96%.** Expect a short response deadline before the substantive stay is resolved.
- **Referral to the full Court: 98%.** Expect Justice Jackson to refer the application rather than finally dispose of this nationwide dispute alone.
- **Amicus count increases: 65%.** Expect at least one additional qualifying amicus entry or distinct submitted brief, with one to three the central count forecast. The short briefing window leaves a substantial possibility of none; submitted briefs count without awaiting acceptance.
- **Timing and form.** Expect the substantive disposition within roughly one to two weeks of the September 24 application, approximately by October 8, through a short order rather than plenary merits adjudication. An interim administrative stay may bridge the response period. Expect some recorded disagreement, but not a full majority opinion resolving all the statutory and constitutional questions.

## Likely basis

The decisive consideration will be continuity with the Court's earlier intervention against substantially similar restrictions, coupled with the disruption of removal operations alleged to follow the sudden dissolution of the appellate stay. The Court will regard the government's objections to classwide restraints and the statutory route for review as sufficiently substantial to preserve its ability to review the judgment before the new relief operates nationwide.

A stay will suspend the operative judgment, not finally hold that every third-country removal procedure is lawful. The distinction between an injunction and declaratory relief or vacatur, and the separate statutory grounds for the final judgment, remain questions for subsequent review. A contrary disposition would most plausibly reflect the Court's acceptance of those distinctions or greater weight on the risk of irreversible removal without an adequate opportunity to assert protection claims.
