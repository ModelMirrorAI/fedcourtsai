# Forecast: DHS v. D.V.D., No. 26A406 (application for a stay)

**Disposition.** The full Court will grant the Solicitor General's application
and stay the District of Massachusetts's February 25, 2026 final judgment
(the classwide declarations and the universal vacatur of the third-country
removal Guidance) pending the filing and disposition of a petition for
certiorari. I expect an unqualified grant, as in June 2025, rather than a
partial one: the Court stayed the preliminary injunction in this same case in
full over the same Section 1252(f)(1) and jurisdiction-channeling arguments,
and the SG's latest application adds a clean statutory point (Section
1231(h)'s bar on reading Section 1231(b) to create enforceable procedural
rights) that the lower courts conceded they were reading past the text to
reach. The order will be short or unexplained, will recite that the
application was presented to Justice Jackson and by her referred to the
Court, and will most likely issue in October 2026, roughly two to four weeks
after the response falls due on September 28 (the 2025 stay took about four
weeks from filing). The main alternative shape is a grant that also treats
the application as a petition and sets the case for argument this Term; that
still resolves as a grant.

**Response requested.** Already on the record: Justice Jackson called for a
response on the day of filing, due September 28 at 4 p.m. No further request
is possible from this vantage, so the increment claim is vacuous for this
cell.

**Referral to the full Court.** Justice Jackson will refer the application
to the Court rather than act alone. A contested SG application with a
requested response is not disposed of in chambers in this era, and the
government's own request that an administrative stay be referred to the full
Court if the Circuit Justice declines to enter one gives her a second reason
to refer. The referral will appear either as its own docket entry or in the
disposing order's "by her referred to the Court" recital.

**Administrative stay.** The snapshot shows Justice Jackson requested a
response without entering the requested immediate administrative stay. I
expect the Court, not the Circuit Justice, to resolve the administrative-stay
request, and to fold it into the disposition on the merits of the application
rather than enter a separate interim order, given the short response window.

**Amicus briefs.** The record shows none. I expect at least one amicus filing
before disposition, most likely on the respondents' side around the
September 28 response deadline (immigration-law and former-immigration-judge
groups, possibly a state coalition supporting the government), so the count
will rise from zero. This is the least certain of the ladder claims because
the window is short.

**Separate writings.** Justice Sotomayor will dissent, joined by Justices
Kagan and Jackson, as in the June 23, 2025 stay in this case. Nothing about
the writings is scored at this stage.

**If certiorari follows.** The government will file a petition, the Court
will grant it, and the most likely merits ground is that Section 1252(f)(1)
bars classwide declaratory relief and universal vacatur that "restrain" the
operation of Section 1231(b), with Section 1231(h) as a second ground against
the statutory-procedure holding. That is conditional context, not a claim
this event resolves.
