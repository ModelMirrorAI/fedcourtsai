# Forecast

The Court will grant the government's application to stay the district court's February 25, 2026 order pending the timely pursuit and disposition of certiorari. Probability of an unqualified grant is **84%**. This predicts interim relief, not a final merits judgment or a certiorari grant. A mixed grant-and-denial order would count as ungranted under this event's contract.

Justice Jackson has already requested a response, due September 28, 2026 at 4 p.m. EDT. That rung is complete: the response-requested increment is stated as 0% and is vacuous under the harness's frozen context, not a prediction that no response will be filed. The record shows no referral to the full Court and no amicus entries. I forecast full-Court referral with **97%** probability. I expect at least one additional amicus entry, with **60%** probability that the count rises above zero, counting submitted briefs under the declared counting rule.

I expect a disposition after the response deadline, most likely between September 29 and October 9, 2026. A temporary administrative stay may bridge consideration of the application; that would not itself settle my forecast of the substantive stay. The principal grant should take the form of a short order, with a separate dissent more likely than a comprehensive majority explanation.

If relief is granted, the likely rationale is preservation of the operative removal policy while the Court reviews serious jurisdictional and remedial questions. The government's application describes earlier stays in this litigation and attributes concrete removal-flight and diplomatic disruption to the renewed order (application, pp. 1–6, 13–16, 37–42). I forecast that continuity with the earlier interim posture will outweigh the changes in the final judgment's legal and remedial form. The Court need not decide at this stage that every aspect of the government's third-country removal procedures satisfies due process. The strongest alternative is a narrower order preserving particular protections while staying other relief, which would lose the unqualified-grant binary if the disposing entry also denies part of the application.
