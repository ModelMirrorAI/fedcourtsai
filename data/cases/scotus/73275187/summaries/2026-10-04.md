The Supreme Court will decide how courts must judge whether a forfeiture punishment is unconstitutionally excessive under the Eighth Amendment.

## What happened

Alaska state troopers seized Kenneth Jouppi's airplane after he was convicted of a misdemeanor for flying a passenger's alcohol into a village that had banned alcohol. Alaska law requires forfeiture of any aircraft used to import alcohol into such a community. Jouppi argued the forfeiture of his $95,000 plane was an excessive fine given that the offense involved only a six-pack of beer. The trial court agreed, Alaska's Court of Appeals sent the case back for more fact-finding, and the Alaska Supreme Court ultimately ruled that the forfeiture was constitutional as a matter of law, without further findings.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked whether, in deciding if a punishment is grossly out of proportion to an offense, judges should measure the seriousness of the offense by looking at the specific person's actual conduct and culpability, or instead by looking at the offense in general, abstract terms that focus on the type of crime and the legislature's broader concerns rather than what the particular person did.

## What each outcome would mean
