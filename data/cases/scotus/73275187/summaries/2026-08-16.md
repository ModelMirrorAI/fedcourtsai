## In brief

The Supreme Court agreed to decide whether a court evaluating a forfeiture as an excessive fine must weigh the specific defendant's own conduct rather than a generic worst-case violator.

## What happened

Alaska charged bush pilot Kenneth Jouppi with a misdemeanor after troopers found beer aboard his plane, bound for a village that had banned alcohol. Under state law, an airplane used to import alcohol into such a community must be forfeited. Jouppi argued forfeiting his $95,000 plane over a six-pack of beer was an excessive fine barred by the Eighth Amendment. Alaska's trial court agreed, but the Alaska Supreme Court held the forfeiture was constitutional, reasoning that even minor alcohol importation causes serious harm to rural communities. Jouppi asked the U.S. Supreme Court to review that decision.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked to decide how judges should measure whether a fine or forfeiture is unconstitutionally excessive: by looking at the seriousness of the specific person's actual conduct, as Jouppi argues many courts do, or by looking more broadly at the general dangers of the type of offense, as Jouppi says the Alaska Supreme Court did. Jouppi contends this approach conflicts with how other state and federal courts, including the federal appeals court covering Alaska, apply the same constitutional standard.

## Where it stands

The Court granted the petition for certiorari on July 20, 2026, meaning it will hear full briefing and argument on the question. A motion to dispense with printing a joint appendix was filed afterward, but the case has not yet been argued or decided.

## What each outcome would mean

Jouppi, the petitioner, asked the Court to take up his case after losing before the Alaska Supreme Court. Because the Court granted review, it will now decide the legal question itself. If the Court ultimately rules for Jouppi, it would find the Alaska Supreme Court applied the wrong standard, likely sending the case back for a new look at whether his forfeiture was excessive. If it rules for Alaska, the state's approach and the forfeiture of Jouppi's plane would be upheld. The Court could also vacate and send the case back without resolving the question for either side.
