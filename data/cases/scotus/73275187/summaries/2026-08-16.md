## What happened

Kenneth Jouppi, a longtime Alaska bush pilot, flew a passenger toward Beaver, a village that bans importing alcohol. Troopers found beer aboard his plane, and Jouppi was convicted of misdemeanor alcohol importation. Under Alaska law, an aircraft used to bring in alcohol must be forfeited. Jouppi argued that forfeiting his $95,000 airplane over a six-pack of beer was an unconstitutionally excessive fine. A trial court agreed, but the Alaska Court of Appeals sent the case back for more fact-finding, and the Alaska Supreme Court ultimately ruled that the forfeiture was constitutional as a matter of law, without further proceedings.

## What the Court is being asked

The case turns on how courts should judge whether a fine or forfeiture is too harsh compared to the offense it punishes. Jouppi's petition says the Alaska Supreme Court looked only at how serious this type of offense could be in general, rather than at how serious his own actual conduct was. Alaska responds that its court did consider Jouppi's specific circumstances and correctly applied the same multi-factor approach used elsewhere. The Court is asked to decide which approach is the right way to measure how severe an offense was when weighing whether a fine or forfeiture went too far.
