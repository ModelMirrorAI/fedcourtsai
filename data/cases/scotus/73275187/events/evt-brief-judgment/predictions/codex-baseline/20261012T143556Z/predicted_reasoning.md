# Forecast

The Court will **vacate the Alaska Supreme Court's judgment and remand** for a proportionality analysis that accounts for Jouppi's own conduct and culpability. The forecast is **8–1**, with Roberts, Thomas, Sotomayor, Kagan, Gorsuch, Kavanaugh, Barrett, and Jackson in the majority and Alito dissenting. Authorship and separate-writing roles are not forecast.

The majority will reject an analysis that substitutes the aggregate harms of alcohol abuse for the gravity of this defendant's offense. A knowing violation and the aircraft's use as an instrumentality will remain important, but neither will eliminate meaningful review of the magnitude of the punitive forfeiture. The Court will retain the demanding gross-disproportionality standard and substantial respect for legislative judgments, not replace that standard with judicial selection of an ideal fine. This is a prediction about how the Court will apply the competing readings of Bajakajian presented in the merits briefs, not a claim that the precedent already decides this aircraft case. See petitioner's merits brief, printed pp. 10–13; respondent's merits brief, pp. 30–36.

The opinion's rule will extend beyond Jouppi: individualized conduct must inform proportionality review of punitive forfeitures. Its remedy will nevertheless be limited. The Court will not hold that every misdemeanor instrumentality forfeiture is unconstitutional, impose a fixed forfeiture-to-fine ratio, or finally determine how much of the passenger's beer Jouppi knowingly transported. It will leave the ultimate excessiveness determination to the state courts. That scope accords with the methodological question presented and the unresolved factual distinctions described in the appendix, pp. 24a–26a.

The predicted dissent will emphasize the plane's direct role in a knowing crime, the history of vessel forfeiture, legislative deterrence judgments, and the distinctive risks faced by remote dry communities—the principal grounds Alaska advances in its merits brief, pp. 16–18 and 47–49. A separate concurrence limiting the reach of the majority's rule is possible but is not part of the committed vote forecast.

Following the December 1, 2026 argument scheduled in the snapshot, expect a decision in spring 2027 or by the end of June 2027. A DIG remains a small alternative if the dispute reduces to fact-specific application of an agreed standard. An equally divided disposition is much less likely; the provisioned record supplies no affirmative reason to forecast a recusal.
