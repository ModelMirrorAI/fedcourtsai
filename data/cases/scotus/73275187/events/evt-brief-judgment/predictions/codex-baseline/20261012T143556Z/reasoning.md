I raise P(disturbed) to **0.80**. The strongest case-specific consideration is the state court's express conclusion that the unresolved individualized facts could not change its answer. Its opinion treats even a six-pack's importation as sufficient societal harm and declines remand to determine culpability for the remaining beer or a wider pattern of wrongdoing (appendix pp. 24a–26a). That creates a focused methodological target, not merely a request to reconsider an unfavorable factual assessment. The petitioner's proposed remedy is renewed analysis, not a demand that this Court itself finally adjudicate excessiveness (petitioner's merits brief pp. 10–13; respondent's brief p. 47).

Bajakajian supplies a plausible route to that correction: punitive forfeitures undergo gross-disproportionality review tied to the defendant's offense. But its distinction between the unreported currency and an actual instrumentality is a real limitation, not something to erase. I checked the precedent through CourtListener, including the instrumentality discussion and the majority's statement of the proportionality standard: United States v. Bajakajian, 524 U.S. 321, 333–39 & nn. 8–9 (1998), opinion IDs 118234 and 9433683.

Alaska's opposition keeps the forecast well below certainty. This is criminal forfeiture following a knowing-conduct conviction, not an innocent-owner civil seizure. The plane directly facilitated the prohibited transportation, unlike currency that merely triggered a reporting requirement. The State supplies historical vessel-forfeiture analogies, stresses threatened as well as realized harm, and argues that the state court already considered the relevant individual facts. It also invokes a $10,000 then-applicable maximum fine, not merely the $1,500 executed fine (respondent's merits brief pp. 16–18, 33–36, 46–49). The six-pack framing is not a finding that only six beers were aboard: the record describes 72 cans and a dispute about the extent of Jouppi's knowledge (appendix pp. 24a–25a). These points could support affirmance even if the Court endorses individualization in general.
