# Forecast

The Court will vacate the Alaska Supreme Court's judgment and remand for a new Excessive Fines Clause analysis. I expect an 8-1 division, with Justice Alito dissenting and the other eight Justices joining the judgment. A reversal label is possible, but vacatur is more likely because the Court can reject Alaska's methodology without itself deciding whether the airplane forfeiture is ultimately excessive on a fully individualized record.

The majority will read *United States v. Bajakajian* to require the gravity side of gross proportionality to focus on the actual defendant's conduct, culpability, relationship to other crime, and concrete harm. It will conclude that Alaska placed impermissible weight on the generalized harms of alcohol abuse, Jouppi's membership in the statutory target class, and comparison with the theoretical maximum fine, despite assuming that his culpability covered only a six-pack and was unconnected to other crime.

The holding will apply generally to punitive forfeitures, not just aircraft or Alaska's dry-community laws, but it will remain narrower than a ruling that this particular $95,000 forfeiture is unconstitutional. The Court will preserve substantial legislative latitude and the demanding gross-disproportionality threshold, then return the case for application of the defendant-specific rule. It will reach the single question presented and leave disputes about the amount of alcohol, other alleged conduct, livelihood, and the property's instrumentality role to the proceedings on remand. A DIG or equally divided affirmance is not a substantial possibility on the present record.
