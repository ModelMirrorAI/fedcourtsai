# Forecast: what the Court will do with No. 25-590, Aldridge v. Regions Bank

**State forecast from.** The docket shows three distributions (conferences of 1/9/2026, 4/2/2026, and 10/9/2026), a response requested after respondent's waiver, one private amicus brief (Samuel L. Bray) in support, a CVSG issued April 6, 2026, the Solicitor General's brief filed August 31, 2026, and petitioners' supplemental brief filed September 16, 2026. The petition is fully briefed for the October 9, 2026 conference.

**Disposition.** The petition will most likely be **denied** (P(any grant) = 0.30). The Solicitor General, invited for its views, recommended denial on vehicle grounds: the plan is a top-hat plan exempt from ERISA's fiduciary rules, Regions's fiduciary status is disputed and unresolved below, and the Fifth Circuit has vacated its panel decision in *Aramark v. Aetna* and taken the surcharge question en banc. The Court follows a Solicitor General denial recommendation in most CVSG cases, and here the recommendation comes with a concrete alternative vehicle on the horizon. If the Court does grant, it will be a plenary grant limited to Question 1 (whether surcharge is available under 29 U.S.C. § 1132(a)(3)), taken essentially as written or narrowed to suits against a fiduciary or trustee, leaving the fiduciary-status and liability questions for remand as in *Harris Trust* and *Mertens*. Question 2 (preemption) will not be granted under any scenario: no circuit conflict is alleged and the Solicitor General says the holding is correct.

**Relists.** Roughly even odds of at least one further distribution past the three the docket shows (P = 0.45). A CVSG'd petition is often resolved at the first conference after the government files, but two features push toward a relist here: the Court's demonstrated interest (it requested a response after a waiver, then called for the Solicitor General's views knowing the top-hat vehicle problem from the BIO), and the likelihood that a Justice writes a statement respecting denial, which routinely costs one or two relists. Expect either an order on the October 13, 2026 order list or one to two relists resolving by early November.

**CVSG.** Already on the docket (April 6, 2026); no further call for the government's views will issue.

**Summary disposition.** Conditional on a grant, a summary route is very unlikely (P = 0.04). No intervening decision of this Court bears on the split, so there is nothing to GVR in light of, and a per curiam reversal of a reasoned, published Sixth Circuit opinion on a question the Court itself left open in *LaRue* and discussed at length in *Amara* is not the Court's practice. A grant means briefing and argument.

**Dissent or statement respecting denial.** Conditional on a denial, P = 0.30 that some Justice writes. The most likely form is a statement respecting denial acknowledging the acknowledged conflict and the Solicitor General's concession that the Sixth Circuit erred, and signaling the Court will take the question in a suit against an undisputed fiduciary (the en banc Fifth Circuit's *Aramark* being the obvious candidate). Justices with a demonstrated interest in equitable remedies and their history (Gorsuch, Thomas, Barrett, Kavanaugh) are the candidates; that attribution is context only.

**If granted (conditional).** The likely ground would be that surcharge against a fiduciary or trustee for breach of duty was exclusively equitable at the divided bench and so falls within "appropriate equitable relief" under *Amara*, with *Montanile* read as reaffirming *Mertens* only as to nonfiduciaries; the Court would reverse the Sixth Circuit's categorical rule and remand for the courts below to decide whether Regions, as rabbi-trust trustee of an ERISA-exempt top-hat plan, is a fiduciary in the relevant sense and whether any plan term was violated.
