# Forecast: what the Court will do with No. 25-590, Aldridge v. Regions Bank

**Disposition: denied**, most likely at or shortly after the October 9, 2026 conference for which the petition is now distributed.

## Relists

The provisioned snapshot (cut at 2026-04-07) shows two distributions: for the January 9, 2026 conference (after which the Court called for a response Regions had waived) and for the April 2, 2026 conference (after which the Court called for the views of the Solicitor General). Forecasting from that two-distribution state, at least one further distribution is near-certain: the Court cannot act on the CVSG without redistributing after the Solicitor General's brief, and the public docket already shows that redistribution (September 16, 2026, for the October 9 conference). I expect the petition to be decided at that conference or after one more relist at most, so the terminal count will be three or four distributions.

## CVSG

Already on the docket (April 6, 2026). The Solicitor General filed on August 31, 2026 and recommended denial: the Sixth Circuit's categorical reading of "equitable relief" is wrong, but the top-hat plan and Regions's disputed fiduciary status make this a poor vehicle, and the preemption holding is correct and unconflicted. No further CVSG can issue.

## Which question, if any

If the Court grants, it will take Question 1 alone, likely as written or reframed to ask whether surcharge is "appropriate equitable relief" under 502(a)(3) in a suit against a trustee of a plan's rabbi trust. Question 2 (preemption of the state-law contract and trust claims) will not be granted on its own: petitioners allege no circuit conflict, and both the respondent and the United States say the Sixth Circuit applied settled Pilot Life / Davila preemption law.

## Summary disposition

Unlikely even conditional on a grant. There is no intervening decision of this Court to GVR in light of, and the Court does not summarily reverse on a question the Solicitor General has just described as a poor vehicle with an unresolved threshold issue (fiduciary status). A grant here would be plenary review on Question 1.

## Dissent or statement respecting denial

More likely than on an ordinary denial but still the minority outcome. The Solicitor General conceded the Sixth Circuit erred and that a genuine Fourth/Sixth versus Second/Seventh/Eighth/Ninth/Eleventh conflict exists, and the Fifth Circuit's Aramark panel decision has been vacated for rehearing en banc. That is the shape in which a Justice sometimes writes a short statement respecting denial noting that the surcharge question remains open and a cleaner vehicle is awaited. Justices Gorsuch or Thomas, who write on equity and remedies, or Justice Kavanaugh, who has written "wrong vehicle, right issue" statements, are the plausible authors. I put the chance of any such writing at about one in five.

## If granted (conditional)

If the Court takes Question 1, the likely ground is that surcharge against a defendant who is a trustee in the equity sense is "appropriate equitable relief" under CIGNA v. Amara, with Mertens and Montanile confined to non-fiduciary defendants, and a remand on whether Regions is such a fiduciary and whether a remediable violation of ERISA or the plan terms is alleged. That would be a reversal or vacatur of the Sixth Circuit on Question 1 with the preemption holding left in place.
