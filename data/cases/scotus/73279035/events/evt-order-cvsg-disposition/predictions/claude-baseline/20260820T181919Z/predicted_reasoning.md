# Forecast — Aldridge v. Regions Bank, No. 25-590 (CVSG moment)

**State forecast from:** the docket as of 2026-04-07 shows **two distributions**
(for the 1/9/2026 and 4/2/2026 conferences) and a **CVSG entered 2026-04-06**,
after the brief in opposition, a cert-stage amicus by remedies scholar Samuel
L. Bray, and the petitioners' reply were all on file.

**Disposition.** The Court is more likely than not to **deny** the petition,
but the grant-family probability is high for a cert docket: **0.36** that the
petition is granted in some form (plenary grant, grant-in-part, or GVR). The
question presented — whether surcharge is available as "appropriate equitable
relief" under ERISA § 502(a)(3) — is a deep, acknowledged split (petitioners
count six circuits allowing surcharge against the Fourth and Sixth denying
it), and the Court's own conduct (response requested after the respondent
waived, two conferences, then a CVSG entered with the vehicle objections fully
briefed) shows serious interest. The decisive branch point is the Solicitor
General's recommendation: the respondent's vehicle attack — a top-hat plan
exempt from ERISA's fiduciary duties, a non-fiduciary defendant, and an
unresolved antecedent liability question — is exactly the kind of objection an
SG brief converts into a "deny, await a better vehicle" recommendation, and the
Court usually follows the SG. If the SG instead says the question is ripe and
this vehicle adequate, a grant limited to QP 1 is the likely shape.

**Relists.** Expect the petition to be **distributed again with near
certainty** (claim: 0.96): the CVSG suspends the petition until the SG files,
and the invitation brief is then distributed for conference. For an April
CVSG, expect the SG's brief around **November 2026–January 2027**, one to two
further distributions, and a disposition in the **winter or spring of the 2026
Term**. Total further distributions: most likely 1–3.

**CVSG.** Already on the docket (2026-04-06) — that is this cell's moment. No
second invitation will issue (claim stated at 0.01; the harness masks it as
vacuous).

**Which question, if granted.** QP 1 as written — the availability of
surcharge under § 1132(a)(3) — is the cert-worthy question and the one the
CVSG is aimed at. QP 2 (preemption of state-law claims on the separate trust
agreement) is framed as conditional on losing QP 1; the likelier grant is
**limited to QP 1**, with QP 2 either denied or held for the answer to QP 1.

**Summary disposition.** Unlikely conditional on a grant (claim: 0.12). No
intervening decision of this Court bears on the split, so there is nothing to
GVR in light of today. The realistic GVR path is indirect: if a cleaner
fiduciary-context vehicle (e.g., a petition from the Fifth Circuit's Aramark
litigation) is granted and decided first, this petition would likely be held
and GVR'd — but that is a minority branch against a plenary grant or a
post-SG-brief denial.

**Dissent from denial.** If the Court denies — most likely following an SG
recommendation to deny on vehicle grounds — the modal denial is quiet, but
with an acknowledged split and a scholar amicus there is a modest chance
(claim: 0.18) of a statement or dissent respecting denial, most plausibly from
a Justice who writes on the scope of equitable remedies (Gorsuch or Thomas).
