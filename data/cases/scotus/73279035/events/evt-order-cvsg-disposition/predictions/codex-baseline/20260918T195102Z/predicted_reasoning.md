# Forecast at the CVSG moment

The Court will most likely deny certiorari after receiving the Solicitor
General's views. The probability of any grant, including a summary route, is
42%. The most likely obstacle is this petition's suitability for resolving
the broader surcharge conflict, rather than the insignificance of that conflict.
The top-hat-plan exemption, the separate trust agreement, and the unsettled
predicate for liability will make this a less attractive vehicle than a case
squarely involving ordinary ERISA fiduciary obligations.

The baseline contains **two distributions**, on December 16, 2025 and March 17,
2026, and the **April 6, 2026 CVSG**. The first distribution preceded the requested
opposition; these are not necessarily two completed deliberations on fully
briefed papers. From that frozen state, I expect at least one further
distribution, with probability **0.96**, and most likely one or two additional
distributions following the government's brief and supplemental party filings.
The CVSG has already occurred. I expect no additional invitation; the required
`cvsg-increment` value is **0.00**, and that claim is vacuous for this cell.

I expect the Court to await the government's submission rather than dispose of
the petition immediately. My timing forecast is an ordinary orders-list
disposition in late 2026 or the first half of 2027, with substantial uncertainty
about the government's filing schedule. This forecasts the path from the
provisioned CVSG moment; it does not assert that no subsequent filing has occurred.

If review is granted, the Court will most likely take the first question:
whether ERISA Section 502(a)(3) permits compensatory surcharge for a beneficiary
against a trustee, with attention to the significance of the defendant's duties.
I expect the Court to leave the second, alternative preemption question outside
the grant rather than use it as the principal reason for review. Plenary
briefing and argument are more likely than a disposition accompanying the cert
order: **P(summary route | grant) = 0.10**. The dispute calls for reconciliation
of equitable-remedies precedents, not straightforward application of a newly
identified controlling judgment.

If certiorari is denied, I expect an unexplained order without a separate
writing: **P(dissent or statement respecting denial | denial) = 0.12**. I do not
forecast individual cert votes or an opinion author. A denial would leave the
judgment below intact without endorsing its interpretation of ERISA.
