# Forecast

The petition will likely be denied after the Solicitor General files the invited brief. The docket showed two distributions when the Court issued its CVSG. Once the government responds, the petition is very likely to be distributed at least once more; one further distribution is the most likely path, with a second possible if the Court holds it for the Fifth Circuit's en banc proceedings in *Aramark Services v. Aetna Life Insurance Co.* A second CVSG is not expected.

If review is granted, the Court is most likely to take the first question in narrowed form: whether ERISA § 502(a)(3) permits make-whole surcharge against the trustee or administrator of a top-hat plan when ERISA itself supplies no fiduciary duty. The separate-contract preemption question is less likely to be taken because the petition identifies no developed circuit conflict on it.

Plenary review is substantially more likely than a summary disposition conditional on a grant. No controlling intervening Supreme Court decision supplies a natural GVR ground, and the antecedent questions about Regions Bank's fiduciary status and an actionable ERISA or plan violation resist summary treatment. If the petition is denied, no Justice is likely to note a dissent or statement respecting denial.

The Court's invitation reflects genuine interest in the conflict over surcharge, but the pending en banc reconsideration of *Aramark* and this case's top-hat-plan, nonfiduciary, predicate-liability complications make waiting for a cleaner vehicle more likely than granting this one.
