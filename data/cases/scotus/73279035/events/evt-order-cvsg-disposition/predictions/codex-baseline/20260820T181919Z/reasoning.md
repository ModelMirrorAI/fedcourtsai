# Rationale

I assign a 0.29 probability to any grant-family disposition and predict denial. The committed statpack gives two useful anchors. Across Terms 2017–2024, the `sal-v3` high-band risk sets pool to 439 implied grants among 1,074 weighted resolved petitions, about 40.9%. The paid scored-segment CVSG cut, which is especially relevant at this declared moment, records 57 grant-family dispositions among 163 resolved CVSG petitions, about 35.0%. I adjust below both anchors because a material piece of the asserted conflict is presently unstable and this petition has unusually serious vehicle defects.

The provisioned April 7 snapshot showed two distributions and the April 6 CVSG. That makes another distribution after the Solicitor General's brief almost mechanical absent withdrawal or dismissal, supporting 0.97 for `relist-increment`. Because a CVSG was already on the docket at prediction time, `cvsg-increment` is vacuous for scoring; the stated 0.01 represents the remote prospect of another post-prediction invitation rather than restating the existing one.

The petition and questions-presented text frame a substantial ERISA issue. Petitioners invoke *CIGNA Corp. v. Amara* and describe a six-to-two circuit split over whether surcharge is “appropriate equitable relief” under § 502(a)(3). They also identify a remedial vacuum: the Sixth Circuit preempted claims under the separate Alabama-law rabbi-trust agreement while refusing surcharge. Those features, the Court's response request, and the CVSG all push upward. The issue has national importance, which supports a 0.68 significance score, though the immediate dispute concerns a top-hat plan for highly compensated employees.

The brief in opposition supplies unusually forceful vehicle objections. Top-hat plans are exempt from ERISA's ordinary fiduciary duties; Regions argues that petitioners identify neither an ERISA violation nor a violated plan term, only alleged breaches of the separate trust agreement. The Sixth Circuit called predicate liability “far from clear.” The bankruptcy settlement and loss of the trust res further complicate causation and remedy. The second question has no asserted circuit split. `documents.json` reports complete extracted text for the 40-page opposition and questions presented, but the 118-page petition extraction is marked truncated; I therefore place less weight on any issue that may depend on omitted appendix material.

Live CourtListener research located the Fifth Circuit's *Aramark* docket. On April 28, 2026, that court granted rehearing en banc and vacated the panel opinion that the opposition had treated as the only post-*Montanile* pro-surcharge circuit authority directly engaging the dispute. En banc argument is set for September 24, 2026. That development does not reveal this case's outcome, but it reduces the maturity of the split and gives the Supreme Court a reason to wait, hold, or deny. It is the principal downward adjustment from the conditional-CVSG anchors.

Conditional on a grant, I put the summary-disposition route at 0.12. A plenary grant would be more coherent because no intervening Supreme Court decision supplies an obvious GVR basis and the antecedent fiduciary-status and liability questions require analysis. I put a separate writing accompanying denial at 0.04: the issue is important, but denial while related en banc litigation matures would not ordinarily invite a public dissent.

The largest uncertainty is the Solicitor General's eventual recommendation, which was not present in the provisioned record and was not located through the available CourtListener search. A recommendation to grant would move the estimate sharply upward; a recommendation to deny on vehicle or prematurity grounds would move it sharply downward.
