## In brief

Former Ruby Tuesday employees asked the Court to revive their lawsuit seeking lost retirement benefits from the bank that managed their plan; the Court declined to hear the case.

## What happened

Jerry Aldridge and other former Ruby Tuesday managers participated in company retirement plans for high-level employees, administered through a trust managed by Regions Bank. After Ruby Tuesday went bankrupt, the employees lost benefits they say Regions should have protected. They sued Regions, seeking "surcharge" (a monetary remedy) under a federal retirement-law provision, plus related state-law claims. The district court ruled against them, finding the state-law claims blocked by federal law and the monetary remedy unavailable under the federal provision. The Sixth Circuit affirmed both rulings.

## What the Court is being asked

The case raised two questions: first, whether employees may seek this kind of monetary compensation from a plan administrator under the federal provision allowing "equitable relief," given disagreement among appeals courts on that point; second, if not, whether federal law blocks employees from instead pursuing related claims under state contract law, potentially leaving them with no remedy at all.

## Where it stands

The Court asked the Solicitor General for the federal government's views, which recommended denying review, in part because the case's unusual plan type made it a poor vehicle for deciding the broader question. The Court denied the petition.

## What each outcome would mean

Aldridge and the other former employees asked the Court to hear the case. The denial leaves the Sixth Circuit's ruling in place: Regions prevails, and the employees cannot pursue either their federal claim for monetary relief or their state-law claims over the lost benefits. The denial does not mean the Supreme Court decided the underlying legal questions were correctly resolved; it simply leaves the lower court's judgment as the final word in this case.
