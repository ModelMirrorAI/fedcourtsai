## In brief

Former Ruby Tuesday executives ask the Court to revive their lawsuit seeking lost retirement benefits from Regions Bank, the trustee that managed their plans.

## What happened

Jerry Aldridge and other former Ruby Tuesday managers participated in company retirement plans for high-level employees, funded through a trust that Regions Bank managed. The petitioners say Regions failed to protect their benefits in several ways before Ruby Tuesday went bankrupt, causing them to lose money owed under the plans. They sued Regions under a federal pension law provision allowing "equitable relief," seeking a remedy called surcharge, and also brought state-law claims. The trial court dismissed the state-law claims as preempted by federal law and ruled surcharge was not available either. The Sixth Circuit affirmed both rulings.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked whether beneficiaries may recover a surcharge, a form of compensation for losses caused by a trustee's or fiduciary's breach, under the law's "appropriate equitable relief" provision, or whether that provision only covers certain narrower remedies. The Court is also asked whether, if that remedy is unavailable, the petitioners' separate state-law claims arising from their trust agreement are nonetheless blocked because federal pension law overrides state law in this area.

## Where it stands

The Court asked the Solicitor General to weigh in, and the federal government filed a brief recommending that the petition be denied, while also arguing the lower court's reasoning on surcharge was flawed. The petition was then distributed for the Court's October 9, 2026 conference, and the docket shows no action since.

## What each outcome would mean

Aldridge and the other petitioners are asking the Court to take up their case. If the Court grants review, it would hear full arguments and decide the legal questions itself, without deciding in advance who is right. If the Court denies review, the Sixth Circuit's ruling against the petitioners stands, leaving them unable to pursue either the surcharge remedy or their state-law claims against Regions Bank. A denial would not mean the Court agrees with the lower court, only that the lower court's decision remains in place.
