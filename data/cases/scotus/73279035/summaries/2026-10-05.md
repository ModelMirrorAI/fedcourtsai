## In brief

Former Ruby Tuesday employees ask the Court to revive their lawsuit against Regions Bank over mismanaged retirement benefits after a lower court barred their claims.

## What happened

Jerry Aldridge and other former Ruby Tuesday managers participated in company retirement plans funded through a trust that Regions Bank administered. After Ruby Tuesday was sold and later went bankrupt, the employees say Regions failed to honor several duties under the trust agreement, costing them benefits. They sued Regions, seeking money through a federal law called ERISA and also raising state-law claims. The district court dismissed everything, and the Court of Appeals for the Sixth Circuit affirmed, holding that the employees could not get the relief they sought under ERISA and that federal law barred their state-law claims.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked whether a plan participant suing under the ERISA provision allowing "equitable relief" can recover money representing lost benefits through a remedy called surcharge, when the defendant is not bound by ERISA's usual fiduciary rules. The Court is also asked, if that remedy is unavailable, whether state-law claims based on a separate trust contract are blocked by ERISA, potentially leaving the employees with no remedy under either body of law.

## Where it stands

The petition has been fully briefed, including an amicus brief and supplemental filings from the employees, and the Court invited the Solicitor General to weigh in; the government has since filed a brief. The case was distributed for a conference in October 2026, and the docket shows no action since.

## What each outcome would mean

The employees, as petitioners, are asking the Court to take up their case. If the Court grants review, it would hear full arguments and decide the legal questions itself, without yet determining who wins. If the Court denies review, the Sixth Circuit's decision affirming dismissal of the employees' claims would stand, leaving Regions Bank's win in place and the employees without the relief they sought in this suit.
