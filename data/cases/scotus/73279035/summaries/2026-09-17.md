## In brief

Former Ruby Tuesday executives ask the Court to revive their lawsuit against Regions Bank over lost retirement benefits.

## What happened

Jerry Aldridge and other former Ruby Tuesday managers participated in company retirement plans for highly paid employees, funded through a trust that Regions Bank managed. After Ruby Tuesday was sold and later went bankrupt, the employees say Regions failed to protect their benefits as the trust agreement required, causing them to lose money. They sued Regions under a federal retirement-law provision allowing "equitable relief," and also under state law. The trial court dismissed both types of claims, and the Sixth Circuit Court of Appeals affirmed, ruling that the remedy sought is not available under the federal provision and that federal law preempts the state-law claims.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked two things. First, whether employees may recover money to make up for lost benefits, through a remedy called surcharge, when suing under the federal law's equitable-relief provision, even against a party that is not a fiduciary with formal duties toward the plan. Second, if that money remedy is unavailable, whether federal retirement law nonetheless bars employees from pursuing separate state-law claims based on a contract connected to the plan, potentially leaving them no remedy at all.

## Where it stands

The petition has been distributed for conference multiple times, most recently for October 9, 2026, after the Court invited the Solicitor General to weigh in and received the government's brief. The docket does not yet show any decision on whether the Court will hear the case.

## What each outcome would mean

Aldridge and the other former employees are asking the Court to take up their case. If the Court grants review, it will decide the legal questions itself, deciding nothing in advance about who should win. If the Court denies review, the Sixth Circuit's ruling stands, meaning Regions Bank keeps its win and the employees' claims for lost benefits remain dismissed. A grant would not guarantee the employees ultimately recover anything; it would only mean the Court will resolve the legal questions on their merits.
