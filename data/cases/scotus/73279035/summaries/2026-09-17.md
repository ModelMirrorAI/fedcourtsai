## What happened

Jerry Aldridge and other former Ruby Tuesday managers participated in company retirement plans designed for highly paid executives, known as "top-hat" plans. Regions Bank administered a trust set up to hold funds for those plans. The employees say Regions mishandled the trust in several ways, including failing to secure funding after a change in company ownership and cutting off benefit payments improperly before Ruby Tuesday went bankrupt, causing them to lose retirement money. They sued Regions under a federal retirement-benefits law and under state law. The lower courts ruled entirely for Regions: a federal appeals court held that the type of relief sought is not available under the federal law's remedy provision, and separately that the state-law claims are blocked because federal law overrides them.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked whether employees in this kind of retirement plan can recover money from a plan administrator, framed as a traditional remedy for mismanaging trust funds, even though that administrator does not carry the usual duties of care that the retirement law normally imposes. The Court is also asked, if that remedy is not available, whether the employees may instead pursue their claims under state contract and trust law, or whether the federal retirement law's broad override of state law blocks that path too, potentially leaving them no remedy at all.

## Where it stands

The case has been distributed for conference more than once, and the Court invited the federal government to weigh in through the Solicitor General, who filed a brief; the petitioners then filed a further supplemental reply. The petition is now awaiting the Court's decision on whether to grant review.
