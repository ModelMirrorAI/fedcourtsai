Several strong negative factors warrant adjusting well downward from the 5.15% `baseline` reached anchor to **0.005** (0.5%):

1. **Solicitor General Waiver and Distribution Without a Response:** On October 29, 2026, the Solicitor General waived the United States' right to respond, and on November 4, 2026, the petition was distributed for the December 4, 2026 conference. Under Supreme Court practice, the Court virtually never grants plenary review without first calling for a response (or having a BIO on file). While the `baseline` band includes petitions where a BIO was filed before first distribution, a petition distributed on a waiver must first clear the low-probability hurdle of a Call for Response (CFR) before it has any realistic prospect of a grant.
2. **Conceded Absence of a Circuit Split:** The petition (`petition.txt` at 4, 6–8) and the District Court's opinion (Appendix B at 8a–9a) expressly acknowledge that there is no circuit conflict. Petitioner characterizes the question as a *res nova* issue arising in "very narrow" circumstances—where co-defendants whose direct appeals were consolidated into a single appellate opinion file *separate* certiorari petitions that are denied on different dates because one co-defendant obtained a 60-day filing extension. Corpus queries (`uv run fedcourts query` for prior dockets `22-508` and `22-729`) and a CourtListener search for § 2255(f)(1) co-defendant certiorari finality decisions confirm that lower courts uniformly hold that a defendant's § 2255(f)(1) clock is governed by his own direct-review finality, not a co-defendant's separate certiorari petition.
3. **Straightforward Application of Controlling Precedent (*Clay* and Rule 12.4):** Under *Clay v. United States*, 537 U.S. 522, 527 (2003), "[f]inality attaches when this Court affirms a conviction on the merits on direct review or denies a petition for a writ of certiorari, or when the time for filing a certiorari petition expires." Here, Petitioner filed his own certiorari petition (`No. 22-508`), which the Court denied on January 23, 2023 (*Green v. United States*, 143 S. Ct. 747 (2023)). Supreme Court Rule 12.4 expressly provides that parties interested jointly in a judgment may petition separately, and unlike Rule 13.3 (which ties the 90-day time to petition for certiorari to any party's timely rehearing petition in the lower court—as noted in *Kemp v. United States*, 596 U.S. 528, 532 (2022)), nothing in 28 U.S.C. § 2255(f)(1) or the Court's rules suspends the finality of one petitioner's judgment after his certiorari petition is denied merely because a co-defendant separately petitions for certiorari. Moreover, the Fifth Circuit's clerical transmittal letter of March 27, 2023 (Appendix D at 19a) explicitly noted that it attached the Supreme Court's order in *Selgas* (`No. 22-729`) and that the mandate had already issued previously.
4. **Poor Vehicle and Procedural Posture:** The decision below is a two-page unpublished single-judge order by Judge Edith H. Jones denying a COA under 28 U.S.C. § 2253(c)(2) and *Slack v. McDaniel*, 529 U.S. 473, 484 (2000). Under *Slack*, when a habeas petition is dismissed on procedural grounds without reaching the underlying constitutional claims, obtaining a COA requires showing both a debatable procedural ruling and a debatable underlying constitutional claim. Furthermore, Petitioner was sentenced in June 2021 to six months' imprisonment and three years' supervised release (`petition.txt` at 3 & n.4, 4a), creating potential custody/collateral-consequence vehicle complications.
