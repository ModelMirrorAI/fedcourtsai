# Forecast — Green v. United States, No. 26-531 (arrival moment)

**The Court will deny the petition.** P(any grant) = 0.01.

- **Distributions.** The docket was opened on Oct 19, 2026 and has not been distributed yet. I expect the petition to go to conference once, probably in late 2026 or early 2027, after the United States waives its response or lets the Nov 20, 2026 response date pass. I expect a denial at that first conference, with no relist. The `relist-increment` claim (at least one distribution after the zero the record shows) is close to certain, because nearly every petition reaches conference. The only exceptions would be a withdrawal or a dismissal before distribution.
- **Response.** I expect the Solicitor General to file a waiver. I do not expect the Court to call for a response.
- **CVSG.** No. The United States is the respondent, so the Court would not invite the Solicitor General's views.
- **Question the Court would take, if it granted (context only).** QP 1 as written: whether a conviction becomes final under § 2255(f)(1) when the defendant's own cert petition is denied, or only once a co-defendant's later-decided petition from the same consolidated appeal is also denied. The Court would leave QP 2, which asks whether a COA should issue, as the vehicle question.
- **Summary route, if granted.** Plenary review is a little more likely than a summary route. A GVR could happen if the Solicitor General confessed error on timeliness, but I see no intervening decision that would prompt one. I put the summary route at 0.35, conditional on a grant.
- **Separate writing on denial.** I do not expect a dissent from denial or a statement respecting denial.
