## Anchor and adjustment

Green is a private petitioner; the United States is the respondent, not a federal petitioner. The frozen band is `baseline` under `sal-v4`, matching the committed statpack's segment table. I use the private-caption arrival floor: the bracketed baseline reached rate, not its terminal-band rate or the terminal zero-relist bucket. Pooling every displayed strictly prior Term, 2017–2025, using the unrounded `prefix_est_grant_rate` and `prefix_weighted_resolved` fields in `metrics/statpack.json`, yields 644 grant-family outcomes over a denial-reweighted resolved denominator of 12,883, or 4.9988%. The 2026 row is excluded. This is the committed pack's estimate, not a fresh query of the live corpus; the pack does not state a build timestamp in its markdown header.

I reduce that approximately 5% anchor to **0.8% P(any grant)** for three principal reasons:

1. **No demonstrated conflict.** The petition, pp. 5–8, describes a previously unaddressed factual configuration, not opposing appellate holdings. Its request is for clarification of an unusually specific interaction between separate cert petitions after consolidated appeals. Novel facts alone do not establish a strong reason for discretionary review.
2. **The proposed distinction looks weak.** Clay v. United States, 537 U.S. 522, 527, 532 (2003), links collateral-review finality to the completion or expiration of certiorari review. Clay does not directly decide this two-petition configuration. Nevertheless, I infer that Green's own denial is the natural endpoint for his individual judgment; consolidation and a later administrative transmittal concerning Selgas do not convincingly reopen Green's completed review. The district court addresses that distinction and the underlying notices in Appendix B, pp. 8a–11a. Rule 13.3's common rehearing clock, as discussed in the petition and appendix, is not itself a rule postponing finality after a party's separate petition has been denied.
3. **The alternative COA question offers little independent leverage.** Slack v. McDaniel, 529 U.S. 473, 484 (2000), requires debatability of both the procedural ruling and a constitutional claim following a procedural dismissal. An absence of a case on identical facts does not by itself establish those requirements. Appendix A applies Slack expressly; the appellate order is terse, but it does not omit the governing test. The petition focuses on finality rather than developing the underlying constitutional claims. Both challenged rulings are unpublished, further limiting this petition's apparent usefulness as a conflict-resolution vehicle.

The alternative governmental-impediment theory was rejected below, but the petition's two questions do not independently present it. I therefore do not treat that rejection as a freestanding bar that would defeat any favorable finality ruling. The possibility of a corrective COA remand, together with genuine collateral-review access stakes, keeps the forecast above zero.

## Other probabilities and stakes

The modal path is one distribution and denial, with no actual relist. The statpack's terminal relist and CVSG cuts describe increasing attention but are not forward hazards from arrival, so I do not substitute their terminal rates for this claim. A CVSG is especially unlikely here (0.2%): the United States is already the respondent and can present its position through the ordinary response process; that response is not a CVSG.

Conditional on the rare grant, I put 60% on a cert-order disposition, principally a corrective COA remand rather than full argument. This is conditional probability, not an extra 60% unconditional chance of relief. I have identified no intervening decision furnishing a routine GVR basis. Conditional on denial, the probability of any noted dissent or statement is 1.5%; I expect no writing and forecast no individual Justice's vote.

The stakes score of 0.18 reflects a potentially generalizable access-to-collateral-review rule but a narrow configuration and no demonstrated broad conflict. It is separate from grant likelihood. My main uncertainty is whether the Court would view the COA threshold as sufficiently mishandled to warrant summary correction, not whether the appendix's earlier cert denials dispose of this new petition.
