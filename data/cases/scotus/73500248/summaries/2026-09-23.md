## What happened

Michael Webb, an incarcerated plaintiff, sued corrections officers Edmund Trombley and Donald Bovair for beating him during an intake interview at Great Meadow Correctional Facility. A jury awarded him $15,000 in damages. Webb's court-appointed lawyers then sought $206,977.50 in attorney's fees. Relying on Second Circuit precedent interpreting a federal prison-litigation law, the district court limited the fee award to $22,500 — 150% of the judgment. The Second Circuit affirmed, saying it was bound by its own prior rulings, and later denied rehearing. Webb now asks the Supreme Court to take up the case.

## What the Court is being asked

The case concerns a law limiting attorney's fees for prisoners who win civil rights lawsuits. That law says fee awards "not greater than 150 percent" of the money judgment must be paid by the losing defendant. Lower courts, including the Second Circuit, have read this to mean fees above that 150% level are barred entirely. Webb's petition argues the text only describes what happens when fees fall at or below that level and says nothing about larger awards, so courts have added a "cap" Congress never wrote. The Court is asked whether that law actually prohibits fee awards above 150% of the judgment, or whether courts have been misreading it.

## Where it stands
