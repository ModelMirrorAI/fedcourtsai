## In brief

A prisoner asks the Court to hold that a federal law does not cap the attorney's fees he may recover after winning his civil rights lawsuit.

## What happened

Michael Webb, an incarcerated plaintiff, sued corrections officers at Great Meadow Correctional Facility for using excessive force against him. A jury awarded him $15,000. His court-appointed lawyers then sought about $207,000 in fees under a federal fee-shifting law. The district court, citing binding Second Circuit precedent, limited the award to $22,500 — 150% of the judgment — based on a provision of the Prison Litigation Reform Act. The Second Circuit affirmed, saying it was bound by its own prior rulings, and later denied rehearing. Webb now asks the Supreme Court to review that limit.

## What the Court is being asked

The case turns on a sentence in a federal law governing fee awards in prisoner lawsuits. That sentence says that if a fee award is not more than 150% of the money a prisoner won, the losing defendant must pay it. Courts have read this to mean fee awards above that amount are barred entirely. Webb argues the sentence, read literally, says nothing about awards above that limit and does not forbid them. The Court is asked whether the law actually imposes this cap on larger fee awards.
