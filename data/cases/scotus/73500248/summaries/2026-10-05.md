## In brief

The Court declined to hear a prisoner's challenge to a federal law capping the attorney's fees he could recover after winning his civil rights lawsuit.

## What happened

Michael Webb, an incarcerated man, sued prison officials after a 2015 beating at Great Meadow Correctional Facility. A jury awarded him $15,000. His appointed lawyers then sought over $200,000 in fees, but the district court limited the award to $22,500—150% of the judgment—citing a federal law governing prisoner lawsuits and circuit precedent interpreting it as capping fees at that level. The Second Circuit affirmed, saying it was bound by that precedent, and later denied rehearing.

## What the Court is being asked

Webb's petition asks the Court to decide whether a federal statute governing attorney's fees in prisoner civil rights cases actually limits fee awards to 150% of the money a prisoner wins, or whether courts have wrongly read in a cap that the law's text does not contain. Webb argues the statute only addresses fees up to that amount and says nothing about larger awards, while lower courts have treated it as an absolute ceiling.
