# Forecast: what the Court will do with No. 25-1346, Webb v. Trombley

**Disposition.** The petition will be denied. The most likely path is a denial on the
order list following the September 28, 2026 conference (the long conference), i.e. the
October 5, 2026 order list. P(any grant, including a GVR) = 0.02.

**Relists.** The docket shows **one distribution** (July 1, 2026, for the September 28
conference) and no relist. I forecast that the petition is **not distributed again**
(P(at least one further distribution) = 0.15). The plausible route to a second
distribution is not a bare relist but a call for a response: New York waived on
June 24, and the Court almost never grants over a waiver without first asking the
respondent to answer. No response had been requested as of September 17, eleven days
before the conference, which is late for a petition the Court intends to look at
closely. If a response is requested, the petition would be redistributed in late 2026
after the brief in opposition arrives; that path, plus ordinary long-conference
volume relists, is what the 0.15 covers. I do not expect a second relist beyond that.

**CVSG.** No. The dispute is between a private prisoner-plaintiff and New York State
officers; the United States is not a party and the Solicitor General's views are not
needed to understand a one-sentence statutory question. P(CVSG after prediction time)
= 0.01.

**Which question, if granted.** The QP as written: whether the second sentence of 42
U.S.C. § 1997e(d)(2) caps a prisoner's fee award at 150% of the money judgment. There
is nothing to narrow; the petition presents a single, cleanly preserved question and
the Second Circuit's summary order rests entirely on its own precedent in *Shepherd v.
Goord*. The petition's "downstream splits" (appellate fees, pre-incarceration conduct,
injunctive-relief exceptions) would not be taken as separate questions.

**Summary disposition vs plenary.** If the Court granted, it would set the case for
argument. There is no intervening decision of this Court to GVR in light of, and the
Court would not summarily reverse eleven circuits' uniform reading. P(cert-order
disposition | grant) = 0.08, essentially the residual chance of a hold-and-GVR should
another petition on the same provision be granted first.

**Separate writing on denial.** Unlikely. P(some Justice notes a dissent from or
statement respecting denial | denial) = 0.04. If one appears, the likeliest form is a
short statement, either from a textualist Justice noting that the literal text of the
second sentence does not read as a cap, or from a Justice troubled by $1.50 fee awards
on nominal-damages verdicts; the more probable outcome is a silent denial.

**Conditional merits note.** If the Court took the case, the petitioner's literal
reading has real textual force, but the uniform circuit construction, the structure of
§ 1997e(d) as a restraint on § 1988 discretion (as *Murphy v. Smith* read it), and the
drafting history quoted in *Murphy*'s footnote 2 would weigh toward affirmance. That
is context only; no opinion issues on a denial.
