## Why 2.5%, rather than the 5.12% anchor

The petition presents a consequential, preserved, discrete statutory issue. Webb obtained a $15,000 excessive-force/failure-to-intervene damages verdict and sought $206,977.50 in attorney fees; the fee cap made the legal issue outcome-determinative. The petition's textual objection to construing a conditional payment direction as an absolute ceiling is intelligible, and the lower courts applied controlling precedent rather than rejecting preservation. These considerations keep the forecast above a near-zero error-correction estimate. Sources: petition pp. 5–12 and 20–22; Appendix A, pp. 2a–5a.

The stronger downward considerations are selection-related, not simply my assessment of who should win. Appendix A, footnote 3, states that every circuit reaching the cap question agrees. The petition's asserted Sixth/Ninth split concerns appellate work, while the pre-incarceration disagreement includes district courts opposing a circuit precedent; neither is the issue resolved by this trial-fee award. Its discussion of injunctive relief likewise concerns a different remedial setting. Thus the vehicle is clean for asking the Court to overturn a consensus, but poorly aligned with the concrete conflicts offered as a reason to intervene. The nonprecedential summary order and the response waiver, without a response request in the baseline, provide no affirmative signal of special Court interest. Sources: petition pp. 16–21 and Appendix A, pp. 4a–5a; snapshot proceedings.

I also checked Murphy v. Smith, 583 U.S. 220 (2018), through CourtListener. Its majority addresses the first sentence's allocation of fees against the prisoner's judgment, not the validity of the second sentence's 150% cap. The dissent's surrounding discussion expressly assumes that damages-linked cap. This is evidence that the cap is an entrenched background understanding, not a holding foreclosing Webb's question. It modestly reduces the likelihood that this challenge becomes an attractive summary disposition. Source: CourtListener opinion 4246854, opening majority discussion and Sotomayor dissent, slip pp. 11–12.

I set P(any grant) to 0.025 after weighing those modest positive vehicle and significance considerations against the absence of a direct conflict and an affirmative attention signal. The adjustment is judgmental, not a fitted estimate. The principal uncertainty is whether the textual objection attracts interest despite circuit consensus; the absent opposition prevents an adversarial check beyond the lower-court record.

The 10% additional-distribution and 0.8% CVSG probabilities are forward judgments, not terminal-table lookups. A response request could delay consideration, but is not itself a declared cert claim. Conditional on a grant, the 8% summary-route probability leaves 92% for plenary treatment; no specific companion case or new holding supplies a GVR mechanism here. The 2.5% conditional denial-writing probability reflects the possibility of attention to access-to-counsel concerns without asserting evidence of any Justice's present interest. These auxiliary probabilities are less firmly calibrated than the main disposition estimate.

The 0.45 significance score prices the nationwide implications for prisoner representation and civil-rights enforcement, independently of the low grant probability. No individual cert votes are supplied.
