# Forecast

The Court will most likely deny Michael Webb's petition without a separate writing. P(any grant), including a GVR or summary reversal, is **2.5%**. The most likely timing is an early-October 2026 order following the September 28 conference named in the snapshot; that is a forecast, not a retrieved calendar confirmation.

The snapshot records **one distribution**, on July 1 for the September 28 conference, and no CVSG. I expect **zero further distributions**. The probability of at least one additional distribution is **10%**; if the petition receives further consideration, one additional distribution is more likely than a long series. I expect no CVSG, with **0.8%** probability of a new invitation before disposition. State correctional litigation implicates federal fee-shifting law, but this petition does not present a distinct federal executive position that appears to require the Solicitor General's advice.

The principal obstacle is the difference between the broad question presented and the narrower disagreements invoked to justify review. The petition asks whether the second sentence of 42 U.S.C. § 1997e(d)(2) imposes any 150%-of-damages attorney-fee cap. The appended Second Circuit order describes agreement among the circuits on that proposition. Disputes about appellate fees, pre-incarceration conduct, and nonmonetary relief do not themselves create a conflict over the trial-fee award in this case. I expect the Court to leave that settled lower-court interpretation undisturbed rather than review this nonprecedential application of it. Denial would not endorse the interpretation on the merits.

If review is granted, I expect the Court to take the single statutory question substantially as presented, limited to the cap on fees associated with obtaining a monetary judgment, not to decide all the collateral exceptions. Plenary briefing and argument is the likelier grant route: **P(summary disposition in the cert order | grant) = 8%**. The record identifies no directly controlling intervening decision requiring a GVR, and overturning the shared circuit interpretation would call for fuller consideration rather than summary error correction.

Conditional on denial, the probability of a noted dissent or statement respecting denial is **2.5%**. I predict none and make no individual-Justice attribution. No merits opinion or vote lineup is forecast unconditionally.
