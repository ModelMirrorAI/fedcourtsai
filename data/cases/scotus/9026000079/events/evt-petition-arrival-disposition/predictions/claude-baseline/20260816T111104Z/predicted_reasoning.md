# Forecast: Acosta-Tapia v. Blanche, No. 26-79 (petition disposition, forecast at arrival)

The docket shows **zero distributions** — this is an arrival-moment forecast, made
before any conference signal exists. The respondent (the Solicitor General) waived
the right to respond on August 14, 2026, three days before the response was due.

**Disposition.** The Court will **deny** the petition. The petition challenges an
unpublished Ninth Circuit memorandum disposition (Hawkins, Rawlinson, Bress) that
*dismissed* — apparently on a threshold/jurisdictional ground — a petition for
review in an immigration removal case, decided without oral argument and with no
noted dissent. That is a weak vehicle from a high-volume, high-denial class.

**Distribution and timing.** With the response waived, the petition will be
distributed promptly for the late-September 2026 "long conference" (or the first
October conference) and I expect it to be **denied at or shortly after its first
conference — no relists**. The relist-increment probability I state (0.96) is the
probability the petition is distributed *at all* from its current zero-distribution
state, which is near-certain for a counseled paid petition that has reached the
waiver stage; it is not an expectation of repeated relisting.

**Call for response.** There is a modest chance (~5–10%) the Court calls for a
response before acting — the one path by which this petition's consideration
stretches into late fall. I do not expect it.

**CVSG.** None. A CVSG invites the Solicitor General's views in cases where the
United States is *not* a party; here the Attorney General is the respondent and
the SG has already appeared and waived. The cvsg-increment probability (0.005) is
effectively the structural floor.

**Which question presented, if taken.** The petition text is not available (filed
in paper form only), so this is inferred: the QP most likely challenges the Ninth
Circuit's threshold dismissal of the petition for review — plausibly a question
about the scope of judicial review under 8 U.S.C. § 1252. If the Court took any
question, it would be a reformulated, narrow jurisdictional one, not a factbound
challenge to the removal ruling itself. (Registered context-only; not scored.)

**Summary-disposition route.** Conditional on a grant-family outcome, I put 0.45
on the Court disposing of the case in the cert order itself — for this profile a
GVR in light of an intervening decision of the current or coming Term is at least
as likely as plenary review, since the Court rarely sets an unpublished,
non-conflict memorandum down for argument.

**Dissent from denial.** None expected (0.02). Statements respecting denial in
immigration cases do occur (most often from Justices Sotomayor or Jackson), but
this case has drawn no visible advocacy attention at the Court beyond a single
rejected out-of-time amicus below, and nothing marks it as the kind of vehicle
that prompts a separate writing.
