# Forecast: what the Court will do with No. 26-79, Acosta-Tapia v. Blanche

**Disposition.** The petition will be **denied**, most likely on the order list
following the September 28, 2026 long conference (the first order list of
October Term 2026), without any separate writing.

**Relists.** The docket shows **one distribution** (for the 9/28/2026
conference) and no response from the Solicitor General, who waived. I expect
no further distribution: the petition is disposed of at the conference it was
first set for. The one realistic path to another distribution is a call for a
response after the conference, followed by redistribution once the brief in
opposition arrives, or a hold behind a better vehicle raising the same
post-*Riley* timeliness question; I put the combined chance of at least one
more distribution at roughly one in eight. A long relist string is not in
prospect.

**CVSG.** None. The United States is the respondent, so there is no Solicitor
General whose views could be invited; the only federal signal available is a
call for a response, which the Court has not made.

**Which question the Court would take, if any.** The petition can only be
about the Ninth Circuit's dismissal as untimely under 8 U.S.C. § 1252(b)(1) as
construed in *Riley v. Bondi*, 606 U.S. 259 (2025): whether the 30-day clock
in a reinstatement case runs from the reinstated removal order even for
noncitizens whose reasonable-fear proceedings predate *Riley*, and whether
equitable tolling or non-retroactivity applies. If the Court ever reaches that
question, it will do so in a case where the arguments were preserved and the
circuit wrote a published opinion. Here the panel held both equitable tolling
and retroactivity forfeited and then alternatively rejected the withholding
and CAT claims on substantial-evidence grounds, so there is no clean question
to grant on; the Court would not take this petition's framing in any form.

**Summary disposition versus plenary review.** Conditional on any grant, the
route is far more likely to be a **GVR** after a hold, in light of a decision
in some other case resolving the post-*Riley* tolling or retroactivity
question, than plenary review of this unpublished memorandum. Plenary review
of this vehicle is very unlikely.

**Dissent from denial.** None expected. The Justices who have written about
reinstatement and withholding-only procedure would be most likely to do so in
a vehicle with a preserved tolling argument and a published opinion, not here.
