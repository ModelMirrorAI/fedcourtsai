# Forecast

The Court will most likely deny Christopher R. Cummins's petition without a separate writing. I assign 4% to any grant, including a GVR or summary reversal. The likely procedural result is denial rather than a merits ruling approving the search.

The snapshot contains two distributions: March 4 for the March 20, 2026 conference, and June 24 for the September 28, 2026 conference. A response request intervened on March 16; the opposition and reply followed in June. Starting from that recorded count of two, I expect no further distribution before denial, with a 16% probability of at least one more. If another distribution occurs, one additional conference is more likely than an extended series. My central timing forecast is an order in late September or early October 2026 following the scheduled September 28 conference; this is a forecast, not a verified release date.

No CVSG appears in the record. I expect none to be requested and assign a 0.5% probability to that increment. This is a state criminal suppression dispute with a threshold finality objection, not an evident occasion to solicit the federal government's institutional views.

If the Court grants any relief, a disposition in the cert order itself is more likely than plenary briefing and argument: 70% conditional on a grant. A GVR for reconsideration in light of Case v. Montana is the most plausible such route, although the interlocutory posture and the opposition's argument that Case reaffirmed the governing standard make even that route unlikely. This conditional forecast is consistent with only 2.8% unconditional probability of a grant accompanied by summary disposition.

If the Court instead takes the case for argument, I expect a question focused on whether prolonged nonresponse, signs of occupancy, and information about drug use supply an objectively reasonable basis for emergency-aid entry without affirmative evidence of injury. That would narrow the petition's framing around the absence of a witnessed disturbance or an explicit emergency report. Any substantive ruling would address the objective emergency-aid threshold, not a categorical constitutional prohibition on every entry lacking such a report.

Conditional on denial, I assign 3.5% to a noted dissent or statement respecting denial. I expect neither and do not forecast a particular Justice's writing or an observable vote tally.
