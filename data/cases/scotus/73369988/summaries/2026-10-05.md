## In brief

The Supreme Court declined to hear a former employee's bid to revive her challenge to an arbitration award favoring her former employer, JPMorgan Chase.

## What happened

Patrice Honeycutt arbitrated an employment dispute with JPMorgan Chase Bank, N.A. and related entities. A first arbitration award for Chase was vacated on appeal because the arbitrator failed to make required disclosures. A second arbitration, before a different arbitrator, again resulted in an award for Chase. Honeycutt asked the Superior Court to set aside that award, citing arbitrator partiality, nondisclosure, and failure to apply the correct legal standard. The Superior Court confirmed the award, the California Court of Appeal affirmed, and the California Supreme Court denied further review. Honeycutt then asked the U.S. Supreme Court to take the case.

## What the Court is being asked

Honeycutt's petition raises three questions: whether a court can uphold an arbitration award after acknowledging uncertainty over which law's standard of review applies, without resolving that uncertainty; whether a court can reject claims that an arbitrator was biased or failed to disclose conflicts without using an objective test for apparent bias; and whether fairness in adjudication requires a court to address information suggesting a lack of neutrality that surfaces during the proceedings, even if not raised as a formal claim.

## Where it stands

Chase waived its right to respond to the petition. The petition was distributed for the Court's September 28, 2026 conference, and on October 5, 2026, the Court denied the petition.

## What each outcome would mean

Honeycutt, who lost in the California courts, asked the Supreme Court to take up her case. By denying the petition, the Court leaves the California Court of Appeal's decision in place, meaning the arbitration award in favor of Chase remains confirmed. The denial does not mean the Supreme Court agreed with the lower court's reasoning; it simply leaves that ruling as the final word in this case.
