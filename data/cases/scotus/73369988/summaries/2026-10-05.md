## In brief

The Court declined to hear a former employee's bid to revive her challenge to an arbitration award upholding her employer's win over her workplace claims.

## What happened

Patrice Honeycutt arbitrated employment-related claims against JPMorgan Chase Bank and JPMorgan Chase & Co. under an arbitration agreement that pointed to both federal and California law. An earlier arbitration award for the bank was vacated by a California appeals court over disclosure problems; a second arbitration, before a different arbitrator, again favored the bank. A trial court confirmed that award, and the California Court of Appeal affirmed, while saying it was unclear whether federal or state law set the standard for reviewing the award. The California Supreme Court denied further review.

## What the Court is being asked

Honeycutt's petition raises three questions: whether a court can uphold an arbitration award without first deciding whether federal or state law governs the review standard; whether a court can reject claims that an arbitrator was biased or failed to disclose conflicts without using an objective test for apparent bias; and whether fair-process guarantees required the appeals court to address neutrality concerns that surfaced in the record but went undiscussed.

## Where it stands

The bank waived its right to respond to the petition. The case was distributed for the Court's September 28, 2026 conference, and on October 5, 2026 the Court denied the petition.

## What each outcome would mean

Honeycutt, as petitioner, asked the Court to take up her case. With the petition denied, the Court will not review the arbitration award, and the California Court of Appeal's decision upholding that award in JPMorgan's favor stands. The denial does not mean the Supreme Court agreed with the lower court's reasoning on the governing legal standard or on the neutrality issues Honeycutt raised; it simply leaves that decision in place without the Supreme Court deciding those questions itself.
