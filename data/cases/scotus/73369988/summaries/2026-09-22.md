## What happened

Patrice Honeycutt arbitrated an employment dispute with JPMorgan Chase. A first arbitration award for the bank was thrown out by a California appeals court because the arbitrator failed to make required disclosures. A second arbitration, before a different arbitrator, again resulted in an award for the bank. A trial court confirmed that award, and the California Court of Appeal affirmed. Honeycutt's petition says the appeals court recognized it was unclear whether federal or state law set the standard for reviewing the award, yet affirmed anyway without deciding which law applied, and without addressing claims that the arbitrator should have disclosed a later public appointment or that an appellate judge's household had an undisclosed institutional tie to the bank's counsel. The California Supreme Court denied further review.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked three things: whether a court can uphold an arbitration award while leaving unresolved whether federal or state law controls how that award gets reviewed; whether a court can reject claims that an arbitrator was biased or failed to disclose conflicts without judging those claims by an objective standard asking how the situation would appear to a reasonable observer; and whether it violates fair-process guarantees for a court to affirm without addressing facts in the record that bear on whether the decision-makers were neutral.
