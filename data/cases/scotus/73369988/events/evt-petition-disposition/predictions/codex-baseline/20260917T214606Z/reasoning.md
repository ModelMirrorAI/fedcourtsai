## Why 1%, rather than the approximately 5.1% anchor

- **Weak conflict presentation.** QP 1 concerns affirmance without choosing federal versus state review rules; QP 2 invokes arbitrator partiality and disclosure; QP 3 concerns judicial neutrality. The petition mentions disagreement about manifest disregard after Hall Street, but does not develop opposing appellate holdings on a question whose resolution would change this judgment. See petition pp. 8–14. I distinguish a claimed legal error from a demonstrated reason for national review.
- **Alternative-analysis obstacle.** The petition acknowledges that the appellate court assumed the federal manifest-disregard standard applied and found no violation. On that account, selecting federal law would not necessarily change the outcome. This is a substantial vehicle concern, not a conclusive finding that the judgment rests on an adequate independent state ground. I have not read the underlying opinion or contract independently.
- **Unpublished, record-dependent decision.** The petition identifies the November 18, 2025 opinion as unpublished (p. 1). Its asserted disclosure failures involve an arbitrator's public commission reappointment and a separate institutional affiliation mentioned during appellate argument (pp. 5–7, 15–17). The relationships' materiality and preservation would require closer record review. An earlier arbitration in the litigation was vacated for nondisclosure, according to the petition, but that does not establish the second arbitrator committed the same violation.
- **Precedent is less categorical than the QP suggests.** Commonwealth Coatings Corp. v. Continental Casualty Co., 393 U.S. 145, 150–52 (1968) (White, J., concurring, joined by Marshall, J.), distinguishes substantial undisclosed dealings from trivial relationships and does not equate all arbitral and judicial standards. I read that concurrence through CourtListener. It supports caution about accepting petitioner's broad appearance-of-bias characterization as a clear, controlling answer to every asserted affiliation. This is not a claim that any particular circuit's interpretation governs this case.
- **No affirmative attention beyond ordinary distribution.** The snapshot shows waiver, no requested response, no amici, and no CVSG. I treat those as absence of strengthening evidence in the supplied record, not proof the Court has rejected the petition. The long interval between distribution and the scheduled conference is not itself a hold or relist signal.

These factors bring my probability of **any grant**, including a GVR or summary reversal, to 0.01. The general importance of impartial arbitration keeps that probability above zero, but does not overcome this petition's weak showing of a clean, outcome-determinative conflict. Lack of a BIO and appendix widens my uncertainty about vehicle quality; it is not affirmative evidence that respondent concedes the allegations.
