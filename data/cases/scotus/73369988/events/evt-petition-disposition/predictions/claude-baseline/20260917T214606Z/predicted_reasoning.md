# Predicted reasoning — Honeycutt v. JPMorgan Chase Bank, N.A. (No. 25-1298)

**Forecast: the petition is denied at or shortly after the September 28, 2026
conference, without a call for a response, without a relist, and without any
separate writing.**

## Relists

The docket shows **one distribution** (July 1, 2026, for the Conference of
September 28, 2026) and no relists. Respondent waived its right to respond on
June 15, 2026, so the petition goes to the long conference on the petition
alone. I expect **no further distribution**: the Court disposes of the
petition on the first list. The only realistic routes to a second
distribution are a call for a response (which would pull the case off the
September 28 list and redistribute it once the response is in) or a
mechanical reschedule of the long conference; I put both together at
roughly one in sixteen, and a genuine relist for a Justice's closer look
well below that.

## Call for the views of the Solicitor General

**No CVSG.** No federal party or federal program is implicated; the dispute
is a private employment-arbitration award reviewed in California state
court. The Court does not invite the Solicitor General into a private
Federal Arbitration Act vacatur dispute from a state intermediate court.

## Which question presented, if any

If the Court took anything, it would not be the questions as written. All
three are framed as error-correction ("whether a court may affirm an
arbitration award after recognizing that it is unclear whether federal or
state law governs ... but without resolving which law applies"). The only
certworthy issue lurking underneath is whether the Federal Arbitration Act's
section 10 vacatur grounds bind a *state* court reviewing an award under an
agreement that invokes the Act, a question left open after Hall Street. The
petition does not frame it that way, does not allege a split among state
high courts or circuits on it, and the court below assumed the federal
standard arguendo and ruled against petitioner under it, so the question is
not outcome-determinative on this record. The Court would leave it for a
petition that presents it cleanly.

## Summary disposition versus plenary review

Conditional on a grant, I lean slightly toward a **cert-order disposition
(GVR)** over plenary review, because there is no plausible four-vote plenary
case on these questions presented, and a grant would most likely arise only
if some intervening decision on arbitrator-disclosure or FAA vacatur
standards issued during OT2026 and the Court swept this petition in with it.
No such decision is pending that I can identify, which is one reason the
unconditional grant probability is so low.

## Dissent from denial

**None expected.** No Justice has signalled interest in re-examining
Commonwealth Coatings or in policing state-court arbitration review, the
opinion below is unpublished, and the due-process theory (Caperton and
Rippo applied to a private arbitrator, and to an appellate panel member's
household affiliation the petitioner expressly declines to characterise as
misconduct) is not the kind of claim that draws a statement respecting
denial.
