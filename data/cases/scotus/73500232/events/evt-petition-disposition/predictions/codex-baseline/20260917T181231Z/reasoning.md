## Case-specific adjustments

The petition's strongest argument is that inconsistent weighting of the excusable-neglect factors affects access to appellate review. A two-day delay, reliance on advice, and asserted absence of prejudice make the claim of inequitable treatment understandable. I retain a nonzero chance of review to address a categorical legal standard or a demonstrable failure to balance the factors.

I nevertheless adjust substantially below the anchor. Questions one and two largely request correction of the application of an established framework to an individual record. The petition describes the Third Circuit opinion as unpublished (Opinions Below, printed page 7). Its own account says the lower courts considered control over delay, experience, prejudice, and good faith; that complicates its competing assertion that they did not perform the required analysis. The allegations of concealment and lack of prejudice are petitioners' contentions, not independently established facts.

The claimed split is not demonstrated cleanly by the petition's presentation (printed pages 15–19). It mixes procedural settings and even cites a district court decision, Deloach, as evidence of an Eighth Circuit position. Its subsequent discussion of Third Circuit precedents requiring holistic balancing (printed pages 22–25) also makes an intracircuit application dispute plausible. I do not treat the petition's prediction that other circuits would excuse this particular delay as proof of contrary holdings on equivalent facts.

Question four has a specific apparent defect. The petition describes the appealed June 4, 2024 order as denial of a motion for relief under Rule 60. Federal Rule of Civil Procedure 58(a)(5), checked in the rule text reproduced by Cornell LII, excepts orders disposing of Rule 60 relief motions from the separate-document requirement; Rule 58(c)(1) governs entry where no separate document is required. On the petition's own description, its proposed 150-day extension therefore does not follow. This is an inference about vehicle weakness, not an independently verified holding of the lower court. The provisioned appendix and lower-court opinion text are unavailable, so I cannot exclude a material difference in how the order should be characterized.
