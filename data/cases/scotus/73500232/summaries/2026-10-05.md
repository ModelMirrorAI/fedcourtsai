## In brief

The Court declined to hear a couple's bid to revive their appeal of a bank's ejectment judgment against them, filed two days late.

## What happened

Mark and Lisa Mazza, representing themselves, lost a long-running property dispute with Bank of New York Mellon, which had won an ejectment judgment forcing their removal from their home. The Mazzas tried to appeal a later order denying them relief from that judgment, but filed their notice of appeal two days after the deadline, saying they relied on a paralegal's mistaken advice and were away on a preplanned vacation. The district court denied their request for more time, finding no excusable neglect and some prejudice to the bank. The Third Circuit Court of Appeals affirmed that denial.

## What the Court is being asked

The Mazzas ask the Court to decide whether the lower courts wrongly refused to excuse their two-day late filing, given that they say the delay was minor, caused by factors outside their control, and caused no harm to the bank. They also argue federal appeals courts apply inconsistent standards for excusing late filings, and ask the Court to settle that inconsistency. Separately, they argue a procedural defect in how the district court issued its order meant their filing deadline had not yet even arrived.

## Where it stands

## What each outcome would mean

By denying the petition, the Court leaves the Third Circuit's decision in place: the Mazzas' appeal remains barred as untimely, and the underlying ejectment judgment favoring Bank of New York Mellon stands.
