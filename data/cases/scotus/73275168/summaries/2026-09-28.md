## In brief

Raymond Poore asks the Court to decide whether limits on deferring to agency rule interpretations also restrict deference to Sentencing Commission guideline commentary.

## What happened

Raymond Poore pleaded guilty to illegally possessing a firearm. His sentence was increased because a prior state conviction was treated as a "crime of violence" under sentencing guideline commentary, even though, Poore argues, the guideline's actual text does not cover his offense. He argued courts should not defer to that commentary given recent Supreme Court rulings limiting deference to agencies interpreting their own rules and statutes. The Seventh Circuit disagreed, following its own prior precedent, and upheld his sentence. Poore says federal appeals courts are evenly split on this question.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked whether the same limits that restrict courts from automatically deferring to an agency's interpretation of its own ambiguous rules should also limit how much weight courts give to the Sentencing Commission's explanatory commentary when that commentary expands what counts as a "crime of violence" for sentencing purposes.
