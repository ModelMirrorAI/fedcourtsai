## In brief

Raymond Poore asks the Court to decide whether judges must defer to Sentencing Commission commentary that expanded his sentence, or whether courts should interpret the sentencing rule independently.

## What happened

Raymond Poore pleaded guilty to illegally possessing a firearm. His sentence was increased because a prior state conviction counted as a "crime of violence" under the federal sentencing guidelines. That classification came not from the guideline's own text, but from commentary the Sentencing Commission wrote to explain it. Poore argued the guideline's text did not cover his offense and that courts should no longer automatically follow the commentary, citing recent Supreme Court decisions limiting deference to agencies' interpretations of their own rules. The Seventh Circuit disagreed, following its own prior precedent, and upheld his sentence. Poore then asked the Supreme Court to review that ruling.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked whether recent limits on how much courts must defer to agencies' interpretations of their own regulations also apply to the Sentencing Commission's commentary explaining the sentencing guidelines, or whether judges may instead continue treating that commentary as binding even when the guideline's own text might not require the result the commentary dictates.

## Where it stands

The petition has been distributed for conference multiple times since September 2025, most recently for the April 17, 2026 conference, after full briefing including the government's opposition and Poore's reply. The docket shows no decision yet on whether the Court will hear the case.

## What each outcome would mean

Poore, as the petitioner, is asking the Court to take up his case. If the Court grants review, it would hear the case fully and decide the deference question itself, without yet deciding who wins. If the Court denies review, the Seventh Circuit's decision upholding Poore's sentence and the use of the guideline commentary stands, leaving his sentence as calculated. The government opposes review, arguing the case is a poor vehicle and may be moot because Poore has completed his prison term, though he remains on supervised release.
