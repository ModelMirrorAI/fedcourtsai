## In brief

A man sentenced to extra prison time asks the Court to decide how much weight judges must give the Sentencing Commission's explanatory notes.

## What happened

Raymond Poore pleaded guilty to illegally possessing a firearm. At sentencing, the judge increased his guideline range based on a prior state conviction, treating it as a "crime of violence" only because of an explanatory note the Sentencing Commission had attached to the relevant guideline, not the guideline's own text. Poore argued that under recent Supreme Court rulings limiting deference to agency interpretations, courts should not automatically follow such notes. The Seventh Circuit disagreed, following its own prior precedent, and upheld his 42-month sentence. Poore asks the Supreme Court to review that ruling.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked whether the restrictions the Court has placed on deferring to an agency's reading of its own rules also limit how much courts must defer to the Sentencing Commission's explanatory notes when those notes expand what counts as a qualifying prior offense for a longer sentence.

## Where it stands

The petition has been distributed for roughly a dozen conferences between September 2025 and April 2026, with full briefing including a government brief opposing review and a reply from Poore. The docket shows no decision yet on whether the Court will hear the case.

## What each outcome would mean

Poore, who brought this appeal, is asking the Court to overturn the Seventh Circuit's decision. If the Court grants review, it would hear the case and decide how much deference such commentary deserves, without yet determining who wins. If the Court denies review, the Seventh Circuit's decision upholding Poore's longer sentence stands, and the government's position prevails. The government also argues the case may be moot since Poore has completed his prison term, though he remains on supervised release.
