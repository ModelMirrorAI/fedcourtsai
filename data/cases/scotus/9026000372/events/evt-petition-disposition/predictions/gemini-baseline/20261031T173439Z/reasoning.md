## Case-Specific Evidence Driving the Downward Adjustment

Because the Court in modern practice does not grant plenary review without first calling for a response, the petition can only be granted if a Justice first requests a response and the case subsequently relists.

### 2. The Court Just Remanded This Exact Mandate-Recall Proceeding 9–0 in *Fuld v. PLO* (2025)
As detailed in `record/documents/petition.txt` and `record/documents/appendix.txt` (reproducing the Second Circuit's March 30, 2026 panel opinion by Judge Koeltl joined by Judges Leval and Bianco, reported at 171 F.4th 575, and the June 18, 2026 order denying rehearing en banc), this petition is the direct sequel to *Fuld v. Palestine Liberation Organization*, 606 U.S. 1 (2025) (Nos. 24-20 & 24-151).
- In 2015, after a seven-week trial, a Southern District of New York jury awarded the *Sokolow* plaintiffs $218.5 million (trebled to $655.5 million under the Anti-Terrorism Act). In 2016, the Second Circuit vacated the judgment for lack of personal jurisdiction (*Waldman I*, 835 F.3d 317), and this Court denied certiorari in April 2018.
- Following Congress's enactment of the ATCA in October 2018, the *Sokolow* plaintiffs moved to recall the 2016 mandate (and also filed a protective parallel suit, *Sokolow v. PLO*, No. 18-cv-12213 (S.D.N.Y.), which remains stayed). After the Second Circuit denied the recall motion in *Waldman II*, 925 F.3d 570 (2019), Congress enacted the Promoting Security and Justice for Victims of Terrorism Act of 2019 (PSJVTA), Pub. L. No. 116-94, § 903, which expressly provided that it "shall apply to any case pending on or after August 30, 2016" (§ 903(d)(2))—one day before *Waldman I*—to reach the *Sokolow* litigation.
- This Court first GVR'd the denial of the motion to recall the mandate in *Sokolow v. PLO*, 590 U.S. 921 (2020), and then, after the lower courts held the PSJVTA unconstitutional under the Fifth Amendment (*Waldman III*, 82 F.4th 64 (2d Cir. 2023)), granted certiorari and reversed 9–0 in *Fuld v. PLO*, 606 U.S. 1 (2025). In *Fuld*, Chief Justice Roberts (writing for seven Justices, with Justices Thomas and Gorsuch concurring in the judgment) specifically noted that a "'key premise' of the PSJVTA was Congress's desire to facilitate 'the adjudication of ATA claims like the plaintiffs'"" (606 U.S. at 20) and reversed and remanded *Waldman III* for further proceedings consistent with its opinion.
- Having twice remanded the *Sokolow* mandate-recall proceeding so the lower courts could apply the PSJVTA, the Court is exceedingly unlikely to grant certiorari now to hold that recalling the 2016 mandate was constitutionally impossible all along.

Because a stay pending certiorari turns directly on whether four Justices are likely to grant review and five Justices are likely to reverse, the immediate, unreferred denial of No. 26A141 is a potent negative signal against certiorari.

### 4. Lack of Circuit Split and Sui Generis Posture
Petitioners (`petition.txt` at 6–17) argue that under *Pennoyer v. Neff*, 95 U.S. 714, 728 (1877), *Burnham v. Superior Court of California*, 495 U.S. 604 (1990), *Roman Catholic Archdiocese of San Juan v. Acevedo Feliciano*, 589 U.S. 57 (2020), and *Plaut v. Spendthrift Farm, Inc.*, 514 U.S. 211 (1995), a judgment vacated for lack of personal jurisdiction is permanently void once direct appeal concludes and cannot be revived via mandate recall. However:
- There is no circuit conflict on this question; the Second Circuit's decision arose in a unique statutory and procedural setting where Congress enacted a targeted statute (§ 903(d)(2)) and Petitioners engaged in post-enactment predicate conduct in 2020 with full statutory notice (`appendix.txt` at 15a–16a).
- The Second Circuit did not hold that Congress *commanded* reopening a final judgment in violation of *Plaut*; rather, it explicitly recognized *Plaut*'s rule (`appendix.txt` at 18a) and exercised its own discretionary appellate authority under *Sargent v. Columbia Forest Products, Inc.*, 75 F.3d 86 (2d Cir. 1996), and *Calderon v. Thompson*, 523 U.S. 538 (1998).
- As the Second Circuit emphasized (`appendix.txt` at 16a n.3, 20a–22a), the 2016 dismissal for lack of personal jurisdiction was *without prejudice*, and the *Sokolow* plaintiffs' protective 2018 lawsuit on the identical claims remains pending in the Southern District of New York. Thus, declining to recall the mandate would not grant Petitioners substantive repose from liability, but would merely force surviving victims of the 2002–2004 attacks to repeat a seven-week trial conducted in 2015.
