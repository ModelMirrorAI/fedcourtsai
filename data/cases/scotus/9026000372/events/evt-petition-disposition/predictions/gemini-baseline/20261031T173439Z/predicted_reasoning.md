# Predicted Reasoning: *Palestine Liberation Organization, et al. v. Mark I. Sokolow, et al.* (No. 26-372)

## Procedural Trajectory from Current Posture

- **Current Distribution State & Relist Forecast (`relist-increment`: 0.08):** The docket currently records **1 distribution**—distributed on October 28, 2026, for the Conference of November 13, 2026—with no prior relists and no call for a response. Both the private respondents (*Sokolow* plaintiffs) and the Federal Respondents (the United States, which intervened below) have waived their right to respond, and no amicus briefs have been filed at the certiorari stage. The Court will almost certainly deny certiorari out of the November 13, 2026 Conference without any further distribution or relist. There is only a modest (~8%) chance that a Justice requests a response (which would trigger at least one additional distribution after briefing) to examine Petitioners' *Plaut v. Spendthrift Farm, Inc.* / void-judgment argument.
- **CVSG Forecast (`cvsg-increment`: 0.005):** The Court will **not** call for the views of the Solicitor General. The United States is already a party to the proceeding (Intervenor-Appellant in the Second Circuit and a named respondent in this Court) and the Solicitor General formally waived the right to respond on October 16, 2026. If the Court wishes to hear from the government, it will simply call for a response from the Federal Respondents rather than issuing a CVSG invitation.
- **Question Presented if Granted (Context-Only):** The petition presents a single question: whether a court of appeals' equitable power to recall a mandate permits it to reinstate a final judgment that was vacated for lack of personal jurisdiction years earlier, based on personal jurisdiction arising under a subsequently enacted statute (the PSJVTA) triggered by post-enactment conduct. If the Court were to grant certiorari, it would take the single Question Presented as framed in the petition.
- **Plenary Review vs. Summary Disposition (`summary-disposition-route`: 0.08):** Conditional on a grant, the Court would set the case for plenary briefing and oral argument rather than disposing of it summarily in the certiorari order. There is no intervening Supreme Court precedent calling for a GVR—to the contrary, the Second Circuit's March 30, 2026 decision was rendered directly on remand from this Court's 9–0 decision in *Fuld v. Palestine Liberation Organization*, 606 U.S. 1 (2025)—and the novel constitutional interplay between *Pennoyer v. Neff*, *Plaut*, and appellate mandate-recall discretion would not be resolved by summary reversal without full merits briefing.
- **Separate Writings on Denial (`dissent-from-denial`: 0.04; `order-writers`):** Conditional on denial, the order will likely issue without any recorded dissent or statement respecting the denial (~96% probability). In the unlikely event (~4%) that a separate writing accompanies the denial, it is most likely to come from Justice Thomas (`0.025`) or Justice Gorsuch (`0.015`)—who concurred in the judgment in *Fuld*—writing a brief statement or dissent addressing the originalist principles of *coram non judice* and Article III finality under *Plaut*.

## Conditional Merits Outlook (If Granted)

If the Court were to call for a response and ultimately grant certiorari, the central merits question would be whether the Second Circuit abused its inherent equitable discretion under *Calderon v. Thompson*, 523 U.S. 538 (1998), or transgressed Article III separation-of-powers limits under *Plaut v. Spendthrift Farm, Inc.*, 514 U.S. 211 (1995), by recalling its November 2016 mandate and reinstating the October 2015 judgment. Even if granted, a majority of the Court would likely affirm the Second Circuit: the 2016 dismissal for lack of personal jurisdiction was without prejudice, plaintiffs had already preserved their claims in a stayed 2018 action (*Sokolow v. PLO*, No. 18-cv-12213 (S.D.N.Y.)), Congress expressly enacted the PSJVTA to cover actions pending on or after August 30, 2016 (Pub. L. No. 116-94, § 903(d)(2)), and this Court twice remanded the mandate-recall proceedings (*Sokolow v. PLO*, 590 U.S. 921 (2020); *Fuld v. PLO*, 606 U.S. 1 (2025)) so the lower courts could adjudicate the motion to recall the mandate under the PSJVTA.
