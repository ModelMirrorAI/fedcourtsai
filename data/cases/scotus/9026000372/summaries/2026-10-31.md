## In brief

The Palestine Liberation Organization asks the Court to undo a reinstated $655.5 million terrorism judgment, arguing the lower court wrongly revived a case once dismissed for lack of jurisdiction.

## What happened

American victims of attacks in Israel sued the PLO and Palestinian Authority under the Anti-Terrorism Act. A jury awarded them $655.5 million in 2015, but the Second Circuit vacated the judgment in 2016 for lack of personal jurisdiction, and the Supreme Court denied review. Congress later passed a law, the PSJVTA, creating jurisdiction based on the defendants' conduct after 2020. After the Supreme Court upheld that law in a related case, the Second Circuit recalled its earlier mandate and reinstated the original 2015 judgment. The PLO and Palestinian Authority sought rehearing, which was denied, and now ask the Supreme Court to take the case.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked whether an appeals court may use its own equitable power to reopen a closed case to bring back a money judgment that was thrown out for lack of jurisdiction years earlier, when the only new jurisdiction available came from a law Congress passed only after the case had already become final.

## Where it stands

The petition has been distributed for the Court's conference on November 13, 2026, after both the victims and the federal government waived their right to respond. The Court has not yet acted on whether to hear the case.

## What each outcome would mean

The Palestine Liberation Organization and Palestinian Authority are asking the Court to step in. If the Court grants review, it would hear full arguments and decide whether reviving the judgment was proper, without yet deciding who wins. If it denies review, the Second Circuit's decision stands, leaving the $655.5 million judgment reinstated in favor of the victims and their families, who prevailed below.
