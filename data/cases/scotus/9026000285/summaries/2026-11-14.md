## In brief

Christopher Ponzo asks the Court to review whether a federal court may order him to forfeit $13.2 million without following required forfeiture procedures.

## What happened

Christopher Ponzo pleaded guilty to fraud charges involving bribes paid to employees of a company that managed an energy-efficiency program. At his sentencing, the government first raised a specific forfeiture amount, $13.2 million, only after the hearing was otherwise over. A written forfeiture order followed weeks later, which the petition says Ponzo had no real chance to contest before it was entered. The United States Court of Appeals for the First Circuit agreed that several procedural steps required for forfeiture had been skipped, but ruled the errors were harmless and upheld the forfeiture order.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked whether a court can finalize an order taking a defendant's property as part of a criminal sentence when the required procedures were not followed — specifically, when the dollar amount sought was disclosed only at sentencing, the forfeiture was not announced aloud at the sentencing hearing itself, the defendant was not given a chance to respond before the forfeiture motion was granted, and the order was entered without the defendant present or able to offer evidence.

## Where it stands

The petition was distributed for the Court's October 9, 2026 conference, after which the Court asked the government to respond. The government's response deadline has since been extended twice, most recently to December 4, 2026, and no action on the petition itself is yet recorded.

## What each outcome would mean

Ponzo, who lost in the lower court, is asking the Court to take up his case. If the Court grants the petition, it would hear full arguments and decide whether the forfeiture procedures used in his case were lawful, without yet deciding who wins. If the Court denies the petition, the First Circuit's ruling upholding the $13.2 million forfeiture order against Ponzo would remain in place.
