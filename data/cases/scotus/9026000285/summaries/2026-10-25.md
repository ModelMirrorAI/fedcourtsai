## In brief

Christopher Ponzo asks the Court to review whether a $13.2 million forfeiture order against him was entered without the notice and hearing federal rules require.

## What happened

Christopher Ponzo pleaded guilty to fraud charges involving a Massachusetts energy-efficiency program. At his sentencing, the government for the first time asked to forfeit $13.2 million in property, and the district court granted that request after sentencing without giving Ponzo a chance to respond or be present. Ponzo argued this violated Federal Rule of Criminal Procedure 32.2, which governs forfeiture procedure. The First Circuit agreed that several procedural steps were skipped but held the errors were harmless and upheld the forfeiture. Ponzo now asks the Supreme Court to review that ruling, pointing to disagreement among federal appeals courts on how such errors should be treated.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked whether a trial judge can order a defendant to forfeit property when the government did not specify the amount until the sentencing hearing, the judge did not announce the forfeiture order out loud at sentencing, the defendant had no chance to respond before the forfeiture was ordered, and the forfeiture order was entered outside the defendant's presence.

## Where it stands

The petition was distributed for the Court's October 9, 2026 conference, after which the Court asked for a response from the government, due November 18, 2026 following a granted extension. The government had initially waived its right to respond before the Court requested one.

## What each outcome would mean

Ponzo, who lost in the lower court, is asking the Court to take up his case and reverse the First Circuit's ruling. If the Court grants review, it would hear full arguments and decide whether the forfeiture procedures used against Ponzo were lawful, without yet deciding who wins. If the Court denies review, the First Circuit's decision upholding the $13.2 million forfeiture order against Ponzo stands, leaving the government's position intact.
