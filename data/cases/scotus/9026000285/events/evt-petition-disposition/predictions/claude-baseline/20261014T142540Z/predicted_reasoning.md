# Forecast — Ponzo v. United States, No. 26-285

**State the docket shows.** Paid petition from the First Circuit, docketed
Sept. 3, 2026. The United States waived response (Sept. 14); the petition was
distributed once, for the Oct. 9, 2026 conference (Sept. 16); the next day the
Court **requested a response**, due Oct. 19, 2026. No CVSG (the United States is
the respondent, so a CVSG cannot arise).

**Relisting.** The Oct. 9 distribution is effectively spent: with a response
called for, the petition will be **redistributed** after the government's brief
in opposition (and any reply) — most likely for a November or early-December
2026 conference. Expect one further distribution; a second relist beyond that
happens only if a Justice takes a serious interest.

**CVSG.** None — the Solicitor General is already the party opponent.

**Disposition.** Denial is the likelier outcome. The response request shows at
least one Justice wants the government's position on the Rule 32.2(b)(4)(B)
question McIntosh v. United States (2024) expressly reserved (601 U.S. at 341
n.5), but the government will likely argue the case is a poor vehicle: the
First Circuit found the errors harmless on its record, and the five-part QP is
fact-bound (no specified amount until sentencing, no oral pronouncement, no
chance to respond, entry outside the defendant's presence).

**If granted**, the Court would likely reformulate the QP to the clean legal
question: whether Rule 32.2(b)(4)(B)'s at-sentencing requirement is a mandatory
claim-processing rule (automatic relief when invoked) or a time-related directive
subject to harmlessness review, as McIntosh held for (b)(2)(B); and perhaps how
harmlessness is measured (the asserted Sixth Circuit *Harrell* / First Circuit
conflict). Plenary review is far likelier than a summary route; a GVR would
require an intervening decision, and none is apparent.

**Should it deny**, expect no separate writing.
