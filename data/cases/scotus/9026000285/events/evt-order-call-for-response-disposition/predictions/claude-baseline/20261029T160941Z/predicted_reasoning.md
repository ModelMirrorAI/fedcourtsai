# Predicted course — Ponzo v. United States, No. 26-285

**Denied, most likely in December 2026 or January 2027, after the United States' response.**
The Court requested a response on 17 September 2026, the day after the petition was
distributed for the 9 October conference following the government's waiver. That takes
the petition off that conference. The response is now due 18 November 2026 (an
extension the live docket shows). Expect the petition to be distributed again once the
response and any reply are in, for a conference in early-to-mid December. The most
likely result is a denial after that one further distribution, with no relist.

- **Further distributions:** one more is near-certain, because the call for response
  forces a redistribution. A relist past that conference is unlikely (roughly one in
  seven). It would happen only if the government's response concedes that the
  circuits are divided on whether Rule 32.2(b)(4)(B) is a mandatory claim-processing
  rule.
- **CVSG:** none. The United States is the respondent, so its views come in its own
  brief in opposition.
- **Expected government position:** oppose. The likely grounds are that the oral
  pronouncement error was harmless on this record because Ponzo had notice of the
  forfeiture and contested the amount, that the claimed split is shallow and rests
  largely on unpublished decisions, and that the case is a poor vehicle because of the
  plea posture and the "otherwise ensure the defendant knows" alternative in the rule.
  A confession of error is possible but unlikely.
- **If granted:** the Court would take a narrowed version of the question presented:
  whether a violation of Rule 32.2(b)(4)(B)'s at-sentencing requirement is subject to
  harmless-error review, the question *McIntosh* n.5 reserved. It would leave aside the
  five fact-bound sub-points of the question as written.
- **Summary route:** if the Court acts favourably, a GVR or a hold-then-GVR is a real
  alternative to plenary review. That would happen if the Court took up a different
  Rule 32.2(b)(4)(B) vehicle (for example, one arising from the Sixth Circuit's
  *Harrell* line) and later sent this case back in light of it.
- **Separate writing on denial:** unlikely. If anyone writes, the likeliest are Justice
  Gorsuch or Justice Jackson, on the due-process and right-to-presence framing.
