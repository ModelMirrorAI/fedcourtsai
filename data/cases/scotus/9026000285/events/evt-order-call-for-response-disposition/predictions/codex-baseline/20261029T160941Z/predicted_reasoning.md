# Forecast of the Court's action

The Court will most likely deny the petition after receiving the requested response or allowing its deadline to run. The provisioned docket contains one distribution, on September 16, 2026, for the October 9 conference, followed by the September 17 response request. It shows no completed conference reconsideration, response brief, or CVSG.

Expect one additional distribution following the response process, then denial without a separate writing. That redistribution would satisfy the distribution-increment claim; it would not, by itself, establish that the Court reconsidered the petition after a conference. P(at least one additional distribution) is 0.98. Beyond that procedural redistribution, the modal path has no further relist, although one or two further conference distributions are plausible if the Court seriously considers the alleged split.

No CVSG is expected: the United States is already the respondent and its requested response supplies the government's position directly. P(a new CVSG) is 0.001. The response request is not an invitation to the Solicitor General to participate as a nonparty.

If review is granted, the likeliest question is a narrowed formulation of the petition's question: whether Rule 32.2(b)(4)(B)'s requirement of forfeiture at sentencing is a mandatory claim-processing requirement, and what remedy follows a preserved violation. The Court would be less likely to take the petition's entire five-part factual formulation or to resolve the proper $13.2 million amount itself. If it reaches the merits, the procedural classification and the opportunity to contest forfeiture are the likely grounds, not an independent excessive-fines holding.

Conditional on a grant, plenary briefing and argument are more likely than disposition in the cert order: P(summary-disposition route | grant) is 0.12. The supplied materials present McIntosh as having reserved this final-order question, not as an intervening decision that clearly dictates a GVR. A summary correction remains a minority possibility if the response reveals an obvious procedural error or concedes relief.

Conditional on denial, P(a noted dissent or statement respecting denial) is 0.06. The modal prediction is an unsigned denial with no writing. If a writing appears, Gorsuch, Sotomayor, or Jackson are plausible authors on procedural-protection grounds; this is a low-confidence contextual forecast, not a prediction of disclosed cert votes.
