## In brief

A school district asks the Court to decide how much deference federal courts owe special-education hearing officers when a parent's lawsuit includes new evidence about a child's progress.

## What happened

C.T., a student with disabilities, was placed by the Galesburg school district's IEP team in a private therapeutic day school rather than his local school's self-contained classroom. His parents challenged the placement, arguing it was too restrictive under federal special-education law. A hearing officer sided with the district. The parents then sought judicial review, and a federal district court held a bench trial, heard new testimony about C.T.'s progress since the hearing, and reversed, ordering the district to educate C.T. in the local classroom instead. The Seventh Circuit affirmed, saying the district court could give the hearing officer's findings reduced weight because it had received substantial new evidence.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked whether a federal court reviewing a special-education placement dispute may rely on evidence about events that happened after the administrative hearing to reach its own conclusion, giving less weight to the hearing officer's original decision, or whether the court must instead evaluate the case based on the circumstances and evidence that existed when the hearing officer decided it.

## Where it stands

The petition was distributed for the Court's October 9, 2026 conference; the docket shows no action from that conference. The Court then asked the parents to respond, with that response now due October 28, 2026, after the parents requested more time to file it.

## What each outcome would mean

Galesburg Community Unit School District No. 205, which lost in the lower courts, is asking the Court to take the case. If the Court grants review, it would hear the case fully and decide the legal question; granting would not itself decide who wins. If the Court denies review, the Seventh Circuit's decision stands, leaving in place the district court's order requiring the district to educate C.T. in the local classroom rather than the therapeutic day school it had proposed.
