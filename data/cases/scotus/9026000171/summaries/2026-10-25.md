## In brief

A school district asks the Court to decide how much weight federal courts must give special-education hearing officers' rulings when courts also hear new evidence.

## What happened

C.T., a student with disabilities, struggled behaviorally at his school in Galesburg, Illinois. The school district's team proposed moving him to a private therapeutic day school, but his parents objected, preferring he stay in a local special-education classroom. A hearing officer sided with the district. The parents then sought judicial review, and a federal district court, after hearing new testimony about events since the hearing, reversed and ordered the district to keep C.T. in the local classroom with added support. The Seventh Circuit affirmed, saying the hearing officer's decision deserved reduced weight because the district court had taken substantial new evidence.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked whether a law letting courts hear new evidence in special-education placement disputes allows a court to discount the hearing officer's decision by relying on evidence about what happened after that decision, effectively deciding the placement question fresh as of trial, rather than judging the original decision based on what was known when it was made.

## Where it stands

The petition was distributed for a conference, after which the Court asked the respondents to file a response, due October 28, 2026. The respondents have since requested more time to file that response. The Court has not yet acted on whether to hear the case.

## What each outcome would mean

Galesburg Community Unit School District No. 205, the petitioner, is asking the Court to take up the case and reverse the Seventh Circuit's decision, which favored C.T.'s parents. If the Court grants review, it would hear full arguments and decide how much deference hearing officers' rulings deserve; a decision for the district could require courts to weigh new evidence differently, while a decision for the parents would leave that approach in place. If the Court denies review, the Seventh Circuit's ruling favoring the parents stands, and C.T. remains in the local classroom arrangement the district court ordered.
