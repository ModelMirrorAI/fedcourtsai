## In brief

A school district asks the Supreme Court to hear its case over how much deference federal courts owe special-education hearing officers when new evidence comes in during judicial review.

## What happened

C.T., a young student with disabilities, was placed by his school district's education plan into a private therapeutic day school, over his parents' objections that this violated his right to be educated alongside non-disabled peers. An administrative hearing officer sided with the school district. The parents then sought judicial review in federal district court, which held a bench trial, heard additional evidence, and reversed, ordering the district to keep C.T. in his local school's program with added support. The Seventh Circuit Court of Appeals affirmed, applying its own standard for how much weight courts must give hearing officers' findings when new evidence is introduced.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked whether a law letting courts hear new evidence beyond the administrative record allows a court to give less weight to the hearing officer's decision based on events that happened after that decision, rather than requiring the court to judge the placement based on what was known at the time it was made. The school district also raises a related question about the proper test for deciding whether a student's placement is the least restrictive setting appropriate for them.

## What each outcome would mean

The school district is asking the Court to take up the case and overturn the Seventh Circuit's ruling. If the Court grants review, it would hear full arguments and decide the legal question itself, without yet deciding who wins. If it denies review, the Seventh Circuit's decision affirming the district court's order favoring C.T.'s parents stands, and the school district would remain bound by the order to keep C.T. in his local school's program with additional support.
