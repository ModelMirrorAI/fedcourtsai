## In brief

A school district asks the Court to decide how much weight judges must give special-education hearing officers' decisions when courts hear new evidence about a student's progress.

## What happened

C.T., a student with disabilities, was placed by Galesburg's school district in a private therapeutic day school under his individualized education plan. His parents objected, arguing he could be educated satisfactorily, and less restrictively, in his existing special-education classroom. A hearing officer sided with the district. The parents then sought review in federal district court, which took new testimony about C.T.'s progress after the hearing and reversed, ordering the district to keep him in his current classroom with added support. The Seventh Circuit Court of Appeals affirmed, saying the hearing officer's decision was owed less deference because the district court had heard significant new evidence.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked whether a law letting courts hear additional evidence in special-education disputes allows judges to reduce the deference normally owed to the hearing officer's decision when that new evidence concerns events after the hearing, effectively letting the court decide the placement question fresh based on later developments rather than reviewing the decision as it stood when made.

## Where it stands

The school district's petition was distributed for the Court's October 9, 2026 conference. Afterward, on September 28, 2026, the Court asked the respondents to file a response, due October 28, 2026; no decision on whether to hear the case has been recorded.

## What each outcome would mean

Galesburg Community Unit School District No. 205, as petitioner, asked the Court to take the case. If the Court grants review, it would hear full arguments and decide how much deference is due; a grant would not itself decide who is right. If the Court denies review, the Seventh Circuit's decision favoring N.T. and P.T., C.T.'s parents, and requiring the district to educate him in his existing classroom with added support, would remain in effect.
