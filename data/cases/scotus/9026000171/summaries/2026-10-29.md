## In brief

A school district asks the Court to decide how much weight judges must give special-education hearing officers when courts hear new evidence about a student's progress after the fact.

## What happened

C.T., a student with disabilities, was placed by his school district's IEP team in a private therapeutic day school rather than his local school's special-needs classroom. His parents challenged this, arguing it violated his right to education in the "least restrictive environment" under the Individuals with Disabilities Education Act. A hearing officer sided with the district. The parents then sought review in federal district court, which held a trial with new testimony about C.T.'s progress after the hearing and reversed, ordering the district to keep him in the local classroom. The Seventh Circuit affirmed, saying the hearing officer's findings deserved less deference once new evidence came in.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked whether, when a law lets courts hear additional evidence in reviewing a special-education placement decision, judges may use evidence about events that happened after the original decision to decide the case fresh, or whether they must instead give the original administrative decision the deference the law requires, judged by what was known at the time it was made.

## Where it stands

The petition was distributed for conference, and the Court then asked the respondents to file a response, which was due and then extended to November 27, 2026. An amicus brief supporting the petition has been filed. The Court has not yet acted on the petition.

## What each outcome would mean

Galesburg Community Unit School District No. 205, the petitioner, asked the Court to take the case after losing in the lower courts. If the Court grants review, it will hear full arguments and decide how much deference reviewing courts owe hearing officers' decisions, deciding nothing yet about who is right. If it denies review, the Seventh Circuit's ruling favoring C.T.'s parents stands, and the district court's order requiring the district to educate C.T. in the local classroom remains in effect.
