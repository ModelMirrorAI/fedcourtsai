## In brief

A school district asks the Court to review how much deference federal courts must give special-education hearing officers when a parent lawsuit includes evidence of events after the hearing.

## What happened

C.T., a young student with behavioral disabilities, was placed by his school district's individualized education plan in a private therapeutic day school rather than his neighborhood school's special classroom. His parents challenged that placement as too restrictive under a federal special-education law. An administrative hearing officer sided with the school district. The parents then sought review in federal district court, which held a trial, heard additional evidence about C.T.'s progress after the hearing, and reversed, ordering the district to keep C.T. in the local classroom with added support. The United States Court of Appeals for the Seventh Circuit affirmed that ruling.

## What the Court is being asked

The district asks the Court to decide how much weight a federal court must give a special-education hearing officer's findings when the court also considers evidence about what happened after the hearing. More specifically, it asks whether a law directing courts to consider new evidence lets a judge effectively start over and decide the dispute based on later developments, rather than giving the hearing officer's original decision the deference the district says the law requires.

## Where it stands

The petition was distributed for conference, after which the Court asked for a response from the parents; both sides have now filed briefs, including a brief in opposition and a supporting amicus brief, and the case awaits the Court's decision on whether to grant review.

## What each outcome would mean

The school district is asking the Court to take up the case and reverse the Seventh Circuit's decision favoring the parents. If the Court denies review, the Seventh Circuit's ruling stands, leaving in place the order requiring the district to educate C.T. in the local classroom with added supports. If the Court grants review, it would hear full briefing and arguments and decide the deference question itself; granting review decides nothing in advance about who wins. The parents' brief argues separately that the district waived its arguments below, which the Court could also consider in deciding whether to take the case.
