## Why move above the anchor, but remain well below a grant call?

**Positive signal:** The Court affirmatively requested a response after initial distribution. That supplies case-specific attention beyond the frozen baseline band. The question also concerns a recurring statutory tension: how receiving additional evidence under 20 U.S.C. § 1415(i)(2)(C) interacts with deference to administrative findings. The petition's reasons section, pages 18–26, contrasts circuit formulations of due weight and challenges the Seventh Circuit's sliding scale. I treat those comparisons as petitioner's advocacy, not independently verified holdings of all the cited circuits.

**Important limiting features:** The appendix's appellate opinion, pages 13a–18a, expressly preserves a due-weight requirement and explains why material new evidence reduced the weight of the administrative findings. It does not announce that administrative findings can simply be ignored whenever a party submits anything new. Its discussion of seven trial witnesses, materially different evidence, and explicit consideration of the administrative record gives the respondent a substantial case-specific answer to the petition's broader framing.

There is also a concrete vehicle concern. The appellate opinion's footnote 2, spanning pages 17a–18a, says the district objected to only one expert's testimony and waived a developed abuse-of-discretion challenge to admission. The petition, page 30, acknowledges that both sides introduced later evidence and distinguishes its objection to weighting from admissibility. That does not eliminate the preserved due-weight question, but it makes the QP's attack on receiving later evidence a less clean vehicle than a fully preserved categorical admissibility dispute.

The petition's additional discussion of different least-restrictive-environment tests, pages 31–36, raises a potentially consequential disagreement. It is not, however, a separately stated QP, and the petition itself says on page 36 that changing the LRE test likely would not have changed the outcome given the court's treatment of the aide evidence. The district court's order, pages 41a–48a, explicitly states the deference standard and bases relief on this child's supports and placement. Together these features leave a meaningful possibility that the Court regards the petition as an objection to application of established standards rather than a clean occasion to settle a dispositive conflict.
