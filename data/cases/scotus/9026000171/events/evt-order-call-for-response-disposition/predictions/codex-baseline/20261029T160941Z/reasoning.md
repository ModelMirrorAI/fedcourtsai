## Why the forecast moves to 12%

The response request is real affirmative attention after the petition was distributed. It warrants a meaningful increase over the 5.11% unconditioned baseline-class anchor, but the prompt supplies no response-request-conditioned estimator. The increase to 12% is a judgmental update, not a measured historical response-request rate.

The petition raises a recurring statutory question: how a reviewing court should reconcile IDEA's permission to receive additional evidence with the due weight owed to state administrative findings. It alleges tension among circuit approaches and ties that tension to post-hearing developments used to reassess a child's placement. The Seventh Circuit's express sliding-scale formulation makes the legal issue more identifiable than a bare challenge to the district court's factfinding. National consequences for school districts and families support taking the petition seriously.

Several features constrain the upward adjustment. The different circuits' formulations do not themselves demonstrate incompatible outcomes on materially identical facts. The petition joins three distinguishable issues: admission of new evidence, the weight given administrative findings, and retrospective reassessment of an educational placement. Its discussion of differing least-restrictive-environment tests is broader than its single question presented. That weakens the claimed clean vehicle for resolving the entire alleged split.

Most importantly, the lower opinion supplies a concrete vehicle concern not apparent from the question alone. At pages 18a–19a and footnote 2, the Seventh Circuit distinguishes admission from weighing and treats the developed challenge to evidentiary admission as waived. This does **not** mean every due-weight argument was waived: the panel separately addressed that challenge. But an evidentiary-admission or categorical post-hearing-evidence ruling could encounter preservation problems. Both sides also supplied additional evidence, as the petition itself acknowledges. The panel says the district court considered the administrative record, stated the due-weight requirement, and relied on materially different expert and post-hearing evidence. These facts permit the Court to regard the result as individualized application of existing doctrine rather than a categorical rejection of administrative deference.

The missing opposition is my largest uncertainty. It could either sharpen a genuine conflict and explain preservation, or establish that the question conflates admissibility, evidentiary weight, and prospective relief. I do not assume what the parents will argue. I would move materially upward for a squarely conflicting decision on a preserved, outcome-determinative legal rule, but this bounded record does not establish that combination.
