## Disposition and Route

- **Certiorari Disposition (`disposition`: 0.05 — Denied):** The Court will deny the petition for a writ of certiorari once the Brief in Opposition is filed and considered.
- **Question Presented if Granted (Context-Only):** If the Court were to grant certiorari, it would take the single question presented as framed in the petition—whether 20 U.S.C. § 1415(i)(2)(C)(i)–(ii) permits a district court reviewing a state administrative IDEA decision to reduce or set aside `Rowley` deference based on post-hearing evidence from the statutory "stay-put" period—likely focusing on the interplay between the "additional evidence" clause, `Rowley`'s "due weight" standard, and `Endrew F.`'s prospective IEP evaluation rule.
- **Summary Disposition vs. Plenary Review (`summary-disposition-route`: 0.08 conditional on grant):** Conditional on a grant, the Court would almost certainly set the case for full merits briefing and oral argument (~92% conditional probability) rather than issuing a summary reversal or a grant, vacate, and remand (GVR) order (`0.08`). No pending case on the Court's merits docket presents this IDEA standard-of-review question to trigger a hold/GVR, and the circuits' varying formulations of "modified de novo" and "sliding scale" review under § 1415(i)(2)(C) would not lend themselves to a summary per curiam reversal.
- **Conditional Merits Ground (Context-Only):** If granted and argued on the merits, the Court would likely reverse or vacate the Seventh Circuit's judgment, holding that judicial review of an IEP and its least-restrictive-environment (LRE) placement under 20 U.S.C. § 1415(i)(2)(C) must evaluate the reasonableness of the IEP prospectively as of the time it was drafted (`Endrew F.`), and that post-hearing developments during a stay-put period cannot be used to strip the administrative hearing officer's contemporaneous findings of "due weight" (`Rowley`).
- **Separate Writings on Denial (`dissent-from-denial`: 0.02):** Upon denial of certiorari, no Justice is expected to author a dissent or statement respecting the denial (`0.02`). If a separate writing were to appear, it would most plausibly come from Justice Thomas, Justice Alito, or Justice Gorsuch questioning federal judicial micromanagement of local school placements, but the record's waiver and factual complications make a separate writing unlikely.
