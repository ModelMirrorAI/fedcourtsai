## In brief

The Supreme Court denied two tax attorneys' request to send their fraud convictions back for reconsideration of where their trial should have taken place.

## What happened

Catherine Chollet and Michael Kohn, Missouri tax attorneys, were convicted in North Carolina federal court of conspiracy and of aiding false tax returns, alongside a North Carolina insurance broker who worked with them. They prepared and filed the returns from Missouri, but prosecutors argued venue was proper in North Carolina because clients, the broker, and his office manager did relevant work there. The Fourth Circuit affirmed their convictions, rejecting their venue challenge along with other arguments. Two days after that ruling, the Supreme Court decided a different case addressing how courts should determine where a crime occurred for venue purposes. The Fourth Circuit later denied rehearing without addressing that new decision.

## What the Court is being asked

The petitioners asked the Court to send the case back to the lower court so it could reconsider, in light of the Court's intervening venue ruling, whether their trial was properly held in North Carolina rather than where they actually did their work. They did not ask the Court to decide the venue question itself, only to let the appeals court take a fresh look with the new ruling in hand.

## Where it stands

The United States waived its right to respond to the petition. The Court distributed the case for its November 13, 2026 conference, and on November 16, 2026, denied the petition.

## What each outcome would mean

Chollet and Kohn, the petitioners, asked the Court to act; the United States, as respondent, did not file an opposing brief. The denial leaves the Fourth Circuit's ruling in place, meaning their convictions stand and the venue question will not get another look from the lower court based on the intervening decision. Had the Court instead granted the request, it would have vacated the lower court's judgment and sent the case back for the Fourth Circuit to reconsider venue, without itself deciding whether the convictions should stand.
