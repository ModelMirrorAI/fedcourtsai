## In brief

Two Missouri tax attorneys convicted of tax crimes in North Carolina ask the Court to send their case back for a new look at where they could be tried.

## What happened

Michael Kohn and Catherine Chollet, Missouri tax attorneys, were convicted in the Western District of North Carolina of conspiracy and of aiding false tax returns, based on a tax strategy they marketed to clients, most of whom lived in North Carolina. They argued at trial and on appeal that they prepared and filed every return from Missouri, so North Carolina was the wrong place to try them. The Fourth Circuit affirmed, pointing to conduct in North Carolina by clients, an insurance agent, and his office manager, plus acts supporting a related scheme. Two days later, the Supreme Court decided a different case, Abouammo, addressing where criminal venue lies. The Fourth Circuit denied rehearing without addressing it.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked whether, in light of its intervening ruling limiting criminal trials to places where a defendant's own conduct making up the crime occurred, the Fourth Circuit should reconsider whether Missouri-based tax preparers could be tried in North Carolina based on other people's actions there and on acts tied to a separately charged scheme.

## Where it stands

The United States waived its right to respond to the petition. The petition was distributed for the Court's November 13, 2026 conference, and the docket shows no action on it since.

## What each outcome would mean

Kohn and Chollet, who lost below, are asking the Court to send their case back to the Fourth Circuit for reconsideration under the new venue ruling. If the Court grants the petition, it could vacate the Fourth Circuit's decision and return the case for the lower court to reconsider venue, deciding nothing itself about who should win. If the Court denies the petition, the Fourth Circuit's ruling upholding their convictions and North Carolina venue stands.
