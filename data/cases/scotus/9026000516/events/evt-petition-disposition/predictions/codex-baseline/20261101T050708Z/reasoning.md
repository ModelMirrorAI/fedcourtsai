## Why move above 5.19%, but not much higher?

The petition expressly asks only for a GVR in light of **Abouammo v. United States, 608 U.S. 597 (2026)**. The provisioned record places the Fourth Circuit judgment on June 9, 2026 and Abouammo on June 11. That sequencing creates a genuine reconsideration argument, not merely a request for ordinary error correction. The tax-return counts also matter independently of the unchallenged conspiracy venue: the appendix confirms sentences of 84 and 48 months (App. 7), and the petition explains the five-year conspiracy maximum (petition pp. 16–17). I do not assume a remand would necessarily eliminate every substantive count.

I independently retrieved Abouammo's revised June 11 opinion through CourtListener, opinion 11343328. Its holding confines venue for the document-falsification offense in 18 U.S.C. § 1519 to the location of the prohibited act, rejects an effects-only rationale, and rejects borrowing another offense's conduct. It expressly describes that holding as narrow and statute-specific. That supports reconsideration of scheme-only reasoning here, but does **not** establish that agents' acts or interstate counseling can never supply venue under a different statute. See Abouammo, 608 U.S. at 603–607; slip opinion pp. 4–9.

The strongest reason against a large upward adjustment is in the lower court's own opinion, rather than the petition's characterization. The Fourth Circuit already used the conduct-element framework and identified aiding, assisting, procuring, counseling, and advising as prohibited acts under § 7206(2). It attributed direct exchanges with North Carolina clients, draft returns, and procurement of information to petitioners themselves (App. 15–17). Section 1519's single falsification act is therefore distinguishable. Whether the charged assistance occurred in North Carolina is more fact-sensitive than whether an investigation there merely felt the effects of a completed falsification elsewhere.

There are additional obstacles. Petitioners presented Abouammo on rehearing, which was denied July 8 without a requested en banc poll (petition p. 9; App. 60). That does not prove a reasoned application of the new decision, but it weakens the premise that the panel lacked any opportunity to reconsider. The petition also concedes that the venue instruction was not challenged on appeal (p. 16). Its count-specific argument, particularly for Simmons's own returns, is its strongest remaining point; however, the district court expressly addressed those counts through knowingly caused information-gathering acts (App. 36). I cannot infer an automatic change in outcome from the appellate discussion's brevity.

**Lawrence v. Chater, 516 U.S. 163, 167–168 (1996)**, independently checked through CourtListener opinion 117987, permits a GVR when intervening developments create a reasonable probability of an outcome-relevant reconsideration, subject to the equities. It does not make a new citation an automatic entitlement to remand. The government's response waiver is a meaningful negative attention signal here, although a Justice may still request a response. Taken together, the genuine intervening ruling raises my estimate to 12%, while the statutory distinction, rehearing opportunity, and waiver keep denial clearly modal. This adjustment is judgment, not an empirically measured rate for Abouammo-based petitions.
