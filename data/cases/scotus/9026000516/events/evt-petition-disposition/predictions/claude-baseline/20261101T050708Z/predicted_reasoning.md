# Forecast — Chollet & Kohn v. United States, No. 26-516

**Most likely outcome: denied, at the 13 November 2026 conference or soon after.** P(grant, which here effectively means a GVR) = 0.10.

- **State of the docket.** One distribution so far (DISTRIBUTED for the Conference of 11/13/2026). The United States waived its response on 21 October. No relists, no CVSG, no amici.
- **Further relists.** Unlikely (0.18). The likeliest way the petition gets distributed again is that the Court asks the Solicitor General for a response before it GVRs a federal criminal case over the government's silence. I expect no such request. The SG waived here, but in at least one other post-*Abouammo* case (Whitehead, No. 25-7462) it filed a memorandum on the GVR question. That makes the waiver read as no acquiescence.
- **CVSG.** Essentially nil. The United States is already the respondent.
- **Question the Court would take.** The only one the petition asks: GVR in light of *Abouammo v. United States*, 608 U.S. 597 (2026). If the Court does act, I'd expect a GVR (P = 0.88 given a grant), not plenary review. Plenary review would be over whether *Abouammo*'s conduct-element rule limits venue for 26 U.S.C. § 7206(2) aiding-and-assisting counts based on third parties' acts and on acts in furtherance of the scheme.
- **Why the Court probably denies.** The Fourth Circuit already had *Abouammo* in front of it. The petitioners pressed it in their rehearing petition, and the panel (Niemeyer, Thacker, Rushing) denied rehearing without a poll. Some justices see a post-rehearing GVR as asking a court to reconsider what it has already declined to reconsider. *Abouammo* also called its holding "discrete and narrow," and § 7206(2)'s "counsels or advises" verbs give the government a communication-based venue theory that survives the decision.
- **Dissent from denial.** Unlikely (0.03). If someone writes, Justice Gorsuch is the likeliest, given his interest in criminal venue and jury-trial rights.
