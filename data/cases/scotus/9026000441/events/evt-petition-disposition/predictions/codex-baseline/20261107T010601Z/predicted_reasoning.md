# Predicted procedural course

The Court will deny the petition, most likely without a separate writing, following its first scheduled conference on December 4, 2026. The forecast starts from **one recorded distribution**, entered November 4, and no existing CVSG. The modal course is a denial on the ensuing order list, not a merits decision endorsing the Fifth Circuit's interpretation.

There will most likely be **no further distribution**. I assign 9% to at least one additional distribution; if that occurs, one more is the most likely increment rather than repeated relists. A rescheduling also satisfies this claim. The Court will not call for the Solicitor General's views; that event has probability 0.1%. Because the United States is already the respondent, any request that it submit an opposition would be a call for a response, not a CVSG.

If the Court grants review, it will most likely take the first question in a focused form: whether FECA's provision of medical benefits constitutes a public plan under 18 U.S.C. § 24(b). It is less likely to take the request to disapprove Anderson or to decide the proper scope of lenity as a free-standing abstract question. Lenity would instead be a possible interpretive consideration within the FECA question.

Conditional on a grant, plenary briefing and argument is the more likely route: 85%, against 15% for disposition in the grant order. No identified intervening decision supplies a straightforward reason to grant, vacate and remand. If review is granted, the competing interpretations of plan and the consequences for the BCBS and money-laundering components will require attention before relief can be specified.

Conditional on denial, the probability of a dissent or statement respecting denial is 4%. No writing is expected. If one appears, Gorsuch is the most likely author, addressing lenity or the breadth of federal criminal statutes rather than announcing a merits holding on FECA. The order-writer forecasts in `prediction.json` are separate independent authorship probabilities, not predictions of which Justices vote to deny.
