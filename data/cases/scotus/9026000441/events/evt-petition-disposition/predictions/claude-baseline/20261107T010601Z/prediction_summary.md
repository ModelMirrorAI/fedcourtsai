We expect the Supreme Court to turn down this case (roughly a 1-in-40 chance it agrees to hear it). Christopher Rydberg was convicted of health-care fraud. He argues that the federal workers'-compensation program he was accused of defrauding does not count as a "health care benefit program" under the fraud statute. The appeals court rejected his argument in a short, unpublished decision and said any error didn't matter anyway. No other courts are said to disagree, and the government didn't bother filing a response, which usually signals a denial.
