The Supreme Court is very likely to deny Christopher Rydberg's petition for review (a 99.2% chance of denial, or a 0.8% chance of being heard). Rydberg, a former pharmacy executive convicted of a $90 million kickback and billing scheme, argues that the Federal Employees' Compensation Act—a federal workers' compensation program—does not count as a "health care benefit program" under the federal health-care fraud statute. However, no lower federal court has ever agreed with that interpretation, the appeals court decided his case in a short unpublished opinion that sets no precedent, his conviction also rested on defrauding a private insurer (Blue Cross Blue Shield), and the federal government waived its right to respond without the Court asking for a reply.
