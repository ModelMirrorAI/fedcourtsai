# Forecast: Padula v. Cirrus Rojas, No. 26-572

**Posture.** The docket shows one distribution (for the conference of December 4, 2026), a waived response, no CVSG and no amicus briefs. The Solicitor General's petition asks for nothing but a hold pending *Rhoney v. Barbosa da Cunha*, No. 26-104, in which certiorari was granted on October 1, 2026 on the same question: whether 8 U.S.C. 1225(b)(2)(A) mandates detention of noncitizens present in the United States without admission.

**What the Court will do.**

- **Hold.** The Court will take no action at the December 4 conference. The petition will sit, with no order, until *Barbosa da Cunha* is decided, most likely between April and the end of June 2027.
- **Then dispose of it by summary order.** If the government prevails in *Barbosa da Cunha* in whole or in material part, the Court will grant, vacate the Seventh Circuit's judgment and remand for further consideration in light of that decision (a GVR). If the Second Circuit is affirmed, the Court will deny this petition. I expect the government to prevail somewhat more often than not, so a GVR is the single likeliest outcome (about 0.62 for any grant).
- **No plenary review.** The Court will not set this case for briefing and argument: the lead case already carries the question. Conditional on a grant, the route is a cert-order disposition (about 0.97).
- **Relists.** No further distribution is likely to appear on the docket. Held petitions are ordinarily disposed of on the order list after the lead decision without a new "DISTRIBUTED" entry. A redistribution would come only from an unusual event, such as a supplemental filing or a mootness suggestion (about 0.10).
- **CVSG.** None. The United States is the petitioner.
- **Question presented.** If the Court acts on the merits of this petition at all, it does so through the lead case's question as written. A GVR here would send the due-process and avoidance reasoning in the Seventh Circuit's opinion back for that court to sort out.
- **Mootness.** The respondent is out of custody on bond, and his removal proceedings continue. If his case ends before the lead decision, a Munsingwear vacatur at the government's request is possible, which would also be a grant.
- **Separate writings.** If the petition is denied after a government loss in the lead case, expect no dissent from, or statement respecting, the denial. Any disagreement would be aired in the lead opinion.
