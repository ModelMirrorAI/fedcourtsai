## Structural Analysis and Adjustments (`0.731` / `0.689` -> `0.58`)
The decisive fact in `petition.txt` (pp. 11–12) is that **the Solicitor General does not seek plenary review**. The entire "Reasons for Granting the Petition" section is three sentences noting that the Court granted certiorari on October 1, 2026 in *Rhoney v. Barbosa da Cunha*, No. 26-104, on the identical question—whether 8 U.S.C. § 1225(b)(2)(A) mandates detention pending removal proceedings of aliens present in the United States without having been admitted—and asking the Court to **hold** No. 26-572 pending *Barbosa da Cunha* and dispose of it accordingly. Respondent's counsel of record (Michael K. T. Tan of the ACLU, who corpus lookup confirms is also respondent's counsel of record in *Barbosa da Cunha*, No. 26-104, and *Putra v. Lopez-Campos*, No. 25-1415, as well as petitioner's counsel in *Buenrostro-Mendez v. Blanche*, No. 26-43) waived a response to allow a routine hold.

Why adjust **downward** from the `federal` band anchor (`0.731`) and the zero-amicus `federal` reference (`0.689`) to **`0.58`**?
- In the general `federal` pool (`0.731`), plenary Solicitor General petitions win certiorari ~75–85% of the time *regardless* of whether the government ultimately wins or loses on the merits nine months later. Here, plenary review has already been granted in *Barbosa da Cunha*.
- In both *Barbosa da Cunha* (175 F.4th 61 (2d Cir. 2026)) and *Cirrus Rojas* (183 F.4th 909 (7th Cir. 2026)), the court of appeals ruled **against** the federal government, holding that interior-present noncitizens who entered without inspection are governed by 8 U.S.C. § 1226(a)'s discretionary bond framework rather than § 1225(b)(2)(A)'s mandatory detention rule.
- Consequently, how the Court disposes of *Padula v. Cirrus Rojas* at post-decision cleanup bifurcates sharply on the merits judgment in *Barbosa da Cunha*:
  1. **Government disturbs the judgment in *Barbosa da Cunha* (~52% unconditional contribution -> `gvr`):** If the Supreme Court reverses or vacates the Second Circuit—either adopting the Fifth Circuit's and Judge Sykes's reading that every unadmitted alien is an "applicant for admission" covered by § 1225(b)(2)(A), or adopting an intermediate rule under which affirmative applications for relief in removal proceedings (Cirrus Rojas applied for asylum, withholding of removal, and CAT protection) trigger "seeking admission"—the Seventh Circuit's categorical holding cannot stand, producing a **GVR** (`gvr`, `granted = 1`).
  2. **Court affirms the Second Circuit in *Barbosa da Cunha* (~40% unconditional contribution -> `denied`):** If the Supreme Court affirms the Second Circuit, the Seventh Circuit's judgment below already matches the Supreme Court's holding, so the Court will **deny** the SG's held petition at cleanup—the exact pattern corpus lookup shows in *United States v. Daniels* (No. 24-1248) and *United States v. Sam* (No. 24-1249), both denied on June 29, 2026 after the Court affirmed the Fifth Circuit in *United States v. Hemani*.
  3. **Mootness / *Munsingwear* vacatur vs. Rule 46 dismissal (~4% `gvr` + ~2% `dismissed`):** Over the ~7 months until *Barbosa da Cunha* is decided, Cirrus Rojas's removal proceedings could conclude. When an adverse published appellate habeas judgment becomes moot while a government certiorari petition is pending through the ordinary completion of immigration proceedings rather than petitioner's unilateral forfeiture, the Solicitor General routinely seeks vacatur under *United States v. Munsingwear, Inc.*, 340 U.S. 36 (1950), which resolves on the order list as **`gvr`** (~4%), while stipulated Rule 46 dismissal accounts for ~2% (`dismissed`, `granted = 0`).
  4. **Plenary grant / summary reversal tail (~2%):** Only ~1.5% plenary `granted` (if *Barbosa da Cunha* and earlier-filed holds like No. 25-1415 became moot before decision, though *Cirrus Rojas* itself had a prior bond-release mootness dispute below that makes it an inferior backup vehicle) plus ~0.5% `summary-reversal`.

Why price a government merits disturbance in *Barbosa da Cunha* at ~52–54% rather than the generic 70.5% SCOTUS merits disturbance rate?
- **Strengths for the government:** § 1225(a)(1) expressly defines "applicant for admission" to include "[a]n alien present in the United States who has not been admitted," *Jennings v. Rodriguez*, 583 U.S. 281, 287, 297 (2018), repeatedly described § 1225(b)(2)(A) as a mandatory catchall for unadmitted applicants, IIRIRA's core 1996 design replaced the "entry" line with "admission" so unlawful border-crossers would not enjoy better procedural terms than port-of-entry applicants, and the current Court has frequently sided with the Solicitor General in Title 8 detention cases (plus at the September 28, 2026 Long Conference it granted the SG's petition in No. 26-104 while holding the alien's Fifth Circuit petition in No. 26-43).
- **Substantial counterweights favoring affirmance:** Six of seven circuits to decide the question (CA2, CA6, CA7, CA8, CA9, CA10, against only a divided CA5 panel) rejected DHS's reading on potent textualist and structural grounds that often attract Justices Gorsuch, Barrett, and Roberts alongside Justices Sotomayor, Kagan, and Jackson: (a) § 1225(b)(2)(A) requires both "an applicant for admission" *and* an examining officer's determination regarding "an alien seeking admission," creating surplusage if the two phrases are coextensive; (b) mandatory detention for all unadmitted interior aliens renders superfluous both IIRIRA's own 1996 cross-references in § 1226(c)(1)(A)/(D) and Congress's brand-new January 2025 Laken Riley Act, 8 U.S.C. § 1226(c)(1)(E), which specifically mandated detention for unadmitted aliens (§ 1182(a)(6)(A)) charged with theft-related crimes; and (c) under *Loper Bright Enterprises v. Raimondo*, 603 U.S. 369 (2024), 28 years of unbroken post-IIRIRA Executive Branch regulations and practice (1997–2025) treated interior-present unadmitted noncitizens as bond-eligible under § 1226(a).

Summing the grant-family branches (`0.56 gvr + 0.015 granted + 0.005 summary-reversal`) yields **`probability = 0.58`** and **`predicted_disposition = "gvr"`**.
