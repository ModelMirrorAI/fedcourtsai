# Predicted Court Action: *Padula v. Cirrus Rojas* (No. 26-572)

- **Conference trajectory and further distributions (`relist-increment`):** The docket currently shows a single distribution (`Nov 17 2026: DISTRIBUTED for Conference of 12/4/2026`), following respondent's November 10, 2026 waiver of a response. At the December 4, 2026 Conference, the Court will take no immediate dispositive action and instead **hold** the petition pending its merits decision in *Rhoney v. Barbosa da Cunha*, No. 26-104 (*cert. granted* Oct. 1, 2026), just as the Solicitor General requests. While held, the petition will sit off the weekly relist cycle until *Barbosa da Cunha* is decided (likely in late spring or June 2027), at which point the Clerk will **redistribute the petition once more** (1 additional distribution, or 2 if a call for response or conference carry-over intervenes) for the post-decision cleanup conference.
- **Solicitor General invitation (`cvsg-increment`):** The Court will **not** call for the views of the Solicitor General. Federal immigration officials represented by the Solicitor General are the petitioners, leaving no non-party federal perspective to invite.
- **Route and terminal disposition (`summary-disposition-route` and `disposition`):** Conditional on a grant, **summary disposition in the certiorari order (`gvr`) overwhelmingly dominates plenary review**. Because *Barbosa da Cunha* already occupies the OT2026 merits docket on the identical question under 8 U.S.C. § 1225(b)(2)(A), the Court will grant, vacate, and remand (*gvr*) the Seventh Circuit's adverse judgment for reconsideration in light of *Barbosa da Cunha* if the federal government prevails in whole or in relevant part on the merits there (or vacate and remand under *United States v. Munsingwear, Inc.*, 340 U.S. 36 (1950), should respondent's habeas custody become moot beforehand). Conversely, if the Court affirms the Second Circuit in *Barbosa da Cunha*, the Seventh Circuit's judgment below will already align with that holding, and the Court will **deny** certiorari at the post-decision cleanup conference.
- **Question presented if plenary review were granted:** Plenary review is very unlikely unless *Barbosa da Cunha* (and earlier-filed held petitions such as *Putra v. Lopez-Campos*, No. 25-1415) became moot or otherwise unsuitable before decision. In that contingency, the Court would take the petition's single statutory question presented as written—whether 8 U.S.C. § 1225(b)(2)(A) mandates detention pending removal proceedings of aliens present in the United States without having been admitted—and, if reaching the merits, likely focus on the textual interplay between § 1225(a)(1)'s definition of "applicant for admission," § 1225(b)(2)(A)'s "seeking admission" clause, and § 1226(a) and (c).
- **Separate writings on the disposing order (`dissent-from-denial` and `order-writers`):** Should the Court deny certiorari—almost certainly in routine cleanup after affirming the Second Circuit in *Barbosa da Cunha*—expect **no separate dissent from or statement respecting the denial**. Any Justices dissenting from an affirmance in *Barbosa da Cunha* (most plausibly Justices Thomas, Alito, or Kavanaugh) will have already published their merits dissents in the lead case days earlier, leaving little reason to write separately on a follow-on cleanup order.
