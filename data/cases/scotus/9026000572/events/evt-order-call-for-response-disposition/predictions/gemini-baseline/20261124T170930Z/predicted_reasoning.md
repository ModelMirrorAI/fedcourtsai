The Court will hold this petition pending its merits decision in *Rhoney v. Barbosa da Cunha*, No. 26-104 (cert. granted Oct. 1, 2026), and will most likely dispose of it by entering a summary **grant, vacate, and remand (`gvr`)** order once *Barbosa da Cunha* comes down.

### Conference trajectory and interim orders

- **Further distributions (`relist-increment`):** The docket currently records **one** distribution (November 18, 2026, for the December 4, 2026 Conference), which was interrupted on November 24, 2026 when the Court called for a response (currently due December 24, 2026) following respondent's November 9 waiver. Barring a voluntary dismissal under Rule 46 before briefing closes, the petition is virtually certain to be distributed again after the brief in opposition is filed (or after any extension expires) in early 2027. At that post-response conference, the Court will hold the petition rather than act on it immediately, followed by one final clean-up distribution after *Barbosa da Cunha* is decided — yielding **two additional conference distributions** beyond the single distribution currently on the docket.
- **No CVSG (`cvsg-increment`):** No CVSG appears on the docket, and the Court will **not** call for the views of the Solicitor General because the Solicitor General already represents the federal petitioners as counsel of record.

### Route and ultimate disposition

- **Summary disposition vs. plenary review (`summary-disposition-route`):** Conditional on a grant, summary disposition in the certiorari order itself (**GVR**) overwhelmingly dominates plenary review for briefing and oral argument. The Solicitor General explicitly asks only that the Court hold this petition pending *Barbosa da Cunha* and dispose of it accordingly, not that the Court grant plenary review or consolidate it. Because *Barbosa da Cunha* already occupies the merits docket on the identical statutory question — and earlier-distributed companion petitions such as *Putra v. Lopez-Campos*, No. 25-1415, are already held ahead of this case — the Court would set *Padula v. Cirrus Rojas* for plenary briefing and argument only in the remote event that *Barbosa da Cunha* (and earlier held vehicles) failed to reach a merits decision due to intervening mootness or dismissal as improvidently granted.
- **Merits-driven clean-up outcome (`gvr` vs. `denied`):** Because the Seventh Circuit ruled *against* the federal government below — holding that 8 U.S.C. § 1226(a)'s discretionary-bond regime rather than 8 U.S.C. § 1225(b)(2)(A)'s mandatory-detention mandate governs noncitizens present in the interior without admission — the clean-up disposition turns directly on the outcome of *Barbosa da Cunha*. The Court is slightly more likely than not to reverse or vacate the Second Circuit's judgment in *Barbosa da Cunha*, which will produce a routine clean-up **GVR** vacating the Seventh Circuit's judgment and remanding for further consideration (including any preserved constitutional Due Process issues). Conversely, if the Court affirms the Second Circuit in *Barbosa da Cunha*, the Seventh Circuit's judgment already accords with that holding and the Court will **deny** certiorari on the clean-up order list.
- **Question presented if granted plenary review:** If the Court were ever to grant plenary review here as a substitute vehicle, it would take the petition's single statutory question presented verbatim — whether 8 U.S.C. § 1225(b)(2)(A) mandates detention pending removal proceedings of aliens present in the United States without having been admitted — and, if reversing, would rest on § 1225(a)(1)'s chapter-wide definition of "applicant for admission" paired with § 1225(b)(2)(A)'s catchall inspection-and-detention mandate while leaving respondent's as-applied Fifth Amendment Due Process claim for remand.

### Separate writings on the disposition order

- **Dissent from denial (`dissent-from-denial`):** Should the Court deny certiorari, **no separate dissent from or statement respecting denial** is expected. Nearly every plausible path to denial is a routine post-merits clean-up order entered days after an affirmance in *Barbosa da Cunha*; any Justices dissenting on the statutory merits (most likely Justices Alito and Thomas) will have already published their full dissenting views in *Barbosa da Cunha* itself and will have no reason to write separately on trailing hold petitions. Similarly, if the Court enters a clean-up GVR, the order will almost certainly issue as an unsigned, routine one-sentence GVR without separate opinions.
