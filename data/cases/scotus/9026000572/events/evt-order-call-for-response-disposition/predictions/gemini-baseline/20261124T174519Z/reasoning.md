## 2. Structural Decomposition of `P(grant) = 0.62` (`predicted_disposition = "gvr"`)

The decisive fact in `record/documents/petition.txt` (pp. 11–12) is that **this is an explicit Solicitor General hold petition**, not a request for plenary review. On October 1, 2026, the Court granted certiorari in *Rhoney v. Barbosa da Cunha*, No. 26-104 (from *Barbosa da Cunha v. Freden*, 175 F.4th 61 (2d Cir. 2026)), on the identical Question Presented: whether 8 U.S.C. § 1225(b)(2)(A) mandates detention pending removal proceedings of noncitizens present in the United States without having been admitted. Here, a divided Seventh Circuit panel (Kolar, J., joined by Jackson-Akiwumi, J.; Sykes, J., dissenting) reversed the Eastern District of Wisconsin's denial of habeas corpus and held that § 1225(b)(2)(A) applies only to noncitizens "seeking admission" at the border/ports of entry, leaving interior arrests governed by discretionary bond hearings under § 1226(a).

The November 24, 2026 `Response Requested. (Due December 24, 2026)` entry carries almost no case-specific merits signal beyond confirming routine Clerk/Chambers procedure: respondent (represented by ACLU counsel Michael Tan) waived a response on November 10, 2026, the petition was distributed on November 17 for the December 4 conference, and the Court never grants, GVRs, or holds a waived SG petition for potential GVR without calling for a response so respondent can flag any vehicle, mootness, or distinguishing ground.

Because the Seventh Circuit ruled **against** the government on the exact statutory ground at issue in *Barbosa da Cunha*, *Padula*'s ultimate cert-stage disposition is an asymmetric function of *Barbosa da Cunha*:
1. **If the government prevails in *Barbosa da Cunha* (reversing or vacating the Second Circuit):** The Court will **GVR** *Padula v. Cirrus Rojas* (`gvr`, which counts as `granted = 1`), unless *Padula* has already been dismissed under Rule 46 or respondent's forthcoming BIO establishes intervening mootness that defeats even vacatur.
2. **If respondent prevails in *Barbosa da Cunha* (affirming the Second Circuit):** Because the Seventh Circuit's judgment already matches an affirmance, the Court will **deny certiorari** (`denied`, `granted = 0`) on the post-opinion cleanup list.

Why adjust downward from `cert_anchor.rate` (`0.731`) and `cert_amicus_line.rate` (`0.689`) to **`0.62`**?
- **Four-vote cert threshold vs. five-vote merits threshold:** Plenary SG petitions in `federal` succeed on four votes to hear an important split. A hold petition from an adverse circuit judgment succeeds (via GVR) only if **five Justices** vote to disturb the judgment in the lead case. While the overall merits disturbed rate in `metrics/statpack.md` is `70.5%` (`n = 560`) and the Roberts Court has frequently backed executive detention readings of §§ 1225–1226 and 1231 (*Jennings v. Rodriguez*, 583 U.S. 281 (2018); *Nielsen v. Preap*, 586 U.S. 392 (2019); *Johnson v. Guzman Chavez*, 594 U.S. 523 (2021)), *Barbosa da Cunha* is considerably closer on the merits than a typical SG appeal.
- **Substantive merits headwinds for the government in *Barbosa da Cunha*:** Six courts of appeals (2d, 3d, 6th, 7th, 10th, and 11th Circuits) have rejected DHS's July 2025 policy shift against two (5th and 8th Circuits, albeit with forceful dissents by Judges Sykes, Kirsch, Lagoa, and Murphy). While the government has strong textual hooks in § 1225(a)(1)'s "deemed for purposes of this chapter an applicant for admission" clause and *Jennings*'s description of § 1225(b)(2) as a catchall, respondents have formidable textualist and historical counterarguments: (a) § 1225(b)(2)(A)'s shift from "applicant for admission" to "alien seeking admission," paired with IIRIRA § 308(d)(2)(D)'s repeal of AEDPA § 414 (which had deemed interior entrants to be "seeking entry and admission"); (b) structural surplusage with § 1226(c)(1)(A) and (E) — including the 2025 Laken Riley Act's specific mandatory-detention triggers for inadmissible interior noncitizens under § 1182(a)(6)(A)(i) — and IIRIRA § 303(b)(2)'s detention-space transition grace period for § 1226(c) alone; and (c) nearly 29 years of unbroken, contemporaneous post-IIRIRA executive practice (1997–2025) treating interior arrests under § 1226(a) (*Loper Bright* / *Skidmore* weight). Given Justice Gorsuch's textualist dissent in *Preap* (5–4) and dissent in *Guzman Chavez* (6–3) alongside Justices Sotomayor, Kagan, and Jackson, the government must hold at least three of Chief Justice Roberts, Justice Kavanaugh, and Justice Barrett (assuming Justices Thomas and Alito vote to reverse) if Justice Gorsuch sides with respondents. I place the probability that *Barbosa da Cunha* produces a reversal, vacatur, or intermediate statutory narrowing calling the Seventh Circuit's categorical rule into question at roughly **0.65**.
