# Predicted Court Reasoning and Procedural Trajectory

## Further Distributions and Hold Trajectory

The docket currently records a single distribution (`DISTRIBUTED for Conference of 12/4/2026` on November 17, 2026), followed on November 24, 2026 by a call for a response due December 24, 2026 after respondent initially waived. Because the petition has been pulled from active December 4 conference resolution pending that response, the petition will be distributed further past its current count of one unless the parties stipulate to dismiss beforehand. Specifically, expect at least two additional petition distributions: one in January or February 2027 once respondent's brief in opposition (and any brief extension or government reply) is filed, at which conference the Court will hold the petition pending *Rhoney v. Barbosa da Cunha*, No. 26-104; and at least one more distribution in late spring or June 2027 for the cleanup conference immediately following the merits decision in *Barbosa da Cunha*.

## Call for the Views of the Solicitor General (CVSG)

The Court will not issue a CVSG. Solicitor General D. John Sauer is counsel of record for the federal petitioners, so the views of the United States are already before the Court on the face of the petition.

## Question Presented and Relationship to Lead Case

The Court will not grant plenary review of this petition unless *Barbosa da Cunha* encounters an intervening mootness or vehicle defect that forces the Court to substitute another pending case (and even then, detained-respondent vehicles such as *Raycraft v. Lopez-Campos*, No. 25-1415, would stand ahead of this case given respondent Cirrus Rojas's prior release on bond). In the unlikely event the Court took this case for plenary review, it would take the petition's single statutory question presented as written: whether 8 U.S.C. § 1225(b)(2)(A) mandates the detention pending removal proceedings of aliens present in the United States without having been admitted.

## Summary Disposition Route vs. Plenary Review

Conditional on the Court granting certiorari in any form, summary disposition in the certiorari order itself — specifically a grant, vacatur, and remand (GVR) in light of *Rhoney v. Barbosa da Cunha* — is overwhelmingly likelier than setting the case for merits briefing and oral argument. The petition explicitly asks only for a hold pending *Barbosa da Cunha* and appropriate disposition afterward.

If the Court reverses or vacates the Second Circuit in *Barbosa da Cunha* — holding that 8 U.S.C. § 1225(a)(1)'s rule deeming unadmitted interior noncitizens "applicants for admission" brings them within mandatory detention under § 1225(b)(2)(A) — the Court will grant certiorari in *Padula v. Cirrus Rojas*, vacate the Seventh Circuit's contrary judgment, and remand for further consideration in light of *Barbosa da Cunha* (leaving any unreached regulatory or as-applied constitutional questions to the lower courts on remand). Conversely, if the Court affirms the Second Circuit in *Barbosa da Cunha* — holding that § 1225(b)(2)(A)'s "seeking admission" trigger applies at the border or ports of entry while § 1226(a) governs interior arrests — the Seventh Circuit's judgment in respondent's favor will already stand in accord with this Court's precedent, and the Court will enter a routine order denying certiorari.

## Separate Writings on Denial or Cleanup

Should the Court deny certiorari, expect no noted dissent from denial and no separate statement respecting denial. Because a denial here will almost certainly arrive on a post-merits cleanup order list after *Barbosa da Cunha* is decided by full opinion, any Justices dissenting from the underlying statutory holding (most plausibly Justices Alito and Thomas) will have stated their views in *Barbosa da Cunha* itself rather than writing separately on held follow-on dockets.
