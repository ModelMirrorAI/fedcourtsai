# Forecast: Padula v. Cirrus Rojas, No. 26-572 (response-requested moment)

**The Court will hold this petition for *Rhoney v. Barbosa da Cunha*, No. 26-104, and dispose of it by summary order after that case is decided — most likely a GVR.**

- **Disposition.** The petition asks for nothing but a hold: its question presented is the one on which the Court granted certiorari in *Rhoney* on October 1, 2026. The Court will not act on it at the December 4, 2026 conference or at the first conference after the response arrives. It will sit until *Rhoney* is decided, which I expect by late June 2027 (argument is expected in late February or early March 2027). If the government prevails in *Rhoney*, in whole or on a ground that unsettles the Seventh Circuit's reasoning, the Court grants, vacates and remands here. If the government loses outright, the Court denies. I put the grant family at 0.60, nearly all of it GVR.
- **Further distributions.** The docket shows one distribution (for the December 4, 2026 conference), made before the response was called for. The petition will be distributed again once the response is in (due December 24, 2026, absent an extension), likely for a January 2027 conference, and then again for the order-list conference following the *Rhoney* decision. Held petitions typically show no public relist entries in between, so expect one to two further distribution entries, not a string of relists.
- **CVSG.** None. The United States is the petitioner.
- **Question presented, if the Court took the case itself.** It will not set this case for plenary review; *Rhoney* already carries the question as written. A plenary grant here, or consolidation with *Rhoney*, is very unlikely this late in the briefing schedule.
- **Summary route.** Conditional on any grant, the cert-order route (GVR in light of *Rhoney*, or a Munsingwear-style vacatur if the respondent's custody dispute becomes moot first) is about 0.97.
- **Separate writings.** Should the Court deny, expect no noted dissent or statement (about 0.03): a denial here would follow a merits loss for the government in *Rhoney*, where any disagreement will already have been written. Should it GVR, expect no separate writing either; the small residual sits with Justices Sotomayor and Jackson (objecting to a GVR) and Justices Thomas and Alito (objecting to a denial).
- **Timing.** Disposition on an order list in the weeks after the *Rhoney* opinion, most likely late June or early July 2027.
- **If the government wins *Rhoney*,** the likely ground is textual: that an alien present without admission is an "applicant for admission" under Section 1225(a)(1) and so is "seeking admission" for Section 1225(b)(2)(A). That ground would directly undo the Seventh Circuit's holding and make the GVR routine.
