# Predicted course — No. 26-572, Padula v. Cirrus Rojas (arrival)

**State forecast from:** docketed October 28–29, 2026. No distributions, no CVSG, and no response yet (it is due November 30, 2026). The Court has ordered paper-only filings under Rule 34.6.

**What the petition is.** The Solicitor General asks the Court to review the Seventh Circuit's July 30, 2026 decision. That decision held that a noncitizen living in the United States without admission is detained under § 1226, so he is entitled to a bond hearing, not to mandatory detention under § 1225(b)(2)(A). On October 1, 2026, the Court granted certiorari on this same question in *Rhoney v. Barbosa da Cunha*, a Second Circuit case set for argument this Term.

**Expected course: held for *Rhoney*, then disposed of in line with it.**
- After the response (or a waiver), the petition will be distributed for a winter conference and then held. It will not be acted on until *Rhoney* is decided, probably in spring or June 2027. After that it will be distributed again for the conference that clears held cases. So at least one further distribution is near-certain, and two or more is likely. The holding itself shows up as no action, not as a string of formal relists.
- **No CVSG**: the federal government is the petitioner.
- **If the government wins *Rhoney*:** a GVR (the Court grants review, vacates the judgment, and sends the case back to the Seventh Circuit to reconsider in light of *Rhoney*). That is the summary route. Plenary review is unlikely, since the lead vehicle is already being argued. The exception would be if *Rhoney* fails as a vehicle (for example, through mootness) and the Court substitutes this case.
- **If the government loses *Rhoney*:** a plain denial, because the Seventh Circuit's judgment would already agree with the Court. The SG might instead ask to dismiss the petition under Rule 46.
- **A mootness wrinkle:** the respondent was granted a $1,500 bond in 2025. If his custody dispute ends before then (removal, a grant of relief, or release that ends the controversy), the SG could ask for a Munsingwear vacatur, which would also read as a GVR.

**If plenary review were granted** (unlikely), the Court would take the SG's question as written: whether § 1225(b)(2)(A) mandates detention pending removal proceedings for people present without admission.

**Separate writing:** unlikely on either route. Justices who disagree with *Rhoney* rarely write again on held follow-on cases. A short statement on a GVR or a denial is possible but improbable.
