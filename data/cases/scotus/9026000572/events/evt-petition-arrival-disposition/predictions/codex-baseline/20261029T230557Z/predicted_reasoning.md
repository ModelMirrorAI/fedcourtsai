# Forecast of the Court's treatment

The modal disposition is a grant, vacatur, and remand (GVR): the Court will set aside the Seventh Circuit's judgment and return the case for reconsideration in light of its decision in the already-granted immigration-detention case No. 26-104, Rhoney v. Barbosa da Cunha. I assign an 80% probability to any grant-family disposition, including this route, not an 80% probability of separate briefing and argument here.

The provisioned docket shows **zero distributions and no CVSG**. I expect an initial distribution after the response deadline of November 30, 2026, followed by a hold for the lead decision. One initial distribution and a possible later return to conference are more likely than a long sequence of active relists. P(at least one further distribution from the present zero-distribution state) is 99.5%; that is not a prediction of a 99.5% chance of a true relist after an initial conference. The likely final order will follow the lead decision, plausibly toward the end of the 2026 Term in 2027 rather than immediately after the first conference.

There will probably be no invitation for the Solicitor General's views: the Solicitor General already represents the federal petitioners and has placed the government's position before the Court. P(a new CVSG) is 0.1%.

If the Court takes an issue here, it will concern whether 8 U.S.C. § 1225(b)(2)(A) requires detention without bond of people who entered without inspection and were later arrested in the country's interior, or whether § 1226(a) makes them eligible for bond hearings. This is an inferred formulation from the lower-court opinion, not a quotation of an unavailable petition. A disposition following the lead case will address that statutory question rather than announce a freestanding constitutional rule about all immigration detention. A separate plenary grant to decide this issue anew is much less likely than a companion remand; P(summary route | any grant) is 94%. A summary reversal deciding the entire dispute independently of the lead case is not the modal summary route.

The Court will probably issue no separate writing with the final order. If it denies review, P(a noted dissent or statement respecting denial | denial) is 6%; no particular Justice's writing is the modal forecast. The small independent order-writer probabilities also allow objections to a GVR, especially from Justices Sotomayor or Jackson, without predicting a cert-stage vote lineup. If the lead ruling preserves the Seventh Circuit's result, denial rather than a GVR is the principal alternative; changed custody circumstances or a narrow lead holding could also prevent the expected remand.
