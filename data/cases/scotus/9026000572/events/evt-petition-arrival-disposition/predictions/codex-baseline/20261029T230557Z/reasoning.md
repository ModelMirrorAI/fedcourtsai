## Why 80%, and why GVR

I move moderately above the 73.30% federal-arrival anchor to **P(any grant)=0.80** because an acknowledged multi-circuit conflict and an already-granted lead make companion treatment unusually plausible. The government has lost on the central statutory question below. If the lead decision rejects the bond-eligibility rule, this judgment is a natural candidate for vacatur and reconsideration without another round of plenary argument. The predicted label is therefore **gvr**, with `granted=1`; it is not a prediction that this particular petition will become the lead merits vehicle.

The increase is limited, rather than a move close to certainty, because cert has been granted in the lead but its merits have not been decided. A lead decision upholding the contrary statutory construction can produce denial here. A narrow or procedurally limited lead ruling, individual custody developments, or arguments in the unprovided petition and opposition can also change the route. The 80% is a judgment about those unresolved contingencies, not a measured conditional success rate from the statpack. The 94% summary-route probability is conditional on a grant and implies approximately 75.2% unconditional probability of a cert-order disposition, mostly a GVR, with 4.8% for a separate plenary grant. It is not itself P(grant).

The 99.5% distribution increment reflects the likelihood of at least an initial distribution from the frozen count of zero; it should not be read as a near-certain true relist. The 0.1% CVSG increment reflects existing Solicitor General participation. The 6% denial-writing forecast is conditional on denial, while the per-Justice order-writer figures are unconditional and also cover possible GVR writings; no per-Justice vote is claimed. The low writing probabilities reflect likely routine companion treatment and are subjective, not estimates from a published per-Justice cert-writing baseline.

The **0.90 big-case score** concerns stakes if the legal issue is decided: eligibility for release hearings for a broad population and the scope of a nationwide detention policy. It does not mean this companion is 90% likely to obtain review or receive separate argument. Although this petition's individual remedy is narrower than the policy dispute, its underlying statutory issue is highly consequential.
