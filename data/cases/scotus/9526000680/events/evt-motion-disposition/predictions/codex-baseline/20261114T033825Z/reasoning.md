## Evidence against relief

The controlling stay framework requires a meaningful prospect of certiorari, a fair prospect of reversal, and likely irreparable harm; urgency alone is insufficient. See *Hollingsworth v. Perry*, 558 U.S. 183, 190 (2010), independently checked through CourtListener opinion 1747. The legal obstacle is principally the first two requirements, not a dismissal of the applicant's disability or the personal stakes.

First, the application describes unpublished state orders and an unfinished foreclosure proceeding. Its own account says the state appellate courts dismissed review for lack of a final foreclosure judgment (printed page 26). Completing a particular interlocutory appellate route does not, by itself, establish reviewability of the underlying litigation under section 1257. The Cox Broadcasting exception is more constrained than a general claim that postponement would harm federal interests: the retrieved majority passage addresses a finally decided federal issue, a potentially litigation-ending reversal, and erosion of federal policy. See *Cox Broadcasting Corp. v. Cohn*, 420 U.S. 469, 482–83 (1975), CourtListener opinion 9426016. Here the application says the federal claims were not adjudicated, and success would principally change the process for further foreclosure litigation. That makes the invoked exception uncertain rather than plainly controlling.

Second, the filing presents an intensely factual dispute about accommodations, counsel, the scope of an earlier order, changed mortgage theories, and the adequacy of state procedures. It identifies no developed split of authority. Citations to established disability-access and due-process principles do not independently show why this particular interlocutory vehicle warrants Supreme Court intervention. I assess that as a weak review vehicle without deciding whether the allegations are true.

Third, the requested order is broad: it would stop the hearing and successor proceedings, restrain pursuit and enforcement of the liability orders, and preserve a specified status quo pending neutral adjudication and Supreme Court review, without security (printed pages 30–31). The record does not establish that the November 16 hearing immediately transfers title or eliminates subsequent state remedies. I do not assume that a hearing date is itself a completed dispossession. Narrower interim relief remains possible and is included in the 3% grant probability.
