## Case-Specific Downward Adjustments

Starting from the `11.7%` scored pool rate and leaning on the `7.0%` private non-capital descriptive cut as initial context, sharp downward adjustments drive `P(granted)` to `0.005` because the application fails every prong of *Hollingsworth v. Perry*, 558 U.S. 183, 190 (2010):

1. **Threshold Lack of Finality Under 28 U.S.C. § 1257(a)**:
   Applicant seeks a stay pending a forthcoming certiorari petition to the Connecticut Supreme Court in an **unfinished** residential mortgage foreclosure. On December 1, 2025, the Connecticut Superior Court entered summary judgment as to **liability only**; it has not yet determined the mortgage debt, appraised property value, form of foreclosure (strict vs. sale), or law days. Applying settled Connecticut law, *Essex Savings Bank v. Frimberger*, 26 Conn. App. 80, 597 A.2d 1289 (1991), the Connecticut Appellate Court dismissed Applicant's interlocutory appeal (`AC 49777`, Apr. 30, 2026) and supervisory motion (`AC 254239`, Mar. 25, 2026) for lack of a final foreclosure judgment, and the Connecticut Supreme Court denied certification (`PSC-250444`, June 2, 2026) and relief on reconsideration (`SC 250398`, Sept. 11/15, 2026).

   Applicant invokes *Cox Broadcasting Corp. v. Cohn*, 420 U.S. 469 (1975), *National Socialist Party of America v. Skokie*, 432 U.S. 43 (1977) (per curiam), and *Malliotakis v. Williams*, No. 25A914 (U.S. Mar. 2, 2026), to claim immediate § 1257(a) finality. None fits. Unlike *Skokie* (an active First Amendment prior restraint on speech during years of appellate delay) or *Malliotakis* (a mandatory state-court racial-redistricting injunction ahead of imminent 2026 congressional election deadlines that could not be undone post-election), further Superior Court foreclosure proceedings are neither ministerial nor unreviewable. Under *Flynt v. Ohio*, 451 U.S. 619, 620–22 (1981) (per curiam), the burden of participating in ongoing state trial proceedings does not satisfy *Cox*'s fourth exception where all federal claims merge into and remain fully reviewable on appeal from the final judgment.

2. **Adequate State Procedural Ground and Timeliness Cloud**:
   Connecticut's *Frimberger* rule is a neutral, general rule of state appellate jurisdiction allocating review of interlocutory liability, continuance, and recusal rulings to the post-judgment appeal. Under *Johnson v. Fankell*, 520 U.S. 911, 916–23 (1997), States have no federal obligation to provide immediate interlocutory appeals even where federal rights are framed as protections against trial burdens. Moreover, certification in `PSC-250444`—the docket named on the Supreme Court's caption—was denied on June 2, 2026, more than 90 days before November 11, 2026 (28 U.S.C. § 2101(c)), forcing Applicant to rely on a collateral September 11/15, 2026 order in a separate docket (`SC 250398`, described in the application itself as a "related pre-appeal reconsideration motion") to assert timeliness.
