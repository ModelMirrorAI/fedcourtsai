## In brief

The Supreme Court declined to revive Mohammed Jabateh's bid to overturn his immigration-fraud convictions through a post-conviction challenge.

## What happened

Jabateh, convicted of immigration fraud and perjury for false statements made during a 2011 immigration interview, was sentenced to 30 years. On direct appeal, the Third Circuit agreed that the fraud statute, properly read, covers only written statements, not his oral ones—but it still upheld those convictions because the error was not "plain" under the standard for unpreserved arguments. Jabateh then sought post-conviction relief, arguing the convictions were unconstitutional. The district court and the Third Circuit both rejected this, ruling that a "relitigation bar" prevented him from reusing the same statutory argument, and that his trial lawyer was not ineffective for failing to raise it.

## What the Court is being asked

The petition asks whether a rule barring prisoners from reusing issues already decided on direct appeal can block post-conviction relief, where the appeals court had actually agreed the conduct wasn't covered by the statute but still upheld the conviction because the error wasn't obvious enough to count under the rules for unpreserved claims. Jabateh argues this combination unfairly forecloses any path to relief from what he says is an unlawful conviction.

## Where it stands

The United States waived its right to respond to the petition. The petition was distributed for the Court's November 13, 2026 conference, and on November 16, 2026, the Court denied it.

## What each outcome would mean

Jabateh, as petitioner, asked the Court to reverse or hear the case. The denial means the Third Circuit's decision stands: Jabateh's fraud convictions and the portion of his sentence tied to them remain in place, and he does not get the post-conviction relief he sought. The denial does not mean the Supreme Court agreed the lower court was right—it simply leaves that ruling undisturbed without deciding the underlying question.
