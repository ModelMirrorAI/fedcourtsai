## In brief

A man convicted of immigration fraud and perjury asks the Court to revive his bid to overturn his fraud convictions through a post-conviction challenge.

## What happened

Mohammed Jabateh was convicted at trial of immigration fraud and perjury, based on false statements he made during a 2011 immigration interview, and sentenced to 30 years. On direct appeal, the Third Circuit agreed that the fraud statute, properly read, covers only written statements, not the oral statements Jabateh made, but it affirmed the fraud convictions anyway because trial counsel had not raised the issue and the error was not "plain" under the standard that applies when an issue is raised for the first time on appeal. Jabateh then filed a motion under a federal law allowing prisoners to challenge their sentences after direct appeal, arguing the fraud convictions were unconstitutional. The district court denied the motion, and the Third Circuit affirmed, holding that a rule against relitigating issues already decided on direct appeal barred relief.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked whether a rule that normally stops prisoners from reusing post-conviction proceedings to relitigate issues already decided against them on direct appeal should also block relief here, where the appeals court's earlier ruling on the same issue actually favored the prisoner on the merits but still upheld the conviction because the error wasn't obvious enough to fix without an objection at trial.

## Where it stands

The petition was filed on September 28, 2026. The government waived its right to respond, and the case was distributed for the Court's November 13, 2026 conference, where the justices will decide whether to take it.

## What each outcome would mean

Jabateh, who lost in the lower courts, is asking the Court to hear his case and reverse. If the Court denies review, the Third Circuit's decision stands, leaving his fraud convictions and consecutive sentence in place. If the Court grants review, it will decide whether the relitigation rule was correctly applied; Jabateh's petition also asks for summary reversal, a quicker route that would overturn the lower court's decision without full briefing or argument. A grant does not itself decide who wins.
