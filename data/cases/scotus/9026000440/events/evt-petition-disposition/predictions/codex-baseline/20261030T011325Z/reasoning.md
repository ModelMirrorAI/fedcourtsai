## Why the asserted error does not make review likely

The petition, especially pp. 9–19, invokes Davis v. United States, 417 U.S. 333 (1974), Fiore v. White, 531 U.S. 225 (2001), and Bousley v. United States, 523 U.S. 614 (1998). Its core contention is that the relitigation rule cannot defeat relief from a conviction for conduct outside the charged statute, and that procedural default was waived or overcome by innocence.

The appendix, App. 5a–9a, explains the narrower obstacle. The panel distinguished relitigation from procedural default and declined to treat a favorable statutory construction announced in the prisoner's own direct appeal as a later intervening change in law. It also rejected deficient performance on the ineffective-assistance claim, rather than resolving Strickland prejudice. App. 14a–20a sets out the district court's related reasoning. The government's alleged waiver of procedural default does not by itself answer this distinct relitigation rationale.

I checked Davis through CourtListener MCP. Its discussion at 417 U.S. 341–342 concerns an intervening legal change after the earlier appeal. That does not settle Jabateh's claim against him, but makes the requested summary reversal less straightforward than the petition suggests. The absence of an intervening decision here is an important distinction. The appendix does not contain a separate adjudication of an actual-innocence gateway; I do not assume that a court has accepted the petition's characterization of that exception. Nor do I confuse exclusion from §1546(a) with factual innocence of the conduct supporting the unaffected perjury counts.

The petition and appendix also mention denial of a different certiorari petition in 2021, following the direct appeal. That is provisioned historical context, not the disposition of the present collateral-review petition. I have not sought or encountered the present petition's outcome.
