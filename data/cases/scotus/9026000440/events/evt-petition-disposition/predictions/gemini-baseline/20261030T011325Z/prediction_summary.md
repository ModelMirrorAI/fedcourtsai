The Supreme Court is very likely to deny Mohammed Jabateh's petition for a writ of certiorari (a 2% chance of a grant, or about one in fifty). Although Jabateh raises a striking legal paradox—the Third Circuit held on his first appeal that the immigration-fraud statute did not cover his oral interview statements, yet upheld his 20-year consecutive sentence because the error was not "obvious" and then barred his post-conviction challenge under 28 U.S.C. § 2255 as an impermissible "relitigation" of the first appeal—the decision below is unpublished, creates no conflict among the federal appeals courts, and arises from a trial record involving severe wartime atrocities. Moreover, the United States waived its right to respond and no outside friend-of-the-court briefs were filed, strongly signaling that the Court will deny review without further briefing.
