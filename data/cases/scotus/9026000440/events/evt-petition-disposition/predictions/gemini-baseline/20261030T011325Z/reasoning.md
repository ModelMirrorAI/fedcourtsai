3. **Prior Cert Denial in the Same Case and Unsympathetic Factual Equities (Downward Adjustment):**

For summary reversal—an extraordinary remedy the petition urges—severe factual equities and the existence of a colorable procedural counterargument (that under *United States v. 4. **Countervailing Doctrinal Tension (Why P(grant) is 2.0% Rather Than <0.5%):**
   The petition is drafted by experienced appellate counsel (Peter Goldberger) and highlights a genuine doctrinal catch-22:
   - On direct appeal, the Third Circuit precedentially held (`974 F.3d at 297`) that Jabateh's oral interview statements did *not* violate 18 U.S.C. § 1546(a)(¶4), which covers only false statements made *in* an application, affidavit, or other document, yet affirmed the 20-year consecutive sentences on Counts One and Two because the novel statutory interpretation was not "obvious" under Rule 52(b).
   - On § 2255 review, Jabateh invoked *Fiore v. White*, 531 U.S. 225 (2001), *Davis v. United States*, 417 U.S. 333 (1974), and *Bousley v. United States*, 523 U.S. 614 (1998), arguing that serving 20 years of imprisonment for conduct that the statute does not make criminal violates Due Process and constitutes "actual innocence" overcoming any procedural default (which the government did not even raise in the § 2255 proceedings).
   - The Third Circuit panel held that the § 2255 "relitigation bar" foreclosed relief because the § 1546(a) claim was raised and rejected on plain-error review on direct appeal, and the "change in law" exception did not apply because the statutory holding occurred *on* Jabateh's direct appeal rather than *after* it.
   This catch-22 (where winning the statutory interpretation argument on direct appeal but losing on Rule 52(b) "obviousness" leaves a defendant worse off under the relitigation bar than a defendant who never raised the issue on direct appeal and invoked *Bousley* actual innocence on § 2255, or a third-party defendant who later invokes *Jabateh* as an intervening change in circuit law under *Davis*) is intellectually striking.
