# Predicted reasoning — Jabateh v. United States, No. 26-440

**Disposition: denied, without a call for a response.** The docket shows one
distribution (for the 13 November 2026 conference), entered after the United
States waived its right to respond on 21 October. No relist and no CVSG.

- **Further distribution: unlikely (about 15%).** The most likely path is denial
  at the 13 November conference. A further distribution comes mainly if a
  Justice asks for a response to the waived petition. The petition asks for
  summary reversal and argues the Third Circuit contradicted *Davis v. United
  States* and *Bousley*, so a call for a response is possible, though less
  likely than not. If one is called for, the petition is redistributed once the
  government's brief is in, early in 2027.
- **CVSG: no.** The United States is the respondent, so the Court has no reason
  to invite the Solicitor General's views. It would call for a response instead.
- **Question the Court would take, if any:** the single QP as written. Does
  the § 2255 relitigation bar foreclose collateral relief where, on direct
  appeal, the circuit agreed the conduct fell outside § 1546(a)'s text but
  affirmed under plain error because the point was not "obvious"? The
  ineffective-assistance and party-presentation complaints would not be taken.
- **Summary route, conditional on any grant:** close to even. The petition
  itself asks for summary reversal and cites recent per curiam reversals. A
  grant would most likely come only after the government responds, perhaps by
  confessing error, followed by a GVR or a summary vacatur. Plenary review of
  the relitigation-bar question is the other route.
- **Separate writing on denial:** unlikely. If anyone writes, the likeliest are
  Justice Gorsuch (who writes on plain-error rigidity and on liberty interests in
  criminal cases) or Justice Sotomayor or Justice Jackson (who write on
  collateral-review barriers to relief for actual innocence). Most likely, no
  one writes.
