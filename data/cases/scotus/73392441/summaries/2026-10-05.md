## In brief

The Supreme Court declined to review a homeowner's challenge to a Washington state court ruling that reformed a deed of trust on his property through summary judgment rather than trial.

## What happened

Alvin White owned residential property in Kitsap County, Washington, subject to a deed of trust. U.S. Bank Trust, N.A. sued to reform the deed, claiming its legal description was erroneous; U.S. Bank National Association later replaced it as plaintiff. The case went to cross-motions for summary judgment, where the trial judge examined a disputed promissory note, commented on its signatures and indentations, and found it appeared authentic, then ruled for the bank without a trial. White objected, arguing this resolved disputed facts without proper trial procedures. The Washington Court of Appeals affirmed, and the Washington Supreme Court denied further review.

## What the Court is being asked

White asks the Court to decide whether a judge can resolve genuinely disputed facts about property rights, such as whether a document is authentic, by weighing evidence and making findings during summary judgment, rather than sending those disputes to a trial. He also asks whether doing so violates guarantees of fair procedure before property is taken and the right to a jury trial in certain civil cases.

## Where it stands

The petition was distributed for the Court's conference on September 28, 2026. On October 5, 2026, the Court denied the petition.

## What each outcome would mean

White, the homeowner, asked the Court to reverse the Washington Court of Appeals' decision, which had sided with U.S. Bank. By denying the petition, the Court leaves that lower court ruling in place: the reformation of the deed of trust and the judgment against White stand. The denial does not mean the Court agreed the trial court's procedure was constitutional; it simply leaves the state court decision undisturbed without the Court deciding the constitutional questions White raised.
