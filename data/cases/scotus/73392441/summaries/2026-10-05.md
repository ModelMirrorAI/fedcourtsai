## In brief

The Supreme Court declined to review a property owner's challenge to a judge deciding factual disputes on summary judgment rather than at trial.

## What happened

A bank sued Alvin White in Washington state court, seeking to reform a deed of trust on his property, claiming its legal description was wrong. U.S. Bank later replaced the original bank as plaintiff. Both sides sought summary judgment, a ruling without trial, and White argued genuine factual disputes remained about the authenticity of the underlying promissory note and the lien's scope. The trial judge examined the note's signature and physical features, found it appeared original, and granted summary judgment for the bank. Washington's Court of Appeals affirmed, and the Washington Supreme Court denied further review.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked whether a judge may decide disputed factual questions affecting someone's property rights by weighing evidence and making findings while ruling on summary judgment, instead of sending those disputes to a trial. White also asks whether that practice conflicts with constitutional guarantees of fair procedure before property is taken and with the right to a jury trial in cases historically tried to juries.

## Where it stands

The petition was distributed for the Court's September 28, 2026 conference, and on October 5, 2026 the Court denied the petition.

## What each outcome would mean

White, the property owner, asked the Court to take up the case after losing in the Washington courts. With the petition denied, the Washington Court of Appeals' decision stands: the trial court's summary judgment ruling in favor of U.S. Bank, including its findings on the note's authenticity, remains in effect. The denial does not mean the Supreme Court agreed the lower courts were right, only that it left their rulings undisturbed without deciding the constitutional questions White raised.
