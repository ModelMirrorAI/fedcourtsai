## Why 0.8%, below the anchor

The petition describes an action to reform a deed of trust's property description, followed by cross-motions for summary judgment, an unpublished Washington Court of Appeals affirmance, and denial of discretionary state review (petition pp. 1, 3–4). It argues that the trial judge improperly resolved authenticity and enforceability disputes through physical inspection of a promissory note rather than a trial (pp. 6–12). These are petitioner's characterizations, not independently established findings from the missing appendix.

Three considerations drive a substantial downward adjustment:

1. **No developed decisional split.** The petition identifies asserted departures from general constitutional and summary-judgment principles, but does not develop a conflict between courts of last resort over a distinct federal rule. Its additional Washington example describes the same alleged practice, not opposing holdings. The unpublished, record-specific decision makes this look primarily like a request for error correction. The current Supreme Court Rules, effective March 16, 2026, Rule 10, distinguish compelling review grounds from alleged factual error or misapplication of an otherwise correct rule. I retrieved that rule directly from the Court's official PDF.
2. **A state-court vehicle for federal-court theories.** The petition's second question invokes Article III and the Seventh Amendment, although the challenged proceedings occurred in Washington state court. Minneapolis & St. Louis Railroad Co. v. Bombolis, 241 U.S. 211, 217–19 (1916), retrieved through CourtListener, expressly distinguishes Seventh Amendment restrictions from state-court procedure. The petition's due-process theory is therefore more pertinent than a direct transfer of federal civil-jury guarantees. Its own acknowledgment that the action seeks equitable relief adds a separate complication to the asserted jury entitlement (p. 12). I do not assume that all disputed facts in an equitable action carry an independent federal jury right.
3. **The disputed premise needs the lower record.** A judge describing an instrument does not, by itself, establish that a genuine material factual dispute was improperly adjudicated. The missing appendix matters: I cannot independently assess the contrary evidence, the appellate grounds, or preservation of each federal theory. Cross-motions do not eliminate genuine factual disputes, but neither does the petition's assertion establish one. The petition's claim that the vehicle is clean (pp. 13–14) is advocacy, not a substitute for those checks.

The affirmative consideration is the petition's concrete account of judicial inspection and its quotations from the summary-judgment discussion, supported by its citations to Anderson and Tolan (pp. 10–12). If the full record demonstrated a clear procedural violation, some grant route remains possible. I have not independently verified those alleged applications to this dispute. This keeps the probability above zero, but does not offset the lack of a developed split and the state/equity complications. The final 0.8% is a judgmental forecast, not a fitted estimate or a measured subpopulation rate.
