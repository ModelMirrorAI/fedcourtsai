# Predicted reasoning — what the Court will do with No. 25-1310

**Disposition: certiorari denied, without comment, on the order list following the September 28, 2026 long conference (expected October 5, 2026).**

## Relists

The docket shows **one distribution** (July 8, 2026, for the Conference of September 28, 2026) and no relist. I expect **no further distribution**: the petition will be considered once at the long conference and denied on the first October order list. The one realistic path to a second distribution is a call for a response — no brief in opposition and no waiver is on the docket, so the Court is considering the petition without any response — after which it would be redistributed once the response arrived. I put that path, plus an ordinary reschedule or relist, at roughly one in eight.

## CVSG

**No call for the views of the Solicitor General.** The parties are a private homeowner-trustee and a private securitization trustee; the underlying claim is reformation of a deed of trust under Washington law; no federal statute, agency, or program is implicated. There is nothing for the United States to have a view on.

## Which question presented, if any

If the Court took anything, it would not be either question as written. QP 1 (may a court resolve disputed facts "in the course of granting summary judgment") is framed as a general attack on summary judgment practice and rests on a single trial-court remark about a note appearing to be an original. QP 2 invokes the Seventh Amendment against a state court, where it has never been held to apply. The only conceivable vehicle would be a narrowed, fact-bound question — whether the state courts applied a federal due-process rule against judicial fact-finding at summary judgment — and the Court will not reformulate a petition to reach that.

## Summary disposition vs plenary review

Conditional on the very unlikely event of a grant, a **summary route is the likelier one** (a bit over even odds): a Tolan v. Cotton-style per curiam correcting fact-weighing at summary judgment is the only plausible form a grant here could take, since no intervening decision supports a GVR and the question as framed is not one the Court would set for argument. A plenary grant is the less likely arm of an already remote branch.

## Dissent or statement respecting denial

**None expected.** No Justice has shown appetite to revisit the constitutionality of summary judgment, the Seventh Amendment prong fails at the threshold in a state case, and the record is a private deed dispute decided in an unpublished opinion. A statement respecting denial is very unlikely.

## Merits, conditionally

If granted and decided, the likely ground would be a narrow application of the settled rule that a court at summary judgment identifies genuine disputes rather than resolving them, confined to the trial court's on-the-record "finding" about the note's authenticity — not any holding about the constitutionality of summary judgment or the reach of the Seventh Amendment.
