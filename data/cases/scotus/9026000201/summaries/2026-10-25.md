## In brief

The federal government asks the Court to review a ruling that struck down enforcement powers Congress gave to the private body overseeing thoroughbred horseracing.

## What happened

Congress created the Horseracing Integrity and Safety Authority, a private corporation, to write and enforce nationwide rules for thoroughbred racing, under Federal Trade Commission oversight. Horse trainers, owners, racetracks, and Texas sued, arguing the arrangement unconstitutionally hands government power to a private entity. After earlier rounds of litigation and a congressional fix to the rulemaking process, the Fifth Circuit ruled that HISA's rulemaking structure is now constitutional but that its enforcement provisions are not: the Authority can investigate, subpoena, search, fine, and sue without the FTC's advance approval. The Sixth Circuit has upheld the same enforcement provisions in a separate case.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked whether it is constitutional for Congress to let a private organization investigate, charge, and penalize rule violations in an industry, with a federal agency reviewing only some decisions after the fact, or whether that arrangement hands government enforcement power to a private actor without adequate federal control.

## Where it stands

The petition has been fully briefed, with responses from multiple respondents and a reply from the federal government. The docket does not yet show the case distributed for a conference or any further action.

## What each outcome would mean

The federal government, along with the Authority, is asking the Court to reverse the Fifth Circuit and uphold HISA's enforcement provisions; the horsemen's groups and other respondents, who won on this point below, ask the Court to affirm. If the Court denies review, the Fifth Circuit's ruling striking down the enforcement provisions stands. If the Court grants review, it will decide the question on the merits: affirming would leave the enforcement provisions invalidated, reversing would uphold them, and vacating would send the case back without resolving the issue for either side.
