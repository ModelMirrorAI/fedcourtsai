## In brief

The federal government asks the Court to review a ruling that a private horseracing regulator's enforcement powers are unconstitutional.

## What happened

Congress created the Horseracing Integrity and Safety Authority, a private corporation, to write and enforce nationwide safety and anti-doping rules for thoroughbred racing, under oversight from the Federal Trade Commission. The National Horsemen's Benevolent and Protective Association and others sued, arguing the arrangement hands government power to a private entity. After earlier rounds of litigation and a congressional amendment, the Fifth Circuit ruled that the Authority's rulemaking role is now constitutional but that its enforcement powers—investigating, subpoenaing, searching, fining, and suing covered persons—are not, because the FTC's oversight comes too late and too narrowly. The FTC and the Authority separately petitioned for review.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked whether a law can let a private organization investigate, prosecute, and penalize rule violations in an industry it regulates, with only limited, after-the-fact review by a federal agency, or whether that arrangement gives away government enforcement power that the Constitution requires federal officials to control.

## Where it stands

The petition was distributed for the Court's conference of December 4, 2026, after full briefing including responses from multiple respondents and a reply from the government; the docket shows no action since that distribution.

## What each outcome would mean

The federal government, as petitioner, is asking the Court to reverse the Fifth Circuit's ruling that struck down the enforcement provisions, a ruling the horsemen's groups and other respondents won below. If the Court grants review, it will decide the enforcement question on the merits; a decision to affirm would leave the private Authority unable to enforce rules without more FTC control, while reversing would let the current enforcement scheme stand. If the Court denies review, the Fifth Circuit's decision against the enforcement provisions remains in effect for this case.
