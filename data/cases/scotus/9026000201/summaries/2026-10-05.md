## In brief

The federal government asks the Court to review a ruling that struck down, on its face, the enforcement powers Congress gave a private horseracing authority under federal law.

## What happened

Congress created the Horseracing Integrity and Safety Authority, a private nonprofit, to help the Federal Trade Commission regulate doping and safety in horseracing. Horsemen's associations and others sued, claiming the law unconstitutionally hands government power to a private entity. After earlier rulings and a congressional fix to the law's rulemaking provisions, the Fifth Circuit held that the law's rulemaking provisions were now valid, but that its enforcement provisions—letting the Authority investigate, subpoena, sanction, and sue—still improperly operate without adequate FTC control, and declared them facially unconstitutional. The FTC and the Authority separately ask the Supreme Court to review that ruling.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked whether a law can let a private organization help enforce federal rules—investigating, penalizing, and suing regulated businesses—without that arrangement itself violating constitutional limits on handing government power to private actors, given that a federal agency can later review some of the private group's decisions.

## Where it stands

The petition has been fully briefed: respondents filed responses, an amicus brief was submitted, and the government filed its reply on September 30, 2026. The docket shows no action yet on whether the Court will hear the case.

## What each outcome would mean

The Federal Trade Commission and other federal officials, as petitioners, are asking the Court to take up the case. If the Court grants review, it will hear full arguments and decide whether the enforcement provisions are constitutional, deciding nothing yet. If it denies review, the Fifth Circuit's ruling striking down those enforcement provisions as facially unconstitutional stays in place, leaving the horsemen's associations and Texas, who challenged the law, with their win below.
