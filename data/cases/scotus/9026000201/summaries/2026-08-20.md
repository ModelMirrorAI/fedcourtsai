## In brief

The federal government asks the Court to review a ruling that struck down part of a federal horseracing law as giving improper enforcement power to a private organization.

## What happened

Congress created the Horseracing Integrity and Safety Act, under which a private nonprofit, the Horseracing Integrity and Safety Authority, proposes rules and handles enforcement for the horseracing industry, subject to Federal Trade Commission oversight. Horsemen's associations and others sued, arguing this arrangement improperly hands government power to a private entity. After earlier rulings and a congressional amendment giving the FTC more rulemaking control, the Fifth Circuit held that the law's rulemaking structure was now constitutional, but that its enforcement provisions—letting the Authority investigate, subpoena, sanction, and sue without needing FTC approval beforehand—were unconstitutional on their face. The FTC now asks the Supreme Court to reverse that enforcement ruling.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked whether letting a private organization help enforce this law, subject to the government agency's after-the-fact review, crosses a constitutional line against giving governmental power to private parties, or whether it is a valid arrangement because the agency retains ultimate oversight.

## Where it stands

The petition for certiorari was filed on August 14, 2026, with a response due September 16, 2026. The Court has not yet acted on it.

## What each outcome would mean

The federal government, along with the Authority in a related filing, is asking the Court to take up the case. If the Court grants the petition, it would hear full arguments and decide whether the enforcement provisions are constitutional, deciding nothing yet on who is right. If it denies the petition, the Fifth Circuit's ruling striking down those enforcement provisions as unconstitutional would stand, leaving the horsemen's associations and Texas, who challenged the law, with that win intact.
