## What happened

Congress created the Horseracing Integrity and Safety Act, which lets a private nonprofit, the Horseracing Integrity and Safety Authority, write and enforce rules for the horseracing industry alongside the Federal Trade Commission. Horsemen's associations, racetracks, and Texas sued, arguing the arrangement improperly hands government power to a private entity. After earlier rounds of litigation and a congressional amendment giving the FTC more control over rulemaking, the Fifth Circuit ruled that the law's rulemaking provisions were now fine, but that its enforcement provisions—covering investigations, subpoenas, searches, fines, and lawsuits—still let the Authority act without enough FTC control, making them unconstitutional on their face. The FTC now asks the Supreme Court to review that ruling.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked whether letting a private organization help investigate, charge, and penalize people in the horseracing industry, subject to later review by the FTC, crosses a constitutional line that limits how much governmental power can be handed to private entities. The FTC argues its oversight is enough because it reviews sanctions and can adjust the private group's rules; the horsemen's groups and Texas argue the private entity effectively controls enforcement without the FTC's real supervision.

## Where it stands

The petition for certiorari, which asks the Court to take up the case, was filed on August 14, 2026, with a response due by September 16, 2026. The Court has not yet acted on the petition, and a related petition from the Authority itself is also pending.
