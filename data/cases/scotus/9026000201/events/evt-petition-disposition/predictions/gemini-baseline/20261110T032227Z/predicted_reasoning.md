Under modern Court practice, petitions slated for plenary grant are routinely relisted at least once to verify vehicle cleanliness and coordinate consolidation and question-selection across multiple related dockets—here, deciding whether to grant only the enforcement private-nondelegation question presented in Nos. 26-199 and 26-201 while holding *Oklahoma* (No. 25-1325) and denying or holding the challengers' cross-petitions on rulemaking nondelegation (No. 26-335) and the Appointments Clause (No. 26-332). Conversely, there is a substantial chance (`0.45`) that the Court grants directly out of the December 4, 2026 Conference without a relist because the Court is already thoroughly familiar with this exact litigation (having stayed the Fifth Circuit's mandate in No. 24A287 and GVR'd Nos. 24-429 and 24-433 in June 2025) and December 4 sits near the cutoff for the current Term's spring argument calendar.

## Scope of Grant and Question Presented

When the Court grants certiorari, it will grant No. 26-201 and consolidate it with the Horseracing Integrity and Safety Authority's companion petition in No. 26-199 for briefing and oral argument, limited to the single question presented in both petitions: whether the enforcement provisions of the Horseracing Integrity and Safety Act of 2020 (HISA), 15 U.S.C. § 3051 *et seq.*, facially violate the private nondelegation doctrine. The Court is unlikely to grant the additional rulemaking-nondelegation question in Texas's cross-petition (No. 26-335) or the Appointments Clause question in Gulf Coast Racing's cross-petition (No. 26-332), as there is no circuit split on either issue—the Fifth, Sixth, and Eighth Circuits have all upheld HISA's amended rulemaking provisions and no court of appeals has held that the Authority's directors are federal officers under *Lebron v. National Railroad Passenger Corp.*, 513 U.S. 374 (1995). The Court will hold *Oklahoma v. United States* (No. 25-1325) pending the disposition of Nos. 26-199 and 26-201.

If granted, the merits disposition will likely turn on two interrelated doctrinal axes under *Sunshine Anthracite Coal Co. v. Adkins*, 310 U.S. 381 (1940), *FCC v. Consumers' Research*, 606 U.S. 656 (2025), and *United States v. Rahimi*, 602 U.S. 680 (2024): (1) whether the FTC's front-end rulemaking authority over the Authority's investigatory and enforcement rules (15 U.S.C. §§ 3053(a)(9)–(10), 3053(e), 3054(c)) combined with back-end *de novo* ALJ and Commission review of sanctions (15 U.S.C. § 3058) renders the Authority sufficiently subordinate to the FTC, and (2) whether, under *Rahimi* and *Moody v. NetChoice, LLC*, 603 U.S. 707 (2024), the undisputed existence of straightforward enforcement applications where the FTC reviews the full evidentiary record *de novo* (and the FTC's December 2025 rule requiring prior Commission approval for subpoenas or civil injunction suits) defeats a facial private-nondelegation challenge, leaving contested front-end investigative practices to as-applied challenges.

## Summary Disposition vs. Plenary Review (`summary-disposition-route`: 0.03)

Conditional on a grant, plenary review with full merits briefing and oral argument is overwhelmingly likely (`0.97`), and a summary disposition or GVR is very unlikely (`0.03`). The Court already GVR'd this case once in light of *FCC v. Consumers' Research*, 606 U.S. 656 (2025) (see *FTC v. National Horsemen's Benevolent & Protective Ass'n*, 145 S. Ct. 2835 (2025)), and on remand both the Fifth Circuit (178 F.4th 224) and the Sixth Circuit (*Oklahoma v. United States*, 163 F.4th 294) issued comprehensive published opinions reaching opposite conclusions under *Consumers' Research*. No new intervening precedent is pending that would warrant a second GVR, save for the small residual possibility (`~0.03`) that the Court grants only one companion docket (such as No. 26-199 or No. 25-1325), holds No. 26-201, and subsequently disposes of No. 26-201 via a post-merits GVR. Nor is the Court likely to summarily reverse a 45-page published court of appeals opinion invalidating an Act of Congress on a major constitutional question where all parties request plenary argument.
