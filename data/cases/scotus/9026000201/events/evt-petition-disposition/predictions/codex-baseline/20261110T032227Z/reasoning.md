## Why this petition is substantially above the anchor

1. **A direct, acknowledged conflict on the same federal statute.** The petition's introduction and reasons for review (printed pp. 2–3, 8, 14–15) identify the Fifth Circuit's facial invalidation of HISA's enforcement provisions and the Sixth Circuit's contrary decision. The staged `decision-below.txt` is dated June 11, 2026 and bears No. 23-10520, matching the snapshot and petition. Its opening expressly declares the enforcement provisions facially unconstitutional and acknowledges disagreement with the Sixth Circuit. This is not merely a petitioner's assertion that different fact patterns should have come out alike.

2. **The federal government seeks review of a judgment invalidating federal legislation.** The petition presents both the circuit conflict and statutory invalidation as independent reasons to grant. The question matters to the operation of a national regulatory scheme; leaving the conflict in place has practical consequences beyond one enforcement proceeding. These features overlap with the federal-band selection, so I do not multiply their effects as independent evidence. They nevertheless distinguish this petition from federal petitions with only narrower error-correction claims.

3. **The responding parties agree that review is warranted.** `brief-in-opposition.txt` combines three responses, not three briefs all asking for denial. The Horsemen expressly acquiesce and favor this vehicle (printed pp. 1, 13–14, 38–41). Gulf Coast likewise acquiesces, while seeking an additional grant on the Appointments Clause (printed pp. 1–4). Texas does not oppose review of the enforcement ruling but wants the rulemaking issue considered too (printed p. 3). Disagreement concerns the merits, the wording of the question, and the scope of companion review, rather than whether the enforcement conflict exists.

4. **A previous remand has not removed the disagreement.** The petition (printed pp. 7–8), the responses, and the matched lower-court opinion describe the earlier GVR following *FCC v. Consumers' Research* and the Fifth Circuit's reissuance of its enforcement holding. That earlier disposition is pre-petition procedural history, not the disposition being forecast. Because the remand has already been tried and the conflict persists, another summary remand in light of the same precedent is less persuasive than plenary review.

5. **The immediate companion is synchronized and addresses the same judgment.** The provisioned `related/26-199.json` shows the Authority's parallel petition from the same Fifth Circuit judgment, with the same December 4 conference. The Gulf Coast and Texas petitions, Nos. 26-332 and 26-335, are also distributed for that conference. Each related-file row is tagged `vided-amicus`, with one shared brief, not an official `Vide` relationship. I treat them as actual related litigation because their docket identities, parties, judgment, and the staged reply independently establish the connection, not because one shared amicus alone proves it. None of these snapshots shows a grant. The government's reply expressly asks for grants and consolidation of Nos. 26-201 and 26-199, denials of the Gulf Coast and Texas petitions, and a hold of Oklahoma No. 25-1325.

## Why the estimate is not certainty

The strongest vehicle concern is identified in the September reply (printed pp. 2–3): whether unresolved district-court claims prevented a final judgment and thus appellate jurisdiction. The government says the claims were voluntarily withdrawn and then expressly abandoned on appeal. I have that account, not a complete independently obtained district-court record, and retain some risk that the Court prefers Oklahoma or grants only a different petition. Oklahoma's current docket was not provisioned or retrieved; its status and comparative advantages here come from the staged filings, not an assumed current grant. A different lead vehicle could produce a later grant-and-remand here, which still counts as a grant under this contract; it could instead leave this petition denied. Companion management, possible intervening developments, and summary correction account for the residual uncertainty. The 0.96 is a judgmental adjustment from the frozen anchor, not the output of a fitted cert model.

The scope dispute is real but less threatening to the enforcement question than to the additional questions. The government accepts a neutral formulation omitting its contested description of the Authority as merely assisting the FTC. The competing accounts of front-end enforcement control and back-end de novo review make a disputed merits case; they do not themselves make certiorari unlikely.

## Procedural and writing claims

- **Further distribution: 0.65.** From the one-distribution baseline, I expect one additional distribution, with a smaller tail for two or more. Strong grant indicators support a short additional examination and coordination among petitions; the already complete briefing also makes a grant at the first scheduled conference a substantial alternative. The pack does not supply a conditional forward hazard for this combination of features.
- **New CVSG: 0.005.** There is no invitation in the snapshot. The Solicitor General already represents the federal petitioners and has filed both a petition and reply, so an invitation to obtain the government's views would add little. This is a judgment about this record, not use of the terminal CVSG cut as a hazard.
- **Summary route conditional on any grant: 0.06.** Plenary review is strongly preferred because the prior GVR failed to resolve a live conflict and the parties contest substantial enforcement and facial-challenge questions. The residual includes a later GVR if another petition becomes the lead vehicle or a new decision intervenes. The implied unconditional split is about 0.9024 plenary grant, 0.0576 summary grant, and 0.04 no grant.
- **Dissent or statement conditional on denial: 0.35.** Denial would be unusual given statutory invalidation and agreed need for review, making an explanation or objection plausible, although silence remains likelier even on that branch. The order-writer probabilities in `prediction.json` are unconditional and small because the overwhelmingly likely plenary grant is ordinarily unexplained. Their sum is not a probability of any writing; the Justice-specific events may overlap. They do not forecast who will author a future merits opinion.
