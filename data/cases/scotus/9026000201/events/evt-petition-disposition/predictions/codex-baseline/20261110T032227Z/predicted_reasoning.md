# Forecast of the Court's action

The Court will **grant certiorari in No. 26-201 for plenary review**, most likely together with the Authority's petition, No. 26-199, and consolidate the enforcement question for briefing and argument. The operative issue will be whether HISA's enforcement provisions are facially unconstitutional under the private-nondelegation doctrine. I expect a neutral formulation of that question rather than one presupposing that the Authority only assists the FTC. Additional rulemaking and Appointments Clause questions are less likely to be included than this enforcement question.

The snapshot records **one distribution**, on November 4, 2026 for the December 4 conference, and no CVSG. From that state I forecast at least one further distribution with probability **0.65**, with **one additional distribution** the central count and a short tail for two or more. The most likely sequence is consideration in December, a short relist, and a grant in December 2026 or January 2027. A first-conference grant without a further distribution is the principal alternative.

The Court will **not call for the Solicitor General's views**; probability of a new invitation is **0.005**. The government's position is already before the Court through its own petition and reply.

Conditional on a grant, the Court will set the enforcement question for briefing and argument rather than decide the case summarily: **0.06** for a disposition riding the cert order and **0.94** for plenary review. A second GVR merely invoking *Consumers' Research* is unlikely after the Fifth Circuit's express reconsideration and reinstatement of its holding. A GVR after a decision in a different lead petition is the more plausible summary alternative.

The expected grant will carry **no separate writing**. Conditional on the less likely denial branch, I assign **0.35** to some noted dissent or statement respecting denial, but still expect an unexplained denial more often than a written one. On that branch, an objection could emphasize the unresolved statutory conflict and national importance; Thomas or Gorsuch are plausible writers on the delegation issue, while Kagan is a plausible writer emphasizing the consequences of leaving statutory invalidation unreviewed. Those are conditional possibilities, not predicted votes or an expectation of a merits opinion accompanying a denial.

If review is granted, the central merits dispute will concern whether the FTC's rulemaking authority and de novo review supply sufficient control over private enforcement, and how the facial-challenge standard bears on claimed autonomous investigative and coercive powers. This cert prediction does not commit to an eventual affirmance, reversal, vote lineup, or merits author.
