# Forecast — No. 26-201

- **Disposition:** Certiorari **granted**, most likely at or shortly after the December 4, 2026 conference, and consolidated with No. 26-199 (HISA's petition from the same Fifth Circuit judgment). The cross-petitions 26-332 and 26-335 are most likely denied or held. Oklahoma v. United States (25-1325) is held and later disposed of in light of the merits decision.
- **Relists:** The docket shows one distribution and no relist. I expect a grant at the first conference. If the Court does relist, it will be once, to coordinate the companion dockets or settle the QP wording.
- **CVSG:** None. The United States is the petitioner.
- **Question taken:** Whether HISA's enforcement provisions are facially unconstitutional under the private nondelegation doctrine. This may follow the challengers' neutral wording, which the SG accepts, rather than the petition's "assist the FTC" wording. The Court will not take the challengers' additional questions from the cross-petitions.
- **Summary disposition:** Not likely. There is no intervening decision to GVR on, and a statute's invalidation calls for plenary review.
- **If denied (unlikely):** That would most plausibly happen because the Court picked another vehicle (26-199 or Oklahoma) and held this one. A separate writing is unlikely in that case. A denial outright might draw a statement from Justice Gorsuch or Justice Thomas.
- **If granted, likely ground:** The Court would ask whether the FTC's rule-approval and de novo review power over the Authority's enforcement (as amended in 2022) keeps the Authority subordinate. My lean is reversal, upholding the Act facially, consistent with the Sixth Circuit and the *Consumers' Research* framework.
