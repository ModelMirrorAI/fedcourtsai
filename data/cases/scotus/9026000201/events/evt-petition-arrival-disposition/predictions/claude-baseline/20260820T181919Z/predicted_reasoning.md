# Forecast — FTC, et al. v. National Horsemen's Benevolent and Protective Association, et al. (No. 26-201)

Forecast at docketing (arrival moment). The docket shows a single entry: the
petition filed August 14, 2026, docketed August 17, with the response due
September 16, 2026. Zero distributions, no CVSG — the record's state at
prediction time, which is what every increment below is forecast from.

## Disposition

**The Court grants this petition, consolidated with the Authority's companion
petition (No. 26-199), and sets the case for plenary briefing and argument.**
Every institutional grant signal points the same way, and each is near the top
of its range:

- The Fifth Circuit held an Act of Congress — the Horseracing Integrity and
  Safety Act's enforcement provisions — facially unconstitutional. The Court's
  usual practice is to grant review of a lower-court invalidation of a federal
  statute even without a conflict.
- There *is* a conflict, and an acknowledged one: the Sixth Circuit in
  Oklahoma v. United States, 163 F.4th 294 (2025), rejected a facial challenge
  to the same provisions of the same statute, and the Fifth Circuit said it was
  "parting ways with" that decision. A federal statute now enforceable in one
  circuit and void in another is a state of affairs the Court does not leave
  standing.
- The petitioner is the Solicitor General, on behalf of the FTC.
- The Court is already invested in this litigation: it stayed the Fifth
  Circuit's mandate in 2024 (No. 24A287) and then GVR'd the prior round of
  petitions (Nos. 24-429, 24-433, 24-465, 24-472, 24-489) in light of FCC v.
  Consumers' Research on August 1, 2025. The Fifth Circuit reissued its
  decision anyway, so the tool short of plenary review has been tried and
  failed.

## Timing and relists

Expect a brief in opposition (or possibly a waiver-then-request sequence) by
mid-September 2026, distribution for one of the first conferences of October
Term 2026 — most likely the late-September long conference or an early-October
conference — and a grant within one to three conferences of first distribution,
i.e., by early November 2026. From the zero-distribution state the record
shows: **the petition will be distributed at least once with near-certainty,
and I expect one to two total distributions before the grant** (the modern
Court routinely relists once before granting). Argument would follow in early
2027 with a decision by late June 2027.

## CVSG

**No CVSG will issue.** The Solicitor General is the petitioner; the
government's views are already before the Court. The claim probability (0.01)
reflects only the formal possibility, not any real scenario.

## Question presented

The Court takes the SG's QP as written — whether HISA's enforcement provisions
facially violate the private nondelegation doctrine. It is a single, clean
question framed by the government; no narrowing is needed. The Court likely
grants this petition and No. 26-199 while holding (or denying) the Oklahoma
challengers' petition (No. 25-1325), which raises additional questions with no
conflict, exactly as the SG proposes.

## Summary disposition

**Unlikely (0.08 conditional on grant).** A second GVR has no vehicle: the
Fifth Circuit already reconsidered in light of Consumers' Research and reissued
its opinion, and no new intervening decision is on the horizon. A summary
reversal is possible but the Court almost never summarily resolves a question
on which it granted a stay, saw a circuit split develop, and has full adversary
presentation available; the SG asks for plenary review. The residual covers a
mootness vacatur if Congress were to amend the Act again mid-pendency.

## If denied

A denial would most plausibly mean the Court granted only the Authority's
companion petition and disposed of this one administratively — in which case
expect **no dissent from denial and no separate writing**. An outright denial
leaving the invalidation standing is a remote scenario; only there would a
noted dissent be likely.

## Conditional merits lean (not a scored claim)

If granted, the Court likely reverses: the stay of mandate, the Consumers'
Research GVR, and that decision's "broadly subordinate" framework all suggest a
majority skeptical of facial invalidation where the FTC holds front-end
rulemaking control and back-end de novo review over the Authority's
enforcement actions.
