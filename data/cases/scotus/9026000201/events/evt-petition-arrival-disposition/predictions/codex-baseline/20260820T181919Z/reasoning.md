# Prediction rationale

I assign a 0.90 probability to a grant and predict a plenary grant. The prior is unusually strong at this arrival moment. The frozen context places the petition in the `federal` band under `sal-v3`, with zero distributions and no CVSG as required by the arrival design. Pooling the committed statpack's federal-band rows for Terms 2017–2025 (all strictly before OT2026) gives 143 estimated grants among 201 resolved federal petitions, or 71.1%. For comparison, the weakest band's bracketed `reached` rate over the same Terms—the whole paid arrival population—is 858/13,163, or 6.5%. The federal class is therefore the relevant published arrival anchor.

I adjust materially above 71.1% because the petition combines several classic grant signals that are visible at docketing. The Solicitor General asks the Court to review a decision facially invalidating enforcement provisions of a federal statute. The petition identifies an acknowledged, outcome-determinative conflict between the Fifth and Sixth Circuits on the same private-nondelegation question. It also describes a parallel petition by the Horseracing Integrity and Safety Authority and explains that this case directly presents the reasoning of the only court of appeals to invalidate the Act. The Court previously vacated the Fifth Circuit's earlier judgment for reconsideration in light of *FCC v. Consumers' Research*, and the Fifth Circuit reinstated its constitutional holding after that remand. That history makes continued denial less likely than plenary resolution.

The main downward considerations are procedural. The related Authority petition, docket 26-199, may become the lead vehicle; the Court could grant only that petition while denying or otherwise disposing of this one. The Court also could conclude that the FTC's December 2025 enforcement rule or the facial posture makes further review premature. Those possibilities keep the estimate below near-certainty.

The record shows zero distributions, so the 0.99 relist-increment claim means at least one future conference distribution, not a repeat conference. A pre-distribution dismissal or withdrawal is possible but especially unlikely for a Solicitor General petition presenting a live circuit conflict. A future CVSG is essentially unnecessary because the United States and FTC are already the petitioners, hence 0.002. Conditional on a grant, I put 0.10 on a cert-order disposition: the Court might summarily reject the Fifth Circuit's facial analysis, but its prior GVR did not resolve the disagreement, and a direct inter-circuit conflict over the scope of private nondelegation is more likely to receive briefing and argument. Conditional on denial, I put 0.06 on a noted dissent or statement; denial of this petition would most plausibly accompany review in the parallel petition, reducing the reason for a separate writing.

The prediction uses the snapshot, the extracted questions presented, and the petition. `documents.json` marks the 137-page petition extraction as truncated, but the extracted text contains the complete petition through its conclusion and the Fifth Circuit opinion; truncation occurs in the appended district-court material. No brief in opposition was provisioned at this arrival moment, when the response was not yet due. I did not use live CourtListener retrieval or seek this case's disposition.
