## In brief

Frederick Allen asks the Court to review a Fourth Circuit ruling that reopened and then dismissed his decade-old copyright suit against North Carolina officials.

## What happened

Allen sued North Carolina officials for using his copyrighted footage of the Blackbeard shipwreck Queen Anne's Revenge. After the Supreme Court ruled in 2020 that a federal law did not strip North Carolina of sovereign immunity, the case returned to the district court, which reopened it and let Allen pursue a different legal theory for overcoming that immunity. In 2024 the district court let that claim proceed. North Carolina officials appealed, and the Fourth Circuit reviewed both the 2024 ruling and the earlier 2021 order reopening the case, ultimately ruling against Allen and ordering his claims dismissed.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked whether an appeals court, while handling an appeal it clearly has power to hear, may also reach back and decide a separate, earlier order that would not otherwise be appealable yet, when that earlier order is not closely tied to the issue properly before it. Allen also asks whether the Fourth Circuit improperly decided the case on a legal theory neither side had argued, which he says would justify reversing without full briefing.

## Where it stands

The petition has been distributed for conference twice, most recently for October 16, 2026, after North Carolina filed a brief opposing review and Allen filed a reply. The docket does not yet show any decision on whether the Court will hear the case.

## What each outcome would mean

Allen, as petitioner, asks the Court to take up the case. If the Court grants review, it would hear full arguments on the appellate-jurisdiction question and decide it, without that grant itself favoring either side. If the Court denies review, the Fourth Circuit's ruling against Allen stands, leaving his claims dismissed. A grant does not mean Allen would win; it only means the Court would decide the legal question presented.
