## In brief

The Supreme Court declined to hear Frederick Allen's bid to revive his copyright lawsuit against North Carolina over Blackbeard shipwreck footage.

## What happened

Allen sued North Carolina officials years ago over the state's use of his copyrighted photos and video of the Blackbeard shipwreck Queen Anne's Revenge. The Supreme Court previously ruled North Carolina had sovereign immunity (protection from certain lawsuits) from the original copyright claims. Back in the trial court, a judge allowed Allen to reopen the case and pursue a new legal theory. North Carolina appealed, and the Fourth Circuit Court of Appeals reviewed that reopening decision alongside the appeal, then ruled against Allen, ending his case. Allen asked the Supreme Court to review that ruling.

## What the Court is being asked

The case asks whether an appeals court, while reviewing one properly appealable order, may also reach back and decide a separate, otherwise unappealable order just because that earlier order led to the one being appealed, or whether appeals courts may only do so in narrower circumstances the Court described in an earlier case. Allen also argued the appeals court decided the case on a legal theory neither side had argued, and asked the Court to reverse on that basis without full briefing.

## Where it stands

The Supreme Court denied the petition on October 19, 2026, after considering it at conferences in September and October 2026.

## What each outcome would mean

Allen, as petitioner, asked the Court to take up the case. The denial means the Court will not hear it, leaving the Fourth Circuit's ruling in place. That ruling went against Allen: it reversed the order that had reopened his case and directed dismissal of his claims, and it did not decide the underlying sovereign-immunity question. The denial is not a ruling on who was right; it simply leaves the Fourth Circuit's decision, and the dismissal of Allen's case, standing.
