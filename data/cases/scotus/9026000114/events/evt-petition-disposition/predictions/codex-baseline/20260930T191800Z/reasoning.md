## Why the merits of the cert request are mixed

The petition asserts an 8-3-2 circuit division over whether pendent appellate jurisdiction extends beyond Swint's two described circumstances (QP 1; petition pp. 16-29). It separately seeks summary reversal for deciding on an allegedly waived jurisdictional theory (QP 2; petition pp. 36-39). That creates both a plausible plenary-review route and a nontrivial summary-correction route. I treat the split count and waiver characterization as advocacy, not independently established facts.

The opposition's most important response is that this panel purported to apply Swint, rather than announce an additional category of jurisdiction (BIO pp. 10-20). The appended opinion supports that narrower description: it states the two categories, then expressly relies on either of them, describing the reopening order as foundational to the immunity ruling (petition appendix pp. 21a-25a). Even if that application stretches Swint, the case may present disputed application rather than a clean disagreement about the permissible legal test. The alternative meaningful-review rationale also complicates a grant directed only at the intertwined-issues formulation.

The unusual prior Supreme Court litigation and Rule 60 reopening dispute compound the vehicle problem. Petitioners argue that a distinct takings theory independently supported reopening and undermine the panel's causal rationale (petition pp. 38-39). Respondents argue that the earlier order itself resolved the relevant immunity theory, and deny that another circuit has rejected jurisdiction on comparable facts (BIO pp. 17-20). I cannot confidently resolve this factual and procedural disagreement from the selected materials. That uncertainty keeps the forecast near, rather than substantially above, the band anchor.

On party presentation, respondents say they always sought review of the earlier order and that the panel merely chose a different jurisdictional rationale; they also dispute express waiver and identify an oral-argument passage acknowledging the intertwined route (BIO pp. 21-23). This is a substantial obstacle to treating the alleged departure as an obvious candidate for summary reversal. The recent party-presentation cases are considered as characterized in the supplied briefs, not as independently verified analogues.

A narrowly targeted CourtListener lookup of **Swint v. Chambers County Commission, 514 U.S. 35, 50-51 (1995)** confirmed that the Court expressly reserved the broader scope question while rejecting jurisdiction on the facts before it. This supports treating the petition's theory seriously without assuming that Swint squarely compels reversal here. No search of this petition or its subsequent history was undertaken. General web searches and an attempted official rules retrieval returned no usable content and supplied no evidence.
