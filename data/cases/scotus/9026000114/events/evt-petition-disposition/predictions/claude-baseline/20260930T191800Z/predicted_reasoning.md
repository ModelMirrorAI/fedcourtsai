# Forecast: what the Court will do with No. 26-114, Allen v. Stein

**Bottom line.** The petition will most likely be denied at or shortly after the
October 16, 2026 conference, without a noted dissent. P(any grant) = 0.13.

## Relists

The docket shows **two distribution entries**: a July 29 distribution for the
September 28 long conference (entered after respondents waived), which was
superseded when the Court **requested a response on August 17**, and a
September 30 redistribution for the October 16 conference now that the brief in
opposition (Sept. 16) and reply (Sept. 25) are in. So the petition has never
actually been considered at conference; October 16 is its first real look, and
the two entries are not two relists. Forecasting from that state: P(at least one
further distribution after October 16) = 0.28. Most of that mass is the grant
path (the Court almost always relists once before granting) plus a modest
chance of a relist while a Justice considers a separate writing or the Court
reschedules. If it is relisted, expect one or two further conferences, not a
long string.

## CVSG

No. No federal party, no federal statute whose administration is at issue, and
the question is one of judge-made appellate-jurisdiction doctrine. P = 0.04.

## Which question, if any

If the Court takes the case, it takes **QP 1** — whether pendent appellate
jurisdiction extends beyond Swint's two circumstances — and probably
reformulates it, because the Fourth Circuit's opinion expressly framed its
holding under both Swint prongs ("linked inextricably", "so interconnected as to
require concurrent review"). A plenary grant would therefore have to be about
what "inextricably intertwined" means, not about whether Swint is a ceiling.
QP 2 (summary reversal for a party-presentation violation) would not be granted
as a plenary question.

## Summary disposition versus plenary review

Conditional on a grant, P(disposed of in the cert order itself) = 0.30. There is
no intervening decision to support a GVR, so the only summary route is a per
curiam reversal on QP 2's party-presentation theory, which the petition
explicitly invites and which the Court has used against the Fourth Circuit
several times recently. I still rate plenary review the likelier grant form,
because the Fourth Circuit was resolving its own jurisdiction, a subject on
which courts routinely act without party prompting, and because respondents
did argue below that the 2021 and 2024 orders were one "ball of wax".

## Dissent from denial

Conditional on denial, P(a dissent from or statement respecting denial) = 0.08.
The petition's party-presentation theme could draw a short statement from a
Justice who has written on the Fourth Circuit's practice, but the case's
procedural history (a plaintiff reopening a suit after a unanimous loss in this
Court) makes a separate writing in the petitioners' favor unlikely.

## If granted (conditional)

If the Court hears the case, the likely ground is that a non-appealable order
granting leave to amend is not "inextricably intertwined" with a later denial of
sovereign immunity merely because it was a but-for antecedent of it, with the
judgment vacated and the case remanded for the Fourth Circuit to decide the
immunity question it left unaddressed. Expect a fairly lopsided vote for that
reading if the case is argued, since the Court's own appellate-jurisdiction
cases (Swint, Microsoft v. Baker, Hall v. Hall) have been unanimous or nearly so.
