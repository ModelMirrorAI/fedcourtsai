# Forecast — No. 26-648, Association of Contracting Plumbers v. City of New York

**State forecast from:** zero distributions, no response, no CVSG, no amicus briefs on the docket.

- **Response and timing.** Respondents (New York City; the New York Secretary of State; the intervenors) will not waive. Expect at least one extension of the December 17, 2026 response date, a brief in opposition in January–February 2027, multiple amicus briefs supporting petitioners by the original response date, and a first conference distribution in February–March 2027.
- **Relists.** The petition will be distributed for conference (near certain). At that first conference the Court will not simply deny on the first look: expect at least one relist or a hold-like delay, in part because the D.C. Circuit (argued September 2026) and Fourth Circuit (argument December 2026) decisions on the same question are likely to arrive around that time. One to two further distributions past the first is my central expectation.
- **CVSG.** Unlikely (about one in ten). The United States already filed as amicus below supporting preemption and is litigating the question affirmatively, so the Court does not need an invitation to learn its view.
- **Disposition.** Denial is slightly more likely than a grant (about 60/40). The denial path is the percolation path: a 1–1 conflict, with several circuits about to weigh in and respondents arguing the Ninth Circuit's decision is narrower than the petition says. The grant path is the acknowledged-split path: the Second Circuit said in terms that it was creating a circuit split on the meaning of 42 U.S.C. § 6297(c), the federal government disagrees with the decision below, and the question governs a growing set of state and local laws.
- **Question presented, if granted.** The Court would take the single question as written — whether a law prohibiting EPCA-covered gas appliances from consuming any energy is a preempted "regulation concerning the ... energy use" of those products — and set the case for argument, most likely in October Term 2027. If granted, the likely ground of decision is textual: the scope of "energy use" (a fixed measured characteristic of the product as manufactured versus actual consumption at the point of use) and the reach of "concerning"; I would expect a divided Court, with reversal more likely than affirmance.
- **Summary disposition.** Very unlikely. No intervening decision supports a GVR, and the question is too contested for summary reversal.
- **Separate writings.** If the Court denies, most likely no noted dissent; a modest chance (about 15%) that one or more of Justices Thomas, Gorsuch or Kavanaugh notes a dissent or writes on the denial given the acknowledged conflict.
