## In brief

A pamphleteer who lost a First Amendment damages claim over qualified immunity asks the Court to clarify whether a legal rule from a prison case applies outside that context.

## What happened

Richard Hershey sued Bossier City and police and security officers after he was told to stop handing out religious leaflets near a concert venue and leave, while another leafleteer was not. The district court dismissed his claims, and a divided Fifth Circuit panel affirmed, holding qualified immunity shielded the officers because no prior case involved materially identical facts. Hershey asks the Supreme Court to decide whether a rule allowing lawsuits to proceed over obvious constitutional violations, even without closely matching precedent, applies to free-speech and religious-exercise claims and not just to the prison-conditions context where the rule first arose. A related petition by the City concerning a separate municipal-liability issue is pending.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked whether a legal principle allowing a civil rights claim to proceed against government officials for an obviously unconstitutional act, even without a prior court decision involving nearly identical facts, applies to free speech and religious exercise claims, or whether it is confined to the prison-conditions context in which it first developed.

## Where it stands

The petition has been fully briefed, including numerous friend-of-the-court filings and a reply, and was distributed for the Court's conference in late September 2026 and again for a conference in early October 2026. The docket does not yet show any ruling on the petition.

## What each outcome would mean

Hershey, who lost below, is asking the Court to take up his case. If the Court grants review, it would hear full arguments and decide the legal question itself, which would not by itself determine who wins. If the Court denies review, the Fifth Circuit's decision stands, leaving qualified immunity in place for the officers and ending Hershey's individual damages claims against them, while his separate claim against the City proceeds separately in the lower courts regardless of this petition's outcome.
