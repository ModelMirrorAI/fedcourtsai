## In brief

Richard Hershey asks the Court to revive civil-rights claims against police who stopped him from handing out religious leaflets outside a concert venue.

## What happened

Hershey, a pamphleteer, was distributing religious leaflets on sidewalks outside a Bossier City arena during a concert when police and security guards threatened him with arrest and made him leave, while allowing another leafleteer distributing commercial material to stay. He sued the officers and the city, claiming the officers violated his First Amendment rights and that the city failed to train them. Lower courts dismissed his claims against the officers on qualified immunity grounds, reasoning that no prior case clearly established that this specific conduct was unconstitutional. A divided Fifth Circuit panel affirmed that dismissal but revived a separate claim against the city; the full court of appeals declined to rehear the case.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked whether a legal rule allowing officials to be held liable for constitutional violations that are "obvious," even without a prior court case involving nearly identical facts, applies to free speech and religious liberty claims, or whether it is limited to claims brought by prisoners under the Eighth Amendment, as Hershey says the Fifth Circuit has held.

## Where it stands

The petition has been fully briefed, including an opposition brief and many supporting briefs from outside groups, and was distributed for the Court's conferences on September 28 and October 9, 2026. The docket does not yet show any decision on the petition.

## What each outcome would mean

Hershey, who lost on this issue in the lower courts, is asking the Court to take up his case. If the Court grants review, it would hear full arguments and decide whether this legal rule extends beyond prisoner claims; a grant alone would not decide who wins. If the Court denies review, the Fifth Circuit's decision stands, leaving Hershey's claims against the individual officers dismissed, though a separate claim against the city would continue in the lower courts regardless.
