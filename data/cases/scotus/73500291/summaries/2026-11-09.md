## In brief

Richard Hershey asks the Court to revive civil-rights claims against police who stopped him from leafleting outside a concert venue, citing qualified immunity.

## What happened

Hershey, a paid pamphleteer, was distributing religious leaflets on sidewalks outside a Bossier City arena during a concert when police and security officers told him to leave and threatened arrest, while not removing a nearby leafleteer for a radio station. He sued the officers and the city, claiming a First Amendment violation. A Fifth Circuit panel held the officers had qualified immunity, a legal protection that shields officials from damages suits unless they violated clearly established law, because no prior case with similar facts existed. A separate panel majority let a related claim against the city proceed. Rehearing en banc was denied over dissent.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked whether a legal rule letting courts find a violation "obvious" enough to deny immunity even without a closely matching prior case, a rule the Court previously applied to prison conditions, also applies to free speech and religious liberty claims, or whether it is confined to the prison context.

## Where it stands

The petition has been fully briefed, including an opposition brief and a reply, along with numerous amicus briefs supporting Hershey. It has been distributed for five consecutive conferences, most recently November 13, 2026, without any recorded action granting or denying it.

## What each outcome would mean

Hershey, who lost on this issue in the lower court, is asking the Court to take up and reverse that ruling. If the Court grants review, it would hear full arguments on whether the obviousness rule extends beyond prison cases to First Amendment claims, without yet deciding who wins. If it denies review, the lower court's decision applying qualified immunity to the officers stands, leaving Hershey unable to pursue damages from them on this record. The city's related appeal on a separate municipal-liability question is noted as pending in a different docketed case.
