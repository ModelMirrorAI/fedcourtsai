## In brief

Richard Hershey asks the Court to revive his civil rights claims against Bossier City officers, arguing qualified immunity should not have shielded them for an allegedly obvious free-speech violation.

## What happened

Hershey, a paid leafleteer, was ordered to leave the grounds of a Bossier City arena while distributing religious pamphlets during a concert, even though another leafleteer handing out radio-station materials was not removed. He sued the officers, security guards, and the City, claiming First Amendment violations. Lower courts dismissed his claims against the officers on qualified immunity grounds, and a divided Fifth Circuit panel and then the full court agreed, though several judges disagreed on the reasoning. A related petition by the City concerning the city's liability is pending separately.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked whether a rule allowing lawsuits over constitutional violations so obvious that no prior similar court case is needed, a rule the Court has applied mainly in prison conditions cases, also applies to free speech and religious liberty claims, or whether it is limited to the context where it first arose.

## Where it stands

The petition has been distributed for three conferences, most recently October 16, 2026, and the docket shows no action on it since. Briefing is complete, including an opposition brief and numerous amicus filings.

## What each outcome would mean

Hershey, as petitioner, is asking the Court to hear his case. If the Court grants review, it would take up the case and decide whether the obviousness rule extends to First Amendment claims, without yet deciding who wins. If the Court denies review, the Fifth Circuit's decision stands, leaving Hershey's claims against the officers dismissed on qualified immunity grounds, while his separate failure-to-train claim against the City continues in the lower courts regardless.
