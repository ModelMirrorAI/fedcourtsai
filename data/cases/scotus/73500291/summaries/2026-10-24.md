## In brief

Richard Hershey asks the Court to revive civil-rights claims against police who stopped him leafleting outside a concert venue, after a lower court granted the officers immunity.

## What happened

Hershey, a paid pamphleteer, was ordered to stop handing out religious leaflets on grounds outside a Louisiana arena during a ticketed concert, while another leafleteer distributing radio-station materials was left alone. He sued the city, officers, and security guards, claiming the officers violated his First Amendment rights and that the city failed to train them. Lower courts dismissed his claims against the individuals on qualified immunity grounds, though a split appeals court allowed a separate claim against the city to proceed. The full appeals court declined further review.

## What the Court is being asked

The case asks whether a legal rule allowing officials to be held liable for constitutional violations so obvious that no prior court case needs to have addressed the exact situation—a rule the lower appeals court has applied to claims about prisoner treatment—also applies to claims about free speech and religious exercise, or whether that court has effectively limited the rule to prisoner cases alone.

## Where it stands

The petition has been distributed for three conferences, most recently October 16, 2026, and the docket shows no decision yet on whether the Court will hear the case.

## What each outcome would mean

Hershey, who lost below, is asking the Court to take up his case. If the Court grants review, it would hear full arguments and decide the legal question itself, without yet deciding who wins. If it denies review, the lower court's decision granting the officers qualified immunity stands, ending Hershey's damage claims against them, while his separate claim against the city over training continues independently. A grant does not indicate which side would ultimately prevail.
