# Forecast: petition disposition

The Court will most likely deny Richard Hershey's petition, leaving the officers' qualified-immunity disposition undisturbed. The probability of any grant, including a GVR or summary reversal, is **26%**. This forecast concerns Hershey's petition, No. 25-1389, not the City's separate municipal-liability petition, No. 25-1323.

The October 5 snapshot records **two distributions**: September 2 for the September 28 conference, and October 5 for the October 9 conference. Thus one additional distribution has already occurred; the forecast starts from two, not one. No CVSG appears.

- **Further distribution:** 42% probability of at least one more distribution beyond those two. The modal path is no further distribution and denial following the scheduled October 9 conference. If another distribution occurs, one or two additional conferences are more likely than a long series. The central timing forecast is resolution during October or November 2026; coordination with the City's separate petition is a possible reason for a longer wait, not an observed hold.
- **CVSG:** 2% probability of a new invitation. The Court is likely to resolve this private civil-rights petition without seeking the Solicitor General's views. The asserted legal error and vehicle questions do not appear to require an executive-branch assessment.
- **Question if granted:** The likely focus is whether the obvious-violation route to overcoming qualified immunity applies to First Amendment speech claims without a factually matching precedent. The Court would more likely narrow to that issue than independently decide the petition's free-exercise formulation, given the opposition's preservation objection. It would not use a grant of this petition alone to decide the separate Monell questions.
- **Route conditional on grant:** 35% probability of disposition in the cert order itself; 65% probability of plenary briefing and argument. A summary correction and remand is plausible if the Court reads the judgment as resting on a categorical exclusion of First Amendment claims from Hope. But disputed preservation, forum status, and the officers' knowledge make a summary merits resolution less likely than plenary review conditional on intervention. No specific intervening decision requiring a GVR has been identified.
- **Writing conditional on denial:** 22% probability that at least one Justice notes a dissent or issues a statement respecting denial. The principal forecast is an unelaborated denial without a separate writing; no individual vote or author is predicted.

The likely reason for declining review is that the petition presents an uncertain vehicle for resolving the categorical question it states. A denial would not endorse the officers' conduct or resolve the underlying First Amendment dispute. If review is granted, the likely legal direction is to reaffirm that an obvious violation can supply fair warning without identical precedent, while leaving the application of that principle to the pleaded facts for further analysis.
