# Predicted reasoning — Hershey v. City of Bossier City, No. 25-1389 (cert stage)

**State forecast from:** the docket shows **two distributions** — distributed on
Sept. 2 for the Sept. 28 long conference, then re-distributed on Oct. 5 for the
Oct. 9 conference with no intervening "Rescheduled" entry, so the petition has
been considered once and relisted once. No CVSG. Fifteen amicus briefs were
filed in support at the cert stage. A cross-petition by the City on the Monell
question (No. 25-1323) is pending and is cited as a related case.

**Disposition.** Most likely **denied** (P(any grant) ≈ 0.24). The petition's
question — whether Hope v. Pelzer's obviousness principle is confined to the
Eighth Amendment — is one the BIO persuasively shows no party and only one
judge below actually disputes; the petitioner never cited Hope in the district
court or on appeal, and the Fifth Circuit refused his out-of-time rehearing
motion. The Court denied review of the underlying Fifth Circuit decision
(Villarreal v. Alaniz) last Term over a dissent. I expect the relist to end in a
denial, likely accompanied by a separate writing, rather than in a grant.

**Further relists.** Roughly even odds of at least one more distribution
(≈ 0.45). The live reasons for a second relist are a Justice writing on the
denial, and the Court wanting to dispose of this petition and the City's
cross-petition together once both are ready. If it goes past two further
relists, a summary disposition becomes the likelier reading.

**CVSG.** Very unlikely (≈ 0.04): no federal party, no federal statute, and
the Court does not ordinarily call for the SG's views on a qualified-immunity
petition that has already been relisted.

**Which question, if taken.** The Court would not take the QP as written; it
would either reformulate to whether the officers' conduct obviously violated
clearly established First Amendment law on these facts, or more plausibly take
the City's Monell question in No. 25-1323 and deny or hold this one.

**Summary disposition versus plenary review.** Conditional on any grant,
about a 0.3 chance it comes in the cert order itself — a short per curiam of
the Taylor v. Riojas kind reversing the qualified-immunity holding — against
plenary review in about 0.7 of grant worlds, most likely paired with the City's
petition. No intervening decision supports a GVR.

**If denied.** About a 0.4 chance of a dissent from, or statement respecting,
denial. The likeliest authors are Justice Sotomayor (who dissented from the
Villarreal denial on the same Fifth Circuit obviousness question) or Justice
Thomas (a standing critic of qualified-immunity doctrine); a short statement
from Justice Gorsuch, whose Browder line the petition quotes, is also possible.

**If granted (conditional).** The Court would most likely hold, narrowly, that
Hope's obviousness route applies to every constitutional claim and remand for
the Fifth Circuit to decide in the first instance whether the violation alleged
here was obvious — not resolve the forum-status question itself.
