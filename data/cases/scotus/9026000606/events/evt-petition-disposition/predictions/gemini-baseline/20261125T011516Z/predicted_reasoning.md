# Predicted Court Action: *Richard Alan Kucera v. United States* (No. 26-606)

## Disposition and Procedural Trajectory

The Court will **deny** the petition for a writ of certiorari.

- **Further distributions / relists (`relist-increment`):** The docket currently shows **1 distribution** — the November 24, 2026 distribution for the Conference of December 11, 2026, following the Solicitor General's November 17, 2026 waiver of the United States' right to respond. The most likely course is an immediate denial on the order list following the December 11, 2026 Conference with **zero further distributions**. The primary nontrivial pathway to an additional distribution (~14% chance) is a chamber-initiated Call for a Response (CFR) from the Solicitor General — prompted by the acknowledged nine-to-one published circuit split and Judge Kelly's separate concurrence below — which would result in a second conference distribution after the Brief in Opposition is filed (and potentially one post-BIO relist) before certiorari is still denied.
- **Call for the Views of the Solicitor General (`cvsg-increment`):** **No CVSG will issue.** The United States is already the named respondent represented by the Solicitor General, so any request for the government's position will take the form of a Call for a Response (CFR) rather than a CVSG invitation.
- **Question presented if granted (context-only):** If the Court were to grant certiorari, it would take the petition's sole Question Presented as framed — whether the First Amendment requires permitting a defendant prosecuted under 18 U.S.C. § 2251(a) for producing child pornography to raise a reasonable, good-faith mistake-of-age affirmative defense — without reopening statutory scienter arguments under *Rehaif* or Kucera's unchallenged child sex trafficking convictions under 18 U.S.C. § 1591(a).
- **Plenary review vs. summary disposition (`summary-disposition-route`):** Conditional on a grant, **plenary review** with merits briefing and oral argument is far likelier than a summary disposition in the cert order (~88% plenary vs. ~12% summary/GVR). No pending Supreme Court merits case addresses 18 U.S.C. § 2251(a) or First Amendment mistake-of-age doctrine to support a hold-and-GVR, the Solicitor General waived rather than confessing error, and the Court would not summarily reverse nine courts of appeals on a constitutional question.
- **Separate writings on the order (`dissent-from-denial` / `order-writers`):** Expect **no separate writing** accompanying the denial order. In the small tail scenario (~1.4%) where a Justice writes a dissent from or statement respecting the denial of certiorari — most plausibly Justice Gorsuch, Justice Sotomayor, or Justice Jackson, and almost certainly only after calling for a response — the writing would highlight the decades-old circuit conflict and Judge Kelly's concurrence while acknowledging the vehicle complications posed by Kucera's undisturbed § 1591 convictions.

## Conditional Merits Outlook

If the Court were nevertheless to grant plenary review, it would likely **affirm** the Eighth Circuit and adopt the nine-circuit majority rule (abrogating *United States v. U.S. District Court (Kantor)*, 858 F.2d 534 (9th Cir. 1988)), holding that the First Amendment does not compel a mistake-of-age defense for in-person child pornography producers under 18 U.S.C. § 2251(a) in light of *New York v. Ferber*, 458 U.S. 747 (1982), *United States v. X-Citement Video, Inc.*, 513 U.S. 64, 72 n.2, 76 n.5 (1994) (distinguishing producers who confront the minor directly from downstream distributors), and the longstanding common-law and statutory tradition of strict age liability in offenses protecting minors from sexual exploitation.
