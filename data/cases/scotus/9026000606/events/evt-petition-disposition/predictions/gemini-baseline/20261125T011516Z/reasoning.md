### Upward Factors (Why `P(grant)` Is Well Above a Routine Criminal Waiver's ~0.3%–0.5%)

1. **Genuine, acknowledged 9-to-1 published circuit split on a federal constitutional question:** The Ninth Circuit holds in *United States v. U.S. District Court (Kantor)*, 858 F.2d 534 (9th Cir. 1988) — codified in Ninth Circuit Model Criminal Jury Instruction 20.24 and reaffirmed in *United States v. Jayavarman*, 871 F.3d 1050 (9th Cir. 2017) — that the First Amendment requires allowing a § 2251(a) defendant to prove by clear and convincing evidence a reasonable mistake of age. Nine other circuits (CA1, CA2, CA4, CA5, CA6, CA7, CA8, CA10, CA11 — including the First Circuit most recently in *United States v. Deschambault*, May 18, 2026) reject that defense. Kucera preserved the objection via a motion in limine and requested jury instruction, and the Eighth Circuit squarely rejected it in a published opinion.
2. **Judge Kelly's separate concurrence below:** Judge Kelly wrote separately to state that, were the panel not bound by *United States v. Wilson*, 565 F.3d 1059 (8th Cir. 2009), and *United States v. Thurber*, 106 F.4th 814 (8th Cir. 2024), she would invite briefing on the constitutional defense because Kucera's case might represent one of the "rare instances" where a defendant acted reasonably based on affirmative age representations. A published circuit concurrence highlighting factual salience makes a chamber-initiated Call for a Response materially likelier than on an ordinary government waiver.

### Downward Factors (Why Denial Remains Overwhelmingly Likely)

1. **Solicitor General waiver at distribution:** The Solicitor General waived a response eight days after docketing, and zero amici appeared before distribution. First, this effectively rules out government acquiescence or a confession-of-error GVR (~1.8% of the `cert_amicus = 0` bucket in `statpack.md` is GVR, almost none of which applies here). Second, the Court virtually never grants certiorari over an SG waiver without first calling for a response and relisting, and even post-CFR petitions deny roughly 85%–90% of the time.
2. **A 38-year dormant outlier split repeatedly passed over by this Court:** *Kantor* (1988) predates *United States v. X-Citement Video, Inc.*, 513 U.S. 64, 72 n.2, 76 n.5 (1994), where this Court specifically contrasted § 2251(a) producers — who confront the minor face-to-face — with downstream § 2252 distributors. Every circuit to address the First Amendment question in the 32 years since *X-Citement Video* has rejected *Kantor*, and this Court has repeatedly denied certiorari on the identical split without recorded dissent, including *Malloy v. United States*, 559 U.S. 991 (2010) (No. 09-523), *Fletcher v. United States*, 565 U.S. 980 (2011) (No. 11-5253), and *Ruggiero v. United States*, 136 S. Ct. 429 (2015) (No. 15-401). Because *Kantor*'s clear-and-convincing threshold rarely impedes federal prosecutions inside the Ninth Circuit and the nine-circuit majority aligns with *X-Citement Video*'s dicta, the Court has shown zero appetite to disturb the status quo.
3. **Severe vehicle defect — unchallenged 18 U.S.C. § 1591 child sex trafficking convictions and 300-month sentence:** Kucera was convicted at the same trial of two counts of child sex trafficking of the same two minors under 18 U.S.C. § 1591(a)(1)–(2), on instructions requiring that he either had a reasonable opportunity to observe the minors or knew/recklessly disregarded their ages *and* knew/recklessly disregarded that force, threats of force, fraud, or coercion would be used, receiving a 300-month (25-year) total prison sentence. Those undisturbed trafficking convictions carry the same 15-year-to-life statutory range under § 1591(b)(1), cast doubt on whether vacating the § 2251(a) counts would alter his 300-month sentence, and severely undermine any First Amendment narrative of "chilled protected adult expression."
