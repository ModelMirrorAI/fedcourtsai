The Supreme Court is very unlikely to hear this appeal — under a two percent chance, or roughly one in fifty-five — and will almost certainly deny review, leaving the lower court's ruling in place. Richard Kucera asks the Court to decide whether the First Amendment gives people charged with producing child pornography a defense when they reasonably believed the teenager involved was at least eighteen, an issue on which one federal appeals court disagrees with nine others. Although one appellate judge wrote separately that Kucera's facts might fit that defense, the federal government waived its right to file a response, the Court has repeatedly declined to review this exact disagreement for decades, and Kucera remains convicted on unchallenged child sex trafficking charges from the same events carrying up to twenty-five years in prison.
