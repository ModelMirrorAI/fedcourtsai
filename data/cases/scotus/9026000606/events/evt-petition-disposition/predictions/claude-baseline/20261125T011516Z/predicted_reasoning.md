# Forecast: Kucera v. United States, No. 26-606

The docket shows one distribution (for the Conference of December 11, 2026), no relist, no CVSG, and a waiver of response by the United States.

- **Disposition.** The Court will deny certiorari, most likely on the order list following the December 11 conference (December 14, 2026), without calling for a response.
- **Further distribution.** Probably none. The one realistic path to another distribution is a Justice requesting a response from the Solicitor General, which would pull the petition off the December 11 conference and lead to redistribution in early 2027; I put that at about one in ten. A true relist after conference consideration is unlikely.
- **CVSG.** None. The United States is the respondent, so the Court would call for a response, not invite the Solicitor General's views.
- **Question presented.** If the Court took the case it would take the single QP as written: whether the First Amendment requires a reasonable mistake-of-age defense to a § 2251(a) production charge. It might add the antecedent statutory-scienter question, which the petition raised at trial but did not present.
- **Summary disposition.** Unlikely. No intervening decision bears on the question, so a GVR has no hook, and the majority position below is too well supported to be summarily reversed. A grant, if any, would be plenary.
- **Separate writing on denial.** None expected. If anyone wrote, it would most plausibly be Justice Sotomayor, Justice Gorsuch or Justice Jackson noting the 15-year mandatory minimum and the acknowledged conflict with the Ninth Circuit.
