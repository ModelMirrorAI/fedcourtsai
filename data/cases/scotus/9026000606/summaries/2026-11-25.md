## In brief

Richard Kucera asks the Court to decide whether the Constitution requires courts to let producers of child pornography argue they reasonably believed their victims were adults.

## What happened

Richard Kucera was convicted in federal district court in South Dakota of producing child pornography and sex trafficking after paying two minors, who he says represented themselves as adults, to let him photograph and engage in sexual activity with them. At trial, he asked the court to instruct the jury that the government had to prove he knew or should have known his victims were minors, or to let him argue he reasonably believed they were adults. The district court refused, following Eighth Circuit precedent, and Kucera was convicted and sentenced to the statute's 15-year mandatory minimum. The Eighth Circuit affirmed, though one judge wrote separately to say she would have considered recognizing such a defense if not bound by that precedent.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked whether the First Amendment, which protects non-obscene sexual depictions of adults, requires that someone charged with producing child pornography be allowed to defend themselves by showing they reasonably and honestly believed, in good faith, that the person depicted was an adult, rather than being convicted regardless of what they believed about the person's age.

## Where it stands

## What each outcome would mean

Kucera, who lost in the lower courts, is asking the Court to take up his case. If the Court grants the petition, it will hear full arguments and decide whether this defense is constitutionally required, resolving a disagreement among the federal appeals courts; granting does not itself decide who wins. If the Court denies the petition, the Eighth Circuit's ruling against Kucera stands, and his conviction and sentence remain in place, without the Court deciding the underlying constitutional question.
