# Forecast of the Court's action

The Court will most likely **deny certiorari without explanation** after receiving the opposition it requested. Probability of any grant is **26%**. The leading procedural reason to decline is that Nevada allowed litigation to proceed on allegedly secular corporate questions at the pleading stage, while leaving religious qualifications uncontested; the Court can wait for a more conclusive record rather than settle the full boundary of religious autonomy now.

## Next steps

The record currently shows **one distribution and no CVSG**. I expect **one further distribution** after the response due November 23, 2026 and any reply, followed by disposition in December 2026 or January 2027. Timing is a forecast, not a scored claim. Another extension could move that window.

Probability of at least one additional distribution is **97%**. The existing response request preceded the originally scheduled conference, so the expected renewed distribution is not itself evidence of a substantive relist. One or two actual relists after renewed consideration are the principal alternative to a prompt denial; a prolonged hold is less likely because no concrete lead case is identified in the supplied materials.

The Court will probably not invite the Solicitor General's views: **2% probability of a new CVSG**. No such invitation is already present, and the case does not present an obvious special federal-government interest requiring consultation.

## If review is granted

The Court would most likely take a narrowed form of the sole question: whether civil courts may decide who governs a religious organization by applying secular organizational rules when religious eligibility and doctrine are not themselves disputed. It need not declare whether neutral principles can ever apply to any nonproperty claim.

Conditional on any grant, **12%** is assigned to a summary disposition in the cert order and **88%** to a route opening plenary review. No identified intervening decision makes a GVR the natural disposition. If the Court reaches the merits, the likely concern is whether enforcing these specific corporate procedures impermissibly determines religious leadership; any resulting protection for internal governance would not necessarily immunize religious organizations from ordinary external civil obligations.

## Separate writings

Conditional on denial, probability of a noted dissent or statement respecting denial is **16%**. The modal denial contains none. Thomas is my leading candidate to write, followed by Gorsuch and Alito, but each remains unlikely. These are writing forecasts, not predictions of an announced cert vote lineup; a Justice's joining another writing is distinct from authoring one.
