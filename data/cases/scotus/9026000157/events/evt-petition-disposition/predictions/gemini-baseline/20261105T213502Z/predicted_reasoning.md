# Predicted Court Reasoning and Procedural Trajectory: *Jasvinder Singh, et al. v. Second Judicial District Court of Nevada, Washoe County, et al.* (No. 26-157)

Before the October 9 Conference met, the Court called for a response on September 22, 2026, and subsequently extended the response deadline to November 23, 2026.

Once Respondents file their Brief in Opposition on or around November 23, 2026 and Petitioners file their cert reply, the Clerk will distribute the petition again for a December 2026 or January 2027 conference. At that post-BIO conference, the most likely trajectory is either a denial on the first post-BIO conference list (representing 2 total distributions) or one additional conference relist (3 total distributions) while chambers examine the 28 U.S.C. § 1257(a) finality objection and the factual overlap between the property-trust claims and the governance claims before denying certiorari.

## 2. Call for the Views of the Solicitor General (`cvsg-increment`)

The Court will **not** call for the views of the Solicitor General. No federal statute, treaty, agency regulation, or federal enforcement program is implicated in this state-court dispute over the corporate bylaws of a Nevada religious nonprofit corporation and First Amendment church-autonomy principles.

## 3. Question Presented and Conditional Merits Scope

The petition presents a single question: whether the First Amendment permits a civil court to invoke "neutral principles of law" beyond the property-ownership context to adjudicate a religious institution's internal governance dispute under organizational documents that define membership and authority in religious terms.

If the Court grants certiorari, it will take the single Question Presented as framed (or with a threshold jurisdictional question added or argued in the merits briefs regarding finality under 28 U.S.C. § 1257(a) and *Cox Broadcasting Corp. v. Cohn*, 420 U.S. 469 (1975)). Conditional on a grant and merits resolution without a jurisdictional dismissal, a majority of the Court would likely hold that *Jones v. Wolf*, 443 U.S. 595 (1979), is confined to disputes over title and ownership of church property and does not license civil courts to superintend internal church elections, membership rolls, or governance structures simply because the relevant bylaw provisions use facially secular procedural terms.

## 4. Summary Disposition vs. Plenary Review (`summary-disposition-route`)

Conditional on a grant, plenary review with full briefing and oral argument is far likelier than a summary disposition (`summary-disposition-route: 0.06`). There is no pending merits case on the OT2026 docket addressing the *Jones v. Wolf* / church-autonomy split for which this petition would be held and GVR'd, and the acknowledged split among federal circuits (Second, Fifth, Sixth, Ninth, Eleventh, and D.C. Circuits) and state high courts makes summary reversal on the cert papers unlikely, especially given the threshold state-court finality and pleading-stage questions.

## 5. Separate Writings on Denial (`dissent-from-denial` and `order-writers`)

The Court will most likely deny the petition without a recorded dissent or statement (`88%` conditional on denial), just as it denied certiorari in *United States Conference of Catholic Bishops v. O'Connell*, No. 25-849, on May 26, 2026. However, there is a meaningful minority chance (`12%` conditional on denial) that one or more Justices—most plausibly Justice Thomas, Justice Alito, or Justice Gorsuch—will author a statement respecting or dissenting from the denial of certiorari noting the deepening conflict between the Fifth Circuit (*Lutheran Church—Missouri Synod v. Christian*, 178 F.4th 193 (5th Cir. 2026); *McRaney*, 157 F.4th 627 (5th Cir. 2025)) and the Ninth Circuit / Nevada Supreme Court (*Puri v. Khalsa*; *Singh*), while observing that the interlocutory pleading-stage posture of this state writ proceeding counsels awaiting a case that has reached final judgment.
