## 2. Case-Specific Signals Moving Away from the Anchor

### Upward Signals (Supporting Certiorari)

1. **Call for a Response (CFR) Following a Waiver:** Respondents initially waived a response on August 21, 2026, and at least one Justice requested a response on September 22, 2026. A CFR is a necessary predicate to a grant after a waiver and screens out the roughly 75–80% of `baseline` petitions that are denied outright without a response.
2. **Cert-Stage Amicus Support (3 Briefs) and Experienced Supreme Court Counsel:** Three amicus briefs supporting petitioners were filed prior to first distribution: (1) the Anglican Church in North America (Jones Day); (2) Former EEOC General Counsel Sharon Fast Gustafson and Rachel N. Morrison (Hunton Andrews Kurth LLP); and (3) the Roman Catholic Archbishop of Las Vegas (Vinson & Elkins LLP). In `metrics/statpack.md`, paid scored-segment petitions with `2+` cert-stage amicus briefs resolve at `18.2% granted, 4.5% gvr` (22.7% grant-family, 76.1% denied, 1.2% dismissed; `n=1,446`, with `11,227` unknown), though that marginal cut pools across petitions that also relisted or received CVSGs. Both sides are represented by experienced Supreme Court litigators (Sidley Austin / Northwestern Clinic for Petitioners; McDermott Will & Schulte for Respondents).
3. **Acknowledged Lower-Court Split on Extending *Jones v. Wolf*'s "Neutral Principles" Doctrine Beyond Property Ownership:**
   - Below, the Nevada Supreme Court explicitly acknowledged that courts "are split as to whether the neutral-principles exception may apply beyond the context of church property disputes" (App. 10a) and joined the Ninth Circuit (*Puri v. Khalsa*, 844 F.3d 1152, 1165 (9th Cir. 2017); *Huntsman v. Corp. of the President of the Church of Jesus Christ of Latter-day Saints*, 127 F.4th 784 (9th Cir. 2025) (en banc)) in holding that "the neutral-principles exception applies beyond the context of church property ownership disputes, so long as the court applying neutral principles can do so 'without resolving underlying controversies over religious doctrine'" (App. 13a).
   - That rule conflicts with decisions of the Fifth, Sixth, and Eleventh Circuits—most recently the Fifth Circuit's published opinion in *Lutheran Church—Missouri Synod v. Christian*, 178 F.4th 193, 201–02 (5th Cir. June 4, 2026) (verified via CourtListener MCP, `opinion_id=11337673`, holding that "the property exception to the church autonomy doctrine is narrow" and does not permit a civil court to adjudicate church governance through a "secular reading of the Church's constitution, bylaws, and policies"), and *McRaney v. North American Mission Board of the Southern Baptist Convention, Inc.*, 157 F.4th 627, 648–51 (5th Cir. 2025), as well as state high courts such as Oklahoma (*Oklahoma Annual Conference of the United Methodist Church, Inc. v. Timmons*, 2023 OK 101, ¶¶ 10–12). Multiple circuit judges have also recently called for Supreme Court review of the "neutral principles" expansion in dissents from denial of rehearing en banc (*Belya v. Kapral*, 59 F.4th 570, 573, 580–82 (2d Cir. 2023); *O'Connell v. U.S. Conference of Catholic Bishops*, 2025 WL 3082728, at *7 (D.C. Cir. Nov. 4, 2025)).

### Downward Signals and Vehicle Obstacles (Supporting Denial)

1. **Interlocutory Pre-Discovery Posture and 28 U.S.C. § 1257(a) Finality Concerns:**
   - The underlying lawsuit is at the motion-to-dismiss stage in Nevada state court (`appendix.txt` at App. 17a–23a, 39a–56a). After the state trial court denied Petitioners' motion to dismiss, Petitioners sought an extraordinary writ of prohibition or mandamus from the Nevada Supreme Court. The Nevada Supreme Court denied the writ and permitted the case to proceed, repeatedly qualifying its holding as preliminary:
     - *"nothing in the relevant sections of NNSS's bylaws or the claims alleged would—**as it appears at this point in the proceedings**—require the district court to evaluate Sikh doctrine, practices, or texts"* (App. 15a).
     - *"At this juncture, the claims presented in RPIs' underlying complaint appear to be secular in nature"* (App. 15a).
   - Although Petitioners invoke *Atlantic Richfield Co. v. Christian*, 590 U.S. 1, 8–12 (2020), and *Cox Broadcasting Corp. v. Cohn*, 420 U.S. 469 (1975), to argue that the denial of an original writ of prohibition is a final judgment under 28 U.S.C. § 1257(a), the Nevada Supreme Court's express *"at this juncture"* and *"as it appears at this point in the proceedings"* caveats show that Petitioners' First Amendment defense is not foreclosed if discovery or summary judgment demonstrates that adjudicating membership records or management authority implicates Sikh religious qualifications.
   - Furthermore, the Supreme Court has repeatedly denied certiorari in interlocutory church-autonomy petitions where religious organizations sought immediate review of denials of motions to dismiss—including *Belya v. Kapral*, 143 S. Ct. 2609 (2023), and, just five months before this snapshot, *United States Conference of Catholic Bishops v. O'Connell*, No. 25-849 (`scotus/73280351`, verified via `fedcourts query --docket-number 25-849`, cert. denied May 26, 2026 after 4 distributions and 15+ amicus briefs, including several of the same amici appearing here).
2. **Blurred Fact Pattern Involving Both Real Property Disposition and Nonprofit Statutory Record-Inspection Claims:**
   - Petitioners frame the question presented as whether the neutral-principles approach extends *beyond* the property-ownership context. Yet the First Claim for Relief and primary catalyst of the underlying complaint (`appendix.txt` at App. 46a–50a) is an alleged attempt by the Management Defendants and an unapproved Subcommittee to transfer the Northern Nevada Sikh Society's real property—the Reno Gurdwara/Temple at 1380 Selmi Drive—into a trust, allegedly in violation of Article One of the Bylaws (*"This corporation, its Management Committee and its assets are vested in general body (Sikh Sangat as a whole) of the State of Nevada and remain to be that forever"*). And the Third Claim for Relief (App. 51a–52a) is a statutory and bylaw demand by members to inspect corporate books, minutes, and bank records (`NRS 82.216(1)`).
   - Once Respondents' Supreme Court counsel (Paul W. Hughes) files the Brief in Opposition on November 23, 2026, the BIO will strongly argue that: (a) the central dispute *is* a dispute over control and disposition of church real property and secular corporate accounting records, making this a poor vehicle to test whether neutral principles apply outside the property context; (b) no party has contested anyone's religious qualifications to be a Sikh or a member under Article Three of the Bylaws (App. 14a); and (c) multi-claim pleading-stage writ review cannot cleanly separate the property-trust and books-and-records claims from the election/membership claims.
3. **No Companion Case Currently Pending on the Merits Docket:**
   - `record/same-question.json` is empty (`petitions: []`, `indexed: 13523`), and corpus/CourtListener checks confirm that no granted OT2026 case raises the *Jones v.
