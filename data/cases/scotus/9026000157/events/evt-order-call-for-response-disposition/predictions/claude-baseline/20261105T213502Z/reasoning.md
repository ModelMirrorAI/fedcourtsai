## What moves me up
- **The Court called for a response** (Sep 22, 2026) after respondents waived (Aug 21). No committed
  table conditions on this. My judgement is that a paid petition with a called-for response grants at
  roughly two to three times the baseline floor, though most such petitions are still denied.
- **Three amicus briefs** before the first conference: the Anglican Church in North America, the Roman
  Catholic Archbishop of Las Vegas, and former EEOC General Counsel. The statpack's `2+` amicus bucket
  (n=1446 resolved) is in the grant family at about 22.7%, against 4% for `0`. That cut describes the
  population at disposition and overlaps with the call for a response, so I do not stack the two.
- **An acknowledged split.** The Nevada Supreme Court itself said courts "are split" (App. 10a). The
  petition lists the 2d, 8th, 9th and D.C. Circuits plus four state high courts against the 5th, 6th and
  11th Circuits plus three state high courts. Church autonomy is an area where several Justices have shown
  interest.
- **Strong counsel**: Sidley Austin, with the Northwestern Supreme Court clinic.

## What holds me down
- **Finality under 28 U.S.C. § 1257.** The decision below is a writ ruling that lets a lawsuit go ahead
  past a motion to dismiss. The petition argues it is final, citing *Atlantic Richfield v. Christian* and
  *Cox Broadcasting*. That argument is plausible, but respondents will surely contest it, and it is a
  classic vehicle problem.
- **O'Connell (No. 25-849)** raised a closely related church-autonomy question. The corpus shows it was
  distributed four times and denied on 2026-05-26. The Court looked hard at that area and passed. The
  petition says O'Connell had appellate-jurisdiction problems that this case lacks, which is fair, but the
  denial shows the Court is wary of interlocutory church-autonomy vehicles.
- **A better vehicle may be coming.** *Christian v. Lutheran Church–Missouri Synod* (No. 26-466) was filed
  in October 2026 and is pending, with no distribution yet. It comes from a federal court of appeals and
  touches church autonomy and neutral principles, though its main question is diversity jurisdiction. The
  Court might prefer to wait, or hold Singh.
- The facts are a local temple-governance dispute, and the bylaw provisions at issue were found
  "secular". Respondents will argue the question is not squarely presented.

Net: from 5% I move up for the call, the amici and the split, to about 18%. I move back down for the
finality and vehicle concerns and the recent O'Connell denial. That gives 0.14.
