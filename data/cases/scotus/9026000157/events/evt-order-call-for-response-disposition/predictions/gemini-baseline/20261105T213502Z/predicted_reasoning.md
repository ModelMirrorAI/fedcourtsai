# Predicted Court Reasoning and Procedural Trajectory: *Jasvinder Singh, et al. v. Second Judicial District Court of Nevada, Washoe County, et al.* (No. 26-157)

- **Further Distributions and Relists (`relist-increment`: 0.98):** The docket as of the September 23, 2026 snapshot records a single distribution (`distribution_count = 1`, distributed on September 16, 2026 for the October 9, 2026 Long Conference), followed by the Court's September 22, 2026 Call for a Response (due October 22, 2026). Because the petition was pulled from the October 9 conference list upon the Call for a Response, it will almost certainly be distributed at least once more (`P = 0.98`) after Respondents file their Brief in Opposition (and Petitioners file their cert reply). Once redistributed for that post-BIO conference, we expect the Court to deny certiorari without extended relisting (either at its first post-BIO conference or after at most one post-BIO relist, yielding 1 to 2 further distributions beyond the single distribution currently on the docket).
- **Solicitor General Invitation (`cvsg-increment`: 0.01):** The Court will not call for the views of the Solicitor General. The dispute arises out of state-court nonprofit corporate litigation between private factions of a local congregation under Nevada corporate law (NRS 82.216(1)) and implicates no federal statute, federal agency, or federal regulatory interest.
- **Question Presented and Conditional Merits Path (Context-Only):** The petition presents a single Question Presented: whether the First Amendment's church-autonomy doctrine allows a civil court to invoke "neutral principles of law" beyond the property-ownership context to adjudicate a religious institution's internal governance dispute under organizational documents that define membership and authority in religious terms. If the Court were to grant review, it would take the single Question Presented as written and would likely reverse or vacate the Nevada Supreme Court's decision, holding under *Serbian Eastern Orthodox Diocese v. Milivojevich*, 426 U.S. 696 (1976), and *Our Lady of Guadalupe School v. Morrissey-Berru*, 591 U.S. 732 (2020), that civil courts may not adjudicate leadership-election and membership-record disputes within a religious organization whose bylaws condition membership and officeholding on religious qualifications, even when framed as secular corporate bylaw claims.
- **Summary Disposition Route (`summary-disposition-route`: 0.05):** Conditional on a grant of certiorari, plenary briefing and oral argument would be far more likely than a summary disposition (`P = 0.05`). No related church-autonomy case is currently pending on the Court's merits docket that would support a hold and subsequent GVR, and the lower courts' open disagreement over the reach of *Jones v. Wolf*'s neutral-principles framework would counsel in favor of full merits consideration rather than summary reversal.
- **Separate Writings on the Disposition Order (`dissent-from-denial`: 0.08):** Conditional on a denial of certiorari, the most likely outcome is an unsigned denial order with no noted dissent or statement. Because the case arises in an interlocutory posture from the denial of a motion to dismiss—where the Nevada Supreme Court expressly qualified its ruling as limited to "this juncture"—even Justices sympathetic to a robust church-autonomy rule will likely view the vehicle as premature, just as the Court denied certiorari without noted dissent in *United States Conference of Catholic Bishops v. O'Connell* (No. 25-849) in May 2026. There remains a modest (`8%`) chance that Justice Thomas, Justice Alito, or Justice Gorsuch authors a dissent from or statement respecting the denial of certiorari flagging the circuit split over extending neutral principles beyond church property disputes.
