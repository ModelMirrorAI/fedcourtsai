## In brief

Sikh Society leaders ask the Court to stop a Nevada lawsuit over the group's governance and property, arguing courts may not referee internal religious disputes.

## What happened

Northern Nevada Sikh Society members who once served on its Management Committee sued the Society's current leaders, claiming they violated the Society's bylaws by forming committees without approval, trying to move the Society's temple into a trust, and withholding membership records. The leaders asked the Nevada courts to dismiss the suit, arguing the "ecclesiastical abstention doctrine" bars civil courts from deciding internal church governance matters. The trial court refused to dismiss, and the Nevada Supreme Court agreed, holding that courts may use "neutral principles of law" to decide such disputes even outside property-ownership cases, so long as they do not have to interpret religious doctrine.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked whether civil courts may resolve internal governance disputes within a religious organization—such as who leads it, who counts as a member, and how it manages its property—using ordinary legal rules, or whether such disputes must be left entirely to the religious organization itself whenever its membership and leadership rules are defined in religious terms.

## Where it stands

The petition has been distributed for conference and the Court asked the respondents to respond; that response deadline has since been extended to November 23, 2026. The docket does not show any further action on the petition.

## What each outcome would mean

The Sikh Society leaders, as petitioners, are asking the Court to reverse the Nevada Supreme Court's decision and shut down the lawsuit. If the Court grants review and eventually sides with the leaders, the case against them would likely end, leaving Society governance outside court control. If it rules for the former committee members instead, their lawsuit over bylaws and the temple trust could proceed in Nevada courts. If the Court denies review, the Nevada Supreme Court's decision allowing the suit to continue stands, and the case returns to the trial court.
