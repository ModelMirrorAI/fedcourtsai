# Forecast: what the Court will do with No. 25-1105, Thompson v. Wilson

**State forecast from.** The docket shows two distributions: one for the
conference of June 11, 2026, which the Court's June 3 call for a response
superseded before the petition was ever considered, and one for the long
conference of September 28, 2026, now that the brief in opposition (July 6),
the reply (July 20) and a supplemental brief (September 9, distributed) are in.
No CVSG. Four accepted cert-stage amicus briefs remain on the docket (American
Farm Bureau Federation, Manhattan Institute, Landmark Legal Foundation, and Cato
Institute with NFIB); a fifth (Liberty Justice Center) was withdrawn. So the
September 28 conference is the petition's **first** real consideration, and
every claim below is an increment from that state.

**Disposition.** Denial, at roughly 86%. The call for a response after Maine
waived shows at least one chambers wanted the State's answer, and Pacific Legal
Foundation has framed a Fourth Amendment question with genuine appeal to the
Justices who read the Amendment through property and trespass rather than
privacy expectations. But the brief in opposition lands two blows the reply does
not fully parry: the "common-law trespass test for reasonableness" framing was
not argued in those terms below (the First Circuit never cited *Jones*), and the
claimed split rests on a pre-*Burger* Ninth Circuit day-care case (*Rush*), a
Sixth Circuit tire-chalking case that expressly declined to reach the point
(*Taylor II*), and Ninth Circuit step-one "was there a search" holdings. Add the
petitioner's own concession below that lobstering is a closely regulated
industry, which removes the *Patel*-style scope question that would make the
case attractive, and the Court most likely lets this one go while the issue
percolates (the Ninth Circuit's August 2026 *Richards v. Newsom* decision, which
the supplemental brief raises, is a 2-1 decision on the same *Burger* limits and
is itself a candidate for en banc or cert).

**Relists.** About a 30% chance of at least one further distribution beyond the
two shown. If the Court is going to grant it will relist once first, as it now
almost always does; a relist without a grant would signal a Justice preparing a
statement or dissent respecting denial. The likelier single outcome is a plain
denial on the October 5 order list following the long conference.

**CVSG.** Unlikely, about 6%. The federal interest is real (the Atlantic States
Marine Fisheries Commission mandate is backed by 16 U.S.C. § 5106, and NMFS runs
its own vessel-monitoring programs), and the Solicitor General would have views
on whether *Jones* trespass principles constrain every federal administrative
inspection regime. But the Court has already taken the cheaper step of calling
for the State's response, the respondent is a State defending its own rule, and
CVSGs on private-petitioner cert petitions against States are rare.

**Which question, if granted.** The Court would not take either question as
written. It would reformulate to a single question: whether the *Burger*
administrative-search framework permits continuous, suspicionless electronic
location monitoring of a closely regulated business's vessel during
non-commercial use, or whether the Fourth Amendment's property-based protections
impose time-and-place limits on such a scheme. QP 2's abstract "trespass versus
expectations of privacy" methodology question would be folded into that rather
than granted on its own.

**Summary disposition.** Conditional on a grant, about 5%. No intervening
decision bears on the administrative-search question. *Chatrie v. United States*
(No. 25-112), decided June 29, 2026, concerned a geofence warrant in a criminal
prosecution and does not resolve whether *Burger* admits trespass limits, so a
GVR in its light is improbable; a per curiam reversal of a published, carefully
reasoned First Circuit opinion on a novel framing is likewise improbable. Any
grant would be plenary.

**Dissent or statement respecting denial.** Conditional on a denial, about 15%.
Justice Gorsuch is the natural author: the petition's originalist, property-based
reading of the Fourth Amendment tracks his *Carpenter* dissent and the *Case v.
Montana* concurrence the petition quotes, and a chambers interested enough to
call for a response may write when the vote falls short. Justice Thomas is the
next likeliest. Most likely, though, the denial is silent.

**If granted (conditional only).** The Court would most likely vacate and
remand, holding that a *Burger* scheme must limit inspections to the times,
places and activities of the regulated business, without adopting a freestanding
"trespass test" for reasonableness. Nothing about that is forecast
unconditionally; a denial produces no opinion.
