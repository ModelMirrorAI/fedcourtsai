# Forecast

The Court will deny certiorari in Frank Thompson v. Carl Wilson. The most likely disposition is an unexplained denial following the September 28, 2026 conference, around the opening of the October Term. This is a forecast of the petition disposition, not a judgment endorsing Maine's surveillance regime.

The snapshot records **two distributions**: May 26 for the June 11 conference and July 22 for the September 28 conference. A response request intervened on June 3. From this two-distribution baseline, I expect **no further distribution** before disposition; the probability of at least one additional distribution is **0.36**. If the petition receives further consideration, one or two additional distributions are more likely than a prolonged sequence. The existing count does not establish two completed substantive conference reviews.

No CVSG is recorded. I forecast **no call for the Solicitor General's views**, with **0.04** probability of a new invitation. The interstate and federal fisheries interests create some possibility of an invitation, but the petition principally asks the Court to apply Fourth Amendment limits to state regulation.

**Conditional on a grant**, the Court will more likely order plenary briefing and argument than dispose of the case in the cert order: **P(summary route | grant) = 0.10**. The likely question would narrow the first question presented to whether a closely regulated fishery may require continuous GPS tracking of vessels during noncommercial use. The second question, concerning property-based protections within administrative-search reasonableness, would supply the doctrinal framework rather than an invitation to invalidate administrative searches generally. If review is granted, the likely focus is the temporal and operational limits of the regulatory exception, not whether lobster fishing belongs to the closely regulated category.

I expect **no noted dissent or statement respecting denial**; **P(a noted dissent or statement | denial) = 0.17**. No particular Justice's vote or writing is predicted. A denial will produce no majority opinion on the questions presented.
