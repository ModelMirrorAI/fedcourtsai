## In brief

A Maine lobsterman asks the Court to review whether requiring GPS trackers on fishing boats violates the Fourth Amendment's protection against unreasonable searches.

## What happened

Maine requires federally permitted lobstermen to install electronic tracking devices on their boats, reporting location data to help manage the lobster fishery. Frank Thompson, a lobsterman, sued the state's marine resources commissioner, arguing the requirement amounts to an unconstitutional search, including when he uses his boat for personal, non-lobstering purposes. The district court dismissed his claim, applying a Supreme Court test for searches of "closely regulated industries," and the First Circuit Court of Appeals affirmed. Thompson asked the Supreme Court to take up his case, while Maine urged the Court to leave the lower court's ruling in place.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked whether requiring lobstermen to carry location-tracking devices on their boats at all times, including outside business hours and uses, is reasonable under the rule that governs searches of businesses in heavily regulated industries. Thompson argues courts must weigh whether the government has physically intruded on private property, not just whether a business owner had reduced privacy expectations. Maine responds that Thompson raised this trespass-based argument for the first time at the Supreme Court and that the lower court correctly applied existing precedent.

## Where it stands

The petition has been fully briefed, including a reply from Thompson and supplemental briefs from both sides, and was distributed for the Court's conference on September 28, 2026. The docket does not yet show any action taken at that conference.

## What each outcome would mean

Thompson, the lobsterman, asked the Court to hear the case. If the Court grants review, it will hear full arguments and decide whether the tracking requirement violates the Fourth Amendment, without yet deciding who wins. If the Court denies review, the First Circuit's ruling upholding Maine's tracking rule stays in place, and Thompson's challenge ends. A decision to send the case back without ruling would leave the question unresolved and require further proceedings below.
