# Forecast

The Court will deny the petition for certiorari, most likely by an unsigned order following the September 28, 2026 conference, in late September or early October 2026. The forecast is denial without a merits opinion, not an endorsement of the underlying judicial conduct.

The baseline records exactly one distribution: the June 24, 2026 entry setting the petition for the September 28 conference. It records no CVSG. I expect zero further distributions and no request for the Solicitor General's views. The probability of at least one additional distribution is 3.5%; if another occurs, one additional distribution is the modal extension rather than a prolonged sequence. The probability of a new CVSG is 0.1%.

If the Court grants review, it will most likely consolidate the three overlapping questions into whether a judge's continued adjudication after service of the litigants' federal civil-rights complaint defeats absolute judicial immunity through the due-process disqualification principles invoked in the petition. It will not take on the entire foreclosure dispute or the petition's collateral judicial-discipline grievances. This describes the question the Court would take, not a forecast of an unconditional merits holding.

Conditional on any grant, plenary briefing and argument is more likely than disposition in the cert order: the summary-route probability is 15%. The materials identify no intervening controlling decision warranting a GVR, and the requested change in immunity doctrine would require substantive reconsideration rather than straightforward application of an already-established exception.

Conditional on denial, I assign 0.8% to any noted dissent or statement respecting denial. The modal outcome is no separate writing; I do not predict an individual Justice's vote or authorship.

The forecast rests on the vehicle's failure to present a demonstrated conflict, the opposition's specific preservation objections, and the distinction between remedies for judicial bias and personal damages liability. Those are reasons to expect the Court to decline this vehicle, not reasons it will publicly give in a routine denial.
