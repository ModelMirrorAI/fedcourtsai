# Forecast: what the Court will do with No. 25-1271, Wain v. Bunnell

**Disposition.** The Court will deny the petition on the order list following the
September 28, 2026 long conference, without comment.

**Relists.** The docket shows one distribution (June 24, 2026, for the September 28
conference) and no relist. I expect no further distribution: this is a pro se,
fact-bound petition with no split, and the long-conference order list clears such
petitions in one pass. The residual chance of a second distribution is a
mechanical reschedule, not a hold or a serious look.

**CVSG.** None. No federal party, statute, or program is implicated; the
Solicitor General has no stake in a state judge's immunity from a private
foreclosure litigant's section 1983 suit.

**Question presented, if taken.** The Court would not take any of the three
questions as written. Each is drafted around this case's own facts (service at
chambers, a ruling three days later, a six-year counterclaim). A grant on the
underlying doctrinal question, whether Caperton and Williams v. Pennsylvania
limit absolute judicial immunity, would need a vehicle with individual-capacity
damages claims and a published circuit decision squarely reaching it. This
petition has neither, so there is no reformulation the Court would find worth
its time here.

**Summary disposition.** Not the likely route, because no route is likely. No
intervening decision of this Court bears on judicial immunity or on the
Caperton line in a way that would prompt a GVR, and a summary reversal of an
unpublished immunity affirmance in a pro se official-capacity case has no
precedent I can point to. Conditional on the near-impossible event of a grant,
I put the cert-order route and plenary review at even odds, tracking the
population share of cert-order grants.

**Dissent or statement respecting denial.** None expected. The petition
raises a judicial-ethics grievance about campaign-finance ties between a
state judge and a bank litigant, a subject that has occasionally drawn a
Justice's separate writing, but the official-capacity posture and the
independent Eleventh Amendment ground make this a poor occasion for one.

**If granted (conditional only).** The Court would most likely affirm the Sixth
Circuit on the narrow ground that official-capacity claims against state judges
are claims against the Commonwealth, without reaching the immunity question.
