## In brief

The Supreme Court declined to hear a Kentucky couple's bid to sue two state judges over rulings in their foreclosure case.

## What happened

Douglas and Elisa Wain lost a long-running foreclosure case in Kentucky state court. After unsuccessful state appeals and recusal requests, they sued the trial judge, Kimberly Bunnell, and an appeals judge, Jacqueline Caldwell, in federal court under a civil-rights law, alleging bias and due-process violations, including that Judge Bunnell ruled against them just days after being served with the lawsuit naming her as a defendant. The federal district court dismissed the suit, finding the judges protected by judicial immunity, a legal rule shielding judges from being sued over their official rulings. The Sixth Circuit Court of Appeals agreed and upheld the dismissal.

## What the Court is being asked

The Wains ask the Court to decide whether judicial immunity still protects a judge's ruling when that ruling comes soon after the judge was personally named as a defendant in a civil-rights lawsuit by the same people the ruling affects. They argue that due-process rules requiring judges to step aside when they have a personal stake in a case should limit immunity in that situation. The judges respond that the Wains never raised this specific argument in the lower courts and that established immunity rules fully cover the judges' actions.

## Where it stands

The petition was distributed for the Court's conference on September 28, 2026, and the Court denied it on October 5, 2026.

## What each outcome would mean

The Wains, as petitioners, asked the Court to overturn the Sixth Circuit's ruling. With the petition denied, the Sixth Circuit's decision stands: the lawsuit against Judges Bunnell and Caldwell remains dismissed, and the judges keep the benefit of judicial immunity from the Wains' claims. Had the Court instead granted the petition, it would have agreed to hear the case and decide the immunity question itself, without yet determining which side would ultimately prevail.
