## In brief

The Supreme Court declined to hear a couple's bid to revive their civil-rights lawsuit against two Kentucky judges over rulings in their foreclosure case.

## What happened

Douglas and Elisa Wain lost a long-running foreclosure case in Kentucky state court. They sued the presiding judge, Kimberly Bunnell, and an appeals-court judge, Jacqueline Caldwell, claiming Bunnell ruled against them three days after being served with this very lawsuit, without disclosing a financial relationship with the bank that sued them. A federal district court dismissed the suit, finding the judges absolutely immune from such claims, and the Sixth Circuit Court of Appeals affirmed. The Wains then asked the Supreme Court to take up the case.

## What the Court is being asked

The Wains asked the Court to decide whether judges automatically keep their immunity from being sued for rulings they make even after they have been personally named as defendants in a civil-rights lawsuit brought by the same people appearing before them, when fairness rules might otherwise require a judge to step aside. The judges answered that the Wains never raised this specific argument in the lower courts and that existing immunity rules already cover the conduct at issue.

## Where it stands

## What each outcome would mean

The Wains, as petitioners, asked the Court to take up their case. By denying the petition, the Court leaves the Sixth Circuit's ruling in place, meaning Judges Bunnell and Caldwell remain shielded by judicial immunity and the Wains' lawsuit stays dismissed.
