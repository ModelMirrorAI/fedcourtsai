## Substantive assessment

The petition's QP 1 and printed pages 15–25 argue that the government exploited inaccurate testimony about military deployment after excluding a recording, and that ordinary plain-error review improperly displaced Napue materiality. I checked **Glossip v. Oklahoma, 604 U.S. 226, 253 n.10 (2025)** through CourtListener: the majority says a defense choice not to pursue the witness's lithium history would not eliminate the prosecution's correction duty. That gives the petition a genuine doctrinal hook. It does not by itself establish that every unpreserved federal Napue claim is exempt from plain-error review; that distinction is my inference, not a contrary holding attributed to Glossip.

I also checked **United States v. Stein, 846 F.3d 1135, 1147 (11th Cir. 2017)**. Its discussion supports the distinction between merely leaving testimony uncorrected and affirmatively capitalizing on it despite defense access to contrary evidence. But the passage addresses the existence of a due-process violation and does not alone establish the petition's claimed categorical conflict over the appellate review standard. Other split authorities remain petition-supplied rather than independently verified. The mix of direct appeals and collateral proceedings, the disputed preservation characterization, and the panel's assumed rather than decided correction duty limit my confidence that this is a clean split vehicle.

For QP 2, printed pages 25–34 argue that appellate reliance on credentials cannot cure missing Rule 702 reliability findings. The petition itself quotes the panel as also finding that experience supported reliable conclusions and that the witnesses would meet all Rule 702 requirements (page 30, footnote 7). That creates a potential mismatch between the question's credentials-only premise and the appellate rationale. Whether the panel's record-specific harmlessness conclusion is inadequate is different from whether circuits disagree on the gatekeeping requirement itself. The asserted intra-circuit inconsistency is also not, by itself, an intercircuit split. Without the appendix and opposition, I cannot resolve those vehicle questions in petitioner's favor.
