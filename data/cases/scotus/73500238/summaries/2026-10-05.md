## In brief

The Court declined to hear Ramesh "Sunny" Balwani's bid to overturn his fraud convictions in the Theranos case.

## What happened

Balwani was convicted on fraud and conspiracy charges connected to the collapse of Theranos, the blood-testing company he ran with Elizabeth Holmes, and is serving a prison sentence. On appeal, he argued that prosecutors relied on testimony later acknowledged as false about Theranos's work with the military, and that two government witnesses gave specialized scientific opinions without the trial court ever screening that testimony for reliability. The Ninth Circuit affirmed his convictions, finding the testimony problems did not require a new trial, and denied rehearing. Balwani then asked the Supreme Court to take up the case.

## What the Court is being asked

Balwani asked the Court to decide two things: first, whether a more forgiving error standard should apply when prosecutors not only let false testimony stand uncorrected but also relied on it in closing arguments the defense had no chance to rebut; and second, whether an appeals court may excuse a trial judge's total failure to screen expert-type testimony for reliability simply by finding, after the fact, that the witnesses were qualified by background and experience.

## Where it stands

The petition was distributed for the Court's conference on September 28, 2026, and on October 5, 2026, the Court denied it; Justice Thomas did not take part.

## What each outcome would mean

Balwani, who lost in the lower court, asked the Court to take up his case. The denial means the Court will not review the Ninth Circuit's decision, so that ruling and his convictions stand. Had the Court instead granted review, the case would have been scheduled for full briefing and argument, and the Court would have decided the legal questions about false testimony and expert-evidence screening without yet determining who wins.
