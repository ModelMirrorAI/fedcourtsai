## In brief

Ramesh "Sunny" Balwani asks the Court to review his fraud convictions, arguing prosecutors used false testimony and unqualified expert opinions against him at trial.

## What happened

Balwani was convicted on fraud and conspiracy charges connected to the collapse of Theranos, the blood-testing company he ran with Elizabeth Holmes, and is serving a prison sentence. On appeal, he argued that a witness gave false testimony about the company's work with the military, which prosecutors then relied on repeatedly in closing arguments, and that two other witnesses gave specialized scientific opinions without the trial court ever checking whether that testimony was reliable. The Ninth Circuit agreed the testimony was false and agreed the opinion testimony should have gone through that reliability check, but still upheld the convictions, and it denied further review. Balwani then asked the Supreme Court to take the case.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked to decide two things. First, when prosecutors know testimony is false and repeatedly rely on it in closing arguments rather than correcting it, must an appeals court apply the relaxed review standard normally used when a defendant did not object clearly at trial, or does a stricter standard apply. Second, can an appeals court excuse a trial judge's total failure to screen expert-like testimony for reliability simply by finding, after the fact, that the witnesses were qualified based on their credentials.

## Where it stands

The petition was filed in May 2026, the government waived its right to respond, and the case was distributed for the Court's conference on September 28, 2026. The docket does not yet show any action taken at that conference.

## What each outcome would mean

Balwani, who lost in the Ninth Circuit, is asking the Court to take up his case. If the Court grants review, it will hear full arguments and decide the legal questions about false testimony and expert evidence, without yet deciding who wins. If it denies review, the Ninth Circuit's decision upholding Balwani's convictions stands, though a denial does not mean the Supreme Court agrees with that ruling.
