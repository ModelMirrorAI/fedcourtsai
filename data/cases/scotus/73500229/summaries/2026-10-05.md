## In brief

Former Shriners Hospitals employees asked the Court to revive their suit over Washington's COVID-19 vaccination mandate; the Court declined to hear it.

## What happened

Jane Elizabeth Roberts and eleven other former employees of Shriners Hospitals for Children sued Washington's governor and Shriners after losing their jobs for refusing COVID-19 vaccination under a state mandate. They argued federal law required that acceptance of the vaccines, which had not received full FDA approval, remain voluntary. A federal district court dismissed their claims, and the Ninth Circuit affirmed, relying on its own earlier ruling in a related case. The employees then asked the Supreme Court to review that decision.

## What the Court is being asked

The employees asked the Court to decide two related questions. First, whether the Constitution's guarantee of due process bars a state from requiring someone to accept a drug that lacked full federal approval, backed by the threat of losing their job, when federal drug law says acceptance of such drugs must be voluntary. Second, whether a state can impose that kind of mandate when a separate federal law shields vaccine makers and administrators from lawsuits, effectively cutting off injured people's ability to sue once the drug is given.

## Where it stands

The petition was distributed for conference twice, after the Court asked for a response from the respondents and received briefing from both sides. On October 5, 2026, the Court denied the petition.

## What each outcome would mean

The employees, as petitioners, asked the Court to take up their case. By denying the petition, the Court leaves the Ninth Circuit's decision in place, meaning the dismissal of the employees' lawsuit against Washington's governor and Shriners Hospitals stands. The denial does not mean the Supreme Court agreed the lower court was correct; it simply ends this case without the Court deciding the constitutional and statutory questions the employees raised.
