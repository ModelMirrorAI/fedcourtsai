## What happened

Jane Elizabeth Roberts and other former employees of Shriners Hospitals for Children in Spokane, Washington, refused to receive COVID-19 vaccines during the pandemic and were fired. They sued Washington's then-governor and Shriners, arguing that federal law required any use of these vaccines (which had not received full FDA approval) to be voluntary, so the state and hospital could not condition their jobs on accepting the shots. The district court dismissed their claims, and the Ninth Circuit affirmed, relying on its earlier decision in a related case, Curtis v. Inslee, which held such mandates permissible under a public-health rationale.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked whether it violates constitutional protections for a state to require someone to accept a drug that federal law otherwise treats as requiring voluntary consent, when refusing means losing a job or benefit. The Court is also asked whether it violates those same protections that accepting the drug, under a federal law granting immunity to those who administer it, cuts off the person's ability to later sue in court if the drug injures them.

## Where it stands

All respondents either waived their right to respond or, after being asked by the Court to respond, filed briefs opposing review; the petitioners have since filed a reply. The case has been distributed for the Court's conference scheduled for September 28, 2026, where the Justices will decide whether to grant certiorari, the process by which the Court agrees to hear a case.
