## What happened

Jane Elizabeth Roberts and other former employees of Shriners Hospitals for Children sued Washington's then-governor and Shriners after they were fired for refusing COVID-19 vaccines during the pandemic. The vaccines were authorized for emergency use but not fully approved. Petitioners argued that federal law required any acceptance of such vaccines to be voluntary, so a job-loss mandate was unlawful. A federal district court dismissed their claims, finding the vaccines "effectively FDA approved." The United States Court of Appeals for the Ninth Circuit affirmed, relying on its earlier ruling in a related case. Petitioners now ask the Supreme Court to review that decision.

## What the Court is being asked

The case raises two related questions. First, whether it violates constitutional protections for a state to require someone to accept an unapproved drug, backed by the threat of losing a job or license, when federal drug law says acceptance of such a drug must be voluntary. Second, whether it is constitutional for a state to impose such a mandate when a federal law grants those who administer the drug broad legal immunity, effectively cutting off the injured person's ability to sue over harm the drug causes.

## Where it stands

The petition has been distributed for conference twice, most recently for September 28, 2026, after the respondents filed briefs opposing review and the petitioners filed a reply. The docket shows no decision yet on whether the Court will hear the case.
