## In brief

Former Shriners Hospitals employees ask the Court to revive their lawsuit challenging Washington's COVID-19 vaccine mandate, which cost them their jobs.

## What happened

Jane Elizabeth Roberts and eleven other former Shriners Hospitals for Children employees sued Washington's then-governor and Shriners after they were fired for refusing COVID-19 vaccination during the state's 2021 mandate. They argued federal drug law required voluntary consent to any unapproved vaccine and that a federal immunity law for such vaccines strips people of their right to sue over injuries, so the mandate violated their constitutional rights. A federal district court dismissed the claims, and the Ninth Circuit affirmed, relying on its recent decision in a related case, Curtis v. Inslee. The employees now ask the Supreme Court to review that ruling.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked whether the Constitution's guarantee of fair treatment by state governments bars a state from requiring people to accept an unapproved drug, backed by the threat of losing their job or benefits, given that federal law says such drugs may only be given with voluntary consent. It is also asked whether that guarantee is violated when accepting the drug triggers a separate federal law that removes the person's ability to sue over any resulting injury.

## Where it stands

The petition has been distributed for the Court's conference on September 28, 2026, after the Court asked for and received responses from both sets of respondents and a reply from the employees. No decision on whether to hear the case has yet been recorded.

## What each outcome would mean

The employees are asking the Court to take up their case. If the Court grants the petition, it will hear full arguments and decide the legal questions itself, deciding nothing yet about who is right. If it denies the petition, the Ninth Circuit's ruling against the employees stands, leaving the dismissal of their lawsuit in place; that denial would not mean the Court agreed with the lower court's reasoning, only that the case ends there.
