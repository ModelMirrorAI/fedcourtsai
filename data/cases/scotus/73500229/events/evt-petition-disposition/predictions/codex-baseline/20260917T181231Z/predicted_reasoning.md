# Forecast of the Court's action

**The Court will deny the petition**, most likely through an ordinary orders list following the September 28, 2026 conference, in late September or early October. This is a timing forecast, not a retrieved schedule of disposition. The forecast conditions on the snapshot's one counted distribution and absence of a CVSG. Both recorded distribution notices concern that same conference. [S1, S2; source key in `retrieval.md`]

**No further distribution is the modal path.** I assign 18% to at least one additional counted distribution beyond the frozen count. If additional consideration occurs, one more conference is more likely than a prolonged series. I expect no CVSG; the probability of a new invitation is 2%. If the Court instead seeks federal views, I expect the request during the opening weeks of the Term, delaying disposition beyond the modal horizon. These are incremental forecasts, not descriptions of already completed acts.

The likely denial will leave the judgment below intact without adopting a merits rationale. My forecast is that the Court will not use this unpublished, threshold-objection-laden case to settle the broad constitutional claims. The reported prior refusal to review the controlling Curtis decision reinforces that prediction, while the requested response leaves a small chance of a different assessment. [S1, S3, S5, S6]

**If review is granted**, I expect the Court to focus on QP1, narrowed to whether federal consent protections for emergency-authorized products preclude conditioning healthcare employment on vaccination, and whether that theory supports the asserted Fourteenth Amendment claim. I do not expect the broader QP2 formulation about prospective loss of court access under PREP Act immunity to drive a standalone grant. These conditional issue-selection forecasts are based on the petition's QPs and the parties' vehicle dispute, not on a forecast of an inevitable majority opinion. [S3, S5-S7]

Conditional on any grant, I assign **35% to a summary disposition in the cert order**, and therefore 65% to the non-summary route. Plenary treatment is the likelier grant route because I identified no intervening merits ruling that supplies an obvious GVR instruction. Coordination with the related petitions requested in the reply remains possible. [S7, p. 12; predictor assessment]

Conditional on denial, I assign **5% to any noted dissent or statement respecting denial**. The modal outcome is no separate writing, and I do not forecast a particular Justice as its author.
