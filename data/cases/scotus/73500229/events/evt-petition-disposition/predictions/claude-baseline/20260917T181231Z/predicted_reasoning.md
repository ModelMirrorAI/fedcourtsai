# Forecast: what the Court will do with No. 25-1327, Roberts v. Ferguson

**Disposition.** The petition will be denied. The Court will not grant plenary
review, and there is no intervening decision that could support a GVR.

**Relists.** The docket shows one conference distribution (first placed on the
September 28, 2026 long-conference list on July 1, then redistributed for the
same conference on September 9 after the briefs in opposition came in). I
forecast from that single-conference state. The most likely path is a denial
at or shortly after the September 28 conference, but a further distribution is
a real possibility, roughly one in three. Two mechanisms could produce one: a
hold so the Court can consider this petition alongside the same counsel's
Boysen v. PeaceHealth (No. 25-1280), which also drew a called-for response and
whose briefs in opposition were filed September 4 and 8, so it will reach the
Court's October conferences; or a Justice taking a conference cycle to decide
whether to write. If relisted, expect one additional distribution, not a
string of them.

**CVSG.** None. The petition invokes the Food, Drug, and Cosmetic Act and the
PREP Act, but the Court has denied the same theories repeatedly this calendar
year without inviting the Solicitor General, and the federal government is not
a party.

**Which question, if any.** If the Court took anything, it would not take
either question as written. Question 1 is a preemption-and-informed-consent
argument dressed as a Fourteenth Amendment question, and Question 2 (that PREP
Act immunity extinguishes court access) was not the basis of the decision
below. A grant would have to reformulate around whether a State may condition
professional licensure on accepting an EUA-labeled vaccine when a licensed,
medically identical version exists. I do not expect the Court to do that work.

**Summary disposition.** Unlikely as a route to any grant in this case
standing alone, because no intervening decision bears on the question. The one
summary path with real mass is derivative: if the Court were to grant Boysen
or another petition in this line, this case would be held and then GVR'd. That
is why the conditional summary-route figure is set well above the ordinary
cert-order share.

**Dissent from denial.** Most likely none, as with the Court's June 1, 2026
denial of the lead case Curtis v. Inslee (No. 25-1119) and the same day's
denials in Sweeney and Horsley. The called-for response after both
respondents waived shows at least one chambers looked twice, which is why I
leave a modest probability of a short statement or dissent, most plausibly
from Justice Thomas or Justice Gorsuch, on the EUA-versus-licensed distinction
or the PREP Act point. It would be a solo or two-Justice writing, not a
four-vote signal.

**If granted (conditional only).** The Court would most likely affirm on the
Ninth Circuit's grounds: the Supremacy Clause supplies no section 1983
damages right, the asserted right to refuse a labeling-distinct version of an
approved vaccine is not fundamental under Glucksberg and Jacobson, and the
procedural due process and equal protection claims fail on rational-basis
review. The state-action and qualified-immunity questions the panel did not
reach would loom as alternative grounds.
