## In brief

A Catholic religious organization asks the Court to revive its claim that a Kentucky zoning denial of a shrine unlawfully burdened its religious exercise.

## What happened

Missionaries of Saint John the Baptist owns a church in Park Hills, Kentucky, and leased a neighboring lot to build a Marian grotto. A local board granted a variance allowing the project, but neighbors Joel and Elizabeth Frederic sued, arguing the board lacked authority under state law because the lot was not on an "arterial street" as the zoning ordinance required. Kentucky's courts agreed the board exceeded its authority, and the Kentucky Supreme Court also rejected the Missionaries' argument that denying the grotto violated a federal law protecting religious land use, holding the group could build a smaller grotto elsewhere and had known the restriction when it leased the land.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked whether refusing to let a religious group build a religious structure on land it chose counts as a serious burden on its religious exercise, and whether a zoning rule that applies the same site requirement to religious and comparable nonreligious buildings alike improperly treats religious institutions worse than nonreligious ones.

## Where it stands

The petition has been distributed for conference twice, most recently for September 28, 2026, after full briefing including the respondents' opposition and the petitioner's reply; the docket shows no ruling yet on whether the Court will hear the case.

## What each outcome would mean

The Missionaries, as petitioner, are asking the Court to take up the case. If the Court grants review, it would hear full arguments and decide what these federal protections require, without yet deciding who wins. If it denies review, the Kentucky Supreme Court's ruling against the Missionaries and in favor of the Fredericses stands, and the grotto could not be built as proposed. A denial would not mean the Court agrees the Kentucky court was right, only that its decision remains in place.
