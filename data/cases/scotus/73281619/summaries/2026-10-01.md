## In brief

The Supreme Court agreed to decide whether denying a Catholic organization's zoning request for a religious shrine imposed an unlawful burden on its religious exercise.

## What happened

Missionaries of Saint John the Baptist owns a church in Park Hills, Kentucky, and leased a neighboring residential lot to build a grotto honoring the Virgin Mary. A local zoning board granted the permits needed, but neighbors Joel and Elizabeth Frederic sued, arguing the board lacked authority to allow construction on land not adjacent to an "arterial street," as the ordinance required. Kentucky's courts agreed with the Fredericks and also rejected the Missionaries' argument that this violated a federal law protecting religious land use. The Kentucky Supreme Court affirmed, finding no substantial burden on religious exercise and no unequal treatment compared to similarly situated secular buildings.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked whether blocking a religious group from building a religious structure on property it chose counts as a serious interference with religious exercise under a federal land-use law, even though the group could build a smaller version elsewhere on land it already owned and knew the zoning rules when it acquired the property.

## Where it stands

After full briefing including many outside groups' briefs, the Supreme Court granted the petition on October 1, 2026, but only as to this first question; it did not take up the separate question about whether the zoning rule treated religious and nonreligious uses unequally. The case now proceeds to full briefing and argument on the merits.

## What each outcome would mean

The Missionaries, as petitioner, asked the Court to hear the case. Because the Court limited review to the first question, it will decide only whether denying the variance was a substantial burden on religious exercise. If the Court affirms, the Kentucky Supreme Court's ruling against the Missionaries stands, and the denial of the permit remains in place. If the Court reverses, the Missionaries would prevail on that legal question, likely requiring further proceedings on whether the burden was justified. If the Court vacates, it would send the case back without resolving the question for either side.
