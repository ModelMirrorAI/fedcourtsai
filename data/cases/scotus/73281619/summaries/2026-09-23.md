## What happened

Missionaries of Saint John the Baptist, Inc. owns a church in Park Hills, Kentucky, and leased a neighboring lot to build an outdoor grotto honoring the Virgin Mary. Local zoning bars such structures unless they sit on a "arterial street," which neither lot does. A city zoning board granted a variance anyway, but neighbors Joel and Elizabeth Frederic sued, arguing the board lacked authority to do so. Kentucky's courts agreed, and the Kentucky Supreme Court also rejected the Missionaries' argument that denying the grotto violated a federal law protecting religious land use.

## What the Court is being asked

The case asks about a federal law limiting how zoning rules can restrict religious property use. One question is whether blocking construction of a religious structure on a group's own land counts as a serious enough interference with religious practice to trigger stricter legal scrutiny, especially when the group knew about the zoning restriction beforehand and might build a smaller version elsewhere. The other question is whether a zoning rule that requires religious buildings to meet a condition not required of some other types of buildings improperly favors nonreligious over religious property owners, even if other nonreligious buildings face the same condition.

## Where it stands

The petition drew many outside briefs supporting the Missionaries' request that the Court hear the case, and the respondents, after two deadline extensions, filed a brief opposing review. The Missionaries have since replied, and the case has been distributed for the Court's conference on September 28, 2026, where the justices will decide whether to take it up.
