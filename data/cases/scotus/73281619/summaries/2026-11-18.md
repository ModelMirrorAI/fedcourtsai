## In brief

A Catholic organization asks the Supreme Court to decide whether denying it permission to build a religious shrine violated a federal law protecting religious land use.

## What happened

Missionaries of Saint John the Baptist wanted to build an outdoor shrine, or grotto, on land next to its church in Park Hills, Kentucky. The city's zoning board approved a permit and variances allowing the project, but neighbors Joel and Elizabeth Frederic sued, arguing the board lacked authority to grant them because the property was not on an arterial street as the zoning code required. Kentucky's courts agreed the permit was unlawful. The Kentucky Supreme Court also rejected the organization's argument that denying the permit violated a federal law, the Religious Land Use and Institutionalized Persons Act, which limits burdens on religious exercise in land-use decisions.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked whether blocking construction of the shrine placed an unlawful burden on the organization's religious exercise, given that a smaller version could be built elsewhere on its property and it knew in advance the zoning rule would apply. The Court is also asked whether the zoning code treated the shrine unequally by requiring churches, but not certain other buildings like golf courses or libraries, to be located on a major road.

## Where it stands

The Court granted review limited to the first question above and has set the case for argument on January 20, 2027. Briefing is underway, and the case has not yet been argued or decided.

## What each outcome would mean

Missionaries of Saint John the Baptist, the petitioner, is asking the Court to overturn the Kentucky Supreme Court's ruling that the zoning denial did not violate the federal law. If the Court sides with the organization, it would mean the denial of the permit must meet a stricter legal test, potentially requiring Kentucky courts to reconsider the case under that standard. If the Court affirms, the Kentucky Supreme Court's ruling stands, and the organization's inability to build the shrine as planned remains in place. A ruling to vacate would send the case back without resolving the question either way.
