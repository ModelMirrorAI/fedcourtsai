I expect the Court to reverse the Kentucky Supreme Court in a 6-3 decision, with the six conservative Justices in the majority and the three liberal Justices in dissent. The majority will likely hold that a land-use regulation prohibiting a religious institution from building a religious structure (such as a grotto) on its own property constitutes a "substantial burden" on religious exercise under RLUIPA, thereby triggering strict scrutiny. The decision will likely establish a broad, categorical rule that prohibiting religious structures on a religious institution's own property inherently constitutes a substantial burden, moving away from more fact-intensive inquiries that focus heavily on alternative locations or procedural history (like the variance denial). Since certiorari was granted only on Question 1, the Court will not reach the Equal Terms Provision issue presented in Question 2. No procedural exits like a DIG or an equally divided Court are expected.
