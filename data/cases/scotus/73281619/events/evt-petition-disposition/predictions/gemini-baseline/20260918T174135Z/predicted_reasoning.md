While the petition points to a deep and widely recognized circuit split over the interpretation of RLUIPA's substantial-burden and equal-terms provisions, severe vehicle problems make a grant unlikely. Specifically, the respondent plausibly argues invited error regarding the substantial-burden test and points out that the equal-terms claim was raised too late in state court to develop a factual record. No CVSG is expected, as this is a local zoning dispute with no federal government interest implicated. If the Court were to grant, it would likely take up the substantial-burden question to clarify the standard, but plenary review would be required to resolve the split; a summary disposition is unlikely. Given the religious liberty context and the presence of conservative justices who frequently champion these issues, there is a reasonable chance of a dissent from denial (e.g., from Justice Alito, Thomas, or Gorsuch) expressing frustration with the lower courts' narrow reading of RLUIPA, even if they agree the vehicle is flawed.
