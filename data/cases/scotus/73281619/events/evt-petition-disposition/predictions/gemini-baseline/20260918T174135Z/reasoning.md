P(grant) 0.05. The prior-Term salience band for an `elevated` petition is around 17%. Furthermore, the petition is on its first relist (distribution count 2), which ordinarily pushes the base rate of a grant higher (to roughly 15-25% for a relisted petition with strong counsel). The petition, filed by First Liberty and Quinn Emanuel, articulates a clean and widely recognized circuit split on two provisions of RLUIPA. However, I adjust my probability downward substantially because the Brief in Opposition highlights fatal vehicle problems. First, the respondent notes that the petitioner invited the error by affirmatively asking the Kentucky Supreme Court to adopt the very test (from the Sixth Circuit's *Livingston* decision) that it now challenges as too narrow. Second, the equal-terms claim was purportedly raised for the first time before the state supreme court, meaning there is no developed factual record regarding whether the religious institution and secular comparators are "similarly situated." The Supreme Court is highly averse to taking up fact-intensive tests without a developed record, and invited error is a classic reason to deny cert. Therefore, despite the relist and the high-profile counsel, a denial is the most likely outcome. The chance of a dissent from denial remains elevated due to the subject matter and the justices involved.
