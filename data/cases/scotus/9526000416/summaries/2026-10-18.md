## In brief

The federal government asks the Court to let it enforce a new Bureau of Prisons policy ending certain gender dysphoria treatments while lower-court litigation over the policy continues.

## What happened

Three inmates diagnosed with gender dysphoria sued, challenging Bureau of Prisons (BOP) decisions, prompted by a 2025 executive order, to stop providing hormone treatment, surgery, and social accommodations for the condition. A district court blocked enforcement, finding the inmates likely to win their claim that BOP acted "arbitrary and capricious" under a law governing agency decisions. After BOP issued a revised 2026 policy with a lengthy supporting record, the district court again blocked it, and renewed that order in August 2026. A divided appeals court panel declined to pause the order while the government's appeal proceeds.

## What the Court is being asked

The government is asking the Court to pause the lower court's order while the case continues on appeal. The dispute centers on whether the agency adequately explained its policy change, properly weighed its own prior experience, and complied with a law limiting court-ordered relief in prison cases; the inmates argue the agency ignored decades of its own experience and that its stated reasons do not hold up.

## Where it stands

This is an emergency application. The government applied for a stay on September 28, 2026; the Court asked for a response, which the inmates filed October 8, 2026, along with amicus briefs on both sides, and the government filed a reply October 9, 2026. The docket shows no ruling yet on the application.

## What each outcome would mean

The government, as applicant, is asking the Court to pause the district court's order. If granted, BOP could enforce its 2026 policy ending the challenged treatments while the appeal proceeds, and the inmates would not receive those treatments during that time. If denied, the district court's order stays in effect, requiring BOP to keep providing the treatments as before while the case continues in the lower courts. Either way, the pause is temporary and does not decide the underlying legal dispute, which remains pending in the appeals court.
