## In brief

The federal government asks the Court to pause a lower court order blocking its policy limiting transgender inmates' medical care while the case continues.

## What happened

Three federal prison inmates diagnosed with gender dysphoria sued the government, challenging a Bureau of Prisons policy that stopped providing sex-trait-modification surgeries, hormone treatments, and social accommodations (like clothing or grooming items matching gender identity) while continuing mental-health treatment. The inmates argued the policy was adopted without adequate reasoning and violates a federal law limiting court-ordered prison relief. A district court blocked the policy nationwide for the whole class of affected inmates. The government appealed, and a divided appeals court panel refused to pause that order while the appeal proceeds. The government then asked the Supreme Court to step in.

## What the Court is being asked

The government is asking the Court to pause the lower court's order while the underlying appeal continues, arguing the policy change was reasonably explained and that the district court's order goes further than a federal law governing prison litigation allows. The inmates have not yet filed their response.

## Where it stands

The application for a stay was filed and the Chief Justice has requested a response from the inmates, due October 8, 2026. The Court has not yet ruled on the request.

## What each outcome would mean

The government, as applicant, is asking the Court to pause the district court's order. Granting the stay would let the government enforce its 2026 policy limiting inmate medical care while the appeal continues, lifting the lower court's block. Denying the stay would leave the district court's order in place, meaning the government must keep providing hormone treatment and social accommodations to the inmate class as before. Either way, this is a temporary decision; it would not resolve the underlying lawsuit, which would continue in the lower courts.
