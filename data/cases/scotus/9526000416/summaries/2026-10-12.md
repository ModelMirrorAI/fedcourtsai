## In brief

The government asks the Court to let it enforce a new policy ending certain gender dysphoria treatments for federal inmates while the underlying lawsuit continues.

## What happened

Three federal inmates diagnosed with gender dysphoria sued, challenging Bureau of Prisons policies that followed a presidential executive order. A district court blocked the government's 2025 policy, finding it likely violated the Administrative Procedure Act. The Bureau then issued a new 2026 policy ending hormone treatment and social accommodations for gender dysphoria while continuing mental-health care, backed by an administrative record. The district court blocked this policy too, and a divided appeals panel declined to stay that order. The government then asked the Supreme Court to step in.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked to pause the lower court's order while the case continues, by deciding whether the government has shown it will likely win its appeal, will suffer irreparable harm without a pause, and that the balance of harms and public interest favor a pause. That requires weighing whether the prison agency adequately explained its policy change and complied with a law limiting court-ordered relief in prison cases.

## Where it stands

The government's application for a stay was submitted to the Chief Justice, who requested a response from the inmates; that response, opposing a stay, and the government's reply have been filed. The docket shows no ruling yet on the application.

## What each outcome would mean

The government, as applicant, is asking the Court to pause the lower court's order. If granted, the Bureau could enforce its 2026 policy ending hormone treatment and social accommodations for gender dysphoria while the appeal continues, and the inmates would lose that care in the meantime. If denied, the district court's order stays in effect, requiring the Bureau to keep providing the earlier treatment while the case proceeds. Either way, the pause itself would not decide who is ultimately right, and the case would continue in the lower courts.
