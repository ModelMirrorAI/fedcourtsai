# Forecast: Trump v. Kingdom, No. 26A416 (application for a stay)

**Moment:** response-filed (October 8, 2026). The record shows: application
submitted to the Chief Justice September 28; a response requested the same day
and filed October 8; two amicus submissions (America's Future; Indiana, Idaho
and other states) on October 8; no referral entry and no reply yet.

## Disposition

The Court will **grant the application in full** and stay the district court's
August 26, 2026 order pending the D.C. Circuit appeal and any petition for
certiorari. P(unqualified grant) = 0.63. The order will be a short unsigned
order, probably with a brief per curiam explanation keyed to deference owed to
prison administrators and to the agency's weighing of contested medical
evidence, and it will come with a dissent.

Why the Court grants rather than denies: the order below is a universal
Section 705 stay plus a nationwide class injunction that nullifies a federal
agency policy adopted under an executive order, which is the shape the Court
has stayed over and over since 2025 (Trump v. Orr, Trump v. California, DHS v.
League of Women Voters, National Park Service v. National Trust). The Court's
majority has treated an injunction against a federal program as irreparable
injury to the government nearly by rule, has stayed two earlier transgender-
policy injunctions on the merits (Shilling, Orr) by six to three, and in
Skrmetti framed the underlying medical questions as ones for politically
accountable actors. Bell v. Wolfish and Turner v. Safley give prison
administration an even stronger deference claim than the passport context.

The residual runs three ways. A **full denial** (about 0.25): the record gap
the district court and the D.C. Circuit majority identified is a genuine State
Farm / Encino Motorcars problem, the government did not appeal the 2025
injunction and waited seven weeks to seek a stay below, and withdrawing
ongoing hormone therapy from roughly a thousand prisoners is a concrete harm
that a Nken balance can weigh against an abstract separation-of-powers injury.
A **partial grant** (about 0.08), which the resolver scores as ungranted: a
stay as to new hormone initiations, surgery and social accommodations while
leaving current recipients' tapering enjoined, or a stay as to surgery alone
since respondents never claimed harm from that provision. A **dismissal or
withdrawal** (about 0.04): the August 26 order is a 90-day PLRA injunction
that lapses around November 24, 2026, and the June 17 order's stay motion was
already dismissed as moot once it expired, so an order that slips past that
date risks the same fate.

## The ladder from here

- **Response requested:** already on the record (September 28). Nothing further
  to forecast; the claim is vacuous for this cell.
- **Referral to the full Court:** will happen, almost certainly recorded in the
  disposing entry itself ("presented to The Chief Justice and by him referred
  to the Court"). A Solicitor General application with a full opposition on
  file is never decided by the Circuit Justice alone. P = 0.92; the complement
  is mostly the dismissal-or-withdrawal branch, in which no referral is ever
  entered.
- **Amicus count rises past 2:** a coin flip, P = 0.5. Both briefs on the
  docket support the applicants and arrived the day the response was due. Amici
  supporting respondents (medical associations, Democratic-led states, civil
  rights groups) have filed on every prior transgender-policy application and
  may still file, and a reply typically draws one or two more on the
  applicants' side; against that, the matter will be decided within weeks and
  the natural filing window has already closed.

## Timing

The Solicitor General files a reply within about a week (by roughly October
15). The Court rules between roughly October 20 and November 6, 2026: two to
four weeks after the response, which is the pattern for the administration's
contested applications in 2026, and comfortably before the August 26 order
lapses in late November. No argument will be set and no deferral.

## Merits content, conditionally

If the Court explains itself, the ground will be deference: that BOP acted
within the zone of reasonableness in weighing contested medical evidence and
institutional-security judgments, that the APA requires no agency to generate
its own clinical studies, and that a universal, class-wide injunction against
a federal prison policy warrants review. The PLRA findings argument will not
carry the order. The dissent (Jackson, joined by Sotomayor and Kagan) will say
the government showed no emergency after sitting on the 2025 injunction, that
the administrative record ignored thirty years of the agency's own practice,
and that the stay cuts off ongoing medical care to a certified class.
