## Why 0.68 rather than the pool's 0.105

The adjustment is substantial and judgmental, not a fitted conditional rate. This is a Solicitor General application challenging an injunction against a nationwide federal policy after appellate stay denial, not a routine individual request drawn at random from the substantive-application pool. Its combination of institutional stakes and separable remedial issues makes both full-Court attention and interim intervention materially more likely than the pooled rate suggests.

The strongest grant argument is the scope of relief. The application says the August 26 order prevents enforcement of the entire policy for all inmates who are or will be diagnosed with gender dysphoria. It contrasts that scope with the alleged harms from interruption of hormones and social accommodations, points to surgical provisions respondents did not seek to litigate in the preliminary motion, and identifies an express severability clause. Its PLRA argument supplies a comparatively narrow route to intervention without requiring the Court to resolve contested treatment evidence. Those assertions appear in application pages 29–33; the claim that required findings were inadequate is advocacy, not an established fact here.

A second route concerns administrative review. The application describes a replacement policy supported by a 43-page explanatory memorandum and a 3,200-page administrative record, with asserted medical-evidence and prison-security grounds. It argues that the district court substituted its judgment and relied on extra-record evidence. If that account is persuasive, the Court can find a fair prospect of appellate reversal without treating presidential preference alone as an adequate administrative explanation. Application pages 12–16 and 24–29 also show that the challenged policy and injunction are not simply the original 2025 actions.

There is meaningful contrary evidence even in this one-sided filing. The D.C. Circuit majority denied a stay on September 18 because BOP allegedly failed to address its own experience under the prior policy, and it rejected the government's PLRA showing. The district court found irreparable injury from loss of hormones and social accommodations. A long administrative record does not itself cure an omitted explanation. The government's assertion that its policy will cause no irreparable injury is disputed advocacy; the asserted tapering plans do not establish that interruption is harmless for every affected inmate. I make no independent medical-efficacy finding.

The traditional stay framework requires attention to likely success, the applicant's irreparable injury, injury to other parties, and the public interest, with the first two especially important: Nken v. Holder, 556 U.S. 418, 434–435 (2009), checked through CourtListener opinion 145884. The application additionally invokes the prospect of Supreme Court review under Hollingsworth at its page 15. National scope strengthens the review-interest argument, but does not automatically establish an urgent injury outweighing inmates' continuity-of-care interests. The months-long proceedings and an injunction maintaining existing treatment leave a credible status-quo argument against immediate intervention.

I also distinguish the appellate dissenter's objection to repeated 90-day injunctions from the government's principal Supreme Court arguments about findings, tailoring, and agency reasoning. I do not treat the dissenter's view as controlling law. The majority's reported forfeiture ruling concerning surgery-related overbreadth is another litigation risk, even though the government contests it at application page 32.

Most importantly, remedial overbreadth can support a partial stay instead of the unqualified grant this cell scores. My working allocation is 0.68 unqualified grant, 0.14 mixed partial relief recorded as denied, 0.17 outright denial, and 0.01 withdrawal or dismissal. This is why a strong expectation of some intervention does not produce an 0.8-plus top-level probability. The allocations are judgmental sensitivity framing, not additional claim fields.

A requested response at 0.97 and full-Court referral at 0.95 reflect the nationally applicable policy, represented opposition, and substantive disagreement in the lower courts. Submission to the Chief Justice is not itself a referral; both are predictions of new acts from the frozen zero state. I allow procedural exits rather than using certainty. None of these three probabilities has a published conditioned baseline.

The 0.77 significance score concerns the scope of prison health-care access and the administrative/remedial consequences, not the probability of a stay. The order could have serious consequences for the affected population while remaining a provisional disposition rather than a nationwide final merits rule. The principal discount on every number is the one-sided documentary record and the absence of a measured comparator rate for this particular governmental application class. I omit per-Justice votes rather than suggesting the docket will reveal a complete lineup.
