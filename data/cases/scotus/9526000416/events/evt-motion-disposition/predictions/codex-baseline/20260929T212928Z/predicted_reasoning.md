# Forecast of the Court's action

The Court will grant the government's application to stay the district court's August 26, 2026 order pending appellate review. The probability of an unqualified grant is **0.68**. A disposition expressly granting in part and denying in part belongs to the ungranted side of this forecast under the interim resolver's denial-first rule. An administrative pause alone is not the substantive disposition predicted here.

The starting record contains only the September 28 application submitted to the Chief Justice: no requested response, no referral to the full Court, and zero amicus entries. From that state:

- The Chief Justice will call for a response on an expedited schedule: **0.97** probability of a response-requested increment.
- The application will be referred to the full Court for disposition: **0.95** probability of a referral increment.
- At least one additional amicus entry will appear: **0.75** probability of an amicus increment. The modal forecast is one to three additional entries, counting submitted briefs as well as accepted briefs and avoiding double counting a submission's acceptance.

I expect a response deadline within approximately a week of submission and a substantive order within roughly one to three weeks, most likely October 2–16, 2026. Timing is a contextual forecast, not an additional declared claim. I expect a short order rather than a plenary merits opinion; publicly noted disagreement is plausible, but I do not forecast a recoverable vote count from a silent order.

If the Court explains a grant, its principal rationale will be that the government has shown sufficient prospects of success on the permissible scope of prison-related injunctive relief and on the district court's review of BOP's policy explanation, together with an institutional injury from preventing implementation of a nationwide prison policy. That would be a provisional stay rationale, not a final adjudication of the medical evidence or every possible constitutional claim.

The strongest alternative is relief limited to the injunction's reach, preserving protection for some inmates or treatments while permitting other policy provisions to operate. Such a mixed order is a substantial reason that the probability of an unqualified grant is lower than the probability of some relief. A denial leaving the injunction in place remains possible if continuity-of-care injuries and the unresolved APA record dominate the interim balance.
