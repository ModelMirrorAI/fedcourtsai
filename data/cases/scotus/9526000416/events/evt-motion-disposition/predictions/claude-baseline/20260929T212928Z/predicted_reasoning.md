# Forecast: Trump v. Kingdom, No. 26A416 (application for a stay)

**Vantage.** The provisioned snapshot ends at the entry that opened this event:
the Solicitor General's application for a stay, submitted to the Chief Justice on
September 28, 2026. At that entry the record shows no response requested, no
referral to the full Court, and no amicus entries. Every claim below is an
increment from that state.

## Disposition

**The Court will grant the stay in full**, staying the District of Columbia
district court's August 26, 2026 order (the universal Section 705 stay and
classwide preliminary injunction against the Bureau of Prisons' 2026
gender-dysphoria policy) pending the D.C. Circuit appeal and any petition for
certiorari. P(unqualified grant) = 0.68. The order will most likely be a short
unsigned order from the full Court, with the three Justices who dissented in
Trump v. Orr (Sotomayor, Kagan, Jackson) noting a dissent, Justice Jackson
writing. The main alternative to a clean grant is a **qualified order**: a stay
of the injunction insofar as it reaches sex-trait-modification surgery and
inmates not currently receiving hormones, while leaving it in force for inmates
whose existing hormone regimen the policy would taper. The government's own PLRA
overbreadth argument invites exactly that carve-out, and the resolver scores it
as ungranted. Outright denial is the second alternative; withdrawal or dismissal
is remote.

Conditional content: if the Court grants, the signal it sends will be the Orr
signal, that the government is likely to prevail because the district court
applied APA review without the deference owed to an agency acting on a
3,200-page record, especially a prison administrator invoking security
judgments, and that a universal Section 705 stay plus a nationwide class
injunction inflicts the irreparable harm the Court has recognized whenever an
Executive policy is blocked wholesale. It will not reach the Eighth Amendment,
which the district court did not rest on. I do not expect the Court to treat the
application as a petition for certiorari before judgment; the D.C. Circuit
appeal is consolidated and briefing there is the ordinary path.

## The escalation ladder

- **Response requested.** Yes, already. The Chief Justice called for a response
  later on September 28, due 4 p.m. October 8, 2026. That entry sits outside the
  arrival-position baseline but is on the live docket, so the increment
  resolves true (P = 0.98; the residual is resolution mechanics, not doubt).
- **Referral to the full Court.** Yes (P = 0.94). Every Solicitor General
  application in the corpus's Term-2026 rows that drew a response request and
  reached a decision was referred, and Orr was referred on decision day. The
  referral will most likely appear on the docket the day the Court rules, as it
  did in Orr, rather than as an early standalone entry. The residual covers a
  withdrawal or an in-chambers disposition, both unlikely for an SG application.
- **Amicus entries.** The count will rise from zero (P = 0.93). Orr drew one
  state-coalition brief inside a two-week response window; this application has
  a ten-day window and a far more mobilized field on both sides (medical
  associations and civil-rights groups for respondents, a state coalition and
  policy groups for the government). Expect three to ten entries, most filed in
  the two days around the October 8 deadline.

## Timing

Response due October 8; a government reply within a day or two. Orr's stay
issued a month after its response deadline; Trump v. California and League of
Women Voters issued seventeen and seventeen days after submission. Expect the
disposition between October 14 and November 6, 2026, with the last two weeks of
October most likely. The PLRA clock is a constraint: the August 26 order expires
by its own terms around November 24, so the Court has reason to act before then
rather than let the application go stale. No administrative stay before the
response arrives; the Chief Justice took none and set an unhurried schedule.
