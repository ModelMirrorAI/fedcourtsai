# Forecast — Pavatt v. Oklahoma, No. 26A661 (stay of execution)

**State on the record:** Application for a stay of execution submitted to Justice Gorsuch (Circuit Justice for the Tenth Circuit) on Nov. 9, 2026. Execution is set for Nov. 12, 2026 at 10 a.m. CT. It is linked to cert petition No. 26-6095 from the Oklahoma Court of Criminal Appeals (PCD-2026-827, a successive post-conviction proceeding). No response has been requested, the application has not been referred, and no amicus briefs are on file.

**Disposition:** **denied**. The Court will deny the stay, and almost certainly deny cert in 26-6095 by the same order or on the same day, on Nov. 11 or 12 before the execution time. The Court very rarely grants a private prisoner's application to stay an Oklahoma execution. The notable exception is Glossip (2023), where the State's own Attorney General confessed error. Nothing like that is present here.

**Referral:** very likely. Justice Gorsuch will refer it to the full Court, which is the standard handling for a capital stay application on the eve of an execution. The denial will read "referred to the Court" and be "denied."

**Response:** Oklahoma will file a brief in opposition and a response to the stay application, probably uninvited, within a day. A formal call for a response from the Court or Justice Gorsuch is unlikely, because the State answers on its own on this timeline.

**Amicus:** none expected.

**Separate writing:** most likely the order is unexplained, perhaps with a notation that one or more Justices (Sotomayor, possibly Jackson) would grant. A written dissent from the denial of the stay is possible but improbable (~10% that anyone writes). If someone writes, it would most likely be Justice Sotomayor, given her statement in *Storey v. Lumpkin* (2022), which the application cites. Justice Jackson is next most likely.

**If it were granted (conditional, unlikely):** the stay would run pending disposition of 26-6095. Plenary review would take the question of whether *Peña-Rodriguez*'s Sixth Amendment exception to the no-impeachment rule extends to juror testimony about reliance on knowingly false prosecutorial argument.
