The Supreme Court is very likely to deny James Dwight Pavatt's emergency application to stay his November 12, 2026 execution, with only a 3% chance (about one in thirty-three) of granting a stay. Pavatt asks the Justices to create a new constitutional exception to the long-standing rule that jurors cannot testify later to impeach their own verdict, relying on a juror's statement about how prosecutors' closing arguments affected sentencing deliberations in his 2001 murder case. Because the Court has strictly limited that exception to cases involving explicit racial bias during jury deliberations—and historically grants only about 3.5% of capital stay requests from prisoners—it is highly unlikely to halt the execution.
