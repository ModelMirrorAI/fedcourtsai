## In brief

James Pavatt asked the Supreme Court to stay his execution while it considers his petition challenging his death sentence.

## What happened

James Pavatt was sentenced to death in Oklahoma. He later obtained a juror's statement saying that prosecutors told the jury, falsely, that Pavatt alone fired the fatal shots, and that this claim led him to vote for death. The Oklahoma Court of Criminal Appeals denied Pavatt's challenge to his conviction based on this new evidence. Pavatt then filed a petition asking the Supreme Court to review that decision, and, because his execution was scheduled for November 12, 2026, he also asked the Court to pause the execution until it rules on the petition. Oklahoma opposed the stay, and Pavatt filed a reply.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked to pause the execution while it decides whether to hear Pavatt's case. The underlying petition asks whether a state's rule normally barring jurors from testifying about their deliberations must give way when a juror says prosecutors used knowingly false information that affected the juror's vote for death, and whether a death sentence obtained that way can stand.

## Where it stands

The application for a stay of execution was submitted to Justice Gorsuch on November 9, 2026. Oklahoma filed a response opposing it the same day, and Pavatt filed a reply on November 10, 2026. The docket shows no ruling yet on the stay application.

## What each outcome would mean

Pavatt, who lost before the Oklahoma Court of Criminal Appeals, is asking the Court to pause his execution. If the Court grants the stay, the execution would not proceed while the Court considers whether to hear his case. If the Court denies the stay, Oklahoma could carry out the execution as scheduled, and the case would not receive further review from the Court. Either way, the stay decision itself would not resolve the underlying legal questions about his sentence.
