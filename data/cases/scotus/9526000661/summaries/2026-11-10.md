## In brief

James Pavatt asks the Court to pause his execution while it considers his appeal claiming prosecutors used false information to win his death sentence.

## What happened

James Pavatt was sentenced to death in Oklahoma. He has filed a petition asking the Supreme Court to review a decision of the Oklahoma Court of Criminal Appeals denying him post-conviction relief. Pavatt's application says a juror has since stated that prosecutors told the jury, falsely, that Pavatt alone fired the fatal shots, and that this claim affected at least one juror's vote for death. Because Oklahoma's rules normally bar jurors from impeaching their own verdicts, Pavatt argues the Constitution requires an exception here. His execution is scheduled for November 12, 2026. The State of Oklahoma has filed a response opposing a stay.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked whether it should pause the execution while it decides whether to hear Pavatt's case. The underlying appeal asks whether the Constitution requires courts to set aside a death sentence, and to let jurors' statements be used as evidence, when new information shows prosecutors presented knowingly false claims that swayed a juror's vote for death.

## Where it stands

Pavatt's stay application was submitted to Justice Gorsuch and the State has filed a response opposing it. The docket does not yet record any ruling on the application or on the underlying certiorari petition.

## What each outcome would mean

Pavatt, who lost before the Oklahoma Court of Criminal Appeals, is asking the Court to pause his execution. Granting the stay would postpone the execution while the Court considers whether to hear his appeal, keeping him alive pending that decision. Denying the stay would leave the execution date in place, and it could proceed before the Court rules on his petition. Either way, a decision on the stay itself does not resolve whether Pavatt's underlying constitutional claims are correct.
