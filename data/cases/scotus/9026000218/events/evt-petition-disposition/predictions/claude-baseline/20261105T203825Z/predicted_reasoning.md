# Forecast: Matthews v. Louisiana, No. 26-218

**Where the docket stands.** The petition has been distributed once, for the
October 9, 2026 conference. Louisiana waived its response on September 16. The
Court then requested a response on September 29. The time for that response
was extended to November 30, 2026. No CVSG appears and there are no relists.

**Further distribution: yes, almost certainly.** Once Louisiana's response is
filed (around November 30), the petitioner can reply, and the petition will
then be distributed again, most likely for a conference in January 2027. I
expect one more distribution and probably a denial at that conference. If the
Court is considering a summary reversal, which is the remedy the petition asks
for in the alternative, expect one or more relists after that, with a per
curiam opinion or a denial with a written dissent to follow.

**CVSG: no.** This is a state criminal post-conviction case. The federal
government has no stake, and the Court essentially never calls for the
Solicitor General's views in such cases.

**Which question, if granted.** The single question presented, as written:
whether the Louisiana courts denied the Brady claim on a sufficiency-of-the-
evidence basis. Any grant is most likely to be a per curiam summary reversal or
a vacate-and-remand in the style of *Wearry v. Cain*, holding that the state
courts focused on the strength of the eyewitness identifications instead of
assessing the cumulative effect of the suppressed evidence. That outcome is
somewhat more likely than plenary review with argument. The Court would not
write a broad rule about Louisiana Brady practice. The Fourth Circuit's
opinion recited the "undermine confidence" standard, and that wording makes a
categorical holding unlikely.

**Most likely outcome: denial.** The suppressed evidence is a non-identification
by a non-testifying witness, a fleeing-man description from a canvass, and a
residence discrepancy for the child eyewitness. That is weaker than the record
in *Wearry*, and two other eyewitnesses identified Matthews at trial. A majority
will probably see it as a fact-bound disagreement over materiality.

**Separate writing on a denial.** There is a real chance of a dissent from
denial or a statement respecting it. Justice Sotomayor and Justice Jackson are
the most likely authors, given their past writing on Brady and on wrongful
conviction cases from Louisiana and elsewhere. On a denial, I put the chance of
any separate writing at about 30%.
