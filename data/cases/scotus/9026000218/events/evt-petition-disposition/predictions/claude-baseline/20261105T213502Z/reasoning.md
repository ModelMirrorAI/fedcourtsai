# Why P(grant) = 0.12

**Inputs read.** The snapshot `record/snapshots/2026-11-05.json` and `context.json` (forward mode, band `baseline` under sal-v7, distribution_count 1, no CVSG, `cert_anchor.rate` 0.0515 over 10 lookback Terms). From the provisioned documents I used `questions-presented.txt` and the petition's text through the statement of the suppressed evidence (`petition.txt`, 50 pages, not truncated). `appendix.txt` was provisioned (truncated). I did not read it beyond what the petition cites. No brief in opposition exists yet: the Court requested one, and it is due November 30, 2026. My view of the State's side is therefore inference only. `same-question.json` lists no similar petitions (empty list), and the docket has no Vide links.

**Anchor.** The frozen cert anchor is 5.15%: the pooled `reached` rate for the baseline band in prior Terms. The per-Term baseline `reached` figures for OT2017–OT2025 run from 4.0% to 6.0%.

**Upward adjustments.**
- *Response requested after a waiver.* The State waived, and the Court called for a response anyway at its first conference. That means at least one Justice, or a clerk's pool memo, flagged the petition. Most such petitions are still denied, but the grant rate among them runs well above the baseline band's.
- *Amicus support.* There are four amicus briefs, including Cato (cross-ideological) and a group of exonerees. In the statpack's amicus cut, the 2+ bucket has n=1,446 resolved: 18.2% granted and 4.5% GVR. That figure is marginal and measured at disposition, so it overlaps with the response-request signal.
- *Counsel and posture.* Elite Supreme Court counsel (Hogan Lovells together with the Southern Center for Human Rights). The petition is framed as a recurring Louisiana error that the Court has corrected before (*Kyles*, *Smith v. Cain*, *Wearry v. Cain*). That makes a per curiam summary reversal a realistic route.
- *Merits look strong as presented.* By the petition's account, the case was a one-day trial built on three inconsistent eyewitnesses with no forensic link. The suppressed canvass notes describe a fleeing man who does not match the petitioner, and the trial court found that the State failed to disclose. I have seen only the petitioner's side.

**Downward adjustments.**
- It is fact-bound error correction from an unreported state intermediate-court writ ruling. The claimed split ("consensus of other courts") is weak and really an application split. The current Court mostly denies petitions like this.
- It is non-capital and the conviction is from 1992. The petitioner's 2021 petition (No. 20-7311) was denied. The Louisiana Supreme Court denied review without opinion.
- The State's coming opposition will probably argue that the lower court did apply materiality, and the appellate opinion's wording may support that. That would make the "wrong standard" framing look like disagreement over the facts.
- Similar Louisiana Brady petitions in recent Terms (e.g. *Brown v. Louisiana*, 2023, which the petition cites) were denied.

**Net.** The response request and amicus signals roughly double or triple the 5% anchor. Fact-boundness and a quiet current appetite for Brady error correction temper that. I settle at 0.12, with about 55% of the grant mass on a summary disposition (summary reversal or GVR-like vacatur) rather than plenary review.

**Other claims.** The petition needs to be distributed again after the response, so the relist increment is 0.96; the 4% covers withdrawal or mootness. The CVSG increment is 0.005 because there is no federal interest. The dissent-from-denial figure is 0.33, given the Sotomayor/Jackson history on Brady and Louisiana cases.

**Where to discount me.** I have no brief in opposition. I did not read the full appendix, so I did not check the Louisiana Fourth Circuit's actual wording against the petition's characterization. The per-band table cannot isolate the response-request signal. My corpus queries added nothing case-specific.
