# Forecast: Matthews v. Louisiana, No. 26-218

**State I am forecasting from.** The docket shows one distribution (for the October 9, 2026 conference). Louisiana waived its response on September 16. The Court then called for a response on September 29, before or at that conference. The response is now due November 30, 2026 after an extension. There is no CVSG. Four amicus briefs are on file: the Innocence Network, Orleans Public Defenders, the Cato Institute, and fifteen Louisiana Brady exonerees.

**Further distributions.** I am close to certain the petition is distributed again. Once the brief in opposition is filed (by November 30) and the reply window closes, it should go to a conference in late December or January 2027. I expect one more distribution before a disposition in most outcomes. If the Court is weighing a summary reversal, or a Justice is writing a dissent from denial, I expect two or more relists after that.

**CVSG.** None. This is a state criminal post-conviction case with no federal interest, so the Court has no reason to invite the Solicitor General.

**Most likely outcome: certiorari denied**, probably at a conference in January or February 2027.

**Question the Court would take.** If it grants, it would take the single question as written: whether the Louisiana courts applied a sufficiency-of-the-evidence test in place of the Kyles/Strickler materiality standard. A plenary grant would probably reframe that as an application of Kyles. It would not announce a new rule.

**Route if granted.** A summary disposition is somewhat more likely than plenary review. The closest model is *Wearry v. Cain* (2016): a per curiam summary reversal of a Louisiana post-conviction Brady denial on materiality grounds. The petition asks for summary reversal explicitly. A Brady materiality ruling is fact-bound error correction, and the Court usually handles that summarily when it acts at all. If the Court summarily reverses, I expect a dissent, most likely from Justice Alito and/or Justice Thomas.

**Separate writing on a denial.** Plausible, at about one in three. The most likely writers are Justice Sotomayor and Justice Jackson. Both have written on Brady/Napue denials and on capital and non-capital state post-conviction cases. A dissent from denial would argue that the Louisiana courts repeated the error *Kyles* and *Wearry* corrected. Members of the Court's conservative wing are unlikely to write on a denial.
