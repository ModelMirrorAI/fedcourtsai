## Case-Specific Adjustments from the 5.15% `baseline` Anchor

### 1. Positive Factors (Upward Pressure on Attention / CFR Hazard)
- **Experienced Supreme Court Counsel and Coordinated Amicus Support:** Petitioner is represented by Jo-Ann Tamila Sagar (Hogan Lovells) alongside the Southern Center for Human Rights (`petition.txt` at 39), and drew four timely cert-stage amicus briefs highlighting systemic *Brady* compliance issues in Orleans Parish and Louisiana state post-conviction review.
- **Dissents Below:** On the Louisiana Fourth Circuit Court of Appeal, Judge Rosemary Ledet Jenkins dissented from the denial of supervisory writs (`App. 7a-12a`), and on the Louisiana Supreme Court, Chief Justice Weimer voted to grant and docket (`App. 1a`).

### 2. Negative Factors (Why Certiorari or Summary Reversal Is Unlikely)
- **Waiver of Response and No Call for a Response (CFR):** Respondent State of Louisiana waived its response on September 15, 2026, and the petition was distributed on November 4, 2026 for the November 20, 2026 conference without a CFR. The Court does not grant plenary review or summarily reverse a state criminal conviction without first calling for a response. While a chamber could still request a response at or before the November 20 conference, most petitions distributed on a waiver are denied on the first conference list.
- **No Genuine Doctrinal Split and Explicit Application of *Kyles* / *Bagley* by the Reasoned Opinion Below:** Petitioner contends (`questions-presented.txt`; `petition.txt` at 17–24) that the Louisiana courts violated *Kyles v. Whitley*, 514 U.S. 419, 434–35 (1995), by denying his *Brady* claim based on the sufficiency of the trial evidence after discounting the suppressed evidence. That characterization rests heavily on the Orleans Parish trial court's brief orally dictated judgment (`App. 16a`) and the Louisiana Supreme Court's two-sentence summary writ denial (`App. 1a-2a`). Under 28 U.S.C. § 1257, however, the operative reasoned decision left undisturbed below is the majority opinion of the Louisiana Court of Appeal, Fourth Circuit (`App. 3a-7a`). That opinion explicitly cited *Brady*, *United States v. Bagley*, 473 U.S. 667 (1985), *Kyles*, *Smith v. Cain*, 565 U.S. 73 (2012), and *Wearry v. Cain*, 577 U.S. 385 (2016) (`App. 4a-6a`), examined all three items of suppressed evidence alongside the trial record, and held that the suppressed evidence was "neither singularly compelling nor collectively material when assessed alongside the trial record" and "not reasonably likely to have changed the result" (`App. 6a`). Even Judge Jenkins in dissent (`App. 7a, 12a`) explicitly stated "I would not consider the merits of the claim" and would have left petitioner's conviction and sentence intact while remanding solely for the trial court to state its findings using the *Kyles*/*Bagley* standard (`App. 7a n.2, 12a`).
- **Substantial Vehicle and Factual Weaknesses in the Underlying *Brady* Record:** A close reading of the trial transcript and police file in `appendix.txt` reveals why this case is a far weaker vehicle than *Smith v. Cain* or *Wearry v. Cain*:
  1. **The jury already heard at trial that eyewitness Natha Rubin was on the scene and could not make an identification:** Petitioner's centerpiece *Brady* claim is that the State suppressed Natha Rubin's April 1, 1992 police statement and her inability to identify Matthews in a six-person photographic lineup (`petition.txt` at 7–8, 25–26; `App. 77a-79a, 103a-106a`). Yet the trial transcript in the appendix (`App. 137a, 140a-141a`) shows that lead homicide Detective Elizabeth Wigginton testified on both direct and cross-examination that Natha Rubin was the first witness interviewed, was present at the scene at the time of the shooting, and was unable to make an identification of Matthews to the date of trial. Moreover, Rubin's written statement (`App. 77a, 79a`) explained that a parked blue van blocked her view of the shooting itself, that she did not see a gun or closely observe the man's face, and that the man she saw walking away toward Robertson Street was a stout young Black male (5'11"–6', ~220 lbs, wearing a multicolored sweatshirt and black jeans)—which largely matched Matthews's build (5'11", 290 lbs, `App. 74a`), the clothing described in the search warrant (black pants and dark multicolored shirt, `App. 157a`), and the direction of flight toward Robertson Street (`App. 83a`).
  2. **Leroy Milton did not see the shooting or the perpetrator's face:** Detective Fejka's field notes (`App. 101a-102a`) show that neighbor Leroy Milton at 2612 Valence Street only looked across the street *after* hearing gunshots, saw a group of men running toward Claiborne Avenue and a stocky, dark-complexioned man wearing a baseball cap running toward S. Robertson Street, and explicitly stated "[h]e didn't see the mans face" (`App. 102a`). His rough age estimate ("about 40 years") of a fleeing man whose face he never saw has minimal exculpatory weight.
  3. **The neighborhood canvass notes do not prove 10-year-old Vanessa Henry lied about living on Valence Street:** Petitioner argues (`petition.txt` at 8–9, 26) that Detective Fejka's field notes (`App. 98a-102a`) impeach 10-year-old eyewitness Vanessa Henry because they do not list her family at 2608 Valence Street. In fact, Fejka's notes merely record brief evening verbal statements from selected adults at seven addresses on the block (and do not list children's names or residents at 2608 Valence Street, nor do they cover 2614 or 2616 Valence Street at all).
  4. **Three trial eyewitnesses, two of whom knew Matthews for years as "Beaver", plus Matthews's own statements:** Unlike *Smith v. Cain* (where a single eyewitness's trial identification was directly contradicted by his own suppressed police statements), three eyewitnesses identified Matthews at trial: Lloyd Martin (who knew Matthews since age 13 as "Beaver" and identified him in a formal statement on April 1, 1992, the day after the murder and *before* Martin's subsequent heroin arrest, which was disclosed and cross-examined at trial, `App. 5a, 81a, 155a`), 12-year-old Dominique Gibson (who knew Matthews from the neighborhood as "Beaver" and was standing right beside the victim handing him a Hawaiian Punch and Hot Fries when Matthews shot him 13 times, `App. 5a, 11a, 81a, 148a-151a`), and 10-year-old Vanessa Henry. None of the withheld evidence impeaches Dominique Gibson. In addition, Matthews met with Detective Herman Cade five hours after the murder and admitted arguing with the victim earlier that day (`App. 75a`), and at trial presented an alibi contradicted by his own statements (`App. 11a, 15a`).
- **Close Corpus Priors from Louisiana Were Denied:** Corpus queries confirm that the Court recently denied certiorari in two closely analogous Louisiana *Brady* petitions even after calling for responses and relisting multiple times:
  - *James Skinner v. Louisiana*, No. 25-1 (`scotus/73110505`), featuring nearly identical amicus support (Innocence Network, Cato Institute, Evan Bergeron, law professors) challenging Louisiana's post-conviction *Brady* practices, was denied on March 30, 2026 after 9 distributions.
  - *David Brown v. Louisiana*, No. 22-77 (`scotus/67123792`), 143 S. Ct. 886 (2023), a capital case raising the exact same legal claim that Louisiana courts improperly evaluated *Brady* materiality under a sufficiency-of-the-evidence test, was denied on April 3, 2023 after 7 distributions (over a dissent from denial by Justice Jackson, joined by Justices Sotomayor and Kagan).

## Rationale for Probability and Secondary Claims

- **`probability` / `disposition` = `0.04`:** Adjusting downward slightly from the 5.15% `baseline` band anchor to reflect the waiver of response (no CFR yet), the Louisiana Fourth Circuit's explicit application of *Kyles*/*Bagley* cumulative materiality, the weak factual value of the suppressed notes relative to the three eyewitnesses and the trial testimony already disclosing Rubin's non-identification, and the denials in *Skinner* (No. 25-1) and *Brown* (No. 22-77), balanced against the four amicus briefs and experienced Supreme Court counsel.

## Key Uncertainties

The primary uncertainty is whether Justice Jackson's or Justice Sotomayor's chambers will request a Call for a Response from Louisiana to scrutinize the Orleans Parish trial court's oral ruling and the state post-conviction record; even if a CFR issues, however, Louisiana's Brief in Opposition will highlight the Fourth Circuit majority's explicit cumulative *Kyles* analysis and the trial transcript showing the jury already heard about Rubin's non-identification, making an ultimate grant or summary reversal unlikely.
