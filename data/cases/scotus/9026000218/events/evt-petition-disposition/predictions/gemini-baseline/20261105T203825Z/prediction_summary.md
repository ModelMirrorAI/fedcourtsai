The Supreme Court is very likely to deny Juan Matthews's request to review his 1992 Louisiana murder conviction, with only a 4% chance of granting review. Matthews argues that prosecutors violated *Brady v. Maryland*—the constitutional rule requiring the government to turn over evidence favorable to a criminal defendant—by withholding police notes from neighborhood witnesses, and that state judges wrongly dismissed his claim because other trial evidence was strong. Although four outside legal groups filed briefs supporting Matthews, the Louisiana appeals court explicitly applied the correct cumulative legal standard and found the withheld notes would not have changed the verdict, especially since three eyewitnesses identified Matthews at trial and the jury was already told that one witness at the scene could not identify him.
