The docket currently records **one distribution** (`distribution_count: 1`), entered on November 4, 2026 for the Conference of November 20, 2026, following Respondent State of Louisiana's October 27, 2026 waiver of its right to respond and with zero amicus briefs filed.

The Court is very likely (92%) to deny the petition at the November 20, 2026 conference without calling for a response (CFR) or relisting the petition for a further distribution. There is a modest 8% probability (`relist-increment: 0.08`) that the petition is distributed at least once more—either because a Justice requests a response from Louisiana (which would trigger at least one subsequent distribution once the brief in opposition is filed) or because a chambers briefly holds or relists the petition to examine the state-court record alongside prior Louisiana *Brady* petitions. Given that the Court just denied *James Skinner v. Louisiana*, No. 25-1, on March 30, 2026 after nine distributions on the identical question presented—and given the independent state procedural bar on the face of the Louisiana Supreme Court's per curiam order here—any relist is unlikely.

## 2. Call for the Views of the Solicitor General (`cvsg-increment`: 0.001)

The Court will not call for the views of the Solicitor General (`cvsg-increment: 0.001`). This case is a state prisoner's collateral challenge to a 1992 Louisiana state-court murder conviction under *Brady v. Maryland*, 373 U.S. 83 (1963), implicating no federal statute, treaty, regulatory program, or federal enforcement interest.

## 3. Question Presented If Granted (Context-Only)

If the Court were to call for a response and grant review, it would take the single question presented in the petition—whether the Louisiana courts erred by denying Petitioner's *Brady* claim based on the sufficiency of the evidence at trial rather than applying *Kyles v. Whitley*, 514 U.S. 419 (1995)—though any grant would also have to confront threshold jurisdictional/procedural questions concerning the Louisiana Supreme Court's explicit reliance on La. Code Crim. Proc. arts. 930.4 and 930.8.

## 4. Summary Disposition vs. Plenary Review Conditional on Grant (`summary-disposition-route`: 0.65)

Conditional on the Court granting review ( after calling for a response), a summary disposition in the certiorari order itself—either a summary reversal under the *Smith v. Cain*, 565 U.S. 73 (2012), and *Wearry v. Cain*, 577 U.S. 385 (2016), line of cases (as Petitioner explicitly requests at Pet. 31–34) or a grant, vacatur, and remand (GVR) directing the state courts to clarify whether their materiality analysis conformed to *Kyles*—is substantially likelier (`0.65`) than full merits briefing and oral argument (`0.35`). The legal standard under *Brady* and *Kyles* is settled, and the petition asks for error correction of what it characterizes as Louisiana's recurring misapplication of that settled standard.

## 5. Separate Writings Upon Denial (`dissent-from-denial`: 0.04; `order-writers`)

Conditional on a denial of certiorari, the Court will most likely issue a routine, unsigned order denying the petition without comment (`96%`). There is an estimated `4.0%` probability (`dissent-from-denial: 0.04`) that one or more Justices author a dissent from, or statement respecting, the denial of certiorari.

If a separate writing is published, the likeliest authors are **Justice Jackson** (`0.025`) or **Justice Sotomayor** (`0.020`), potentially joined by **Justice Kagan** (`0.005` as author). Justice Jackson, joined by Justices Sotomayor and Kagan, authored a dissent from the denial of certiorari in *Brown v. Louisiana*, 143 S. Ct. 886 (2023), criticizing the Louisiana Supreme Court's handling of *Brady* materiality, and Justice Sotomayor has repeatedly written on *Brady* enforcement in Louisiana. However, because no Justice wrote separately when the Court denied *Skinner v. Louisiana* (No. 25-1) on March 30, 2026, and because the Louisiana Supreme Court's order in *Matthews* rests on an alternative state timeliness and successive-petition bar (`La.C.Cr.P. arts. 930.4 & 930.8`), a separate writing here is unlikely.
