The Supreme Court is very likely to deny Juan Matthews's petition for review, with only a 1.5% chance of granting the case (about one in sixty-seven). Matthews, who was convicted of murder in Louisiana in 1992, argues that prosecutors violated *Brady v. Maryland*—the constitutional rule requiring the government to turn over favorable evidence to the defense—and that the state courts used the wrong legal test when they concluded the withheld police files would not have changed the verdict. However, the state waived its right to respond, the Supreme Court recently turned down a nearly identical Louisiana appeal backed by seven outside supporter briefs, and the Louisiana Supreme Court also rejected Matthews's claim on an independent state-law deadline rule because it was filed more than thirty years after his conviction.
