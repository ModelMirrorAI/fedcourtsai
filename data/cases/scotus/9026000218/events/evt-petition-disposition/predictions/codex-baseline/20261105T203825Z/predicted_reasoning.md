# Forecast of the Court's disposition

The Court will most likely deny certiorari after Louisiana's requested response and any timely reply are processed. The present docket shows **one distribution and no relist yet**. I expect **one further distribution**, most naturally in early 2027 after the November 30, 2026 response deadline and ordinary briefing intervals, followed by denial. Another substantive conference cycle is possible but is not my modal path. The probability of at least one additional distribution is 99%; that includes redistribution caused by the response request rather than only a merits-driven relist.

The Court will not call for the Solicitor General's views (0.6% probability of a new CVSG). This petition concerns application of settled constitutional disclosure obligations in a state criminal case, not a distinct federal administrative or sovereign interest requiring the government's advice.

Denial will most likely be unsigned and without explanation. The state appellate majority's express discussion of cumulative materiality gives the Court a basis to leave a fact-specific application dispute undisturbed without revising Brady doctrine. This describes the likely institutional reason for declining review; I do not predict a majority opinion or an announced rationale accompanying denial.

If review is granted (14% overall), the Court will most likely address whether the Louisiana courts actually evaluated the withheld evidence cumulatively under Brady's confidence-in-the-verdict standard, rather than whether sufficient trial evidence remains. That is a narrowed treatment of the petition's sole question, not an adoption of a new disclosure rule. Conditional on a grant, I assign 65% to disposition in the cert order itself, principally an unsigned summary reversal with remand for proper materiality analysis. I do not expect a routine GVR tied to an identified intervening decision. Plenary review is the less likely grant-side route.

Conditional on denial, I assign 18% to a dissent or statement respecting denial. No separate writing is the modal forecast; if one appears, Sotomayor is the leading potential author, followed by Jackson. Any writing would most likely stress cumulative impeachment, the lower-court dissent, and the need to enforce existing disclosure precedents rather than announce a different Brady test.
