# Forecast of the Court's action

The Court will deny the petition after Louisiana's requested response and the ensuing consideration. The baseline contains **one distribution** and **no CVSG**. I expect **one additional distribution**, with a decision most likely in early 2027, after the November 30, 2026 response deadline and the reply and circulation period. That redistribution completes briefing after the earlier waiver; it need not represent a substantive relist following conference consideration. The probability of at least one additional distribution is **98%**. Multiple further distributions are possible but are not the modal forecast.

The Court will not call for the Solicitor General's views. The probability of a new CVSG is **0.5%**. The question concerns application of established constitutional disclosure obligations in a state criminal case, rather than an unresolved federal institutional interest.

The likely denial will carry no explanation. The record permits the Court to regard the appellate decision as a fact-dependent materiality determination rather than an express repudiation of its Brady precedents. Denial would leave the judgment below standing without endorsing that court's assessment of the withheld evidence.

**If review is granted**, the Court will focus on the petition's single question: whether the courts evaluated the withheld evidence cumulatively for its effect on confidence in the verdict, rather than merely asking whether the trial evidence could sustain conviction. It will not use this case to replace the settled Brady materiality rule with a new rule. Conditional on a grant, **80%** of the probability is on disposal in the cert order rather than full briefing and argument. Within that route, summary reversal and remand to apply the proper standard is more plausible than a GVR keyed to an unidentified intervening decision. An order itself correcting the constitutional error would be a summary reversal, not merely a GVR for reconsideration in light of another case.

**If review is denied**, the probability of a noted dissent or statement respecting denial is **18%**; an ordinary, unwritten denial is the forecast. If a separate writing appears, Sotomayor is the leading author candidate and Jackson the next, consistent with the independent author probabilities in `prediction.json`. This forecasts writing, not a disclosed vote tally. The unconditional probability of any grant is **14%**.
