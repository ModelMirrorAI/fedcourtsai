# Forecast of the Court's action

The Court will deny the petition. The response request will produce further examination of the alleged suppression-of-evidence error, but the petition will not secure plenary review. A denial will not endorse the lower courts' analysis or resolve whether the withheld evidence should have changed the result.

The baseline shows **one distribution and no true relist**. Expect one additional distribution after the requested response arrives or its time expires, then denial without a genuine post-conference relist. That ordinary redistribution is the most likely increment; a more extended path involving one or two genuine relists is secondary. The September 29 response request should not be treated as a completed review at the originally scheduled October 9 conference.

The Court will not invite the Solicitor General's views. This petition concerns a state conviction and the application of an established disclosure rule; no distinctive federal executive interest calls for that additional step.

The principal obstacle is the nature of the decision below. The appellate majority recites cumulative materiality and confidence in the verdict, then treats the withheld material as weak in light of other eyewitness identifications and impeachment already known at trial. The petition therefore asks the Court to look behind that recitation and correct the application of settled law to a disputed record, rather than resolve a clean conflict over the legal test.

**If review is granted**, the Court will focus on the petition's single question: whether Louisiana improperly rejected Matthews's Brady claim by relying on trial-evidence sufficiency rather than the cumulative effect of suppressed evidence on confidence in the verdict. It will not need to announce a new disclosure obligation or decide actual innocence. A useful narrowed formulation would ask whether the state courts applied the required cumulative materiality inquiry to the three categories of withheld information.

Conditional on a grant, a cert-order correction is more likely than plenary briefing and argument: 75% for summary disposition. The leading intervention route is a short summary reversal applying existing precedent and remanding for proper consideration. A routine GVR tied to an intervening decision is not the principal forecast; the provisioned record identifies no controlling intervening decision or granted same-question companion.

The denial will most likely have no separate writing. Conditional on denial, there is a 22% chance of a noted dissent or statement respecting denial. If a Justice writes critically, Sotomayor is the leading individual candidate, with Jackson next. No majority opinion, author of a denial, or adjudicated merits holding is predicted.
