## Case-Specific Adjustments
Starting from the **5.15%** `baseline` anchor, I adjust upward slightly to **6.0%** (`0.06`) based on the balance of positive signals and strong doctrinal/structural headwinds:

### Upward Factors
1. **Call for Response + Experienced Supreme Court Counsel + Amicus Support:** The petition was filed by experienced Supreme Court practitioners at Hogan Lovells (led by Jo-Ann Tamila Sagar) and drew three supporting amicus briefs on September 21, 2026—from The Innocence Network, the Cato Institute, and exoneree Patrick O'Neal—before the Court called for a response on September 29, 2026.
2. **Lower-Court Dissents and Compelling Factual Equities:** Judge Sandra Cabrina Jenkins wrote a detailed dissent in the Louisiana Fourth Circuit Court of Appeal (App. 7a–12a) concluding that the suppressed police files undermined confidence in the 1993 verdict under *Kyles v. Whitley*, 514 U.S. 419 (1995), and *Wearry v. Cain*, 577 U.S. 385 (2016), and Justice Piper Griffin voted to grant and docket in the Louisiana Supreme Court (App. 2a). Petitioner has served over 33 years for a 1992 murder in which the sole physical evidence did not link him to the crime, one of the three trial eyewitnesses (Larry Moses) later executed a recantation affidavit before dying prior to the evidentiary hearing, and the lead investigator (Detective Kenneth Harris) suppressed eyewitness accounts in other Orleans Parish prosecutions.

### Downward / Limiting Factors
1. **Pure Fact-Bound Error Correction with No Split of Authority:** The single question presented—whether the Louisiana courts erred by denying Petitioner's *Brady* claim based on the sufficiency of the evidence at trial—asserts no conflict among federal courts of appeals or state high courts over the legal standard. It asks exclusively for fact-intensive error correction (primarily via summary reversal, Pet. 14, 34–35) of how state courts weighed a 1992 trial record.
2. **The Controlling Reasoned State-Court Decision Expressly Cited *Kyles* and *Bagley*, and *Klein v. Martin*, 607 U.S. 213 (Jan. 26, 2026), Forecloses Petitioner's Framing:**
   - Because the Louisiana Supreme Court denied review in a one-line order (App. 1a–2a), the last reasoned state-court judgment is the Louisiana Fourth Circuit's August 27, 2025 decision (App. 3a–7a). Unlike the brief state district court order (App. 13a–16a), the Fourth Circuit majority **expressly cited and applied** *Kyles v. Whitley* and *United States v. Bagley*, 473 U.S. 667 (1985), holding that the district court "carefully weighed the cumulative effect of the suppressed evidence and found it insufficient to undermine confidence in the verdict" (App. 4a).
   - Just eight months before this CFR, the U.S. Supreme Court issued an 8–1 per curiam summary reversal in *Klein v. Martin*, 607 U.S. 213 (Jan. 26, 2026) (which Petitioner attempts to distinguish at Pet. 29). In *Klein*, the Court rejected the Fourth Circuit's conclusion that a state appellate court had covertly applied a forbidden *Kyles* sufficiency-of-the-evidence test merely because it emphasized the strength of the remaining evidence linking the defendant to the crime. Reaffirming *Strickler v. Greene*, 527 U.S. 263, 292–294 (1999), the Court held in *Klein* that when a state court states the governing *Brady* materiality rule and finds on the whole record that strong independent evidence supports the conviction such that there is no reasonable probability of a different result, its decision is legally sound. Given *Klein*, a majority of the current Court is highly unlikely to fault the Louisiana Fourth Circuit's opinion here.
3. **Track Record in Nearly Identical Louisiana *Brady* Petitions (*Skinner v. Louisiana*, No. 25-1, and *Brown v. Louisiana*, No. 22-77):**
   -Corpus and precedent retrieval reveals two closely analogous recent petitions from Louisiana raising the exact same *Kyles* materiality-versus-sufficiency claim:
     - *Brown v. Louisiana*, No. 22-77 (`scotus/67123792`), a capital *Brady* petition from the Louisiana Supreme Court, was **denied** on April 3, 2023 after 7 distributions, over a dissent by Justice Jackson joined by Justices Sotomayor and Kagan.
     - *Skinner v. Louisiana*, No. 25-1 (`scotus/73110505`), a non-capital post-conviction *Brady* petition from Louisiana supported by virtually the **identical amicus coalition** (Innocence Network, Cato Institute, and Patrick O'Neal), was **denied** on March 30, 2026 after 9 distributions, with Justices Sotomayor, Kagan, and Jackson noting dissent.
   - This pattern strongly suggests that the CFR in *Matthews* was requested by one of the three Justices who dissented in *Brown* and *Skinner* (Sotomayor, Kagan, or Jackson) to examine the State's response and potentially author or join a dissent from the denial of certiorari—not that five Justices are prepared to summarily reverse (as the Court once did a decade ago in *Wearry v. Cain*, 577 U.S. 385 (2016), and *Smith v. Cain*, 565 U.S. 73 (2012)) or that four Justices will grant plenary review.
4. **Evidentiary Complexities Highlighted by the Fourth Circuit Majority:** As the Fourth Circuit majority noted (App. 4a–6a), neither of the two undisclosed witnesses (Natha Rubin and Leroy Milton) actually witnessed the shooting or saw anyone holding a weapon; both told police they did not see the face of the person they observed near the scene; and their physical descriptions conflicted with each other (a young, heavy-set man vs. a 40-year-old, slim man). Meanwhile, three witnesses at trial (Dominique Gibson, Vanessa Henry, and Larry Moses) identified Matthews as the shooter, including Gibson, who knew Matthews from the neighborhood and was standing on the street in close proximity to the shooting. Even if the withheld police notes further impeached 10-year-old Vanessa Henry's vantage point, they did not directly impeach Gibson's identification.
