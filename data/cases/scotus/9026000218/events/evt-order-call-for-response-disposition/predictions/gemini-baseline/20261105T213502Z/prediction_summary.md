The Supreme Court is very likely to deny Juan Matthews's petition for review, with only about a 1 in 17 chance (6%) of taking the case. Matthews argues that New Orleans prosecutors violated his constitutional right to a fair trial in 1992 by withholding police notes that could have cast doubt on eyewitnesses who identified him as the shooter. Although the Court asked Louisiana to respond to the petition after four outside legal groups filed briefs supporting Matthews, the Court rarely steps in to re-weigh evidence in an individual criminal case when the state appeals court already cited the correct legal rules and three eyewitnesses identified the defendant at trial.
