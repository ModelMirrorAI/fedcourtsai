## Disposition and Summary-Disposition Route
- **Predicted Disposition (`denied`, P(any grant) = `0.06`):** The Court will **deny** the petition for a writ of certiorari. Although the Court called for a response after Louisiana waived, the petition seeks fact-bound error correction of a state post-conviction materiality ruling with no split of authority, and the last reasoned state-court decision (the Louisiana Fourth Circuit Court of Appeal) expressly cited and applied *Kyles v. Whitley*, 514 U.S. 419 (1995), and *United States v. Bagley*, 473 U.S. 667 (1985), finding that the cumulative effect of the suppressed police materials did not undermine confidence in the verdict given the remaining eyewitness identifications. Following the Court's recent per curiam decision in *Klein v. Martin*, 607 U.S. 213 (2026), and its denial of certiorari in *Skinner v. Louisiana*, No. 25-1 (Mar. 30, 2026), there are not four votes for plenary review or five votes for summary reversal.

Cain*, 577 U.S. 385 (2016), or a grant, vacate, and remand (GVR)—than to set this fact-bound state post-conviction record for full merits briefing and oral argument (`0.25`).
- **Question Presented and Conditional Merits Ground:** If the Court were to grant review, it would take the petition's sole Question Presented as written—whether the Louisiana courts erred by denying Petitioner's *Brady* claim based on the sufficiency of the evidence at trial—and, if deciding the merits, would likely issue a narrow, fact-specific per curiam opinion holding that the state courts misapplied *Kyles v.
