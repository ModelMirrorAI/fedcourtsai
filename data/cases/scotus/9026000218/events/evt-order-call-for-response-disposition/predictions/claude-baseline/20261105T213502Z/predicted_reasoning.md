# Forecast — Matthews v. Louisiana, No. 26-218

- **State of the docket:** distributed once (for the 10/9/2026 conference). A response was requested on 9/29/2026 and is due 10/29/2026, so it is not yet filed. There is no CVSG.
- **Relists:** the petition will be redistributed once Louisiana's response and any reply are in, most likely for a conference in late November or December 2026; this is nearly certain. I expect it to be decided at that first post-response conference or after one relist. A run of two or more relists would signal a summary reversal or a dissent from denial being written.
- **CVSG:** none. No federal interest is at stake in a state-court Brady case.
- **Disposition:** denial is the most likely outcome (about 83%). If the Court acts for the petitioner, I put even odds on a cert-order disposition (a per curiam summary reversal in the mould of Wearry v. Cain, or a GVR instructing the Louisiana courts to apply Kyles materiality) versus plenary review.
- **Question taken, if granted:** the QP as written, narrowed to whether the Louisiana court applied a sufficiency-of-the-evidence test rather than Kyles's "undermines confidence" standard. The broader claim that Louisiana courts err systemically would not be adopted as such.
- **Dissent from denial:** if the petition is denied, about a 30% chance of a separate writing, most likely from Justice Sotomayor, possibly joined or accompanied by Justice Jackson, criticizing the sufficiency framing. If the Court instead summarily reverses, a dissent from Justice Thomas or Justice Alito is plausible.
