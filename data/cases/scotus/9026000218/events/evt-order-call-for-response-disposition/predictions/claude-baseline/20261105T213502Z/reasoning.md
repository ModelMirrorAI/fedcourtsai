- `questions-presented.txt`, `petition.txt` (read in full for the statement and the reasons) and the appendix listing. The QP: whether the Louisiana courts erred by denying a Brady claim on the basis of the sufficiency of the trial evidence, contrary to Kyles and Strickler. The petition expressly asks for summary reversal and points to Smith v. Cain (2012) and Wearry v. Cain (2016, a per curiam summary reversal) as earlier corrections of Louisiana courts. It also cites Klein v. Martin, 607 U.S. 213 (2026), which I take from the petition alone to be a recent Brady per curiam. The facts as the petition tells them: a 1992 one-day trial resting on three inconsistent eyewitnesses (two of them children brought forward by the victim's mother), and no physical evidence. The suppressed material: canvass notes recording a witness who described a roughly 40-year-old man fleeing; notes suggesting the central child witness did not live at the address she testified from; and a non-identification by an independent eyewitness at a photo lineup. The trial court called the non-disclosure "undisputed".

## Adjustments (my judgement; no committed table conditions on a called-for response)
- **Response requested (up).** It shows that at least one Justice, or a clerk's memo, found the petition worth answering. Most CFR petitions are still denied, but in the paid pool their grant rate runs well above the baseline band. I'd put a generic CFR paid petition around 8–12% for the grant family.
- **Amici (up).** Four cert-stage amici put the case in the statpack's `2+` bucket (n=1543; denied 76.1%, granted 18.2%, GVR 4.5%). That cut is marginal and overlaps with the CFR signal, so I don't stack it fully.
- **Summary-reversal profile (up).** The case is error correction against a state court with a documented record of being corrected on this exact point (Smith, Wearry). The petitioner has elite counsel (Hogan Lovells with SCHR), the State did not defend the judgment at first, and the Court then asked it to. That is the classic path to a per curiam reversal or a GVR. The recent Brady per curiam the petition cites suggests the Court is still willing to write on Brady materiality summarily, in either direction.
- **Down.** The case is fact-bound and non-capital, it arises on successive state post-conviction review of a 1992 conviction, and a prior cert petition (20-7311) was denied in 2021. The "Louisiana courts routinely apply a sufficiency test" framing is weak as a split, and the decision below is an unreported intermediate-court writ ruling. Louisiana's response may raise procedural bars (timeliness or repetitiveness under Louisiana post-conviction law) that cloud the vehicle. The current Court's majority rarely summarily reverses in a criminal defendant's favour, though Wearry, Andrew v. White and Glossip show it can.

The net is roughly three times the anchor: 0.17. I call it a denial, because the most likely single outcome is still a denial after a relist or two. Conditional on a grant, I put about 0.5 on a cert-order disposition (summary reversal or GVR) against plenary review, because the petition asks for summary reversal and the facts fit Wearry's pattern.
