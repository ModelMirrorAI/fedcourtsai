## In brief

Juan Matthews, convicted of a 1992 murder, asks the Court to review Louisiana's denial of his claim that prosecutors wrongly withheld favorable evidence from his defense.

## What happened

Matthews was convicted of second-degree murder in 1992 based on eyewitness testimony, with no physical or forensic evidence tying him to the crime. Decades later, he obtained previously undisclosed police files revealing that other witnesses gave accounts conflicting with trial testimony, including one witness who viewed a photo lineup containing Matthews's picture but did not identify him. Matthews sought post-conviction relief in Louisiana courts, arguing this suppressed evidence should void his conviction. The trial court and intermediate appeals court found the state had indeed failed to disclose evidence but ruled it was not significant enough to matter, given the trial evidence supporting guilt. The Louisiana Supreme Court denied further review, with one justice noting she would have taken up the case.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked whether Louisiana courts used the wrong legal standard when evaluating claims that prosecutors withheld favorable evidence. The petition argues that courts should ask whether undisclosed evidence undermines confidence in the trial's outcome, not whether the evidence presented at trial was independently enough to support a conviction. Matthews contends Louisiana courts have repeatedly applied the latter, incorrect approach.

## Where it stands

The case was distributed for the Court's October 9, 2026 conference. Louisiana waived its right to respond, but the Court then requested a response; Louisiana has since obtained two extensions of time, most recently to a date after the snapshot. The docket shows no decision yet on whether the Court will hear the case.

## What each outcome would mean

Matthews is asking the Court to take up his case and overturn the Louisiana courts' rulings against him. If the Court grants review, it would hear full arguments on whether Louisiana applied the wrong standard, with no outcome yet decided. If the Court denies review, the Louisiana courts' decisions upholding Matthews's conviction and denying him relief would stand, leaving his conviction and sentence in place.
