## In brief

Juan Matthews, convicted of a 1992 murder, asks the Supreme Court to overturn Louisiana courts' rejection of his claim that prosecutors withheld evidence favorable to his defense.

## What happened

Matthews was convicted of second-degree murder in 1992 based largely on eyewitness testimony, with no physical or forensic evidence connecting him to the crime. Decades later, he obtained previously undisclosed police records, including witness statements that did not identify him as the shooter and notes questioning one eyewitness's account. He sought post-conviction relief, arguing the withheld evidence undermined confidence in his conviction. A Louisiana trial court denied relief, finding the evidence was not significant enough to matter. The Louisiana Court of Appeal affirmed, with one judge dissenting. The Louisiana Supreme Court declined to review the case, over one justice's dissent.

## What the Court is being asked

The question is whether the Louisiana courts used the wrong legal test in rejecting Matthews's claim that prosecutors improperly withheld favorable evidence. Matthews argues the courts should have asked whether the withheld evidence was significant enough to undermine confidence in the verdict, but instead asked only whether the evidence presented at trial was enough, on its own, to support his conviction.

## Where it stands

The petition has been fully briefed, Louisiana has waived its right to respond, and the Court granted a later motion extending the time for any response to November 30, 2026. The petition has not yet been distributed for the Court's consideration of whether to hear the case.

## What each outcome would mean

Matthews is asking the Court to take up his case and overturn the rulings against him. If the Court grants the petition, it would hear the case in full and decide whether Louisiana courts applied the correct legal standard; a grant alone would not decide who wins. If the Court denies the petition, the lower courts' rejection of Matthews's claim stands, leaving his conviction in place.
