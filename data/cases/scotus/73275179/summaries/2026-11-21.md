## In brief

Gun owners asked the Supreme Court to strike down Cook County's ban on AR-15-style rifles; the Court granted review, consolidated the case with a similar Connecticut challenge, and heard argument.

## What happened

Cutberto Viramontes and other Illinois residents sued Cook County, arguing its ban on "assault weapons," including AR-15-style rifles, violates the Second Amendment. The district court ruled for the county, and the Seventh Circuit affirmed, relying on its earlier decision in a related case upholding similar bans. The petitioners argued the lower courts misapplied Supreme Court precedent on which weapons receive constitutional protection, and that the Seventh Circuit wrongly faulted them for not building a fuller evidentiary record. Cook County countered that no circuit conflict existed warranting review, that the case was a poor vehicle because petitioners had forfeited arguments below, and that its ordinance was consistent with historical traditions of regulating dangerous weapons.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked to decide whether the Constitution protects the right to possess AR-15-style and similar semiautomatic rifles, and specifically whether a ban on such rifles infringes that right. This requires deciding what counts as a constitutionally protected "arm," and whether a weapon's popularity among the public, its potential military use, or its dangerousness determines whether a government may ban it outright.

## Where it stands

The Court granted certiorari, consolidated this case with a related challenge to Connecticut's rifle ban, and heard oral argument in December 2026. The case was fully briefed, including an amicus brief from the federal government supporting the challengers, and awaits decision. A petitioner has separately asked to be dismissed from the case, and the parties have disputed whether intervening events made the case moot, with that dispute still before the Court.

## What each outcome would mean

Viramontes and the other gun owners are asking the Court to overturn the lower court's ruling that upheld Cook County's ban. If the Court reverses, it would hold that the Second Amendment protects AR-15-style rifles, likely striking down Cook County's ban and similar bans elsewhere. If the Court affirms, the ban would stand, leaving Cook County free to prohibit these rifles. If the Court vacates, it would send the case back without resolving whether such bans are constitutional.
