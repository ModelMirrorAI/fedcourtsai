## In brief

Viramontes and other gun owners asked the Supreme Court to strike down Cook County's ban on AR-15-style rifles under the Second Amendment, and the Court agreed to decide the question.

## What happened

Cook County bans the sale and possession of certain semiautomatic rifles, including AR-15s, defining them as "assault weapons." Cutberto Viramontes and other challengers sued, arguing the ban violates their right to keep and bear arms. A federal district court ruled for the County, and the Seventh Circuit affirmed, relying on its earlier decision in a related case that such rifles are not constitutionally protected and that, even if they were, bans on them are consistent with history. The challengers appealed to the Supreme Court, which also agreed to review a similar case from Connecticut and combined it with this one.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked whether the Constitution protects the right to possess AR-15-style semiautomatic rifles, or whether governments may ban them entirely. The case turns on whether such rifles count as "arms" protected by the Second Amendment at all, and if so, whether a history of regulating particularly dangerous or unusual weapons allows them to be banned despite their widespread ownership.

## Where it stands

The Court granted review and consolidated this case with a related Connecticut case for one hour of argument. The case has been fully briefed, including a brief from the United States supporting the challengers, and argument was set for December 2, 2026. The case now awaits that argument and a decision.

## What each outcome would mean

Viramontes and the other challengers are asking the Court to overturn the Seventh Circuit's ruling, which favored Cook County. If the Court reverses, it would hold that AR-15-style rifles are constitutionally protected and that Cook County's ban cannot stand, though the case could be sent back to lower courts to apply that ruling. If the Court affirms, Cook County's ban would stand and such rifles could continue to be prohibited. If the Court vacates the decision below, it would set that ruling aside and send the case back without deciding the question for either side.
