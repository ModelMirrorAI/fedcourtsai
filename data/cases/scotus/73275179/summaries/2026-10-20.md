## In brief

Gun owners challenging Cook County's ban on AR-15 style rifles have had their case taken up by the Supreme Court, which heard arguments on the ban's constitutionality.

## What happened

Cutberto Viramontes and other gun owners sued Cook County, Illinois, arguing that its ban on AR-15 platform and similar semiautomatic rifles violates the Second Amendment. Lower courts ruled against them: the district court granted summary judgment to the County, and the Seventh Circuit affirmed, relying on its earlier decision in Bevis v. City of Naperville, which held that such rifles are more akin to military weapons than arms protected for self-defense, and that Viramontes had failed to build a factual record disputing that conclusion. Viramontes appealed to the Supreme Court, arguing the lower courts misapplied the Court's precedents on which weapons the Second Amendment protects.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked whether the Second Amendment protects the right to possess AR-15 platform and similar semiautomatic rifles, including whether a ban on such rifles is consistent with the historical tradition of firearms regulation, and whether these rifles count as "arms" covered by the amendment's text in the first place.

## Where it stands

The Court granted cert, consolidating this case with a related case from the Second Circuit (Grant v. Higgins) for one hour of oral argument. Briefing proceeded through the summer, numerous amicus briefs were filed including one from the Solicitor General supporting the petitioners, and the Court set the case for oral argument on December 2, 2026. The docket shows the case has been circulated and the Seventh Circuit's record was received, but no decision has yet been recorded.

## What each outcome would mean

Viramontes and the other gun owners are asking the Court to overturn the Seventh Circuit's ruling that upheld Cook County's ban. If the Court reverses, it would hold that AR-15 platform and similar semiautomatic rifles are protected by the Second Amendment, likely striking down the County's ban and allowing Petitioners to acquire such rifles. If the Court affirms, Cook County's ban would stand, and the rifles would remain illegal to possess there. The Court could also vacate the decision and send the case back for further proceedings without resolving the question for either side.
