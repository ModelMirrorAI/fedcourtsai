## In brief

Gun owners asked the Court to decide whether the Second Amendment protects a right to possess AR-15 platform and similar semiautomatic rifles.

## What happened

Cutberto Viramontes and other plaintiffs sued Cook County, Illinois, challenging its ordinance banning so-called "assault weapons," including AR-15 and AK-platform rifles, as a violation of the Second Amendment. Lower courts ruled against the plaintiffs based on circuit precedent holding that such rifles fall outside, or are not protected by, the Second Amendment. The Seventh Circuit affirmed, saying the plaintiffs had failed to build a factual record showing their case differed from that precedent. The petition asks the Supreme Court to review that ruling.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked whether the Constitution protects a right for ordinary citizens to own AR-15 platform and similar semiautomatic rifles, or whether governments may ban them as weapons that are too dangerous, too military in character, or otherwise outside the kinds of arms the Second Amendment covers.

## Where it stands

The Court granted the petition, consolidating it with a related case from the Second Circuit, and the cases were briefed and argued on the merits; the case now awaits a decision.

## What each outcome would mean

Viramontes and the other individual gun owners and advocacy groups asked the Court to take up this case. If the Court rules for Viramontes, it would hold that AR-15 platform and similar semiautomatic rifles are constitutionally protected, meaning Cook County's ban (and similar bans elsewhere) could not stand as applied to those weapons. If the Court rules for Cook County, the ban would be upheld, and such rifles could continue to be prohibited there. The Court could also send the case back to the lower courts for further proceedings without deciding the question for either side.
