## In brief

Gun owners and gun-rights groups challenged Cook County's ban on AR-15-style rifles, and the Supreme Court granted review, consolidated the case with a similar Connecticut challenge, and heard argument.

## What happened

Cutberto Viramontes and other individuals and gun-rights organizations sued Cook County, arguing its ban on "assault weapons," including AR-15-style rifles, violates the Second Amendment. The county countered that these weapons are dangerous, military-style firearms not suited for ordinary self-defense and that every circuit to consider the question has upheld similar bans. The district court and the Seventh Circuit, relying on circuit precedent, ruled for the county, holding that Viramontes had not built a record showing the rifles were constitutionally protected "arms." Viramontes asked the Supreme Court to take the case and overturn that ruling.

## What the Court is being asked

The question is whether the Constitution's guarantee of a right to keep and bear arms protects the right to own AR-15-style and similar semiautomatic rifles, or whether governments may ban them as unusually dangerous weapons not commonly used for lawful self-defense.

## Where it stands

The Court granted certiorari, consolidated the case with a related challenge to a Connecticut ban (Grant v. Higgins), and the case was briefed and argued on December 2, 2026. After full briefing, one of the original petitioners asked to be dismissed from the case and the county respondents suggested the case might be moot; the petitioners have disputed that suggestion. The case awaits decision.

## What each outcome would mean

Viramontes and the other gun owners and groups are asking the Court to strike down the county's ban. If the Court rules for Viramontes, Cook County's ban on AR-15-style rifles would be struck down, and similar bans elsewhere would be called into serious doubt. If the Court rules for Cook County, the ban would stand, leaving the county free to prohibit these rifles. The Court could also send the case back without resolving the merits if it finds the dispute moot or otherwise unsuitable for decision at this stage.
