## What happened

Cutberto Viramontes and other gun owners sued Cook County, Illinois, arguing that its ban on so-called "assault weapons," including AR-15 platform rifles, violates the Second Amendment. Existing appeals-court precedent already rejected similar challenges to this ordinance, but the plaintiffs hoped to have that precedent overturned. A federal district court allowed discovery, and while the case was pending, the Seventh Circuit decided a related case, Bevis, upholding similar bans against a preliminary challenge. The district court then ruled for the County, and the Seventh Circuit affirmed, saying the plaintiffs failed to build a factual record strong enough to distinguish their case from Bevis. The plaintiffs then asked the Supreme Court to take up their case.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked whether the Constitution protects the right to own AR-15-style and similar semiautomatic rifles, or whether governments may ban them the way Cook County has. The dispute centers on how to decide which weapons count as constitutionally protected "arms" and whether such rifles are common enough, and suited to lawful self-defense, to fall under that protection—or instead resemble military weapons that history has allowed governments to restrict.
