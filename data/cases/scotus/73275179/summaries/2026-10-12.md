## In brief

The Supreme Court heard a challenge to Cook County's ban on AR-15-style rifles, and a companion case involving Connecticut's similar ban.

## What happened

Cutberto Viramontes and other Illinois residents sued Cook County, arguing its ban on AR-15-style rifles and similar semiautomatic weapons violates the Second Amendment. The district court ruled for the county, citing Seventh Circuit precedent (Bevis v. City of Naperville) holding such bans constitutional. Viramontes argued he had not had a fair chance to build an evidentiary record under that precedent. The Seventh Circuit affirmed, saying Viramontes failed to develop a factual record sufficient to challenge that precedent. Viramontes petitioned for Supreme Court review, and the Court later consolidated this case with a similar challenge to Connecticut's ban, Grant v. Higgins.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked whether the right to keep and bear arms protects people's ability to possess AR-15-style rifles and similar semiautomatic weapons, or whether governments may ban them as unusually dangerous or as weapons suited mainly for military use.

## Where it stands

The Court granted certiorari, consolidated this case with Grant v. Higgins, and set it for argument. The case was fully briefed, with merits briefs, numerous amicus briefs, and participation by the Solicitor General, and argument was scheduled for December 2026.

## What each outcome would mean

Viramontes and the other challengers asked the Court to strike down the county's ban. If the Court rules for Viramontes, it would hold that AR-15-style rifles are constitutionally protected arms that Cook County (and Connecticut, in the companion case) cannot ban, reversing the lower courts and likely sending the cases back for entry of judgment favoring the challengers. If the Court rules for Cook County, the ban would stand, upholding the lower courts' conclusion that such bans are consistent with the right to keep and bear arms. The Court could also send the case back without deciding the question either way.
