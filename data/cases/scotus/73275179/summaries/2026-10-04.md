## In brief

Gun owners ask the Court to decide whether Cook County's ban on AR-15-style rifles violates the Second Amendment, after lower courts rejected their challenge.

## What happened

Cutberto Viramontes and other gun owners sued Cook County, Illinois, arguing its ban on AR-15-style rifles and similar firearms violates the Second Amendment. The district court ruled for the county, relying on earlier circuit precedent upholding the same ordinance. The Seventh Circuit affirmed, holding that Viramontes failed to build a factual record sufficient to challenge that precedent. Viramontes petitioned for Supreme Court review, arguing lower courts need guidance on which weapons the Second Amendment protects. Cook County opposes review, arguing there is no conflict among appeals courts, since every circuit to consider the question has upheld similar bans, and that Viramontes's case is a poor vehicle because he did not properly develop evidence below.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked whether the Constitution protects a right to possess AR-15-style rifles and similar semiautomatic firearms, meaning such weapons could not be banned the way Cook County's ordinance bans them.

## Where it stands

The Court granted review, consolidating this case with a related one, and heard oral argument on December 2, 2026. The case awaits decision.

## What each outcome would mean

Viramontes and the other gun owners asked the Court to take the case. If the Court rules for Viramontes, Cook County's ban on AR-15-style rifles would be struck down, and similar bans elsewhere could also be vulnerable. If the Court rules for Cook County, the ordinance would stand, upholding the county's authority to prohibit these weapons. The Court could also send the case back to the lower courts without resolving the constitutional question, for example if it finds the evidentiary record inadequate to decide the issue.
