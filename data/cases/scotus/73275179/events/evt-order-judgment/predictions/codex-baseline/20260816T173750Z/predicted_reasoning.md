# Forecast

The Court will reverse the Seventh Circuit, most likely 6–3, with the Chief Justice and Justices Thomas, Alito, Gorsuch, Kavanaugh, and Barrett in the majority and Justices Sotomayor, Kagan, and Jackson in dissent. A vacatur and remand for application of a corrected test is the principal alternative, but the judgment below will still be disturbed.

The majority will answer the granted question by holding that AR-15-platform and materially similar semiautomatic rifles are “Arms” within the Second Amendment's plain text. It will reject the Seventh Circuit's treatment of semiautomatic civilian rifles as effectively equivalent to fully automatic M16s and hold that widespread lawful possession prevents classifying these rifles as both dangerous and unusual. Because Cook County's law is a categorical possession ban, historical rules addressing misuse, public terror, gunpowder storage, or disproportionate defensive force will not supply a sufficiently analogous tradition.

The holding will extend beyond this ordinance to categorical bans on AR-15-platform and materially similar semiautomatic rifles nationwide, while reserving questions about other weapon classes, magazine limits, and regulations short of a ban. The dissent will emphasize military similarity, mass-shooting lethality, the absence of a developed trial record on lawful defensive use, and a historical tradition of limiting unusually dangerous weapons.

A dismissal as improvidently granted or an equally divided affirmance is not expected. The consolidated grant supplies a vehicle for resolving the broad question rather than leaving the Seventh Circuit's framework intact.
