# Merits Judgment Prediction: *Cutberto Viramontes, et al. v. Cook County, Illinois, et al.* (No. 25-238, consolidated with *Grant v. Higgins*, No. 25-566)

## I. Executive Summary

- **Event:** `evt-brief-judgment` (Merits stage, moment: `briefed`, target: `judgment`)
- **Predicted Outcome:** Favorable to Petitioners (`granted: true`, `probability: 0.88`)
- **Most Likely Disposition:** `reversed` (`0.62`), followed by `vacated` (`0.26`), `affirmed` (`0.11`), and `dismissed` (`0.01`)
- **Predicted Vote Split:** **6–3** (Roberts, C.J., and Thomas, Alito, Gorsuch, Kavanaugh, and Barrett, JJ., in the majority; Sotomayor, Kagan, and Jackson, JJ., dissenting)
- **Predicted Majority Opinion Author:** Justice Kavanaugh

After holding the petition in *Viramontes v. Cook County* (No. 25-238, 7th Cir.) across 22 conferences—beginning in December 2025 while the Court considered and decided its OT2025 Second Amendment cases, *United States v. Hemani* (June 18, 2026) and *Wolford v. Lopez* (June 25, 2026)—the Supreme Court granted certiorari on June 30, 2026, and consolidated *Viramontes* with *Grant v. Higgins* (No. 25-566, 2d Cir.). Both cases present the identical, unadorned Question Presented: *"Whether the Second and Fourteenth Amendments guarantee the right to possess AR-15 platform and similar semiautomatic rifles."*

With both sides' merits briefs and the amicus brief of the United States now on the record, the doctrinal, empirical, and institutional signals point overwhelmingly toward a **6–3 decision invalidating categorical bans on AR-15s and functionally equivalent semiautomatic rifles**.

---

## II. Procedural Posture and Strategic Vehicle Selection

Understanding how these consolidated cases reached the merits stage explains both why the Court granted review and how it is likely to fashion its mandate:

1. **The Seventh Circuit Posture (*Viramontes*, No. 25-238):** Petitioners Cutberto Viramontes, Christopher Khaya, the Second Amendment Foundation, and the Firearms Policy Coalition challenged Cook County's Blair Holt Assault Weapons Ban (Cook County, Ill. Code §§ 54-211 to 54-212), which bans the possession, acquisition, or transfer of semiautomatic rifles with specified features (such as a pistol grip, adjustable stock, barrel shroud, or flash suppressor) as well as over 125 named models, including all AR-15s. Following summary judgment for the County, a Seventh Circuit panel (Sykes, C.J., Brennan & St. Eve, JJ.) affirmed on June 2, 2025. The panel held that its prior preliminary-injunction decision in *Bevis v. City of Naperville*, 85 F.4th 1175 (7th Cir. 2023)—which concluded that AR-15s are not protected "Arms" under the Second Amendment's plain text because they are "indistinguishable" from military M16 machineguns—controlled, and faulted Petitioners for not hiring expert witnesses to build a factual record under the *Bevis* standard.
2. **The Second Circuit Posture (*Grant*, No. 25-566):** Petitioners Eddie Grant, Jr., et al., challenged Connecticut's statutory ban (Conn. Gen. Stat. §§ 53-202a to 53-202c) on possessing semiautomatic centerfire rifles with similar common features (pistol grip, adjustable stock, flash suppressor, or forward pistol grip) and named models including AR-15s. On August 22, 2025, the Second Circuit (*Nat'l Ass'n for Gun Rights v. Lamont; Grant v. Rovella*, Nos. 23-1162, 23-1344) affirmed the denial of a preliminary injunction, assuming Step One coverage *arguendo* and upholding the ban at Step Two on the theory that AR-15s are "unusually dangerous" and analogous to historical restrictions on bowie knives, percussion-cap pistols, and slungshots.
3. **Why the Court Consolidated Both Cases:** At the cert stage, Cook County leaned heavily on the argument that *Viramontes* was a poor vehicle because the Seventh Circuit rested on Petitioners' purported failure to compile an expert discovery record in the district court, whereas *Viramontes* was in a final summary-judgment posture (remediable after *Harrel v. Raoul*, 144 S. Ct. 2491 (2024), where Justice Thomas noted the interlocutory posture of *Bevis*/*Harrel*). Meanwhile, *Grant* had a fully developed preliminary-injunction record and a clean Step Two holding from the Second Circuit, without any procedural waiver dispute. By consolidating *Viramontes* (final judgment from CA7) and *Grant* (preliminary injunction from CA2), the Court eliminated any vehicle obstacle and positioned itself to issue a definitive ruling on the substantive constitutional question.

## III. Doctrinal Analysis After *Wolford v. Lopez* and *United States v. Hemani*

### 1. Step One ("Plain Text") and Respondents' Critical Concession

Just four months before merits briefing closed in this case, the Court decided *Wolford v. Lopez*, No. 24-1046 (June 25, 2026), by a 6–3 vote (Alito, J., joined by Roberts, C.J., and Thomas, Gorsuch, Kavanaugh, and Barrett, JJ.). *Wolford* synthesized the *Bruen* Step One inquiry into three strict subsidiary questions:
1. Does the law apply to "the people" (all members of the political community)?
2. Does it concern any form of "Arms" ("any weapon customarily used for offensive or defensive purposes," *Heller*, 554 U.S. at 581, 584)?
3. Does the law place any restrictions on either "keep[ing]" (possession) or "bear[ing]" (carrying) of arms?

Crucially, Justice Barrett's concurrence in *Wolford* (joined in relevant part by Thomas and Gorsuch, JJ., and echoing the majority) underscored that courts may **not** "smuggle additional limits, drawn from our regulatory tradition, into the plain-text stage of the inquiry. The answer is and always has been no." (*Wolford*, slip op. at 2 n.1 (Barrett, J., concurring)).

Recognizing that the Seventh Circuit's *Bevis* holding—which excluded AR-15s from the definition of "Arms" at Step One by equating them with M16 machineguns—is indefensible under *Heller*, *Bruen*, and *Wolford*, **Respondents explicitly abandon the Seventh Circuit's Step One rationale in their merits brief**:
> *"Respondents agree that the restricted firearms are covered by the Second Amendment's text because they are bearable 'Arms.' The question becomes whether the laws are 'consistent with the principles that underpin our regulatory tradition.' Rahimi, 602 U.S. at 692."* (Resp. Br. 38).

This concession is fatal to the reasoning on which the Seventh Circuit decided *Bevis* and *Viramontes*. Because Respondents concede that Petitioners are among "the people" who seek to "keep and bear" bearable "Arms," Cook County's and Connecticut's bans are **presumptively unconstitutional**, and the burden rests entirely on Respondents at Step Two. That alone vitiates the Seventh Circuit's holding below that *Viramontes* failed to build an evidentiary record to carry a Step One plaintiff's burden.

### 2. Step Two: The *Heller*/*Bruen* "Common Use" Rule vs. Respondents' "Unusually Dangerous / Public Terror" Theory

The central merits dispute is how the historical tradition of weapon bans operates at *Bruen* Step Two:

- **Petitioners and the United States (as Amicus Curiae):** Rely directly on *District of Columbia v. Heller*, 554 U.S. 570, 624–27 (2008), *Caetano v. Massachusetts*, 577 U.S. 411, 417 (2016), and *New York State Rifle & Pistol Ass'n v. Bruen*, 597 U.S. 1, 21, 47 (2022). In *Heller* and *Bruen*, the Court already conducted the historical survey of weapons regulations—from the Statute of Northampton's prohibition on carrying "dangerous and unusual weapons" to terrorize the public, through 19th-century restrictions—and distilled the controlling constitutional principle: the Second Amendment protects weapons that are **"in common use at the time" for lawful purposes like self-defense**, and permits categorical bans only on weapons that are **"both dangerous and unusual"** (a conjunctive test, see *Caetano*, 577 U.S. at 417 (Alito, J., concurring)).
- **Empirical Reality of AR-15 Common Use:** Just last year, in *Smith & Wesson Brands, Inc. v. Estados Unidos Mexicanos*, 605 U.S. 280, 297 (2025), a **unanimous** Supreme Court (per Justice Kagan) stated as a matter of settled fact that AR-15s are **"both widely legal and bought by many ordinary consumers"** and that **"[t]he AR-15 is the most popular rifle in the country."** Over 20 to 30 million AR-15s and similar semiautomatic rifles are lawfully owned by millones of Americans; they account for roughly 20% of all retail firearm sales; and they are completely legal under federal law and the laws of 40 States. Unlike the automatic M16, an AR-15 fires only one round per trigger pull—the exact same firing mechanism as the semiautomatic handguns held protected in *Heller*, *McDonald*, *Bruen*, and *Wolford*, and long recognized by the Court in *Staples v. United States*, 511 U.S. 600, 612 (1994), as rifles that "traditionally have been widely accepted as lawful possessions."
- **Respondents' Step Two Theory:** Respondents argue (Resp. Br. 14–37) that our historical tradition permits legislatures to ban subsets of weapons that "pose unprecedented societal concerns, are disproportionately used in crime relative to their utility for self-defense, and precipitate public terror." They point to 19th-century laws regulating bowie knives, slungshots, brass knuckles, swordcanes, and pocket pistols, as well as Prohibition-era restrictions on Thompson submachine guns (machineguns).
- **Why Respondents' Analogues Fail Under *Bruen* and *Wolford*:**
  1. **How vs. Why Mismatch:** As both Petitioners (Pet. Br. 38–42) and the Solicitor General (U.S. Br. 24–28) demonstrate, almost all 19th-century bowie-knife and pistol statutes restricted **concealed carry** (or taxed sales), rather than imposing a **total ban on peaceable home possession** of a class of firearms. Look at *Bruen*, 597 U.S. at 50–55, and *Wolford*, slip op. at 19–24: the Court rigorously rejects historical analogues that regulated a narrower manner of carry when invoked to justify a sweeping ban. The few outlier state decisions or statutes that went further fail *Bruen*'s widespread-acceptance requirement (*Wolford*, slip op. at 7–8).
  2. **Disguised Interest-Balancing:** Respondents' three-part test—asking courts to weigh whether a rifle's "use in mass shootings and violent crime" outweighs its "utility or suitability for self-defense" and whether ergonomic features (like a pistol grip, adjustable stock, or barrel shroud that actually improve accuracy and safe handling) make a rifle "too dangerous"—is indistinguishable from the judge-empowering interest-balancing inquiry that *Heller*, *McDonald*, *Bruen*, and *Wolford* emphatically rejected.
  3. **Semiautomatic Rifles Are Not Machineguns:** Respondents' reliance on 20th-century machinegun restrictions (the 1934 National Firearms Act and 1920s state bans) fails because those laws drew a bright line between **automatic** weapons (which fire continuously with a single trigger pull and were never in common civilian use) and **semiautomatic** firearms (which have been commercially sold to millions of American civilians since the late 19th and early 20th centuries without prohibition until 1989).

## IV. Vote Count, Alignment, and Authorship

### 1. Vote Count and Justice-by-Justice Alignment (6–3)

Certain vote for Petitioners.
- **Justice Alito (`grant`):** Authored *McDonald*, *Wolford v. Lopez* (2026), and the *Caetano* concurrence explaining that "common use" is a conjunctive test and that weapons widely owned across the nation cannot be banned; joined Justice Thomas's dissent from denial of cert in *Snope*. Certain vote for Petitioners.
- **Justice Gorsuch (`grant`):** Authored *United States v. Hemani* (June 18, 2026, affirming an as-applied Second Amendment challenge to 18 U.S.C. § 922(g)(3)); joined *Bruen* and *Wolford*; and joined Justice Thomas's dissent in *Snope* concluding that AR-15s are clearly protected arms in common use. Certain vote for Petitioners.
- **Justice Kavanaugh (`grant`):** Authored the seminal dissent in *Heller v. District of Columbia* (*Heller II*), 670 F.3d 1244, 1269–96 (D.C. Cir. 2011), arguing at length that D.C.'s ban on semiautomatic rifles (AR-15s) is unconstitutional under *Heller* because semiautomatic rifles are in common use and functionally indistinguishable from semiautomatic handguns. In *Snope v. Brown*, 145 S. Ct. at 1534 (2025), Justice Kavanaugh wrote separately to state that **"under this Court's precedents, the Fourth Circuit's decision is questionable"** because **"the AR-15 is in common use by law-abiding citizens"** and "millions of Americans own AR-15s," predicting that the Court would address the issue "in the next Term or two." Certain vote for Petitioners.
- **Chief Justice Roberts (`grant`):** Joined *Heller*, *McDonald*, *Bruen*, and *Wolford* in full, and authored *Rahimi*. While the Chief Justice voted to uphold the domestic-violence disarmament statute in *Rahimi* where historical surety and going-armed laws disarmed adjudicated physical threats, he has never waveringly voted against categorical bans on law-abiding citizens keeping or carrying common firearms (*Heller*, *McDonald*, *Bruen*, *Wolford*). Moreover, the Chief Justice joined the unanimous opinion in *Smith & Wesson* recognizing the AR-15 as the most popular rifle in the country. Strong vote for Petitioners.
- **Justice Barrett (`grant`):** Joined *Bruen* and *Wolford* in full, and wrote a forceful concurrence in *Wolford* rejecting efforts to dilute Step One or uphold broad firearm restrictions based on generalized public safety concerns rather than specific historical principles. Even if she writes or joins a passage clarifying the doctrinal relationship between Step One ("Arms") and Step Two ("common use" vs. "dangerous and unusual"), both paths lead directly to invalidating categorical bans on America's most popular rifle. Strong vote for Petitioners.
- **Justices Sotomayor, Kagan, and Jackson (`deny`):** All three dissented in *Bruen* and *Wolford* (and dissented or concurred separately in *Hemani*). They are highly likely to dissent here, adopting Respondents' argument that legislatures have historical authority to respond to modern mass shootings by restricting military-derived semiautomatic rifles with combat-oriented features.

### 2. Authorship Prediction (`Kavanaugh`)

Justice Kavanaugh is the natural majority opinion author. Fifteen years ago on the D.C. Circuit in *Heller II*, he wrote the foundational judicial opinion analyzing why semiautomatic rifle bans fail *Heller*'s text-and-history and common-use tests—an opinion cited extensively by Petitioners and the Solicitor General. In *Snope* (2025), he publicly signaled that the Court would take up the issue within a Term or two. Chief Justice Roberts frequently assigns major constitutional opinions to Justice Kavanaugh when Kavanaugh can hold all six conservatives together behind a clear, administrable rule (preserving explicit assurances that automatic machineguns like the M-16 remain prohibitable). Justice Thomas, Justice Alito (who just authored *Wolford* in June 2026), or Chief Justice Roberts himself are secondary possibilities.

## V. Calibration of Probabilities and Disposition Choice (`reversed` vs. `vacated`)

### 1. Overall Probability of Favorable Outcome (`0.88`)

In the OT2025 statpack baseline, the Court reversed or vacated the judgment below in **72.3%** (34 of 47) of merits decisions overall, and in **66.7%** (2 of 3) of cases originating from the Seventh Circuit. Here, the case-specific indicators push the probability of a petitioner victory well above the 72.3% base rate to **0.88**:
- Four Justices (Thomas, Alito, Gorsuch, Kavanaugh) are already on record stating that AR-15 bans are unconstitutional or questionable under *Heller*/*Bruen*.
- All six conservative Justices just united in *Wolford v. Lopez* (June 2026) to reverse the Ninth Circuit in a Second Amendment challenge, and a majority also upheld the Second Amendment claim in *United States v. Hemani* (June 2026).
- The United States Solicitor General has filed a strong amicus brief supporting Petitioners and urging reversal.
- Respondents conceded Step One coverage in their merits brief, collapsing the Seventh Circuit's *Bevis* rationale on which the decision below rested.

A residual **12% probability** (`0.11` affirmed, `0.01` dismissed) accounts for the small chance that two conservative Justices (most plausibly Chief Justice Roberts and Justice Barrett) are persuaded by Respondents' emphasis on mass-shooting lethality or specific firearm features, or that a vehicle/standing issue arises.

### 2. Breakdown Between `reversed` (`0.62`) and `vacated` (`0.26`)

When the Supreme Court rules for petitioners in a Second Amendment case, it must choose between **reversing** the judgment below outright (`reversed and remanded`) or **vacating** the judgment below and remanding for further proceedings under the corrected legal standard (`vacated and remanded`).
- **Why `reversed` (`0.62`) is the primary prediction:**
  1. Both Petitioners' merits brief ("The judgments of the courts of appeals should be reversed," Pet. Br. 50) and the Solicitor General's amicus brief ("The judgments of the courts of appeals should be reversed," U.S. Br. 29) explicitly ask for **reversal**, not vacatur.
  2. In *Bruen*, *Rahimi*, and most recently *Wolford v. Lopez* (June 25, 2026), the Court's judgment line was **"reversed and remanded"**, not vacated.
  3. The Question Presented is a pure question of constitutional law: whether the Second and Fourteenth Amendments guarantee the right to possess AR-15 platform and similar semiautomatic rifles. Once the Court holds that AR-15s and functionally similar semiautomatic rifles are in common use and cannot be categorically banned, both Cook County's ordinance and Connecticut's statute are unconstitutional as applied to those rifles, leaving nothing for the Seventh or Second Circuits to re-adjudicate under a different test.
- **Why `vacated` (`0.26`) carries substantial secondary weight:**
  1. In *Viramontes*, the Seventh Circuit panel affirmed summary judgment on the ground that *Bevis* controlled and Petitioners had not developed a record under *Bevis*. Because Respondents have now abandoned *Bevis*'s Step One holding and the Court will repudiate *Bevis*'s "military-grade weaponry" test, there is a meaningful possibility (~26%) that the Court either vacates and remands *Viramontes* for application of the *Heller* common-use standard to the remaining 125 named firearms and feature combinations, or reverses in *Grant* while vacating in *Viramontes*. However, because the central holding will directly resolve the unconstitutionality of banning AR-15s—the core relief Petitioners sought in both courts—outright **reversal** (`0.62`) is more than twice as likely as vacatur (`0.26`).
