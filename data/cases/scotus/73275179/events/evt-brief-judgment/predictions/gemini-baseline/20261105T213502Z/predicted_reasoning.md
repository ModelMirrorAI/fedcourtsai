# Predicted Reasoning of the Court: *Cutberto Viramontes, et al. v. Cook County, Illinois, et al.* (No. 25-238, consolidated with No. 25-566)

## 1. Judgment and Vote Lineup

- **Judgment**: **Reversed** (the judgments of the Seventh Circuit in *Viramontes v. Cook County*, No. 25-238, and the Second Circuit in *Grant v. Higgins*, No. 25-566, are reversed and the cases are remanded for further proceedings consistent with the Court's opinion). No procedural exit (such as dismissal as improvidently granted or an equally divided Court) is likely: both cases arrive from final summary judgments with full standing and clean facial constitutional challenges.
- **Vote Split**: **6–3**.
  - **Majority (6)**: Chief Justice Roberts, Justice Thomas, Justice Alito, Justice Gorsuch, Justice Kavanaugh, and Justice Barrett.
  - **Dissent (3)**: Justice Sotomayor, Justice Kagan, and Justice Jackson.

## 2. Questions Reached and Doctrinal Ground (`majority-ground`)

The Court will reach the single Question Presented in full across the consolidated cases: whether the Second and Fourteenth Amendments guarantee the right to possess AR-15 platform and similar semiautomatic rifles.

1. **Step One — Plain Text Coverage ("Arms")**:
   Reaffirming *District of Columbia v. Heller*, 554 U.S. 570, 581–82 (2008), *New York State Rifle & Pistol Ass'n v. Bruen*, 597 U.S. 1, 24, 28 (2022), and *Wolford v. Lopez*, 609 U.S. 185, 146 S. Ct. 2032, 2043, 2049 (2026), the majority will hold that semiautomatic rifles—including the AR-15 and AK-47/Galil platforms challenged by Petitioners—are plainly bearable "Arms" within the meaning of the Second Amendment's text. The Court will reject the Seventh Circuit's holding in *Bevis v. City of Naperville*, 85 F.4th 1175 (7th Cir. 2023), and below that plaintiffs bear a threshold step-one burden to prove that a bearable firearm is not "exclusively or predominantly useful in military service" or is "in common use" before the Second Amendment's plain text applies. As *Wolford* made clear, historical or regulatory limits belong at step two, where the government bears the burden of justification.

2. **Step Two — Historical Tradition ("In Common Use" vs. "Dangerous and Unusual Weapons")**:
   Turning to *Bruen*'s second step, the majority will hold that under *Heller*, *McDonald v. City of Chicago*, 561 U.S. 742 (2010), *Caetano v. Massachusetts*, 577 U.S. 411 (2016) (per curiam), and *Bruen*, the historical tradition of prohibiting "dangerous and unusual weapons" is conjunctive and protects arms that are "in common use at the time" by law-abiding citizens for lawful purposes including self-defense. Relying on undisputed national data and the Court's own recognition in *Smith & Wesson Brands, Inc. v. Estados Unidos Mexicanos*, 605 U.S. 280, 297 (2025), that "[t]he AR-15 is the most popular rifle in the country" and is "both widely legal and bought by many ordinary consumers" (with tens of millions owned across more than 40 states), the majority will conclude that AR-15 platform and similar semiautomatic rifles are indisputably in common use for lawful purposes.
   The Court will reject Respondents' attempt to analogize flat bans on possessing common semiautomatic rifles in the home to the English Statute of Northampton, colonial trap-gun laws, 19th-century concealed-carry or affray restrictions on Bowie knives and slungshots, or Prohibition-era restrictions on automatic machineguns. Just as *Heller* held that a jurisdiction may not ban handguns merely because long guns remain available, a state or municipality may not ban the nation's most popular category of semiautomatic rifles merely because bolt-action rifles or handguns remain available.

## 3. Breadth of the Holding (`ground-breadth`)

The majority will announce a **categorical constitutional rule** invalidating state and local bans on the possession, acquisition, or sale of AR-15 platform and functionally similar semiautomatic rifles in common civilian use, rather than issuing a narrow, record-specific remand limited to the summary-judgment posture in Cook County. At the same time, the majority opinion will carefully cabin its holding in two respects:
- It will reaffirm *Heller*'s statement that fully automatic firearms—such as military M16 machineguns—and conversion devices that enable automatic fire remain outside the scope of the holding; and
- Because the Question Presented in both consolidated cases is limited to "AR-15 platform and similar semiautomatic rifles," the Court will decline to adjudicate standalone restrictions on detachable magazine capacity or non-rifle firearms that are not before the Court.

## 4. Opinion Authorship and Separate Writings

- **Majority Opinion Author**: **Chief Justice Roberts** (modal at `0.26`) or **Justice Kavanaugh** (`0.25`), with **Justice Thomas** (`0.18`) as the third leading candidate. Chief Justice Roberts often retains marquee Second Amendment decisions (as in *Rahimi* and *Wolford*) to ensure a unified six-Justice Court and clear doctrinal boundaries preserving bans on machineguns, while Justice Kavanaugh authored the foundational *Heller II* dissent and the *Snope v. Brown* statement that framed the "common use" inquiry for these cases.
- **Concurring Opinions**:
  - **Justice Thomas** (`0.55`) is likely to write a concurring opinion (if he does not deliver the opinion of the Court) emphasizing the Founding-era militia tradition under *United States v. Miller* and criticizing lower courts' resistance to *Heller* and *Bruen*.
  - **Justice Kavanaugh** (`0.42`) or **Justice Barrett** (`0.38`) may write a brief concurrence clarifying the limits of the Court's holding (e.g., as to machineguns and conversion devices) or the relationship between the "common use" test and historical analogical reasoning.
- **Dissenting Opinion**:
  - **Justice Sotomayor** (`0.55`), joined by Justices Kagan and Jackson, is expected to author the principal dissent. The dissent will argue that AR-15 platform rifles are military-derived weapons engineered for maximum lethality and rapid multi-round fire in mass shootings, and that under *Bruen* and *Rahimi* states and localities have ample authority to respond to unprecedented modern mass-casualty gun violence by prohibiting unusually dangerous weapons, consistent with centuries of Anglo-American regulation of especially lethal arms.
