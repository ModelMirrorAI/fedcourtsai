# Predicted Court Reasoning: *Cutberto Viramontes, et al. v. Cook County, Illinois, et al.* (No. 25-238)

## I. Predicted Judgment and Vote Lineup

- **Judgment Class**: `reversed` (the Court will reverse the judgment of the Seventh Circuit in No. 25-238 and the judgment of the Second Circuit in consolidated No. 25-566, *Grant v. Higgins*, and remand both cases for further proceedings consistent with its opinion).
- **Vote Split**: 6–3.
  - **Majority (6)**: Chief Justice Roberts and Justices Thomas, Alito, Gorsuch, Kavanaugh, and Barrett.
  - **Dissent (3)**: Justices Sotomayor, Kagan, and Jackson.
- **Procedural Exit Assessment**: Neither a dismissal as improvidently granted (`dismissed-as-improvidently-granted`) nor an affirmance by an equally divided Court is live. Cook County's argument that the *Viramontes* petitioners forfeited step-two historical arguments by stipulating that *Bevis v. City of Naperville*, 85 F.4th 1175 (7th Cir. 2023), foreclosed their claim below and seeking summary affirmance in the Seventh Circuit will be rejected. The Court intentionally consolidated *Viramontes* (final summary judgment from the Seventh Circuit) with *Grant v. Higgins* (interlocutory appeal from a preliminary-injunction denial in the Second Circuit where no such stipulation occurred), ensuring an unobstructed vehicle. Moreover, conceding that binding circuit precedent controls in order to expedite appellate and Supreme Court review does not waive the underlying constitutional contention, and under *Bruen* and *Wolford v. Lopez*, 609 U.S. ___, 146 S. Ct. 2039 (2026), the government bears the legal burden at step two to establish a historical tradition of firearm regulation.

## II. Question Reached, Doctrinal Ground (`majority-ground`), and Breadth (`ground-breadth`)

### 1. Question Reached
Both consolidated petitions present a single, identical question: *"Whether the Second and Fourteenth Amendments guarantee the right to possess AR-15 platform and similar semiautomatic rifles."* Although the Cook County ordinance and Connecticut statute also regulate certain semiautomatic pistols, shotguns, and large-capacity magazines, the *Viramontes* plaintiffs challenged Cook County's ordinance solely as applied to semiautomatic rifles, and the *Grant* petitioners narrowed their question presented at certiorari to match. The Court will reach the single question presented as framed and will decline to adjudicate restrictions on non-rifle firearms or magazine capacity.

### 2. Doctrinal Ground (`majority-ground`)
Applying the two-step framework synthesized in *District of Columbia v. Heller*, 554 U.S. 570 (2008), *New York State Rifle & Pistol Ass'n v. Bruen*, 597 U.S. 1 (2022), and *Wolford v. Lopez*, 609 U.S. ___, 146 S. Ct. 2039 (2026), the majority will hold that Cook County's and Connecticut's bans violate the Second and Fourteenth Amendments:

1. **Plain Text (Step One)**: Under *Wolford*, the plain-text inquiry asks three subsidiary questions: whether the law applies to "the people," whether it concerns "Arms" ("any weapon customarily used for offensive or defensive purposes"), and whether it restricts "keeping" or "bearing" them. Law-abiding adult citizens are part of "the people," criminalizing possession restricts "keeping" and "bearing," and semiautomatic rifles are "Arms" under *Heller*'s definition of all bearable firearms. The majority will explicitly reject the Seventh Circuit's holding in *Bevis* (followed in *Viramontes* and *Barnett v. Raoul*) that AR-15s fall outside the plain text of "Arms" as "militaristic" weapons or that common-use/dangerous-and-unusual analysis belongs in step one as a textual threshold the plaintiff must prove.
2. **Historical Tradition (Step Two)**: At step two, the government bears the burden of showing that a categorical ban on a class of bearable arms is consistent with the Nation's historical tradition of firearm regulation. Under *Heller* and *Bruen*, the relevant historical tradition governing bans on specific types of weapons is the common-law and Founding-era tradition of prohibiting the carrying or use of "dangerous and unusual weapons" to the terror of the people. As *Heller* and *Bruen* established, weapons that are "in common use at the time" for lawful purposes such as self-defense cannot be banned as "dangerous and unusual." Because semiautomatic rifles—which fire only one round per trigger pull—have been widely marketed to civilians for over a century and the AR-15 is the most popular rifle in America (owned by tens of millions of law-abiding citizens for self-defense, target shooting, and hunting), AR-15 platform and similar semiautomatic rifles are indisputably in common use for lawful purposes.
3. **Rejection of Respondents' Analogues**: The majority will hold that 18th-century trap-gun laws (which regulated a dangerous *mode* of unattended discharge rather than a class of firearms), 19th-century Bowie-knife and pocket-pistol laws (which overwhelmingly restricted *concealed carry* rather than possession in the home), and 20th-century Prohibition-era restrictions on fully automatic machineguns (which came too late to establish Founding-era meaning and targeted automatic weapons that never entered common civilian use) do not justify banning semiautomatic rifles in common use today. Nor does *Bruen*'s reference to "dramatic technological changes" or "unprecedented societal concerns" license courts to resurrect means-end interest balancing for popular semiautomatic firearms.

### 3. Breadth of the Holding (`ground-breadth`)
The majority will announce a **categorical constitutional rule** invalidating flat prohibitions on the possession of AR-15 platform and functionally similar semiautomatic rifles by law-abiding citizens across both consolidated cases, rather than issuing a narrow procedural remand limited to the Seventh Circuit's step-one error. At the same time, following the line urged by the Solicitor General, the majority will expressly cabin its holding in two respects:
1. It will distinguish **fully automatic firearms (machineguns)**—which fire multiple rounds with a single function of the trigger and have been restricted since the National Firearms Act of 1934 and 18 U.S.C. § 922(o) without achieving common civilian use—confirming that bans on machineguns and destructive devices remain constitutional.
2. It will confine its judgment to the question presented concerning **AR-15 platform and similar semiautomatic rifles**, leaving other provisions of state or local laws (such as large-capacity magazine limits or non-rifle restrictions) outside the scope of the decision.

## III. Opinion Authorship and Separate Writings

### 1. Majority Author (`majority-author`)
**Justice Kavanaugh** (`0.280`) and **Chief Justice Roberts** (`0.260`) are the two likeliest authors of the Opinion of the Court, followed by **Justice Thomas** (`0.150`), **Justice Alito** (`0.100`), **Justice Gorsuch** (`0.080`), and **Justice Barrett** (`0.070`).
- Among the six conservative Justices, Thomas authored *Bruen* (2022) and *Cargill* (2024), Alito authored *McDonald* (2010) and *Wolford v. Lopez* (June 2026), Roberts authored *Rahimi* (2024), and Gorsuch authored *VanDerStok* (2025) and *Hemani* (June 2026).
- Justice Kavanaugh has not yet delivered a Second Amendment majority opinion for the Court, yet he authored the seminal circuit dissent in *Heller v. District of Columbia* (*Heller II*), 670 F.3d 1244, 1269–96 (D.C. Cir. 2011), arguing that *Heller*'s common-use test invalidates bans on AR-15s and other semiautomatic rifles while preserving bans on automatic rifles, as well as the solo statement respecting the denial of certiorari in *Snope v. Brown*, 145 S. Ct. 1534 (2025), anticipating this exact grant. Chief Justice Roberts is the primary alternative if he retains the assignment himself (as in *Rahimi*) to craft an institutional opinion that squarely protects semiautomatic rifles in common use while emphasizing the validity of machinegun bans.

### 2. Separate Writings (`separate-writers`)
- **Concurrences**: Even with a unified six-Justice majority opinion, one or two concurrences are likely:
  - **Justice Thomas** (`0.45`) or **Justice Gorsuch** (`0.30`) may write separately to critique lower-court resistance to *Heller* and *Bruen* (building on Thomas's *Friedman*, *Harrel*, and *Snope* writings) or to emphasize Founding-era militia and self-defense history.
  - **Justice Kavanaugh** (`0.35`, if not assigned the majority) or **Justice Barrett** (`0.32`) may write a concurrence emphasizing the doctrinal limits of the holding (such as the preservation of machinegun prohibitions and longstanding regulations under *Heller*) or clarifying the role of common-use evidence within *Bruen*'s historical framework.
  - **Justice Alito** (`0.22`) may write to address empirical claims regarding self-defense and crime statistics, as he did in *Bruen* and *Caetano*.
- **Dissents**:
  - **Justice Sotomayor** (`0.55`) and **Justice Jackson** (`0.48`, who authored the principal dissent in *Wolford v. Lopez* in June 2026) are the likeliest authors of a principal and/or supplemental dissent, joined by **Justice Kagan** (`0.25`). The dissent will argue that the AR-15's military lineage, muzzle velocity, and role in mass shootings place it squarely within the Nation's tradition of regulating uniquely lethal weapons that pose unprecedented societal danger.
