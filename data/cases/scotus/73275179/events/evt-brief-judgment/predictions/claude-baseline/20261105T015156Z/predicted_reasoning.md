# Predicted outcome — Viramontes v. Cook County (No. 25-238, consolidated with No. 25-566, Grant v. Higgins)

**Judgment: reversed.** The Court reverses the Seventh Circuit's judgment upholding Cook County's ban on AR-15-platform rifles and remands. It most likely directs, or at least clearly signals, entry of judgment for the petitioners, since the case reached the court of appeals on a final judgment on the pleadings. The consolidated Second Circuit case (a preliminary-injunction appeal) is reversed or vacated alongside it.

**Vote: 6–3 on ideological lines.** Majority: Roberts, Thomas, Alito, Gorsuch, Kavanaugh, Barrett. Dissent: Sotomayor, Kagan, Jackson. The least certain majority votes are Barrett's and the Chief Justice's. Neither is likely to defect on the bottom line, but either could write or join a narrower opinion on how to apply the standard. A 5–4 split or a fractured majority is the main alternative. A DIG is not a live option: the Court relisted this petition 22 times before granting it, consolidated it with the Connecticut case, and the United States is participating in argument.

**Ground.** At the textual step, AR-15s are "Arms." The majority rejects the Seventh Circuit's *Bevis* approach, which took "military-style" weapons out of the Second Amendment's text. Barrett's *Wolford v. Lopez* concurrence already criticized "smuggling" historical limits into the text inquiry. At the history step, the Court treats *Heller*'s common-use rule as the controlling historical principle: arms commonly possessed by law-abiding citizens for lawful purposes are not "dangerous and unusual" and cannot be banned outright. It rejects the Second Circuit's "unusually dangerous" analogue test and the respondents' launcegay, trap-gun and Bowie-knife analogues as justifications for a possession ban.

**Breadth.** The rule is categorical as to bans on possessing AR-15-class rifles. The majority likely relies on nationwide ownership and the rifles' legality in roughly 40 States, as the United States urges, rather than a case-specific empirical record. It expressly leaves open magazine-capacity limits, feature-based regulation short of a ban, machine guns, and the exact threshold for "common use." Kavanaugh, author of the *Snope* statement, may write or join a concurrence stressing those limits.

**Who writes.** The case was argued in the December 2026 sitting. My leading candidates for the majority opinion are Kavanaugh (his *Snope* statement), Thomas (author of *Bruen*), Roberts (who often keeps the Term's largest cases), and Barrett and Alito. I expect a principal dissent from Sotomayor or Jackson, possibly Breyer-style with Kagan joining. Jackson is the likeliest to write separately even if she joins another dissent. Thomas is likely to concur and press for a broader rule if he is not the author. Barrett and Gorsuch may concur on method.
