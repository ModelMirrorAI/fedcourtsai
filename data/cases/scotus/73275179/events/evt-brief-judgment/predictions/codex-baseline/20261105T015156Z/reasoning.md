## Substantive adjustment

**The strongest reason for disturbance is the legal threshold below.** The Seventh Circuit affirmed because petitioners had not developed a sufficient record to escape Bevis, which placed on them a burden to show that these rifles differed materially from military-grade weaponry. The panel also identified late exhibits and admissibility concerns. This is not merely a party's description: it is in the actual appended order, petition pp. 2a–5a. Petitioners counter that semiautomatic rifles are plainly arms before any historical exception is considered, and that civilian common use defeats a categorical prohibition. Their merits brief, printed pp. 24–29, presents the issue as correction of the legal standard, not a request to reweigh one expert. The legal-standard route makes some form of reversal or vacatur considerably more likely than an affirmance based entirely on this evidentiary shortfall.

**The United States supports petitioners and requests reversal and remand in both cases.** Its merits amicus, printed pp. 5–7 and 32, treats AR-15s as protected arms in common lawful use, rejects military resemblance as a disqualifier, and proposes longstanding widespread legality as evidence of common use. That is a useful substantive alternative to ownership surveys alone. I follow its bottom line but do not assume that the Court will adopt its precise federal/state-legality proxy as a universal test. This is an actual government merits position, not a CVSG recommendation, and no presumed government win rate is added. Source: `merits-amicus-united-states.txt`, summary, sections C–E, and conclusion.

**Cook County presents a real historical argument, not merely a public-safety slogan.** Its summary and argument, printed pp. 11–18 and 48–54, invoke restrictions on exceptionally dangerous weapons, including affray, trap guns, Bowie knives, and slungshots. It argues for analogy responsive to technological change and disputes whether raw ownership equals common use for self-defense. Rahimi independently confirms that historical analogies need not be identical and that both the reason for and the extent of a burden matter. That verified primary authority is the strongest general-law reason not to set the forecast near certainty: United States v. Rahimi, 602 U.S. 680, 692–693, 700–701 (2024), CourtListener opinion 11066646, searched for “historical twin.”

My inference is nonetheless that a majority will reject the move from those traditions to a categorical prohibition of ordinary private possession of these rifles. Restrictions on terrorizing public carry or particular harmful deployment are not necessarily comparable in burden to a class-wide possession ban. The respondent also repeatedly emphasizes legislative choice and unusually dangerous characteristics; petitioners identify that formulation as a return to the policy balancing their proposed reading of Heller excludes. I regard petitioners' reading as more likely to command the decisive Justices, without treating either side's claims about ballistics, actual defensive incidents, or historical prevalence as adjudicated empirical facts. Sources: petitioners' merits brief, printed pp. 24–25 and common-use argument; respondent's merits brief, printed pp. 11–18 and 48–54.

**The main uncertainty is the scope of the remedy and rule, rather than whether rifles qualify as arms at all.** The record defects described in the Seventh Circuit appendix support a narrower vacatur-and-remand route. The companion is a preliminary-injunction case, whereas this cell concerns the Seventh Circuit's final summary-judgment disposition; consolidation does not erase that difference. It gives the Court two legal routes to consider, but I do not assume that the companion supplies missing admissible evidence in this case. Sources: petition appendix pp. 2a–5a; petitioners' merits brief, printed pp. 21–24. I retain 15% for vacatur and 9% for affirmance principally because of record concerns, flexible historical analogy, and the possibility of a narrower understanding of common use. A DIG is possible but not the central route: the provisioned record identifies no mootness, settlement, or recusal obstacle.

The issue-specific evidence is strongest for **Kavanaugh**: his Heller II dissent expressly treated semiautomatic rifles as protected under Heller and distinguished them from fully automatic weapons. I verified the dissent itself, Heller v. District of Columbia, 670 F.3d 1244, 1269–1270 (D.C. Cir. 2011), CourtListener opinion 9484844. This is evidence of his approach, not binding Supreme Court law. Thomas's and Alito's favorable common-use positions are set out through their separate writings cited in the petitioners' and government briefs. My six-Justice majority is a substantive inference from those positions and the briefs' competing applications of the existing framework, not a verified promise of anyone's vote. Roberts and Barrett are the greatest uncertainties within my forecast majority, particularly over whether to announce a categorical rule or require more historical analysis. I expect the three forecast dissenters to find the County's historical-dangerousness account more persuasive. None of those predicted votes is derived from an argument transcript.

The authorship distribution is deliberately diffuse. The pack's strictly-prior authorship rows contain 247 decisions in OT2020–OT2024. For context, Kavanaugh authored 28 Court opinions and 32 separate writings while seated in 238 recorded decisions; Thomas 31 and 64 / 238; Barrett 28 and 23 / 232; Sotomayor 29 and 48 / 238; and Jackson 14 and 38 / 147. Those are raw recorded counts, not the harness's shrunken baselines. I elevate Kavanaugh to 0.34 as majority author because his directly on-point earlier writing supplies a natural route through the rifle question. Thomas remains a substantial alternative at 0.22. I elevate Barrett's separate-writing chance to 0.30 for a narrower methodological account, and Sotomayor's to 0.50 as the leading dissent-author candidate. Jackson is another plausible separate dissenter. Actual opinion assignment is unknowable before argument, and joining a dissent is not authorship. ## Limitations and significance

The open-web tool returned no visible content for the three attempts logged in `retrieval.md`; no web result supplied evidence. CourtListener primary-opinion retrieval worked and supplied the two independent authorities discussed above. The default uv cache was read-only; an ephemeral `UV_CACHE_DIR=/tmp/uv-cache` override resolved command execution without a package install. These were tooling limitations, not missing merits inputs or a block.

The **0.95 significance score** measures the nationwide importance of defining protection for a widely disputed rifle class and the limits of categorical weapons bans, rather than grant probability. Its basis is the question presented, consolidated posture, and competing merits arguments already provisioned. I have not used post-decision publicity, another prediction, any realized-outcome file, or the prohibited topic-label artifacts.
