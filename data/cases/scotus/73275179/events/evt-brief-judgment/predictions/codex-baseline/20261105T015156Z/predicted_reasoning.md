# Forecast of the Court's judgment and reasoning

## Judgment and lineup

The Court will **reverse** the Seventh Circuit's judgment and remand for proceedings implementing its Second Amendment holding. The expected split is **6–3**: Roberts, Thomas, Alito, Gorsuch, Kavanaugh, and Barrett for reversal; Sotomayor, Kagan, and Jackson dissenting. This forecast concerns Viramontes's judgment, not an automatic order entering a particular preliminary injunction in the consolidated Connecticut case.

## Majority ground

The Court will hold that AR-15 platform and similar semiautomatic rifles are arms and that Heller's protection of arms in common lawful civilian use bars categorical bans on this class. A civilian rifle does not lose textual coverage merely because it resembles, or shares features with, military weaponry. The majority will reject exceptional dangerousness as an independent sufficient basis for prohibiting this commonly possessed class.

The historical comparison will distinguish rules concerning terrifying public carry or dangerous methods of use from a general prohibition on lawful private possession. The majority will reject a requirement that owners prove frequent defensive shootings before a class of commonly possessed rifles receives protection. It will also reject the availability of other firearms as a sufficient answer to the constitutional objection.

The Court will reach the granted rifle-possession question, not resolve separate magazine-capacity challenges or the validity of machinegun prohibitions. Its holding will not require adopting the United States' precise survey of jurisdictions as the exclusive common-use test.

## Breadth

The announced rule will protect **the rifle class against categorical possession bans**, rather than turn on these plaintiffs' individual needs or end with instructions to collect better expert evidence. It will therefore reach beyond Cook County and the named plaintiffs. The Court will leave other forms of firearms regulation and distinct weapon categories open rather than declare every restriction on a semiautomatic firearm invalid.

## Opinion authors and separate writings

Kavanaugh is the leading forecast author of the Court's opinion; Thomas is the next most likely. Kavanaugh's earlier rifle-specific reasoning offers a direct route to a majority holding without an unnecessarily comprehensive theory of all weapons regulation. The authorship probabilities retain substantial room for Barrett, Roberts, Alito, and Gorsuch rather than assuming an assignment before argument.

Sotomayor is the leading candidate to write the principal dissent defending historical analogy and legislative authority over exceptionally dangerous weapons; Kagan and Jackson are the forecast dissenting joiners. Jackson is another substantial separate-writing candidate. Barrett is the leading candidate for a narrower methodological concurrence, with Thomas also a substantial concurrence possibility if someone else writes the majority. These are probabilistic writing forecasts, not an assertion that every forecast dissenter or concurring Justice will write.

## Alternative routes

The principal alternative is **vacatur**, requiring the Seventh Circuit to apply a corrected textual and historical framework without resolving every common-use question itself. The insufficient-record discussion makes that route more credible than a sweeping remedial order. Affirmance is less likely but remains possible if the decisive Justices accept the County's historical-dangerousness principle or find the plaintiffs' proof inadequate even under the proper rule. A DIG is a small residual possibility; the forecast is a signed merits decision, not a procedural exit or equal division.
