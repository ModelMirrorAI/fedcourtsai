## 2. Case-Specific Adjustments (`5.17%` Anchor → `1.5%`)

### Modest Upward Factor
- **Constitutional facial invalidation of a municipal ordinance:** First Amendment speech petitions involving the line between *Reed v. Town of Gilbert*, 576 U.S. 155 (2015), and *City of Austin v. Reagan National Advertising of Austin, LLC*, 596 U.S. 61 (2022), carry broader doctrinal interest than fact-bound civil disputes, and there is roughly a 15% chance that a municipal-government organization (such as the International Municipal Lawyers Association or Florida League of Cities) files a cert-stage amicus brief before the December 21, 2026 response deadline.

### Decisive Downward Factors
1. **Complete absence of a circuit split:** Every federal court of appeals since *Reed* to address a panhandling ordinance targeting in-person requests for an immediate donation has held it facially content-based—including *Norton v. City of Springfield*, 806 F.3d 411, 412–13 (7th Cir. 2015), *Rodgers v. Bryant*, 942 F.3d 451, 456 (8th Cir. 2019), *Reynolds v. Middleton*, 779 F.3d 222 (4th Cir. 2015), and *Thayer v. City of Worcester*, 576 U.S. 1048 (2015) (GVR'd in light of *Reed*). CourtListener searches across all twelve regional and D.C. circuits confirm that *zero* federal courts of appeals since *City of Austin* (April 2022) have upheld an immediate-donation panhandling ordinance as content-neutral. The post-*Austin* authorities cited in the Petition's Table of Authorities are readily distinguishable, as Judge Newsom showed (Slip Op. 38–40 & nn.16–17): *National Federation of the Blind of Texas, Inc. v. City of Arlington*, 109 F.4th 728, 734–35 (5th Cir. 2024) (and two district-court donation-bin decisions) regulated physical, unattended donation receptacles across the board regardless of signage or whether the operator was charitable or for-profit, whereas *Mazo v. New Jersey Secretary of State*, 54 F.4th 124 (3d Cir. 2022), and *Project Veritas v. Schmidt*, 125 F.4th 929 (9th Cir. 2025) (en banc), did not involve solicitation at all.
2. **Severe vehicle flaw #1 — Daytona Beach's commercial-solicitation concession defeats QPs 1 & 2 independently:** Judge Newsom rested his content-discrimination holding on *two* independent grounds (Slip Op. 34–35). First, Daytona Beach `"candidly concede[d] that 'the law does not regulate commercial solicitations.' Br. of Appellant at 26. That fact alone takes the ordinance outside Heffron's protection and betrays its content-based-ness."` Under *Heffron v. International Society for Krishna Consciousness, Inc.*, 452 U.S. 640, 649 (1981), and *City of Austin*, 596 U.S. at 72–73, solicitation restrictions are content-neutral only `"so long as they do not discriminate based on topic, subject matter, or viewpoint"`—meaning they must apply evenhandedly to commercial and non-commercial solicitation alike. Because Daytona Beach Code § 66-1 exempts commercial solicitation, the Court could not reach QPs 1 and 2's abstract question about temporal ("immediate donation") lines without confronting a conceded subject-matter distinction fatal under *City of Austin* itself.
3. **Severe vehicle flaw #2 — QP 3 is non-outcome-determinative and narrow tailoring is unchallenged:** Question Presented 3 asks whether traffic safety is a compelling government interest. Even if circuits vary in phrasing on that point (e.g., *Solantic, LLC v. City of Neptune Beach*, 410 F.3d 1250, 1268 (11th Cir. 2005); *Camp Hill Borough Republican Ass'n v. Borough of Camp Hill*, 101 F.4th 266 (3d Cir. 2024); *Rash v. Lafayette County*, 192 F.4th 429 (5th Cir. 2026)), Judge Newsom **expressly assumed arguendo** that Daytona Beach's traffic-safety interest was compelling (and agreed its infectious-disease interest was compelling) before holding § 66-1 failed narrow tailoring on two independent grounds: (a) deposition testimony from Deputy City Manager Dru Driscoll admitting the City had never attempted to address its concerns by enforcing existing content-neutral ordinances against traffic obstruction, trespass, and disorderly conduct; and (b) fatal underinclusiveness under *Brown v. Entertainment Merchants Ass'n*, 564 U.S. 786, 802 (2011), because approaching motorists to gather petition signatures or make non-immediate requests poses identical traffic hazards yet goes unregulated (Slip Op. 42–45). Answering QP 3 for the City cannot alter the judgment.
4. **Fractured partial-remand posture:** While the affirmed $80,000 conditional damages stipulation and narrowed permanent injunction against Police Chief Jakari Young support certiorari jurisdiction under 28 U.S.C. § 1254(1), the Eleventh Circuit vacated the declaratory judgment as to 11 of the 18 challenged subsections (`§§ (c)(3)(c)–(f)` and `(c)(4)(b)–(h)`) for a trial on standing and vacated universal injunctive language under *Trump v. CASA, Inc.*, 606 U.S. 831 (2025), remanding for further proceedings.
5. **Unanimous conservative panel, zero en banc interest, and lack of a Rule-of-Four coalition:** Judge Newsom's meticulous 49-page opinion was joined in full by Judges Brasher and Tjoflat (affirming District Judge Wendy Berger on the merits), and the Eleventh Circuit denied rehearing en banc on August 17, 2026 with **no active judge requesting a poll** (`lower-court-docket.txt`). On the Supreme Court, the three *City of Austin* dissenters (Justices Thomas, Gorsuch, and Barrett) argued for *stricter* enforcement of *Reed* and explicitly cited *Norton v. City of Springfield*, 806 F.3d at 412, with approval (`596 U.S. at 99`); meanwhile, the *City of Austin* author (Justice Sotomayor) and Justices Kagan and Jackson carefully confined *Austin*'s solicitation passage to content-agnostic regulations of solicitation generally and have virtually no incentive to grant review to reinstate criminal penalties and overturn damages awarded to indigent panhandlers. Even if Justice Alito or Justice Kavanaugh were abstractly sympathetic to municipal panhandling regulations, assembling four votes to grant on this flawed vehicle is implausible.
6. **Counsel profile:** Petitioner's counsel of record (Michael H. Kahn of Melbourne, FL, appearing alongside trial/appellate counsel Jeffrey S. Weiss of Orlando, FL) has no prior Supreme Court merits or repeat-player cert footprint.
