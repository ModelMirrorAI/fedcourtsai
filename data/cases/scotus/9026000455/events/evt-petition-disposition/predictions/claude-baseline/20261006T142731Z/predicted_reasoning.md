# Forecast: what the Court will do with No. 26-455, Chaffins v. Sharp

**Disposition.** The petition will be denied without comment, most likely on an
order list in December 2026 or January 2027.

**Response.** The respondents are ten North Dakota human-service-zone and county
officials sued in their individual capacities. Expect a waiver of the right to
respond filed well before the November 4, 2026 due date, and no call for a
response from the Court: the decision below is a two-page unpublished Rule 47B
affirmance with no reasoning, so there is nothing for a response to engage.

**Distributions and relists.** The snapshot shows the petition at zero
distributions; the only distribution on the docket so far is of the sealed-filing
motion (26M8), heard at the September 28, 2026 conference and granted on
October 5, 2026, which is when the petition was docketed. From that state the
petition will be distributed once, for a conference three to five weeks after
the waiver lands, and denied at that conference. No relist: there is no split to
check, no record to pull, and no Justice's chambers has a reason to hold it. The
only route to a count other than one is a dismissal under Rule 46 or a defect in
the filing, both remote.

**CVSG.** None. No federal party, no federal statute at issue beyond section
1983 itself, and no federal interest a Solicitor General would be asked about.

**Which question, if any.** If the Court took the case it would take the first
question only, the Younger question, since the Rooker-Feldman question as framed
turns on how the petitioner's complaint was characterized below, which the
unpublished affirmance never says. In practice neither question will be taken:
the court of appeals affirmed "on any basis supported by the record" without
identifying one, so the Court cannot tell which doctrine, if either, decided the
case.

**Summary disposition versus plenary review.** Conditional on any grant, the
likelier form by far is a GVR in light of an intervening decision clarifying
Younger or Rooker-Feldman rather than plenary review; a reasoned-opinion-free
affirmance of a pro se dismissal is not a vehicle the Court sets for argument.
No such intervening decision is pending that I can identify, which is why the
grant number itself is small.

**Dissent from or statement respecting denial.** None expected. The Younger and
Rooker-Feldman concerns the petition raises have drawn separate writings in
other cases, but those came on reasoned published opinions with developed
records, not on a Rule 47B affirmance of a sealed district-court order.
