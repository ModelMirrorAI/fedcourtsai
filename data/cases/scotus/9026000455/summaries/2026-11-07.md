## In brief

A mother asks the Court to revive her civil rights lawsuit over her child's warrantless removal, which lower courts dismissed on jurisdictional grounds.

## What happened

Leanda Chaffins alleges that North Dakota human services officials removed her child without a warrant or court order in September 2022, before any abuse report was filed or state proceedings began. She sued the officials involved under federal civil rights law, seeking damages and other relief for the removal and for alleged deprivation of her parental rights. The district court dismissed her case for lack of jurisdiction, holding that two doctrines limiting federal courts' power applied: one barring interference with ongoing state court proceedings, and one barring review of state-court judgments. The Eighth Circuit affirmed without addressing the merits of her constitutional claims.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked whether federal courts may decline to hear civil rights claims about conduct that happened before any state court case began, simply because state custody proceedings were filed afterward. The Court is also asked whether a separate rule, which bars federal courts from reviewing state court judgments, blocks claims seeking damages for conduct that occurred before any judgment was entered, where the plaintiff is not asking a federal court to reverse that judgment.

## Where it stands

The petition was distributed for the Court's September 28, 2026 conference, and the docket shows no decision on it since. Respondents have waived their right to respond.

## What each outcome would mean

Chaffins, the mother, asks the Court to take up her case. If the Court grants the petition, it would hear the case in full and decide whether the dismissal of her lawsuit was correct, without yet determining who wins. If it denies the petition, the Eighth Circuit's decision affirming dismissal of her lawsuit would stand, leaving her unable to pursue her claims in federal court on the current record.
