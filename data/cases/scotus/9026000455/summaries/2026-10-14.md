## In brief

A mother asks the Court to revive her civil rights lawsuit over the warrantless removal of her child, which lower courts dismissed on jurisdictional grounds.

## What happened

Leanda Chaffins says North Dakota human services officials removed her child from her custody in September 2022 without a warrant or court order. She sued under federal civil rights law, seeking damages and other relief, arguing the removal violated her constitutional rights. The district court dismissed her case for lack of jurisdiction, holding that two legal doctrines applied: one that lets federal courts step back when related state court proceedings are ongoing, and another that bars federal review of claims that amount to challenging a state court judgment. The Eighth Circuit affirmed without addressing the merits of her claims. Chaffins, representing herself, now petitions the Supreme Court.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked whether a federal court may decline to hear civil rights claims about conduct that happened before any state court case began, simply because state custody proceedings were filed afterward. The Court is also asked whether a rule that blocks federal review of claims attacking a state court judgment applies here, where Chaffins says she is not challenging any judgment but only the officials' earlier conduct in removing her child.

## Where it stands

## What each outcome would mean

Chaffins, the petitioner, is asking the Court to take up her case. If the Court grants the petition, it will hear the case in full and decide whether her lawsuit can go forward in federal court; a grant alone does not decide who is right. If the Court denies the petition, the Eighth Circuit's decision affirming dismissal of her claims stands, and her lawsuit remains dismissed.
