## In brief

A mother asks the Court to revive her civil rights lawsuit over her child's warrantless removal, which lower courts dismissed on jurisdictional grounds.

## What happened

Leanda Chaffins says North Dakota human services officials removed her child without a warrant or court order in September 2022, with a report of abuse filed only afterward. She sued the officials and others involved under federal civil rights law, seeking damages and other relief for the removal, arguing the violation happened before any state court was involved. The district court dismissed her case, holding that federal courts should defer to the related state custody proceedings and that a doctrine barring review of state-court judgments also applied. The Eighth Circuit affirmed without reaching the merits of her constitutional claims, and rehearing was denied.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked whether federal courts may decline to hear civil rights claims over conduct that happened before any state court proceeding began, just because state custody or termination proceedings were filed later. It is also asked whether a separate rule barring federal review of state-court judgments blocks claims for damages over earlier official conduct, when the plaintiff is not asking a federal court to overturn what a state court decided.

## Where it stands

The petition has been fully briefed. All respondents have filed waivers of their right to respond, and the docket shows no indication yet that the Court has acted on the petition itself.

## What each outcome would mean

Chaffins is asking the Court to take up her case and reverse the dismissal below. If the Court grants the petition, it would hear full briefing and argument on whether her claims can proceed in federal court; a grant alone would not decide who is right. If the Court denies the petition, the Eighth Circuit's decision affirming dismissal of her lawsuit would stand, leaving the respondents' win in place and her claims unable to proceed in federal court as currently framed.
