## In brief

A North Dakota mother asks the Court to revive her civil rights lawsuit over her child's removal without a warrant, which lower courts dismissed on jurisdictional grounds.

## What happened

Leanda Chaffins alleges that North Dakota human service officials removed her child without a warrant, court order, or showing of imminent danger in September 2022, before any abuse report was filed or state court proceedings began. She sued the officials and others involved for violating her constitutional rights. The district court dismissed her case, ruling it lacked jurisdiction because of ongoing state custody proceedings and because her claims were tied to state-court orders. The Eighth Circuit affirmed without addressing her constitutional claims, and rehearing was denied.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked whether a federal court can refuse to hear civil rights claims about conduct that happened before any state court case started, simply because state custody proceedings were filed later. It is also asked whether a doctrine that bars federal review of state-court judgments can block claims seeking damages for separate wrongful conduct by officials, when the plaintiff is not asking a federal court to overturn what the state court decided.

## Where it stands

The petition was distributed for the September 28, 2026 conference, and the docket shows no action recorded on it since. Several respondents have since waived their right to respond, and the case was distributed again for the December 4, 2026 conference.

## What each outcome would mean

Chaffins, who lost in the lower courts, is asking the Court to take up her case. If the Court grants the petition, it will hear the case in full and decide whether her claims can proceed in federal court; a grant alone decides nothing yet. If it denies the petition, the Eighth Circuit's dismissal stands, leaving Chaffins unable to pursue these claims in federal court, though a denial does not mean the Court agrees the dismissal was correct.
