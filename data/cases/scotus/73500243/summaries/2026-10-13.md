## In brief

The Court declined to review a forensic examiner's bid to escape a wrongly convicted man's lawsuit over her disputed palm print identification.

## What happened

Clemente Aguirre-Jarquin spent fourteen years in prison, ten on death row, after Donna Birks, a latent print examiner, testified that a print on the murder weapon matched his palm. Years later an investigation found Birks had sent the required second check of her work to a colleague she considered unable to verify it properly, and reviewers concluded her identification was wrong. After new DNA evidence and another suspect's confessions emerged, Florida courts vacated Aguirre's conviction and the state dropped the charges. Aguirre sued Birks and others. The lower courts denied Birks qualified immunity, a legal protection for officials, on Aguirre's claim that she fabricated evidence, finding a jury could decide the question; the appeals court also chose not to review related fact-bound issues, leaving them for further proceedings.

## What the Court is being asked

The case asks whether Birks could be held liable for violating Aguirre's rights without proof she knew her identification was false, whether it was already clearly established that her conduct was unlawful at the time, and whether the fabricated evidence actually caused Aguirre's conviction given his own trial testimony that he had handled the knife.

## Where it stands

The petition was distributed for conference twice, and the Court denied it on October 13, 2026.

## What each outcome would mean

Birks, the losing party below, asked the Court to take up the case and overturn the appeals court's ruling against her. The denial leaves the Eleventh Circuit's decision in place, meaning Aguirre's lawsuit against Birks continues in the lower courts, including the fact questions about her intent and whether her evidence caused his conviction. The denial does not mean the Court agreed Birks should lose; it simply leaves that question for the lower courts to resolve.
