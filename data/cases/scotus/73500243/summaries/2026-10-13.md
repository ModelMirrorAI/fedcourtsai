## In brief

The Court declined to hear a forensic examiner's bid for immunity from a wrongfully convicted man's lawsuit over her palm print identification.

## What happened

Clemente Javier Aguirre-Jarquin spent fourteen years in prison, ten on death row, after Donna Birks, a latent print examiner, testified that a palm print on the murder weapon matched him. Years later an investigation found her identification unreliable, and new DNA evidence implicated another person; Aguirre's conviction was vacated and charges dropped. He sued Birks and others, claiming she fabricated the identification in violation of his due process rights. The district court and the Eleventh Circuit Court of Appeals both denied Birks qualified immunity, a legal protection shielding officials from suit unless they violated clearly established law, letting the fabrication claim proceed. The appeals court declined to also consider her arguments about whether the evidence actually caused his conviction.

## What the Court is being asked

Birks asked the Court to decide whether her identification could count as fabricated evidence without proof she knew it was false, whether it was clearly established at the time that her method of verification was unconstitutional, and whether the lower court should have also decided whether her evidence actually caused Aguirre's conviction given his own trial testimony that he handled the knife.

## Where it stands

The petition was distributed for two conferences, after which the Court requested a response from Aguirre; both sides then filed briefs, and on October 13, 2026, the Court denied the petition.

## What each outcome would mean

Birks, the examiner, asked the Court to take up the case. By denying the petition, the Court leaves the Eleventh Circuit's decision in place: Birks does not get qualified immunity on the fabrication claim, and Aguirre's lawsuit against her continues in the lower courts. The denial does not mean the Court agreed with the appeals court's reasoning; it simply leaves that ruling standing without deciding the underlying questions itself.
