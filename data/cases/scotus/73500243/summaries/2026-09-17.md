## What happened

Clemente Aguirre-Jarquin was convicted of a 2004 double murder in Florida, based partly on testimony from Donna Birks, a police latent print examiner, that his palm print matched one found on the murder weapon. Aguirre spent 14 years in prison, including time on death row, before new DNA evidence and another suspect's admissions led Florida courts to order a new trial; the state then dropped all charges. Aguirre sued Birks and others under a federal civil-rights statute, claiming the print testimony violated his right to a fair trial. The district court refused to grant Birks qualified immunity, a legal protection shielding officials from suits unless they violated clearly established law. The Eleventh Circuit Court of Appeals agreed, letting that claim proceed while ruling for Birks on a related claim.

## What the Court is being asked

Birks asks the Court to decide several related questions: whether her mistaken print testimony can be blamed for Aguirre's imprisonment at all, given that Aguirre himself testified he had handled the murder weapon; whether an examiner who honestly believed her work was accurate, but was later shown wrong, can be held to have violated due process; and whether it was clearly established, back in 2004-2006, that asking a certified but possibly declining colleague to verify a print match could count as fabricating evidence, such that Birks should have known her actions were unlawful.

## Where it stands

The petition has been distributed for conference twice, most recently for October 9, 2026, after the Court requested a response from Aguirre and received both an opposition brief and a reply from Birks. The Court has not yet decided whether to hear the case.
