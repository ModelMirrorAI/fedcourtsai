## In brief

A forensic examiner asks the Court to review a ruling letting a wrongfully convicted man's lawsuit over her print identification proceed to a jury.

## What happened

Clemente Aguirre-Jarquin was convicted of a 2004 double murder and sentenced to death, based substantially on testimony by Donna Birks, a forensic print examiner, that a print on the knife matched his palm. Years later an internal investigation found Birks' identification unreliable, and new DNA evidence and another person's confessions led Florida courts to vacate his conviction; the state dropped the charges. Aguirre sued Birks and others. The district court denied Birks qualified immunity (protection from suit for officials unless they violated clearly established law) on a claim that she fabricated evidence, and the Eleventh Circuit affirmed that denial while ruling for her on other claims and leaving some issues for the trial court to decide later.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked whether a claim that fabricated evidence caused a wrongful conviction fails as a matter of law when the convicted person's own trial testimony covered similar ground, whether proving this kind of due-process violation requires showing the examiner actually knew her conclusion was false, and whether the law was clear enough by 2004-2006 to deny Birks immunity for routing her verification to a colleague she doubted was competent.

## Where it stands

The petition has been fully briefed, with Aguirre waiving an initial response before the Court asked for one, and both sides have now filed briefs; it was distributed for the Court's conference on October 9, 2026, with no action yet recorded since then.

## What each outcome would mean

Birks, the examiner, is asking the Court to take up the case. If the Court grants review, it will hear full arguments and decide the legal questions itself, deciding nothing about who wins simply by agreeing to hear it. If it denies review, the Eleventh Circuit's decision stands, meaning Aguirre's lawsuit against Birks continues in the lower courts without a ruling from the Supreme Court on these questions.
