# Forecast: what the Court will do with No. 25-1341, Birks v. Aguirre-Jarquin

**Disposition.** The Court will deny the petition. P(any grant) = 0.12.

**State forecast from.** The docket shows two distribution entries: the first
(June 17, 2026) for the September 28 long conference, entered after respondent
waived; the Court then called for a response on July 31, the brief in
opposition arrived August 31, the reply September 10, and the petition was
redistributed (September 16) for the October 9, 2026 conference. No conference
has yet considered it, so the true relist count is zero; the second entry is
the post-response redistribution, not a relist. No CVSG.

**Relists.** The petition will most likely be decided at or within one
conference of October 9. I put the chance of at least one further distribution
entry (a relist or a reschedule) at 0.38: a called-for response marks
chambers-level interest, and both of the live grant routes (a per curiam
reversal) and the live denial-with-writing route (a dissent from denial) need
extra conferences, but the modal outcome is a quiet denial on the first
consideration. If it is relisted, expect one to three relists, not a long
string.

**CVSG.** None. No federal party, statute, or program is implicated; this is a
Section 1983 damages suit against a county sheriff's-office print examiner.
P(CVSG) = 0.02.

**Which question, if taken.** The Court would not take the five questions as
written. The only question with any traction is the clearly-established-law
question (petitioner's third through fifth questions, collapsed): whether
Eleventh Circuit precedent on knowing fabrication gave fair notice that
routing a print verification to a colleague the examiner believed incompetent
was unconstitutional. The causation and materiality questions (QP 1) were not
decided below and will not be taken; the mens rea question (QP 2) is framed on
a premise the court of appeals rejected (it inferred knowledge, not mere
error), so the Court would not reach it as a freestanding merits question.

**Summary disposition versus plenary review.** Conditional on a grant, a
summary disposition is the likelier route (0.55): a per curiam reversal on
clearly-established law in the Zorn v. Linton (March 2026) mold, or a GVR
citing Zorn. Plenary review is the less likely half because the questions are
fact-bound, the posture is interlocutory, and the petition identifies no
outcome-determinative split.

**Dissent from denial.** Conditional on a denial, P(some Justice writes) =
0.10. If anyone writes it will be a short dissent or statement from Justice
Alito, Justice Thomas, or Justice Gorsuch objecting that the Eleventh Circuit
defined clearly established law at too high a level of generality. The most
likely outcome is a bare denial on the order list.

**If granted (conditional only).** If the Court grants, the ground would be
that no precedent placed beyond debate that a certified examiner's choice of a
credentialed but doubted verifier amounted to knowing fabrication, and the
Court would reverse the denial of qualified immunity on Count I without
addressing causation or the elements of a fabrication claim.
