## 2. Synchronized Relist with *Robinhood Markets, Inc. v. Sodha* (No. 25-944)

The single most important docket signal explaining the November 9, 2026 relist is the simultaneous Conference consideration of a closely related Ninth Circuit securities petition: *Robinhood Markets, Inc. v. Sodha*, No. 25-944 (`scotus/73280982`).

1. **Direct doctrinal dependency**: In *Robinhood*, petitioners seek review of the Ninth Circuit's published, split decision in *Sodha v. Golubowski*, 154 F.4th 1019 (9th Cir. 2025), addressing (a) the standard for actionable omissions under Section 11(a)'s "misleading" prong (where the Ninth Circuit rejected the First Circuit's "extreme departure" test from *Shaw v. Digital Equipment Corp.* and equated the duty to disclose interim developments with materiality) and (b) the scope of disclosure duties for "known trends or uncertainties" under Item 303 of Regulation S-K.
2. **The *ON24* panel explicitly relied on *Sodha* (*Robinhood*)**: In the 8-page unpublished memorandum disposition below (`decision-below.txt`, pp. 7–8), the Ninth Circuit panel cited *Sodha v. Golubowski* as its controlling authority on both the second and third questions presented in *ON24*:
   - On Section 11(a) misleading omissions (QP2), the panel cited *Sodha*, 154 F.4th at 1032, 1034, for the propositions that ON24's risk warnings did not "fairly warn" investors of pre-IPO churn and that materiality alone ("significantly altering the total mix of information") satisfied the omission standard (`decision-below.txt`, p. 7).
   - On Item 303 (QP3), the panel's entire two-sentence holding reversing the district court rested exclusively on *Sodha*: first, that a "trend" under Item 303 need not meet any "minimum duration" requirement (*Sodha*, 154 F.4th at 1037), and second, that Item 303 "imposes a broader duty of disclosure than Section 11's 'misleading' prong" and "requires more disclosure than the materiality test typically used in securities law" (*id.* at 1039) (`decision-below.txt`, pp. 7–8).
3. **Synchronized conference schedule**: In *Robinhood* (No. 25-944), the Court called for the views of the Solicitor General on June 1, 2026 (`cvsg_date: 2026-06-01`). After the Solicitor General filed the United States' amicus brief, *Robinhood* was distributed for the November 6, 2026 Conference—the exact same conference as *ON24*'s first post-BIO distribution—and on November 9, 2026, *Robinhood* was also relisted (`distribution_count = 3`) for the November 13, 2026 Conference. Petitioners in *ON24* highlighted this overlap in both their Petition (pp. 7, 25–34) and Cert Reply Brief (pp. 3, 10–12), expressly requesting that the Court hold *ON24* pending *Robinhood* if it does not grant plenary review in *ON24*.

## 3. Standalone Vehicle Assessment vs. Hold-and-GVR Pathway

Evaluating *ON24* across its two potential paths to a grant-family outcome (`granted` vs. `gvr`) explains both why plenary review in *ON24* is unlikely and why its overall `actual_granted` probability (`0.31`) exceeds the 25.10% `elevated` base rate:

### A. Weaknesses as a Standalone Plenary Vehicle (~12% probability of plenary grant)
- **Unpublished, interlocutory memorandum disposition**: Unlike *Sodha v. Golubowski* (a 60-page published opinion with a partial dissent by Judge Rawlinson), the decision in *ON24* is an 8-page non-precedential memorandum disposition remanding at the Rule 12(b)(6) pleading stage.
- **Absence of amicus support**: Despite experienced Supreme Court counsel (Kannon Shanmugam of Paul, Weiss for ON24 and underwriters), *ON24* attracted zero cert-stage amicus briefs (`amicus_briefs: null`), whereas *Robinhood* drew three private amicus briefs (Professor Joseph Grundfest, Washington Legal Foundation, and SIFMA/Chamber of Commerce) plus the Solicitor General's CVSG brief.
- **Fact-bound framing dispute on Question 1 (the *Facebook* issue)**: ON24's first question presented reprises the question granted and then dismissed as improvidently granted in *Facebook, Inc. v. Amalgamated Bank*, 604 U.S. 4 (2024): whether forward-looking risk-factor disclosures are misleading when they fail to disclose past or "near-certain" materialization of the risk. However, *ON24* suffers from the same threshold characterization dispute that complicated *Facebook*. As Respondent emphasizes (`brief-in-opposition.txt`, pp. 12–19), the Ninth Circuit panel affirmed dismissal of two of the three challenged risk disclosures precisely because they did not state or imply that the risk had not yet occurred (`decision-below.txt`, pp. 3–6), and reversed only as to a warning stating that customer churn "could occur in the future" when the complaint alleged ON24 was already experiencing a pre-IPO surge in non-renewals and down-sells (`decision-below.txt`, pp. 6–7). Having DIG'd *Facebook* in November 2024 over a nearly identical pleading dispute, the Court is unlikely to select an unpublished, amicus-free memorandum disposition to revisit QP1 on plenary review.
- **Inferior vehicle on Questions 2 and 3**: On Section 11(a) omissions and Item 303, *Robinhood* (No. 25-944) is the published decision that announced the Ninth Circuit law applied in *ON24*. Furthermore, on QP3, Respondent raises a plausible preservation objection (`brief-in-opposition.txt`, pp. 23–25) that ON24's Ninth Circuit appellate brief argued only the "two-month rule" for Item 303 trends rather than the materiality standard later decided in *Sodha*.

### B. Strength as a Hold-and-GVR Candidate (~19% probability of GVR)
- Because the *ON24* panel's Item 303 holding (`decision-below.txt`, pp. 7–8) and Section 11(a) omission analysis (`decision-below.txt`, p. 7) rest directly on *Sodha v. Golubowski*, if the Court grants certiorari in *Robinhood* (No. 25-944), standard Court practice is to **hold** *ON24* pending the merits disposition of *Robinhood*.
- Conditioned on *Robinhood* having received a CVSG and a post-CVSG relist, there is a high likelihood (~55–65%) that the Court grants certiorari in *Robinhood*, and if *Robinhood* is granted, a high likelihood that *ON24* is held for it (~50–55% unconditional probability of a hold, or ~30% unconditional probability of a hold where *Robinhood* is granted and reverses/vacates on grounds implicated in *ON24*).
- If the Court reverses or vacates *Sodha* in *Robinhood*—particularly on Item 303's disclosure standard or Section 11(a)'s omission standard—the Court would very likely **GVR** (*grant, vacate, and remand*) *ON24* in light of *Robinhood* so the Ninth Circuit can re-evaluate the revived Item 303 and Section 11(a) claims without its vacated *Sodha* precedent. Even though the *ON24* panel also relied on *In re Alphabet* for the risk-factor statement, the panel separately reversed the dismissal of the Item 303 claim solely under *Sodha* (`decision-below.txt`, pp. 7–8), making a post-*Robinhood* GVR the standard disposition if *Sodha* is disturbed.
- Conversely, if *Robinhood* is denied ( ~35–45%), or if *ON24* is denied outright without a hold because the Court views it as a fact-bound unpublished application (~20–25%), or if *ON24* is held for *Robinhood* and later denied after *Robinhood* is decided (~10–11%), *ON24*'s terminal disposition is `denied` (`granted = 0`).

### C. Synthesis of Headline Disposition Probability
Combining the two grant-family pathways yields:
- `P(plenary grant)` ≈ **0.12**
- `P(GVR after hold for Robinhood)` ≈ **0.19**
- `P(actual_granted = 1)` = **0.31** (`granted: 0`, `predicted_disposition: "denied"`, `confidence: 0.69`).

This places `probability` (`0.31`) 5.9 percentage points above the 10-Term `elevated` risk-set base rate (`0.2510`), reflecting the substantial GVR pathway created by the synchronized relist with *Robinhood* (No. 25-944), while keeping `denied` as the modal terminal disposition (`0.69`).
