# Forecast of the Court's action

The Court will most likely deny certiorari without a separate writing. I assign a 14% probability to any grant, including a grant-vacate-remand order. The petition's broader securities-disclosure issues will not, in the modal outcome, overcome the difficulties of using an unpublished pleading-stage decision whose result depends on what customer-churn risks the complaint plausibly alleges had already begun to occur.

## Procedure from the recorded state

The snapshot shows **one distribution and no CVSG**. I expect **one further distribution**, with an 82% probability that the count rises at all. The existing distribution occurred before the Court requested the response that was filed on September 21. A second entry will more likely reflect completion of the briefing cycle than a substantive relist after full consideration. Several additional relists are not my modal forecast.

I expect **no separate CVSG in ON24**; the probability of a new invitation is 4%. The related Robinhood petition's invitation, as described in the supplied filings, offers a more economical channel for obtaining the government's views on overlapping disclosure questions.

The modal disposition window is the next several weeks to a few months after October 6, 2026, following redistribution. A meaningful alternative is a hold for Robinhood extending well into 2027. A hold does not itself count as a grant, and I do not predict that the previously listed September 28 conference necessarily considered the completed briefing.

## If the Court grants

Conditional on any grant, I assign **65%** to disposition in the cert order itself. The principal route is a GVR following a relevant ruling in Robinhood; outright summary reversal is much less likely. That GVR forecast is conditional on a future ruling that materially affects the panel's reasoning, not an assertion that such a ruling already exists or will necessarily occur.

If the Court instead selects this petition for plenary review, I expect a narrowed version of question one: when warning of future business harm is misleading because the issuer already knows of customer churn, even before the alleged aggregate financial harm has occurred. I do not expect the Court to embrace the premise that the panel imposed liability for wholly unmaterialized risks without examining the distinction. Questions two and three are more likely to matter through a companion disposition than through a grant of all three questions as written. This question-selection prediction is conditional context, not a prediction of an unconditional merits opinion.

Conditional on denial, the probability of any noted dissent or statement respecting denial is **2.5%**. I expect none and make no individual-Justice writing prediction. A denial will leave the lower-court ruling standing without resolving the competing readings of Section 11 or Item 303.
