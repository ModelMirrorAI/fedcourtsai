# Forecast: what the Court will do with No. 25-1376, ON24, Inc. v. Leadersel Innotech ESG

**Bottom line.** The petition will most likely be **denied** (about 86%), but not soon.
The modal path runs through the companion petition *Robinhood Markets, Inc. v. Sodha*,
No. 25-944, where the Court called for the Solicitor General's views on June 1, 2026
and no brief had been filed as of this cell: the Court will most likely sit on this
petition until Robinhood's cert question is settled, and the single likeliest *grant*
form here is a GVR in light of a Robinhood merits decision rather than plenary review.

## Distributions and relists

The docket shows **one** distribution (July 15, 2026, for the September 28, 2026
conference), entered while respondent's waiver was on file. A Justice then **requested a
response** on July 22, which pulled the petition off that conference list; the brief in
opposition arrived September 21 and no reply or redistribution is on the docket as of
October 6. So the one distribution shown is a formality the Court never acted on.

- Expect petitioners to file a reply within days and the Clerk to **redistribute** the
  petition for an October or early-November 2026 conference. A further distribution is
  close to certain; only a Rule 46 dismissal or withdrawal before then would prevent it.
- Expect the petition **not** to be decided at that first real conference. The likelier
  course is that it is **held** (or quietly relisted/held without action) pending the
  Solicitor General's brief in Robinhood, which on the usual CVSG calendar lands in
  December 2026 or January 2027, with Robinhood's cert disposition following in
  January or February 2027. If Robinhood is granted, this petition is held through the
  Robinhood merits decision (likely by late June 2027), so the terminal distribution
  count here could reach three or more. Roughly a 55% chance the hold happens at all.
- If the Court instead takes it up on its own, expect **one** real conference and a
  denial, possibly after a single relist.

## CVSG

No CVSG here. The Court already has the Solicitor General's views coming in Robinhood on
the overlapping Section 11 / Item 303 questions, and it does not usually invite the SG
twice on one issue cluster. A separate invitation on question 1 (the Facebook
risk-factor question) is conceivable but unlikely, about 5%.

## Which question, if any

If the Court grants plenary review on this petition's own terms it would take
**question 1** (whether describing not-yet-materialized risks as hypothetical is a
misleading omission), the question it granted in *Facebook v. Amalgamated Bank* and then
dismissed as improvidently granted. It would most likely reformulate it to fix the
premise the BIO attacks (whether the risk had "begun to materialize"), and would leave
questions 2 and 3 to Robinhood. Plenary review here is unlikely, though: the decision
below is an unpublished memorandum disposition, the posture is interlocutory (reversal
of a dismissal, remand for further proceedings), and the BIO's vehicle argument, that the
panel found the risk had already started to materialize so question 1 is not presented,
is the same fact-bound dispute that sank Facebook at argument.

## Summary disposition versus plenary review

Conditional on any grant, a **summary disposition is the likelier route** (about 70%):
if Robinhood is granted and the Ninth Circuit's Sodha framework is reversed or vacated,
this petition, whose panel vacated submission to await Sodha and ordered supplemental
briefing on its effect, is a natural GVR candidate. A plenary grant as a companion to
Robinhood is possible but unlikely given the unpublished decision and the thinner record
of amicus interest here (none at the cert stage, against three in Robinhood).

## Dissent from denial

If denied, expect **no separate writing** (about 5% chance of one). Securities-disclosure
denials rarely draw dissents, the ruling below is non-precedential, and a denial that
follows a Robinhood denial would be unremarkable. A short statement respecting denial
from a Justice flagging the Ninth Circuit's risk-factor line is the only realistic form.

## Conditional merits note

If the Court ever reached question 1 on this record, it would most likely side with
petitioners' framing that a risk disclosure is not misleading unless the warned-of harm
has occurred or is virtually certain, consistent with the First, Third, and Tenth
Circuit decisions the petition cites. That is conditional on a grant that is unlikely.
