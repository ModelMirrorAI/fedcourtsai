## In brief

ON24 asks the Court to review a Ninth Circuit ruling that revived shareholder claims accusing the company of misleading IPO investors about customer turnover risks.

## What happened

ON24 runs a webinar and virtual-events platform that gained many pandemic-era customers. Leadersel Innotech ESG, representing a class of investors, sued under securities law, claiming ON24's 2021 IPO documents misleadingly described customer non-renewal as a mere future possibility when the company's internal data already showed that risk was materializing. A district court dismissed the case with prejudice. The Ninth Circuit's panel reversed most of that dismissal in an unpublished ruling, allowing the bulk of the claims to proceed, and later denied rehearing.

## What the Court is being asked

ON24 raises three questions about when a company's statements in offering documents become legally misleading. The first asks whether a company violates the law by describing a risk as merely hypothetical when, ON24 argues, the risk had not actually started happening yet. The second asks whether a company can be held liable for leaving out information related to something it disclosed, even if that omission did not make any specific statement misleading. The third asks whether a disclosure rule requiring companies to describe known business trends was violated here by requiring disclosure of facts ON24 says were not significant enough to matter. Leadersel answers that the lower court found the omitted facts were significant and did make ON24's statements misleading, so none of these questions is actually presented by this case.

## Where it stands

The petition has been distributed for conference twice, most recently for November 6, 2026, after full briefing was completed, including Leadersel's opposition brief and ON24's reply. The docket shows no decision yet on whether the Court will hear the case.

## What each outcome would mean

ON24, as petitioner, is asking the Court to take up the case. If the Court grants review, it would hear full arguments and decide the legal questions itself, deciding nothing in advance about who is right. If the Court denies review, the Ninth Circuit's decision reviving most of Leadersel's claims stays in place, and the lawsuit would continue in the lower courts. A denial would not mean the Court agrees with the Ninth Circuit's reasoning, only that the decision stands.
