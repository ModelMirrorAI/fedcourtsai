## In brief

ON24 asks the Court to review a Ninth Circuit ruling that let shareholder Leadersel's securities fraud claims over ON24's IPO disclosures go forward.

## What happened

Leadersel, representing a class of investors, sued ON24 and related parties, claiming ON24's 2021 IPO documents misleadingly described the risk of customer non-renewal as merely hypothetical when, according to the complaint, ON24 already knew some customers were not renewing and others had signaled they would not. A district court dismissed the case with prejudice, finding the allegations inconsistent and the risk disclosures adequate. The Ninth Circuit reversed in part, reviving most of the claims in an unpublished ruling, and later denied rehearing. ON24 then asked the Supreme Court to take the case.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked to decide whether a company's warning that a risk might happen in the future becomes misleading once that risk has started to occur, even if it hasn't fully played out. It is also asked whether a company can be held liable for leaving out information connected to something it disclosed, even if that omission did not make any specific statement misleading, and whether rules requiring disclosure of known business trends extend to facts the company says were not significant enough to matter.

## Where it stands

The petition has been distributed for multiple conferences, most recently one scheduled after the snapshot date, following full briefing including a response and a reply. The docket shows no action yet on whether the Court will hear the case.

## What each outcome would mean

ON24 is asking the Court to take up the case and reverse the Ninth Circuit's decision, which had revived Leadersel's lawsuit. If the Court grants review, it would hear full arguments and decide these disclosure questions itself, deciding nothing yet about who wins. If it denies review, the Ninth Circuit's decision stands, and Leadersel's lawsuit against ON24 continues in the lower courts. The Court could also hold the petition pending a related case, Robinhood Markets v. Sodha, without resolving it now.
