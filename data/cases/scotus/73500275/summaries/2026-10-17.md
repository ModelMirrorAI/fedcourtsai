## In brief

ON24 asks the Court to review a Ninth Circuit ruling reviving investors' securities-fraud claims over its 2021 IPO disclosures about customer retention.

## What happened

Leadersel Innotech ESG, representing a class of IPO purchasers, sued ON24 and related parties, claiming IPO documents misleadingly described customer loyalty and growth while omitting that many pandemic-era customers had signaled they would not renew. A district court dismissed the case with prejudice. The Ninth Circuit, in an unpublished decision, reversed as to most of the challenged statements, holding the complaint plausibly alleged that the risk of customer non-renewal had already begun to materialize before the IPO and that ON24's securities-filing disclosures should have said so. The court also revived a related claim that ON24 failed to disclose required information about known business trends. ON24 then asked the Supreme Court to review that ruling.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked whether a company's warning that a business risk "may" occur becomes misleading if the risk had already begun to happen, and whether that question conflicts with how other appeals courts handle similar risk warnings. The petition also asks whether a company can be held liable for leaving out information connected to something it did disclose, even without a showing that the omission made any specific statement misleading, and whether a company's required discussion of known business trends must cover information that may not be significant enough to matter to investors.

## Where it stands

The petition has been fully briefed, with a response and a reply filed, and the case was distributed for the Court's conference on November 6, 2026. No order on the petition has yet been recorded.

## What each outcome would mean

ON24 and other defendants are asking the Court to take up the case. If the Court grants review, it would hear full arguments and decide the disclosure questions itself, without yet deciding who wins. If the Court denies review, the Ninth Circuit's decision reviving Leadersel's claims stands, and the case would continue in the lower courts. The Court could also hold the petition pending its decision in a related case, Robinhood Markets v. Sodha, which raises overlapping questions; that would delay any decision here without resolving it either way.
