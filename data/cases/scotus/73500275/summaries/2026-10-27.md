## In brief

ON24 asks the Court to review a Ninth Circuit ruling that revived shareholder claims that its IPO materials misleadingly described customer non-renewal as a future risk.

## What happened

Leadersel, representing a class of ON24 shareholders, sued ON24 and its underwriters, claiming ON24's 2021 IPO documents misled investors by describing customer non-renewal as a hypothetical future risk when, the complaint says, ON24 already knew some customers signed during the pandemic intended not to renew. A federal district court dismissed the case. The Ninth Circuit's appeals panel, in an unpublished ruling, reversed in part, holding Leadersel had adequately alleged that ON24 knew the risk of customer "churn" had already begun before the IPO and disclosed it only as a future possibility. The panel also revived a related claim that ON24 failed to disclose required information about business trends. The appeals court declined to rehear the case.

## What the Court is being asked

ON24 asks the Court to decide whether a company's risk warnings can be considered misleading if they describe a business risk as merely possible in the future when, as ON24 disputes, the risk had not actually happened yet or was not close to certain. ON24 also asks whether companies can be held liable for leaving out information connected to a disclosed topic even if that omission does not make any specific statement misleading, and whether a regulation requiring disclosure of known business trends can require disclosing information ON24 says was not significant enough to matter.

## Where it stands

The petition has been distributed for conference twice, most recently for November 6, 2026, after full briefing including Leadersel's opposition and ON24's reply. The docket shows no record yet of the Court's decision on whether to hear the case.

## What each outcome would mean

ON24, which lost before the Ninth Circuit, is asking the Court to take up and reverse that ruling. If the Court grants review, it would hear full arguments and decide the disclosure questions itself, deciding nothing yet about who wins. If the Court denies review, the Ninth Circuit's decision reviving Leadersel's lawsuit stands, and the case would continue in the lower courts. ON24's reply brief also asks that, if not granted outright, the petition be held pending the Court's decision in a related case, Robinhood Markets v. Sodha, which raises similar questions.
