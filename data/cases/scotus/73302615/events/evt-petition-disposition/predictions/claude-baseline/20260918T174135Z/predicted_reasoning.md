# Forecast: what the Court will do with No. 25-1256

**State forecast from.** The docket shows two distributions: the first (for the
June 18, 2026 conference) produced a call for a response rather than a denial,
after respondent had waived; the second (August 26) placed the fully briefed
petition on the September 28, 2026 long conference. No CVSG is on the docket.
No amicus has appeared. Petitioner's former counsel filed a withdrawal letter
on August 26 and no counsel of record for petitioner appears on the docket.

**Disposition.** Denial is the likelier outcome, but this is a genuinely
contested petition, not a routine one: I put roughly a one-in-five chance on
the grant family. The petition presents an acknowledged split on whether the
Second Hickenlooper Amendment carries a domestic-nexus requirement, and the
decision below (Katsas, J., unanimous) says out loud that it departs from the
Second and Fifth Circuits. Against that, the Court will weigh the interlocutory
posture and the fact that the act-of-state holding was reached only through
pendent appellate jurisdiction on a collateral-order appeal of an immunity
ruling that petitioners do not challenge here. That vehicle problem, plus the
BIO's point that the issue has been outcome-determinative essentially never
outside this case in forty years, is what makes denial more likely than not.

**Relist.** The September 28 conference is the first at which the Court has
the BIO. I expect the petition to be distributed at least once more, more
likely than not: either because the Court calls for the Solicitor General's
views (which redistributes the petition after the SG files), or because the
Court relists it once or twice before granting or before a Justice finishes a
statement respecting denial. If the Court denies outright on October 5 with no
further distribution, that would be the single most common individual outcome,
but the combined paths to another distribution outweigh it.

**CVSG.** A real possibility, roughly one in three. The Court has invited the
Solicitor General in this exact line of FSIA-expropriation cases before
(this case's own 2016 trip, Philipp, Simon), the statute itself reserves a role
for the President's foreign-policy judgment, and the executive branch has an
unusually direct interest in litigation against PDVSA in 2026. If it comes, it
comes from the September 28 conference or the first October conferences, with
the SG filing in the spring of 2027 and the petition resolved late in OT2026.

**Which question, if granted.** The QP as written: whether a damages claim for
a foreign taking triggers § 2370(e)(2) where neither the property nor its
proceeds entered the United States. The Court would not reach petitioners'
new "based upon" argument, which the BIO correctly notes was not pressed
below. A grant would likely also direct the parties to address, or the Court
would itself first decide, whether the D.C. Circuit properly exercised pendent
appellate jurisdiction over the act-of-state defense.

**Summary disposition.** Very unlikely conditional on a grant. There is no
intervening decision to GVR in light of (Republic of Hungary v. Simon predates
and was applied by the decision below), and a fresh, acknowledged split is not
summary-reversal material. A grant means plenary review.

**If denied.** Most likely a bare denial. There is a modest chance of a
statement respecting denial, most plausibly one noting that the interlocutory
posture rather than the merits explains the denial, or a short dissent from a
Justice who views the D.C. Circuit's reading as an expansion of judicial
second-guessing of foreign sovereign acts.

**Withdrawal or dismissal.** A small but non-trivial tail: petitioner's
counsel has withdrawn and no successor appears, and the political control of
PDVSA has been in flux in 2026. A settlement or an abandoned petition would
resolve as dismissed or withdrawn rather than denied.

**If granted, the likely merits ground.** Conditional only: the Court's
textualist majority would find the Amendment's text contains no territorial
limit, as Simon's description of the statute already suggests, so a grant is
likelier to end in affirmance than reversal, which is itself a reason the Court
may see less need to take the case.
