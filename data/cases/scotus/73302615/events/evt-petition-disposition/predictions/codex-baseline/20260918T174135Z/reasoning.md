## Why 14%, rather than the 17.2% anchor

**Reasons for review.** The petition identifies a concrete territorial-scope disagreement, not merely alleged error in applying settled doctrine. It cites the Second and Fifth Circuits and the New York and Texas high courts, and reports the D.C. Circuit's express rejection of a domestic-nexus requirement. Its strongest presentation is at printed pages 3–5 and 21–23. This dispute can affect foreign-expropriation litigation and foreign relations. The June 8 call for a response after waiver is affirmative evidence of interest, though it is not a CVSG or proof of four votes.

**Reasons against review.** The opposition's principal responses are substantial: the conflicting authorities are decades old; the issue recurs infrequently; other jurisdictional and merits barriers limit its reach; and the case remains interlocutory. Most importantly, the act-of-state merits defense reached the court of appeals through pendent jurisdiction accompanying an immunity appeal. The opposition argues that resolving the proposed statutory question would first require accepting that jurisdictional extension. I treat this as a vehicle risk, not a proven jurisdictional defect. The opposition also says some new textual arguments were not raised below. That is not equivalent to forfeiture of the entire territorial-scope question. See opposition printed pages 10–18, 25–27, and 35.

**The two distributions overstate repeated merits consideration.** The first was followed by a response request before the scheduled conference; the next followed the requested opposition and summer interval. The mechanical count remains two, but I discount the inference that this is a petition surviving two fully briefed conferences. I do not multiply an independent relist bonus onto an anchor already conditioned on the band.

**Representation uncertainty modestly lowers review prospects.** I retrieved the precise August 26 letter linked by the snapshot. Former counsel reports that PDVSA instructed the firm that it was no longer authorized to act, that the firm would submit a withdrawal notice, and that it did not know whether substitute counsel would appear. This is withdrawal of representation, **not withdrawal of the petition**, and does not establish settlement or mootness. Nevertheless, uncertain continued advocacy makes this a less attractive vehicle. I give this a modest downward adjustment, not a presumption that the petition will disappear. The separate flag records this material additional input.

Taken together, the live disagreement and response request keep the probability well above a routine low-signal petition, but the vehicle and representation concerns outweigh a modest upward adjustment from those strengths. **14% is a judgmental probability, not a fitted estimate or a claim to percentage-point precision.** The most consequential uncertainties are how seriously the Court views the old conflict, whether it wants executive-branch input, and whether representation is regularized.
