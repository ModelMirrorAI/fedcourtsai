# Forecast

The Court will deny the petition. P(any grant), including a GVR or summary reversal, is **14%**. The principal reason to leave this case unreviewed is the combination of an old, infrequently litigated disagreement and an interlocutory vehicle with a pendent-appellate-jurisdiction complication. Denial would not endorse the D.C. Circuit's statutory interpretation.

## Further proceedings

Between them, the Court requested a response after a waiver, and the opposition was filed. No CVSG is recorded. The second distribution therefore does not establish that the Court considered the fully briefed petition twice.

- **Further distribution: 30%.** The modal forecast is zero additional distributions beyond the two recorded, followed by denial on an order list after the September 28 conference. If another distribution occurs without a CVSG, one additional conference is more likely than a long sequence of relists. A CVSG would produce a substantially longer timetable.
- **New CVSG: 16%.** The modal forecast is no invitation. If the Court seeks the Solicitor General's views, an invitation following the September 28 conference or an early autumn reconsideration is the likeliest timing. The foreign-relations consequences of the statutory question make consultation a meaningful alternative to immediate denial.
- **Summary route conditional on any grant: 8%.** Plenary briefing and argument are substantially more likely than a GVR or summary reversal if review is granted. The reviewed materials identify no newly intervening controlling decision that supplies a straightforward remand instruction; the 2025 Simon decision already predates the decision below.
- **Separate writing conditional on denial: 4%.** Expect an ordinary denial without a noted dissent or statement. No particular Justice is forecast to write.

## Question if review is granted

The Court would take the petition's single territorial-scope question substantially as presented: whether a damages claim for a foreign taking triggers 22 U.S.C. § 2370(e)(2) when neither the taken property nor property exchanged for it entered the United States. It would not expand this grant to reopen the separate FSIA immunity or personal-jurisdiction rulings that the opposition says petitioners have not challenged. Any merits opinion would need to distinguish the act-of-state exception from the FSIA's separate jurisdictional nexus requirements.

The August 26 counsel letter predicts neither a petition withdrawal nor a dismissal by itself. Denial remains the modal disposition despite the representation uncertainty it discloses.
