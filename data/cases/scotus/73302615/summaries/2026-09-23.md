## What happened

Helmerich & Payne International Drilling Co. (H&P), a U.S. energy company, sued Petróleos de Venezuela, S.A. and its subsidiary PDVSA Petróleo, S.A. after Venezuela nationalized H&P's Venezuelan drilling subsidiary in 2010, seizing its rigs and other property. H&P sought damages, arguing the taking violated international law. PDVSA argued the courts should stay out of the dispute under the act-of-state doctrine, which generally bars U.S. courts from judging the validity of a foreign government's acts within its own territory. The district court and the D.C. Circuit both ruled against PDVSA, holding that a federal law called the Second Hickenlooper Amendment removes that protection here. PDVSA asked the full D.C. Circuit to reconsider; that request was denied.

## What the Court is being asked

The law at issue strips away the usual rule against second-guessing a foreign government's taking of property when someone asserts rights to property based on a confiscation that violated international law. PDVSA argues this override should only apply when the seized property, or something exchanged for it, actually ends up in the United States — and here, everything stayed in Venezuela. H&P and the lower courts read the law's wording as containing no such location requirement. The Court is asked to decide which reading is correct.

## Where it stands

The petition has been distributed for conference more than once, and the respondent has filed a brief opposing review. A letter from PDVSA's former counsel regarding withdrawal from the case was also recently filed. The case has not yet been scheduled for argument, and the Court has not acted on the petition.
