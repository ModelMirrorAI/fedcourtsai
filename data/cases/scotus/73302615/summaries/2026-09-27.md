## In brief

Petróleos de Venezuela and a subsidiary ask the Court to review a ruling letting Helmerich & Payne pursue a lawsuit over Venezuela's seizure of its drilling business.

## What happened

Venezuela nationalized the Venezuelan drilling subsidiary of Helmerich & Payne International Drilling Co. in 2010, and Helmerich & Payne sued Petróleos de Venezuela (PDVSA) and its subsidiary in U.S. court, seeking damages. After years of litigation over jurisdiction, PDVSA argued the case should be barred by the act-of-state doctrine, which normally stops U.S. courts from judging the validity of a foreign government's official acts within its own territory. The district court and the D.C. Circuit both ruled against PDVSA, holding that a federal statute strips away that protection here even though the seized property never entered the United States. PDVSA asked the full D.C. Circuit to reconsider; that request was denied.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked whether a law Congress passed to narrow the act-of-state doctrine applies when a company sues a foreign government for money over property the government seized abroad, if that property, or anything received in exchange for it, never came into the United States. PDVSA says the law should not reach such cases; Helmerich & Payne says the law's wording contains no such limit.

## Where it stands

The petition has been distributed for conference more than once, and the respondent has filed a brief opposing review. The docket's most recent entry notes a letter from PDVSA's former counsel about withdrawing from the case; the Court has not yet acted on the petition.

## What each outcome would mean

PDVSA is asking the Court to take up the case. If the Court grants review, it would hear full arguments and decide whether the statute covers property that never reached the United States, without yet deciding who wins. If the Court denies review, the D.C. Circuit's decision stands, leaving Helmerich & Payne free to continue pursuing its damages claim against PDVSA in the lower courts.
