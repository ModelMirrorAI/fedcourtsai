## In brief

The Supreme Court declined to hear a Venezuelan state oil company's challenge to a ruling that let a U.S. drilling company's expropriation lawsuit proceed.

## What happened

Helmerich & Payne International Drilling Co., a U.S. company, sued Petróleos de Venezuela, S.A. and its subsidiary PDVSA Petróleo, S.A. after Venezuela nationalized the assets of H&P's Venezuelan subsidiary in 2010. PDVSA argued that a legal principle called the act-of-state doctrine, which generally bars U.S. courts from judging the validity of a foreign government's official acts, should block the suit because the seized property never entered the United States. The district court and the D.C. Circuit Court of Appeals both ruled against PDVSA, holding that a federal law called the Second Hickenlooper Amendment prevented the act-of-state doctrine from barring the claim regardless of where the property was located.

## What the Court is being asked

The question was whether a law limiting a court doctrine that normally shields a foreign government's official acts from judicial review applies to a damages claim even when the seized property, or anything exchanged for it, never came into the United States.

## Where it stands

The petition was distributed for conference twice, and the Court denied it on October 5, 2026, leaving the lower court's decision in place. Justice Kavanaugh did not take part in the decision.

## What each outcome would mean

PDVSA, the company that asked the Court to take the case, sought to overturn the D.C. Circuit's decision allowing H&P's lawsuit to continue. By denying review, the Court leaves that ruling in place: H&P's case against PDVSA can proceed in the lower courts, and the act-of-state doctrine does not block it even though the disputed property stayed in Venezuela. The denial does not mean the Court agreed with the lower court's reasoning; it simply leaves that decision standing.
