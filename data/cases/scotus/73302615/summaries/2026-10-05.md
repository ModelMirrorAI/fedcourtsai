## In brief

The Supreme Court declined to hear a Venezuelan state oil company's bid to block a U.S. driller's expropriation lawsuit on act-of-state grounds.

## What happened

Helmerich & Payne, a U.S. drilling company, sued Petróleos de Venezuela (PDVSA) and its subsidiary over Venezuela's 2010 nationalization of its Venezuelan drilling subsidiary's rigs and other property. After years of litigation over jurisdiction, PDVSA argued that courts should avoid judging the validity of Venezuela's actions under the "act-of-state doctrine," but a federal law called the Second Hickenlooper Amendment blocks that doctrine in certain property-rights cases tied to foreign takings. The district court and the D.C. Circuit both ruled against PDVSA, holding the law applied even though the property never reached the United States.

## What the Court is being asked

The question is whether a law limiting a court doctrine that normally shields foreign governments' official acts from judicial review applies even when the seized property, or anything traceable to it, never came into the United States. PDVSA says the law should not reach such cases; Helmerich & Payne says the law's wording does not require any connection to the United States.

## Where it stands

The petition was distributed for conference twice, and the Court requested a response from Helmerich & Payne after an initial distribution. On October 5, 2026, the Court denied the petition.

## What each outcome would mean

PDVSA and its subsidiary, as petitioners, asked the Court to take up and reverse the D.C. Circuit's ruling. By denying the petition, the Court leaves that ruling in place: Helmerich & Payne's lawsuit over the Venezuelan expropriation may continue, and the Second Hickenlooper Amendment's limit on the act-of-state doctrine applies even though the seized property stayed abroad. The denial does not mean the Court agreed with the lower court's reasoning; it simply leaves that decision standing, and the case returns to the lower courts for further proceedings.
