## Why 0.58 rather than the pooled 0.105

The upward adjustment is large and judgmental, not an estimated likelihood ratio. The requested and now-filed response demonstrates attention beyond an unexamined application, but neither act implies agreement with the applicants. The substance supplies the larger adjustment:

- The challenged remedy transfers extensive authority over an entire state prison healthcare system to a receiver for a minimum five-year term. The application describes approximately 25,000 patients across nine complexes and identifies staffing, contracting, and budget powers. Those consequences make preserving the status quo pending an already expedited appeal a substantial argument rather than an ordinary request to delay compliance.
- The application, principally pages 17–25, frames a statutory and remedial question: whether the court adequately considered recent improvements and less intrusive compliance measures before ordering a receivership. Its reliance on increased spending, staffing, and rollout of a changed care model is advocacy, not independently established proof that constitutional care has been achieved. Nevertheless, the record provides a plausible narrower basis for interim intervention without categorically rejecting receiverships.
- The asserted October 19 takeover precedes the Ninth Circuit's December argument. A stay could prevent an institutional handover followed shortly by another handover if the appeal succeeds. That practical asymmetry favors a temporary pause, although postponement also exposes prisoners to serious potential harm.

The strongest downward considerations are concrete, not boilerplate. The district court's August 5 order, Appendix 3–6, expressly disputes that it ignored post-2023 performance or tried no intermediate measures. It identifies monitors, hearings, status conferences, and a staffing pilot, finds ongoing unconstitutional care, and considers the resulting harm to prisoners greater than the claimed state injury. The application itself acknowledges earlier contempt proceedings and substantial fines; calling the receivership a first resort depends on the applicants' proposed reset of the relevant history in 2023. Spending more and hiring more do not by themselves refute findings about actual care. The Ninth Circuit majority denied a stay, subject to reconsideration by the merits panel. Judge Forrest favored only an administrative stay and referral to that panel, not an unconditional endorsement of the state's substantive position.

I give the application's CASA-based historical-equity theory limited independent weight: it is presented as an additional question, and a stay could rest on the narrower remedial issue. I have not independently verified the authorities cited in the filing. A general official-source search for the Nken stay standard returned no usable result; the standard and competing applications of it are available in the provisioned district court order. The missing opposition and truncated appendix argue against a highly confident grant forecast. Together, these considerations leave a modest grant lean, **0.58**, with denial still quite plausible. The number prices an unqualified disposing grant, not any interim assistance or eventual merits success.
