# Forecast: what the Court will do with application 26A370

**Posture at prediction time (snapshot 2026-09-25).** Arizona's corrections
director and health-services director applied on September 16, 2026 to stay the
District of Arizona's July 17, 2026 order appointing a receiver over the
State's prison health-care system, effective October 19, 2026. Justice Kagan,
as Circuit Justice, called for a response on September 18 (due September 25);
respondents (the *Jensen* plaintiff class and Disability Rights Arizona) filed
their opposition on September 25. The docket shows **no referral to the full
Court yet** and **zero amicus entries**.

## Disposition

**Denied, probability of an unqualified grant 0.35.** The application will be
referred by Justice Kagan to the full Court and decided by the Court before the
October 19 effective date, most likely in the window of about October 6 to
October 17, with no administrative stay needed because the order has not yet
taken effect.

The Court's likeliest order is a denial in one or two sentences, quite possibly
noting that the Ninth Circuit has expedited the appeal (argument in December
2026) and that its motions panel left the stay open to reconsideration by the
merits panel. A denial will probably draw a noted dissent or a short statement
from at least one of Justices Alito, Thomas, or Gorsuch, framed around the PLRA's
least-intrusive-means requirement and state sovereignty over prisons. Neither
the existence nor the authorship of any such writing is a scored claim here.

The alternative I give roughly one chance in three is a grant of a stay
"pending disposition of the appeal in the Ninth Circuit and, if a petition for
certiorari is timely filed, pending disposition of that petition", on a 6-3 or
5-4 line, with a dissent from the three Justices appointed by Democratic
presidents. A partial grant (for example staying only the Appointment Order's
provisions for waiving Arizona statutes while letting the receiver take office)
is conceivable but unlikely; under the pre-registered collapse it would resolve
as ungranted.

## Escalation ladder from here

- **Response requested.** Already on the record (September 18), so this rung is
  vacuous for the cell. I put 0.03 on any further call for a supplemental
  response after prediction time.
- **Referral to the full Court.** Not yet shown. Probability 0.90 that the
  matter is referred to the Court rather than decided by Justice Kagan alone:
  a state applicant, a response called for, a receivership over a state agency,
  and elite counsel are the profile the Circuit Justice routinely refers, and
  the eventual order will most likely recite "presented to Justice Kagan and
  by her referred to the Court".
- **Amicus entries.** Zero so far, nine days after the application and on the
  day the opposition was filed, which is when supporting amici usually appear.
  Arizona's legislative leaders filed as amici in the Ninth Circuit and could
  repeat that here, and a coalition of States is plausible on the federalism
  framing. Probability 0.35 that the count rises above zero before disposition.
- **Timing.** Applicants will file a reply within about two to four days; the
  Court will act in the first half of October. I do not expect the disposition
  to slip past October 19 without an administrative stay, and I expect no
  administrative stay because the Court has time to rule outright.

## If the Court grants (conditional)

The likely ground is that the applicants have shown a fair prospect that the
Ninth Circuit or this Court will hold the receivership was not the least
intrusive means under 18 U.S.C. 3626(a)(1)(A) where no post-injunction
contempt-and-purge cycle was tried, with the transfer of a core state function
to a private receiver treated as irreparable sovereign injury. The CASA
equitable-authority question would be mentioned at most in a separate writing;
respondents' brief shows it was not raised in the district court, and the
applicants themselves say the Court need not reach it.
