# Forecast of the Court's action

The Court will grant the application to stay the receivership orders pending further appellate proceedings. The probability of an unqualified grant is **0.62**. This is a forecast of interim relief, not of eventual reversal of the underlying injunction or a grant of certiorari. A mixed grant-and-denial disposing order counts against this grant probability under the interim resolver's convention.

The provisioned arrival record shows one submission entry, dated September 16, 2026, and no requested response, referral to the full Court, or amicus entries. From that frozen starting state:

- Justice Kagan will call for a response: probability **0.90**.
- The application will be referred to the full Court: probability **0.87**.
- The amicus count will increase beyond zero: probability **0.65**. I expect one or a few additional entries, likely addressing institutional remedies or state interests. Submitted briefs count, not just accepted ones.

I expect a disposing order before the requested October 19, 2026 takeover date, most likely in early or mid-October after a short response schedule. A temporary administrative stay may precede it, but the central forecast is a substantive stay pending appellate review, not merely a scheduling pause. Timing is a forecast, not a separately scored claim.

The likely order will be short rather than a plenary merits opinion. If the Court explains its action, I expect emphasis on avoiding an immediate transfer of state authority while the expedited appeals test whether the receivership satisfies the PLRA's limits and equitable necessity. The Court will not need to hold that federal courts categorically lack receivership authority or excuse the underlying constitutional violations. Its order will leave the 2023 injunction's obligations distinct from the disputed transfer of control. Denial remains a substantial alternative if the continuing patient harms and long history of unsuccessful remedies outweigh the State's status-quo argument.
