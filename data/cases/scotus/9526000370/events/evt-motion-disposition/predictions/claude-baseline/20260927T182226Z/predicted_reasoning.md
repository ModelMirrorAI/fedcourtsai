# Forecast: Thornell v. Jensen, No. 26A370 (application for a stay of a prison-healthcare receivership)

**Bottom line.** The Court will most likely **deny** the application, with an unqualified grant at roughly one chance in three. The receivership over Arizona's prison healthcare system will take effect on October 19, 2026 as scheduled, and the State's remedy will be the expedited Ninth Circuit appeal argued in December 2026.

## Procedural claims (the ones the docket resolves)

**Response.** My baseline shows the application submitted to Justice Kagan on September 16 with nothing after it. The Court *will* call for a response: a State applicant represented by Paul Clement seeking to stop the transfer of a core state function to a court-appointed receiver is exactly the shape a Circuit Justice never denies on the papers alone. (The live docket in fact already shows Justice Kagan requesting a response on September 18 and the respondents filing it on September 25, so this rung has fired; see `reasoning.md`.)

**Referral to the full Court.** The disposing order will recite that the application was "presented to Justice Kagan and by her referred to the Court." A single-Justice in-chambers disposition is unlikely here: the applicant is a sovereign State, the Ninth Circuit split 2–1 with Judge Forrest favoring an administrative stay, the application invokes last Term's *Trump v. CASA* and this summer's *Trump v. California* on interference with a sovereign's internal operations, and the relief sought would displace a state agency head. A Circuit Justice acting alone on that would be unusual for either outcome. I put referral at about 85%.

**Amicus filings.** The Arizona House Speaker and Senate President filed an amicus brief supporting the stay in the Ninth Circuit and are the obvious candidates to file here, possibly joined by a multistate coalition. Against that, eleven days have passed since the application without any amicus entry on the Supreme Court docket, and the response window has now closed; amici at the application stage usually appear within a week of filing. I expect the amicus count to rise past zero with about 40% probability, most likely by a single state-legislative-leadership brief.

**Timing and form.** The State asked for a decision before October 19, and the Court will honor that: expect the order in the window of roughly October 6–17, after the applicants' reply. A denial will be a one-line order, quite possibly with no noted dissent, though a short dissent from Justices Alito and/or Thomas is plausible. If the Court grants, the likeliest form is a stay "pending disposition of the appeal in the Ninth Circuit and, if a petition for certiorari is timely filed, pending disposition of that petition," with the three liberal Justices dissenting. An administrative stay while the full Court considers the matter is possible but not the modal path, because the effective date already sits comfortably after the briefing.

## Why denial is more likely than grant

- **The question is fact-bound.** The appointment of a receiver is an exercise of remedial discretion after fourteen years of litigation, two contempt findings, roughly $2.2 million in fines, a vacated settlement, and a three-week trial. The State's "legal error" framing (the court should have looked only at the post-2023 window and should have run one more contempt cycle) is really an argument about how the district court weighed the record, and *Brown v. Plata* names receivers among the remedies a court "must consider." Several Justices insist on certworthiness as a threshold for a stay, and a one-State, one-record dispute with no circuit split is a weak candidate.
- **The novel hook is disclaimed.** The *CASA* founding-era-antecedent argument is the one genuinely certworthy question in the application, and the application itself says the Court "need not resolve" it. The respondents credibly say it was never raised below. The Court rarely grants emergency relief on a question the applicant is not asking it to decide.
- **The appeal is already expedited.** Argument in December 2026, with the motions panel expressly inviting the merits panel to revisit the stay. The Court's in-chambers tradition treats a stay of a matter actively pending in the court of appeals as rarely granted.
- **The equities cut against the State.** The response documents preventable deaths and a finding of noncompliance with 131 of 154 injunction requirements. The harm the State identifies is the transfer of authority itself, which is reversible, and its unrecoverable-cost claim is unquantified.

## Why a grant is a real possibility

- The current majority has repeatedly granted emergency relief to sovereign applicants against lower-court orders it views as intruding on their internal operations, and the application frames the receivership squarely in those terms (a private individual exercising "all duties and authorities vested by law" in a Governor-appointed, Senate-confirmed director, with power to seek waivers of Arizona statutes). A State is the strongest class of applicant on this docket.
- The PLRA's least-intrusive-means text gives the State a statutory argument that does not depend on *CASA*, and a receiver given five years to do what the State was given under three is a rhetorically effective point.
- Judge Forrest's partial dissent supplies a ready-made intermediate disposition, and elite counsel raises the floor.

## Conditional merits content

If the Court grants, it will do so without opinion or with a brief per curiam resting on the PLRA's least-intrusive-means requirement rather than on *CASA*; it would not reach whether federal courts may ever impose a receiver on a non-consenting state agency. If it denies, no majority writing will accompany the order.
