## Baseline and adjustment

I computed the prescribed 2016–2025 pool from the committed `metrics/statpack.json` interim Term rows, cross-checking the corresponding markdown section: **31 unqualified grants / 296 resolved substantive applications = 10.473%**. The nonzero contributions are 2024 (14/70) and 2025 (17/226); the other eight eligible Terms contribute zero resolved observations. The pool exceeds the floor of 50. I excluded 2026 entirely and did not use the all-Term pack rate.

Coverage is a major qualification: 2024 has 972 unparsed applications, 2025 has zero, and the full eligible ten-Term window has 9,480 unparsed applications. These are committed-pack observations, not a freshly queried corpus estimate; the pack files inspected expose no build timestamp or newest-pull stamp, so their corpus vintage is unverified. I made no current-corpus or per-case last-pulled claim. The case-specific vintage is the provisioned September 18 snapshot; the application manifest records a September 23 fetch of a September 16 filing.

The raw pool is an anchor, not a response-request-conditioned rate. Selection favors applications already drawing attention. I move materially above it because this is a state application contesting a statewide transfer of executive, contracting, personnel, and fiscal authority, because the transfer has a concrete upcoming start date, and because a response was affirmatively requested. None of those facts supplies an empirical likelihood ratio; **0.60 is a judgmental forecast**, not a calibrated regression estimate or evidence of predictive skill against the pool.

The application presents two potentially substantial legal routes: the narrower PLRA/last-resort objection, and the broader historical-equity objection invoking CASA (application pp. 17–29). I treat the latter as the applicants' proposed extension, not an established rule that receiverships are impermissible. A stay could rest on the narrower concern while avoiding a categorical holding. The costs of switching control twice and the already-expedited appeal strengthen the preservation-of-control argument. Counsel's detailed presentation helps identify those issues but does not independently establish their correctness.

The strongest downward adjustment comes from the actual lower-court orders, not the applicants' characterization of them. In App.3–6 the district court says it considered performance under the 2023 injunction, found the defendants' compliance methodology flawed, and tried monitors, hearings, status conferences, and a staffing pilot. It finds that continued unconstitutional healthcare will substantially injure the prisoners. The claim that no lesser intervention was tried is therefore disputed, not an accepted fact. The Ninth Circuit denied a stay subject to merits-panel reconsideration; Judge Forrest favored an administrative stay and referral, not an express final determination that the State must prevail (App.1–2). That dissent supports a procedural pause more clearly than it supports ultimate success. These factors prevent a high-confidence grant prediction despite the sovereign and remedial stakes.
