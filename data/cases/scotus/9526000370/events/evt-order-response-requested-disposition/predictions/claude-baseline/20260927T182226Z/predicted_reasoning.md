# Predicted reasoning — Thornell v. Jensen, No. 26A370 (stay of prison-healthcare receivership)

**Forecast: the application is denied, most likely by the full Court after referral, before the receivership takes effect on October 19, 2026.** P(unqualified grant) = 0.20.

## What the Court will do with the application

1. **Referral.** Justice Kagan called for a response on September 18 and the response landed September 25. She will not act alone on a 189-page application from a State represented by Paul Clement seeking to stop a receivership over an entire prison health system. I expect the application to be **referred to the full Court** (probability 0.78), with the referral appearing on the docket once the applicants' reply is in, around the end of September or the first days of October. The residual is the chance Kagan denies in chambers with a short explanation pointing to the expedited Ninth Circuit appeal, which the Circuit Justices have done on matters the court of appeals is actively handling.

2. **No further call for a response.** The rung has already fired. A second request (for a supplemental filing) is rare on an application of this shape; I put it at 0.02.

3. **Amicus filings.** The docket shows **zero** amicus entries as of September 27. In the Ninth Circuit the Arizona Senate President and House Speaker filed as amici supporting the stay, and a multi-state coalition is the natural next filer at this Court. But eleven days have passed with nothing, the reply will close briefing within days, and application-stage amici usually appear in the first week. I put the chance that at least one amicus entry (Latin-form entry or "Amicus brief of X submitted") appears before disposition at **0.50**.

4. **Disposition and timing.** The Court will rule between roughly October 3 and October 17, honoring the applicants' request for a decision before October 19; an administrative stay is unlikely because there is enough time to decide the merits of the application. The most likely order is a **denial by the Court**. I expect a noted dissent or a separate statement from at least one Justice (Alito, Thomas, or Gorsuch are the likeliest) in perhaps a third of denial scenarios, given the receivership's federalism dimension and Judge Forrest's partial dissent below; a denial "without prejudice to renewal after the Ninth Circuit rules" is a plausible form. If the Court grants, it will be a stay pending the Ninth Circuit's disposition of the expedited appeal (argument calendared for December 2026) and, if the State loses there, the filing and disposition of a certiorari petition; a grant limited to that window is the shape a grant would take, and it would count as an unqualified grant under the interim resolver.

## Why denial is the likelier outcome

- **Posture.** Both courts below denied a stay, the Ninth Circuit expedited the appeal with argument in December, and the motions panel expressly left its denial "subject to reconsideration by the panel assigned to decide the merits." The Court is reluctant to step in ahead of a court of appeals that is moving quickly and has reserved the question.
- **Fact-bound merits.** The application's core argument is that the district court skipped intermediate steps and ignored post-2023 progress. Respondents answer with a fourteen-year record: two contempt findings, over $2 million in fines, a vacated settlement, a three-week trial, monitors' reports of preventable deaths, and a staffing pilot that met 22% of its requirements. Whether a receiver was the "least intrusive means" on that record is an abuse-of-discretion question, and the Court rarely grants emergency relief to correct one.
- **Certworthiness gap.** The application spends two sentences on certiorari. Its potentially certworthy hook, that *Trump v. CASA* casts doubt on federal equitable authority to impose receivers on non-consenting state institutions, was not raised in the district court and the application itself says the Court "need not resolve" it. *Brown v. Plata* names receivers among the remedies courts "must consider."
- **Harm framing cuts against the State.** The receiver was nominated by the Department itself, must endeavor to follow Arizona law, and needs the district court's leave to waive any state law; the injunction being implemented was stipulated to. That makes the irreparable-harm showing thinner than in the election and immigration applications the Court has been granting.

## Why 0.20 rather than lower

The current Court is receptive to federalism and PLRA-restraint arguments, a court-appointed private individual controlling a state agency's budget, staff, and contracts for five years is an unusually intrusive remedy, the applicant is a State with a bipartisan posture (Democratic Governor and Attorney General defending the Department), and a stay limited to the Ninth Circuit's expedited decision is a modest ask that avoids the "transfer and transfer back" problem the application stresses. A Ninth Circuit judge would have granted an administrative stay. Those factors keep a grant well within reach, but the pending-appeal posture and the fact-bound record make denial the better bet.

## Conditional merits note (context only)

If the Court does grant a stay and the case later reaches it on certiorari after the Ninth Circuit affirms, the question it would take is the PLRA "least intrusive means" question as applied to receiverships, not the CASA equitable-authority question, which is forfeited in this record.
