The Court has already called for a response (requested by Circuit Justice Kagan, due September 25, 2026). Given the significant federalism implications of a district court imposing a private receivership over a state prison system, I forecast that Justice Kagan will refer the application to the full Court.

Before the application is decided, I expect at least one amicus brief to be filed, likely by a coalition of states supporting Arizona's challenge to the broad use of equitable receivership powers.

I predict the Court will ultimately grant the stay. The applicants have established a strong likelihood of success on the merits, primarily by relying on the Court's recent decision in *Trump v. CASA* to argue that federal courts lack the equitable authority to force state institutions into private receiverships without consent. Furthermore, they present a strong case under the Prison Litigation Reform Act (PLRA) that the receivership is not the least-intrusive means necessary to correct the constitutional violations. The State has also demonstrated irreparable harm to its sovereign interests if the receivership takes effect. 

The application will likely be resolved within two weeks after the response is filed, probably in early to mid-October 2026. If the Court grants the stay, it may do so with a brief order referencing *CASA* and the PLRA constraints on structural injunctions. Expect dissents from the liberal wing of the Court emphasizing the district court's factual findings of the State's prolonged recalcitrance and failure to remedy constitutional violations in prison healthcare.
