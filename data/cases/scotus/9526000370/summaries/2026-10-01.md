## In brief

Arizona prison officials asked the Supreme Court to pause a court order putting a private receiver in charge of prison healthcare; the application was denied.

## What happened

In a long-running lawsuit over healthcare in Arizona's prisons, a federal district court found constitutional violations and issued a detailed injunction in 2023 requiring reforms. After finding continued noncompliance, the court ordered the prison healthcare system placed into receivership, stripping the Department's director of authority over healthcare and transferring it to a court-appointed receiver for at least five years. The Department appealed to the Ninth Circuit and asked both that court and the district court to pause the receivership while the appeal proceeds. Both courts refused, with one Ninth Circuit judge dissenting. The Department then asked the Supreme Court to step in.

## What the Court is being asked

The application asks whether the receivership order should be paused while the Department's appeal moves forward, on the grounds that a receivership is supposed to be used only as a last resort and the district court allegedly skipped less drastic options and did not adequately weigh the Department's progress. The underlying appeal separately raises whether courts have authority to impose this kind of receivership on a state prison system at all.

## Where it stands

Justice Kagan requested a response from the respondents, received their opposition and the Department's reply, and then denied the stay application on October 1, 2026.

## What each outcome would mean

The Department, which runs Arizona's prisons, asked the Court to pause the receivership. Granting the pause would have kept healthcare authority with the Department while its appeal continued. Denying it, which is what happened, allows the receivership to take effect and the receiver to assume control of prison healthcare operations while the Department's appeal proceeds in the Ninth Circuit, where the underlying dispute over the receivership's validity remains unresolved.
