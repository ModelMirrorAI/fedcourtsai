## In brief

Arizona prison officials asked the Supreme Court to pause a court-ordered receivership stripping them of control over prison healthcare while their appeal continues; a Justice denied the request.

## What happened

Prisoners in Arizona's prison system sued over inadequate healthcare years ago, leading to a 2023 injunction requiring sweeping changes. After finding Arizona had not come into compliance, a federal district court ordered the prison healthcare system placed into receivership, appointing a private receiver with broad authority over staffing, budgeting, and contracts for at least five years. Arizona officials, led by corrections director Ryan Thornell, appealed to the Ninth Circuit and asked both that court and the district court to pause the receivership pending appeal. Both courts refused, with one Ninth Circuit judge dissenting. Thornell then asked the Supreme Court to step in before the receivership was set to begin.

## What the Court is being asked

The application asks the Court to pause the receivership while the underlying appeal proceeds, arguing that handing control of a state prison system to a private receiver goes beyond what the law allows except as a true last resort, and that doing so here interferes with state sovereignty before the Ninth Circuit can decide whether the receivership was lawful in the first place.

## Where it stands

Justice Kagan, acting as Circuit Justice for the Ninth Circuit, requested a response from the prisoners, received replies from both sides, and then denied the stay application on October 1, 2026.

## What each outcome would mean

Thornell, representing Arizona's corrections department, sought the pause. With the application denied, the receivership order takes effect as scheduled, transferring authority over prison healthcare to the court-appointed receiver while the Ninth Circuit appeal continues on its expedited schedule, with oral argument set for December 2026. Had the application instead been granted, the receivership would have been paused and the department would have retained control of prison healthcare in the meantime. Either way, the denial does not decide the appeal itself, which still proceeds in the Ninth Circuit.
