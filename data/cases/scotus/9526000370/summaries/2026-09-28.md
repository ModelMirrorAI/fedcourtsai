## In brief

Arizona prison officials ask the Supreme Court to pause a court order placing the state's prison healthcare system under a private receiver while their appeal proceeds.

## What happened

Since 2012, prisoners in Arizona's state prisons have been suing over healthcare conditions, resulting in a 2023 injunction requiring extensive reforms. In February 2026, after finding continued noncompliance, the district court ordered Arizona's prison healthcare system into receivership, later appointing a named individual with sweeping authority over healthcare staffing, budgeting, and contracts. Arizona officials appealed to the Ninth Circuit and asked both the district court and the Ninth Circuit to pause the receivership pending appeal. Both courts refused, with one Ninth Circuit judge dissenting. The state then asked the Supreme Court to step in before the receivership takes effect.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked whether to pause the receivership order while the state's appeal moves forward, based on whether the state has shown it is likely to win that appeal, would suffer harm without a pause, and whether a rule limiting prison remedies to the least intrusive measures necessary permits placing a state prison system into receivership as a first response rather than after trying less drastic steps.

## Where it stands

The application for a stay was submitted to Justice Kagan, who requested a response from the prisoners, and that response has been filed. The docket shows no ruling yet on the stay request.

## What each outcome would mean

Arizona officials are asking the Court to pause the lower court's order. If the Court grants the stay, the state's Department of Corrections would keep control of prison healthcare while the Ninth Circuit appeal continues, and the receiver would not take over. If the Court denies the stay, the receivership would proceed as scheduled, transferring healthcare authority to the appointed receiver while the appeal continues. Either way, this decision would not resolve the underlying appeal, which continues separately in the Ninth Circuit.
