# Forecast of the Court's action

The Court will most likely deny certiorari after obtaining New York City's response. The response request will lead to renewed consideration, but not necessarily four votes to review the case. The principal obstacle is the Second Circuit's characterization of the case as a failure to supply evidence at summary judgment, rather than a clean disagreement about the constitutional status of electronic weapons.

The snapshot contains one distribution, entered August 19 for the September 28, 2026 conference. The September 8 response request followed the City's waiver. No opposition or CVSG appears in this record. Expect at least one additional distribution after the response deadline or subsequent briefing, usually followed by denial without an extended series of further relists. The 0.995 distribution-increment probability measures another distribution entry, not a substantive relist after an actual conference; the response-requested moment masks this nearly automatic increment.

No invitation to the Solicitor General is expected: P(a new CVSG) is 0.015. The dispute concerns local and state electronic-weapons restrictions and a constitutional threshold issue, rather than an identified federal administrative or statutory interest requiring the government's views.

There is a substantial minority chance of relief: P(any grant) is 0.32. Conditional on a grant, an order-stage disposition is more likely than plenary briefing and argument, with probability 0.72. The most plausible favorable route is a grant, vacatur, and remand for reconsideration in light of Wolford as described in the petition; a summary merits reversal is a secondary possibility. The petition identifies Wolford as postdating the April judgment and clarifying the threshold inquiry. A hold for Viramontes, whose grant the petition reports, is another possible intermediate step, not a separate final disposition.

If plenary review occurs, expect the Court to focus on the first question presented: whether plaintiffs must prove common use before a weapons ban implicates the Second Amendment's text. The second question, concerning electronic arms specifically, supplies the concrete application. The Court would be less likely to decide the entire historical justification for the bans under the broader third question without further proceedings below. If the petitioners prevail, the likely ground is that treating common use as a plaintiff's threshold evidentiary burden improperly foreclosed the constitutional inquiry; that would not itself establish that every electronic-weapons restriction is invalid.

Conditional on denial, a noted dissent or statement respecting denial has probability 0.26, but silence remains the leading forecast. Thomas and Alito are the likeliest separate writers, given the weapons-ban issue and the petition's reliance on Alito's Caetano concurrence. Their unconditional order-writing probabilities are 0.14 and 0.08. The remaining Justice-specific probabilities are lower and are independent probabilities, not an allocation of one authorship slot. No majority-opinion author or cert vote lineup is forecast.

If the Court denies without a hold, expect disposition within roughly one to three months after responsive briefing is complete. A hold for another weapons case could extend the petition into the following spring or summer. This is a forecast from the provisioned response-request record, not a report of anything that occurred after it.
