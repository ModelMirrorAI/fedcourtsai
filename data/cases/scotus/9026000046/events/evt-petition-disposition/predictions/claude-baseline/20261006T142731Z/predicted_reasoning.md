# Forecast: what the Court will do with No. 26-46, Calce v. City of New York

**Bottom line.** The Court will not decide this petition on its own merits any time soon. It will hold it for *Viramontes v. Cook County* (No. 25-238, consolidated with *Grant v. Higgins*, No. 25-566; argued December 2, 2026) and, once those cases are decided, dispose of it in the cert order itself. The modal outcome is a **grant, vacate, and remand** in light of *Viramontes*/*Grant*, most likely in late June or early July 2027. Plenary review of this case is unlikely; outright denial is the main alternative.

## Procedural claims that resolve against the docket

**Relists.** The docket shows **one distribution** (August 19, 2026, for the September 28 conference). The City waived; the Court called for a response on September 8 and then extended the response date to November 23, 2026. The petition will be redistributed once the brief in opposition and reply are in, so I expect at least one further distribution with near certainty, most likely for a January 2027 conference. After that it will sit as a hold: expect a long gap with no docket activity rather than serial relists, and then a final distribution or an order at the end of OT2026 after *Viramontes*/*Grant* come down. So: at least one more distribution (≈97%), and more likely two to three over the life of the petition, with the last one coming after the companion decision.

**CVSG.** No. No federal party, no federal statute, and the Court has not invited the Solicitor General into state-law arms-ban cases of this kind; with a directly relevant merits case already pending, there is nothing a CVSG would add. ≈3%.

**Which question the Court would take.** If the Court ever granted plenary review, it would take **QP 1** (whether a challenger must show an arm is "in common use" to bring a ban within the Second Amendment's plain text), possibly merged with QP 2 as applied to stun guns and tasers. It would not take QP 3 (the ultimate constitutionality of the bans) as a free-standing question, because the courts below never reached step two. But plenary review is the minority path: the same methodological question is already before the Court in *Viramontes*/*Grant* (review limited to whether the Second and Fourteenth Amendments protect AR-15-platform rifles "in common use"), and *Grant* comes from the Second Circuit under the very framework (*Antonyuk*, *NAGR v. Lamont*, *Gomez*) the summary order below applied.

**Summary disposition versus plenary review.** Conditional on any grant, the route is overwhelmingly the cert order itself (≈88%). The most likely form is a GVR "for further consideration in light of" *Viramontes* and *Grant*, which the petition itself asks for in the alternative. A *Caetano*-style summary reversal is possible but less likely than a GVR: the Court has a pending vehicle on the methodological point and will prefer to let the Second Circuit apply it, and the summary order below framed its holding as a Rule 56 evidentiary failure, which gives the Court a reason to remand rather than reverse outright. A plenary grant, whether as a companion for argument in spring 2027 or after *Viramontes* leaves the step-one placement question open, is the residual ≈12% of grants.

**Timing.** Response due November 23, 2026; reply about two weeks later; redistribution for a January 2027 conference; held through the argument and decision in *Viramontes*/*Grant*; disposition in the clean-up orders at the end of June or the first days of July 2027.

**If denied: dissent from or statement respecting denial.** A denial would most plausibly come in the *Viramontes* clean-up orders if that decision cuts against petitioners or leaves the step-one question untouched. Conditional on denial, I put the chance of some separate writing at ≈45%: Justice Thomas (joined by Justice Alito in *Caetano*) has written on stun-gun bans and on the Court's handling of arms-ban petitions, and Cooper & Kirk will frame the denial as abandoning *Caetano*; against that, a denial that follows a fresh merits opinion on common use is the kind the Court usually lets pass in silence.

## Conditional merits content

If the Court reaches the merits in this case, it will hold that "common use" is not a plain-text precondition and that stun guns and tasers are "arms" under *Heller* and *Caetano*, placing the dangerous-and-unusual inquiry at the historical step with the burden on the government. That is a forecast about the Court's reasoning only in the unlikely plenary branch; on the GVR branch the Court will say nothing beyond the citation to *Viramontes*/*Grant*.
