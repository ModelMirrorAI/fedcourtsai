# Forecast of the Court's action

I predict a grant, vacatur, and remand rather than plenary review or a final
Supreme Court judgment invalidating the bans. The probability of any grant,
including a GVR or summary reversal, is 60%. The likely remand asks the Second
Circuit to reconsider its threshold common-use requirement in light of the
intervening Wolford decision described in the petition. A hold for the related
arms case identified in the petition is a secondary route to eventual remand.

The snapshot records exactly one distribution, on August 19, 2026, for the
September 28 conference. The Court then requested a response on September 8;
the response deadline was extended to November 23, 2026. I expect a further
distribution after response and reply briefing, most likely followed by zero
to two additional distributions before disposition. My probability of at least
one additional distribution is 95%. This forecasts the recorded distribution
increment, not necessarily a substantive relist after actual conference
consideration: redistribution after a requested response can satisfy it.
I expect disposition after the November response deadline, potentially in
early 2027 or later if the petition is held for related litigation.

No CVSG is recorded. I predict none, with a 1% probability of a new invitation:
the dispute concerns a constitutional challenge to local arms restrictions,
and the Court can obtain the relevant adversarial presentation from the City.
A request for the City's response is not a request for the Solicitor General's
views.

Conditional on a grant, I assign 85% to disposition in the cert order itself,
rather than scheduling briefing and argument. A GVR is more likely than a
summary reversal because reconsideration of the allocation of proof need not
finally decide whether the bans survive historical analysis. If the Court
instead grants plenary review, I expect it to focus on Question 1: whether
challengers must establish common use to invoke the Amendment's textual
coverage. Question 2 is closely related; I do not expect the Court to reach
Question 3's ultimate validity of both bans without first resolving the
threshold issue. Conditional on a merits decision favorable to petitioners,
the likely ground separates the definition of protected arms from the
historical justification for regulating them, leaving the latter for remand.

If review is denied, I expect an unexplained order rather than a separate
writing, but assign 30% to some Justice noting a dissent or a statement
respecting denial. I do not forecast individual cert votes or authorship.
