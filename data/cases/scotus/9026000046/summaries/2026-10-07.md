## In brief

Gun rights advocates ask the Court to decide who must prove a weapon is common enough for constitutional protection before a ban on it can be challenged.

## What happened

Several individuals and two gun-rights groups sued New York City and New York State officials, arguing that laws banning possession of stun guns and tasers violate the right to keep and bear arms. One plaintiff, Amanda Kennedy, was charged after using a stun gun to fend off an attacker. The district court ruled for the city, holding that the plaintiffs had not proven stun guns and tasers are "in common use," a showing the court said was required before the challenged laws could even be considered to affect Second Amendment rights. The Second Circuit Court of Appeals affirmed, agreeing that the plaintiffs bore that burden and had not met it with the evidence they submitted.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked to decide whether someone challenging a ban on a category of weapon must first prove the weapon is commonly owned before a court will even consider whether the ban touches on the right to keep and bear arms, or whether that common-ownership question instead comes up later, as something the government must address in defending the ban. The petition also asks the Court to decide, more specifically, whether bans on stun guns and tasers affect that right at all, and whether these particular bans are unconstitutional.
