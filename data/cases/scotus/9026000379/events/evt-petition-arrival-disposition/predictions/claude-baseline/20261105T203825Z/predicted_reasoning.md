# Forecast — Genalo v. D.C., No. 26-379

- **Disposition:** Certiorari before judgment granted (about 80%). The case would be set for plenary briefing and argument, most likely in the January–April 2027 window, as the replacement for *Genalo v. Black* (No. 25-886). The Court granted that case in June 2026, and it was dismissed in September 2026 before argument.
- **Distributions:** None so far. The petition will be distributed once briefing closes after the Dec 8 response, and I expect a decision at its first or second conference. One relist at most is likely. A short hold is possible if the Court is coordinating with the related §1225 detention petitions.
- **CVSG:** None. The Solicitor General is the petitioner.
- **Question taken:** Both QPs as written: whether prolonged §1226(c) detention can trigger a due-process bond hearing, and the burden and standard of proof at such a hearing. The Court will probably not narrow the grant to the statutory §1225 question, despite the respondents' counsel asking it to.
- **Summary disposition:** Unlikely. No intervening decision resolves the question. A GVR becomes plausible only if a related grant is decided first.
- **If denied:** Likely only because of mootness concerns or a preference for awaiting the Second Circuit's judgment. A dissent or statement is plausible (about 30%), most likely from Justice Alito or Justice Thomas.
- **If granted, likely ground:** The Court would address whether *Jennings v. Rodriguez* and *Demore v. Kim* leave room for an as-applied due-process bond hearing claim. The government's position (no such point, or at least no clear-and-convincing burden on the government) has a fair prospect of prevailing on at least the burden question.
