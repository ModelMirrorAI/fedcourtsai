## In brief

Iowa's attorney general asks the Court to revive the state's law criminalizing immigrants' reentry after removal, which lower courts blocked as conflicting with federal immigration authority.

## What happened

Iowa enacted a law making it a state crime for a noncitizen to reenter or be found in Iowa after removal, requiring judges to order such people to leave the country, and barring courts from pausing prosecutions while federal immigration proceedings continue. Iowa Migrant Movement for Justice and two individuals sued, arguing the law is unconstitutional because federal law controls immigration. A federal district court blocked the law, and the Eighth Circuit affirmed, finding the challengers likely to succeed because the law conflicts with federal immigration authority. Iowa's attorney general, the only defendant who appealed, asked the Supreme Court to review that decision.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked two questions. First, whether people suing before any enforcement have been injured enough to bring the case, when the state's attorney general says she will not enforce the law against them. Second, whether a state law that largely mirrors federal immigration crimes can be blocked entirely because it might sometimes limit federal officials' discretion not to prosecute, even though the challengers do not claim lower courts disagree with each other on this point.

## Where it stands

The petition has been distributed for several conferences since September 2026, most recently rescheduled before being distributed again; on October 19, 2026, the Court invited the Solicitor General to file a brief giving the federal government's views. No decision on whether to hear the case has been recorded.

## What each outcome would mean

Brenna Bird, Iowa's attorney general, asks the Court to take up the case and reverse the Eighth Circuit. If the Court grants review and later rules for Bird, Iowa's law could take effect, allowing the state to prosecute reentry independently. If it rules for Iowa Migrant Movement for Justice instead, the law would remain blocked. If the Court denies review, the Eighth Circuit's decision blocking the law stays in place, and the case would continue in the lower courts. The Solicitor General's invited brief will inform whether the Court takes the case at all.
