## In brief

Iowa's Attorney General asks the Court to revive a state law criminalizing immigrants' reentry after the Eighth Circuit blocked it as conflicting with federal immigration law.

## What happened

Iowa passed a law making it a state crime for a noncitizen to enter or be found in Iowa after being deported or denied admission, and requiring judges to order such people to leave the country. Iowa Migrant Movement for Justice and two individual residents sued, arguing the law is unconstitutional because federal law controls immigration enforcement. A federal district court blocked the law, and the Eighth Circuit Court of Appeals agreed, affirming the block and rejecting rehearing. Iowa's Attorney General, the only defendant who appealed, asks the Supreme Court to take the case.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked two questions. First, whether the people challenging the law had the right to sue before it was ever enforced against them, given that Iowa's Attorney General says the law does not actually apply to them. Second, whether a state law that largely copies federal immigration crimes can be struck down entirely just because it might sometimes limit federal officials' discretion not to prosecute, even though state and federal criminal laws often overlap in other areas.

## Where it stands

The petition has been fully briefed, including an opposition brief, several friend-of-the-court briefs, and a reply. It was distributed for a late-September 2026 conference, rescheduled, and then distributed again for an October 2026 conference; the docket does not yet show a decision.

## What each outcome would mean

Iowa's Attorney General is asking the Court to hear the case. If the Court grants review, it will decide the standing and preemption questions itself, with no outcome yet determined for either side. If the Court denies review, the Eighth Circuit's decision blocking Iowa's law stands, leaving the challengers' legal victory and the injunction against enforcement in place. Either way, the order so far is only about whether the Court will hear the case, not who ultimately wins.
