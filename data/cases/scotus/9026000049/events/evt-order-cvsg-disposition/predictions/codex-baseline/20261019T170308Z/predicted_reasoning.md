# Forecast of the Court's action

The Court will grant plenary review after receiving the Solicitor General's views. The probability of any grant, including a GVR or other grant-family disposition, is **0.58**. The principal attraction is the boundary between permissible state assistance in immigration enforcement and an independently administered state criminal and removal scheme, together with the breadth of facial preemption relief.

The baseline contains **three distribution entries**, for the September 28, October 9, and October 16, 2026 conferences. The first two were followed by rescheduling entries; they should not be described as three completed substantive conference considerations. From that recorded count of three, I predict at least one additional distribution with probability **0.98**. The central trajectory is a distribution after the United States files its brief, followed by one further distribution before disposition: approximately two additional distribution entries. I expect resolution in spring or early summer 2027, with a later decision possible if the Court awaits related litigation. This is a forecast horizon, not a scheduled date.

A CVSG was already entered on October 19, 2026. I predict no new CVSG request; the structured increment is **0.00**, and this already-triggered claim is vacuous for scoring. The next relevant development will be the government's response to the existing invitation. I expect that response to favor review or other relief for Iowa, given the federal position described in the petition's account of the rehearing proceedings. That recommendation remains a forecast, not an observed filing.

If granted, the Court will principally address the second question: whether federal immigration law permits facial obstacle preemption of Iowa's parallel illegal-reentry regime on the ground that state enforcement interferes with federal enforcement discretion. I expect the standing question also to remain in the case as a threshold issue, rather than assume the Court can bypass it. The forecast is a grant on both questions, with the preemption question driving the case's significance. A decision limited to standing remains a material alternative.

Conditional on a grant, plenary review is the expected route: **0.18** for disposition in the cert order itself and **0.82** for a proceeding beyond that order. The competing standing theories and the distinction between parallel criminalization and state-directed removal favor briefing and argument rather than immediate summary reversal. A GVR could become appropriate if an intervening decision changes the governing framework, but none is established in the baseline as a controlling new ground requiring that route.

Conditional on denial, I predict no separate writing as the more likely result, while assigning **0.35** to some dissent from or statement respecting denial. This is an aggregate existence forecast, not a forecast of a disclosed cert vote count or an assignment to an individual Justice.
