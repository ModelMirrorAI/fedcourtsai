# Forecast — Bird v. Iowa Migrant Movement for Justice (No. 26-49)

**State forecast from.** The docket shows three distributions: for the 9/28 and 10/9
conferences (both rescheduled before they were held), then for 10/16. On 10/19 the
Court invited the Solicitor General to file a brief expressing the views of the
United States. So the petition is now at its CVSG; no further conference will be
held on it until the SG's brief is in.

**Disposition: granted (P ≈ 0.55).** Plenary review is the likelier outcome, most
likely once the SG brief is filed and the petition is redistributed. That would be
a winter or spring 2027 conference, depending on how fast the SG files.

**Relisting.** After the SG's brief and the parties' supplemental briefs, expect the
petition to be distributed again at least once (P ≈ 0.9). A CVSG petition is almost
always put back on a conference list. Withdrawal or mootness before then is the
only realistic way it is not: Iowa could amend SF2340, or the district court's
CASA remand could narrow the injunction. Expect one or two further distributions
before disposition.

**CVSG.** Already issued (10/19/2026). A second CVSG will not issue.

**Expected SG position.** The United States first sued Iowa over SF2340, then
dropped that suit. It later filed an amicus brief supporting Iowa's request for
rehearing en banc, saying SF2340 furthers federal immigration purposes. So I expect
the SG to recommend a grant, at least on QP 2 (obstacle preemption through federal
prosecutorial discretion). It may also recommend QP 1 or suggest the Court
reformulate the question.

**Question taken, if granted.** Most likely QP 2, either as written or reframed
around *Arizona v. United States* and whether a state crime with elements
coterminous with 8 U.S.C. § 1326 is facially obstacle-preempted. QP 1 (credible
threat of enforcement when the State disavows some applications) may ride along.
The Court could also take only QP 2 and leave standing for the remand. Context
only.

**Summary route.** Unlikely (≈ 0.08 conditional on a grant). No intervening decision
calls for a GVR, and a summary reversal of a published circuit preemption ruling
on a question this contested is improbable.

**If denied.** A denial would most likely mean the SG recommended denial on vehicle
grounds: the preliminary-injunction posture, the pending CASA remand, and the
respondents' point that at least one plaintiff has standing on any view. If it
is denied, I expect a dissent from denial or a statement respecting denial
(≈ 0.35), most plausibly from Justice Alito or Justice Thomas, with Justice
Gorsuch possible.
