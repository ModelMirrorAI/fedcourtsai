# Forecast

The Court will most likely deny the petition. I assign **40% to any grant**, including a GVR or summary reversal; the categorical forecast is denial, not an adjudication endorsing the lower court's merits reasoning.

The snapshot contains **two distributions**: September 2 for the September 28, 2026 conference, and October 5 for the October 9 conference. A September 28 entry says the petition was rescheduled. Thus the record establishes two distributions, but not two completed substantive conferences.

- **Further distribution: 58%.** Expect one additional distribution beyond the two recorded, rather than an extended sequence of repeated relists. Additional examination of the standing vehicle and the federal government's interest is more likely than immediate disposal at the next conference. On the no-CVSG path, the expected decision window is the next several conferences, during October or November 2026.
- **New CVSG: 30%.** No invitation appears in the baseline. The modal prediction is no CVSG, but an invitation within the next several conferences is a substantial alternative because the petition concerns federal immigration authority and the parties describe federal support for Iowa below. If an invitation issues, expect disposition to be delayed for months rather than weeks.
- **If review is granted**, the principal issue will be QP 2: whether the federal immigration scheme permits Iowa's parallel illegal-reentry and removal regime, including whether the challenged provisions may be facially enjoined despite asserted lawful overlapping applications. Expect attention to the actual statutory regime rather than an abstract rule about all overlapping state and federal criminal laws. QP 1's enforcement-disavowal issue remains a threshold vehicle question; it is less likely to become the sole reason for plenary review because the asserted standing of an organizational member may survive the proposed disavowal rule.
- **Summary route conditional on a grant: 12%.** Plenary briefing and argument are much likelier than a judgment in the cert order. The competing statutory theories, standing record, and scope of the injunction resist a straightforward summary correction. No identified intervening controlling decision in the materials supplies a concrete GVR trigger.
- **Separate statement or dissent conditional on denial: 30%.** Expect an unexplained denial without a separate writing in the modal outcome. The competing state-sovereignty and obstacle-preemption positions nevertheless make a recorded objection plausible. This forecast is aggregate; it does not assign cert votes or a particular author.

Denial is the modal endpoint because the Court can leave these issues to further development rather than choose this preliminary-injunction record as the vehicle for a broad immigration-preemption ruling. If it grants, the merits dispute will concern the scope of federal exclusivity and the limits of facial relief; no unconditional merits holding or majority opinion is forecast here.
