# Forecast: Bird v. Iowa Migrant Movement for Justice, No. 26-49

**Bottom line.** P(any grant) = 0.38. The modal outcome is still a denial, but this is a far-above-baseline petition: a state Attorney General petitioner, a 25-State amicus brief, two Senators and four organizational amici at the cert stage, a published dissental calling the question "about as important as it gets," and a federal government that told the Eighth Circuit the Iowa law furthers federal purposes. If the Court takes a state illegal-reentry case this Term, this is a plausible vehicle; the main things holding it back are the absence of a merits circuit split and the pending Tenth and Eleventh Circuit decisions on the Oklahoma and Florida laws.

## Relists

The docket shows **two distribution entries** (distributed for the 9/28 conference, rescheduled, re-distributed for the 10/9 conference). The petition has therefore been **considered zero times**: the first entry was a pre-conference reschedule, not a relist after consideration. I forecast from that state.

- I expect the petition to be **distributed at least once more** after 10/9 (P = 0.55). Grants almost always follow at least one relist under current practice, a CVSG produces a fresh distribution when the SG's brief arrives, and a dissent from denial usually costs a relist or two as the writing circulates. A quiet denial on 10/14 is the main path to no further distribution, and I put that at a bit under half.
- Conditional on a grant without a CVSG, expect one to three relists and a grant order in late October or November 2026, with argument in the spring sitting. Conditional on a CVSG, expect the SG's brief around spring 2027 and a decision on the petition in May or June 2027.

## CVSG

The United States is not a party (it dismissed its own suit in 2025) but has a stated interest: it supported Iowa's rehearing petition as amicus below and has filed in support of Florida's parallel law. No SG amicus brief was filed at the cert stage. A **CVSG is a real possibility but less likely than not** (P = 0.25): the Court often invites the SG on preemption questions where the United States is absent, but here the government's position is already on record and the Court may see no need to wait on it. The reschedule before the first conference is consistent with the Court lining the case up beside something else (a companion petition or an invitation), but I do not read it as strong evidence either way.

## Which question the Court would take

If it grants, the Court takes **Question 2** (whether overlap between a state illegal-reentry crime and 8 U.S.C. 1326, plus the federal executive's prosecutorial discretion, suffices to obstacle-preempt the state law on its face), most likely rewritten to ask whether Iowa's SF2340 is preempted by federal immigration law. The facial-challenge framing (Salerno / Moody v. NetChoice) would travel with it. **Question 1** (standing after an enforcement disavowal) is unlikely to be granted on its own or at all: the BIO's point that at least one MMJ member (David) has standing under Iowa's own reading is a strong vehicle objection, and the Court would reach standing only as a threshold matter.

## Summary disposition

**Unlikely** (P = 0.06 conditional on a grant). There is no intervening decision of this Court to GVR in light of, and the question is contested enough, with a unanimous panel below and every lower court agreeing on the merits, that a per curiam reversal is implausible. A grant means plenary review.

## If denied

A denial carries a meaningful chance of a **separate writing** (P = 0.28): a dissent from denial, or a statement respecting denial noting that the question should be resolved once the Tenth and Eleventh Circuits rule. Justice Thomas is the likeliest author given his Kansas v. Garcia concurrence attacking purposes-and-objectives preemption, with Justice Alito or Justice Gorsuch joining. If the denial is driven by percolation (waiting on the Oklahoma and Florida appeals), it is more likely to be silent.

## If granted (conditional only)

If granted and argued, the likelier outcome is reversal or vacatur of the preliminary injunction on the facial-challenge ground: that a state law mirroring a federal crime has at least some constitutional applications, so a facial pre-enforcement injunction cannot stand, leaving as-applied challenges for later. A broad holding that state illegal-reentry crimes are field-preempted is the less likely direction with this bench.
