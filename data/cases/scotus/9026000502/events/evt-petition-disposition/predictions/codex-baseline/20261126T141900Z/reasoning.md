The strongest reason for review is the trial judge’s announced willingness to draw an adverse inference each time Odom invoked the Fifth Amendment. The same prosecutor’s office was asking about the death in a custody case and prosecuting her for it. Immunity covered questions about her demeanor and competency while leaving that central allegation exposed. *Baxter v. Palmigiano* permits a civil factfinder to consider silence in the face of probative evidence. How much independent evidence supported deprivation therefore matters. Odom emphasizes the trial judge’s finding that the child had suffered no abuse, but the judge found statutory grounds for deprivation.
