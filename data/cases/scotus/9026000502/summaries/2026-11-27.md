## In brief

A mother asks the Court to review how Oklahoma courts handled self-incrimination and notice rights in the case that led to her child being declared deprived.

## What happened

Rachel Odom's daughter was taken into state custody after the child's father died of a gunshot wound in Odom's home; Odom was arrested on suspicion of murder. The same prosecutor's office that charged her criminally also brought a child-deprivation case. The petition says Odom received no notice of the hearing where emergency custody was extended, and that at the later deprivation trial, prosecutors called her as a witness, the court allowed an adverse inference when she invoked her right against self-incrimination, and immunity was granted only on topics helpful to the state's case. The trial court found the girl deprived; the Oklahoma Court of Civil Appeals affirmed, and the Oklahoma Supreme Court denied further review, with one justice dissenting.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked whether a state may call a parent to testify in a child-custody case while separately prosecuting her for the same conduct, penalize her silence with an adverse inference, and limit immunity to only the topics that help the state's position. It is also asked whether letting a parent participate in later hearings makes up for a complete, conceded lack of notice and opportunity to be heard at the earlier hearing where the state first took custody of her child.

## Where it stands

The petition was distributed for an October 2026 conference, and the docket shows no decision on it since. An emergency stay application filed in the case was denied by Justice Gorsuch, and the case was later distributed again for a December 2026 conference.

## What each outcome would mean

Odom, the mother, is asking the Court to take up her case and overturn the rulings against her. If the Court grants review, it would hear the case fully and decide the constitutional questions, without yet deciding who wins. If it denies review, the Oklahoma Court of Civil Appeals' decision upholding the deprivation finding stands, leaving the adverse-inference and notice rulings undisturbed. Separately, the denial of the stay application means the proceedings below were not paused and may continue while the petition remains pending.
