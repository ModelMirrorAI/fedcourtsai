# Forecast of the Court's action

The Court will deny the petition without a separate writing, most likely in an order list following the September 28, 2026 conference, in late September or early October. The snapshot records exactly one distribution, entered June 17 for that conference, and no CVSG. My modal forecast is zero additional distributions and no call for the Solicitor General's views. I assign 3.5% to at least one additional distribution and 0.1% to a new CVSG. If another distribution occurs, one additional distribution is my most likely increment. The summer interval before the already scheduled conference is not itself a relist.

The principal obstacle to review will be the petition's vehicle: its account says the constitutional claims were not raised at the merits hearing and were treated as forfeited on appeal. Its four questions also combine challenges to particular factual findings and record handling with much broader attacks on the Illinois Domestic Violence Act. The Court will most likely decline to use this record to decide those broad constitutional questions. This is a forecast of case selection, not a prediction that the denial order will explain its reasons or endorse the lower court's constitutional analysis. (Provisioned petition, pp. 11–15; questions presented, p. i.)

Conditional on a grant, the most plausible question is a narrowed version of QP 1: whether the application of the protective-order regime to the alleged public prayer walk and related expression violated the First Amendment. A clean ruling would first require the Court to get past preservation and the disputed factual premises; I do not predict adoption of the petitioners' account as fact. I do not expect the Court to take the entire multi-amendment challenge in QP 3 or the general complaints about counsel and appellate record handling in QPs 2 and 4.

Conditional on any grant, I assign 30% to a disposition in the cert order itself, and 70% to plenary review. A summary route is therefore not my leading grant scenario. I have identified no intervening decision requiring a GVR; Rahimi predates the September 2025 appellate decision and is not a new development since that judgment. A hypothetical summary route would depend on a clear defect becoming apparent from the actual lower-court record, which is not provisioned here. (United States v. Rahimi, 602 U.S. 680 (2024), decided June 21, 2024; snapshot's lower-court decision date.)

Conditional on denial, I assign 1.5% to any noted dissent or statement respecting denial. I predict none, and make no individual-Justice vote or writing forecast. The timing forecast is contextual rather than a scored claim.
