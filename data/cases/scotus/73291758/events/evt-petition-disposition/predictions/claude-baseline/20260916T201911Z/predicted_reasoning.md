# Forecast: Dittmer, et ux. v. Dittmer, No. 25-1245 (evt-petition-disposition)

**Disposition.** The Court will deny the petition on the first order list
following the September 28, 2026 conference (the long conference), without
comment.

**Relists.** The docket shows exactly **one** distribution (June 17, 2026, for
the Conference of 9/28/2026) and no relist. I expect no further distribution:
the petition will be considered once and denied. The residual chance of a
second distribution entry is a reschedule rather than a substantive relist —
the long conference occasionally carries a few over — not a sign of interest.

**CVSG.** None. The dispute is between private family members over a state
civil protective order under 750 ILCS 60; no federal statute, program, or
party is implicated, and the Solicitor General has no stake to be asked about.

**Which question, if any.** None as written. The four questions are
fact-bound and overlapping (a compound First/Second/Fifth/Fourteenth Amendment
challenge to the specific orders, an ineffective-assistance claim in a civil
protective-order case, a facial vagueness attack on the Illinois Domestic
Violence Act, and an allegation of procedural irregularities below). If the
Court were somehow to act, it would not be by taking any of these questions
for briefing and argument; the only conceivable grant-side route is a GVR
in light of an intervening decision bearing on civil protective orders and
the Second Amendment, and no such decision is pending that I can identify.
So conditional on a grant, a summary disposition (GVR) is much the likelier
route than plenary review.

**Response.** The response was due June 3, 2026 and the docket shows neither
a brief in opposition nor a waiver; the petition was distributed anyway. I do
not expect the Court to call for a response before acting.

**Dissent from denial.** None. No Justice will note a dissent from or write a
statement respecting the denial: the constitutional claims were held forfeited
below, the record is a two-year family protective order, and the petition
supplies no vehicle for a statement on the Second Amendment or free-exercise
questions it gestures at.

**If granted (conditional only).** A plenary grant is not a realistic scenario.
If the Court acted at all it would be a GVR, and the remand would turn on
whatever intervening authority prompted it rather than on the questions
presented.
