## In brief

Two men whose asylum status was revoked after criminal convictions ask the Court to decide whether they can still become lawful permanent residents.

## What happened

Tamer Wassily and Byron Velasquez Arreaga were each granted asylum years ago, then had that status terminated after criminal convictions. Both later sought to adjust to lawful permanent resident status under a federal law allowing such adjustment for "any alien granted asylum." Immigration judges and the Board of Immigration Appeals disagreed about whether termination of asylum status forecloses this path, ultimately ruling against both men. The Second Circuit, in a divided decision, held that only noncitizens with current asylum status may adjust to permanent resident status, rejecting a contrary view from the Fifth Circuit. Wassily and Velasquez Arreaga appealed to the Supreme Court.

## What the Court is being asked

The question is whether someone who was once granted asylum, but later lost that status, can still apply to become a lawful permanent resident under the relevant immigration statute, or whether only people who currently hold asylum status can do so.

## Where it stands

The Court granted review, and the case has been fully briefed and argued; it was set for argument on November 30, 2026, and now awaits decision.

## What each outcome would mean

Wassily and Velasquez Arreaga, who lost their earlier bids in the lower court, are asking the Court to reverse that decision. If the Court rules in their favor, former asylees whose status was terminated would remain eligible to apply for permanent residency, subject to other requirements. If the Court affirms, the lower court's rule would stand nationwide: only noncitizens with current asylum status could use this path, and those whose asylum was terminated, including Wassily and Velasquez Arreaga, would remain ineligible and subject to removal.
