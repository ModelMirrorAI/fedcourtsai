## In brief

Two men granted asylum years ago, then stripped of it after crimes, ask the Court whether they may still seek green cards under federal immigration law.

## What happened

Tamer Wassily and Byron Velasquez Arreaga were each granted asylum, then had that status terminated after criminal convictions the government deemed particularly serious crimes. Both sought to adjust to lawful permanent resident status, a step toward citizenship. Immigration judges and the Board of Immigration Appeals disagreed about whether termination of asylum automatically blocks that path. The Second Circuit, consolidating both cases, ruled that only noncitizens who currently hold asylum status may adjust their status, rejecting the petitioners' argument that anyone ever granted asylum remains eligible; one judge dissented. The Second Circuit's rehearing requests were denied, and the men asked the Supreme Court to take up the question, noting that other appeals courts disagreed on the issue.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked to decide whether a law letting the government grant permanent residency to "any alien granted asylum" covers only people who currently have asylum status, or also covers people who were granted asylum in the past even if that status was later taken away.

## Where it stands

The Court granted review, and the case has been fully briefed and argued; it was scheduled for argument on November 30, 2026, and now awaits decision.

## What each outcome would mean

Wassily and Velasquez Arreaga, as petitioners, asked the Court to reverse the lower court's ruling against them. If the Court affirms, the Second Circuit's rule stands: once asylum is terminated, the path to permanent residency under this provision is closed, and both men remain subject to removal. If the Court reverses, it would hold that former asylees can still seek permanent residency, reopening that possibility for them and others whose asylum was terminated. If the Court vacates, it would send the case back without deciding the question for either side.
