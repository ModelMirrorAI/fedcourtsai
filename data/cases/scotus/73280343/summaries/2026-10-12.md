## In brief

Two men whose asylum status was revoked after criminal convictions ask the Court to decide whether they can still seek green cards under a federal immigration law.

## What happened

Tamer Wassily and Byron Velasquez Arreaga were each granted asylum years ago, then had that status terminated after criminal convictions. Both later sought to adjust to lawful permanent resident status, a step toward citizenship. Immigration judges and the Board of Immigration Appeals ultimately ruled that once asylum is terminated, a person cannot adjust status under the relevant law. The Second Circuit agreed, denying their petitions in a split decision, with one judge dissenting. The court acknowledged it was siding with the Fourth Circuit and departing from a Fifth Circuit ruling that reached the opposite conclusion.

## What the Court is being asked

The law lets the government grant permanent resident status to "any alien granted asylum" who meets certain conditions. The Court is asked whether that phrase covers only people who currently hold asylum status, or whether it also covers people who were granted asylum in the past even if that status was later taken away.

## Where it stands

The Court granted review, and the case has been fully briefed and argued; it was argued on November 30, 2026, and now awaits decision.

## What each outcome would mean

Wassily and Velasquez Arreaga, as petitioners, are asking the Court to reverse the lower court. If the Court affirms, the rule that terminated asylum status forecloses adjustment of status would stand, and both men would remain ineligible to become permanent residents under this provision. If the Court reverses, it would hold that people whose asylum was terminated can still be considered for adjustment of status, and their cases would likely return to the lower courts for further proceedings under that standard. If the Court vacates, it would set aside the Second Circuit's decision and send the case back without deciding the question for either side.
