# Rationale

I assign a 0.67 probability that the judgment below is disturbed and predict a reversal. This is a forward, merits-at-grant forecast. The June 29, 2026 grant falls in OT2025, and the August 16 snapshot shows no merits briefs or argument: petitioners' opening merits brief was not due until August 24 and respondent's brief until October 2. I therefore worked from the petition, questions presented, docket posture, and pre-grant authorities, not from merits advocacy or oral argument. The context's cert salience band and one distribution are spent grant signals and did not inform the merits probability.

The committed merits statpack is the quantitative anchor. For an OT2025 grant, its available rows within the ten strictly prior grant Terms are OT2017-OT2024. They contain 359 disturbed judgments among 515 parsed judgments (69.7%), with 515 parsed among 557 granted cases (92.5% coverage); the same rows publish 57 pool-guard exclusions. The sample clears the 30-judgment floor. Recent-Term coverage remains imperfect, so I treat the rate as a baseline rather than a case-specific answer.

The case-specific evidence leaves my estimate slightly below that baseline. Reversal is favored by a clean and preserved circuit split, the absence of an alternative ground in Wassily's case, and petitioners' strong textual contrast: Congress expressly required continuing conditions elsewhere in Section 1159 but did not say that asylum must remain in effect. “Any alien granted asylum” also reads naturally as historical. Granting review after the government opposed certiorari is at least consistent with the Court wanting to reject the government's categorical rule.

The counterarguments are substantial. The Second and Fourth Circuits both adopted the government's present-status reading; “adjust[ing]” a person's “status” can support that view; neighboring Section 1158 uses similar words for a current condition; and the 1990 note expressly extends treatment “regardless” of termination to a specified cohort. Those points could attract a conservative majority, and a grant to resolve a split is not itself a vote to reverse. They keep the estimate near the merits baseline and make Roberts, Thomas, Barrett, and Kavanaugh the largest lineup uncertainties.

The predicted 6-3 lineup assumes the ordinary nine-Justice roster because the provisioned record contains no participation or recusal information. I place Gorsuch and the three Democratic appointees on the historical-eligibility reading, Alito and Kavanaugh on the government's structural reading, Thomas and Barrett with the text-focused majority, and Roberts with the dissent. The judgment label is `reversed` rather than `vacated` because accepting petitioners' categorical legal rule resolves the ground on which the Second Circuit denied review and calls for reversal and remand.

The 47-page petition and extracted questions presented were complete. The 22-page brief in opposition was provisioned with `empty_text: true`; its content was therefore unavailable, not absent. I reconstructed the opposing position only from the petition's account of the Second Circuit, the Fourth Circuit, and the government's arguments. The petitioners' reply was linked on the docket but not provisioned as text. Those gaps reduce confidence, especially as to arguments the government will refine on the merits.
