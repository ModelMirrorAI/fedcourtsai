# Forecast

The Court will reverse the Second Circuit and remand, likely by a 6-3 vote. The majority will reach the question presented as framed and hold that a person whose asylum was terminated may remain eligible to seek adjustment to lawful-permanent-resident status under 8 U.S.C. § 1159(b).

The majority's principal ground will be textual and structural. “Any alien granted asylum” will be read to describe a completed historical event. The opinion will emphasize that the same subsection separately requires an applicant to “continue[] to be a refugee” and to be admissible at the time of adjustment, while the neighboring refugee provision expressly requires that admission “has not been terminated.” Those express continuity clauses will make the absence of a current-asylum requirement decisive. The Court will treat the 1990 statutory note's “regardless of whether” parenthetical as a clarifying provision for that note's special cohort, not as a reason to add a restriction to Section 1159(b).

The holding will be categorical on eligibility but limited in consequence: termination of asylum alone does not disqualify an applicant, while every other statutory prerequisite, any admissibility waiver, and the Executive's ultimate discretion remain open. The Court will therefore reverse rather than merely vacate, and it will reject a DIG or an equally divided disposition.

Thomas, Sotomayor, Kagan, Gorsuch, Barrett, and Jackson will form the majority. Roberts, Alito, and Kavanaugh will dissent on the ground that “adjust[ing]” a person's “status,” neighboring uses of “granted asylum,” and the 1990 note together require a current asylum status. The judgment is most likely by the end of June 2027.
