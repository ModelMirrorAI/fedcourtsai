# Predicted Reasoning

The Court will reverse the judgment of the Second Circuit. 

The majority will hold that the plain text of 8 U.S.C. § 1159(b)—which permits the adjustment of status for "any alien granted asylum"—refers to the historical fact that the alien was granted asylum at some point in the past. It will reject the Second and Fourth Circuits' interpretation that the phrase implicitly requires the alien to maintain that asylum status at the time of the adjustment application. 

The Court will likely rely on the absence of explicit language requiring continuing status in § 1159(b), contrasting it with other provisions in the INA where Congress expressly required an ongoing status. As a result, the Court will adopt a categorical rule that the termination of asylum status does not automatically render an alien ineligible for adjustment to lawful permanent resident status under Section 1159(b).

Expect a majority opinion authored by a textualist conservative (such as Justice Gorsuch or Justice Barrett) joined by the liberal justices, with a likely dissent from Justices Thomas and Alito relying on the broader statutory structure and context to argue that an alien without present asylum status cannot be considered an "alien granted asylum."
