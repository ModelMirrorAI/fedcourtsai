**Case-specific adjustments:**
The question presented is whether noncitizens who were "granted asylum" but whose asylum was later terminated are eligible to adjust to LPR status under 8 U.S.C. § 1159(b). The Second Circuit (judgment below) held they are categorically ineligible. The Fifth Circuit has held they are eligible.
The petitioners argue a strong textualist position that "granted asylum" describes a past historical event, and the statute does not explicitly demand current asylum status. The respondent (Solicitor General) argues the structure and tense imply a present status requirement. Given the current Court's strong preference for strict textualism and its willingness to overturn agency or lower court interpretations that add unwritten requirements, the petitioners' textual argument is very likely to appeal to a majority of the Justices, including the liberal wing (favoring the immigrant petitioners) and textualist conservatives (Gorsuch, Kavanaugh, Barrett). 
Therefore, I adjust slightly upwards or keep it aligned with the base rate (0.70), finding it highly probable that the Court will reverse the Second Circuit's categorical bar.

**Uncertainties:**
The main uncertainty is whether the conservative justices who normally defer to stricter immigration enforcement (e.g., Thomas, Alito) will find the statutory context compelling enough to override the plain text historical reading of "granted asylum." Thus, I predict a 6-3 or 7-2 split rather than a unanimous decision.
