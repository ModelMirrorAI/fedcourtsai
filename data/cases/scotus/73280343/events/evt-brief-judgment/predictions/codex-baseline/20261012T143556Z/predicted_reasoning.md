# Forecast of the Court's judgment

The Court will **reverse** the Second Circuit and remand for further proceedings, holding that termination of asylum does not itself categorically disqualify a previously granted asylee from seeking adjustment under 8 U.S.C. § 1159(b). The predicted vote is **5–4**: Sotomayor, Kagan, Gorsuch, Barrett, and Jackson in the majority; Roberts, Thomas, Alito, and Kavanaugh dissenting. The probability assigned to a disturbed judgment, including reversal, vacatur, or a mixed judgment, is **57%**.

The majority will distinguish the historical asylum grant from the continuing conditions Congress expressly listed. It will place particular weight on the express nontermination requirement in the neighboring refugee-adjustment provision and the continuing-refugee and current-admissibility requirements in subsection (b). It will decline to add an unexpressed continuing-asylum condition. The majority will recognize that past participles can denote present conditions, but find the local statutory contrasts more persuasive than that general grammatical possibility. Lenity will not be necessary to the holding.

The rule will reach former asylees generally, rather than creating an equitable exception for these two petitioners or only for criminal-conviction terminations. Its breadth will concern access to the adjustment process, not entitlement to its result. Applicants will still need continuing refugee qualification, satisfaction of the remaining statutory conditions, any necessary permissible waiver, and a favorable discretionary determination. The Court will not restore terminated asylum or order permanent residence for either petitioner.

The dissent will read the statute as authorizing transition from presently held asylum status to permanent-resident status, emphasizing parallel wording in Section 1158(c) and Congress's express treatment of certain former asylees in 1990. The central division will concern interpretation of the eligibility gateway, not the Court's authority to grant humanitarian relief itself. No particular author or additional concurrence is forecast.

The case will proceed to the November 30, 2026 argument shown in the snapshot and receive a merits disposition during the ensuing months, most likely by the end of June 2027. A DIG or equally divided affirmance is not the expected route: no concrete jurisdictional or participation defect appears in the provisioned material. Timing is a forecast, not a scored claim.
