# Rationale

## Forecast and information boundary

I assign a 20% probability to an unqualified grant of the application and predict denial. This is a judgmental estimate with substantial uncertainty, not an estimate calibrated to a published capital-stay base rate. A mixed grant-and-denial order counts as ungranted under this event's resolver; a temporary administrative pause is not necessarily the application's final disposition.

The baseline actually read was `record/snapshots/2016-11-04.json`. It identifies application 16A450, Thomas Douglas Arthur v. Alabama, linked with petition 16-595, arising from the Supreme Court of Alabama. Its sole proceeding is the November 3, 2016 application for a stay of execution submitted to Justice Thomas. At this arrival-position boundary there is no recorded response request, referral to the full Court, or amicus entry. Those absences describe the supplied moment, not the application's eventual trajectory. The application text, petition, response, and related-docket snapshot were not provisioned. I therefore cannot identify the substantive constitutional claim, preservation issues, execution date, or the lower court's reasoning and do not infer them from Arthur's name.

The context calls this a `forward` cell even though its snapshot and opening entry are from November 2016 and this run is dated October 30, 2026. I did not treat that label as permission to inspect ten years of subsequent history. I used the supplied arrival record, a conservative pre-2016 corpus filter, and a pre-boundary general precedent only. The temporal inconsistency is flagged, without asserting a docket-confirmed disposition.

## Baseline and legal assessment

The committed `metrics/statpack.md`, section "The interim docket (applications)," supplies no eligible empirical anchor for application Term 2016. Every displayed Term from 2006 through 2015 has zero resolved substantive applications and zero grants; the pooled eligible denominator is therefore zero, below the required 50. These are coverage gaps, not evidence that historical stays never occurred. I do not substitute the current pack-wide rate, the 2016 row, later Terms, or cert salience rates. The context's null band is appropriate for an interim application. The corpus query used `--decided-before 2016` notwithstanding the absent environment clock and returned no prior rows; it therefore supplied no empirical analogues. Its transfer report is documented in `retrieval.md`.

Barefoot v. Estelle, 463 U.S. 880, 895–96 (1983), retrieved through CourtListener opinion 111017, explains that an execution stay pending certiorari is not automatic: it requires a reasonable prospect of review, a significant possibility of reversal, and irreparable harm. I use that general principle, not Barefoot's separate habeas procedural posture as a substitute for this application's unknown grounds. Execution makes the harm unusually irreversible, but it does not itself supply the missing review-worthy claim. The record establishes representation by counsel and a pending linked petition, yet neither proves a substantial legal defect. These considerations support denial as the modal call while leaving a meaningful chance of protective relief. The 20% estimate expresses uncertainty about an unseen application, not a finding that its arguments lack merit.

## Procedural increments and writings

Starting from no requested response, I assign 65% to a subsequent request: the irreversible stakes make an adversarial submission useful, but a response could arrive voluntarily or the application could be rejected without a formal request. Starting from no referral, I assign 90% to referral to the full Court; a capital stay is more likely to receive collective attention than an ordinary individual-Justice application. Neither number is taken from a terminal ladder bucket. Starting from zero amicus entries, I assign 8% to an increase, allowing for submitted briefs as well as accepted Latin-form entries. Urgency limits the opportunity for additional filings, although the actual execution schedule is unavailable.

The independent order-writer probabilities use the eight Justices seated on the snapshot date, including Kennedy, Ginsburg, and Breyer and excluding later appointees. They concern authorship, not joining a writing or casting a stay vote. The highest probabilities are for Sotomayor and Breyer, but an order without a separate opinion remains the most likely form. These are low-confidence judgments rather than empirical writing rates.

I cannot reliably associate that memory with this precise application or distinguish temporary relief from final disposition. That is possible outcome contamination, not pre-decision evidence; I did not use it to set the probability or retrieve this application's disposition.
