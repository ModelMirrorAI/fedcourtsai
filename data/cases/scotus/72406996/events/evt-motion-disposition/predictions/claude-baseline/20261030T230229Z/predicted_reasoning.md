# Forecast: Arthur v. Alabama, No. 16A450 (stay of execution)

**What the record shows.** One docket entry: on Nov 3, 2016, Thomas Douglas Arthur applied for a stay of his death sentence, submitted to Justice Thomas as Circuit Justice for the Eleventh Circuit. The application is linked with cert petition No. 16-595, which comes from the Supreme Court of Alabama. No response has been requested, the application has not been referred to the full Court, and no amicus briefs are on the docket.

**Disposition.** I expect the stay to be **denied** (P(unqualified grant) = 0.20). Execution-day stay applications in capital cases usually fail. This one has more going for it than most, for two reasons. First, it is tied to a pending cert petition. Second, that petition appears to attack Alabama's judicial-override scheme after *Hurst v. Florida* (2016), and some Justices had openly signalled interest in that question. Under the Court's practice, four votes to grant cert do not on their own produce a stay, which takes five. With eight Justices sitting, that is a high bar.

**Referral.** Very likely (0.85). Justice Thomas would ordinarily refer a contested capital stay application to the full Court instead of acting alone. The disposition will probably be a full-Court order.

**Response requested.** Unlikely (0.10). On an execution-day application the State usually files an opposition without being asked, so the Court rarely issues a formal call for a response.

**Amicus.** Very unlikely (0.03). An execution-day timetable leaves no room for amicus filings.

**Timing.** I expect a disposition within a day, on or around the scheduled execution date of Nov 3–4, 2016.

**Separate writing.** If the stay is denied, a dissent from Justice Sotomayor and/or Justice Breyer is plausible, given their writings on method-of-execution and *Hurst* questions. If it is granted, expect at most a short statement. Conditionally: if the Court grants, the likeliest route is a stay pending disposition of 16-595.
