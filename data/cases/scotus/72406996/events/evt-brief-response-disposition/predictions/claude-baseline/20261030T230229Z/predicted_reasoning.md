# Forecast — Arthur v. Alabama, No. 16A450 (stay of execution)

**What the record shows from this vantage:** a capital application for a stay of execution, submitted to Justice Thomas (Circuit Justice for the Eleventh Circuit) on 3 November 2016, the scheduled execution date. Alabama filed its response the same day, without being asked. No call for a response, no referral to the full Court, and no amicus briefs are on the record. The application is linked with cert petition No. 16-595.

- **Disposition:** I expect the stay to be **denied**, with P(an unqualified grant) = 0.25. Last-day capital stay applications are denied far more often than not. This one runs above that norm because the linked petition raises a Hurst v. Florida challenge to Alabama's judicial-override sentencing scheme, a question the Court was actively sorting out in 2016. The Court was also sitting eight-member. A stay needs five votes, and four Justices can grant cert but cannot stop the execution, which keeps the grant probability down.
- **Response requested:** this almost certainly will not happen (0.03). Alabama has already filed its response, and the execution is hours away.
- **Referral to the full Court:** very likely (0.85). On execution night, the Circuit Justice routinely refers a capital stay application to the Court.
- **Amicus:** no amicus brief is likely to be filed before the disposition (0.03).
- **Timing:** I expect a disposition on the night of 3 November 2016, before or around the execution window.
- **Separate writings:** if the stay is denied, expect at most a noted dissent, possibly with a short writing from Justice Breyer or Justice Sotomayor (Breyer has a long record of writing on prolonged death-row confinement). If it is granted, a statement from a Justice explaining their vote is possible.
