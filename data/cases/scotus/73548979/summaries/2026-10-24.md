## In brief

The Clemente family asks the Court to revive claims that Puerto Rico's commemorative license-plate program took their father's trademark without compensation and that Puerto Rico lacks immunity from suit.

## What happened

Clemente Properties and relatives of baseball player Roberto Clemente sued Puerto Rico's governor and other officials, claiming a state program selling commemorative license plates and tags bearing Clemente's name and image infringed their trademark rights and took their property without payment, violating the Lanham Act and the Constitution's takings protections. A federal district court dismissed the claims. The First Circuit affirmed dismissal of the takings claim and ruled that Puerto Rico has sovereign immunity that the Lanham Act does not override, but it revived some trademark claims against officials sued personally, sending those back to the lower court.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked whether property protections against uncompensated government takings apply the same way to intangible property, like trademarks, as they do to physical property, and whether Puerto Rico can claim the same immunity from federal lawsuits that states have, and if so, whether a federal trademark law already removes that immunity.

## Where it stands

The petition has been distributed for conference twice, most recently October 16, 2026, after full briefing including a reply and a supplemental brief; the docket shows no decision yet on whether the Court will hear the case.

## What each outcome would mean

Clemente Properties and the family members, who lost on these claims in the lower courts, are asking the Court to take up the case and reverse. If the Court grants review, it would hear full arguments on both questions before deciding who is right. If it denies review, the First Circuit's rulings stand: the takings claim stays dismissed, and Puerto Rico and its officials remain shielded by immunity from the trademark claims brought against them officially, though other trademark claims against officials personally continue in the lower court regardless.
