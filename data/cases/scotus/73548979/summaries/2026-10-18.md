## In brief

Roberto Clemente's family asks the Court to revive claims that Puerto Rico took their trademark rights in his name without compensation.

## What happened

Clemente Properties and relatives of baseball player Roberto Clemente sued Puerto Rico's governor and other officials, claiming Puerto Rico used Clemente's name and image on commemorative license plates and registration tags without permission, raising about $15 million for a sports-district fund. They argued this violated the Lanham Act, a federal trademark law, and amounted to an uncompensated taking of property. A district court dismissed the claims, and the First Circuit affirmed dismissal of the takings claim and the official-capacity Lanham Act claims, citing Puerto Rico's sovereign immunity, while reviving some individual-capacity Lanham Act claims for further proceedings.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked whether a special rule that automatically requires compensation for certain government takings of property, normally applied to physical property, must also apply when the government takes intangible property like a trademark. The Court is also asked whether Puerto Rico can claim the same immunity from lawsuits that states have, and if so, whether the Lanham Act already overrides that immunity for trademark claims.

## Where it stands

The petition has been fully briefed, including amicus briefs and a reply, and was distributed for conferences in October 2026. The docket does not yet show any decision on the petition.

## What each outcome would mean

Clemente Properties and the Clemente family, as petitioners, are asking the Court to hear the case. If the Court grants review, it would hear full arguments and decide the takings and immunity questions itself, without yet deciding who wins. If the Court denies review, the First Circuit's decision stands: the takings claim and official-capacity claims against Puerto Rico remain dismissed, while the individual-capacity Lanham Act claims the First Circuit revived continue in the lower courts regardless of the Court's action here.
