# Predicted course — Clemente Properties v. Pierluisi-Urrutia (No. 25-1426)

**Where the docket stands.** Two distributions are on the record: the petition was first distributed for the 10/9/2026 conference, and on 10/13 it was redistributed for 10/16/2026. That is one relist after a conference where it was considered. No CVSG has been entered. A Rule 15.8 supplemental brief (9/21) asks for a CVSG as a fallback to a grant.

**Disposition: denied (P(grant) ≈ 0.22).** The most likely outcome is denial after one or more further relists, not a denial on 10/16 with nothing else attached. A first relist is what the Court now does before nearly every grant, but it also happens when a Justice is preparing a separate writing respecting denial.

**Further relists.** I expect the petition to be distributed again after the 10/16 conference more often than not on two paths: a dissent from denial being drafted, or the Court holding the petition while it decides between granting and a CVSG. A grant or a clean denial on 10/16 ends the distributions. Overall about 0.4 for at least one more distribution.

**CVSG.** Unlikely (≈0.08). The United States is not a party. The federal interest runs through the Lanham Act's abrogation provision (15 U.S.C. §1122) and the USPTO Director's public remarks that the petitioners cite. The Court seldom calls for the SG's views after it has already relisted, and the two questions are constitutional and sovereign-immunity questions the Court can decide on its own.

**Which question, if granted.** QP 2 (whether Puerto Rico has any sovereign immunity in federal court, and whether the Lanham Act abrogates it) is the more likely draw. The Court flagged the immunity question as open in *Financial Oversight & Management Board v. Centro de Periodismo Investigativo* (2023) and called that case a "singularly inapt vehicle" only because Puerto Rico itself was not a party. Here the Commonwealth is the respondent. QP 1 (per se takings of intangible property) has vehicle problems: the petitioners disclaimed any Penn Central theory, and the First Circuit treated other theories as waived. If the Court granted, it might limit the grant to QP 2 or reformulate QP 1 narrowly around trademarks.

**Summary disposition.** Very unlikely (≈0.05 conditional on a grant). No intervening decision supports a GVR, and the questions are not the kind the Court decides by summary reversal.

**Dissent from denial.** Conditional on a denial, about 0.35 that some Justice writes. The likeliest author is Justice Thomas, who wrote in *Centro de Periodismo* that it is hard to see how state-style sovereign immunity applies to Puerto Rico. Justice Gorsuch is the next most plausible. A relist followed by a denial is the classic shape of such a writing.
