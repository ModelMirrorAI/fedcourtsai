# Forecast

The Court will most likely deny certiorari in Clemente Properties, Inc. v. Pierluisi-Urrutia. I assign a 32% probability to any grant, including a limited grant, GVR, or summary reversal.

The baseline contains **two conference distributions**, for October 9 and October 16, 2026: one relist has already occurred. I narrowly expect another distribution beyond those two, with a **55% probability** of at least one additional distribution before disposition. The central procedural path is one further relist, followed by denial without a separate writing, in late October or November 2026. That horizon is a forecast, not a scored claim.

No CVSG is on the baseline docket. I assign a **30% probability** to a future invitation to the Solicitor General. My modal prediction is no invitation, but this is a substantial alternative to prompt disposition: the trademark question and Puerto Rico's federal status give the United States identifiable interests. If the Court requests the government's views, I expect that request during the next few conferences and final disposition to move into 2027, after the government's submission. The prediction of another distribution includes a later redistribution after any such invitation; it is not limited to next week's conference.

If the Court grants review, I expect it to select the first question, narrowed to whether the alleged governmental appropriation of a trademark must be analyzed under a categorical takings rule despite the property's intangible nature. The broader question whether Puerto Rico possesses state-equivalent sovereign immunity is a credible alternative basis for review, but I do not expect a grant of both questions to be the central grant path. If the Court takes the immunity question, its likely focus will be the source and extent of territorial immunity rather than a freestanding holding that every state is subject to Lanham Act damages.

Conditional on a grant, I assign only **10%** to disposition in the certiorari order itself and **90%** to plenary consideration. The disagreement concerns the extension and interaction of doctrines, and the record identifies no intervening controlling decision that naturally supplies a GVR. A USPTO official's speech is not such a decision. If granted on the takings question, the likely merits dispute will concern appropriation of an exclusionary property interest versus infringement remediable through ordinary trademark law; this is conditional context, not a prediction that the Court will issue a merits opinion.

Conditional on denial, I assign **25%** to some noted dissent or statement respecting denial. No separate writing remains the more likely result. If one appears, Justice Thomas is the most plausible author or participant because of his previously expressed objection to assuming Puerto Rico has the States' inherent immunity. That attribution is context only; the structured claim predicts the existence of any such writing, not a Justice's vote.
