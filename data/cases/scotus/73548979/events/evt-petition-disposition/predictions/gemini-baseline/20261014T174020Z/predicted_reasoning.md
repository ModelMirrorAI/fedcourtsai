The petition has been distributed twice so far (one relist). Expect a decent chance of at least one further relist as the Court considers the substantial issues presented. The questions involve the application of the Takings Clause to intangible property (a trademark) and the sovereign immunity of Puerto Rico under the Lanham Act. While the issues are significant and have drawn three conservative amicus briefs at the cert stage, the lack of a clean circuit split makes a grant unlikely. A CVSG is possible given the federal statutory question regarding abrogation of immunity, but not highly probable. If granted, the Court would likely take up the first question regarding the Takings Clause, given the broader constitutional implications, while leaving the narrower sovereign immunity issue aside. A summary disposition (like a GVR) is unlikely as there are no obvious intervening decisions to point to. Should the Court deny the petition, there is a reasonable likelihood of a dissent from denial from the Court's more conservative members who may wish to clarify the scope of the Takings Clause concerning intangible property rights.
