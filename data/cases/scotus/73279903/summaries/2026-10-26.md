## In brief

A Christian youth ministry asks the Court to revive its claim that Oregon illegally stripped its grant funding over its religious hiring practices.

## What happened

Youth 71Five Ministries, a Christian nonprofit serving at-risk youth, had received Oregon grants for years. When Oregon added a rule barring grant recipients from discriminating in employment based on religion, among other traits, the state withdrew 71Five's conditional grant award and excluded it from the program because 71Five requires staff to affirm a Christian statement of faith. 71Five sued, claiming violations of its free exercise, religious-autonomy, and expressive-association rights. The district court denied a preliminary injunction and dismissed the case. The Ninth Circuit's merits panel largely agreed, ruling the rule neutral and generally applicable, but ordered a narrower injunction protecting 71Five's religious hiring only for activities that receive no grant funding.

## What the Court is being asked

The case raises two questions. First, whether a religious organization may sue to affirmatively enforce its right to run its own internal affairs without government interference, or whether that right can only be raised as a defense once already sued by someone else. Second, whether a state violates religious freedom protections by requiring a religious organization to give up its right to hire only people who share its faith as a condition of receiving public grant money.

## Where it stands

The petition has been distributed for conference twice, most recently for April 17, 2026, after full briefing including an opposition brief, supporting amicus briefs, and a reply. The docket shows no decision yet on whether the Court will hear the case.

## What each outcome would mean

Youth 71Five, which lost key parts of its case below, is asking the Court to take up its appeal. If the Court grants review, it would hear the case fully and decide the legal questions; a grant would not itself determine who wins. If the Court denies review, the Ninth Circuit's decision stands, leaving in place its rulings against 71Five on religious autonomy and free exercise, while its narrower injunction protecting non-grant-funded hiring would remain in effect and the case would continue in the lower courts on remaining claims.
