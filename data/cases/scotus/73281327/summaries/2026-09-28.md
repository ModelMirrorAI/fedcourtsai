## In brief

Nicole Pileggi asks the Court to review a privacy lawsuit over a newspaper website's video-tracking practices, in a case that mirrors one the Court has already agreed to hear.

## What happened

Nicole Pileggi sued Washington Newspaper Publishing Company, owner of the Washington Examiner, claiming it violated a federal video privacy law by sharing her video-watching history on its website with Facebook without her consent. She argued that subscribing to the paper's free email newsletter made her a protected "consumer" under the law. The district court and the D.C. Circuit both disagreed, ruling that to be a "consumer," a person must have obtained an audio-visual product or service, not just any newsletter, from the company. Pileggi's petition and Washington Newspaper's response both note that the Court has already agreed to decide the identical legal question in a separate case, Salazar v. Paramount Global.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked to decide what counts as a "consumer" under the video privacy law: whether that term covers anyone who buys or subscribes to anything from a company that also provides videos, or only people who specifically obtain video-related goods or services from that company.

## Where it stands

The petition has been distributed for the Court's conference of May 14, 2026, after full briefing including Washington Newspaper's opposition. The docket shows no ruling yet on whether the Court will hear the case.

## What each outcome would mean

Pileggi, who lost in the lower courts, is asking the Court to take up her case. Both sides suggest the Court may instead hold the petition until it decides the similar Salazar case, then act accordingly. If the Court grants review, it would hear the case in full and decide the legal question; if it denies review, the D.C. Circuit's ruling against Pileggi stands. The Court could also grant, vacate, and send the case back for further review in light of its decision in Salazar, deciding nothing yet for either side.
