## What happened

Two middle-school brothers, D.A. and X.A., wore "Let's Go Brandon" sweatshirts to their Michigan school. School staff told them to remove the shirts, saying the phrase stood in for a vulgar insult about President Biden. Their family sued, arguing the school violated their free-speech rights. A federal district court ruled for the school district, and a divided Sixth Circuit panel affirmed, holding that school officials could reasonably treat the phrase as vulgar even though it is political speech. One judge dissented, arguing the phrase was not obviously profane and that schools should need more than a hunch about hidden meaning to restrict such speech. The family then asked the Supreme Court to take the case.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked to decide how far schools can go in restricting student speech that touches on politics but that a school considers crude or offensive, even when the words themselves are not openly vulgar. The family argues schools may only restrict speech that is obviously profane or lewd on its face, and that this phrase does not meet that bar. The school district responds that officials may reasonably treat a coded phrase as carrying its underlying vulgar meaning, regardless of its political message, and that no meaningful disagreement exists among lower courts on the issue.
