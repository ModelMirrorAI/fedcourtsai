## What happened

Two brothers, D.A. and X.A., wore "Let's Go Brandon" sweatshirts to their Michigan middle school. School officials told them to remove the sweatshirts, saying the phrase's origin as a stand-in for a vulgar phrase aimed at then-President Biden made it prohibited under the school's dress code banning profane or vulgar clothing. The students, through their mother, sued the school district and two staff members, arguing the sweatshirts were protected political speech that caused no disruption. A federal district court ruled for the school, and a divided Sixth Circuit panel affirmed, holding officials could reasonably treat the phrase as vulgar given its origin. One judge dissented, arguing the phrase was not obviously vulgar and that the ruling gave schools too much discretion. The full Sixth Circuit declined to rehear the case.

## What the Court is being asked

Schools generally cannot restrict student speech unless it disrupts school activities, but a longstanding exception lets schools restrict speech that is vulgar or lewd, even political speech, without showing disruption. The Court is asked whether that exception covers speech that only indirectly or through its origins carries a vulgar meaning, or whether it applies only when the words themselves are plainly vulgar or lewd on their face.

## Where it stands

The petition has been fully briefed, including an opposition brief and a reply, and multiple outside groups have filed supporting briefs.
