# Forecast: what the Court will do with No. 25-1143, D.A. v. Tri County Area Schools

**Bottom line.** The petition is more likely denied than granted, but it is a live candidate: P(grant) 0.20. If the Court acts, the likelier route is plenary review, not a summary disposition.

## Where the docket stands

The snapshot (2026-09-15) shows **two distributions**: one on May 12, 2026 for the May 28 conference, which was overtaken when the Court **requested a response** on May 18 after respondents had waived, and a second on July 1, 2026 for the **September 28, 2026 long conference**, following the brief in opposition (June 17) and the reply (July 1). The Court has therefore **not yet considered the petition at any conference**; the September 28 conference is its first real look. Four cert-stage amicus briefs support the petitioners (First Amendment Scholars, The Buckeye Institute, Defending Education, National Coalition Against Censorship). No CVSG is on the docket.

## Relists

From this two-distribution state, I expect **at least one further distribution with probability 0.42**. A grant of a paid petition today almost always follows at least one relist so the Court can check the vehicle, so most of the grant mass (0.20) sits inside this number. The rest is relists that end in denial, most often because a Justice is writing separately, and the occasional reschedule, which the harness counts as a distribution. Conditional on a relist happening, expect one to two further conferences rather than a long string; there is no pending merits case to hold this one for.

## CVSG

**No.** P(a CVSG after prediction time) 0.02. The dispute is between private students and a Michigan school district under the First Amendment; no federal statute, program, or party is implicated, and the Court has not sought the Solicitor General's views in its recent student-speech cases at the cert stage.

## Which question the Court would take, if it grants

The petition's single question as written: whether Fraser permits schools to censor nondisruptive political speech that is not plainly profane or lewd. If the Court grants, I expect it to take that question essentially as framed, perhaps trimmed to whether Fraser's exception reaches euphemistic or coded speech whose surface words are not themselves lewd or profane. The Court would not reformulate around qualified immunity, because the injunctive claim against the district keeps the constitutional question dispositive for at least the younger student, who is still enrolled. (This paragraph is context only; no scorer resolves it.)

## Summary disposition

Unlikely: conditional on a grant, P(the case is disposed of in the cert order itself) 0.05. There is no intervening decision of this Court to GVR against, the Sixth Circuit's opinion is a published, reasoned 2-1 decision that is not summarily reversible on its face, and the doctrinal question is one the Court would want briefed and argued.

## Dissent from denial

If the Court denies, P(some Justice notes a dissent or files a statement respecting the denial) 0.30. The obvious author is Justice Alito, joined by Justice Thomas, who dissented in May 2025 from the denial in the First Circuit "There Are Only Two Genders" shirt case and framed student political speech as an issue of national importance; a FIRE petition with a vigorous circuit dissent invoking Tinker is the kind of vehicle that draws a repeat writing, though the coded-profanity facts here are less attractive to write on than a plainly political slogan.

## If granted, the likely ground (conditional only)

Conditional on a grant, I expect reversal or vacatur on the ground that Fraser's exception is confined to speech that is lewd, vulgar, or profane on its face or in its plain meaning to a reasonable observer, with a euphemism carrying political content governed by Tinker's disruption standard, and a remand on qualified immunity for the damages claims. Nothing here forecasts an opinion in the far more likely denial scenario.
