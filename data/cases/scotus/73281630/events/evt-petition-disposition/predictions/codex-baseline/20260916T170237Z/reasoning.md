## Why 32%, rather than the 17.2% anchor

**Reasons for an upward adjustment.** The petition presents a focused constitutional question about political apparel, backed by a published divided Sixth Circuit decision and a dissent urging clarification. The petition, pp. 13–16, identifies a concrete methodological disagreement with the Third Circuit's en banc B.H. decision: whether ambiguous political speech receives protection beyond speech a school reasonably classifies as vulgar. That is more substantial than an assertion that the lower court simply misapplied a settled test. Its vehicle discussion, pp. 27–28, emphasizes undisputed political content and lack of disruption. The snapshot also lists four supporting amicus filings and a Court-requested response after waiver. I regard that combination as evidence of attention beyond an ordinary paid petition, although not four votes to grant.

**Reasons denial remains more likely.** The BIO, pp. 19–23, makes a substantial distinction between disagreement about methodology and a demonstrated conflict in outcomes. It argues that B.H.'s ambiguous breast-cancer-awareness message differs from a slogan the students admittedly understood as conveying a profane insult, and that Chandler's treatment of union buttons does not establish the broad rule the petition attributes to the Ninth Circuit. These arguments weaken the claim of an unavoidable multi-circuit conflict. The parties' competing accounts of Fraser also make this less suitable for summary correction: the BIO emphasizes that Fraser itself involved innuendo rather than simply explicit prohibited words, whereas the petition emphasizes passive political expression and the risk of delegating constitutional boundaries to individual educators. I do not resolve that contested merits question as if a cert vote were a merits vote.

The BIO, pp. 23–24, also raises qualified immunity, which the lower-court dissent would have left for remand. This is a vehicle discount, **not an established complete bar**: the BIO's own procedural account, p. 6, says the suit sought declaratory and injunctive relief as well as damages, and a school district is also a respondent. The supplied excerpts do not establish the present viability of every requested remedy. I therefore do not assume immunity necessarily disposes of the whole case, but the unresolved issue reduces its cleanliness.

Finally, two distribution entries do not establish two completed substantive conferences. Here the first was followed by a call for a response before its scheduled conference, and the second places the completed briefing on the September 28 conference calendar. This is a plausible administrative redistribution after the response rather than the strong signal of repeated post-conference deliberation. I preserve the harness count and band while discounting an overly bullish interpretation of that count. Summer pendency likewise is not affirmative evidence of a hold for another case.

Taken together, these features support a meaningful but bounded increase to **32% P(any grant)**, leaving denial the modal disposition. The adjustment is a judgment, not a fitted estimate or the output of an independently verified split search.
