# Predicted reasoning — Maxwell v. Dinis (No. 25-5930), merits judgment (briefed moment)

**Judgment: vacated (and remanded). P(judgment below disturbed) = 0.80.**

The Fifth Circuit affirmed the dismissal of Maxwell's §2241 petition on the ground
that, under its *Melot v. Bergami* rule, a claim lies in habeas only if success would
automatically entitle the prisoner to accelerated release from his sentence. The
Solicitor General has confessed error on that holding, and the Court appointed an
amicus to defend the judgment below. I expect the Court to side with the parties:
a claim that BOP unlawfully withheld First Step Act time credits, seeking an earlier
move from prison into a halfway house or home confinement, is cognizable under
§2241.

**Judgment class.** Vacatur and remand rather than outright reversal: the
government's alternative grounds (failure to exhaust, which the district court
found, and the §3621(b) bar on review of BOP placement "designations") were not
decided by the court of appeals, and the Court's usual course is to leave such
questions for the court below to take up first. "Reversed and remanded" is the
close second label; both disturb.

**Vote.** 7–2. Majority: Roberts, Alito, Sotomayor, Kagan, Kavanaugh, Barrett,
Jackson. Dissent: Thomas and Gorsuch, on a historically narrower view of the writ
(at the Founding, release from all custody, not a transfer between forms of
custody that remain the sentence) — the position the appointed amicus argues. A
6–3 or 8–1 lineup is plausible; Alito is the swing-risk vote.

**Ground.** The majority extends *Preiser*: release from prison to prerelease
custody is like release to parole, a release from a more restrictive form of
confinement, and so falls within the core of habeas. It rejects the Fifth
Circuit's "automatic accelerated release" test, and probably cites *Skinner* /
*Wilkinson v. Dotson* on claims that would reduce the prisoner's level of custody.
It will not reach exhaustion (the second QP was excluded when the Court limited
the grant) or the §3621(b) review bar.

**Breadth.** Narrow: tied to time-credit claims that would require transfer out
of prison. It will not say that every placement or conditions claim sounds in
habeas, and it leaves open whether habeas is the exclusive vehicle.

**Procedural exits.** A DIG is live, not just conceivable. The government invites
one on the ground that the §3621(b) question would make the QP academic and that
petitioner may soon be moved to a halfway house anyway. I put it at roughly 7–8%.
If the petitioner's transfer moots the case before decision, the likelier
disposition is a *Munsingwear* vacatur, which still disturbs the judgment.
Affirmance, whether the amicus prevails on the merits or the Court affirms on
exhaustion as an alternative ground, I put at about 12–13%.
