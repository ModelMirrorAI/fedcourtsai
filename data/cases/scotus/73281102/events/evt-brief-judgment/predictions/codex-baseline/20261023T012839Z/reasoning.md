## Why 0.78 rather than the anchor or near-certainty

The strongest upward adjustment is unusually direct agreement between the litigating parties on the limited question. The petitioner argues that § 2241's custody language, § 2243's remedial flexibility, historical practice, and release-to-parole precedents permit claims seeking a lower level of custody. The respondent independently accepts the narrower proposition that release from prison into community prerelease custody is habeas relief despite continuing legal custody. See petitioner merits brief, summary at printed pp. 11–15; respondent merits brief, introduction at pp. 2–3 and summary at pp. 14–16. This is not simply the inference that the Court granted review because it dislikes the lower court.

The provisioned Fifth Circuit opinion, appendix pp. Ap.1.1–Ap.1.2, treats transfer to a halfway house or home confinement as insufficient accelerated release and points Maxwell toward a civil-rights action. That creates a relatively clean legal issue. Both merits briefs distinguish release from physical imprisonment from termination of the sentence. Their discussion of Preiser v. Rodriguez and Jones v. Cunningham supplies a route to relief without creating a general judicial power over routine prison placements. References here characterize the briefs' arguments; they do not report an independently retrieved disposition of Maxwell's case.

There are substantial downward adjustments. First, agreement on cognizability does not eliminate a live defense of the judgment: the government maintains that administrative exhaustion and § 3621(b)'s review bar defeat the claim. Its merits brief, pp. 35–42, asks for vacatur and first-instance consideration below but also expressly invites a DIG. An alternative-ground affirmance would count as undisturbed even if the Court agreed with Maxwell on the granted question. Second, the respondent reports likely eligibility for a halfway house in January 2027, potentially before an ordinary merits judgment; that makes mootness a concrete issue, although an actual transfer and complete mootness cannot be assumed from eligibility alone. A mootness vacatur and a DIG have opposite values on the scoring axis. Third, I lack the court-appointed amicus brief, so agreement between the supplied briefs cannot substitute for testing the best defense of the circuit's rule.

My working judgment-class allocation is **63% vacated, 14% reversed, 1% affirmed-in-part/reversed-in-part, 10% affirmed, and 12% DIG**, with no separately material equally divided probability absent a disclosed recusal. The vacatur allocation includes a small possibility of vacatur on mootness grounds rather than the forecast merits ground. Thus the disturbed labels sum to 78%, while 22% leaves the circuit judgment standing. This allocation expresses judgmental uncertainty, not measured frequencies. Vacatur is the modal label because the narrow legal correction leaves unresolved defenses and does not establish entitlement to release.

## Votes, breadth, and stakes

The seven-Justice majority is a lower-confidence forecast than the judgment. A narrow statutory holding centered on actual departure from prison can draw votes across methodological lines. I place Thomas and Alito on the dissenting side because a more restrictive boundary around habeas or a procedural exit is the strongest counterposition; this is a forecast, not an observed position or an assertion that they expressed views in this case. The petitioner's reliance on the execution-of-sentence discussion in Jones also makes conservative agreement quite plausible, so I do not treat a dissent as necessary to the result. No argument transcript or Justice-specific signal was provisioned or retrieved.

The semantic forecast deliberately avoids the petitioner's broadest possible level-of-custody rule. It predicts a specific parole-like physical-release ground and, separately, a rule limited to community prerelease custody rather than all placement or conditions claims. The **0.59 stakes score** reflects national importance to federal earned-credit litigation and the habeas/civil-rights boundary, tempered by the narrow procedural question and the fact that cognizability alone does not award any prisoner credits or a transfer.
