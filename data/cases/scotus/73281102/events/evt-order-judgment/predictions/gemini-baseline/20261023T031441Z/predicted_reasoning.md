# Predicted Reasoning: *William Maxwell v. Albert Thomas, III, Warden* (No. 25-5930)

## 1. Predicted Judgment and Vote Lineup

- **Predicted Judgment:** `reversed` (reversing the judgment of the United States Court of Appeals for the Fifth Circuit and remanding for further proceedings, with `vacated` as a close secondary form of disturbance).
- **Predicted Vote Lineup:** **9–0** (Roberts, C.J., and Thomas, Alito, Sotomayor, Kagan, Gorsuch, Kavanaugh, Barrett, and Jackson, JJ., in the majority). The primary alternative lineup is **7–2** or **8–1**, with Justice Thomas and/or Justice Alito either concurring in the judgment or dissenting on historical habeas scope or case-specific vehicle grounds.

## 2. Scope of Review and Procedural Posture

The Court's June 1, 2026 grant order explicitly limited certiorari to the first question presented, as reformulated:

> *"Whether a claim regarding application of time credits under the First Step Act of 2018, 132 Stat. 5195-5208 (codified in relevant part at 18 U.S.C. §§3631–3635), seeking accelerated transfer to a halfway house or home confinement, can be brought in a habeas petition under 28 U.S.C. §2241."*

The Court declined to grant review on Petitioner's second question presented (whether administrative exhaustion is intertwined with the merits under *Perttu v. Richards*, 605 U.S. 460 (2025)). Because the Solicitor General agrees with Petitioner that 28 U.S.C. § 2241 is an available vehicle for First Step Act (FSA) time-credit claims seeking accelerated transfer to prerelease custody, the Court appointed an amicus curiae (Brian W. Barnes) to defend the Fifth Circuit's judgment below.

A procedural exit—specifically a dismissal as improvidently granted (DIG)—is a minor tail risk (~4%) if merits briefing uncovers severe record ambiguity over whether Maxwell's pro se filings below preserved a claim for earned time credits under 18 U.S.C. §§ 3631–3635 rather than solely discretionary Elderly Offender Pilot Program or § 3621(b) placement requests. However, because the Solicitor General highlighted that exact record argument in the Brief in Opposition and the Court nonetheless granted certiorari on the reformulated statutory question, the Court is strongly expected to resolve the threshold legal question and remand all case-specific exhaustion and preservation issues to the Fifth Circuit. No recusal is indicated on the docket, making an affirmance by an equally divided Court negligible.

## 3. Doctrinal Basis of the Majority (`majority-ground`)

**Proposition:** Applying *Preiser v. Rodriguez*, 411 U.S. 475 (1973), *Wilkinson v. Dotson*, 544 U.S. 74 (2005), and *Jones v. Hendrix*, 599 U.S. 465, 475 (2023), the majority holds that 28 U.S.C. § 2241(c)(3)'s "in custody" requirement encompasses challenges to the execution of a federal sentence that seek a quantum reduction in the level or place of custody—including mandatory application of First Step Act earned time credits under 18 U.S.C. §§ 3632(d)(4)(C) and 3624(g)(2) to accelerate transfer from a prison facility to a halfway house or home confinement—rejecting the Fifth Circuit's rule limiting habeas solely to claims that accelerate complete release from custody.

The majority's reasoning will rest on three converging textual and precedential pillars:

1. **The Text and Structure of 28 U.S.C. § 2241 and the First Step Act:** Under 18 U.S.C. § 3632(d)(4)(C), First Step Act time credits earned by an eligible prisoner *"shall be applied toward time in prerelease custody or supervised release,"* and the Bureau of Prisons Director *"shall transfer eligible prisoners, as determined under section 3624(g), into prerelease custody or supervised release."* Section 3624(g)(2) defines the two forms of prerelease custody as placement in a residential reentry center (halfway house) or home confinement. A prisoner claiming that the BOP has unlawfully refused to calculate or apply statutory time credits that mandate transfer out of a secure prison facility into home confinement or a halfway house is directly challenging the *"execution"* of his federal sentence and alleging that he is being detained *"in a place or manner not authorized by the sentence"* and *"unlawfully been denied parole or good-time credits."* *Jones v. Hendrix*, 599 U.S. at 475.
2. **Rejection of the Fifth and Eighth Circuits' "Complete Release Only" Rule:** The Fifth Circuit below (relying on *Melot v. Bergami*, 970 F.3d 596, 599 (5th Cir. 2020), and aligned with the Eighth Circuit in *Fortner v. Eischen*, No. 24-3596 (8th Cir. Mar. 13, 2026)) held that habeas relief under § 2241 is available only if a favorable determination would automatically entitle the prisoner to accelerated release from BOP custody altogether, and that halfway houses and home confinement are merely different "conditions of confinement." The Court will reject that dichotomy. As the Court recognized in *Skinner v. Switzer*, 562 U.S. 521, 534 (2011) (quoting *Wilkinson v. Dotson*, 544 U.S. at 86 (Scalia, J., concurring)), habeas corpus reaches claims whose relief would *"terminate custody, accelerate the future date of release from custody, [or] reduce the level of custody."* Moving out of a secure correctional institution into one's own home or a community-based halfway house effects a quantum change in the level and place of custody, not a routine adjustment to internal prison conditions (such as cell assignment, diet, or medical care).
3. **Harmonizing Federal Post-Conviction Remedies:** Relegating federal prisoners to a civil-rights or *Bivens* action for statutory time-credit claims seeking transfer to prerelease custody—as the Fifth Circuit did below—would leave federal prisoners without any statutory vehicle to enforce mandatory FSA time credits toward prerelease custody and would split a single statutory provision (18 U.S.C. § 3632(d)(4)(C), which governs credits toward both prerelease custody and supervised release) across two different procedural regimes.

## 4. Breadth of the Holding (`ground-breadth`)

**Proposition:** The majority announces a categorical rule across federal prisoners that any claim seeking application of statutory time credits under 18 U.S.C. §§ 3632(d)(4)(C) and 3624(g) to accelerate transfer from secure prison confinement into prerelease custody (a residential reentry center or home confinement) is cognizable in habeas under 28 U.S.C. § 2241, while declining to extend § 2241 to ordinary prison-transfer or conditions-of-confinement claims within secure facilities and remanding case-specific administrative-exhaustion and eligibility defenses to the Fifth Circuit.

Rather than issuing a fact-bound ruling tied solely to Maxwell's individual sentence, the Court will establish a clear nationwide rule resolving the circuit split in favor of the Second, Third, Sixth, and Ninth Circuits (e.g., *Woodall v. Federal Bureau of Prisons*, 432 F.3d 235 (3d Cir. 2005); *Levine v. Apker*, 455 F.3d 71 (2d Cir. 2006)). At the same time, to maintain a firm boundary between § 2241 and ordinary prison-conditions litigation, the majority will cabin its holding to transfers between qualitatively distinct levels of custody—specifically, from secure institutional confinement to community-based prerelease custody (halfway house or home confinement) pursuant to statutory time-credit or placement mandates—expressly distinguishing garden-variety facility-to-facility transfers, security-classification disputes, and internal conditions of confinement. Finally, following its standard practice as a court of review rather than first view, the Court will decline to adjudicate the government's alternative arguments regarding administrative exhaustion and the sufficiency of Maxwell's pro se pleadings, leaving those issues for the Fifth Circuit on remand.
