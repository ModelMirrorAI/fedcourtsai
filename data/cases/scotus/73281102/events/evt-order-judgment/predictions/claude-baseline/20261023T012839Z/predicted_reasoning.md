# Predicted outcome — Maxwell v. Thomas (No. 25-5930), judgment after grant

**Judgment: reversed** (and remanded), disturbing the Fifth Circuit's judgment. Vacatur and remand is the
close alternative label; both disturb.

**Vote: 7–2.** Roberts, Sotomayor, Kagan, Gorsuch, Kavanaugh, Barrett and Jackson in the majority;
Thomas and Alito dissenting, on the view that a request for transfer to a halfway house or home
confinement goes to the conditions or place of confinement rather than the fact or duration of custody, so it
falls outside core habeas. A unanimous judgment is the main alternative.

**Question reached.** The Court took only the limited question: whether a First Step Act time-credit claim
seeking accelerated transfer to prerelease custody may be brought under 28 U.S.C. §2241. It does not reach
the Perttu/exhaustion question (QP II), which it declined to grant.

**Ground.** The majority will hold that such a claim challenges how the sentence is carried out and the
level of custody. Prerelease custody in a residential reentry center or home confinement is a
less restrictive form of custody, and moving a prisoner into it is the kind of relief habeas has traditionally
given. The Court will lean on *Jones v. Hendrix*'s statement that §2241 covers a prisoner detained "in a place
or manner not authorized by the sentence" or "unlawfully denied … good-time credits," and on the
*Preiser*/*Wilkinson v. Dotson*/*Skinner* formulation, which includes claims that would "reduce the level of
custody." It rejects the Fifth Circuit's *Melot* test, which requires that success automatically
accelerate release. The appointed amicus will argue for the judgment below, and the majority will reject
that argument.

**Breadth.** The rule is categorical for this class of claims, not limited to Maxwell. The Court will
likely not say whether §2241 is the *exclusive* vehicle. It remands for the lower courts to take up exhaustion,
which the government pressed as an alternative ground but the court of appeals never reached.

**Procedural exits.** A DIG is unlikely but possible, because the exhaustion finding below could make the
question academic for this petitioner. The Court granted over that vehicle objection, though, and appointed an
amicus to defend the judgment, which signals it means to decide the question. No recusal is apparent, so an
equally divided affirmance is not live.
