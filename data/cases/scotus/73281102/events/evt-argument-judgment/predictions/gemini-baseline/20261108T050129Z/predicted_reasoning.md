# Predicted Reasoning of the Court: *William Maxwell v. Sandra Dinis, Acting Warden* (No. 25-5930)

## 1. Judgment and Vote Lineup

- **Judgment**: The judgment of the United States Court of Appeals for the Fifth Circuit is **vacated**, and the case is remanded for further proceedings consistent with the Court's opinion (`vacated`).
- **Vote Split and Lineup**: **9-0**. All nine Justices (Roberts, C.J., and Thomas, Alito, Sotomayor, Kagan, Gorsuch, Kavanaugh, Barrett, and Jackson, JJ.) join the judgment and opinion of the Court.
- **Authorship**: **Justice Kavanaugh** delivers the opinion of the Court (with Justices Gorsuch, Kagan, Barrett, and Chief Justice Roberts as the principal alternative assignees). Most likely no Justice writes separately, though there is a moderate possibility of a brief concurrence by Justice Alito or Justice Thomas emphasizing that threshold defenses such as administrative exhaustion and 18 U.S.C. § 3621(b) remain open for consideration on remand.

## 2. Questions Reached vs. Left for Remand

The Court reaches and resolves the sole Question Presented on which certiorari was granted: whether a federal prisoner's claim that earned time credits under the First Step Act of 2018, 18 U.S.C. §§ 3624(g), 3632(d)(4)(C), entitle him to accelerated transfer out of prison into prerelease custody (a residential reentry center / halfway house or home confinement) is cognizable in a habeas corpus petition under 28 U.S.C. § 2241.

In accordance with its ordinary practice as a court of review, not of first view, the Court declines to resolve in the first instance the government's alternative arguments for affirming the dismissal of Maxwell's petition—specifically:
1. Whether Maxwell failed to exhaust administrative remedies (the ground on which the district court relied, but which the Fifth Circuit declined to address); and
2. Whether 18 U.S.C. § 3621(b)'s bar on judicial review of "a designation of a place of imprisonment under this subsection" applies to a prisoner's claim for mandatory transfer to prerelease custody using earned time credits under 18 U.S.C. §§ 3624(g) and 3632(d)(4)(C) (an argument the government did not raise in the Fifth Circuit or in its brief in opposition), including whether the government forfeited that argument.

The Court also rejects any suggestion to dismiss the writ as improvidently granted or as moot, noting that the Fifth Circuit decided the case solely on a categorical, published rule barring § 2241 review of First Step Act prerelease-custody claims and that Maxwell's claim for additional earned time credits remains live (including because earned time credits can be applied toward up to 12 months of early supervised release under 18 U.S.C. §§ 3624(g)(3) and 3632(d)(4)(C)).

## 3. Doctrinal Ground and Breadth of the Holding

### A. Doctrinal Ground (`majority-ground`)

The Court holds that under 28 U.S.C. § 2241 and *Preiser v. Rodriguez*, 411 U.S. 475 (1973), a federal prisoner's claim that earned time credits under the First Step Act (18 U.S.C. §§ 3624(g), 3632(d)(4)(C)) entitle him to accelerated transfer out of a prison facility into prerelease custody in a halfway house or home confinement challenges the fact or duration of physical imprisonment and is therefore cognizable in habeas corpus.

The Court's reasoning rests on three pillars:
1. **Precedent Governing Conditional Release From Prison**: Under *Preiser v. Rodriguez*, 411 U.S. at 487–489, *Wilkinson v. Dotson*, 544 U.S. 74 (2005), *Skinner v. Switzer*, 562 U.S. 521, 534 (2011), and *Jones v. Hendrix*, 599 U.S. 465, 475 (2023), a prisoner who seeks application of statutory time credits to shorten his stay behind prison walls and obtain earlier conditional release into the community brings a core habeas claim, even though he remains under legal custody and subject to supervision and revocation after leaving prison. When Congress abolished federal parole in the Sentencing Reform Act of 1984, it replaced parole with supervised release and prerelease custody (home confinement and residential reentry centers) to serve the same transitional reintegration function.
2. **Statutory Text and Structure of the First Step Act**: The First Step Act creates a mandatory entitlement ("shall transfer eligible prisoners ... into prerelease custody or supervised release," 18 U.S.C. § 3632(d)(4)(C)) and expressly distinguishes prerelease custody in a residential reentry center or home confinement from imprisonment in a prison facility. In particular, 18 U.S.C. § 3624(g)(5) provides that if a prisoner violates the conditions of prerelease custody, the Director of the Bureau of Prisons may revoke prerelease custody and require the prisoner to serve the remainder of his term *"in prison"*—confirming that a prisoner in home confinement or a halfway house is no longer confined "in prison."
3. **Historical Habeas Practice**: The writ of habeas corpus ad subjiciendum historically tested whether the custodian had lawful authority to detain the petitioner in the specific form and place of confinement imposed (*In re Bonner*, 151 U.S. 242 (1894)), not merely whether the petitioner was entitled to unconditional freedom from all legal restraint.

### B. Breadth of the Holding (`ground-breadth`)

The Court adopts a narrow rule limited to claims seeking accelerated release out of a prison facility into community prerelease custody (a residential reentry center or home confinement), expressly declining to decide whether transfers between different prison facilities or security levels within prison are cognizable in habeas and leaving alternative defenses such as administrative exhaustion and 18 U.S.C. § 3621(b) for remand.

By anchoring its holding on the transition *out of a prison facility* into non-prison prerelease custody in the community, the Court resolves the circuit split over First Step Act time-credit claims while avoiding any need to rule on whether prison-to-prison transfers (such as transfers from a supermax facility to a medium-security prison or minimum-security prison camp) can be brought in habeas corpus rather than in a conditions-of-confinement action.
