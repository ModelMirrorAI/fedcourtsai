**Anchor.** The statpack merits section publishes an `excluded` count, so it is quotable. The grant Term is OT2025 (cert granted June 1, 2026). Pooling grant Terms 2015–2024 strictly before that gives 376 disturbed of 534 parsed, **70.4%**. That covers OT2017–OT2024 only, because 2015 and 2016 have no parsed rows; coverage in those Terms is near-complete (for example 2024: 77 parsed of 79 granted). The pool clears the 30-judgment floor.

**Adjustments up.**
- **Respondent confesses error.** The United States, as respondent, agrees that the Fifth Circuit's rule is wrong and asks for vacatur. The Court had to appoint an amicus to defend the judgment, and at argument that amicus did not defend the Fifth Circuit's reasoning. Cases in this posture are disturbed far more often than the base rate.
- **Bench lean.** Sotomayor, Kagan and Jackson were plainly hostile to the amicus's position. Barrett and Kavanaugh both asked, in substance, why the Court cannot decide the QP and leave §3621(b) for remand, and the SG agreed it could. Gorsuch pressed both sides on line-drawing (the "camel's nose") without signalling a vote. Thomas probed whether prerelease custody is really "release", which favours the amicus. Alito favoured a DIG. A question from the bench is not a vote; I read these as moderate leans only.
- **The argument-features counts are consistent with this reading.** Coverage: 117 Justice turns, 116 attributed, 4/4 segments. Jackson spoke the most (1,044 words), and her questioning was split across all three advocates. Kagan's words fell mostly on the amicus (228 of 289). The counts are not votes.

**Adjustments down, and why not further.**
- **DIG risk (~10%).** The SG floated a DIG on the §3621(b) review bar and impending mootness. Alito endorsed it ("buyer's regret"). Jackson and Sotomayor rebuked the government for raising this only now. Barrett and Kavanaugh treated §3621(b) as something to leave for remand. Five votes to DIG looks unlikely.
- **Mootness.** The projected release date is January 22, 2027. If the Court does not decide first and finds the case moot, the usual result would be Munsingwear vacatur, which counts as disturbed. A dismissal without vacatur would not. The petitioner's argument that the credits still bear on supervised release (citing Chafin) gives the Court a way to keep the case alive.
- **Affirmance (~5%).** The amicus's argument that prerelease custody is still BOP custody, so the claim concerns conditions of confinement, may appeal to Thomas and possibly Alito. I do not see five votes for it.

Net: 1 − (DIG ≈ 0.10 + affirm ≈ 0.05) ≈ 0.85.

**Judgment label.** I chose `vacated` over `reversed` because the SG asked for vacatur and the petitioner agrees that exhaustion is live on remand. Plain reversal is quite possible, but both labels count as disturbed.

**Votes.** For the per-Justice reference I used Terms ≤ OT2024. Disturb rates in OT2024 ran 58.8% (Alito) to 76.9% (Kavanaugh), and Thomas was 32 of 52 (61.5%), so Thomas and Alito disturb least often. That fits my case-specific reading that they are the likeliest dissenters, or would write separately for a DIG. Gorsuch is the most uncertain majority vote.

**Discounts.** I did not read the amicus's merits brief (none was staged) or the replies in full. The mootness and DIG mechanics are the main uncertainty. I have no knowledge of this case's outcome.
