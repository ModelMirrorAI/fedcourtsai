# Predicted Court decision

## Judgment and votes

The Court will **vacate the Fifth Circuit's judgment and remand**, by **7–2**. Roberts, Sotomayor, Kagan, Gorsuch, Kavanaugh, Barrett, and Jackson will join the disposition; Thomas and Alito will dissent. This removes the appellate court's categorical habeas obstacle without ordering Maxwell's transfer, computing his credits, or resolving every alternative basis for dismissal.

The exact judgment label is a separate forecast: I expect vacatur, not merely an unspecified win for Maxwell. Reversal and remand is the strongest alternative because the appellate court applied an erroneous legal rule. A DIG is a live procedural alternative, but not the modal result. There is no disclosed recusal making an equally divided affirmance a realistic principal scenario.

## Majority ground

The Court will hold that §2241 permits an eligible prisoner to claim that earned First Step Act credits require an earlier transfer from prison to home confinement or a halfway house. The relevant distinction will be between an attack on ordinary prison conditions and a demand for release from physical imprisonment into a legally distinct community-based custody arrangement. Continuing legal custody does not make release from prison irrelevant to habeas.

The majority will apply the logic of **Preiser v. Rodriguez** and its treatment of release on parole. It will reject the proposition that a person must seek unconditional freedom from all restraints for habeas to be the proper vehicle. The identity of the supervising executive agency and the possibility of escape liability will not outweigh the nature of the requested statutory release from prison. This is an application of existing habeas doctrine, not an overruling of the conditions-versus-release distinction.

## Breadth and reserved questions

The majority will deliberately adopt a **narrow prison-to-community holding**, not Maxwell's broadest formulation covering every material reduction in custody restrictions. It will not decide whether transfer from a high-security prison to a minimum-security prison, or an ordinary classification challenge, belongs in habeas. The opinion will treat First Step Act prerelease custody as sufficiently analogous to parole without constructing an exhaustive taxonomy of all possible custody changes.

It will decide the reformulated question in the June 1 grant, leaving the original petition's exhaustion/jury-trial question outside its holding. It will reserve the application of §3621(b)'s judicial-review bar, the exhaustion dispute, the accuracy and application of Maxwell's credits, and any remaining eligibility questions for the lower courts. Recognizing a habeas vehicle will not establish that every otherwise cognizable First Step Act challenge survives another statutory bar.

## Writings

My point author forecast is **Justice Barrett**, with **Justice Kavanaugh** the closest alternative. The anticipated opinion follows the route Barrett expressly identified at argument: answer the limited habeas question and leave a disputed, separately developed review-bar defense for remand. This is a weak assignment prediction rather than a claim to know the sitting's allocation.

I expect **Justice Alito to dissent, joined by Justice Thomas**, emphasizing the unresolved review bar, impending transfer, and the hazards of drawing a prison/prerelease line before those issues are resolved. Thomas is the strongest alternative dissent author and may additionally stress the continuing BOP custodial relationship. Gorsuch is most likely to join the narrow majority, although his security-level hypotheticals make a limiting concurrence or an additional dissent plausible. My modal forecast includes no separate concurrence.

## Procedural timing

The Court will likely seek to resolve the case before the projected January 22, 2027 transfer. It will reject a discretionary DIG based only on unresolved alternative defenses and possible future mootness. I do not predict a holding that release can never moot a time-credit case, or adoption of Maxwell's supervised-release argument. If events overtake the case, a mootness-related vacatur or a DIG could replace the substantive opinion forecast here; that contingency is included in the probabilities rather than hidden in the judgment call.
