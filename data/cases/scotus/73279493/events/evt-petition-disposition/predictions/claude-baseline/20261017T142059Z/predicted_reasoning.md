# Forecast — Watson v. Bradsher, No. 25-651

**Disposition: certiorari denied** (P(grant) ≈ 6%).

- **Distributions.** The docket shows one distribution (for the 2/20/2026
  conference), followed by a Court-requested response (Feb 2, 2026). The
  requested brief in opposition does not appear on the record I was shown.
  Once a response is filed the petition is re-distributed for a fresh
  conference, so I expect **at least one further distribution** (≈88%) —
  most likely exactly one, followed by a denial without further relisting.
  A second relist beyond that would be a strong grant signal and I do not
  expect it.
- **CVSG.** No. Both parties are private; the federal interest in the
  scope of § 523(a)(6) is real but diffuse, and the Court rarely calls for
  the SG's views in private bankruptcy-tort discharge disputes.
- **Question presented, if granted.** The Court would take a reformulated
  QP 1 — whether § 523(a)(6)'s "willful" prong is satisfied by a debtor who
  intends the act (confinement) but subjectively believes it lawful, i.e.
  the subjective-vs-objective split (6th/9th/10th vs 5th/11th) — and leave
  QP 2's fact-bound "implied malice from excessiveness" framing behind.
- **Summary disposition.** Unlikely; no intervening decision of this Court
  bears directly on § 523(a)(6) such that a GVR is called for. If the Court
  grants, it sets the case for plenary review.
- **Dissent from denial.** Not expected; no Justice has signalled interest
  in the question, and the case lacks the features that draw separate
  writings.
