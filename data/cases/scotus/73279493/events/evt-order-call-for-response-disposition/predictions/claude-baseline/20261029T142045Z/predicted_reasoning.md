# Forecast: Watson v. Bradsher, No. 25-651 (response-requested moment)

**State forecast from.** The petition was distributed once, for the 2/20/2026
conference, and on Feb 2, 2026 the Court requested a response, due March 4,
2026. It has not been relisted and there is no CVSG.

**Disposition.** Cert will be **denied**. The call for a response shows that
someone in chambers read the petition. The Court's reply will be the
respondent's brief in opposition. Most petitions that draw such a call are
still denied.

**Further distribution.** This is close to certain. After the brief in
opposition is filed, or the response time runs out, the petition will be
distributed again for a spring 2026 conference. Expect it to be decided at that
first post-response conference. Relisting it again after that is unlikely.

**CVSG.** None is expected. This is a private dispute over the discharge of a
state-court tort judgment. The United States is not a party, and the
bankruptcy-policy interest here is too small for the Court to want the SG's
views.

**If granted.** The Court would most likely take a reformulated version of QP 1,
on whether § 523(a)(6) "willful … injury" requires the debtor's subjective
intent to invade a legally protected interest, so that a genuine belief of
legal justification negates willfulness. That framing reaches the
Sixth/Ninth/Tenth vs. Fifth/Eleventh disagreement over subjective and objective
tests after *Geiger*. QP 2, on implied malice from "excessiveness," is
fact-bound and would be folded into QP 1 or left behind.

**Summary route.** A grant would most likely lead to plenary review. No
intervening decision invites a GVR, and a summary reversal of a published
circuit opinion on a contested statutory question is improbable.

**Separate writing.** I expect no dissent from denial and no statement
respecting it.
