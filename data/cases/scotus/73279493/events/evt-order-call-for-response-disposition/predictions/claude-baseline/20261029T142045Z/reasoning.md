# Reasoning for P(grant) = 0.07

**Inputs read.** The snapshot `record/snapshots/2026-02-03.json`, which cuts
off on the response-request day. I also read `context.json` (forward mode,
band `baseline` under sal-v4, distribution_count 1, no CVSG, Term 2025), the

**Anchor.** The `baseline` band's bracketed *reached* rate from the statpack's
sal-v4 segment table. I pooled the Terms strictly before OT2025, which are
OT2017–OT2024. Per Term the rates are 4.9, 4.8, 4.7, 4.5, 5.7, 5.9, 5.9 and
5.9%, each on n of about 1,200–1,750. That pools to about 5.4%. The band cut on

relist-0 bucket reads 1.8% grant family. Both are where-it-ended figures, so
they understate this case.

**Adjustments.**
- *Up:* The response request is the main new signal. No committed table
  conditions on it. My judgment is that a called-for response roughly doubles
  the odds against an otherwise-unsignalled paid petition. The petition also
  pleads a real, recognized split: Markowitz, Su and Moore against Miller and
  Kane on the subjective vs. objective reading of "willful" after *Geiger*.
  The opinion below is published (147 F.4th 1330), and the issue recurs in
  bankruptcy courts.
- *Down:* The vehicle is poor in several ways. The judgment rests on a general
  jury verdict that the courts below split between slander, which was
  discharged, and false imprisonment. The Eleventh Circuit's analysis leans on
  implied malice from "excessive" conduct, which is fact-bound. The dollar
  stakes are small and the parties are private. The § 523(a)(6) post-*Geiger*
  split is long-standing, and to my knowledge the Court has passed on it
  repeatedly. Counsel are not repeat SCOTUS practitioners.

Net, I land at 7%, a little above the band anchor. I hold this loosely. Most of
the uplift comes from the response request, and I cannot calibrate that signal
from the pack.

**Other claims.** relist-increment is 0.97, because a re-distribution after the
response is routine. The harness masks this claim at this moment. CVSG is 0.03,
since it is a private dispute. summary-disposition-route given a grant is 0.08.
dissent-from-denial given a denial is 0.03, because the issue is technical and
low-salience. Gorsuch, with his textualist and common-law-tort interest, is the
most likely writer if anyone writes.

**Caveats.** This cell runs on 2026-10-29, about eight months after the
cutoff, so the petition has very likely been disposed of already. I did not
check the live docket or search for the disposition, so that I would not see
the outcome. I do not recall this petition's outcome from training. Corpus
priors (`fedcourts query`) gave generic recent grants and nothing specific to
this petition. I did not use CourtListener MCP.
