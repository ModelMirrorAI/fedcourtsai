We predict the Supreme Court will deny review in *Stanley Kappell Watson v. Shenekka Bradsher*, giving the petition a 4% chance of being granted. The case stems from an altercation at a Georgia lounge in which Watson, then a county commissioner, falsely accused two women of stealing his wallet—which he had actually left in his car—and demanded police detain them. After a state jury awarded the women $150,500 for slander, false imprisonment, and battery, Watson filed for bankruptcy. Lower federal courts ruled that two-thirds of the debt, tied to false imprisonment, could not be erased in bankruptcy because the injury was willful and malicious. Although the Supreme Court requested a response from the women, the Eleventh Circuit decision under review did not actually rule on the underlying bankruptcy-law question because Watson failed to properly preserve it on appeal, making the case a poor vehicle for Supreme Court review.
