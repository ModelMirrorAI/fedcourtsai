## 2. Case-Specific Adjustments

Starting from a ~5.2% risk-set anchor (and ~6% generic post-CFR private-party prior), the specific features of *Watson v. Bradsher* warrant a net downward adjustment to **4.0% (`0.04`)**:

### A. Severe Vehicle and Preservation Defects (Strong Downward Adjustment)
A careful reading of the opinions below in `record/documents/petition.txt` reveals a fatal vehicle flaw that will become immediately apparent once Respondents file their Brief in Opposition:
1. **The Decision Below Did Not Rule on the Question Presented:** In the decision under review (*In re Watson*, 147 F.4th 1330, App. 1a–14a), the Eleventh Circuit explicitly held that Watson was **barred from challenging** the nondischargeability of the false-imprisonment debt. In the first appeal (*Watson I*, 2022), the district court affirmed that the false-imprisonment debt was willful and malicious under 11 U.S.C. § 523(a)(6), reversed as to slander per se, and remanded to the bankruptcy court for reanalysis of slander and damages allocation (App. 59a–92a). On remand (*Watson II*), in the second appeal to the district court (App. 28a), and in his opening brief to the Eleventh Circuit (App. 8a–10a), Watson challenged **only** the two-thirds allocation of compensatory damages, punitive damages, and attorney's fees—not the underlying nondischargeability of the false-imprisonment liability.
2. **Waiver and Law of the Case:** As the Eleventh Circuit explained (App. 8a–10a), even though the district court's 2022 remand order (*Watson I*) was interlocutory and thus not immediately appealable to the Eleventh Circuit, Watson forfeited his right to review of the false-imprisonment liability determination by failing to raise it in his second appeal to the district court (*Watson II*) and in his opening brief to the Eleventh Circuit (raising it only in his reply brief).
3. **Implication for Certiorari:** To reach the Question Presented on § 523(a)(6) and *Kawaauhau v. Geiger*, 523 U.S. 57 (1998), the Supreme Court would first have to overturn the Eleventh Circuit's routine application of appellate waiver and law-of-the-case rules—an issue the Question Presented (`record/documents/questions-presented.txt`) does not even frame. Why did the Court call for a response on February 2, 2026? Because Respondents never appeared or filed a response to the petition, and the petition's framing makes it appear at first glance as though the Eleventh Circuit issued a published opinion deepening a circuit split under *Geiger*. A clerk recommending a CFR to hear from the absent respondents before disposing of a published circuit opinion is standard practice; once the Brief in Opposition points out the waiver holding on pages 8a–10a of the appendix, the petition is an obvious deny.

### B. Illusory Circuit Split on the Facts Found Below (Downward Adjustment)
Petitioner invokes the genuine, well-known tension among the circuits over whether *Geiger*'s "substantial certainty" test for "willful" injury under § 523(a)(6) is strictly subjective (e.g., *In re Su*, 290 F.3d 1140 (9th Cir. 2002)) or permits an objective showing (e.g., *In re Miller*, 156 F.3d 598 (5th Cir. 1998)). However:
- On the **slander** claim, where the subjective/objective distinction actually mattered because the bankruptcy court found Watson genuinely believed the women stole his wallet, the bankruptcy court on remand applied the **subjective** standard from the Ninth Circuit's *In re Su* decision and ruled **in Watson's favor**, holding the slander debt dischargeable (App. 50a–53a).
- On the **false imprisonment** claim, the district court in *Watson I* found that Watson intentionally procured the women's confinement (causing Bradsher to be handcuffed and placed in a patrol car while taunting her and wielding his authority as a county commissioner after a search of their purses found no wallet) and that confinement itself is the intended injury in false imprisonment, accompanied by excessive, bad-faith behavior establishing malice. There is no square circuit split holding that a debtor who intentionally instigates a physical detention and abuses official authority is immunized from § 523(a)(6) merely because he subjectively suspected a theft.

### C. Positive Signals (Modest Offset)
The only factors keeping the probability at 4.0% rather than <1% are (1) the Court actively called for a response on February 2, 2026, showing at least one chamber flagged the petition for closer inspection, and (2) the underlying opinion is published (*147 F.4th 1330*) and concerns a federal bankruptcy statute (*11 U.S.C. § 523(a)(6)*). Even so, the waiver/law-of-the-case barrier is virtually insurmountable at the cert stage.
