## What happened

Stanley Watson, a former county commissioner, wrongly accused Sheneeka Bradsher and Zarinah Ali of stealing his wallet at a bar and pressed police to arrest them; he found his wallet the next day. A Georgia jury awarded the women damages for slander, battery, and false imprisonment. After Watson filed for bankruptcy, the women sued to keep that debt alive, arguing it resulted from deliberate wrongdoing. Bankruptcy and district courts eventually ruled the slander debt could be wiped out, because Watson genuinely believed his accusation was true, but the false-imprisonment debt could not, because he intended the confinement itself. The Eleventh Circuit affirmed, and Watson asks the Supreme Court to review that decision.

## What the Court is being asked

Federal law lets people escape old debts in bankruptcy except for debts caused by intentional, harmful wrongdoing. The petition asks the Court to decide what counts as that kind of wrongdoing when someone genuinely, if mistakenly, believed his actions were legally justified. Specifically, it asks whether "injury" for this purpose means any real-world harm the person meant to cause, or only harm the person knew was legally wrongful — and whether a debt for wrongly confining someone can survive bankruptcy even though the debtor sincerely thought the confinement was lawful.

## Where it stands

The petition was filed in December 2025 after a court-approved extension, and the case was distributed for the Court's February 20, 2026 conference. The Court then asked the other side to file a response, due March 4, 2026, so the petition has not yet been acted on.
