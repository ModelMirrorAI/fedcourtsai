## In brief

A debtor asks the Court to decide whether his false-imprisonment debt can be excused from bankruptcy discharge despite his genuine but mistaken belief his conduct was lawful.

## What happened

Stanley Watson, a former county commissioner, accused Sheneeka Bradsher and Zarinah Ali of stealing his wallet at a bar and pressed police to arrest them. His wallet was later found in his own car. A state-court jury awarded the women damages for false imprisonment, slander, and battery. After Watson filed for bankruptcy, the women sought to have that debt excluded from discharge. The bankruptcy court found Watson genuinely believed the women had taken his wallet, discharged the slander and battery debts, but held the false-imprisonment debt nondischargeable. The district court and the Eleventh Circuit affirmed that result.

## What the Court is being asked

The case turns on a bankruptcy law provision that keeps debts for "willful and malicious injury" from being wiped out. The Court is asked whether that kind of injury means simply causing harm in fact, or whether it requires intending to violate someone's legal rights. Relatedly, the Court is asked whether a debtor who honestly but mistakenly believed his actions were legally justified can still be found to have caused that kind of injury, or whether courts may find such intent simply from how excessive the conduct looked.

## Where it stands

Watson filed his petition for certiorari on December 2, 2025. The Court distributed the case for its February 20, 2026 conference and, rather than acting then, asked the respondents, Bradsher and Ali, to file a response, due March 4, 2026.

## What each outcome would mean

Watson, the debtor, is asking the Court to take up the case and overturn the Eleventh Circuit's ruling. If the Court grants review, it would hear full arguments and decide what "injury" means under this discharge exception, without yet deciding who wins. If it denies review, the Eleventh Circuit's decision stands, leaving Watson's debt to Bradsher and Ali for false imprisonment nondischargeable.
