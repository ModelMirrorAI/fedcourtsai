## What happened

Paul Maney and other Oregon prisoners sued the state and several corrections officials, arguing that Oregon's overall response to COVID-19 in its prisons between March 2020 and May 2022 was unreasonable and violated the constitutional ban on cruel and unusual punishment. A federal district court had earlier found the state's initial pandemic response met constitutional standards, but later allowed the case to proceed as a class action and denied the officials qualified immunity, a legal protection that shields government officials from being sued personally unless they violated clearly established rights. The Ninth Circuit Court of Appeals affirmed that denial, ruling a jury should decide whether the state's overall, multi-year response across its prison facilities was reasonable. Oregon then asked the Supreme Court to step in.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked two related questions. First, whether prison officials can be held responsible for a pandemic response's overall reasonableness across many facilities and years, rather than for specific individual decisions or actions. Second, whether it was already clearly settled, at the time officials acted, that this kind of overall response could count as cruel and unusual punishment, given that a judge had ruled the opposite in 2020, before the officials would be protected by qualified immunity from being sued personally.

## Where it stands

After the Solicitor General's office requested and received extensions, Oregon filed its petition and the prisoners initially waived their right to respond before the Court asked for a response anyway. Both sides then jointly asked to dismiss the petition, and the Court dismissed it in July 2026.
