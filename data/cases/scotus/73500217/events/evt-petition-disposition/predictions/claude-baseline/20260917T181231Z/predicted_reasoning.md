# Forecast: what the Court will do with No. 25-1317

**Disposition.** The petition will be **denied** on the order list following the
September 28, 2026 Long Conference (i.e., the first October 2026 order list), without
a call for a response and without separate writing. P(any grant) = 0.012.

**Relists.** The docket shows **one** distribution (June 24, 2026, for the Conference
of 9/28/2026) and has never been relisted. I expect **no further distribution**:
P(at least one more) = 0.10. The realistic paths to a second distribution are a
call for a response (respondent waived on June 12 and the Court distributed twelve
days later without requesting one), a Long-Conference reschedule, or a Justice
holding it to write. None looks likely on this record.

**CVSG.** None is on the docket, and I expect none: P = 0.01. The dispute is between
a private physician and a private hospital; the Court would call for the respondent's
views before the Solicitor General's, and it has done neither.

**Which question, if any.** If the Court took anything it would not take the QP as
written (a comparative "same right as service members" framing that does not map onto
a holding). It would reformulate to: whether 21 U.S.C. § 360bbb-3(e)(1)(A)(ii)(III)
creates an implied private right of action enforceable against a private employer
notwithstanding 21 U.S.C. § 337(a). I do not expect it to take that either.

**Summary disposition versus plenary review.** Conditional on a grant, P(disposed of
in the cert order itself) = 0.30. There is no intervening decision of this Court to
GVR against and no pending merits case on EUA informed consent, so a grant would more
likely mean plenary review than a summary route, though on a vehicle this thin any grant
is improbable to begin with.

**Dissent or statement respecting denial.** Conditional on denial, P(some Justice
writes) = 0.03. The Justices who have written on vaccine-mandate denials did so on
constitutional free-exercise claims against government mandates. This is an implied
statutory cause of action against a private hospital, resting on Cort v. Ash, which
the current Court reads narrowly; I expect a silent denial.

**If granted (conditional only).** The likely ground would be that § 337(a) and
Buckman foreclose a private FDCA remedy and that § 360bbb-3(e) conditions the
Secretary's authorization rather than conferring an individual right under
Sandoval and Gonzaga, producing an affirmance. That outcome makes a grant
even less attractive to the Court, since the Ninth Circuit already reached it.
