No CVSG is anticipated, as no federal party is involved in the suit and the United States' interests are not directly implicated by a private suit against a hospital. Furthermore, the respondent waived their right to respond, indicating a lack of stakes that would draw the Court's attention.

If granted, the Court would likely affirm the Ninth Circuit's ruling that there is no private right of action for civilians to enforce informed consent requirements for EUA drugs under 21 U.S.C. § 360bbb-3. The Court would likely leave the difference between military and civilian provisions to Congress.

A summary disposition (GVR) is highly unlikely as there is no intervening decision or change in law that bears on the issue. Should the Court deny the petition, expect no separate writing or dissent from denial, as the case poses a standard procedural bar (lack of a private right of action) rather than a deeply contested constitutional issue.
