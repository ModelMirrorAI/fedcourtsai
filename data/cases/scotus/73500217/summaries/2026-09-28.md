## In brief

A surgeon asks the Court to revive her lawsuit claiming she has the same right to sue over vaccine informed-consent violations as service members do.

## What happened

Providence Health & Services – Oregon required Dr. Melanie Crites-Bachert, a surgeon with operating privileges there, to get a COVID-19 vaccine to keep her privileges. She refused, citing a federal law provision requiring that people be informed of their option to accept or refuse products authorized for emergency use, and was suspended. She sued Providence in 2023. The district court dismissed her case, and the Ninth Circuit affirmed in November 2025, holding that the relevant federal drug law is enforceable only by the government, not by private lawsuits. The Ninth Circuit denied rehearing in December 2025.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked whether a civilian can sue to enforce her right to be informed of the option to refuse an emergency-use product, the same way courts have allowed service members to do under a related military law provision. Crites-Bachert argues the two provisions come from the same legislation and should be read to give the same enforcement right to civilians and to members of the armed forces alike.

## Where it stands

Providence waived its right to respond to the petition, and the case was distributed for the Court's September 28, 2026 conference. The docket shows no action since that distribution.

## What each outcome would mean

Crites-Bachert, the surgeon, is asking the Court to take up her case. If the Court grants the petition, it would agree to hear the case and decide the informed-consent question itself, without yet deciding who wins. If it denies the petition, the Ninth Circuit's ruling against her stays in place, meaning her lawsuit remains dismissed and Providence's win in the lower courts stands.
