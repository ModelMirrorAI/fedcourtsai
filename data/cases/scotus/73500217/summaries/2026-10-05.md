## In brief

A surgeon denied hospital privileges for refusing a COVID-19 vaccine asked the Supreme Court to revive her lawsuit; the Court declined.

## What happened

Dr. Melanie Crites-Bachert held surgical privileges at Providence Health & Services – Oregon. Providence required staff members to receive a COVID-19 vaccine, which was authorized for emergency use. When she refused, Providence suspended her privileges. She sued, arguing a federal law protecting informed consent to emergency-use products gave her a right to sue. The district court dismissed her case, and the Ninth Circuit affirmed, holding that federal drug law reserves enforcement to the government and that she had no private right to sue. The Ninth Circuit denied rehearing, and she asked the Supreme Court to review that decision.

## What the Court is being asked

The case asks whether a civilian can sue to enforce a right to informed consent before receiving a drug authorized for emergency use, the same way the law has been read to let members of the armed forces sue, or whether that right can only be enforced by the government itself.
