## In brief

The Court declined to hear a former credit union executive's challenge to a law that blocked him from suing in federal court to stop an agency's enforcement case against him.

## What happened

Jeffrey Moats led a Texas credit union until regulators placed it under the National Credit Union Administration's control and fired him. The NCUA later charged him with financial misconduct and sought penalties through its own in-house proceeding, run by an administrative law judge. Moats sued in federal district court, arguing that the judge was unconstitutionally shielded from removal and that he was entitled to a jury trial, among other claims. The district court dismissed his suit, ruling that a federal statute stripped it of jurisdiction to interfere with the agency's proceeding. The Fifth Circuit affirmed, relying on its related ruling in a similar case involving bank regulators.

## What the Court is being asked

The case asks whether a law barring courts from interfering with notices or orders issued in this kind of agency enforcement action also blocks a lawsuit that challenges the constitutionality of the agency's structure and procedures themselves, rather than challenging a specific order. It also asks whether Congress may constitutionally cut off court access for this kind of structural challenge at all, requiring the challenger to go through the full agency process first.

## Where it stands

The petition was distributed for the Court's October 9, 2026 conference, and on October 13, 2026, the Court denied the petition.

## What each outcome would mean

Moats asked the Court to take up his case. By denying the petition, the Court leaves the Fifth Circuit's ruling in place: Moats cannot bring his constitutional challenge in federal district court while the NCUA's enforcement proceeding is pending, and he must pursue his claims through the agency process and later appeal to a federal appeals court if he receives an adverse order. The denial does not decide whether the Fifth Circuit's reasoning was correct.
