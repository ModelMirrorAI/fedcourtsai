## In brief

A former credit union executive asks the Court to let him sue in federal court to stop an agency enforcement proceeding he says is unconstitutional.

## What happened

Jeffrey Moats led a Texas credit union until regulators placed it into conservatorship and fired him. After disputes over his termination benefits and property, the National Credit Union Administration (NCUA) charged him with financial misconduct and sought millions in penalties through its in-house enforcement process. Moats sued in federal district court, arguing the administrative law judge hearing his case is unconstitutionally shielded from removal and that he is entitled to a jury trial rather than an agency proceeding. The district court dismissed his suit, ruling that a federal banking statute strips courts of jurisdiction to interfere with NCUA proceedings. The Fifth Circuit affirmed, relying on its companion ruling in a similar case against the FDIC.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked whether the statute governing credit union enforcement actions prevents federal district courts from hearing constitutional challenges to the agency's structure and procedures before the enforcement proceeding concludes, or whether it only limits review of specific agency orders. The Court is also asked, more broadly, whether Congress may constitutionally bar district courts from hearing this kind of structural challenge at all, requiring a challenger instead to wait and raise the claim only after the agency proceeding ends, on appeal to a regular appeals court.

## Where it stands

The petition was distributed for the Court's October 9, 2026 conference, and the docket shows no action on it since. Moats's reply brief was also distributed at that time.

## What each outcome would mean

Moats, who lost in the lower courts, is asking the Court to take up his case. If the Court grants review, it will hear full briefing and decide whether his suit may proceed in district court or must wait; a grant decides nothing by itself about who is right. If the Court denies review, the Fifth Circuit's ruling stands, and Moats must pursue his constitutional claims through the NCUA's administrative process first, with review available later only in a federal court of appeals. The petition also asks, as an alternative, that the Court hold the case pending its decision in a related case, Johnson v. United States Congress, rather than granting or denying it outright.
