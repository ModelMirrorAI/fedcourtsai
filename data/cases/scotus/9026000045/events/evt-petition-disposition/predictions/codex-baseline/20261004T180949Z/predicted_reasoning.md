# Forecast of the Court's action

The Court will most likely deny this petition without a separate writing. The record shows exactly one distribution, on September 23, 2026, for the October 9 conference, and no CVSG. My modal path is denial following that conference or a subsequent October order list, without another distribution. I assign a 30% probability to at least one additional distribution; within that alternative, one or two further distributions are more likely than a prolonged series.

The Court will not call for the Solicitor General's views. The Solicitor General already represents the federal respondents and has filed their opposition. The probability of a new CVSG is 0.5%, not the probability that the government supplies a brief it has already supplied.

The principal alternative to prompt denial is a hold for Johnson v. United States Congress, the pending case identified in both provisioned briefs. I put that path at roughly 25%, overlapping the additional-distribution forecast rather than adding to it: a hold need not produce a fresh distribution immediately. A hold would push disposition beyond the October conferences and potentially until the related decision later in the Term. Most of that hold probability still ends in denial; a hold is not itself a grant.

If the Court grants plenary review, it will most likely take the first question, focused on whether 12 U.S.C. 1786(k)(1) bars an immediate district-court challenge to the constitutionality of the agency adjudicative process. It is less likely to decide the second question's broad Article III limit on Congress's jurisdiction-channeling power. If a merits opinion follows, a narrow statutory treatment of structural challenges is the likelier ground than a categorical prohibition on channeling constitutional claims through appellate review.

Conditional on any grant, I assign 35% to disposition in the cert order itself and 65% to plenary review. The principal summary route is a GVR after a relevant intervening decision in Johnson, not an immediate summary reversal simply applying Axon. The explicit-preclusion dispute gives the Court a substantive distinction to address before treating Axon as dispositive.

Conditional on denial, I assign 12% to any noted dissent or statement respecting denial. The modal forecast remains no public separate writing; I do not forecast an individual Justice's vote or attribute a denial to an expressed merits rationale.
