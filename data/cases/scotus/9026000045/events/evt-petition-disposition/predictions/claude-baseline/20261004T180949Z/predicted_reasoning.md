# Forecast: Moats v. National Credit Union Administration Board, No. 26-45

**Disposition.** The Court will deny certiorari. P(any grant) = 0.13.

**State forecast from.** The docket shows **one** distribution (entered Sep 23, 2026, for the Conference of Oct 9, 2026), no CVSG, one amicus brief (New Civil Liberties Alliance) at the petition stage, a Solicitor General brief in opposition, and a reply.

**Relists.** The most likely single path is denial on the Oct 13, 2026 order list with no further distribution. But two paths add a distribution entry: (a) the Court holds the petition for *Johnson v. United States Congress*, No. 25-735 (cert. granted Apr. 6, 2026), which the petition expressly requests; a held petition is redistributed once the lead case is decided, which would fall in spring 2027; (b) an ordinary relist for a closer look at the Article III question. I put P(at least one more distribution) at 0.40, with roughly 60 percent of that mass coming from the hold path. If relisted on the ordinary path, I expect one or two further distributions, not a long string.

**CVSG.** None. The United States is already the respondent and has filed its brief; a CVSG is structurally unavailable. P = 0.01.

**Question the Court would take, if any.** QP 1 as written, confined to the textual question whether 12 U.S.C. 1786(k)(1)'s express preclusion clause reaches structural constitutional claims absent a clearer statement. The Court would not take QP 2 (an Article III bar on channeling structural claims to post-proceeding appellate review), which has no lower-court support and which Axon itself did not reach.

**Summary disposition vs plenary.** Conditional on any grant, the likelier route is a GVR after a hold for *Johnson* rather than plenary review: P(summary route | grant) = 0.65. A plenary grant would need the Court to revisit *Board of Governors v. MCorp Financial* (1991), which applied the materially identical 12 U.S.C. 1818(i)(1) by its plain terms, and to do so with no circuit split and after having denied the same question in *Bohon v. FERC* in 2024.

**Dissent or statement respecting denial.** Unlikely. P(some Justice writes | denial) = 0.08. If one appears it would most plausibly be a statement from Justice Gorsuch or Justice Thomas noting the unresolved Axon question, not a dissent.

**If granted (conditional only).** The Court would most likely hold that the statute's "no court shall have jurisdiction to affect by injunction or otherwise the issuance or enforcement of any notice or order" language is a channeling provision that does reach structural claims, affirming on the text and MCorp, while leaving the Article III question open. A reversal would rest on a Webster v. Doe clear-statement rationale limited to structural claims.
