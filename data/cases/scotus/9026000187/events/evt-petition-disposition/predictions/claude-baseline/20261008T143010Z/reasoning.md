# Why 0.08

**Anchor.** `record/context.json` freezes `band: baseline` under `sal-v4`, which matches the statpack table's version. Pooling the baseline band's bracketed `reached` rate over every Term strictly before OT2026 (OT2017–OT2025, OT2026's row being empty) gives about 637 grants over n=12720, ≈ 5.0%. That is the private-class floor and the yardstick this cell is scored against. Petitioner is a private party (a detainee's guardian), so no caption-class adjustment applies. The modern-cert whole-docket rate and the relist-0 bucket (1.2% granted) sit below it and are not the right population for a distributed paid petition.

**Up from the anchor: the response request.** On October 5, after the petition had been distributed with no response filed, the Court called for a response. That is the strongest ordinary signal a distributed cert docket can carry short of a relist or CVSG: at least one chambers wanted the respondent's answer before voting. The statpack has no cut for it, so I price it from the public record of this very question. The two prior petitions on the same split that drew a response request (Jordan v. Howell, No. 23-210; Lewis County v. Helphenstine, No. 23-259) were then denied or held and dismissed, and the one that did not (Hartman v. Short, No. 23-1097) was denied at its first conference. I read the request as roughly doubling the anchor, to about 10%, not more.

**Down from there: the vehicle.** I read the published Fifth Circuit opinion on CourtListener (the petition's appendix was not provisioned). Three things in it cut against a grant:

1. The panel majority (Smith, J.) never mentions Kingsley or the objective/subjective question. It applies circuit precedent (Cope v. Cogdill) and affirms because Dr. Shaw lacked subjective knowledge, with a footnote rejecting a "mailbox rule" constructive-notice argument. Nothing in the opinion or the petition shows that petitioner asked the Fifth Circuit to adopt the objective standard; the petition itself says it "does not seek review of the Fifth Circuit's decision under a subjective standard." Preservation is at best unclear.
2. Judge Wiener's concurrence in the judgment would affirm on qualified-immunity prong two: even if Shaw was deliberately indifferent, no clearly established law in 2020 made his conduct unlawful. That is an alternative ground the respondent will press in the BIO, and it makes the question presented arguably not outcome-determinative, because a Fifth Circuit doctor in 2020 could not have violated a clearly established *objective* standard the circuit had never adopted. The Court routinely denies split petitions with that defect.
3. The facts are thin for the petitioner even under an objective standard: the record as the panel describes it is that Shaw agreed with the OB/GYN's induction plan and did not open an email attachment on a Sunday morning. Petitioner's own section IV argues a jury "could" find for her, which concedes the case would turn on a fact-bound summary-judgment question rather than the standard alone.

Beyond the opinion: the petition is short (17 pages of argument), cites few authorities, misattributes a quotation, and comes from a solo practitioner rather than a Supreme Court specialist; no amicus has appeared, where Lewis County drew three and Hartman one. And the Court's revealed preference matters: four petitions on this exact split since 2021, including a plaintiff-side Fifth Circuit petition (Cope, No. 21-783), have all failed. The Court is either waiting for a vehicle where the standard is cleanly dispositive, or is content to let the split stand; this petition is not that vehicle.

**Net.** Anchor 5% → ~10% for the response request → 0.08 after the vehicle discount. I did not go lower because the response request is real and recent, the split is mature and acknowledged on both sides, and the Court does periodically take a sympathetic jail case when a chambers pushes it.

**The other claims.**
- `relist-increment` 0.96: the response request takes the petition off the October 16 conference, and redistribution after the BIO and reply is standard practice; the residual is a settlement or Rule 46 dismissal before redistribution, which is exactly what happened in No. 23-210.
- `cvsg-increment` 0.02: no federal interest, and none was invited in any prior petition on this question.
- `summary-disposition-route` 0.15: the statpack's cert-order share of the grant family is ~47% across all modern-cert grants, but that share is driven by GVRs after intervening decisions, and none exists here. The residual is a hold-and-GVR behind a better vehicle granted this Term, of which I found no sign.
- `dissent-from-denial` 0.10: no noted dissent accompanied the prior denials; the sympathetic facts leave room for a statement respecting denial.

**Uncertainty and where to discount me.** I could not read the BIO (not yet filed) or petitioner's Fifth Circuit brief, so the preservation point is inference from the opinion's silence. If the BIO concedes the standard is outcome-determinative, or an amicus coalition appears, the vehicle discount is too large. The response-request multiplier is my own estimate from three analog dockets, not a published cut. Corpus retrieval contributed nothing beyond confirming it carries no subject filter; the forecast rests on the provisioned petition, the statpack, the Fifth Circuit opinion, and the three prior dockets listed in `retrieval.md`. No disposition of this petition was found or exists; the live docket read on October 8, 2026 matches the provisioned snapshot.

**Big-case score 0.55.** The question is a genuine, decade-old, nine-circuit split on the constitutional standard governing medical care for every pretrial detainee in the country, and the facts would draw coverage; but the doctrinal reach is one standard in one class of §1983 claim, not a term-defining case.
