# Forecast: Congious v. Shaw, No. 26-187 (response-requested moment)

**State forecast from.** The petition was docketed as filed June 25, 2026 and
distributed once (for the October 16, 2026 conference). The Court requested a
response on October 5, 2026, due November 4, 2026. No response, CVSG, or
amicus brief appears on the record.

**Disposition: denied.** The Court will deny certiorari once the response is in.
The split the petition describes is real and well developed. It asks whether
*Kingsley v. Hendrickson*'s objective standard governs a pretrial detainee's
denial-of-medical-care claim, and it divides 2d/4th/6th/7th/9th from
5th/8th/10th/11th. The Court has passed on this question several times since
*Kingsley*, and this case is a weak vehicle for it:

- **An alternative qualified-immunity ground.** Qualified immunity was raised
  below and granted by the district court. Judge Wiener's concurrence in the
  judgment rests on it entirely: no clearly established right. A ruling for
  petitioner on the standard would not change the outcome if qualified immunity
  still attaches. The response will press this point.
- **Facts that may fail under either standard.** The panel's key fact is that
  Dr. Shaw had not opened the morning email before the birth, and he had an
  induction plan in place. Whether a "should have known" standard changes the
  result is contestable.
- **A modest presentation.** The Fifth Circuit applied circuit precedent
  (*Cope v. Cogdill*) in a short opinion that never engages the
  objective-standard question.

**Relist / timing.** The petition will be distributed again once the response
(or a reply) is in, most likely for a conference in late November or December
2026. I expect a denial at that first post-response conference. A relist beyond
that is possible but not my expectation.

**CVSG.** None expected. The respondent is a private jail doctor in a
damages suit, and no federal program interest is directly at stake.

**If granted.** The Court would take the question as written (objective
vs. subjective standard for detainee medical-care claims) and would likely
grant plenary review rather than dispose summarily. No intervening decision
supports a GVR.

**Separate writing.** A dissent from denial or a statement respecting denial is
unlikely but possible. Justice Sotomayor or Justice Jackson are the most
plausible authors, given the egregious facts and the persistent split.
