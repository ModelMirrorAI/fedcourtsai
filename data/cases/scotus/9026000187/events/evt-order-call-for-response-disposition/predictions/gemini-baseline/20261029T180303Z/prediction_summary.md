I predict the Supreme Court will deny review in this case, with an 8% chance of granting the petition (about one in twelve). Although the petition raises an important disagreement among federal appeals courts over whether a pretrial jail detainee suing for inadequate medical care must prove a doctor consciously knew of the risk or only that the doctor acted unreasonably, this case is a flawed vehicle for resolving that question. The lower courts also ruled that the jail medical director is shielded by qualified immunity—a legal doctrine that protects government officials from lawsuits unless they violated clearly established law at the time—which would likely block the lawsuit even if the Supreme Court changed the constitutional standard going forward.
