## What happened

Chasity Congious was a pregnant pretrial detainee at a Texas jail when she went into labor alone in her cell; her baby, Z.C.H., was found unresponsive and died ten days later. Congious sued the jail's medical director, Dr. Aaron Shaw, claiming he denied her adequate medical care in violation of her constitutional rights. The district court granted summary judgment to Shaw, finding he lacked personal knowledge that she faced a serious risk of imminent labor and was protected by qualified immunity, a legal doctrine shielding officials from damages unless they violated clearly established rights. The Fifth Circuit affirmed, applying a standard requiring proof Shaw personally knew of the risk.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked whether claims like this one, brought by a jailed person awaiting trial who says officials denied needed medical care, should be judged by whether the official actually knew of the danger, or instead by whether a reasonable official in that position should have recognized it, regardless of what they personally knew.
