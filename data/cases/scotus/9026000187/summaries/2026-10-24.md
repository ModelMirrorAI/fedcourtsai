## In brief

A mother whose newborn died in a jail cell asks the Court to decide what standard governs claims that jail medical staff failed to provide care to pretrial detainees.

## What happened

Chasity Congious was a pretrial detainee at the Tarrant County Jail when she gave birth alone in her cell; her baby, Z.C.H., died ten days later. Congious sued Dr. Aaron Shaw, the jail's medical director, claiming he violated her Fourteenth Amendment rights by failing to ensure she received care, after he did not act on an email reporting she had abdominal cramps. The district court granted summary judgment to Shaw, finding he lacked subjective knowledge of a serious risk, and the Fifth Circuit affirmed, with one judge concurring only in the result.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked whether a pretrial detainee's claim that jail officials failed to provide needed medical care should be judged by whether the official actually realized the danger and ignored it, or instead by whether the official's conduct was unreasonable under the circumstances, regardless of what the official personally knew. The petition says federal appeals courts are split on which test applies.

## Where it stands

The petition was distributed for the Court's October 16, 2026 conference; the docket shows no action from that conference. On October 5, 2026, the Court requested a response from Shaw, due November 4, 2026.

## What each outcome would mean

Congious is asking the Court to take up her case and reverse the Fifth Circuit's ruling for Shaw. If the Court grants the petition, it would hear the case and decide which legal standard applies, without yet deciding who wins. If it denies the petition, the Fifth Circuit's ruling for Shaw stands, and Congious's claim remains rejected. A grant does not mean either side's position is correct; it only means the Court will review the question.
