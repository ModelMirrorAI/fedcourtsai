## In brief

A man asks the Court to revive his lawsuit against five media companies after lower courts dismissed his claims of electronic surveillance as not substantial enough to support federal jurisdiction.

## What happened

Ahmed Shaikh, representing himself, sued Fox News, Paramount, Warner Bros. Discovery, NBCUniversal, and Disney, claiming they hacked his devices, installed hidden cameras, and used mind-reading technology to monitor him and use his private moments in their broadcasts. The media companies asked the district court to dismiss the case, arguing the claims were too insubstantial to give the court jurisdiction. The district court agreed and dismissed the case without reaching the companies' other arguments. The Eleventh Circuit Court of Appeals affirmed, calling the allegations "utterly divorced from reality," and later denied rehearing.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked whether the lower courts were wrong to treat the surveillance and interception claims as so insubstantial that no federal court had power to hear them at all, rather than addressing whether the claims had legal merit, and whether that dismissal conflicts with rulings from other appeals courts on similar privacy-law claims. The petition also asks whether a court that dismisses a case for lack of jurisdiction can still rule on separate requests for sanctions against the other side for alleged misconduct during the litigation.

## Where it stands

The petition was filed and two respondents, The Walt Disney Company and Fox News Network, have each filed a waiver of their right to respond. The docket shows no further action since those filings.

## What each outcome would mean

Shaikh, who lost in the lower courts, is asking the Court to take up his case. If the Court grants the petition, it would hear the case fully and decide the jurisdiction and sanctions questions; a grant alone would not mean Shaikh wins. If the Court denies the petition, the Eleventh Circuit's decision affirming dismissal of Shaikh's claims stands, and the media companies' win below remains final.
