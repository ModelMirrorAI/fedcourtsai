## In brief

A man asks the Court to revive his lawsuit accusing major media companies of spying on him through hacked devices and mind-reading technology.

## What happened

Ahmed Shaikh, representing himself, sued five media companies—Fox News, Paramount Global, Warner Bros. Discovery, NBCUniversal, and Disney—claiming they hacked his devices, planted hidden cameras, and used technology to read his thoughts for use in broadcasts. A federal district court dismissed the case, finding the claims "patently insubstantial" and therefore outside its jurisdiction. The court did not rule on Shaikh's request for sanctions against Disney. The court of appeals affirmed, agreeing the allegations had no plausible foundation, and later denied rehearing.

## What the Court is being asked

Shaikh raises two issues. First, he argues the lower courts wrongly treated his claims as too insubstantial to even give the court jurisdiction, when courts elsewhere have let similar surveillance-technology claims proceed as ordinary disputes over the facts of the case. Second, he argues that once a court decides it lacks jurisdiction over a case, it should still be able to rule on pending requests for sanctions against a party, rather than treating itself as powerless to act on them.

## Where it stands

The petition was filed and docketed in November 2026, with the respondents' response due December 3, 2026. The Court has not yet acted on the petition.

## What each outcome would mean

Shaikh, who lost in the lower courts, is asking the Court to take up his case. If the Court grants the petition, it would hear full arguments and decide the jurisdictional and sanctions questions; a grant alone would not determine who wins. If the Court denies the petition, the lower court's dismissal of Shaikh's lawsuit against the media companies stands, and the case remains closed.
