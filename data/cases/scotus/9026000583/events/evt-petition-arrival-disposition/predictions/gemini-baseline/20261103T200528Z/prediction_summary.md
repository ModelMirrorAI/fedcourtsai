The Supreme Court is almost certain to deny review in *Ahmed Shaikh v. Fox News Network, LLC, et al.* (a 0.2% chance of review, or about 1 in 500). Representing himself without a lawyer, Ahmed Shaikh sued five major television networks, claiming they used hidden cameras and government mind-reading technology to broadcast his private thoughts. Both the federal trial court in Florida and the Eleventh Circuit Court of Appeals dismissed the lawsuit because the factual claims were wholly implausible and because Shaikh failed to properly argue diversity jurisdiction (the rule allowing federal courts to hear lawsuits between citizens of different states) on appeal. Because the lawsuit rests on implausible factual allegations and presents no preserved legal conflict among lower courts, the Justices will decline to hear the case without comment.
