Three case-specific factors drive a steep downward adjustment from the 5.19% `baseline` reached anchor to `0.001`:
1. **Self-Represented (*Pro Se*) Filing**: The snapshot (`IsCounselofRecord: false`, `Attorney: "Ahmed Shaikh"`, with `snapshot_contact_scrub` redacting five contact fields on the Petitioner side) and the Eleventh Circuit opinion confirm that petitioner is proceeding *pro se*. Even on the paid docket, unrepresented petitions without amicus support face a near-zero grant rate.
2. **Dismissal as Patently Frivolous under *Bell v. Hood***: The Eleventh Circuit affirmed the Middle District of Florida's (No. 6:24-cv-01894) Rule 12(b)(1) dismissal of petitioner's 267-page complaint against five media companies (Fox News, Paramount Global, Warner Bros. Discovery, NBCUniversal, and Disney) under the Electronic Communications Privacy Act and Florida tort law. The panel held that petitioner's allegations—that law school professors and workers installed hidden cameras in his apartments and car and that major television networks used clandestine government "AI mind-reading technology" to read his thoughts and mimic him on broadcasts—were "utterly divorced from reality" and "wholly insubstantial and frivolous" under *Bell v. Hood*, 327 U.S. 678, 682–83 (1946).
3. **Abandonment of Any Conceivable Legal Issue Below**: Although the Eleventh Circuit noted in passing that its precedent had not previously addressed whether factual frivolousness defeats diversity jurisdiction under 28 U.S.C. § 1332 (as opposed to federal-question jurisdiction under 28 U.S.C. § 1331), the court expressly held that Shaikh failed to brief and therefore abandoned any argument regarding diversity jurisdiction on appeal. Thus, even that narrow doctrinal observation is unpreserved and embedded in a frivolous factual vehicle.
