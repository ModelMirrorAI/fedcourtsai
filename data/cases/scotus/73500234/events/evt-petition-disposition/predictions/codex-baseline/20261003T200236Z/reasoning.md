## Base rate and adjustments

The committed `metrics/statpack.md` publishes a matching `sal-v4` band table. I pooled the bracketed baseline **reached** rates across every rendered Term strictly before 2025: 2017 through 2024. Using the unrounded `prefix_est_grant_rate` and `prefix_weighted_resolved` fields in the matching `metrics/statpack.json` gives 593 grant-family cases over a weighted denominator of 11,580, or **5.1209%**. I excluded the 2025 and 2026 rows and did not use the much lower rate among petitions that terminated in baseline. These are figures from the committed pack read October 3, not a fresh live-corpus query; I did not establish a newer corpus vintage.

For context only, the pack's modern-cert disposition section implies about 2.82% grants including GVRs, and its Ninth Circuit cut about 3.2%. Those broader populations mix fee classes and do not replace the paid, private-petitioner reached-band anchor. The relist and CVSG sections describe terminal states, not forward hazards. In the paid segment, the zero-relist bucket has about 1.7% grants including GVRs, the one-relist bucket 13.3%, and the two-relist bucket 40.9%; the CVSG bucket has 34.9% versus 6.3% without one. I use those cuts only to recognize that later Court attention would materially change the information set, not to pretend this first-distribution petition is already in a terminal bucket.

My **6% probability of any grant** is a modest upward adjustment from 5.12%, not a high-confidence assessment of a compelling conflict.

- **Reasons above the anchor:** The petition raises a recognizable institutional concern: whether factual conclusions reached under a jurisdictional dismissal standard improperly survived this Court's reversal when the case returned under summary-judgment rules. The change in burdens and treatment of disputed facts is substantive, not merely a renamed motion. The Court's prior involvement makes a corrective intervention more plausible than in an otherwise anonymous fact dispute. The accrual question also has consequences for federal easements over private land beyond this particular road. See petition, introduction and pp. 13–27.
- **The strongest obstacle:** The opposition quotes the Ninth Circuit as independently affirming after de novo review even apart from law of the case. If that account is sound, success on question one does not remove the second ground supporting judgment. The opposition also says both lower courts recognized the changed standard, and the earlier Supreme Court ruling reserved the implications on remand. That makes a straightforward defiance-of-mandate characterization less convincing. See opposition pp. 5–6, 8–12.
- **The asserted conflict is contestable:** The petitioner invokes general discovery-rule decisions and Werner, plus the district-court decision in Elk Mountain Safari. The opposition distinguishes the appellate Quiet Title Act cases involving a private easement over federal land from a federal easement over private land, and distinguishes reversal on the jurisdictional issue from wholesale vacatur of all previously decided issues. A district-court analogue and an asserted intracircuit inconsistency do not establish a square intercircuit conflict. I find the petition's constructive-notice theory arguable, but the briefs do not establish that two circuits would necessarily decide the same posture differently. See petition pp. 17–24; opposition pp. 11–14.
- **Disputed factual framing:** The petition emphasizes unauthorized use, underage drinking, a later public-access sign, and other private easement holders. The opposition emphasizes decades of ordinary recreational access and alleges that the private-easement-holder alternative was not raised below. I do not resolve those disputes as facts. Their existence makes this a less clean vehicle for the broad rule the question presented seeks. See petition pp. 6–12, 19–23; opposition pp. 2–3, 10–11.

The response extensions are scheduling facts, not affirmative Court interest. The absence of a CVSG or a second distribution is likewise not proof of disinterest; those events simply have not occurred in the record shown. The public opposition and substantial briefing make a definitive denial forecast possible without waiting for further retrieval.

## Other numerical judgments

The 20% probability of another distribution allows for inspection of the earlier mandate and the claimed discovery-rule conflict, while retaining no further distribution as the modal path. It is a subjective forward probability, not a row copied from the terminal relist table. The 0.5% CVSG estimate reflects that the Solicitor General has already presented the United States' position in this case.

The 45% summary-route probability is **conditional on a grant**, not an unconditional 45% chance of summary relief. The petition expressly requests summary reversal, and a correction of the Court's own remand supplies a plausible summary route. Nevertheless, the alternative accrual holding and contested conflict make plenary consideration slightly more likely within the grant branch. No identified intervening decision compels a routine GVR. The 5% probability of a separate denial writing is **conditional on denial**; the property-rights and remand concerns supply a small possibility, but the record supplies no specific indication of a Justice's present interest.

The stakes score of 0.35 is independent of the grant probability. Access to quiet-title adjudication and compliance with Supreme Court mandates have broader significance, but the immediate dispute concerns a particular road easement and a fact-sensitive notice question, not a demonstrated nationwide regulatory or constitutional upheaval.

The principal uncertainty is whether the petitioner's reply and the actual appellate record undermine the government's alternative-ground and factual-framing arguments. The forecast should be discounted accordingly; complete extraction of the supplied briefs is not equivalent to a complete litigation record.
