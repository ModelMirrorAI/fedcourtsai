## In brief

A Montana landowner asks the Court to revive his long-running suit over a federal road easement, arguing lower courts wrongly ruled his claim too late to bring.

## What happened

Larry Wilkins owns land burdened by a 1962 easement granting the government a road across his property. He sued in 2018, arguing the easement allows only limited government use, not public access, and that the Forest Service failed to maintain the road properly. After this Court previously ruled on a different issue in the same case, the trial court again held Wilkins's claims were filed too late under the Quiet Title Act's 12-year deadline, relying partly on its earlier ruling and partly on a fresh review of the evidence. The Ninth Circuit affirmed, finding the claims accrued decades ago based on the public's long history of using the road.

## What the Court is being asked

Wilkins raises two questions. First, whether a court handling a case sent back after this Court reversed an earlier ruling may still treat that earlier ruling as settled and binding, rather than starting fresh. Second, whether a property owner's deadline to sue the government over a disputed easement can start running based on ordinary people's use of the road - including, Wilkins says, illegal use like underage drinking - rather than based on something the government itself did or said.

## Where it stands

The United States filed a brief opposing review, and Wilkins filed a reply. The case was distributed for the Court's October 9, 2026 conference, and the docket shows no action since then.

## What each outcome would mean

Wilkins, as petitioner, is asking the Court to take up the case. If the Court grants review, it will decide these questions itself after full briefing and argument; a grant would not yet decide who is right. If the Court denies review, the Ninth Circuit's ruling stands, leaving Wilkins's claims dismissed as untimely and ending this lawsuit against the government. The government argues denial is appropriate because the lower courts agreed on the outcome and no conflict with other courts exists.
