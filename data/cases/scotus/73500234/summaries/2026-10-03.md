## In brief

Larry Wilkins asks the Court to revive his property-rights lawsuit against the federal government over a road easement, after lower courts ruled it was filed too late.

## What happened

Wilkins owns land crossed by a road easement the government holds for Bitterroot National Forest access. He sued under the Quiet Title Act, arguing the easement was meant only for limited government use, not general public access. A district court first dismissed the suit as filed too late, treating the time limit as jurisdictional. The Supreme Court reversed that view in 2023, ruling the time limit was not jurisdictional, and sent the case back. On remand, the district court again found the suit untimely, applying its earlier timeliness finding as "law of the case," and the Ninth Circuit affirmed, also independently concluding the public's longstanding use of the road should have alerted Wilkins to the government's position more than twelve years before he sued.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked two things: first, whether a lower court can treat its own earlier ruling as settled once the Supreme Court has reversed the judgment it came from, rather than reconsidering the issue fresh; second, whether the clock on Wilkins's claim can start running based on ordinary or even unlawful use of the road by private individuals, rather than requiring some action by the government itself.

## Where it stands

The petition has been fully briefed, with the government opposing review and Wilkins filing a reply. It was distributed for the Court's October 9, 2026 conference, and the docket shows no action since.

## What each outcome would mean

Wilkins, who lost in the lower courts, is asking the Court to take up the case. If the Court grants the petition, it will hear the case and decide these questions, which would decide nothing by itself about who wins. If it denies the petition, the Ninth Circuit's ruling stands, leaving Wilkins's suit barred as untimely and the government's view of the easement's scope in place.
