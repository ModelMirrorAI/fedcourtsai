## In brief

The Court declined to hear a Montana landowner's bid to revive his long-running lawsuit over public use of a road easement crossing his property.

## What happened

Larry Steven Wilkins sued the United States in 2018, seeking to limit public use of a road easement across his Montana property that the government had acquired in 1962. After years of litigation, including an earlier trip to the Supreme Court over whether the Quiet Title Act's filing deadline was jurisdictional, the district court again ruled against Wilkins on remand, holding his claims were filed too late. The Ninth Circuit affirmed, applying the "law of the case" doctrine and separately concluding, after its own review, that the public's long history of using the road put Wilkins on notice of the government's position years before he sued. Wilkins asked the Supreme Court to review that decision.

## What the Court is being asked

The case raised two questions: first, whether a lower court may treat its own earlier rulings as settled once the Supreme Court has reversed a related part of the case; and second, whether a deadline for filing a property dispute against the government can start running based on ordinary or even unauthorized use of land by members of the public, rather than only based on the government's own actions.

## Where it stands

The petition was distributed for the Court's October 9, 2026 conference, and on October 13, 2026, the Court denied the petition.

## What each outcome would mean

Wilkins, the property owner, asked the Court to take up the case; the government opposed review. The denial leaves the Ninth Circuit's ruling in place, meaning Wilkins's lawsuit remains barred as untimely and the dispute over the easement's scope stays unresolved in his favor on the government's terms. The denial does not mean the Supreme Court agreed with the Ninth Circuit's reasoning on either question Wilkins raised; it simply leaves that court's judgment standing.
