## In brief

The Supreme Court declined to hear Custodia Bank's challenge to the Federal Reserve's denial of its application for a master account.

## What happened

Custodia Bank, a Wyoming-chartered bank serving cryptocurrency companies, applied for a master account from the Federal Reserve Bank of Kansas City in 2020. The Reserve Bank denied the application in 2023, citing risks from Custodia's crypto-focused, largely unsupervised business model. Custodia sued the Federal Reserve Board of Governors and the Kansas City Reserve Bank, arguing federal law required the Fed to grant master accounts to any legally eligible institution, leaving Reserve Banks no discretion to refuse. The district court ruled against Custodia, and the Tenth Circuit affirmed, over a dissent, holding that federal law gives Reserve Banks discretion to deny such accounts even to eligible applicants.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked whether a 1980 banking law, which says the Fed's services "shall be available" to banks that are not Federal Reserve members, eliminates the Reserve Banks' longstanding discretion to decide whether to accept a particular institution's deposits, or whether that discretion survives alongside the availability requirement.

## Where it stands

The petition was distributed for the Court's November 13, 2026 conference, and Custodia filed a reply brief shortly before that conference. The Court denied the petition on November 16, 2026.

## What each outcome would mean

Custodia, as the party that asked the Court to review the Tenth Circuit's ruling, sought to overturn that decision and revive its claim to a master account. The denial leaves the Tenth Circuit's ruling in place, meaning Custodia's lawsuit fails and the Federal Reserve Bank of Kansas City's denial of its master account application stands. The denial does not mean the Supreme Court agreed with the Tenth Circuit's reasoning; it simply leaves that decision as the final word in this case. Other banks in the Tenth and Second Circuits, which reached similar conclusions, remain subject to those rulings as well.
