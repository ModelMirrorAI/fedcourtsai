### 3. Downward Adjustments (Why We Settle at 8.0%)
Those upward pressures are largely offset by four formidable obstacles documented in `brief-in-opposition.txt` (which combines both the Solicitor General's BIO for the Board of Governors and FRBKC's BIO) and `appendix.txt`:
1. **Complete Absence of a Circuit Split (and Uniform Contrary Authority):** Petitioner concedes (Pet. 13) that there is no circuit split. The only two circuits to decide whether 12 U.S.C. § 248a(c)(2) strips Reserve Banks of discretion under 12 U.S.C. § 342—the Tenth Circuit below (157 F.4th 1235) and the Second Circuit in *Banco San Juan Internacional, Inc. v. Federal Reserve Bank of New York*, 176 F.4th 163, 178, 182 (2d Cir. 2026)—both held that Reserve Banks retain discretion to deny or close master accounts. Every district court to reach the merits has agreed (`brief-in-opposition.txt`, Board BIO 11; FRBKC BIO 13–14). Moreover, a related case is currently percolating in the Ninth Circuit (*PayServices Bank v. Federal Reserve Bank of San Francisco*, No. 24-2355), and `record/same-question.json` shows zero pending SCOTUS petitions sharing this question (`petitions: []`).
2. **Strong Textual and Precedential Support for the Decision Below:**
   - Under *Farmers & Merchants Bank v. Federal Reserve Bank of Richmond*, 262 U.S. 649, 662 (1923), Section 342's language that a Reserve Bank "may receive ... deposits" is permissive, not mandatory. When Congress enacted the Monetary Control Act of 1980 (MCA), it amended Section 342 directly to insert "or other depository institutions" right after "member banks" while retaining "may receive."
   - Section 248a(c)(2) is a fee-schedule pricing principle addressed to the Board of Governors ("The schedule of fees prescribed pursuant to this section shall be based on the following principles: ... (2) All Federal Reserve bank services covered by the fee schedule shall be available to nonmember depository institutions and such services shall be priced at the same fee schedule applicable to member banks ..."). Reading that pricing principle to give uninsured, non-federally-regulated nonmember banks an *absolute, nondiscretionary* entitlement to a deposit account—when even federally regulated member banks enjoy no such absolute right under § 342—is textually implausible.
   - Congress's 2022 enactment of 12 U.S.C. § 248c (the Toomey Amendment) requires the Board to publish a database of master-account requests and specify whether each request was "approved, rejected, pending, or withdrawn," confirming that requests by legally eligible institutions can be "rejected."
3. **Serious Vehicle and Preservation Flaws:**
   - Petitioner's strongest hook for cert without a circuit split is constitutional avoidance (arguing that if regional Reserve Bank presidents have discretion to deny master accounts, they exercise executive power in violation of the Appointments Clause and Article II removal principles, citing *Trump v. Slaughter*, 609 U.S. 422 (2026), and *Trump v. Cook*, 609 U.S. 528 (2026)).
   - However, the Tenth Circuit expressly held that Custodia **waived** its Appointments Clause challenges both in the district court (by omitting its challenge to Reserve Bank directors from its amended complaint) and on appeal (by failing to argue in its opening brief that the FRBKC president is a principal officer) (`appendix.txt`, Pet. App. 42a–44a). Custodia also never raised a presidential removal challenge below (`brief-in-opposition.txt`, Board BIO 19).
   - Furthermore, Custodia chose an all-or-nothing mandamus posture below: it abandoned its APA claim against FRBKC and declined to challenge the specific risk-based grounds for FRBKC's denial (which documented that Custodia is an uninsured, non-federally supervised crypto-focused institution with significant BSA/AML and liquidity risk gaps).
4. **Ongoing Federal Reserve Rulemaking:** Following Executive Order 14,405, the Board of Governors issued a notice of proposed rulemaking in May 2026 (91 Fed. Reg. 30,627) to establish specialized "Payment Accounts" for novel charter-holders, giving the Court an additional institutional reason to allow administrative and lower-court percolation to continue (`brief-in-opposition.txt`, Board BIO 21–22).

Netting these factors out, we move modestly above the 5.19% anchor to **0.08** (8.0%).

## II. Rationale for the Declared Claims (`cert-v3`) and Distributions

1. **`disposition` (`0.08`):** Matches the top-level `probability` of any grant (plenary grant or summary disposition/GVR).
2. **`relist-increment` (`0.25`):** The snapshot currently records `distribution_count: 1` (for the November 13, 2026 conference). While most paid petitions at their first conference are denied without a relist (in `metrics/statpack.md`, 10,204 of 13,713 resolved paid-segment petitions end in the `0` relist bucket, so roughly ~25.6% of all paid petitions get relisted at least once), this petition has a call for a response, eleven amicus briefs, and constitutional/banking issues that may draw a second look or a short relist while a Justice considers a separate statement. Conversely, the vehicle flaws and lack of a split are clear on the face of the briefs. We price `P(relist-increment)` at **0.25**.
3. **`cvsg-increment` (`0.005`):** The Federal Reserve Board of Governors is already a named Respondent represented by the Solicitor General, who filed a full Brief in Opposition. The Court does not invite the Solicitor General to file an amicus brief when the Solicitor General is already counsel of record for a federal agency party on the docket (and the harness masks `cvsg-increment` on federal-party captions for that reason). We state a near-zero probability of **0.005**.
4. **`summary-disposition-route` (`0.15`):** Conditional on the Court granting certiorari, 85% of the probability mass lies on plenary review and 15% on a summary disposition in the cert order (specifically, a GVR). There is no clean intervening precedent that the Tenth Circuit failed to consider; *Trump v. Cook*, 609 U.S. 528 (2026), and *Trump v. Slaughter*, 609 U.S. 422 (2026), were discussed by the parties in the cert briefing, and the Tenth Circuit rested its Appointments Clause ruling on waiver rather than the merits. Still, conditional on a grant, there is a small (~15%) chance the Court would GVR for further consideration of the constitutional avoidance issues in light of *Cook* and *Slaughter*.
5. **`dissent-from-denial` (`0.06`) and `order-writers`:** Conditional on denial, there is a ~6% chance (`0.06`) that at least one Justice notes a dissent or files a statement respecting the denial of certiorari. The Tenth Circuit panel and en banc dissents by Judge Tymkovich (joined by Judge Eid) highlight federalism concerns over state-chartered banks and the unaccountability of regional Federal Reserve Banks—themes that resonate strongly with **Justice Gorsuch** (from the Tenth Circuit, assigned `0.030`) and **Justice Thomas** (who frequently writes separately on Appointments Clause, nondelegation, and banking/administrative structure issues, assigned `0.025`), and to a lesser extent **Justice Alito** (`0.015`) and **Justice Kavanaugh** (`0.008`). The remaining Justices are assigned baseline low order-writing probabilities (`0.001`–`0.002`).

- **Appendix Truncation Note:** As recorded in `record/documents/documents.json`, `appendix.txt` was capped at 150,000 characters (`truncated: true`), omitting the final pages of Judge Tymkovich's en banc dissent and the statutory reproduction at the end of the appendix.
