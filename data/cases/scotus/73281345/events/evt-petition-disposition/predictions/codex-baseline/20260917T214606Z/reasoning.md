## Why 7%, below the band anchor

The principal downward adjustment is that the asserted split is materially less clean than the question presented suggests. The petition argues that the Fifth Circuit categorically treats phones as interstate instrumentalities while the Tenth Circuit requires an actual nexus, and invokes federalism concerns and separate lower-court writings to demonstrate broader interest (petition pp. 5-16). But the government distinguishes generic motor vehicles from communications networks and points to existing Tenth Circuit telephone precedent (opposition pp. 7-8).

I checked the cited comparator itself through CourtListener: United States v. Chavarria, 140 F.4th 1257, 1265, 1268 (10th Cir. 2025), opinion filed June 16, 2025, slip opinion pp. 16, 23. It expressly permits some classes of objects to be treated as interstate instrumentalities and confines its holding to an inadequately specified motor vehicle under the kidnapping statute. That supports the government's distinction; it does not establish that Elkins would win in the Tenth Circuit. The broader reasoning still creates genuine doctrinal tension, so I do not treat the petition as frivolous or the conflict as conclusively disproved.

The second downward adjustment is vehicle quality. The opposition says the trial record includes internet searches, email, and a GPS system using servers in the United States and abroad, offering alternative jurisdictional paths even if telephone use alone were insufficient (opposition pp. 8-9). These are the government's representations, not my independent findings about the trial evidence. The petition responds in advance that the categorical jury instructions covered phones, internet, and email, that the Fifth Circuit supplied no alternative ground, and that the government proved no actual effect on interstate commerce (petition pp. 16-17). That makes the issue preserved and potentially consequential, but also makes its resolution depend on a more complicated record than the broad question suggests.

The procedural signal also warrants discounting. Although the frozen distribution count is two, the Court requested a response before the first scheduled conference, after the government waived response. Redistribution after the requested opposition and reply is weaker evidence of repeated substantive conference consideration than a genuine post-conference relist. I retain the frozen elevated anchor but reduce the weight of that raw count. The summer interval and two response extensions are not, by themselves, evidence of a hold for another case.

Upward considerations keep the forecast above a routine low-interest petition: the Court affirmatively requested a response; a criminal-defense association filed an amicus brief; the preserved question concerns a recurring boundary of federal criminal power; and the remaining life sentence makes the issue consequential for this petitioner. Those considerations support some chance of review but do not overcome the uncertain split and alternative factual grounds. The net 7% is a judgmental adjustment, not the output of a fitted model or a claim that each factor has a measured independent effect.

## Other probabilities and stakes

The 22% further-distribution probability allows additional attention following the requested response, but does not assume the two observed distributions were two completed conferences. The 0.2% new-CVSG probability reflects the government's already-filed party response, which is not itself a CVSG. The 8% summary-route probability is conditional on grant: a broad change to the instrumentalities doctrine more naturally calls for plenary review, and no specific intervening-decision or mootness predicate appears in the materials reviewed. The 12% writing probability is conditional on denial, reflecting the federalism issue's potential to attract a statement while keeping an unelaborated denial the clear favorite. These subsidiary figures are subjective forecasts, not published transition rates.

The stakes score is 0.60, separate from grant likelihood. A broad answer could affect multiple federal criminal statutes employing communications-based jurisdictional hooks. This is more significant than a purely fact-bound sentencing dispute, but this particular vehicle might yield only a narrow ruling. The main uncertainties are the unreviewed reply and trial record, the strength of the asserted conflict as applied to communications networks, and whether the response request reflects interest beyond checking the government's position.
