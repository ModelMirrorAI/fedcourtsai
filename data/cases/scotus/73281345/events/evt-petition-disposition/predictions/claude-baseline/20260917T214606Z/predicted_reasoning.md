# Forecast: Elkins v. United States, No. 25-1061 (evt-petition-disposition)

**Bottom line: the Court denies certiorari at or shortly after the September 28, 2026 conference, without a summary disposition and most likely without any separate writing.**

## Procedural forecast

- **Distributions so far: two.** The docket shows a distribution for the April 17, 2026 conference, superseded when the Court called for a response on April 13, and a fresh distribution on July 29 for the September 28 long conference. The petition has therefore not yet been *considered* at a conference; September 28 is its first real look, now with the Solicitor General's brief in opposition and the reply on file.
- **Relist:** most likely the petition is denied on the October 5 order list (or the long-conference clean-up list) without a further relist. I put a further distribution at roughly one in five: the paths to a relist are a Justice wanting time for a statement respecting denial on the Commerce Clause point, or an ordinary hold-over from the crowded long conference. A relist does not on this record signal a coming grant.
- **CVSG:** none, effectively certain. The United States is the respondent, so the Solicitor General's views are already before the Court in the brief in opposition.
- **Question presented, if taken:** the Court would not take the question as written ("Is the Fifth Circuit's categorical approach to instrumentalities of interstate commerce constitutional?"). It would reformulate to the concrete question whether 18 U.S.C. § 2261A(2) is a valid exercise of the commerce power as applied to a defendant whose only proven use of a facility of interstate commerce was intrastate use of a telephone and the internet. I expect it not to take any version.
- **Summary disposition:** very unlikely even conditional on a grant. There is no intervening decision to GVR in light of, and the Court would not summarily reverse a conviction on a Commerce Clause theory that every circuit to consider it has rejected. If the Court grants, it sets the case for argument.
- **Dissent from or statement respecting denial:** unlikely (about 8% conditional on denial). The one plausible author is Justice Thomas, whose Lopez concurrence and Taylor dissent the petition leans on; but the petition offers him no phone-specific circuit conflict to point to, and the facts (a stalking conspiracy the sentencing court found the petitioner masterminded, ending in the victim's murder) make a separate writing on federalism grounds unattractive.

## If granted (conditional only)

If the Court did grant, the likelier outcome is affirmance on a narrow ground: telephones and the internet are per se instrumentalities or channels of interstate commerce under Pensacola Telegraph and Lopez category 2, so no case-specific showing of an effect on commerce is required, leaving Chavarria's motor-vehicle holding untouched. A reversal would require the Court to adopt the Tenth Circuit's case-by-case reading for phones, which no circuit has done.
