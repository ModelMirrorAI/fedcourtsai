## In brief

Holly Ann Elkins asks the Court to decide whether the federal cyberstalking law can be applied to her based on her phone use alone.

## What happened

Elkins was convicted of conspiring to cyberstalk and cyberstalking her fiancé's ex-girlfriend, who was later murdered by the fiancé, and of a related firearm charge. She argued the cyberstalking law could not constitutionally apply to her because her conduct did not actually affect interstate commerce, even though she used a phone. The trial court and the Fifth Circuit rejected that argument, treating phone use itself as enough to satisfy Congress's commerce power, following existing circuit precedent. The Fifth Circuit separately threw out her firearm conviction on other grounds but left her cyberstalking convictions and life sentence in place.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked whether a rule treating phones as automatically covered by Congress's commerce power, regardless of whether a person's actual use of a phone affected interstate business or trade, is constitutional, or whether the government must instead show some real connection to interstate commerce in each case.

## Where it stands

The petition has been distributed for conference twice, most recently for September 28, 2026, after full briefing including the government's opposition and Elkins's reply. No decision on whether to hear the case has been recorded yet.

## What each outcome would mean

Elkins, who lost in the lower court, is asking the Court to take up her case. If the Court grants review, it will decide the commerce-power question in full, without yet determining who wins. If it denies review, the Fifth Circuit's ruling stands, leaving her cyberstalking convictions and life sentence intact under the rule that phone use alone justifies federal jurisdiction. A denial would not mean the Court agrees that ruling was correct, only that it lets it stand.
