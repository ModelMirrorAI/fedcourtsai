## What happened

Holly Ann Elkins was convicted of conspiring to stalk and cyberstalking her fiancé's ex-girlfriend, Alyssa Burkett, who was later murdered by Elkins's fiancé, Andrew Beard. Elkins was also convicted of a firearms charge tied to that killing. The Fifth Circuit vacated the firearms conviction, ruling the cyberstalking offense was not necessarily a "crime of violence," but upheld her cyberstalking and conspiracy convictions and her life sentence. On appeal, Elkins argued that using a phone alone should not let the federal government regulate her conduct under the Commerce Clause, which governs congressional power over interstate commerce. The Fifth Circuit disagreed, following its own precedent that phone use, even within a single state, counts.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked whether it is constitutional for appeals courts to treat certain everyday tools, like phones, as automatically falling under federal commerce power whenever they are used, regardless of whether that particular use had any real connection to interstate commerce. Elkins argues that some courts, including the Tenth Circuit, instead require proof that the specific use actually affected interstate commerce, and that the stricter approach is the correct one. The government responds that treating phones this way is well established and consistent with prior rulings, and that Elkins's case is a poor test of the question because she also used other tools, like the internet, email, and GPS.

## Where it stands

The petition has been distributed for conference twice, most recently for September 28, 2026, after full briefing including the government's opposition and Elkins's reply. The Court has not yet acted on the petition.
