# Forecast: what the Court will do with No. 26-337, Child v. Unum Life Insurance Co. of America

**Disposition.** The petition will be denied, most likely on the order list following the October 9, 2026 conference (October 13 or 14, 2026). Respondent has waived its response, so the petition reaches conference on the petition alone.

**Relists.** The docket shows one distribution (for the conference of 10/9/2026) and no relist. I expect no further distribution. The one realistic route to a second distribution is a call for a response: the facts are sympathetic (an insured quadriplegic for four decades, eighteen years of premiums, benefits denied at claim time), and a Justice or clerk could want Unum's account of the "existing loss" clause before denying. I put that at roughly one in eight; if it happens, expect a response in late 2026 and a redistribution for a conference in early 2027, followed by denial.

**CVSG.** None. No federal statute, agency, or federal party is implicated; the question is Iowa insurance law and the Erie method of applying it. The Solicitor General has no stake.

**Which question, if taken.** The QP as written is not a question the Court would take. It asks whether a court of appeals correctly predicted Iowa law, dressed as an Erie question of "federal judicial methodology." There is no circuit split and no conflict with a state high court, because the petition itself says no Iowa appellate court has addressed the known-loss doctrine in this setting. If the Court acted at all, it would not reformulate the QP for plenary review; it would act summarily (below).

**Summary disposition versus plenary review.** Conditional on any grant, a summary route is likelier than argument. The petition's alternative request is a grant, vacate, and remand with instructions to certify the controlling Iowa-law question to the Iowa Supreme Court, on the model of McKesson v. Doe. That is the only plausible form of relief here, since the Court does not hear argument to review one circuit's reading of one state's insurance regulation. Even so, a McKesson-style vacatur is rare (a handful in a generation), and this panel opinion, unlike the Fifth Circuit's in McKesson, rests primarily on the policy's plain terms and on the Iowa statute's six-month preexisting-condition provision, with the known-loss principle as a supporting rationale. There is no intervening decision of this Court to GVR against.

**Dissent or statement respecting denial.** Unlikely. The facts are the kind that can draw a sympathetic sentence, but no Justice has made Erie-fidelity in diversity insurance cases a cause, and the panel's primary ground (policy text plus the Iowa preexisting-condition statute) gives a would-be dissenter little to write against. Expect a bare denial.

**If granted (conditional only).** The most probable form would be a per curiam vacatur directing the Eighth Circuit to consider certifying to the Iowa Supreme Court whether Iowa Code § 514G.7(3)(b) and Iowa Admin. Code r. 191-39.8 bar a guaranteed-issue LTC insurer from denying a claim on the basis of a claim-time health-status determination. Plenary review resolving the Erie question on the merits is the least likely of all outcomes.
