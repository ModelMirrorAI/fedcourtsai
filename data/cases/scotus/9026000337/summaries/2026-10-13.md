## In brief

The Supreme Court declined to hear Denise Child's bid to revive her long-term care insurance claim against Unum after lower courts ruled against her.

## What happened

Denise Child, quadriplegic since 1980, enrolled in a group long-term care insurance policy through her employer in 2003 without Unum asking any health questions, under what is called "guaranteed issue" coverage. She paid premiums for eighteen years. When she filed a claim in 2021, Unum investigated her health history, determined her condition predated the policy's effective date, and denied the claim. A federal district court and the Eighth Circuit Court of Appeals both ruled for Unum, holding that Iowa law did not require covering losses that existed before coverage began.

## What the Court is being asked

The petition asks whether a federal court handling a case under its diversity jurisdiction (hearing a state-law dispute because the parties are from different states) wrongly overrode Iowa's insurance regulations. Child argues Iowa rules bar an insurer from waiving health questions when issuing guaranteed-issue coverage and then investigating an applicant's health only after a claim is filed. She contends the lower court instead relied on a general legal doctrine, not grounded in Iowa law, to allow that after-the-fact investigation.

## Where it stands

Unum waived its right to respond to the petition. The case was distributed for the Court's October 9, 2026 conference, and on October 13, 2026, the Court denied the petition.

## What each outcome would mean

Denise Child asked the Court to take up her case. By denying the petition, the Court leaves the Eighth Circuit's ruling in place, meaning Unum's denial of Child's claim stands and Child does not recover benefits under the policy. The denial does not mean the Court agreed with the lower court's reasoning; it simply leaves that decision as the final word in this case.
