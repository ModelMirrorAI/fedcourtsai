## In brief

The Supreme Court declined to review a disabled policyholder's bid to revive her lawsuit against Unum over denial of her long-term care insurance claim.

## What happened

Denise Child, who has been quadriplegic since 1980, enrolled in a guaranteed-issue group long-term care policy through her employer in 2003, meaning Unum asked no health questions and accepted her regardless of her condition. She paid premiums for eighteen years. When she filed a claim in 2021, Unum investigated her health history and denied the claim, finding her limitations existed before coverage began. Child sued for breach of contract, bad faith, and fraudulent misrepresentation. The district court granted summary judgment to Unum, and the Eighth Circuit affirmed, reasoning that the policy and Iowa law covered only losses arising after coverage took effect, not preexisting ones.

## What the Court is being asked

Child's petition asks whether a federal court, when deciding a case under state law, may apply a general judge-made rule against covering already-known losses to justify an insurer's after-the-claim health investigation, when a state's insurance regulations specifically bar insurers from asking health questions at claim time for policies that waived such questions when coverage began.

## Where it stands

The petition was distributed for the Court's October 9, 2026 conference, and on October 13, 2026 the Court denied the petition.

## What each outcome would mean

Denise Child, the petitioner, asked the Court to hear her case after losing in the lower courts. Because the Court denied the petition, it will not review the Eighth Circuit's decision, and that ruling against Child stands: Unum's denial of her long-term care claim remains in place. A denial of this kind does not mean the Court agreed with the lower court's reasoning, only that the case will not be heard.
