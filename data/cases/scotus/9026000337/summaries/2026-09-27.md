## What happened

Denise Child enrolled in a group long-term-care insurance policy through her employer in 2003, choosing "guaranteed issue" coverage that let her enroll without answering health questions. She has been quadriplegic since 1980 but worked for decades and paid premiums for about eighteen years. When she filed a claim in 2021, Unum Life Insurance Company investigated her health history and denied the claim, finding her condition existed before coverage began. Child sued for breach of contract and related claims. The district court and the Eighth Circuit Court of Appeals both ruled for Unum, holding that the policy and Iowa law permitted the denial. Child, the petitioner, now asks the Supreme Court to review that decision; Unum, the respondent, has waived its right to file a response.

## What the Court is being asked

Iowa has rules, based on a model used by insurance regulators nationally, that let insurers skip health questions when issuing "guaranteed issue" coverage, but then forbid the insurer from asking those same health questions later, at claim time, to deny a claim. The Court is asked whether a federal court handling a lawsuit between residents of different states may get around that state rule by instead applying a separate, judge-made rule saying insurance does not cover losses that already existed before the policy began, allowing an insurer to deny a claim based on a health condition it identified only after the claim was filed.
