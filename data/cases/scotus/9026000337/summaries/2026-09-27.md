## In brief

A former Unum policyholder asks the Court to review a ruling that let the insurer deny her long-term care claim based on a preexisting condition.

## What happened

Denise Child, who has been quadriplegic since 1980, enrolled in a guaranteed-issue group long-term care policy through her employer in 2003, without any health questions asked. After she filed a claim in 2021, Unum Life Insurance Company reviewed her condition as of the 2003 effective date, found her disability predated the policy, and denied benefits. Child sued for breach of contract, bad faith, and fraud. The district court granted summary judgment to Unum, and the Eighth Circuit affirmed, reasoning that the policy and Iowa law only bar excluding coverage for losses arising from preexisting conditions within six months after coverage begins, not losses that already existed when coverage started.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked whether a federal court handling a state-law insurance dispute may use a court-created rule against covering "known losses" to allow an insurer to examine an applicant's health only after a claim is filed, when state insurance regulations exempted that applicant's coverage from any health screening at signup and separately restrict after-the-fact health investigations at claim time.

## Where it stands

The petition was filed September 9, 2026. Unum waived its right to respond, and the case was distributed for the Court's October 9, 2026 conference. The docket shows no action since that distribution.

## What each outcome would mean

Denise Child, as petitioner, is asking the Court to take up her case. If the Court grants review, it would hear full arguments and decide the legal question itself, without yet determining who wins. If it denies review, the Eighth Circuit's decision affirming judgment for Unum stays in place, and Child's claim denial stands. Either outcome leaves open, or forecloses, Child's alternative request that the case be sent back so Iowa's courts can clarify the state-law question themselves.
