# Forecast of the Court's action

The Court will most likely **grant, vacate, and remand Citibank's case for reconsideration following action in the linked government case, No. 26-589**. A GVR is the most likely individual disposition, although its probability alone is below one half. I assign 64% to all grant-family outcomes combined and 34% to denial.

The starting docket has **zero distributions and no CVSG**. I expect an initial distribution after the response process, probably during the winter of 2026–2027, absent extensions. A distribution after this snapshot is 98% likely and satisfies the declared increment claim even if the Court never relists the petition. My central procedural path is an initial consideration followed by a hold while the government petition is resolved, rather than repeated free-standing consideration of the bank's contract defenses. I expect roughly one or two distribution entries before an initial grant or hold posture; a later distribution may follow disposition of the companion. I do not expect a long series of independent relists. The principal denial alternative is denial at or soon after the first conference, alongside an unsuccessful government petition.

The Court will **not call for the Solicitor General's views**. Federal respondents already participate, and the linked petition supplies the government's own vehicle and position. A CVSG remains only a 0.5% possibility.

If the Court takes this petition for plenary review, it will most likely address a **narrowed version of the single question presented**: whether the district court may maintain the disbursement injunction against the government's financial intermediary when the underlying grant claims must proceed in the Court of Federal Claims, or when the companion ruling otherwise removes the basis for the injunction. I do not expect plenary review devoted primarily to ordinary construction of Citibank's account agreements or a broad categorical immunity for all financial agents.

Conditional on a grant, a **summary disposition in the cert order is more likely than separate briefing and argument**, at 62.5%. The central route is to keep the bank petition pending for the lead litigation and then send it back in light of that decision. That is a forecast of a future companion decision, not a claim that a dispositive intervening ruling already exists. Direct summary reversal for inadequate bank-specific injunction findings is possible but substantially less likely. A consolidated or companion plenary grant remains a substantial alternative if the Court wants to settle all remedial questions together.

If review is denied, the Court will most likely deny without an explanatory opinion or noted dissent. The chance of any dissent from, or statement respecting, denial is 4% conditional on denial. No Justice is likely to write; Jackson is the most plausible individual writer if the eventual disposition prompts a separate discussion of grant-funding relief and jurisdiction. These are order-writing forecasts, not predictions of a majority opinion accompanying a denial.
