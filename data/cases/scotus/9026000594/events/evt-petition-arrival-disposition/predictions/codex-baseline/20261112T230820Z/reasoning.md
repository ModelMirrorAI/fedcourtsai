# Rationale

## Call and conditioning state

I assign **64% to any grant-family disposition**, including a grant, vacatur, and remand (GVR), and select **GVR** as the single most likely disposition. My approximate unconditional allocation is GVR 37%, summary reversal 3%, plenary grant 24%, denial 34%, and dismissal or withdrawal 2%. Thus the conditional probability of a disposition in the cert order, given a grant, is (37 + 3) / 64 = 62.5%. A GVR, unlike a summary reversal, returns the case for reconsideration rather than deciding its merits outright.

This is the cert-stage arrival event, not an application for emergency relief and not the merits of the grant-funding controversy. I read `record/snapshots/2026-11-06.json`, `record/context.json`, and the November 5, 2026 docketing event. The snapshot records the November 2 petition, a December 7 response deadline, **zero distributions**, and **no CVSG**. Those absences define the arrival moment; they are not evidence that the Court has considered and rejected the petition. The context is forward, Term 2026, `baseline` under `sal-v8`. I use the common provisioned baseline without supplementing this petition's current docket. I did not retrieve its disposition, subsequent history, or another forecast.

## Why the companion matters here

The petition, printed pp. 1–2, 8–11, and 23–25, is unusually explicit about its derivative character. Citibank seeks to avoid an injunction requiring payment while the government may remain entitled to direct it not to pay. The petition says that if the government obtains review, the bank's petition should accompany it. This offers the Court a practical reason to hold and later dispose of Citibank's petition alongside the federal one, even without finding the bank's freestanding contract defenses independently worthy of plenary review.

The independently provisioned `decision-below.txt` matches both the August 4 judgment date and the consolidated case numbers. It confirms that six judges sustained the restriction on EPA's termination notices, while the bank-related remainder survived an equally divided court. The appendix, printed pp. 1a–4a, reproduces that judgment. This is a fractured disposition, not a developed appellate majority explaining a broad rule against federal financial agents. The vacated panel had treated the bank injunction as derivative, according to the petition at pp. 8–9 and 19. That analysis is useful to understand the proposed remedial link, but it is not the governing judgment after rehearing.

The government's asserted Tucker Act channeling issue also has a genuine doctrinal connection to recent grant cases. I independently read the Supreme Court's April 4, 2025 order in **Department of Education v. California, 604 U.S. 650, 651 (2025)**, through CourtListener opinion 10839607. It concluded at the stay stage that the government was likely to establish lack of district-court jurisdiction to order the contractual payments at issue and pointed to the Court of Federal Claims. That is a reason to take the companion's jurisdictional question seriously, not a holding that every challenge to a grant termination belongs there. The petition's discussion of **NIH v. American Public Health Association, 145 S. Ct. 2658 (2025)**, pp. 17–19, is further advocacy in the same direction; my attempts to locate that opinion through MCP returned no results, so I do not claim independent verification of its details.

## Why not higher

There is no demonstrated inter-circuit conflict on Citibank's precise question. The QP asks whether this injunction can remain in place, a case-specific formulation. Contract construction, the bank's own agreements, and ordinary injunction factors could be resolved below. Interlocutory posture and the en banc tie on the bank-related relief reduce the prospect of a durable national rule in this vehicle. The appendix's August 7 order, pp. 116a–117a, extends the administrative stay while timely cert petitions are pending; that tempers immediate pressure for Supreme Court intervention and does not itself predict a grant.

I also distinguish the petition's rhetoric from the primary judgment. The en banc judgment does give reasons for its treatment of EPA and the repeal of the funding statute; it is not wholly unreasoned. What is missing is a majority rationale sustaining the specific disbursement-related relief against Citibank. Nor does a bank's promise to obey a future court order itself establish that an injunction is unnecessary. Grantees' asserted private contract rights and their distinction between already-disbursed funds and an obligation to pay new money are potentially significant obstacles to the bank's reasoning. A Supreme Court decision favoring the government need not automatically dispose of every independent claim against the bank.

## Procedural claims and stakes

The **98% `relist-increment` probability** means at least one future distribution relative to the present count of zero. At this arrival moment the first distribution alone satisfies that claim; it is emphatically not a 98% prediction of a second conference or a true relist. A hold for the companion makes additional distributions plausible, but I do not equate a hold or rescheduling with a merits-positive relist signal. A **0.5% CVSG probability** reflects the government's direct participation through the Solicitor General: its position can be supplied as a party, without inviting an amicus view.

The paid-segment statpack's terminal relist buckets have resolved denominators 10,204, 2,532, 495, and 483 for zero, one, two, and three-plus; the corresponding grant-family shares are approximately 1.8%, 13.5%, 41.2%, and 37.3%. The CVSG bucket has 164 resolved cases and a 34.8% grant-family share, versus 13,550 and 6.4% without a CVSG. These are terminal, pooled descriptive cuts, not the forward hazard from zero distributions. The snapshot contains no amicus entries; the context's amicus count is null, and support may still develop. The terminal zero-amicus bucket has 11,109 resolved cases and a 3.8% grant-family share, versus 1,159 and 14.9% with one and 1,446 and 22.7% with two-plus. I do not treat this arrival docket's present silence as membership in a final zero-amicus bucket or add these overlapping cuts to the anchor.

I expect no separate writing. The **4% dissent-or-statement probability is conditional on denial**. The independent per-Justice order-writer probabilities cover writings on any disposition and need not sum to one. Jackson has the highest, still small, number because of her independently observed writing in Department of Education; I do not predict that joining another Justice's opinion is authoring one.

The **0.76 stakes score** reflects the roughly $20 billion funding controversy described in the appendix's panel opinion, pp. 11a–12a, and implications for private federal financial intermediaries. It is not a second expression of grant likelihood; this petition's own issue is narrower than the underlying public-funding dispute.
