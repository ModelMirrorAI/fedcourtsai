Expect the Court to reverse the Fifth Circuit's judgment. The Court will likely uphold the federal prohibition on home distillation as a valid exercise of Congress's taxing power under the Necessary and Proper Clause. The majority opinion will rest its holding on the historical pedigree of the 160-year-old restriction and the practical difficulty of monitoring home stills, concluding that the location restriction is plainly adapted to preventing evasion of the federal excise tax on distilled spirits. The Court will likely confine its rationale to this specific historical and practical context of alcohol excise taxes rather than announcing a sweeping new rule that any ban on taxable activity is automatically constitutional. Expect a 7-2 or 6-3 split, with Justices Thomas and Gorsuch dissenting on structural federalism grounds, arguing that a flat ban on local activity exceeds Congress's enumerated powers and improperly exercises a general police power.
