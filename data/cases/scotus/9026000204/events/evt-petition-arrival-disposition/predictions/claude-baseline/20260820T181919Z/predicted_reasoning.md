# Forecast — Department of Justice v. McNutt, No. 26-204 (petition disposition, forecast at docketing)

The Court will grant certiorari. This is the Solicitor General petitioning from
a court of appeals decision (CA5, rehearing en banc denied June 9, 2026) that
held a federal statute — the 160-year-old home-distilling location restriction,
26 U.S.C. 5178(a)(1)(B) — unconstitutional, in an acknowledged square conflict
with the Sixth Circuit's decision in *Ream v. Department of the Treasury*, 174
F.4th 480 (2026), where a petition by the losing challengers is already pending
(No. 26-93, filed July 20, 2026). Each of those three features — statute
invalidated below, SG as petitioner, square circuit split on the identical
question — is independently a strong grant signal; together they make denial
implausible while the invalidation stands.

**Procedural path.** The docket shows zero distributions — the petition was
docketed August 18, 2026 and the response is due September 17, 2026. I expect:

- The petition **will be distributed** for conference in late September or
  October 2026, near the start of OT2026. From the current zero-distribution
  state, at least one further distribution is near-certain; the only realistic
  failure modes are a pre-conference withdrawal or mooting event (e.g.
  legislation legalizing home distilling), which I put at a few percent.
- **One or two distributions before the grant**: the Court's recent practice is
  to relist once before granting, so the most likely trajectory is
  distribution, one relist (in part to coordinate with *Ream*), then a grant in
  November or December 2026 — argument in the spring 2027 sittings and a
  decision by early summer 2027.
- **No CVSG.** The United States is the petitioner; the Court calls for the
  Solicitor General's views only when the government is not before it. This is
  as close to a structural zero as the claim allows.
- **Plenary review, not a summary route.** The split needs an opinion, and there
  is no intervening decision to GVR in light of. The one real summary-route
  scenario is vehicle choice: if the Court instead takes *Ream* (No. 26-93) as
  the vehicle, this petition would be held and later GVR'd (or granted and
  consolidated). I think the Court more likely grants the government's own
  petition — the judgment that actually invalidated the Act of Congress is this
  one — possibly hearing it alongside *Ream*; conditional on a grant I put the
  cert-order-only route (a hold-then-GVR) at roughly 12%.
- **Question presented as written.** The petition presents a single clean QP;
  no narrowing or reformulation is needed and I expect none.
- **If denied** (which I put at well under a tenth), the denial would leave a
  federal statute unenforceable in the Fifth Circuit while enforceable in the
  Sixth; I would expect a reasonable chance (~25%) of a statement or dissent
  respecting denial noting the split, most plausibly from a Justice focused on
  the Court's obligation to review invalidations of federal statutes.

**Conditionally on the merits** (context only, not part of the scored claim):
if granted, the likely ground is the *Felsenheld*/*Ream* line — the location
restriction as a permissible rule for the manufacture of a taxed good under
the Necessary and Proper Clause — and the government's position would more
likely than not prevail, reversing the Fifth Circuit.
