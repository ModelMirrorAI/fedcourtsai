# Prediction rationale

I assign a 0.95 probability to any grant and predict a plain grant. The matching `sal-v3` statpack gives the arrival-time anchor: pooling the federal band's bracketed `reached` figures over OT2017 through OT2025 produces 143 grant-family dispositions among 201 resolved petitions, or 71.1%. Because `federal` is the strongest band, its terminal and risk-set rates coincide. I did not use the terminal relist-zero cut, which would condition on a future trajectory that is unknown at arrival.

This petition is substantially stronger than even that selected-population anchor. The Solicitor General seeks review of a Fifth Circuit judgment invalidating a 160-year-old federal statute. The Sixth Circuit recently upheld the identical provision in *Ream* on irreconcilable Necessary and Proper Clause reasoning. The petition also identifies concrete vehicle advantages: standing is undisputed here, associational standing reduces mootness risk, and the question excludes *Ream*'s additional Commerce Clause issue. Those features make review of this case, with *Ream* held, much more likely than denial. I retain a five-percent denial/dismissal tail because this is an arrival forecast made before the response, which could disclose a preservation, standing, mootness, or remedial defect, and because the Court could prefer the companion vehicle.

The frozen distribution count is zero. The 0.995 relist-increment probability therefore prices an increase above zero—ordinary initial distribution—not solely a later re-listing after the first conference. The 0.001 CVSG-increment probability reflects that the United States is already the petitioner and the Solicitor General is counsel. Conditional on a grant, I put 0.04 on a cert-order disposition: the square constitutional conflict is much more likely to receive plenary review than a GVR or summary reversal. Conditional on denial, I put 0.08 on any noted dissent or statement; a denial would most likely rest on a quiet vehicle choice rather than a disagreement worth separate writing.

The provisioned questions-presented text and the petition's certworthiness and merits sections were readable. `documents.json` marks the 110-page petition text as truncated; the truncation affects the later appendix rather than the question presented or the petition's argument. No brief in opposition was available, as expected at docketing, so the forecast is necessarily based on the government's presentation and should be discounted for unknown adversarial vehicle arguments. The 0.68 big-case score reflects a meaningful federal-powers and tax-administration question tempered by the narrow home-distilling setting.
