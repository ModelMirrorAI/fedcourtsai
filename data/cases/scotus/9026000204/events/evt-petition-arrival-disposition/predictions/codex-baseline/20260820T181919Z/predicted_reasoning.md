# Predicted court action

The record shows zero conference distributions and no CVSG at this arrival moment. The petition will almost certainly receive an initial distribution. I expect the Court either to grant after that conference or to carry the petition through one true relist before granting; multiple additional relists are less likely.

The Court will likely grant the petition, use this case as the plenary vehicle, and hold the companion petition in *Ream*. The Fifth and Sixth Circuits reached opposite answers about the constitutionality of the same federal provision, and this case presents the dispute without *Ream*'s standing disagreement and additional Commerce Clause question. The Court is likely to take the single question presented substantially as written: whether 26 U.S.C. 5178(a)(1)(B)'s location restrictions are necessary and proper to carry Congress's taxing power into execution.

No CVSG is expected because the United States is the petitioner and the Solicitor General has already presented the federal government's views. A grant should lead to briefing and argument rather than a GVR or summary reversal; the constitutional circuit conflict calls for a precedential resolution. If the Court reaches the merits, it is likely to uphold the restriction as a measure plainly adapted to collecting the distilled-spirits tax and reverse the Fifth Circuit, relying on the long history of evasion, inspection difficulties in homes, and the latitude described in *McCulloch*, *Felsenheld*, and *Comstock*.

If review is denied instead, the likelier explanation is a vehicle problem exposed by the response or selection of the companion case, and the denial will probably carry no separate dissent or statement.
