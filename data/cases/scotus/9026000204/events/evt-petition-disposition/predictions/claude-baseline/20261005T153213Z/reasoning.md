# Rationale for the numbers

**P(grant) = 0.92.** This is a cert-stage, `moment: distribution`, `forward` cell. `record/context.json` freezes `band: federal` under `sal-v4`, `distribution_count: 1`, `cvsg_date: null`, `term: 2026`, and I read the provisioned snapshot `2026-10-05.json`.

*Anchor.* The statpack's "Segment base rate by salience band (sal-v4)" table matches my context's salience version and carries a `federal` column. Pooling the bracketed `reached` figures over every rendered Term strictly before 2026 (OT2017 through OT2025, nine rows, weighted n = 202) gives a grant-family rate of **0.708** (roughly 143 grants); the last five Terms alone give 0.737 (n = 95). The marginal "Cert petitions by salience band" cut agrees: federal = granted 48.5% + gvr 22.3%. The modern whole-docket rate is a few percent and is not the relevant population here.

*Adjustments upward, which together move me from 0.71 to 0.92.*
- The Fifth Circuit held a federal statute unconstitutional. The petition's reliance on *Iancu v. Brunetti*'s "usual" grant practice in that posture is well founded, and the federal band's pooled rate includes many SG petitions that lack this feature.
- A square, acknowledged circuit split: *Ream v. Department of the Treasury*, 174 F.4th 480 (6th Cir. Apr. 21, 2026), upheld the same provision against the same challenge eleven days after the Fifth Circuit's decision. I confirmed both opinions exist as published decisions through CourtListener (McNutt, CA5 No. 24-10760, filed 2026-04-10; Ream, CA6 No. 25-3259, filed 2026-04-21).
- The brief in opposition does not oppose. Respondents, represented by Baker & Hostetler with Buckeye Institute and American Federalism Project co-counsel, open by agreeing certiorari is warranted and ask the Court to grant here and in *Ream*. The only contested point is vehicle choice and scope, not whether to grant.
- Procedural tempo: the SG waived the 14-day Rule 15.5 wait and the petition was distributed for the October 9 conference within six days of the BIO, the shape of a petition being queued for this Term's argument calendar.
- The companion docket. I fetched the *Ream* docket (No. 26-93) from supremecourt.gov: it was distributed for the September 28 conference, **rescheduled** on September 8, and re-distributed on September 23 for the **October 9** conference, the same day this petition was distributed for the same conference. The Court deliberately paired the two petitions. Eleven amicus entries sit on the *Ream* docket, including a multistate brief led by Ohio. That is pre-snapshot public information about a companion case, legitimate forward signal, and I note it in `flags.json` as decisive context.

*What holds the number below ~0.97.* The main residual risk is not denial on the merits of the cert question but vehicle selection: the Court could grant *Ream* alone and hold this petition. I put that branch near 0.06. In it, this petition is later GVR'd if the Court upholds the statute (still a grant), but denied if the Court strikes it down, since the Fifth Circuit's judgment would then be correct. I split that roughly evenly, giving about 0.03 of denial probability from the hold branch. I add about 0.03 for an outright denial of both petitions and a residual for dismissal or mootness. The government's vehicle argument (clean standing for McNutt and the association, a single question, no commerce-power baggage) is strong, and the Court normally defers to the SG's vehicle preference when the SG is the petitioner, so I do not weight the hold branch more heavily than this.

**Claims.**
- `disposition` 0.92: restates the top-line number.
- `relist-increment` 0.70: the docket shows one distribution. Among grant-family outcomes in the paid scored segment (relist cut), only about a fifth resolve from a single distribution; the rest show at least one more. The pull toward a direct grant from the October 9 conference (SG petition, early-Term calendar need, uncontested BIO) is real, so I sit below the ~0.8 the shape alone would suggest. A hold-for-*Ream* branch also produces a later redistribution.
- `cvsg-increment` 0.01: the SG is the petitioner; a CVSG is structurally impossible. Not literally zero only because the claim is banked and a non-zero floor is honest.
- `summary-disposition-route` 0.05 (conditional on grant): plenary review is what both sides ask for; the only summary path is a GVR following a *Ream* merits decision upholding the statute, which requires the less likely vehicle choice first.
- `dissent-from-denial` 0.35 (conditional on denial): the likeliest denial path is a follow-on to a *Ream* merits ruling, which draws no writing; the rarer outright denial of an SG petition where a statute was struck down almost certainly would.

**Stakes = 0.6.** A Necessary and Proper Clause case with a federal statute invalidated, a multistate amicus coalition on the companion docket, and a topic with wide popular appeal. Doctrinally it is the taxing power's regulatory perimeter after *NFIB*, not the center of the Term, so I place it above the median granted case but well below the marquee cases.

**Documents used.** `questions-presented.txt`, the petition's introduction and reasons-for-granting section (`petition.txt`, 110 pages, `truncated: true` in `documents.json`; the truncation cuts into the appendix, not the argument I read), and the full brief in opposition (20 pages, not truncated, text extracted). No `empty_text` flags.

**Where to discount me.** The relist probability is the softest number: whether the Court grants from the first conference is a practice that has varied Term to Term and I have no per-Term cut for it. The anchor's federal rows are thin (n between 11 and 41 per Term), and the pooled 0.71 blends posture types the pack cannot separate. The corpus `query` I ran returned eight modern granted priors but carried no caption or petitioner-class fields in the rows I printed, so it informed the shape of distribution counts only and did not move the number.
