# Forecast: what the Court will do with No. 26-204, Department of Justice v. McNutt

**Disposition.** The Court will grant certiorari and set the case for plenary briefing and argument, most likely in the February or March 2027 sitting. The grant will issue from the October 9, 2026 conference or the one immediately after it. A denial is a remote possibility and would arise only indirectly, if the Court chose the Sixth Circuit's *Ream* petition (No. 26-93) as its vehicle instead, held this petition, and then resolved *Ream* in a way that leaves the Fifth Circuit's judgment undisturbed.

**Relists.** The docket shows **one** distribution so far, for the conference of October 9, 2026. I expect at least one further distribution before the petition is decided, most likely a single relist to the following conference, consistent with the Court's practice of relisting once before granting to screen for vehicle problems. The counter-scenario is a grant announced straight out of the October 9 conference, which the early-Term calendar pressure and the Solicitor General's Rule 15.5 waiver make a live possibility; that is why my relist probability sits at 0.70 rather than higher. If the Court instead holds this petition for *Ream*, it will be redistributed after *Ream* is decided, which also registers as a further distribution.

**CVSG.** None. The Solicitor General is the petitioner, so there is no one to invite.

**Which question.** The Court will take the government's question presented as written: whether the location restriction on distilling in 26 U.S.C. 5178(a)(1)(B) is necessary and proper to Congress's power to lay and collect taxes. It will not reach the commerce-power theory the respondents and the *Ream* petitioner press, because the government abandoned that theory on appeal in this case and no court of appeals has ruled on it; the Court's grant order will not expand the question to include it. The likelier companion treatment is a grant here with *Ream* held, rather than a consolidated grant of both, because the government is the petitioner here, every judge below found standing, and the respondent association forecloses mootness, while *Ream* carries a standing dissent and an extra question.

**Summary disposition.** Unlikely. No intervening decision of this Court bears on the split, the government seeks plenary review, and both sides agree the question warrants argument. A GVR of this petition becomes possible only in the hold-for-*Ream* branch, and then only if the Court upholds the statute there.

**Dissent from denial.** In the unlikely event of a denial, a noted dissent is a coin flip tilted slightly against. A denial following a merits ruling in *Ream* that invalidates the statute would draw no separate writing, since it would simply follow the decision; an outright denial of a Solicitor General petition where a federal statute was struck down would very probably draw one, but that branch is the least likely of all.

**If granted.** Conditionally only: the Court would likely reverse the Fifth Circuit, treating the location restriction as a reasonable rule for the manufacture of a taxed good under *Felsenheld* and *McCulloch*, while writing in terms that reaffirm an outer limit on the Necessary and Proper Clause. A merits cell will forecast that properly when the time comes.
