# Forecast of the Court's action

The Court will grant certiorari and set this case for plenary briefing and argument. The principal reason for taking it will be the conflicting appellate decisions on the constitutionality of the federal home-distilling prohibition, coupled with the government's request to review a judgment invalidating federal legislation. Respondents also request review, while defending the judgment on the merits.

The provisioned docket records **one distribution**, on September 23, for the October 9, 2026 conference, and no CVSG. I expect **one additional distribution** before a grant, with two additional distributions a secondary possibility. The probability of at least one further distribution is **0.72**; an immediate grant following the first conference remains a substantial alternative. My central timing forecast is a grant during October or November 2026, rather than a prolonged hold. Timing is not a scored claim.

The Court will not call for the Solicitor General's views: the Solicitor General already represents the federal petitioners and has supplied their position. The probability of a new CVSG is **0.002**.

If review is granted, the Court will take the government's taxing-power/Necessary and Proper Clause question substantially as presented: whether the location restrictions in 26 U.S.C. 5178(a)(1)(B), including the home-distilling prohibition, validly implement Congress's power to collect taxes. It will not use this petition to undertake an independent reconsideration of Gonzales v. Raich. The likelier companion-case treatment is to hold Ream while this case proceeds, rather than broaden this case to decide every commerce-power theory. Consolidation remains possible, but the forecast here concerns this petition alone.

Conditional on any grant, the probability of disposal in the cert order itself is **0.035**. Plenary consideration is much likelier than a GVR or summary reversal: the competing constitutional rationales call for an argued resolution, and the supplied materials identify no intervening controlling decision requiring a remand. If granted, the central merits dispute will concern the relationship between restricting production locations and collecting excise taxes, including the constitutional limits on using that relationship to regulate local conduct. This is not a prediction of a merits judgment or of an unconditional opinion.

Conditional on denial, the probability of a noted dissent or statement respecting denial is **0.40**. The modal denial branch is therefore an unexplained order without a separate writing, although leaving an acknowledged conflict over an invalidated federal statute would create a substantial reason for a public objection. I do not forecast a particular Justice or a cert vote lineup.
