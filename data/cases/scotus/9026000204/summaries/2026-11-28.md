## In brief

The government asks the Supreme Court to uphold the longstanding federal ban on distilling spirits at home, after an appeals court struck it down.

## What happened

Several individuals who wanted to distill spirits at home, along with a hobby distillers' association, sued the Department of Justice and the Alcohol and Tobacco Tax and Trade Bureau, arguing that the federal ban on home distilling goes beyond Congress's constitutional powers. A district court agreed and permanently blocked enforcement against them. The Fifth Circuit affirmed, holding the ban was not a valid use of Congress's taxing power because it reduces, rather than raises, revenue by preventing distilling rather than taxing it. Days later, the Sixth Circuit reached the opposite conclusion in a similar case, upholding the same law. The government asked the Supreme Court to resolve the conflict.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked whether the law barring spirits from being distilled in homes and nearby structures is a valid way of carrying out Congress's power to collect taxes, or whether it goes beyond that power. The respondents, while agreeing the lower court ruled correctly, also agree the Supreme Court should take up the case to settle the conflict between the appeals courts.
