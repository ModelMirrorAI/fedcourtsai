## In brief

The federal government asks the Court to review a ruling that struck down the longstanding ban on distilling spirits at home as exceeding Congress's taxing powers.

## What happened

Hobby distillers and an advocacy association sued the Department of Justice and the Alcohol and Tobacco Tax and Trade Bureau, challenging a federal law that bars producing distilled spirits in a home or nearby shed or yard. A district court ruled the ban unconstitutional and blocked its enforcement against the challengers. The Fifth Circuit affirmed, holding the ban is not a valid use of Congress's power to tax, because it reduces rather than raises revenue and oversteps limits on federal authority. Days later, the Sixth Circuit upheld the same type of ban in a different case, creating a conflict between appeals courts.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked whether a long-standing law prohibiting distilling spirits in homes and connected spaces is a valid way for Congress to carry out its power to collect taxes on alcohol, or whether it goes beyond that power and intrudes on authority reserved to the states.

## Where it stands

The petition has been fully briefed, including a reply from the government, and was distributed for the Court's conference on October 9, 2026. The docket does not yet show any decision on whether the Court will hear the case.

## What each outcome would mean

The federal government, as petitioner, is asking the Court to take up the case and reverse the Fifth Circuit's ruling. If the Court grants review, it will hear full arguments and decide whether the home-distilling ban is constitutional, without presuming either side correct. If it denies review, the Fifth Circuit's decision striking down the ban stands, leaving it unenforceable against these challengers, though the conflicting Sixth Circuit ruling remains in place elsewhere. The respondents, individual distillers and their association, support Supreme Court review as well, though they argue the lower court's ruling in their favor was correct.
