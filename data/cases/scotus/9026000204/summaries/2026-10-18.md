## In brief

The federal government asks the Court to reinstate its ban on home distilling after an appeals court struck it down as exceeding Congress's taxing power.

## What happened

Hobby distillers, including Scott McNutt and the Hobby Distillers Association, sued the Department of Justice and the Alcohol and Tobacco Tax and Trade Bureau, challenging a law that bars producing distilled spirits in a home or nearby shed or yard. A federal district court ruled the law unconstitutional, and the Fifth Circuit agreed, holding the prohibition was not a valid use of Congress's power to tax. That decision conflicts with a Sixth Circuit ruling in a separate case, Ream, which upheld the same prohibition. The government appealed to the Supreme Court. McNutt's side opposes the government's framing of the case but agrees the Court should take it up.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked whether this ban on producing distilled spirits at certain locations, including homes, is a valid tool for carrying out Congress's power to collect taxes on alcohol, or whether it goes beyond that power.

## Where it stands

The petition was distributed for a conference on October 9, 2026, and again for a conference on October 16, 2026, the day before this snapshot. The docket does not yet show any order granting or denying the petition.

## What each outcome would mean

The government, which lost in the lower court, is asking the Court to take up the case. If the Court grants the petition, it will hear full arguments and decide whether the law is constitutional, deciding nothing yet about who is right. If it denies the petition, the Fifth Circuit's ruling striking down the law stands, at least as applied to McNutt and the Hobby Distillers Association. The parties also dispute whether the Court should additionally take up the related Ream case or instead hold it pending this one.
