The federal government asked the Supreme Court to review a ruling striking down the longstanding ban on home distilling, and the Court agreed to hear the case.

## What happened

Four individuals and a hobby distillers group sued the Department of Justice and the Treasury Department's Alcohol and Tobacco Tax and Trade Bureau, arguing that the federal law banning distilling spirits at home exceeds Congress's constitutional powers. A district court agreed and permanently blocked enforcement against them. The Fifth Circuit affirmed, holding the ban was not a valid exercise of Congress's power to tax, since it reduces rather than raises revenue. That ruling conflicts with a Sixth Circuit decision in a similar case, called Ream, which upheld the same law. The government then asked the Supreme Court to step in.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked whether the law barring distilling in homes and nearby structures is a lawful way for Congress to support its power to tax distilled spirits, even though the ban itself does not raise any revenue.
