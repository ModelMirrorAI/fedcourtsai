## What happened

Federal law has long banned distilling spirits in homes, attached yards or sheds, and certain other locations, as part of the system for collecting the excise tax on distilled spirits. Scott McNutt, the Hobby Distillers Association, and other members sued the Department of Justice and the Alcohol and Tobacco Tax and Trade Bureau, arguing the location ban is unconstitutional. A federal district court agreed and permanently blocked enforcement against them. The Fifth Circuit affirmed, holding the restriction exceeds Congress's power, disagreeing with a Sixth Circuit decision that upheld the same restriction in a similar case. The government now asks the Supreme Court to review the Fifth Circuit's ruling.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked whether Congress had the constitutional authority to ban distilling spirits in homes and similar locations as a way of helping collect the tax on distilled spirits, or whether that ban goes beyond what is needed to carry out the taxing power. The government argues the location rule helps prevent tax evasion by keeping distilling out of places where it is hard to inspect. The challengers, whose position is described in the lower court rulings, argue the ban does not raise revenue and instead punishes conduct Congress cannot otherwise reach, exceeding its enumerated powers.

## Where it stands
