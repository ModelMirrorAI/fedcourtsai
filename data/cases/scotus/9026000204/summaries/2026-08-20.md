## In brief

The federal government asks the Supreme Court to reinstate a law banning home distilling after a lower court struck it down.

## What happened

Scott McNutt, the Hobby Distillers Association, and other individuals who want to distill spirits at home sued the Department of Justice and the Alcohol and Tobacco Tax and Trade Bureau, arguing that the longstanding federal ban on distilling spirits in homes and connected spaces is unconstitutional. A district court agreed and permanently blocked enforcement against them. The Fifth Circuit affirmed, holding the location ban was not a valid use of Congress's tax power. This conflicts with a Sixth Circuit decision in a similar case, Ream, upholding the same ban.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked whether Congress's power to raise taxes lets it also ban distilling spirits in certain locations, including homes, as a way of making that tax easier to collect and evasion harder to hide.

## Where it stands

The government filed its petition for certiorari on August 14, 2026, and a response is due September 17, 2026. The Court has not yet acted on the petition.

## What each outcome would mean

The government, which lost in the Fifth Circuit, is asking the Court to take the case. If the Court grants the petition, it will hear the case fully and decide whether the location restrictions are constitutional, deciding nothing yet about who wins. If the Court denies the petition, the Fifth Circuit's ruling striking down the restrictions stands, and the government cannot enforce the ban against McNutt and the other challengers.
