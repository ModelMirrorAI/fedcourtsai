# Forecast

The Court will grant review of the national-bank preemption question after receiving the Solicitor General's views. The most likely individual disposition is a plenary grant; the probability of any grant, including a GVR or other summary grant, is 68%.

The snapshot records one distribution, for the September 28, 2026 conference, and an October 5, 2026 invitation to the Solicitor General. From that state, expect two additional distributions before disposition: a return to conference after the government's brief and one further consideration. The probability of at least one additional distribution is 97%. This predicts the recorded distribution increment, including the post-CVSG return, not necessarily two conventional relists following actual conference consideration. The government's briefing will delay disposition for months rather than days.

The CVSG has already occurred. No new CVSG is predicted; the required increment claim is 0 and is vacuous under the existing-CVSG mask. The substantive content of the government's forthcoming recommendation is not assumed to be known.

If the Court takes this petition for plenary review, it will take the petition's single question: whether the National Bank Act preempts application of state interest-on-escrow laws to national banks. The New York law will supply the concrete setting for applying the significant-interference standard, rather than a freestanding challenge to every aspect of the OCC's new rules. The Court's reason for taking the case will be the renewed, acknowledged appellate conflict over application of its earlier guidance, not merely disagreement with the result below.

Conditional on a grant in this docket, plenary review is more likely than disposition within a cert order: 65% versus 35%. The principal summary route is a hold followed by a GVR after the Court decides the same issue in another vehicle. The competing Flagstar and Conti vehicles described in the parties' briefs make that a meaningful alternative. A summary reversal is less likely than either plenary consideration or a GVR because the regulatory developments and competing readings require analysis beyond a straightforward mandate correction.

If the petition is denied, expect an ordinary denial without a separate writing. The probability of any noted dissent or statement respecting denial, conditional on denial, is 8%; no particular Justice's participation is forecast. No unconditional merits judgment or merits opinion is predicted.
