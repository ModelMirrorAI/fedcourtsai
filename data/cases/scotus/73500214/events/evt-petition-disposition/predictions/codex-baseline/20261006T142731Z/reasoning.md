That historical proceeding is independently described in the provisioned filings; no web-derived information entered the forecast. No current docket lookup, subsequent-history search, outcome artifact, or other predictor's output was used.

This is the anchor, not the overall docket rate or the private-petitioner arrival floor.

Those all-Term cuts are descriptive cross-checks, not additional independent evidence to multiply into the prior-Term anchor. The modern all-fee cert table and originating-circuit cut cover a broader, less selected population and do not replace the high-band anchor.

These figures describe the committed statpack available in this run, not a freshly queried corpus. Its inspected metadata do not provide the corpus-wide newest-pull and newest-snapshot vintage; I did not pull or query the corpus. ## Why 68%, rather than 35% or near certainty

The strongest upward adjustment is the unusually concrete disagreement after an earlier Supreme Court remand. The petition's introduction and pages 16-24 identify the Second Circuit's express disagreement with the First Circuit over substantively comparable interest-on-escrow requirements. The BIO, pages 23-25, contests the depth and maturity of that conflict but itself characterizes it as effectively a 1-1 split. Thus this is not only a petitioner's unsupported assertion that the circuits disagree. The Ninth Circuit supplies additional inconsistent results, although the BIO persuasively distinguishes its reliance on older circuit precedent from a fresh application of the 2024 framework.

The previous remand has not removed the practical uncertainty. That increases the value of a further decision answering the concrete preemption question rather than once again announcing only a method. The petition, pages 22-25, describes laws in fourteen states and broader consequences for banking preemption. The snapshot records briefs from state banking supervisors and a multistate coalition. These reinforce importance, though I do not infer the detailed content of the unprovisioned amicus briefs. The actual CVSG is direct evidence of attention, already substantially represented by the high-band anchor and not counted a second time as an independent numerical multiplier.

The principal downward adjustments come from the BIO. Pages 10-11 and 25-29 describe new OCC rules, effective June 18, 2026, addressing escrow powers and preemption. On the respondent's account, neither appellate court has considered their consequences. I treat that as a supported argument in the provisioned BIO, not an independently verified conclusion that the regulations control this litigation. It offers a serious reason for further lower-court development, a remand route, or an unfavorable government recommendation. The prior unanimous remand does not itself establish that the Second Circuit must lose again.

Vehicle competition matters separately from issue importance. The petition, pages 25-27, presents this case as the clean, previously tested vehicle and identifies problems with Flagstar and the rehearing posture in Conti. The BIO, pages 29-32, directly responds: it emphasizes Flagstar's final judgment and record, reports waiver of nonjurisdictional vehicle objections there, and asks the Court to take another case and hold this one. I cannot treat the chance that the Court addresses the issue somewhere as this petition's plenary-grant probability. Nevertheless, a hold followed by a GVR here still counts as a grant under this cell's target definition. That keeps the any-grant probability materially above the probability of this docket becoming the lead argued case.

Balancing a concrete renewed split, prior Court involvement, substantial stakes, and actual CVSG against regulatory uncertainty and vehicle competition yields a subjective 68% probability of any grant. This is an appreciable, deliberately nonmechanical upward adjustment from 34.97%, not an estimated causal effect or a fitted model. A consistent allocation is 44.2% plenary grant, 23.8% summary grant-family disposition, 30% denial, and 2% dismissal or withdrawal. The largest uncertainties are the government's recommendation and whether another vehicle supplies a controlling decision that changes the treatment of this judgment.

## Ancillary claims and significance

The 97% distribution-increment forecast follows from the pending CVSG and the expectation of renewed consideration after briefing. It does not use the terminal relist table as a transition matrix. Two additional distributions are the point forecast; one is a substantial alternative. The 0% CVSG increment states no additional call and is masked because the call is already in the frozen context. The 35% summary-route probability is conditional on a grant, not an unconditional probability. The 8% separate-writing probability is conditional on denial and reflects the ordinary expectation of an unexplained denial even in a substantial statutory case; it is not a forecast of individual cert votes.

The significance score is 0.72, independently of the grant forecast. The supplied question and briefs concern household escrow compensation across multiple states and the national/state boundary in bank regulation. That is broader than a private damages dispute, but more specialized than a constitutional ruling changing government structure or a nationwide electoral rule.

One passage in the supplied petition, printed page 17, reverses which of the First and Second Circuits found preemption. Its neighboring discussion, the question presented, and the BIO consistently give the opposite allocation: the Second Circuit finds New York's law preempted, while the First Circuit rejects preemption of Rhode Island's law. I use the consistently corroborated allocation and flag the isolated internal contradiction without asserting whether it originated in the filing or extraction.
