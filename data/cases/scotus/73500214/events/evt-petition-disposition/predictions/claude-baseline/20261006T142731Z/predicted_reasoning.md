# Forecast: what the Court will do with No. 25-1313, Cantero v. Bank of America (II)

**State forecast from.** The docket shows **one distribution** (for the
September 28, 2026 conference) and a **CVSG entered October 5, 2026**. The
petition has therefore already cleared its first conference without a denial,
and the Court has asked the Solicitor General for the views of the United
States. Two cert-stage amicus briefs are on file (the Conference of State Bank
Supervisors; New York and 25 other states plus the District of Columbia).

**Disposition.** More likely than not the Court grants review, in this case or
in a form that reaches this case: P(any grant) = 0.52, and the single most
likely disposition is a plenary grant. The question presented is the one the
Court unanimously took and decided in 2024, the Second Circuit's remand
decision is divided, and it expressly declines to follow the First Circuit's
post-*Cantero* ruling in *Conti*. The Court has shown it regards this issue as
worth its time and this petition as a clean vehicle, having granted it over
*Kivett* in 2023 against the Solicitor General's recommendation.

The chief route to a denial runs through the Solicitor General. The OCC's May
2026 Escrow Powers Rule and Preemption Determination put the executive branch
on the bank's side of the merits, and the United States may recommend that the
Court let courts address those regulations first, or that it take *Kivett v.
Flagstar* (No. 25-1350), where the bank lost, as the vehicle and hold this
petition. If the Court holds this case behind *Flagstar*, this petition ends in
a GVR (a grant) if the Court rules against preemption and a denial if it rules
for it.

**Relists.** Near-certain further distribution: after the Solicitor General
files, the petition will be redistributed at least once (P = 0.97). Expect one
redistribution, possibly a second if the Court is sorting this petition against
*Flagstar* and the *Conti* rehearing petition (No. 25-1004).

**CVSG.** Already on the docket (October 5, 2026); no further call is
expected. Expect the brief of the United States in roughly May 2027 and a
disposition in June 2027. If the petition is instead held behind *Flagstar*,
the disposition slips to the end of the following Term.

**Which question.** If the Court grants, it takes the question presented as
written, the same question it granted in 2023: whether the National Bank Act
preempts state interest-on-escrow laws as applied to national banks. A
reformulation is unlikely because the petition presents a single, certified
question with no ancillary issues. Where the Court consolidates with
*Flagstar*, the operative question stays the same.

**Summary disposition.** Conditional on a grant, a cert-order disposition
(GVR) is the minority route: P = 0.22. The GVR path is the hold-behind-*Flagstar*
scenario above; no intervening decision now exists that would justify an
immediate GVR, and the Court's 2024 vacatur already sent the case back once, so
a second bare vacatur without argument is unlikely.

**Dissent from denial.** Conditional on a denial, P(some Justice writes) =
0.20. A denial would most likely follow a Solicitor General recommendation to
wait for the OCC regulations to be tested in the lower courts, which the
Justices who joined the 2024 opinion could accept without writing. A statement
respecting denial noting the open split is the likeliest writing if one comes.

**If granted (conditional).** The Court would apply its own *Barnett Bank*
comparative framework to the New York statute rather than restate it, and the
2024 opinion's skepticism of preemption rules that sweep in laws of minor
practical effect makes a ruling against preemption more likely than not. The
status of the OCC's 2026 Preemption Determination under 12 U.S.C. § 25b would
become a live second issue at the merits stage.
