# Forecast: what the Court will do with No. 25-1313, Cantero v. Bank of America (second trip)

**State forecast from.** The docket shows one distribution (for the September 28, 2026 Long Conference) and a CVSG entered October 5, 2026. Two states-side amicus briefs (New York plus 26 states and DC; the Conference of State Bank Supervisors) are on file. The Court entered a CVSG the same day in the companion bank-side petition, Flagstar Bank v. Kivett (No. 25-1350, from the Ninth Circuit), so the pair is again moving together, as it did in 2023.

**Relists.** The petition will be redistributed at least once more: after the Solicitor General files, the Clerk redistributes for conference as a matter of course. Expect the SG's brief in the spring of 2027 (an October CVSG typically returns in time for a May or June conference; a December filing for a January conference is the faster alternative), followed by one to two conferences before an order. A single further distribution is the modal case; a second relist is plausible if the Court is deciding how to pair this petition with Flagstar.

**CVSG.** Already on the docket; no further call will issue.

**Disposition.** Grant, on balance. The Court is more likely than not to take up the interest-on-escrow question again this cycle: the Second Circuit expressly split from the First Circuit (Conti) on indistinguishable statutes, the Ninth Circuit sits on the other side as well, fourteen states' laws are affected, the respondent bank concedes the question warrants review in some vehicle, and the Court already granted this very case in 2023 over the SG's recommendation. The live uncertainty is vehicle choice rather than certworthiness. The three routes:

1. **Plenary grant of this petition, alone or consolidated with Flagstar** (most likely among grant outcomes, roughly 40 percent of all outcomes). This case is the vehicle the Court chose last time, arrives on the same clean certified question, and lets the Court review the circuit it has already reversed once. Consolidation with Flagstar is attractive because the two cases present mirror-image outcomes on identical laws.
2. **Grant Flagstar, hold this petition, then dispose by GVR or denial** (roughly 30 percent). The current SG, with the OCC's May 2026 preemption determination on the books, will very likely side with preemption on the merits and may prefer the bank-petitioner vehicle; the Kivett respondents have waived the HOLA vehicle objection. On this path this petition is granted only if the Court ultimately rejects preemption and vacates the Second Circuit (a GVR); it is denied if the Court upholds preemption.
3. **Deny everything** (roughly 25 to 30 percent). The SG recommends letting lower courts address the new OCC rule and the Court agrees; or the Court concludes the 2026 OCC determination makes the pre-rule split academic.

**Which question.** If granted, the Court takes the question presented as written, which is the same question it granted in 2023. No reformulation is needed; the only likely addition is an express direction to address the significance of the OCC's 2026 preemption determination and Escrow Powers Rule, which the 2024 opinion reserved in a footnote.

**Summary disposition.** Conditional on a grant, a GVR is a real but minority route (about one in four grants), arising only on the held-behind-Flagstar path where the Court rules against preemption. A summary reversal is very unlikely: the Second Circuit applied the framework the Court prescribed, and the Court does not summarily reverse applications of its own multi-factor test.

**Dissent from denial.** If the petition is denied, a separate writing is unlikely. A denial would most probably come either after the SG recommends denial or after a hold resolved by a pro-preemption merits ruling in Flagstar, and in neither posture is a dissent from denial customary. A short statement from Justice Jackson or Justice Sotomayor respecting the denial is the main possibility.

**If granted, on the merits (conditional only).** The Court would again decline both categorical positions. The likelier result is a second vacatur of the Second Circuit for over-reading Franklin and treating RESPA and TILA silence as a broad grant of power, with several Justices open to the view that an OCC determination made under section 25b's procedural constraints deserves weight. The outcome is genuinely close; the Court has signaled no appetite for either field-style preemption or a strong presumption against it.
