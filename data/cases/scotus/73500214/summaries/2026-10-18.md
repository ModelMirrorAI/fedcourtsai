## In brief

Mortgage borrowers ask the Court to hear a second round of their case over whether federal banking law blocks New York's requirement that banks pay interest on mortgage escrow accounts.

## What happened

Alex Cantero and other New York homeowners sued Bank of America, arguing it owed them interest on money held in mortgage escrow accounts, as required by New York law. The bank argued federal banking law preempts that state requirement for national banks. In 2024, the Supreme Court vacated an earlier Second Circuit ruling for the bank, and told the appeals court to use a more careful, comparative method for deciding preemption questions. On remand, a divided Second Circuit panel again ruled for the bank, holding the state law preempted, while one judge dissented. The panel acknowledged its decision conflicts with a First Circuit ruling reaching the opposite result for a similar Rhode Island law.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked whether federal law governing national banks overrides, or preempts, state laws like New York's that require banks to pay a minimum interest rate on mortgage escrow accounts.

## Where it stands

The petition was distributed for the Court's September 28, 2026 conference; the docket shows no decision from that conference. On October 5, 2026, the Court invited the Solicitor General to file a brief giving the federal government's views, and that invitation remains pending.

## What each outcome would mean

Cantero and the other homeowners are asking the Court to take up the case. If the Court grants review, it would hear full arguments and decide whether New York's law applies to national banks, with no outcome assumed in advance. If the Court denies review, the Second Circuit's ruling for Bank of America stands, and the law is treated as preempted in that circuit, while a conflicting ruling favoring borrowers stands in the First Circuit. The Solicitor General's views, once filed, could inform whether the Court acts either way, but do not themselves decide the case.
