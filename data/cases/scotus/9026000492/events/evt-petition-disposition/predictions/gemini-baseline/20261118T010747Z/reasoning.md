The 2.68% `baseline` zero-amicus pool mixes contested BIO petitions with uncontested waiver petitions; double-waiver private civil petitions sit well below that pool mean.
- **Square doctrinal foreclosure under *Bristol-Myers Squibb* and *Ford Motor Co.*, with an illusory split:** Petitioners represent roughly 540 non-Illinois plaintiffs consolidated in Cook County who were neither prescribed Zantac in Illinois nor purchased, ingested, or suffered injury from it in Illinois, suing non-Illinois brand manufacturers Boehringer Ingelheim (Delaware/Connecticut) and Sanofi (Delaware/New Jersey). That posture repeats *Bristol-Myers Squibb Co. v. Superior Court*, 582 U.S. 255 (2017), where an 8–1 Court held that California lacked specific personal jurisdiction over nonresident Plavix plaintiffs injured outside California — and specifically held that contracting with an in-state company (McKesson) to distribute the drug nationally does not supply the missing affiliation between the forum and the nonresidents' claims. *Ford Motor Co. v. Montana Eighth Judicial District Court*, 592 U.S. 351 (2021), expressly reaffirmed *Bristol-Myers Squibb* and distinguished it because the *Ford* plaintiffs were forum residents injured in the forum State. Every post-*Ford* appellate decision petitioners cite on the "expansive" side of their asserted split (*Yamashita*, *Hood*, *LNS Enterprises*, *Doucet*, *Dilworth*) involved forum-resident plaintiffs or in-forum injuries (and two of them actually *affirmed* jurisdictional dismissals), while the Sixth Circuit's *Canaday* decision cited in the petition applied *Bristol-Myers Squibb* to bar nonresident FLSA opt-ins. No federal circuit or state high court exercises specific personal jurisdiction in the posture presented here.
- **Poor vehicle and nonprecedential intermediate state posture:** The decision below (`2025 IL App (1st) 241362-U`) is a unanimous, unpublished, nonprecedential summary order under Illinois Supreme Court Rule 23(b). Furthermore, the factual nexus is exceptionally attenuated: Boehringer divested its OTC Zantac rights to Sanofi at the end of 2016 (leaving roughly 96% of the ~540 plaintiffs, who alleged ingestion only from 2017 onward, without any temporal tie to Boehringer's pre-2017 contract with independent Illinois packager PCI), PCI packaged only part of Boehringer's tablets before Boehringer shipped them to Ohio and California distribution centers, no plaintiff traced an ingested tablet to Illinois, and Sanofi's alleged tie rested merely on nationwide wholesale transactions with Illinois-headquartered retailer Walgreens.
