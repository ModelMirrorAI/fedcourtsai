# Predicted Court Action and Procedural Trajectory

The Court will deny the petition for a writ of certiorari on the routine order list following the December 4, 2026 Conference, without calling for a response, without relisting, and without any separate writing.

- **Further distributions / relists (`relist-increment`: 0.035):** The docket currently records a single distribution (`distribution_count: 1`, distributed November 17, 2026 for the Conference of December 4, 2026) after both respondent groups (Boehringer Ingelheim and Sanofi) waived their right to respond and zero cert-stage amicus briefs were filed. Expect **zero further distributions**. A second distribution would require either an administrative reschedule before conference, a Justice calling for a response (which triggers redistribution once a brief in opposition is filed), or a conference hold/relist. None is likely: there is no pending Supreme Court Fourteenth Amendment personal-jurisdiction case to hold this petition behind, and the unpublished Illinois intermediate appellate order squarely applies *Bristol-Myers Squibb Co. v. Superior Court*, 582 U.S. 255 (2017), to nonresident plaintiffs injured outside the forum.
- **Call for the Views of the Solicitor General (`cvsg-increment`: 0.001):** No CVSG has issued, and **the Court will not issue a CVSG**. The petition raises a pure Fourteenth Amendment state-court personal-jurisdiction question in private state-law tort suits with no federal party, no federal statutory or regulatory scheme, no preemption question presented, and no brief in opposition on file.
- **Question presented if granted (context-only):** Only one question is presented. If the Court were unexpectedly to call for a response and grant certiorari, it would narrow the question presented to the specific third-party supply-chain facts preserved in the record — namely, whether a nonresident pharmaceutical manufacturer's use of an in-state contract packager or sales to an in-state national retail chain satisfies the "arise out of or relate to" requirement for claims brought by nonresident plaintiffs who neither purchased nor ingested the product, nor suffered injury, in the forum State. If reached on the merits, a large majority would affirm dismissal on the ground that *Ford Motor Co. v. Montana Eighth Judicial District Court*, 592 U.S. 351 (2021), did not displace *Bristol-Myers Squibb*'s requirement of a forum-claim affiliation where neither the plaintiff nor the injury has any connection to the forum State.
- **Summary disposition vs. plenary review conditional on grant (`summary-disposition-route`: 0.22):** Conditional on the remote event that the Court grants review, **plenary review after calling for a response is substantially likelier than summary disposition in the cert order**. Summary reversal is virtually foreclosed because the Illinois Appellate Court applied rather than defied *Bristol-Myers Squibb* and *Ford Motor Co.*, and no pending Supreme Court merits case on Fourteenth Amendment corporate specific jurisdiction provides a plausible grant-vacate-and-remand (GVR) anchor (save for the slight possibility of a later-granted OT2026 personal-jurisdiction vehicle or procedural vacatur).
- **Separate writings on denial (`dissent-from-denial`: 0.004; `order-writers`):** Conditional on denial, **expect an unsigned one-line denial order with no noted dissent or statement respecting the denial**. While Justices Thomas and Gorsuch have previously written separately in *Ford Motor Co.* to question aspects of modern corporate personal-jurisdiction doctrine and Justice Sotomayor dissented alone in *Bristol-Myers Squibb*, an unpublished state intermediate appellate order arriving on dual respondent waivers with severe factual attenuation (third-party packaging of a subset of tablets prior to 2017, where nearly all plaintiffs ingested the product later) is an unappealing vehicle for a separate statement, and a Justice inclined to write would ordinarily call for a response first.
