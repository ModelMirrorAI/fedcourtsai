# Forecast: City of Bossier City v. Hershey, No. 25-1323

**State forecast from.** The docket shows two distributions: for the September 28, 2026 long conference and, after a relist, for the October 9, 2026 conference. No CVSG. Three amicus briefs support the petition (Louisiana, Texas and Mississippi; the International Municipal Lawyers Association; the National Association of Police Organizations). The companion petition, Hershey v. City of Bossier City, No. 25-1389 (the qualified-immunity half of the same Fifth Circuit judgment), was distributed and relisted on exactly the same two dates and carries roughly fifteen amicus briefs. The two are being considered as a pair.

**Disposition.** The likelier single outcome is denial, but a grant is a live possibility: P(any grant) = 0.35. The paths to a grant, in order of weight: (a) both petitions granted and consolidated, so the Court can decide the Monell question and the Hope v. Pelzer qualified-immunity question off one record, as Judge Oldham's en banc dissent framed them; (b) the City's petition granted alone, on the strength of the claimed 3-1 split with the Second, Sixth and Eighth Circuits; (c) the companion granted alone with this petition held, resolving later as a GVR or denial. The main path to denial is that the Court reads the panel majority the way the brief in opposition does: Judges Dennis and Ho treated the right as clearly established and the complaint as alleging no First Amendment training at all, so the two questions presented are not cleanly posed by this interlocutory, pleadings-stage record.

**Relists.** P(at least one more distribution beyond the two shown) = 0.45. The pair is heavily briefed on two intertwined issues, and the Court now routinely relists two or three times before either granting a paired set or denying with a writing. Expect a decision on the petition by the end of October 2026 either way; a third or fourth relist would favor a dissent from denial over a grant.

**CVSG.** Unlikely: 0.07. Section 1983 municipal liability does not implicate the federal government's own exposure, and no federal party or program is in the case. A CVSG would come only if the Court wants the Solicitor General's view on how single-incident liability interacts with qualified immunity across both petitions.

**Which question, if granted.** The Court would take the City's first question essentially as written (whether single-incident Monell liability can rest on a right that was not clearly established) and would likely fold the second question (the "complete lack of training" pleading standard) into it rather than granting it separately. If both petitions are granted, the Monell question would be argued alongside Hershey's question whether Hope v. Pelzer's obviousness principle applies to First Amendment claims. If granted, the likely ground would be that deliberate indifference requires notice of a clear constitutional duty, reversing the Fifth Circuit on Monell; the companion question could go the other way.

**Summary disposition.** Conditional on a grant, P(disposed of in the cert order) = 0.08. There is no intervening decision to GVR in light of, and a 10-7 en banc vote on a pleading-stage Monell question is not summary-reversal material. A GVR becomes plausible only in path (c), after a merits ruling in the companion case.

**Dissent from denial.** Conditional on denial, P(some Justice writes) = 0.40. A relisted pair with state amici and a seven-judge en banc dissent is the shape that draws a Thomas or Alito dissent from denial on Monell, or a short statement respecting denial; the more likely writings on the pair as a whole would attach to the companion's qualified-immunity denial rather than to this docket.
