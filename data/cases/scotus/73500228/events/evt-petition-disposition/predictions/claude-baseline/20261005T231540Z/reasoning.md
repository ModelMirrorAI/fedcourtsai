# Rationale for the numbers

**P(grant) = 0.35.**

**Anchor.** `record/context.json` freezes band `elevated` under `sal-v4` for docket Term 2025 with `distribution_count: 2`. The statpack's "Segment base rate by salience band (sal-v4)" table renders Terms 2017-2026; pooling the bracketed `reached` figure for `elevated` over the eight Terms strictly before 2025 (2017-2024) gives 484/2810 = 17.2%. That is the yardstick the evaluator scores against, and my starting point. For shape only, the relist-count cut (paid scored segment) pooled over the terminal buckets 1, 2 and 3+ gives a grant-family rate of about 20% for a petition that has reached one relist, with 28% going on to a second; the pack warns that a reschedule also adds a distribution entry, so both figures are diluted by petitions that were never actually considered twice. This petition's second distribution is a true relist: it was on the September 28 long-conference list and then redistributed for October 9.

**Adjustments up (to roughly double the anchor).**
- The relist is from the long conference, and the companion petition No. 25-1389 was distributed and relisted on the identical dates (confirmed on supremecourt.gov; see `retrieval.md`). The Court is weighing the pair, which is how a consolidated grant looks from outside. The companion carries roughly fifteen amicus briefs from a cross-ideological set (Manhattan Institute, Cato, religious-liberty groups, ACLU-affiliated organizations per the SCOTUSblog case page), which raises the chance that something in this case is granted.
- Petition quality: Jeffrey Wall (Gibson Dunn) for the City, a 10-7 en banc denial with a seven-judge Oldham dissent, three amicus briefs including three States, and a split pleaded against named opinions by Chief Judges Sutton (Arrington-Bey, Hagans) and Colloton (Szabla). The petition (read in full from `record/documents/petition.txt`) presents QP1 as a clean tension: the same panel granted qualified immunity because the right was not clearly established yet allowed single-incident Monell liability.
- Importance: single-incident Monell liability has had no Supreme Court guidance since Connick (2011); the question recurs in every circuit.

**Adjustments down (pulling back toward the anchor).**
- Vehicle. The brief in opposition (Paul Clement, read in full from `record/documents/brief-in-opposition.txt`) argues the panel majority found the right clearly established and the complaint to allege "no training whatsoever," so neither QP is actually presented; it recasts the only certworthy issue as the Fifth Circuit's cabining of Hope v. Pelzer, which is Hershey's petition. A clerk reading Judge Dennis's and Judge Ho's opinions will find support for that reading. The per curiam with three separate opinions and shifting 2-1 majorities is a messy record.
- Posture. The judgment is interlocutory: reversal of a Rule 12(b)(6) dismissal with remand for discovery. The Court can take it but usually prefers a final judgment on Monell questions (Canton, Brown, Connick all came after trial).
- The facts cut against the City with the Justices most likely to want to narrow Monell: the respondent is a religious leafleter removed from a sidewalk while a commercial leafleter stayed, represented by First Liberty. Conservative Justices may prefer to grant the companion rather than, or in addition to, this one.
- The BIO's second-question answer (the panel found a complete failure to train) makes QP2 look factbound.

**Net.** Scenario weights I actually hold: both petitions granted ~0.25; the City's granted alone ~0.07; the companion granted alone with this one held, later resolving as a GVR ~0.03 of grant-family mass; denial (with or without a writing) ~0.45; the rest is further relists that resolve into those buckets. Sum of grant-family mass ~0.35. `predicted_disposition` is `denied` because that remains the single most likely resolution.

**Other claims.** `relist-increment` 0.45: the pair is dense and the current Court relists repeatedly; 28% is the diluted corpus continuation figure for terminal bucket counting, and true relists on a paired set run higher. `cvsg-increment` 0.07: no federal interest; slight bump because a CVSG out of a long-conference relist does happen on section 1983 questions. `summary-disposition-route` 0.08: no intervening decision; the only route is a GVR after a companion merits ruling. `dissent-from-denial` 0.40: relisted denials with state amici often draw a writing, but the pair's likelier writings attach to the companion's qualified-immunity denial.

**Big-case score 0.55.** Stakes, not odds: the outer bound of single-incident Monell liability affects every municipality, and the paired Hope question would be a significant qualified-immunity decision. Not a blockbuster.

**Where to discount me.** I cannot see conference dynamics; the relist could be for a dissent from denial as easily as for a grant. My reading of the companion's amicus roster comes from a WebFetch summary of the SCOTUSblog page, not the briefs. The corpus `query` surface gave no topical priors (the citation filter is sparse by design and the era filter returns recency-ranked rows), so the comparison class is the statpack band table, not retrieved Monell petitions. I hold no knowledge of this petition's outcome; both conferences post-date my training data.
