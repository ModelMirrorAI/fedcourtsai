The petition has been distributed twice (relisted once) following the Long Conference. Expect perhaps one or two further relists as the Court considers this petition alongside the respondent's cross-petition (No. 25-1389) regarding qualified immunity. A CVSG is highly unlikely given the lack of federal interest in this local police liability matter.

The Court is likely to deny this petition. The Fifth Circuit's decision allowed the respondent's Monell failure-to-train claim to proceed past the pleadings stage. Although the petitioner City raises questions about single-incident liability under *Connick v. Thompson*, the panel found that the respondent adequately alleged a complete failure to train on First Amendment rights. The core dispute driving this litigation is the Fifth Circuit's restrictive interpretation of *Hope v. Pelzer* regarding qualified immunity, which is the subject of the cross-petition. If the Court grants plenary review or summarily reverses in the cross-petition (finding the right was clearly established), the City's Monell arguments in this petition become even weaker. Consequently, a denial of this petition is the most probable outcome.

If granted, a summary disposition (such as a GVR in light of a decision in the cross-petition) is more likely than plenary review on the Monell pleading standards. Should the Court deny review, a dissent from denial is possible but unlikely, as the conservative justices may prefer a cleaner vehicle to narrow Monell liability.
