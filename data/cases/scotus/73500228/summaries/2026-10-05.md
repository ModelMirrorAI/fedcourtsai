## In brief

A city asks the Court to review a ruling that let a leafletter's lawsuit over his removal from a public arena's grounds proceed against the city.

## What happened

Richard Hershey was distributing religious leaflets on a sidewalk outside a Bossier City, Louisiana arena during a concert when police and security officers told him to leave and threatened arrest, while allowing another leafletter distributing commercial material to stay. Hershey sued the officers and the city, claiming the officers violated his First Amendment rights and that the city failed to train officers on those rights. A magistrate judge recommended dismissing all claims; the district court agreed. A divided Fifth Circuit panel affirmed dismissal of the claims against the individual officers on qualified immunity grounds but revived the claim against the city. The full Fifth Circuit denied rehearing by a 10-7 vote.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked whether a lawsuit accusing a city of inadequate training can go forward without claiming the officers violated a right that was already clearly established in the law, and separately, whether such a claim can proceed by alleging the city failed to train officers on the narrow situation at hand, rather than a complete failure to train on the constitutional right generally.

## Where it stands

The petition has been distributed for two conferences, most recently October 9, 2026, after full briefing including an opposition brief, several amicus briefs, and a reply; the docket does not yet show a decision on whether the Court will hear the case.

## What each outcome would mean

Bossier City asks the Court to take up the case and reverse the ruling that revived Hershey's claim against it. If the Court grants review and later rules for the city, Hershey's claim against the city would be narrowed or dismissed; if it rules for Hershey, the claim would proceed as the Fifth Circuit allowed. If the Court denies review, the Fifth Circuit's decision allowing Hershey's claim against the city to proceed stands, and the case continues in the lower courts. A related petition by Hershey himself, concerning the officers' qualified immunity, is pending separately.
