## In brief

Bossier City asks the Court to limit when cities can be sued for inadequate police training, after a man's lawsuit over being stopped from leafleting outside an arena.

## What happened

Richard Hershey distributed free religious vegetarian leaflets on a public sidewalk outside a Bossier City arena during a concert. Police officers and private security guards, citing the area as private property, told him to leave and threatened arrest, while allowing another leafleteer distributing commercial material to continue. Hershey sued the city and individual officers. The district court dismissed all claims. A divided Fifth Circuit panel affirmed dismissal of claims against the officers on qualified immunity grounds but revived Hershey's claim against the city for failing to train officers on First Amendment rights. The full Fifth Circuit denied rehearing by a 10-7 vote.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked whether a person suing a city over inadequate training of its employees must show that the underlying conduct violated a right that was already clearly established in the law, and whether that person must allege the city provided no relevant training at all, rather than training that fell short only as applied to the specific situation involved.

## Where it stands

The city's petition has been distributed for conference twice, most recently for the October 9, 2026 conference, and the docket shows no action on it since. Hershey has filed his own petition in a related case (25-1389) asking the Court to review the qualified-immunity ruling against the officers.

## What each outcome would mean

Bossier City, which lost on the training-liability question below, is asking the Court to step in. If the Court grants review, it would hear the case and decide the training-liability questions itself, deciding nothing in advance about who wins. If it denies review, the Fifth Circuit's decision stands, leaving Hershey's claim against the city alive to proceed in the lower courts, while the officers remain shielded by qualified immunity. Hershey's brief urges denial of the city's petition while separately asking the Court to take up his own petition on qualified immunity instead.
