## In brief

Bossier City asks the Supreme Court to overturn a ruling letting a pamphleteer's lawsuit over his removal from a public arena's grounds proceed against the city.

## What happened

Richard Hershey distributed religious vegetarian leaflets on a sidewalk outside a Bossier City arena during a concert. Police officers and private security guards told him to stop and leave, threatening arrest, while allowing another person distributing commercial leaflets to remain. Hershey sued the city and the individual officers and guards under a federal civil-rights law. The district court dismissed all his claims, ruling the officers had qualified immunity (protection from damages suits unless they violated clearly established rights), the security guards were not government actors, and the city could not be held liable. A divided Fifth Circuit panel affirmed dismissal of the claims against the officers and guards but revived Hershey's claim against the city for failing to train its employees on the First Amendment. The full appeals court declined to rehear the case by a 10-7 vote.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked whether a city can be held responsible for a single incident of employee misconduct, based on a failure-to-train theory, even if the underlying constitutional violation was not clearly established at the time, and even if the city's alleged training gap was narrow and specific to the facts of the case, rather than a complete absence of training on the constitutional right generally.

## Where it stands

The petition has been fully briefed, including a brief in opposition and a reply, and has been distributed for three consecutive conferences, most recently October 16, 2026. The docket does not show any decision yet on whether the Court will hear the case.

## What each outcome would mean

Bossier City, which lost on this issue below, is asking the Court to take up the case. If the Court grants review and later rules for the city, Hershey's claim against the city would be cut off, leaving only the already-dismissed claims against individual officers and guards, which Hershey is separately asking the Court to review. If the Court rules for Hershey, the city's liability claim would proceed in the lower courts. If the Court denies review, the Fifth Circuit's decision allowing Hershey's claim against the city to proceed stands, and the case returns to the district court for further proceedings on that claim.
