## In brief

Bossier City asks the Court to review a ruling that let a pamphleteer's lawsuit over his removal from a concert venue proceed against the city.

## What happened

Richard Hershey says he was distributing religious pamphlets on a sidewalk outside a Bossier City arena during a concert when police officers and private security guards, citing the venue's private-event status, ordered him to leave under threat of arrest, while allowing another leafleteer distributing commercial material to continue. Hershey sued the city and individual officers. The district court dismissed all claims, and a divided Fifth Circuit panel affirmed dismissal of the claims against the individual officers on qualified-immunity grounds but revived Hershey's claim that the city had failed to train its officers on First Amendment rights. The full Fifth Circuit denied rehearing by a 10-7 vote.

## What the Court is being asked

The case concerns when a city can be sued over a single incident for not training its employees on constitutional rights. The city asks whether such a claim requires showing that the officers' conduct violated a clearly established right, and whether it requires showing a complete absence of training on the constitutional right generally, rather than training tailored to the specific situation that arose.

## Where it stands

The petition has been fully briefed, including a brief in opposition and a reply, and has been distributed for five consecutive conferences, most recently November 13, 2026. The docket shows no action yet on whether the Court will hear the case.

## What each outcome would mean

Bossier City, which lost on the municipal-liability claim below, is asking the Court to take up and reverse that ruling. If the Court grants review and later rules for the city, Hershey's claim against the city would likely be dismissed. If it rules for Hershey, the city's potential liability for failing to train its officers would go forward in the lower courts. If the Court denies review, the Fifth Circuit's decision allowing Hershey's claim against the city to proceed stands, and the case returns to the lower courts for further proceedings on that claim.
