## In brief

The government asked the Supreme Court whether a federal agency, rather than a court, may fine a farm over its foreign-worker visa program.

## What happened

Sun Valley Orchards, a New Jersey farm, hired foreign and domestic workers through the H-2A agricultural visa program. The Department of Labor found that the farm violated program rules on housing, meals, transportation and work guarantees, and an agency judge imposed over $500,000 in penalties and back wages. Sun Valley sued, arguing this agency process violated its right to have the case heard by a court under Article III. The district court sided with the Department. The Third Circuit reversed, ruling that the claims involved "private rights"—resembling a breach-of-contract suit seeking money—that the Constitution reserves for courts, not agency judges. The Department of Labor asked the Supreme Court to review that ruling.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked whether the Constitution lets the Department of Labor itself decide, through its own in-house judges, whether a farm must pay penalties and back wages for violating the terms of employing foreign agricultural workers, or whether such cases must instead go to a federal court. A related question is whether the underlying immigration law actually gives the Department authority to resolve these cases internally at all.

## Where it stands

The Court granted review and the case was argued on November 10, 2026. It now awaits decision.

## What each outcome would mean

The Department of Labor, which lost in the lower court, is asking the Court to reverse and uphold its authority to decide these cases itself. If the Court affirms, the Third Circuit's ruling stands: Sun Valley's win remains in place, and the Department could no longer use its in-house judges to impose penalties and back wages for H-2A violations in that circuit, instead needing to sue in federal court. If the Court reverses, the Department's internal enforcement process would be upheld, and Sun Valley's challenge to the penalties against it would fail. The Court could also vacate and send the case back for further proceedings without deciding who is right.
