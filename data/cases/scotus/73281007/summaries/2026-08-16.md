## What happened

Sun Valley Orchards, a family farm in New Jersey, hired foreign workers through the H-2A visa program along with domestic workers doing similar jobs. The Department of Labor investigated and found the farm had violated the terms of its job order contracts, involving things like kitchen access, transportation, housing conditions, and early termination of some workers. DOL's own in-house judges assessed over half a million dollars in penalties and back wages. Sun Valley challenged the process in federal court, arguing it was unconstitutional for DOL to decide the case itself rather than a real court. A district court sided with DOL, but the Third Circuit Court of Appeals reversed, ruling that this kind of dispute must be decided by a court, not the agency itself.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked whether the Constitution required this dispute to be decided by a court instead of the Department of Labor's own judges, given that the government was seeking penalties and back pay for alleged violations of workers' employment contracts. The Court is also asked to interpret a specific immigration statute and decide whether it actually gives the Department of Labor the power to decide these cases itself, as opposed to only allowing the agency to pursue such penalties in court.

## Where it stands

The Court granted cert limited to these two questions and the case has been fully briefed, with both sides and numerous outside groups filing briefs. Argument is scheduled for November 10, 2026.
