## In brief

The Department of Labor asks the Supreme Court whether it may fine farm employers for H-2A worker-contract violations without using federal courts.

## What happened

Sun Valley Orchards, a New Jersey farm, hired foreign and domestic workers under the H-2A agricultural visa program in 2015. After an investigation, the Department of Labor's own administrative judges found the farm had violated workers' job-order terms covering kitchen access, pay deductions, early termination, transportation, and housing, and ordered it to pay over $500,000 in penalties and back wages. Sun Valley challenged that process in federal court, arguing the agency's in-house adjudication violated the Constitution. The Third Circuit agreed, ruling that the claims resembled a breach-of-contract suit that must be heard by a court rather than decided by the agency itself.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked whether the Constitution's separation of powers requires these penalty cases to be decided by a federal court rather than by the Labor Department's own administrative judges, and separately, whether the underlying immigration law actually gives the Department authority to decide such cases itself in the first place.

## Where it stands

The Court granted review limited to these two questions, and the case has been fully briefed and argued; it was set for oral argument on November 10, 2026, and now awaits decision.

## What each outcome would mean

The Department of Labor, which sought the Court's review, asks the Court to uphold its authority to adjudicate these cases internally. If the Court rules for the Department, agencies could continue deciding H-2A penalty cases themselves, and Sun Valley's liability could be reinstated. If the Court rules for Sun Valley, such cases would have to go through federal courts instead, leaving the Third Circuit's ruling against the Department in place. The Court could also send the case back for further proceedings without resolving the question for either side.
