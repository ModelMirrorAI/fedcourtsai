## In brief

The Court will decide whether the Labor Department may itself decide, rather than send to federal court, penalty cases against farms in a foreign farmworker visa program.

## What happened

Sun Valley Orchards, a New Jersey farm, employed foreign workers under the H-2A agricultural visa program alongside domestic workers. The Labor Department's own judges found the farm violated workers' job contracts on issues like kitchen access, transportation, and early terminations, and imposed over $500,000 in penalties and back wages. Sun Valley challenged the process, arguing the Constitution required a real federal court, not the agency's in-house judges, to decide the case. The Third Circuit agreed, ruling that these claims resembled a breach-of-contract lawsuit and that the immigration-related exception allowing agency adjudication did not extend this far.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked whether the Constitution's guarantee of independent courts prevents the Labor Department from using its own in-house judges, rather than federal courts, to decide penalty and back-pay claims against employers for violating H-2A worker contract terms. A second question asks whether the federal H-2A statute even gives the agency authority to hold such in-house proceedings in the first place.

## Where it stands

The Court granted the petition limited to these two questions, briefing is complete, and the case has been set for argument on November 10, 2026.

## What each outcome would mean

The Department of Labor is asking the Court to reverse the lower court's ruling. If the Court affirms, Sun Valley's win stands, and the agency could not use its in-house judges to impose these penalties, instead having to bring such cases in federal court. If the Court reverses, the agency's authority to adjudicate these claims in-house would be upheld, and Sun Valley's liability could be reinstated. If the Court vacates, it would send the case back without resolving which side is right, leaving further proceedings to the lower courts.
