**Case Specifics:**
The Department of Labor (petitioner) is appealing a Third Circuit decision that struck down a federal statute/agency scheme as unconstitutional under Article III, heavily relying on *SEC v. Jarkesy*. The respondent, Sun Valley Orchards, argues that assessing monetary penalties against a domestic farm for labor violations is a common-law penalty requiring an Article III court.

Although the conservative majority recently restricted agency adjudication in *Jarkesy*, that opinion expressly preserved exceptions for public rights—including immigration and government benefits. The government's petition emphasizes this, arguing that the H-2A program provides an immigration benefit, and thus conditions on that benefit are public rights.

I adjust the probability to 0.70 (very close to the baseline) because while the current Court is skeptical of administrative state overreach, the liberals (Sotomayor, Kagan, Jackson) will reliably vote to uphold the agency, and the government only needs two conservative institutionalists (like Roberts, Kavanaugh, or Barrett) to agree that this falls within the recognized immigration/benefits exception. The Court frequently grants certiorari when a lower court strikes down a federal statute to reverse and correct overly broad applications of its own recent precedents.
