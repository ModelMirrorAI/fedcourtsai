I forecast the Court will reverse the judgment of the Third Circuit, holding that the Department of Labor's assessment of monetary remedies for H-2A labor violations does not violate Article III. 

**Judgment and Vote Lineup**
The Court will reverse the Third Circuit in a 6-3 decision. Chief Justice Roberts and Justices Sotomayor, Kagan, Kavanaugh, Barrett, and Jackson will form the majority. Justices Thomas, Alito, and Gorsuch will dissent.

**Doctrinal Ground**
The majority will rest its holding on the public-rights doctrine, specifically the historical exceptions for immigration and government-created benefits/privileges. The Court will conclude that because the H-2A visa program is an immigration benefit Congress created, the terms and conditions attached to that privilege—including monetary remedies for violating labor conditions—fall within the public-rights exception recognized in *SEC v. Jarkesy*. Consequently, Congress may constitutionally assign the initial adjudication of these disputes to an executive agency (the Department of Labor) without requiring an Article III court or a jury trial.

**Breadth of the Ground**
The Court will adopt a relatively broad rule that conditions attached to immigration privileges and government benefits generally fall outside Article III and Seventh Amendment requirements, rather than narrowly confining its holding to the specific administrative or statutory structure of the H-2A program alone.

**Separate Writings**
Chief Justice Roberts is likely to author the majority opinion, carefully distinguishing *Jarkesy* by emphasizing its own carved-out exceptions for immigration and public rights. Justice Thomas will likely author a dissent, joined by Justices Alito and Gorsuch, arguing that penalizing a domestic employer for labor violations resembles a traditional common-law claim that cannot be shielded from Article III review merely because the workers were admitted on visas.
