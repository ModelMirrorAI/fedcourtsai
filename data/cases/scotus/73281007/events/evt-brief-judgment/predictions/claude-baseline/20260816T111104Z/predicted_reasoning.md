# Forecast: Department of Labor v. Sun Valley Orchards, LLC (No. 25-966), merits judgment

**Judgment class: `affirmed`.** The Court will leave the Third Circuit's judgment
standing — Sun Valley wins. P(judgment below disturbed) = 0.45, so this is a
close call, but affirmance is the modal outcome. The government must win both
granted questions to obtain reversal — that §1188(g)(2) authorizes DOL's
in-house adjudication *and* that Article III permits it — while Sun Valley
needs only one, and both questions independently give this Court a path it has
recently shown appetite for.

**Which question the Court reaches.** The Court will decide QP1 (Article III)
and rest the judgment on it, treating the statutory question either as
resolved briefly in the government's favor (the 1986 delegation plus forty
years of implementing regulations) or as unnecessary to decide. The
alternative path — affirmance on statutory-avoidance grounds under QP2,
reading "imposing appropriate penalties" as saying nothing about *where*
liability is conclusively adjudicated (as FCC v. AT&T, 608 U.S. ___ (2026),
read "impose" in 47 U.S.C. §503) — is the likeliest route by which a broader
coalition forms, and I give it meaningful weight; it still ends in affirmance.

**Vote lineup: 5-4.** Majority to affirm: Thomas, Alito, Gorsuch, Barrett,
Kavanaugh. Dissent: Roberts, Sotomayor, Kagan, Jackson. The three Jarkesy
dissenters will vote to uphold DOL's adjudication. Roberts is the majority's
most gettable member for the government: he wrote both Jarkesy and FCC v.
AT&T, and his Jarkesy opinion itself cited Oceanic Steam's immigration
category approvingly — if the government wins, it wins by taking Roberts,
Kavanaugh, and one more through that door, 6-3 or 7-2 the other way. I expect
Gorsuch to write for the Court if Sun Valley prevails (he has been the Court's
most committed voice on Article III adjudication limits since Axon and his
Jarkesy concurrence), with Sotomayor writing the principal dissent. Expect at
least one separate concurrence (Thomas, on public-rights first principles) —
he dissented alone even from FCC v. AT&T's saving construction.

**Ground and breadth** (the two semantic claims, in prose): the majority will
hold DOL's claims were in substance common-law contract claims seeking legal
remedies — civil penalties, which Jarkesy called all-but-dispositive, plus
back wages functioning as contract damages — so the private-rights presumption
controls, and Oceanic Steam's immigration line will be confined to exactions
tied to admission and exclusion rather than extended to domestic
employment-law obligations. The opinion will be written narrowly: conclusive
agency adjudication of contract-like monetary remedies is out, admission,
exclusion, and status adjudication expressly untouched, no categorical rule
about penalty adjudication in benefit programs.

**Procedural exits.** A DIG is unlikely (~2%): Sun Valley has ceased farming
operations and its brief spends a footnote defending liveness (DOL treats the
award as a collectible debt), so mootness noise exists, but the Court granted
with that footnote in view. An equally divided affirmance requires a recusal
nobody has noted. If the government wins QP1 outright, expect a clean
reversal and remand rather than a vacatur — the statutory question was granted
and will be resolved, not left open.

**Timing.** Argument is set for November 10, 2026. A 5-4 or 6-3 decision in an
argued November case typically lands late in the Term: I expect the decision
between March and June 2027, more likely May–June given the constitutional
stakes and probable separate writings. (Timing resolves against nothing; it is
stated to discipline the rest.)
