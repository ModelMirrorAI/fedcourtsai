# Forecast

The Court will affirm the Third Circuit, most likely by a 6–3 vote. The judgment below will therefore remain undisturbed. The controlling opinion will likely begin with the second granted question and hold that 8 U.S.C. § 1188(g)(2) does not clearly give the Department of Labor power to conclusively adjudicate and enter binding monetary awards against H-2A employers.

The majority will distinguish authority to “impose” a penalty from authority to make an agency assessment itself the final obligation to pay. It will emphasize that the provision neither specifies agency adjudication nor supplies a de novo Article III enforcement action, while separately directing the Secretary to seek equitable relief. Reading that silence to authorize final agency judgments would press against the serious Article III problem identified in *Jarkesy* and illuminated by *FCC v. AT&T*. The Court will avoid deciding whether all enforcement of H-2A program conditions concerns private rights.

Chief Justice Roberts and Justices Thomas, Alito, Gorsuch, Kavanaugh, and Barrett will vote to affirm; Justices Sotomayor, Kagan, and Jackson will dissent. A separate writing by Justice Gorsuch, likely joined by Justice Thomas, will argue that DOL adjudicated private rights because it imposed penalties and back wages on contract-like employment claims, and that the historical immigration exception does not extend from admission and border control to binding domestic employment-liability judgments. The dissent will read § 1188(g)(2) to authorize the agency process and characterize compliance with H-2A employment terms as a condition of a government-conferred immigration benefit.

The majority's rule will be narrow. It will not unsettle executive adjudication of admission, exclusion, removal, or other immigration-status questions, and it will leave differently worded penalty statutes for another case. A dismissal as improvidently granted or an equally divided affirmance is unlikely; the two expressly granted questions and the complete lower-court judgment provide a direct route to decision.
