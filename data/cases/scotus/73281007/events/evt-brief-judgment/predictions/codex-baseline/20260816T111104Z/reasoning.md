# Rationale

I assign a 0.40 probability that the Court disturbs the Third Circuit's judgment and predict an affirmance. In the committed ten-Term window preceding OT2025, the published rows for OT2017–OT2024 contribute 359 disturbed judgments among 515 parsed judgments, or 69.7%. The pool covers 515 of 557 granted cases (92.5%) and reports 57 pool-guard exclusions. That is a strong reversal prior, but it covers the parsed slice and the most recent Terms remain affected by pendency and uneven coverage.

The case-specific evidence warrants a large downward adjustment. A unanimous Third Circuit panel held that DOL's proceeding resembled a common-law contract action and produced traditionally legal relief: $211,800 in civil penalties and $344,945.80 in back wages. *Jarkesy* makes the nature of the claim and remedy central, and the same six-Justice conservative alignment is likely to view a binding award made by the enforcing agency skeptically. The Court also added a statutory question that the government's petition had not presented. That points toward constitutional avoidance, especially after the pre-event decision in *FCC v. AT&T*, where eight Justices sustained an agency assessment only because it did not itself create an obligation to pay and the government had to prove liability in a de novo jury trial. DOL's award instead becomes final after internal review and receives deferential judicial review.

The government's best argument keeps the probability of disturbance substantial. H-2A participation is a government-conferred privilege tied to the admission of foreign workers, and *Oceanic Steam* permits executive assessment of monetary penalties enforcing conditions on bringing aliens into the country. Section 1188(g)(2) also expressly authorizes the Secretary to take necessary actions, “including imposing appropriate penalties.” A majority could treat the employment conditions as public rights, reverse the Third Circuit, and remand. The Solicitor General's success in obtaining review of a decision invalidating federal enforcement authority also cuts against overconfidence in affirmance.

The most likely lineup is the six conservative Justices voting to affirm and the three liberal Justices dissenting. I expect the controlling rationale to be statutory and narrow, with Thomas and Gorsuch pressing the broader Article III/private-rights ground in a concurrence. That rationale forecast is less certain than the judgment forecast because the Justices could instead form a six-Justice majority directly on Article III.
