The Third Circuit (Hardiman, joined by Porter and Smith) reversed the district court and directed judgment for Sun Valley, holding under *SEC v. Jarkesy* that DOL could not adjudicate H-2A civil penalties and back wages in-house. It did not reach the statutory-authority argument. The Supreme Court's grant order added that statutory question itself (question 2), which I read as a sign that at least some Justices wanted an alternative route to ruling against the agency.

## Why far below the base rate

The argument is the evidence, and it read as a majority for the respondent. A question is not a vote; I treat each of these as a lean, with the confidence noted.

- **Roberts** (strong lean to affirm): told the government three times that *Oceanic Steam* was about a condition of entry and that the government was "leveraging" entry into workplace regulation; said the comprehensiveness of the scheme hurt rather than helped; gave the respondent an open invitation at the end to describe the harm to individual rights. He wrote *Jarkesy*.
- **Gorsuch** (very strong): import/commerce hypothetical to show the theory has no stopping point, got the concession that the immigration privilege "always" remains conditional, pressed *Wong Wing* on confiscating property, and walked the respondent through a friendly summary. He also raised the statutory route (the *FCC v. AT&T* reading of "impose").
- **Thomas** (moderate): suggested debarment is the natural regulatory tool and that immigration then cannot carry penalties; one neutral question to the respondent.
- **Kavanaugh** (moderate): quoted *Tull*/*Jarkesy* that civil penalties are enforceable only in courts of law, minimized the disruption (only the standard of review changes), and asked the respondent for a limiting principle that would save §1324a penalties — which reads as drafting help. His clarity concern cuts slightly the other way.
- **Barrett** (moderate): offered a narrowing reading of *Oceanic Steam* (exaction tied to clearance papers), objected to the government's "mix-and-match" of exceptions, and pressed alien-fine hypotheticals. She also pushed the respondent hard on how broad his rule is, ending by noting his fallback resembles the government's germaneness test — which could mean a narrow affirmance, or doubt.
- **Alito** (genuinely uncertain): asked the government why it conceded the contract analogy at all and listed reasons it is a stretch; called the respondent's proposed opinion a "hodgepodge." But he also asked how the respondent wins without the contract analogy. I put him with the majority at little better than even, on the strength of his *Jarkesy* vote.
- **Kagan, Sotomayor, Jackson** (strong lean to reverse): each argued the government's case to the respondent — this is an admissions condition, the liability source is the regulations, the respondent's test is novel.

`argument-features.json` (210 of 211 Justice turns attributed, all 3 segments attributed) agrees on where the pressure fell: Gorsuch 33 turns at the government against 8 at the respondent, Roberts 14 against 10; Kagan 4 against 15, Jackson 10 against 21, Sotomayor 15 against 20. Counts are not votes, but they match my reading.

So I count five to six votes to affirm. The government's own posture reinforces it: asked for the narrowest way to lose, counsel named the statutory path and de novo court review, and the rebuttal was largely a plea for clarity if it lost.

## Where the 0.26 comes from

- Outright reversal needs two of Roberts/Thomas/Gorsuch/Kavanaugh/Barrett/Alito to join the three clear votes. Alito is plausible; a second is hard to find. Roughly 0.15.
- A mixed judgment (for example penalties barred but back wages allowed, or the reverse) or a vacatur for a de novo-review remedy counts as disturbed. Roughly 0.08–0.10. The Third Circuit's instruction to enter judgment for Sun Valley is the kind of mandate a narrower holding might trim.
- A DIG or equal division is negligible with nine sitting and a clean vehicle.

A statutory affirmance (question 2) still leaves the judgment standing, so it sits in the 0.74.

## Where to discount me

I may be over-reading hostile questioning of the government as votes; Barrett and Kavanaugh both showed discomfort with where the respondent's line falls, and a Court that cannot agree on a line might reverse narrowly. The vote on Alito is close to a coin flip. My semantic claims bet on an Article III holding; a holding on the statute alone is perhaps a 30% outcome given affirmance, since the statute drew little attention at argument beyond Gorsuch.

For votes, the statpack's per-Justice section (Terms before OT2025, e.g. OT2024: Roberts with the judgment 54 of 56, Kavanaugh 52 of 56, Jackson 40 of 56) is a general reference only; my lineup comes from the transcript and the *Jarkesy* alignment (6–3 on the same ideological line).
