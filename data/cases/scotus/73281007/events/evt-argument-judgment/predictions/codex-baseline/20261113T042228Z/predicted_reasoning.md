# Forecast of the Court's decision

## Judgment and lineup

The Court will **affirm** the Third Circuit's judgment, preserving Sun Valley's right to an Article III adjudication of the disputed monetary liability. The predicted vote is **5–4**: Roberts, Thomas, Gorsuch, Kavanaugh, and Barrett in the majority; Alito, Sotomayor, Kagan, and Jackson dissenting. This does not decide that Sun Valley complied with the employment rules or cannot owe money. It decides which adjudicatory process can conclusively establish that liability.

Roberts is the point prediction for the opinion of the Court. Barrett and Gorsuch are substantial alternative authors. Roberts will frame the case as a bounded application of Jarkesy rather than a general dismantling of agency adjudication. Alito will write separately to reject the contract analogy or the proposed historical limit. Kagan will write the principal defense of treating this enforcement scheme as an immigration public right. The exact number and alignment of dissenting writings are less secure than the existence of disagreement. Gorsuch or Thomas may add a concurrence emphasizing Article III's structural protection; neither is part of the categorical writing call in the vote block.

## Majority ground

**Applying Jarkesy's Article III private-rights presumption, the majority will hold that DOL's binding adjudication of H-2A employment-related monetary liability falls outside the immigration public-rights exception.** Adjudicating workplace obligations resembling contract claims is distinct from deciding admission, exclusion, or immigration status, notwithstanding the employer's participation in a federal visa program.

The majority will focus on the nature of the claim being adjudicated, not solely on the immigration setting of the regulatory program. It will emphasize that the disputed obligations concern wages and working conditions at a domestic farm, extend to corresponding domestic workers, and do not themselves determine anyone's permission to enter or remain. Describing participation as a federally granted privilege will not permit the government to attach conclusive executive adjudication to every related monetary obligation. Oceanic Steam will be distinguished rather than overruled.

## Breadth

**The majority will state a rule governing H-2A employment-related monetary adjudications generally, not merely Sun Valley's particular violations, but will expressly leave administrative monetary penalties within other established public-rights categories and immigration-status enforcement unresolved rather than requiring every agency civil penalty to be adjudicated in court.**

The decision will not invalidate the H-2A program, prohibit administrative decertification, or decide the validity of every immigration-related penalty. It will distinguish meaningful judicial adjudication of private rights from deferential judicial review of an agency's binding factual determination. It will not decide a free-standing Seventh Amendment question merely because Jarkesy's jury-trial reasoning informs the Article III analysis.

## Statutory question and alternatives

The constitutional question will supply the controlling reason to affirm. The Court will not rest its judgment solely on the absence of express authorization for administrative adjudication in Section 1188(g)(2), and will not adopt an independent major-questions holding. It may discuss the distinction between imposing penalties and seeking injunctions without endorsing binding agency adjudication of private rights. The Court will leave the mechanics of any future lawful monetary enforcement to further proceedings rather than reconstruct the entire statutory scheme in this case.

A statutory-only affirmance remains the principal alternative path to the same undisturbed judgment. A government victory is a substantial minority scenario: a majority could treat federal working-condition requirements as germane terms of admission and preserve the program under the established immigration exception. Neither a DIG nor an equally divided affirmance is an expected route on the present record, which shows participation by all nine Justices.
