## 2. Procedural Posture and the Fading of Question 2 at Oral Argument

An ALJ and the Administrative Review Board ordered Sun Valley Orchards to pay **$212,250 in civil money penalties** and **$344,945.80 in back wages** ($557,195.80 total) for violating H-2A meal, housing, and three-quarters work-hour obligations covering H-2A guestworkers and domestic workers in corresponding employment. After the District of New Jersey upheld the order on all grounds, a unanimous Third Circuit panel (Judge Matey) reversed and instructed entry of judgment for Sun Valley under **Article III**, declining in footnote 6 to reach Sun Valley's statutory, Article II, and Eighth Amendment arguments.

Although the Court added Question 2 (whether 8 U.S.C. § 1188(g)(2) authorizes DOL to adjudicate monetary remedies administratively) five weeks before deciding *FCC v. AT&T* on June 4, 2026, **statutory avoidance drew virtually no interest at the November 10, 2026 argument**:

1. **Why *FCC v. AT&T* cut against a statutory off-ramp:** In *FCC v. AT&T*, Chief Justice Roberts (for an 8–1 Court, Thomas dissenting) upheld FCC administrative forfeitures specifically because 47 U.S.C. § 504(a) gave carriers a statutory right to refuse payment and demand a district-court de novo jury trial—contrasting § 504(a) with *Jarkesy*, where the agency's own factual findings bound the regulated party subject only to deferential judicial review. Here, § 1188(g)(2) provides no de novo collection suit and expressly contrasts executive authority for imposing penalties with judicial suits seeking injunctive relief and specific performance—the exact textual distinction *Jarkesy* (603 U.S. at 118) treated as authorizing in-house agency penalty adjudication.
2. **Near-total bench silence on Question 2:** Across 117 transcript pages, only Justice Gorsuch briefly tested Question 2 with Petitioner's counsel (4 turns, Tr. 42–44), dropping the point once Petitioner contrasted § 504(a)'s express de novo trial clause with § 1188(g)(2)'s text; Justice Sotomayor had already noted that this statute is not structured like the FCC's (Tr. 36); and **zero Justices asked Respondent's counsel a single question about Question 2** or about severing back wages from civil penalties. Question 1 (Article III) will almost certainly drive the outcome.

## 3. Oral Argument Synthesis and Per-Justice Assessment

`record/argument-features.json` (**coverage: 210 of 211 Justice turns attributed across 3 of 3 attributed segments; 1 opening turn outside segments; 0 unresolved labels**) shows nearly even overall engagement (**102 turns / 3,257 words** during Petitioner's time vs. **108 turns / 3,939 words** during Respondent's), but a sharp split across the bench. Treating bench questioning as a directional lean rather than a committed vote, we combine `argument-features.json` with qualitative reading of all 117 transcript pages:

- **Kagan (4 turns / 130 words to Pet.; 15 / 979 to Resp. — est. P(disturb) ~ 0.91):** Strongly supported DOL, characterizing H-2A certifications as conditions on foreign workers' admission whose breach amounts to visa fraud, and rejecting Respondent's characterization of regulatory parity standards as private contract enforcement.
- **Jackson (10 / 339 to Pet.; 21 / 859 to Resp. — est. P(disturb) ~ 0.90):** Pressed Respondent repeatedly that historical public-rights precedents (*Oceanic Steam*, *Lloyd Sabaudo*, *Helvering*, *Origet*, *McElrath*) routinely upheld administrative monetary liability and common-law-adjacent claims, and challenged Respondent's unlawful-status test as circular where visa compliance is conditioned on workplace terms.
- **Sotomayor (15 / 433 to Pet.; 20 / 690 to Resp. — est. P(disturb) ~ 0.88):** Pressed Respondent on why immigration public rights do not stop at the physical border given post-entry deportation proceedings and 40 years of IRCA administrative enforcement, remarking that the Court would have to write very carefully to let Respondent win.
- **Alito (1 / 155 to Pet.; 7 / 290 to Resp. — est. P(disturb) ~ 0.72):** Notably favorable to Petitioner. His single turn to Petitioner suggested DOL did not even need to concede *Jarkesy* step 1 because the Third Circuit's private-contract analogy was flawed (workers are non-parties and civil penalties run to the government, Tr. 9–10), and during Respondent's argument he criticized Respondent's effort to freeze immigration penalties at *Oceanic Steam*'s facts as producing a hodgepodge opinion (Tr. 89–90).
- **Kavanaugh (12 / 271 to Pet.; 13 / 387 to Resp. — est. P(disturb) ~ 0.50):** The pivotal median vote. To Petitioner, he emphasized *Tull* and *Jarkesy*'s statements that punitive civil penalties belong in courts of law and downplayed disruption by noting judicial review already exists. In Round 2 with Respondent (Tr. 103–105), he confirmed that under *FCC v. AT&T* the practical difference narrows to who initiates court proceedings and de novo vs. deferential review, and elicited a narrow principle to rule for Sun Valley while preserving 8 U.S.C. § 1324a unlawful-employment fines. Yet in Round 1 (Tr. 87) he warned Respondent that drawing internal lines within the immigration exception undermines systemic clarity.
- **Barrett (11 / 821 to Pet.; 12 / 460 to Resp. — est. P(disturb) ~ 0.45):** Genuine swing vote leaning slightly toward affirmance. She pressed Petitioner hard on *Wong Wing* property limits, suggested *Oceanic Steam* rested on tying vessel fines to outgoing border-clearance papers rather than freestanding penalties, and critiqued Petitioner's blending of immigration and public-benefit categories (Tr. 50–56). In Round 2 with Respondent (Tr. 106–107), however, she observed that once Respondent concedes some immigration penalties are valid, Respondent's status-focused line strongly resembles the Government's own germaneness standard.
- **Roberts (14 / 302 to Pet.; 10 / 111 to Resp. — est. P(disturb) ~ 0.40):** Skeptical of Petitioner's effort to leverage past border entry into administrative control over ongoing domestic employment conditions (Tr. 5–7, 11–12), and invited Respondent to address Kavanaugh's disruption concerns and highlight individual-rights harms (Tr. 94). Still, as author of both *Jarkesy* and *FCC v. AT&T* with a 94.1% majority-join rate, he frequently joins emerging majorities to shape narrow opinions.
- **Thomas (2 / 34 to Pet.; 2 / 52 to Resp. — est. P(disturb) ~ 0.22):** Both questions contrasted administrative decertification (revoking a public privilege) with monetary penalties taking private property—tracking his *Jarkesy* concurrence and lone *FCC v. AT&T* dissent.
- **Gorsuch (33 / 772 to Pet.; 8 / 111 to Resp. — est. P(disturb) ~ 0.18):** Strongly opposed Petitioner on limiting principles and *Wong Wing*, then walked Respondent through a five-point summary supporting affirmance (Tr. 102–103).
