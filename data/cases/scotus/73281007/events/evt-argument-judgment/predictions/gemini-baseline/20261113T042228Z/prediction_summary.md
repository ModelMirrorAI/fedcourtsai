We predict the Supreme Court is slightly more likely than not—at a little over half (55%)—to reverse the lower court's ruling by a 7–2 vote and revive the Department of Labor's $557,000 back-pay and penalty order against Sun Valley Orchards. Although the Court generally requires government civil-penalty cases to go before life-tenured federal judges and juries rather than agency hearing officers, a narrow majority is likely to rule that enforcing wage and housing promises tied to temporary H-2A foreign farmworker visas falls within Congress's longstanding immigration authority.
