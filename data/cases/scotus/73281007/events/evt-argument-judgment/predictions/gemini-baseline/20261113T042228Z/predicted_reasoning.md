# Predicted Reasoning of the Court — *Department of Labor, et al. v. Sun Valley Orchards, LLC* (No. 25-966)

## Predicted Judgment and Lineup

- **Judgment:** **Reversed and remanded** (`judgment: "reversed"`), by a **7–2** vote (with **6–3** reversal as the closest variant, and procedural exit via DIG or equally divided Court unlikely given all nine Justices' active participation and Sun Valley's continuing corporate existence and monetary liability).
- **Majority:** Chief Justice Roberts (most likely author, with Justices Kavanaugh, Barrett, and Kagan as principal alternative assignees), joined by Justices Alito, Sotomayor, Kagan, Kavanaugh, Barrett, and Jackson.
- **Dissenting:** Justices Thomas and Gorsuch.

---

## 1. Threshold Statutory Holding (Question 2: 8 U.S.C. § 1188(g)(2))

The Court reaches both Questions Presented, addressing Question 2 first because Sun Valley argues as an alternative ground for affirmance that 8 U.S.C. § 1188(g)(2) does not authorize the Department of Labor (DOL) to adjudicate monetary remedies in administrative proceedings.

1. **Textual contrast between imposing penalties and seeking judicial injunctions:** Interpreting § 1188(g)(2)'s authorization for the Secretary of Labor to take necessary compliance actions—specifically contrasting imposing appropriate penalties with seeking appropriate injunctive relief and specific performance—the Court holds (following *SEC v. Jarkesy*, 603 U.S. 109, 118 (2024)) that Congress authorized the Secretary to impose monetary penalties directly in administrative proceedings while requiring judicial suits for equitable injunctions and specific performance.
2. **Distinction from *FCC v. AT&T, Inc.* (2026):** Unlike 47 U.S.C. § 504(a) in *FCC v. AT&T*, which expressly made unpaid FCC forfeiture orders recoverable only through a district-court de novo trial, neither § 1188(g)(2) nor 28 U.S.C. § 2461(a)'s catchall venue rule requires a de novo civil suit where Congress has committed penalty imposition to the agency.
3. **Back-wage compliance awards:** Reading § 1188(g)(2)'s broad compliance clause alongside four decades of continuous H-2A administrative practice since 1987 and subsequent statutory amendments leaving that structure intact, the Court concludes that Congress authorized DOL to adjudicate both civil money penalties and back-wage compliance orders administratively.

## 2. Constitutional Holding and Breadth (Question 1: Article III)

On Question 1, the Court reverses the Third Circuit and holds that DOL's administrative enforcement of H-2A employer obligations falls within **Article III's public-rights exception for sovereign regulation of nonimmigrant admission conditions**, while **cabining that holding narrowly** so as not to disturb *SEC v. Jarkesy* outside historically grounded public-rights categories:

1. **Rejection of the Third Circuit's common-law contract framing:** The majority holds that DOL's enforcement proceeding does not adjudicate private common-law contract claims between an employer and employees. H-2A wage, housing, meal, and work-hour obligations are prescribed by federal statute and regulation (`8 U.S.C. § 1188(a)(1), (c)(3)`), enforced by the sovereign in its own name, and designed to ensure that importing foreign agricultural labor does not undercut domestic workers.
2. **Application of *Oceanic Steam Navigation Co. v. Stranahan*, 214 U.S. 320 (1909):** Reaffirming *Jarkesy*'s recognition of alien admission and exclusion as a historic public-rights category where executive officers may impose monetary sanctions without an Article III court (603 U.S. at 129–130), the majority holds that Congress's plenary authority to exclude foreign laborers includes conditioning temporary H-2A admission on sponsoring employers' compliance with germane wage and working-condition requirements—and enforcing violations of those admission conditions administratively after entry, including parity protections for domestic workers in corresponding employment under § 1188(a)(1)(B).
3. **Narrow breadth preserving *Jarkesy*:** Rather than adopting the Government's broad trans-substantive theory that Congress may attach administrative civil penalties to any federal benefit, license, or regulatory privilege (such as postal access), the majority confines its holding to sovereign immigration and guestworker-admission programs where the enforced employer duties are germane conditions of foreign nationals' entry and stay, explicitly leaving *Jarkesy* and *Tull v. United States*, 481 U.S. 412 (1987), intact outside historically recognized public-rights categories.
4. **Remand:** Because the Third Circuit bypassed Sun Valley's remaining preserved challenges (including Article II removal/appointments claims and Eighth Amendment Excessive Fines claims), the Court reverses the judgment below and remands for further proceedings.

## 3. Predicted Separate Writings

- **Principal Dissent (Justice Gorsuch, joined by Justice Thomas):** Contends that under *Jarkesy* and *Tull*, extracting punitive civil penalties and back-pay damages from a U.S. business over domestic farm labor disputes (including U.S. citizen workers) deprives the employer of private property on contract-like claims requiring an Article III court and jury; confines *Oceanic Steam* to border-entry determinations enforced via vessel-clearance exactions; cites *Wong Wing v. United States*, 163 U.S. 228 (1896), against leveraging past entry to strip domestic constitutional protections; and argues § 1188(g)(2) should be construed to require judicial collection under 28 U.S.C. § 2461(a).
- **Separate Dissent (Justice Thomas):** Emphasizes his originalist distinction (from his *Jarkesy* concurrence and *FCC v. AT&T* dissent) between revoking a public privilege such as H-2A certification, which executive officers may do administratively, and imposing binding monetary liability on vested private property, which requires Article III judicial power.
- **Concurrence (Justice Kavanaugh or Justice Alito):** Highlights the narrow, predictable boundary of the immigration-admission holding—confirming that longstanding INA employer-sanction provisions (`8 U.S.C. §§ 1188, 1324a, 1324c`) remain valid while reiterating that outside historic public-rights domains, *Jarkesy* requires an Article III forum or a *FCC v. AT&T*-style de novo trial option.
