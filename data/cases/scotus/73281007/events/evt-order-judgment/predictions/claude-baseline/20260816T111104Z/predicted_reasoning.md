# Forecast: what the Court will do with the judgment in DOL v. Sun Valley Orchards (No. 25-966)

**Judgment class.** The Court will **affirm** the Third Circuit's judgment for Sun
Valley, most likely on the second question presented: 8 U.S.C. § 1188(g)(2) —
which authorizes the Secretary "to take such actions, including imposing
appropriate penalties … as may be necessary to assure employer compliance" —
does not clearly authorize *binding in-house adjudication* of civil money
penalties and back wages, as opposed to assessment followed by enforcement in
an Article III court. The template is *FCC v. AT&T* (decided June 4, 2026,
8–1, Roberts, C.J.): there the Court saved the FCC's forfeiture scheme
precisely because a forfeiture order binds no one until the government prevails
in a de novo jury trial in district court, and it read the power to "impose" a
forfeiture as compatible with court adjudication. DOL's H-2A scheme has no such
backstop — the ARB's order is final, subject only to deferential APA review —
so the feature that saved the FCC condemns DOL, and the same reading of
"impose" supplies a narrow statutory affirmance that avoids a constitutional
holding. If the Court instead reaches Article III, I expect the same bottom
line by a closer vote: the claims are contract-enforcement claims seeking legal
remedies under *Jarkesy*'s claims-and-remedies method, and the government's
immigration public-rights theory is marred by the scheme's coverage of
domestic workers in corresponding employment (51 of the 147 workers here).

This is a probabilistic call, not a confident one: P(judgment disturbed) =
**0.45**. The plausible reversal path is real — *Jarkesy* itself expressly
preserved an immigration public-rights category, citing *Oceanic Steam* and
*Lloyd Sabaudo* (administrative penalties on carriers for breaching conditions
of bringing aliens in), and the government's "conditions on the privilege of
importing foreign workers" framing sits close to that line. The three *Jarkesy*
dissenters (Sotomayor, Kagan, Jackson) are near-certain votes for the
government on the merits, so reversal needs only two of the six *Jarkesy*
majority Justices. Roberts and Kavanaugh are the most gettable. My outcome
distribution: reverse outright ≈ 0.33; affirm on statutory grounds ≈ 0.28;
affirm on Article III ≈ 0.22; affirmed in part/reversed in part (e.g.,
penalties versus back wages, or H-2A versus domestic workers) ≈ 0.07;
vacate-and-remand ≈ 0.05; DIG ≈ 0.02; equally divided ≈ 0.01.

**Vote lineup.** Central scenario (an affirmance): **6–3**, Roberts writing the
majority (he authored both *Jarkesy* and *FCC v. AT&T* and will want to keep
control of this line), joined by Thomas, Alito, Gorsuch, Kavanaugh, and
Barrett; Gorsuch concurring separately to urge the broader Article III/Seventh
Amendment ground (as he did in *Jarkesy*); Sotomayor writing the dissent for
Kagan and Jackson, defending both the statutory authorization and the
constitutionality of public-benefit-condition adjudication. If instead the
government wins, I expect a 5–4 or 6–3 reversal built from the three liberals
plus Roberts and Kavanaugh (possibly Barrett), resting squarely on the
immigration public-rights category — which is why my disturbed probability
sits at 0.45 rather than lower.

**Ground and breadth (the graded claims, in prose).** The majority ground will
be statutory-with-avoidance as stated above; the holding will be narrow —
confined to the H-2A scheme and its contract-based monetary remedies, leaving
admission/exclusion and status-linked immigration adjudication untouched and
leaving DOL free to sue in district court. The Court will not overrule *Atlas
Roofing* (respondent asks, in the alternative; the Court will not take the
invitation).

**Which question the Court resolves.** The Court granted both questions and
will likely resolve the case on QP2 (statutory), reaching QP1 only if a
majority concludes the statute does authorize adjudication. The Court itself
added the statutory question at respondent's urging — the avoidance route was
deliberately opened.

**Procedural exits.** A DIG is unlikely (~0.02): the case was cleanly
presented, briefed on schedule, and no vehicle defect has surfaced. No recusal
is apparent, so an equally divided affirmance is a residual (~0.01).

**Timing** (unscored, stated for discipline): argument is set for November 10,
2026; I expect a decision between March and June 2027, most likely May–June
2027 given the separation-of-powers stakes and probable separate writings.
