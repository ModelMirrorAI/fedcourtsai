# Rationale

I assign a 0.44 probability that the judgment below is disturbed and predict an affirmance. The committed merits baseline for the ten grant Terms before OT2025 is 359 disturbed judgments out of 515 parsed judgments, or 69.7%. Those rows cover 515 of 557 granted cases with 57 cert-order dispositions excluded by the pool guard. I move materially below that baseline because this grant presents two independent paths to leave the Third Circuit's judgment intact and because the Court added the unresolved statutory-authority question to the government's constitutional question.

The grant-moment record consists principally of the petition, the March 2026 cert-stage brief in opposition, the questions presented, and the April 27 grant order. The grant order reformulated the case to ask both whether Article III permits DOL's adjudication and whether 8 U.S.C. § 1188(g)(2) authorizes it. The second question supplies a narrow route: the statute authorizes the Secretary to take enforcement actions and to impose penalties, but it does not expressly establish an in-house adjudicatory forum or state that an agency order conclusively determines contested monetary liability. Constitutional avoidance and the Court's recent insistence on close attention to claimed public-rights exceptions make that route plausible.

The Article III merits also support affirmance. DOL described the job order as a work contract and obtained civil penalties and back wages after an ALJ resolved contested facts. Those claims and remedies resemble traditional actions at law. The government's best response is substantial: H-2A participation is a federal immigration benefit, the employment conditions protect the domestic labor market that Congress must assess before admitting workers, and *Oceanic Steam Navigation Co. v. Stranahan* upheld executive assessment of monetary penalties tied to conditions on bringing aliens into the country. The phrase “including imposing appropriate penalties” also supports an inference that Congress expected agency assessment rather than a separate civil action. Those considerations keep reversal at 0.44 rather than making affirmance overwhelming.

The six-to-three lineup is a point forecast, not a claim of high confidence. The conservative Justices have been the most skeptical of agency adjudication of common-law-like monetary claims; the three liberal Justices are more likely to accept the public-rights characterization and the government's reading of the statute. The largest uncertainty is whether the majority views the H-2A conditions as integral to a public privilege, which could produce a reversal without weakening the Court's general Article III rule.
