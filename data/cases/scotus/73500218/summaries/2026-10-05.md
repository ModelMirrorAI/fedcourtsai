## In brief

Joan Farr asked the Court to order relief in her long-running case against a former celebrity manager and others, and the Court declined to take it up.

## What happened

Joan Farr, representing herself, filed earlier lawsuits against Alexandra Grant, grantLove LLC, attorney Scott Sims, the law firm Ziffren Brittenham LLP, and WME IMG, LLC, among others. A federal district court in Missouri dismissed her case, and the United States Court of Appeals for the Eighth Circuit affirmed that dismissal, later denying rehearing. Farr then asked the Supreme Court to step in through a petition for a writ of mandamus, an order directing a lower court or official to act, filed alongside a separate petition for certiorari she describes as pending. Her petition says she seeks to act as "next friend" for actor Keanu Reeves and to have him and other parties added to the case.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked whether Farr may act on behalf of a third party in pursuing this case under an exception that sometimes allows one person to litigate for another who cannot act for himself, and whether, if the case were sent back for trial, that third party and additional parties should be added to the lawsuit.

## Where it stands

The United States and the other respondents waived their right to respond. The petition was distributed for the Court's September 28, 2026 conference, and on October 5, 2026 the Court denied the petition.

## What each outcome would mean

Farr, as petitioner, asked the Court to order relief in her favor and to let her add Reeves and other parties to the case. By denying the petition, the Court left the Eighth Circuit's decision affirming dismissal of Farr's case standing, meaning Grant, grantLove LLC, Sims, Ziffren Brittenham LLP, WME IMG, and the government defendants prevail, and Farr's case is not revived or sent back for further proceedings.
