## In brief

Joan Farr, representing herself, asks the Court to order a lower court to let her reopen her case and add actor Keanu Reeves as a plaintiff.

## What happened

Joan Farr, a pro se litigant, sued a group of defendants including Alexandra Grant, GrantLove LLC, attorney Scott Sims, Ziffren Brittenham LLP, WME IMG, LLC, and federal government agencies and officials. The district court dismissed her case, and the United States Court of Appeals for the Eighth Circuit affirmed that dismissal and later denied rehearing. Farr now asks the Supreme Court to step in directly, filing a petition for a writ of mandamus, a request that a court be ordered to take a particular action, rather than an ordinary appeal. She says she is acting as Reeves's "next friend," filing on his behalf because she says he cannot act for himself.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked whether it should order the court of appeals to reopen the case so it can be sent back for trial, and whether Farr may file on behalf of Reeves as someone acting for a person who cannot represent himself, and whether Reeves and other parties should be added to the case if it goes back to trial.

## Where it stands

The United States and the other respondents have all filed waivers of their right to respond. The petition was distributed for the Court's conference of September 28, 2026, and the docket shows no action recorded since that conference.

## What each outcome would mean

Farr, as petitioner, is asking the Court to order further proceedings. If the Court grants the writ, it would direct the lower courts to take further action, such as reopening the case for trial, which Farr seeks. If the Court denies the writ, the Eighth Circuit's decision affirming dismissal of Farr's case stands, and the case stays closed. Either way, resolving this petition would not itself decide the underlying claims against Grant, Sims, or the other named parties.
