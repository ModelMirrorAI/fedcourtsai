# Predicted reasoning — In re Joan Farr, No. 25-1315 (evt-petition-disposition)

**Disposition.** The Court will deny the petition for a writ of mandamus at or shortly after the September 28, 2026 conference, in the orders list of Monday, October 5, 2026 (the long-conference order list). No respondent has filed anything but a waiver, so nothing remains to be briefed before the Court acts.

**Relists.** The docket shows exactly one distribution (July 1, 2026, for the conference of September 28, 2026); the petition has never been relisted. I expect no further distribution. The only realistic way a second distribution appears is a clerical reschedule of a long-conference case or a Justice briefly holding the petition, and neither is likely for a pro se extraordinary-writ petition that every respondent has waived.

**CVSG.** None. The United States is already a named respondent, represented by the Solicitor General, and has waived its response; the Court does not invite the views of a party that has already declined to file. No CVSG will issue.

**Question the Court would take, if any.** None. A Rule 20 mandamus petition asks the Court to compel a lower court to act rather than to review a judgment; the underlying matter is a dismissed private civil suit (Farr v. Grant, W.D. Mo. No. 4:24-cv-00439, affirmed by the Eighth Circuit in No. 25-1525 on October 10, 2025) against a private individual, her company, a talent agency, and an entertainment law firm, with the United States joined as a party. There is no question of federal law of general importance, and the extraordinary-writ standard (Rule 20.1: exceptional circumstances, adequate relief unavailable in any other form or from any other court) is not met where ordinary appellate review was available and was in fact taken.

**Summary disposition versus plenary review.** Conditional on any grant, the route would be a summary disposition in the order itself rather than briefing and argument: the Court does not set pro se mandamus petitions for argument. I hold that conditional at about three in four, while regarding the grant itself as remote.

**Dissent from denial.** None. Expect a one-line denial with no separate writing from any Justice. If the Court says anything more than "denied," the likelier addition is a Rule 8 abuse-of-process warning or bar order aimed at a repeat filer, not a dissent, and I do not expect that on a paid petition either.
