The Court will deny the petition. The most likely route is an unsigned order following the December 4, 2026 conference, with disposition in December. The record currently shows one distribution and no relist. I expect no additional distribution, assigning an 18% probability to at least one more. If the Court delays action for further consideration or a separate writing, one or two further distributions are more likely than a prolonged hold.

The Court will not call for the Solicitor General's views. The United States is already a party, and its Solicitor General has filed an opposition explaining the government's position. The probability of a new invitation is 0.1%; no invitation date is forecast.

The petition's broad question about Congress's authority over wholly foreign commerce will not overcome the weakness of the asserted conflict and this case's procedural complications. The decision below ties its result to alleged effects on U.S. oil markets, while the petition's other-circuit authorities involve different statutory language, different conduct, or an insufficient showing of effects. The consent judgment and resulting question of appellate finality add an obstacle to using this case for a major constitutional ruling. A denial will not decide whether the appellate court's interpretation was correct.

If review is granted, the Court is more likely to set the case for briefing and argument than decide it summarily: I assign 8% to a disposition in the cert order conditional on a grant. No specific intervening authority supports a grant-vacate-remand. The likely question taken would narrow the petition's sole question to whether the foreign-commerce requirement of 18 U.S.C. 2339B(d)(1)(E) permits forfeiture based on wholly foreign material-support transactions alleged to have substantial effects on commerce with the United States. If granted, statutory construction and the adequacy of that connection would offer a narrower ground than announcing the full constitutional boundary. Appellate jurisdiction would need to be satisfied first.

Conditional on denial, there is a 14% probability of a dissent or statement respecting it, but the modal prediction is no separate writing. Thomas is the most likely author if one appears, given his prior request in Baston for clarification of the foreign-commerce power. Gorsuch is a less likely author. These possibilities account for a meaningful chance of a short delay without making either a writing or another distribution the central forecast.
