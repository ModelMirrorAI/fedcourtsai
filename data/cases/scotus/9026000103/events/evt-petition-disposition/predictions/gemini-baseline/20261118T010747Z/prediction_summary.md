The Supreme Court is very likely to deny review in this case, with only a 1.5% chance (about one in sixty-five) of taking it up. A Turkish trading company asks the Court to overturn the government's $50 million civil forfeiture—a legal seizure of property tied to crime—of sanctioned Iranian oil linked to terrorism financing, arguing that Congress's constitutional power over foreign trade requires direct trade with the United States rather than foreign oil sales that affect American prices. Although two Justices have previously questioned broad federal authority over conduct abroad, lower courts are not genuinely divided over large-scale commercial oil smuggling, and an unusual procedural shortcut in the trial court creates a threshold appellate-jurisdiction problem that makes this a poor test case.
