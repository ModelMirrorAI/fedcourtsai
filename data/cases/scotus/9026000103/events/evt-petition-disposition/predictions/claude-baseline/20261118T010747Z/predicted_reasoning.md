# Forecast — No. 26-103, All Petroleum-Product Cargo Onboard the M/T Arina v. United States

The docket shows one distribution (for the December 4, 2026 conference) and no relist. The Court will deny certiorari, most likely on the order list following that first conference (December 7, 2026), without a relist.

- **Relist:** not expected. If the petition is relisted, the likeliest reason is a Justice preparing a writing respecting the denial, not movement toward a grant; in that event expect one or two relists followed by a denial.
- **CVSG:** none. The United States is the respondent and has already opposed.
- **Question presented:** if the Court took the case, it would take the petitioners' single question, likely reframed around the statute — whether an offense "affects ... foreign commerce" under 18 U.S.C. 2339B(d)(1)(E) when the transactions occur wholly abroad among foreign actors — and it might add a question on appellate jurisdiction over the consented-to judgment.
- **Summary disposition:** unlikely. No intervening decision supports a GVR, and the D.C. Circuit's ruling is not a candidate for summary reversal. A grant would mean briefing and argument.
- **Separate writing:** most likely none. There is a small chance that Justice Thomas, who dissented from denial on the Foreign Commerce Clause in Baston v. United States (2017), notes a dissent or writes again, possibly joined by Justice Gorsuch.
- **If granted (unlikely):** the government would be favored to win on narrow grounds — that the admitted allegations of effects on U.S. oil prices satisfy any plausible test — leaving the outer limits of the foreign commerce power undecided.
