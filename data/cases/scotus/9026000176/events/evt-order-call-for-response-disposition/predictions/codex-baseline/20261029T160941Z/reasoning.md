## Why 0.40 rather than 0.24 or a majority grant prediction

Three features move me above the 24.4% state-band anchor.

- The Court affirmatively requested a response after the petition had been distributed. That indicates some interest beyond ordinary administrative docketing, although it does not establish support from four Justices or warrant treating the case as already relisted.
- The petition presents a recognizable state-sovereignty and habeas-limits issue. Its central argument is that a retrial, conviction, and new sentence require a new challenge to a new judgment, with state remedies exhausted first. Pitchess, Maleng, and Magwood provide a concrete existing-authority framework for that argument. The petition specifically asks for correction or summary reversal, and the divided decision supplies a developed dissent rather than a merely asserted error.
- The earlier petition in this litigation drew a recorded dissent from Thomas joined by Alito, as the provisioned petition reports. That does not settle the new jurisdictional question or show two additional grant votes. It does identify an existing audience for concerns about this litigation's treatment of federal habeas limits.

Two substantial counterweights keep denial the modal call.

First, the petition expressly acknowledges that it cannot claim a true traditional circuit split. Its cited decisions from other circuits do not decide the same conditional-writ enforcement question. The case therefore competes principally as error correction, even if its sovereign petitioner presents that error as recurrent and structural.

Second, the appellate majority does not simply claim unlimited supervisory jurisdiction. In the retained appendix, pp. 7a–15a, it treats the retrial as noncompliance with the original instruction to exclude the victim's identification; it also finds no effective vacatur of the original conviction. It distinguishes Pitchess as involving a new ground for relief after compliance with the original conditional writ. The opinion additionally identifies a waiver problem concerning compliance, though it reaches the issue anyway. These are genuine obstacles to the petition's description of a clean new-judgment boundary. A Justice can disagree with the court below yet regard the enforcement-versus-new-claim distinction, the vacatur record, or the writ's wording as reasons not to take this vehicle. Thapar disputes those premises, but his dissent does not remove them.

The move from 24.4% to **40% any grant** is consequently a judgmental adjustment, not a fitted estimate for response-requested state habeas petitions. A coherent approximate outcome allocation is denial 0.59, other nongrant exits 0.01, plenary grant 0.14, summary reversal 0.24, and GVR 0.02. The grant-side components total 0.40, and the two summary components total 0.26, giving the recorded **0.65 conditional summary-route probability**. The small GVR component allows uncertainty about developments not in the record; I identify no intervening decision that makes it the leading route.

## Other elicited quantities and limits

The 0.99 distribution-increment probability reflects a procedural next step from the recorded count of one, not a 99% chance of substantive review or a contested relist signal. The cohort masks this claim, as the prompt specifies. The 0.02 CVSG increment reflects the absence of a distinctive need for Solicitor General input; it is not zero because the Court can seek views in a case without a federal party. The 0.20 writing-existence probability is conditional on denial. Thomas has the highest unconditional authorship probability, but most denial probability still entails no writing. Joining someone else's opinion is not counted as writing in the per-Justice distribution. No unobservable cert vote lineup is asserted.

The **0.40 stakes score** is separate from the 0.40 review probability; their numerical equality is incidental. The potential holding would matter to conditional habeas remedies, state retrials, and the enforcement of federal judgments. The immediate noncapital, record-specific dispute is nevertheless narrower than a major change to substantive constitutional rights or a nationally consequential regulatory regime.
