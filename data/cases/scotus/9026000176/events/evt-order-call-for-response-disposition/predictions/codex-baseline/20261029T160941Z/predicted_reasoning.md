# Forecast of the Court's action

The Court will most likely deny the petition after receiving the requested response and considering the completed briefing. The forecast assigns 0.40 to any grant, including summary reversal or a grant-vacate-remand, and 0.60 to no grant. Denial, rather than withdrawal or dismissal, is the modal disposition.

The baseline records **one distribution**, on September 23, 2026, for the October 9 conference, followed by the October 1 request for a response due November 2. It records **no CVSG and no amicus-brief entries**. The next step will be a further distribution after the response or expiration of its deadline: P(at least one additional distribution) = 0.99. This next distribution is not itself evidence that a completed conference has already relisted the petition. The modal path is one additional distribution and then denial; the review path more likely includes one or two additional conference reconsiderations. The response-requested cohort masks the distribution-increment claim, but the forecast still states it.

The Court will not invite the Solicitor General's views: P(a new CVSG) = 0.02. The dispute is over enforcement of a federal habeas writ against a state custodian, not a question for which an executive-branch recommendation is needed to identify the government's regulatory position.

If review is granted, the question will concern the jurisdiction to enforce an existing conditional habeas writ after a state retrial and new judgment. The Court would take the petition's jurisdictional question, or narrow it to whether a new conviction ends enforcement jurisdiction notwithstanding an asserted violation of the original writ. It would not use this petition to decide afresh whether the original identification testimony justified habeas relief.

Conditional on a grant, **summary disposition is more likely than plenary briefing and argument**, at 0.65. The principal summary route is a per curiam reversal under existing habeas and exhaustion principles, not a GVR based on an identified intervening decision. If the Court takes that route, it will reject treating the first habeas proceeding as an unrestricted means of challenging the second judgment. Plenary review remains plausible because separating enforcement of an existing order from adjudication of a new constitutional claim is contestable on this record.

Conditional on denial, P(a dissent from denial or statement respecting denial) = 0.20; no separate writing remains the modal prediction. Thomas is the most likely author, given the prior proceeding described in the petition; Alito is a potential joiner, which is not itself authorship. The order-writers probabilities cover writings on either a denial or a grant-side order, including possible objections to a summary reversal, and are not cert-vote predictions.
