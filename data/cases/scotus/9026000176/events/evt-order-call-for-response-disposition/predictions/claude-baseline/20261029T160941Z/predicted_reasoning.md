# Forecast — Davis, Warden v. Smith (No. 26-176), after the call for a response

**State of the docket.** Petition filed July 27, 2026; distributed once (for the
October 9, 2026 conference); on October 1, 2026 the Court requested a response, due
November 2, 2026. No relist and no CVSG yet; the response is not on the record.

**Disposition.** More likely denied than granted, but this is a live petition. I put
roughly a one-in-four chance on the grant family. The Court will wait for Smith's
response and then distribute the petition again, most likely for a December 2026 or
January 2027 conference. If the Court is interested, expect one or more relists after
that first post-response conference before any order.

**Relist.** A further distribution is near-certain: the petition will go back to
conference once the response is filed or the time to file runs out. Beyond that, I
expect at least one true relist if any Justice is writing — whether a summary-reversal
opinion or a dissent from denial.

**CVSG.** None. This is a state-prisoner habeas dispute between an Ohio warden and
a released prisoner; the federal government is not a party, and the Court does not
usually invite the Solicitor General's views in this posture.

**Route if granted.** About an even split between a summary reversal (a per curiam
holding that the habeas court lost jurisdiction once Smith was retried, reconvicted
and resentenced under a new judgment, citing Pitchess, Maleng and Magwood) and plenary
review of the question presented. The petition expressly invites summary reversal,
the Sixth Circuit opinion is unpublished and drew a dissent from Judge Thapar, and the
Court has a history of correcting Sixth Circuit habeas grants per curiam. Pointing
toward plenary review instead: whether a habeas court may enforce its own conditional
writ when the state does not comply is a real doctrinal question, and the petitioner
concedes there is no clean circuit split. If the Court grants plenary review, it would
take the question as written, perhaps narrowed to a court's authority to grant
further relief on the original petition once a new state judgment exists.

**Should it deny.** I expect a separate writing about as often as not. Justice Thomas,
joined by Justice Alito, dissented from denial of the State's earlier petition in this
same case (No. 24-421) and criticized the Sixth Circuit's handling of AEDPA. A second
dissent from denial by Justice Thomas, joined by Justice Alito and possibly Justice
Gorsuch, is the single most likely separate writing.
