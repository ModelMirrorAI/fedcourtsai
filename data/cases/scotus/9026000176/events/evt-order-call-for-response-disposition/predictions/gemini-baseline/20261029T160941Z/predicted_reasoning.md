- **Solicitor General Invitation (`cvsg-increment`: 0.01):** The Court will not call for the views of the Solicitor General. This is a state criminal habeas corpus dispute under 28 U.S.C. § 2254 involving Ohio state-court retrial procedure and conditional writ enforcement, with no federal agency or federal statutory program at issue.
- **Question Presented Taken (if granted):** If the Court grants plenary review, it will take the single Question Presented in the petition as written: whether federal courts have jurisdiction under 28 U.S.C. § 2254 to grant an unconditional writ of habeas corpus enforcing a prior conditional writ after the habeas petitioner was retried, reconvicted, and resentenced in state court.
- **Summary Disposition vs. Plenary Review (`summary-disposition-route`: 0.42 conditional on grant):** Conditional on the Court granting review (overall grant-family probability 0.24), plenary review is slightly more likely (0.58 conditional share, or ~0.14 unconditional) than a summary reversal or GVR in the certiorari order itself (0.42 conditional share, or ~0.10 unconditional). Although the Warden invites summary reversal and state-on-top habeas grants often take the summary-reversal route, the doctrinal question at the intersection of *Pitchess v. Davis*, 421 U.S. 482 (1975), *Magwood v. Patterson*, 561 U.S. 320 (2010), and conditional-writ enforcement after a completed retrial has not been squarely resolved by a prior Supreme Court holding, making plenary briefing slightly more probable than per curiam summary reversal if four or more Justices vote to intervene.
- **Dissent from Denial and Separate Order Writings (`dissent-from-denial`: 0.30 conditional on denial):** If the Court denies certiorari (probability 0.74), there is a substantial likelihood (0.30) of a published dissent from the denial of certiorari. Justice Thomas, joined by Justice Alito, already authored a published dissent from the denial of certiorari in the first iteration of this exact case (*Davis v. Smith*, 145 S. Ct. 93 (2025)), and is the overwhelming candidate (`Thomas: 0.24`, `Alito: 0.08`, `Gorsuch: 0.04` in `order-writers`) to author a dissent or statement criticizing the Sixth Circuit's nullification of Smith's second state-court conviction.
- **Conditional Merits Ground (if granted):** If the Court grants review or summarily reverses, it would likely hold that under 28 U.S.C. § 2254(a), *Magwood v. Patterson*, 561 U.S. 320 (2010), and *Pitchess v. Davis*, 421 U.S. 482 (1975), once a state conducts a retrial resulting in a new judgment of conviction and sentence, the prisoner's custody is pursuant to the new state-court judgment rather than the original judgment, extinguishing the federal habeas court's jurisdiction over the original petition and requiring the prisoner to exhaust state remedies before challenging errors at the retrial.
