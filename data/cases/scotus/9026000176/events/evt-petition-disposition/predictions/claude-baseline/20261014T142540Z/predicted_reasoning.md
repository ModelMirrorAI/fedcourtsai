# Predicted reasoning — Davis v. Smith, No. 26-176 (evt-petition-disposition)

**State of the docket.** Paid petition by the Ohio Warden (Ohio Solicitor General),
filed July 27, 2026. Distributed once (for the October 9, 2026 conference); no relists
yet. On October 1, 2026 the Court **requested a response**, due November 2, 2026. No
CVSG.

**Relists.** Near-certain further distribution: once the brief in opposition (and any
reply) is in, the petition will be redistributed for a November/December 2026
conference. I expect at least one more distribution, and plausibly one or two relists
after that if the Court is weighing a summary reversal or a dissent from denial.

**CVSG.** None expected. The petitioner is a State and the question is federal-habeas
jurisdiction over state judgments; the Court does not invite the Solicitor General in
this posture.

**Disposition.** Denial remains somewhat more likely than not, but this is a
well-above-baseline candidate for intervention: the Sixth Circuit (Clay, J., joined by
Cole, J.; Thapar, J., dissenting) affirmed an *unconditional* writ on the original
petition after the respondent had been retried, reconvicted, and resentenced and had
neither appealed in state court nor filed a new petition. That sits uneasily with
*Pitchess v. Davis* (no continuing supervision of a retrial under a conditional writ),
*Magwood* (a new judgment is a new judgment), and exhaustion under §2254(b). The same
case drew a dissent from denial by Justice Thomas, joined by Justice Alito, in Jan. 2025.

**If granted, the route.** Roughly even odds between a summary per curiam reversal
(the Court's familiar response to Sixth Circuit habeas grants it views as clearly
wrong) and plenary review of the QP as written. A GVR is unlikely; no intervening
decision bears on the question. If plenary, the Court would take the single QP as
written, framed around whether a habeas court retains jurisdiction to grant further
relief once the custody rests on a new state-court judgment.

**If denied.** A separate writing is quite likely — Justice Thomas (with Justice
Alito, possibly Justice Gorsuch) dissenting from denial, as on the earlier petition.
