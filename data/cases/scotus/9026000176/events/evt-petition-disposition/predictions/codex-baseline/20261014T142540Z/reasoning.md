## Anchor and adjustment

The committed `metrics/statpack.md` and its JSON counterpart provide the anchor. The file's latest commit is dated October 13, 2026; I did not query or refresh the live corpus, so these are committed-pack figures, not a claim about current corpus-wide freshness. The matching `sal-v4` table renders Terms 2017–2026. Excluding this case's Term 2026 and pooling **every shown prior Term**, the state's bracketed reached rates imply **99 grants / 421 weighted resolved petitions = 23.5154%**, calculated from the JSON's unrounded prefix rates and weighted denominators. I use the reached population, not petitions that ended in the state band, and not the much lower all-petition rate. The state classification is already in that anchor and is not counted as another upward adjustment.

The broader modern-cert section contains 661 grants and 586 GVRs against 42,514 denials and 907 dismissals, illustrating why the whole-docket prior is much lower. The originating-Sixth-Circuit cut is also low and mixes different petitioner populations; it does not replace the state-petitioner anchor. The paid-segment terminal relist buckets show sharply greater grant shares at two and three-plus relists, while the CVSG cut has a much larger grant share than its no-CVSG counterpart. Those are terminal population descriptions, not forward probabilities of another distribution or of a CVSG from this petition's present state.

I raise the 23.5% anchor to **38%** on balance:

- **Affirmative attention:** the October 1 request for a response is more informative than distribution alone. It requires the Court to obtain adversarial briefing before making a likely grant, but does not demonstrate that four Justices favor review. It strongly raises the probability of another recorded distribution independently of grant likelihood. Source: snapshot proceedings.
- **Plausible error-correction vehicle:** the State squarely invokes the jurisdictional consequences of a new judgment and the limits on federal supervision of state retrials. Judge Thapar's dissent agrees that the original conviction was displaced and that the State complied with the writ. The petition reports prior Thomas/Alito concern about the earlier round of this litigation. Those features make summary intervention a live possibility rather than a remote tail. Sources: petition pp. 1–3, 10–14; appendix pp. 19a–20a.
- **A serious opposing account:** the majority says the State neither vacated the original conviction in the required manner nor complied with the actual exclusion condition. It distinguishes enforcing an existing conditional writ from entertaining a new, unexhausted challenge to retrial. Its account makes the case less clean than the petition's categorical formulation suggests. Sources: appendix pp. 5a–7a and 13a–17a.
- **Limited conflict and vehicle strength:** the challenged appellate opinion is unpublished, and the petition expressly stops short of claiming a true square circuit split. Several of its other-circuit examples concern custody or replacement judgments in different postures. The dispute over what the retrial testimony established, what the original writ excluded, and whether vacatur occurred gives the Court a reason to see case-specific error correction rather than a clean national rule. Sources: petition pp. 4, 13–14, 24–25; appendix pp. 13a–17a. The petition also reports that no judge requested an en banc vote, which is not itself a merits endorsement.

I separately checked **Pitchess v. Davis, 421 U.S. 482, 487–490 (1975)** through CourtListener (opinion 109256). It reversed relief on a new, unexhausted claim concerning destroyed evidence after the State complied with the original conditional writ. Its restriction on continuing supervision supports the State's theme, but its facts also make the appellate majority's original-condition/new-claim distinction nonfrivolous. I therefore do not treat Pitchess as mechanically deciding the present petition. No outcome or subsequent history of the target petition was retrieved.

The earlier dissent reported in the petition increases that probability, but I do not infer any Justice's present vote or treat the earlier denial as the disposition being forecast.

The **0.46 stakes score** is independent of grant likelihood: the issue has nationwide implications for federal habeas remedies and state retrials, but arises in a comparatively narrow procedural setting. The main forecasting limitations are the missing forthcoming opposition, the truncated appendix, and uncertainty about whether the Court sees an obvious jurisdictional error or a fact-specific enforcement dispute. The numerical adjustments are judgmental, not a fitted model or measured response-request uplift.
