## In brief

A state warden asks the Court to decide whether federal courts could order a prisoner's release after he was retried and reconvicted in state court.

## What happened

David Smith was convicted in Ohio of attempted murder and related charges based partly on a witness's identification of him. Federal courts later found that identification process unconstitutional and ordered Ohio to release Smith unless it retried him without using that identification. Ohio retried Smith, and a jury convicted him again. Smith then asked the federal district court, rather than Ohio's courts, to find that the state still violated the earlier order and to order his outright release. The district court agreed and granted an unconditional release order. The Sixth Circuit affirmed, over a dissent, and later denied rehearing. The warden, representing the state, now asks the Supreme Court to review that decision.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked whether a federal court that ordered a prisoner's conditional release can still intervene in his case, based on the original order, after the prisoner was retried and convicted again in state court. The warden argues federal courts lose authority once a new conviction replaces the old one, and that Smith needed to raise his objections in the state courts first rather than returning to the same federal court.

## Where it stands

The petition was distributed for the Court's October 9, 2026 conference. After that conference, the Court asked Smith to respond to the petition, with the response due November 2, 2026.

## What each outcome would mean

Davis, the warden, is asking the Court to take up the case. If the Court grants the petition, it will hear full arguments and decide whether the federal courts had authority to order Smith's release; a grant does not by itself decide who wins. If the Court denies the petition, the Sixth Circuit's decision affirming Smith's release stands, leaving Smith's federal release order in place. Because the Court has only asked for a response so far, no decision on whether to hear the case has been made.
