## In brief

An Ohio warden asks the Court to decide whether federal courts could keep ordering a prisoner's release after he was retried and reconvicted in state court.

## What happened

David Smith was convicted in Ohio state court of attempted murder and other crimes based on a victim's identification of him. He won a federal writ of habeas corpus, a court order for release, after a federal appeals court found the identification process unconstitutionally suggestive. The order was conditional: Ohio could retry him within 180 days without using that identification. Ohio retried Smith, and a jury convicted him again. Smith then asked the same federal court to find that the state still violated the original order and to make his release unconditional. The district court agreed and ordered him released; the Sixth Circuit affirmed over a dissent arguing the federal courts had lost jurisdiction once Smith was retried and resentenced.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked whether a federal court that granted habeas relief based on a prisoner's first conviction still has power to order relief after that prisoner has been retried, convicted again, and sentenced again in state court, or whether a challenge to the new conviction must instead start over in state court.

## Where it stands

The warden's petition was distributed for a October 9, 2026 conference, after which the Court asked Smith to respond; the deadline for that response was then extended, at Smith's request, to December 2, 2026. The docket shows no action yet on whether the Court will hear the case.

## What each outcome would mean

Davis, the warden, is asking the Court to take up the case and reverse the Sixth Circuit's decision, which left Smith's release in place. If the Court grants the petition, it would hear full briefing and argument and decide whether the lower federal courts had jurisdiction to order Smith's release after his retrial; a grant alone would not decide who wins. If the Court denies the petition, the Sixth Circuit's decision affirming Smith's release stands, and the case ends without the Supreme Court resolving the question.
