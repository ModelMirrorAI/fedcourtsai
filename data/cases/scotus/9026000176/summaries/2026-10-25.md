## In brief

An Ohio warden asks the Court to decide whether federal courts could order a twice-convicted man's release after his retrial, once he had a new state conviction.

## What happened

David Smith was convicted in Ohio of attempted murder and other crimes based on a victim's identification of him. Federal courts found the identification procedure unconstitutional and ordered Ohio to retry him without using that identification or release him. Ohio retried Smith, and a jury convicted him again. Smith then asked the same federal district court to treat the original order as satisfied in his favor, arguing the retrial still relied improperly on the victim's testimony. The district court agreed and ordered Smith's unconditional release. The Sixth Circuit affirmed, over a dissent, and later denied rehearing by the full court. The warden, Cynthia Davis, now asks the Supreme Court to review that ruling.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked whether federal courts still have authority to grant habeas relief to a prisoner based on an earlier conviction once that prisoner has been retried and convicted again in state court, producing a new judgment, or whether at that point the prisoner must instead raise any objections in the state courts first.

## Where it stands

The petition was filed and distributed for a conference; the Court then asked Smith to respond. Smith's deadline to respond was later extended, to December 2, 2026, so the case has not yet been considered again.

## What each outcome would mean

Cynthia Davis, the warden, is asking the Court to take up the case and reverse the Sixth Circuit's decision upholding Smith's release. If the Court grants review and later rules for Davis, it would hold that the federal courts lacked authority to order Smith's release based on the original case once he was retried and convicted again, potentially returning him to custody. If it rules for Smith, the lower court's decision allowing his release would stand. If the Court denies review, the Sixth Circuit's ruling in Smith's favor remains in effect, and Davis would have no further avenue in this case.
