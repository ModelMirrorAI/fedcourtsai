## In brief

Guam's Attorney General asks the Court to revive his bid to lift a 1990 injunction against Guam's abortion law after an appeals court called the case moot.

## What happened

In 1990, a federal court permanently blocked Guam from enforcing an abortion law, based on Roe v. Wade. After the Supreme Court overruled Roe in Dobbs, Guam's Attorney General, Douglas Moylan, asked the district court to lift the injunction. The court refused, partly because Moylan did not respond to an argument that the law was invalid from the start for a separate reason. While his appeal was pending, Guam's own high court ruled that the legislature had separately repealed the law. The Ninth Circuit then dismissed Moylan's appeal as moot, reasoning that with no statute left, there was nothing left to enjoin.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked whether an appeals court may dismiss, as no longer a live dispute, a challenge to a longstanding court order when the order's original legal basis has been overruled, but the underlying law it blocked has separately been repealed by the legislature.

## Where it stands

The petition has been distributed for conference, and the Court later asked for a response, which the opposing side has now filed. No action on the petition itself is yet recorded.

## What each outcome would mean

Moylan, the Attorney General, asks the Court to take up and reverse the Ninth Circuit's ruling. If the Court grants review, it would hear full arguments on whether the appeal was wrongly dismissed as moot, without yet deciding who is right. If it denies review, the Ninth Circuit's dismissal stands, leaving the old injunction in place and Moylan's challenge to it over, a result the opposing physician, Dr. Freeman, defends as correct and already leaves in place.
