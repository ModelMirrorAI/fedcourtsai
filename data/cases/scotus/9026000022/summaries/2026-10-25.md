## In brief

Guam's Attorney General asks the Court to revive his bid to lift a decades-old injunction against Guam's abortion law after the Ninth Circuit called the case moot.

## What happened

In 1990, a federal court permanently blocked enforcement of a Guam law restricting abortion, based on Roe v. Wade. After the Supreme Court overruled Roe in Dobbs v. Jackson Women's Health Organization, Guam's Attorney General, Douglas Moylan, asked the district court to lift the injunction, arguing its legal basis was gone. The district court refused, and while his appeal was pending, the Supreme Court of Guam separately ruled that the underlying law had already been repealed by later legislation. Relying on that ruling, the Ninth Circuit dismissed Moylan's appeal as moot without deciding whether the injunction could survive Dobbs. The Ninth Circuit later denied rehearing.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked whether a federal appeals court may declare a case moot, and so avoid deciding it, when the challenge is to an ongoing court order that was based entirely on a legal rule the Supreme Court has since discarded.

## Where it stands

The petition was distributed for conference, and the Court then asked the respondents to respond rather than acting on it. Several respondents have since received extensions, with their response now due in late November 2026.

## What each outcome would mean

Moylan, as petitioner, asks the Court to take up the case and overturn the Ninth Circuit's mootness ruling. If the Court grants review and later rules for Moylan, the courts would have to decide whether the old injunction can still stand after Dobbs. If it rules for the respondents, who include the Guam Society of Obstetricians and Gynecologists, Dr. William Freeman, and Guam's Governor, the Ninth Circuit's dismissal would stand and the injunction's fate would not be reexamined through this case. If the Court denies review, the Ninth Circuit's decision remains in place.
