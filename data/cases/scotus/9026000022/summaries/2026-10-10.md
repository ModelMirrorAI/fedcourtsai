## In brief

Guam's Attorney General asks the Court to revive his bid to lift a decades-old injunction against Guam's abortion law after the lower court called the challenge moot.

## What happened

In 1990, a federal court permanently blocked enforcement of Guam's abortion law, Public Law 20-134, based on Roe v. Wade. After the Supreme Court overruled Roe in Dobbs v. Jackson Women's Health Organization, Guam's Attorney General, Douglas Moylan, asked the district court to lift the injunction, arguing its legal basis was gone. The district court refused. While his appeal was pending, Guam's territorial supreme court separately ruled that the law had been repealed by later legislation. Relying on that ruling, the Ninth Circuit dismissed Moylan's appeal as moot, without deciding whether the injunction could survive Dobbs. The Ninth Circuit denied rehearing.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked whether a federal appeals court may dismiss as moot a request to lift a long-standing injunction, when that injunction rests entirely on constitutional reasoning the Supreme Court has since rejected, without ever deciding whether the injunction can still stand.

## Where it stands

The petition was distributed for the Court's September 28, 2026 conference, after which the Court asked the respondents to respond; that response, originally due September 25, 2026, has been extended to November 24, 2026 for all respondents. The docket shows no further action since.

## What each outcome would mean

Moylan, Guam's Attorney General, is asking the Court to take up the case. If the Court grants review, it would hear the case and decide whether the Ninth Circuit was wrong to dismiss the challenge as moot, without yet deciding who wins. If the Court denies review, the Ninth Circuit's dismissal stands, leaving the injunction against Guam's abortion law in place, and the mootness question undecided by the Supreme Court. A denial would not mean the Court agrees the case was moot, only that the Ninth Circuit's ruling remains in effect.
