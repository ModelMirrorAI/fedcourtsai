# Forecast — Moylan v. Guam Society of Obstetricians & Gynecologists, No. 26-22

**Disposition: denied (P(any grant) ≈ 0.13).**

**Distributions.** The record shows one distribution (for the 9/28/2026 conference). That
conference date was overtaken when the Court called for a response on Aug 26. The respondents'
time has since been extended to Nov 24, 2026. Expect the petition to be distributed again once
the briefs in opposition and any reply are in, most likely for a conference in early January
2027. Expect a decision at that first post-response conference or after one further relist. A
long relist run is unlikely unless a Justice is drafting a separate writing.

**CVSG.** None expected. The dispute concerns a territorial statute, an injunction issued by a
territorial federal district court, and a Guam intra-government fight between the Attorney
General and the Governor. No federal programme or interest of the United States is at stake.

**Why denial is the likeliest result.** The Supreme Court of Guam, which is authoritative on
Guam law, held that P.L. 20-134 was impliedly repealed. That leaves the 1990 injunction
restraining enforcement of a statute that no longer has force. The Ninth Circuit's one-line
mootness dismissal is unpublished. The petition identifies no circuit split; it distinguishes
*McCorvey v. Hill* rather than showing a conflict. Guam's own chief executive is aligned with
the respondents. Any plenary opinion would mainly be a mootness lesson with no live statute
behind it.

**If the Court acts, the route is summary.** The likeliest form of relief is a GVR-type order
(grant, vacate and remand) in the *Munsingwear* line. Vacating a judgment whose review was
cut off by mootness is the ordinary course. The Court could also direct the lower courts to
vacate or dissolve the Roe-based injunction rather than leave it standing. The respondents'
answer may make this easier to grant, if they say they have no interest in keeping an
injunction against a repealed law. Plenary review on the QP as written (whether a Rule 60(b)(5)
challenge to an injunction resting on overruled precedent can be dismissed as moot) is less
likely than a summary disposition. If the Court did take the case, it would likely reformulate
the question around *Munsingwear* vacatur and the equitable duty to dissolve stale decrees
(*Agostini*, *Horne*).

**Separate writing on a denial.** Probably none. If there is one, the likeliest authors are
Justice Thomas or Justice Alito. Either might write a short statement noting that a Roe-based
federal decree remains formally in force, echoing Judge VanDyke's statement respecting the
denial of rehearing en banc.
