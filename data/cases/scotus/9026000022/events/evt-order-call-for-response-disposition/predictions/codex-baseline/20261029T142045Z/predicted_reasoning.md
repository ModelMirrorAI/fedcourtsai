# Forecast of the Court's action

The Court will deny this petition, most likely without an explanatory opinion. The narrow mootness posture will control its treatment: a ruling dissolving the federal injunction would still leave the Guam Supreme Court's determination that the underlying territorial statute was repealed. The Court will not use this petition to decide a broad new constitutional abortion rule.

The baseline has one distribution and an August 26 response request. Expect one further distribution after the response process, not a prolonged sequence of substantive relists. A second additional distribution is possible but is not the central forecast. Measured from this baseline, the probability of at least one additional distribution is 0.99; this counts administrative redistribution as well as genuine reconsideration. Expect disposition in the autumn or early winter of 2026 after the response and any reply, rather than an immediate disposition on the original September 28 conference schedule. That timing is a forecast from the staged record, not a report of later docket events.

The Court will not call for the Solicitor General's views; the probability of a new CVSG is 0.035. Guam's territorial status creates a possible federal interest, but the live obstacle is the effect of the territorial repeal ruling and the particular injunction, not a demonstrated need for the government's broader policy view.

If review is granted, expect the Court to address a narrowed version of the single question presented: whether this particular Rule 60(b)(5) appeal is moot, and what should happen to the existing federal orders following territorial repeal. A grant would not itself decide whether Guam can enforce the old abortion prohibition. Conditional on any grant, a cert-order vacatur/remand is slightly more likely than plenary briefing and argument (0.55 versus 0.45). The likely summary route would concern the proper treatment of the orders after mootness, not a direct command authorizing enforcement of a repealed law. If the Court takes plenary review, expect the dispute to center on effectual relief and the relationship between mootness and prospective decrees rather than reconsideration of Dobbs.

On the predicted denial, expect no separate writing (0.14 conditional probability of any noted dissent or statement). Should a Justice write, Thomas or Alito is the likelier author, with Gorsuch a smaller possibility. No majority opinion or cert vote lineup is predicted.
