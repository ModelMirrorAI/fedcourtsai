The call for a response is a modest positive signal beyond the state-caption anchor, but the prompt supplies no measured response-call multiplier. The following unusually concrete vehicle defects more than offset it:

- The Ninth Circuit's short April 28, 2025 order, retained in Appendix A at 2a, dismisses the appeal as moot in light of the Guam Supreme Court's territorial-law decision. Removing the federal injunction would not itself revive a repealed territorial statute. The petition's post-Dobbs framing does not eliminate that separate obstacle to effectual relief.
- Most importantly, Appendix F at 61a expressly says the panel's dismissal was the right call. At 67a–68a, Judge VanDyke explains that the territorial judgment independently defeats enforcement and describes the Fifth Circuit's McCorvey decision as reaching the same result. His criticism of Roe's enduring consequences is not a dissent advocating reversal of this disposition. This makes the petition's invocation of his statement substantially weaker than its introduction suggests.
- The petition at page 16 itself acknowledges McCorvey's similar approach. It distinguishes the pre-Dobbs setting, but does not demonstrate conflicting circuit holdings on the actual mootness question.
- Appendix B at 5a–8a records the district court's conclusion that the Attorney General had not carried his Rule 60(b)(5) burden, including failure to answer an Organic Act/legal-nullity argument and to justify vacating the injunction in its entirety. These are additional vehicle and tailoring complications, not findings here that every alternative objection necessarily succeeds.

The upward case for review is real: an ongoing injunction founded on overturned constitutional precedent, a sovereign enforcement interest, the affirmative response request, and a narrower possible vacatur remedy. The petition's Agostini, Horne, and Munsingwear arguments give those concerns a legal structure. Nevertheless, prospective-relief doctrine does not by itself answer whether this appeal can provide practical relief after territorial repeal. I discount from 24.4% to 12%, a judgmental adjustment, not a fitted estimate or a measured effect of any one feature.
