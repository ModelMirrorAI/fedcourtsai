# Forecast: Moylan v. Guam Society of Obstetricians & Gynecologists, No. 26-22

**Bottom line: the petition will be denied, most likely at a January 2027 conference, without argument and without a CVSG.**

## Procedure from here

- **Distributions.** The docket shows **one** distribution (for the September 28, 2026 conference), which was overtaken by the August 26 response request; the response is now due November 24, 2026. Expect at least one further distribution once the brief in opposition and any reply are in — a redistribution for a January 2027 conference is close to certain. Beyond that, I expect **no genuine relist**: one conference after redistribution, then denial. A single relist is possible if a Justice is weighing a statement, but two or more is unlikely.
- **CVSG.** No. The United States is not a party, no federal statute or program is implicated, and the question is a mootness ruling resting on a territorial court's reading of territorial law. The Court will not call for the Solicitor General's views.
- **Timing.** Denial in January or February 2027.

## What the Court would take, if it took anything

If granted, the Court would take the question presented essentially as written — whether a Rule 60(b)(5) appeal from a Roe-based injunction becomes moot once the enjoined statute is held repealed — but it would almost certainly narrow it to the Munsingwear point the petition raises in Part III: whether a court of appeals that dismisses such an appeal as moot must vacate the unreviewed district-court order denying relief. The broader "supervisory responsibility" framing of Part I would not survive.

## Summary route versus plenary review

Conditional on any grant, a **summary disposition is the likelier route** — a grant, vacate, and remand with instructions on Munsingwear vacatur of the district court's order, rather than a case set for argument. There is no intervening decision of this Court to GVR in light of, but the vehicle is too thin for plenary review: a two-sentence dismissal order, no circuit conflict (the Fifth Circuit's McCorvey v. Hill reached the same result and the petition does not claim otherwise), and a statute the Supreme Court of Guam has declared without force. A per curiam summary reversal of the mootness holding itself is improbable, because the Ninth Circuit's most sympathetic judge wrote that mootness "was the right call."

## Separate writing on denial

Probably none. A statement respecting denial from Justice Thomas or Justice Alito about federal injunctions lingering after Dobbs is the realistic alternative, and the response request shows at least one chambers looked twice. But the writing most likely to attract that interest — Judge VanDyke's statement below — already concedes mootness and explains that the injunction does not bind Guam as to any future abortion law, which leaves little for a dissenter to say about the disposition. I expect a bare denial roughly four times in five.

## Conditional merits note

If the Court reached the merits, I would expect it to affirm the mootness dismissal, holding that federal courts are bound by the Guam Supreme Court's construction of Guam law and that an injunction against a statute that no longer exists gives a Rule 60(b)(5) movant nothing to win; the only live possibility for the petitioner would be a Munsingwear-style vacatur of the district court's denial order, not revival of the enforcement question.
