# Forecast

The Court will most likely deny certiorari after receiving the requested opposition. The probability of any grant, including a GVR or summary reversal, is **14%**.

The baseline records **one distribution**, entered August 19 for the September 28, 2026 conference, and **no CVSG**. The Court requested a response on August 26, and the latest recorded extension makes all respondents' responses due November 24, 2026. I expect **one further distribution**, with an additional substantive relist possible but not the modal path, and a disposition in December 2026 through February 2027 if briefing proceeds on that schedule. The probability of at least one additional distribution is **95%**. That forecast includes redistribution after the response request; it does not imply that the Court substantively considered the petition at the originally scheduled conference.

I expect **no CVSG**: probability **4%** of a new invitation. The territorial setting and Organic Act background give the United States a possible institutional interest, but the immediate issue is whether relief against this particular injunction remains effectual after the underlying statute's implied repeal. If an invitation issues, I expect it after the opposition and renewed distribution, in winter 2026–27, delaying final disposition.

The likely obstacle to review is the territorial-law determination that the old abortion statute no longer has force. The Court is unlikely to treat this as a clean conflict over whether Dobbs generally permits reconsideration of Roe-based injunctions. The record instead presents a narrower mootness problem, with additional questions about the scope of the requested relief and the district court's forfeiture analysis. Denial would leave those questions unresolved, not endorse the constitutional basis of the original injunction.

**If review is granted**, I expect the question to focus on whether an extant injunction supplies a live controversy, or warrants vacatur, despite the authoritative implied repeal of the statute it restrains. I do not expect a broad reconsideration of Dobbs. Conditional on any grant, I assign **60%** to disposition in the cert order itself, most plausibly a narrow vacatur/remand addressing the proper treatment of the injunction, and **40%** to plenary review. Dobbs already preceded the challenged appellate order, so this is not a forecast of an automatic GVR following a newly intervening decision. Any summary relief would require the Court to identify a present procedural error.

Conditional on denial, I assign **15%** to a noted dissent or statement respecting denial. The modal outcome is an unsigned denial without a separate writing. If a writing appears, Thomas or Alito would be plausible authors of concerns about lingering Roe-era decrees; that attribution is context only, not a forecast of individual cert votes.
