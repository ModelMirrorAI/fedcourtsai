## In brief

Asbestos claimants ask the Court to decide whether a company's use of the "Texas Two-Step" bankruptcy maneuver should let them resume suing it in state court.

## What happened

Michael Herlihy, Ann Herlihy, and the estate of Peter Bergrud developed mesothelioma or related illness after exposure to asbestos products made by CertainTeed. CertainTeed split itself into two companies, transferring its asbestos liabilities to a new entity, DBMP, LLC, which then filed for bankruptcy, automatically pausing asbestos lawsuits nationwide, including the claimants' own suits. The claimants asked the bankruptcy court to lift that pause as to their own cases, arguing DBMP filed in bad faith because CertainTeed remained wealthy and solvent. The bankruptcy court and district court refused, and a divided Fourth Circuit panel affirmed, holding the company's financial health was irrelevant; a dissenting judge argued the maneuver was bad faith.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked whether a company's pursuit of bankruptcy through this restructuring technique counts as acting in bad faith, such that a court must lift the automatic pause on lawsuits against it, or whether the company can keep that protection even though it was profitable and not in financial distress when it filed.

## Where it stands

The petition was distributed for the Court's November 13, 2026 conference, after which the Court asked DBMP to respond; DBMP's lawyer then was granted an extension to file that response by January 14, 2027.

## What each outcome would mean

The claimants, Herlihy and the Bergrud estate, are asking the Court to overturn the Fourth Circuit's ruling, which left in place the lower courts' refusal to lift the lawsuit pause. If the Court eventually grants review and rules for the claimants, DBMP could lose the automatic protection shielding it from their lawsuits, letting those cases proceed in state court. If the Court denies review or rules for DBMP, the pause continues under the Fourth Circuit's approach. No decision has yet been made on whether the Court will hear the case.
