## In brief

Asbestos claimants ask the Court to decide whether DBMP's bankruptcy, created through a corporate split to isolate asbestos liabilities, was filed in bad faith.

## What happened

Michael and Ann Herlihy and the Estate of Peter Bergrud sued DBMP and its corporate family over asbestos exposure that caused mesothelioma. CertainTeed, DBMP's corporate predecessor, split itself into two companies, keeping most assets in one and assigning asbestos liabilities to the other, DBMP, which then filed for bankruptcy. This triggered an automatic pause on lawsuits against DBMP. The claimants asked the bankruptcy court to lift that pause, arguing DBMP's filing was made in bad faith since it was financially healthy. The bankruptcy court and district court disagreed, and a divided Fourth Circuit panel affirmed, with a dissenting judge calling the maneuver an abuse of bankruptcy law.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked whether a company's use of this corporate-split-and-bankruptcy strategy to handle tort liabilities, when the company is not in financial distress, counts as bad faith sufficient to justify lifting the automatic pause on lawsuits during the bankruptcy.

## Where it stands

The petition was distributed for the Court's November 13, 2026 conference. After DBMP initially waived its right to respond, the Court requested a response, due November 30, 2026.

## What each outcome would mean

The Herlihys and the Bergrud estate, as petitioners, are asking the Court to take up their case and reverse the Fourth Circuit's ruling, which left DBMP's bankruptcy protections in place. If the Court grants review, it would hear the case and decide the legal question; this would not itself decide who wins. If the Court denies review, the Fourth Circuit's decision stands, leaving the pause on the claimants' lawsuits against DBMP in effect while the bankruptcy proceeds.
