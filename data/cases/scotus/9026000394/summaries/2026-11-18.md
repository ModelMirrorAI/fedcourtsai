## In brief

Asbestos victims ask the Court to revive their bid to pursue civil claims against DBMP, a company they say was created solely to absorb and shield asbestos liability in bankruptcy.

## What happened

Michael and Ann Herlihy and the estate of Peter Bergrud sued CertainTeed over asbestos exposure that caused mesothelioma. CertainTeed split itself into two companies, moving its asbestos liabilities into a new entity, DBMP, which then filed for Chapter 11 bankruptcy. That filing automatically paused all lawsuits against DBMP, including the petitioners' claims. The petitioners asked the bankruptcy court to lift that pause so they could pursue their claims in state court, arguing DBMP's bankruptcy was filed in bad faith because CertainTeed remained profitable and could pay the claims outside bankruptcy. The bankruptcy court and, on appeal, the district court and a divided Fourth Circuit panel all ruled against the petitioners, finding no bad faith. One Fourth Circuit judge dissented.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked whether a company's use of this corporate-split-then-bankruptcy strategy counts as acting without good faith, which under bankruptcy law can justify pausing the automatic halt on lawsuits so that creditors' claims can go forward in another court.

## Where it stands

The petition was filed and distributed for the Court's November 13, 2026 conference. DBMP waived its right to respond, but the Court later asked for a response, due November 30, 2026; DBMP has since asked for more time to file it. The petition awaits the Court's decision on whether to hear the case.

## What each outcome would mean

The Herlihys and the Bergrud estate are asking the Court to take up their case. If the Court grants the petition, it would hear the case and decide whether DBMP's bankruptcy filing lacked good faith, with no outcome yet determined for either side. If the Court denies the petition, the Fourth Circuit's ruling stands, meaning the automatic pause on the petitioners' lawsuits against DBMP remains in place and their claims continue to be handled through the bankruptcy process rather than state court.
