## Reasons for meaningful upside

The sole question asks whether pursuing bankruptcy through a Texas Two-Step lacks good faith and supplies cause to lift the automatic stay under 11 U.S.C. § 362(d). Petition pp. 14–18 identifies an entrenched disagreement with the Third Circuit's financial-distress requirement and with other circuits' rejection of the Fourth Circuit's combined subjective-bad-faith/objective-futility test. The Fourth Circuit actually applied that combined test to both § 1112(b) and § 362(d), and treated the asserted absence of financial distress as insufficient here (appendix pp. 13a–18a). This is more substantial than a bare disagreement about a single discretionary ruling. The published divided panel, denial of rehearing, deliberate forum selection, and broad amicus coalition strengthen the case for intervention.

The independently retrieved Third Circuit amended LTL opinion, March 31, 2023, confirms the central legal contrast: a good-faith filing requires financial distress, and the value of the funding backstop was central to finding none. Its conclusion declines to condemn every divisional-merger bankruptcy categorically. Thus it supports a serious doctrinal disagreement, not the proposition that LTL already decides this petition's precise question. The official opinion's opening and conclusion, and petition pp. 14–18, are the relevant sources.

## Why denial remains more likely

The strongest brake is **vehicle fit**. The majority expressly says that a motion to dismiss the bankruptcy, the legality of the divisional merger itself, and constitutional eligibility questions are not before it (appendix pp. 9a–10a). The Third Circuit's LTL decision involved dismissal under § 1112(b), whereas this petition challenges denial of relief from a stay under § 362(d). The majority also found no adequate showing of subjective bad faith, credited an uncapped funding arrangement and the asbestos-specific § 524(g) process, and applied abuse-of-discretion review (appendix pp. 14a–18a). Those features allow a respondent to argue that the purported split is broader than the issue necessary to decide this appeal. The petition's account is advocacy; the majority supplies a materially different account of whose assets remain available to claimants.

The petition itself acknowledges recent denial of the Bestwall petition, No. 25-1013 (petition p. 20). A historical corpus lookup confirms denial on June 1, 2026 after one distribution. That is not this case's outcome and is legitimate pre-snapshot context. It suggests reluctance to intervene in this family of disputes, although the present petition's narrower statutory framing may improve on Bestwall. Purdue Pharma's grant, confirmed by a separate corpus lookup for No. 23-124, establishes only that mass-tort bankruptcy can command review; its different issue and federal petitioner make it a poor numerical comparator. A broad corpus query returned unrelated recent grants, including an interim application; I assign those no case-specific weight.

My 24% is consequently a substantial upward adjustment from 5.09%, driven by the response request, developed legal disagreement, and actual amicus support, tempered by the different statutory postures, deferential review, and the Court's recent opportunity to engage related issues. The main uncertainty is whether the opposition can convincingly narrow the conflict, not whether the topic is important.

## Other forecasts and limits

The 98% further-distribution probability mainly reflects ordinary redistribution after the requested response, not confidence in a substantive relist. I assign 12% to a new CVSG: federal bankruptcy administration and the states' involvement provide reasons to seek views, but the ordinary adversarial briefing can supply the legal arguments. Conditional on grant, plenary review is strongly favored; no identified intervening lead decision makes a GVR natural, and the contested standard and record make summary reversal unattractive. Thus the summary-route probability is 5%. Conditional on denial, I assign 14% to a noted dissent or statement; the low unconditional per-Justice writing probabilities also allow concurrence or statement writing on a grant. They are not predictions of private cert votes.

The significance score of 0.80 measures access to civil courts, delayed compensation, and nationwide mass-tort restructuring incentives, independently of grant odds.
