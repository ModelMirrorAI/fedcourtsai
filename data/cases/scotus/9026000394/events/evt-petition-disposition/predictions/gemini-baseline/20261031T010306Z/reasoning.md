# Certiorari Prediction: *Michael Herlihy, et al. v. DBMP, LLC* (No. 26-394)

## 2. Case Overview and Procedural Posture
Petitioners Michael and Ann Herlihy and the Estate of Peter L. Bergrud are two individual asbestos-claimant groups among roughly 60,000 tort claimants in the Chapter 11 bankruptcy proceeding of Respondent DBMP, LLC in the Western District of North Carolina. DBMP was formed in 2019 via a Texas divisional merger ("Texas Two-Step") of CertainTeed Corporation ("Old CertainTeed"), which allocated its asbestos liabilities and certain operating assets to DBMP while allocating its remaining business assets to CertainTeed LLC ("New CertainTeed"), paired with an uncapped funding agreement obligating New CertainTeed to fund DBMP's bankruptcy administration and a 11 U.S.C. § 524(g) asbestos trust.

In August 2021, the bankruptcy court denied a motion by the Official Committee of Asbestos Personal Injury Claimants to lift the automatic stay (and entered a preliminary injunction protecting New CertainTeed). Neither the Committee nor any claimant appealed that 2021 order or moved to dismiss the Chapter 11 case under 11 U.S.C. § 1112(b). Almost three years later, in April 2024, Petitioners filed individual motions under 11 U.S.C. § 362(d) for relief from the automatic stay to liquidate their state-law tort claims in Washington and California state courts, arguing that DBMP's Texas Two-Step bankruptcy lacked good faith because the enterprise is solvent and non-distressed.

The bankruptcy court denied the motions, applying the Fourth Circuit's balancing framework for lift-stay motions under *In re Robbins*, 964 F.2d 342 (4th Cir. 1992), as well as the bad-faith standard of *Carolin Corp. v. Miller*, 886 F.2d 693 (4th Cir. 1989). The district court affirmed. A divided panel of the Fourth Circuit (Judge Niemeyer, joined by Judge Harris; Judge King dissenting) affirmed on February 11, 2026, and the Fourth Circuit denied rehearing en banc without a poll on April 20, 2026.

## 4. Repeated Cert Denials in Related Fourth Circuit "Texas Two-Step" Cases
Corpus queries confirm that the Supreme Court has repeatedly declined to review the Fourth Circuit's handling of Texas Two-Step asbestos bankruptcies, even in cases presenting cleaner, threshold challenges brought by the official creditors' committees:
- ***Official Committee of Asbestos Claimants of Bestwall LLC v. Bestwall LLC*, No. 25-1013:** Certiorari denied on **June 1, 2026**, from *Bestwall LLC v. Official Committee of Asbestos Claimants*, 148 F.4th 233 (4th Cir. 2025), despite extensive amicus support.
- ***Official Committee of Asbestos Claimants v. Bestwall LLC*, No. 23-675, and *Esserman v. Bestwall LLC*, No. 23-702:** Certiorari denied on **May 13, 2024**, from *In re Bestwall LLC*, 71 F.4th 168 (4th Cir. 2023).

Given that the Court declined to review *Bestwall* just five months ago in No. 25-1013, there is virtually no appetite to take up the exact same Fourth Circuit corporate-restructuring controversy in an inferior procedural posture.

## 5. Severe Vehicle and Merits Flaws
Even if four Justices were interested in addressing whether "financial distress" is required for a Chapter 11 reorganization or a § 524(g) asbestos trust—the issue addressed by the Third Circuit in *In re LTL Management, LLC*, 64 F.4th 84 (3d Cir. 2023) (*LTL I*) and *In re LTL Management, LLC*, 130 F.4th 65 (3d Cir. 2025) (*LTL II*)—this case is an exceptionally poor vehicle:
- **Lift-Stay Motion (§ 362(d)) vs. Motion to Dismiss (§ 1112(b)):** Unlike *LTL Management*, which arose from a motion to dismiss the Chapter 11 petition under 11 U.S.C. § 1112(b) filed at the outset of the bankruptcy by the official claimants' committee, neither Petitioners nor the Official Committee ever moved to dismiss DBMP's Chapter 11 petition. Instead, Petitioners sought discretionary individual relief from the automatic stay under 11 U.S.C. § 362(d) four years into the bankruptcy.
- **Alternative Grounds Below (*Robbins* Balancing):** As both the District Court (App. 63a–74a) and the Fourth Circuit majority (App. 9a–12a) emphasized, the narrow question presented on appeal was whether the bankruptcy court abused its discretion in denying individual stay relief under the multi-factor balancing test of *In re Robbins*, 964 F.2d 342 (4th Cir. 1992). The Fourth Circuit explicitly noted: *"To be clear, we do not have before us any motion to dismiss the Chapter 11 petition so as to challenge whether Chapter 11's § 524(g) reorganization procedure has been appropriately invoked in this case. Nor do we have the question before us whether the pre-bankruptcy Texas Two-Step process is appropriate or legal."* (App. 9a). Because the bankruptcy court's discretionary *Robbins* balancing (avoiding piecemeal litigation of 60,000+ claims in state courts while a § 524(g) estimation and mediation process is underway) independently supports the denial of stay relief to two individual claimants, the Court could not reach the broader Texas Two-Step / *Carolin* dismissal standard without untangling discretionary case-management determinations.
- **Distinction from *LTL Management* on § 524(g):** In *LTL Management* (which involved cosmetic talc claims), the Third Circuit expressly declined to decide whether a debtor qualifying for 11 U.S.C. § 524(g)'s explicit statutory mechanism for asbestos trusts could satisfy good faith without immediate insolvency. Here, the Fourth Circuit relied heavily on DBMP's invocation of § 524(g) to address 60,000+ pending asbestos suits and decades of future claims (citing *Truck Insurance Exchange v. Kaiser Gypsum Co.*, 602 U.S. 268 (2024)).

## 6. Conclusion
Combining the ~5.1% baseline cert rate with (1) Respondent's waiver of a response and no CFR prior to distribution, (2) zero cert-stage amicus briefs, (3) the Court's June 1, 2026 denial of certiorari in *Bestwall* (No. 25-1013), and (4) the poor vehicle posture of an abuse-of-discretion appeal from an individual § 362(d) lift-stay motion four years into an unchallenged Chapter 11 proceeding, certiorari is overwhelmingly likely to be denied. I assign a probability of **0.01** (1%).
