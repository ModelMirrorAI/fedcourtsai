At that post-BIO distribution, the most likely trajectory is that the petition is **not relisted further** (0 post-BIO relists, i.e., 2 total distributions across the docket's life) or relisted at most once before certiorari is denied.

2. **Call for the Views of the Solicitor General (`cvsg-increment` = 0.03):**
   No CVSG is currently on the docket, and the Court will very likely **not** issue a CVSG (`cvsg-increment` = 0.03). While mass-tort bankruptcy questions occasionally implicate the United States Trustee Program, this dispute concerns private tort claimants seeking individual relief from the automatic stay under 11 U.S.C. § 362(d)(1) in a six-year-old private asbestos bankruptcy, and the Court declined to invite the Solicitor General's views in the closely parallel *Bestwall* petitions from the same circuit and district (Nos. 23-675, 23-702, and 25-1013).

3. **Disposition (`disposition` = 0.14; `predicted_disposition` = `denied`):**
   The Court will deny the petition for a writ of certiorari after the Brief in Opposition is filed and considered at conference. Once Respondent's opposition highlights the procedural posture—that DBMP's Chapter 11 case was never sought to be dismissed under 11 U.S.C. § 1112(b), that Petitioners seek individual stay relief under § 362(d)(1) years into the reorganization while 60,000+ other claimants remain stayed, that the Fourth Circuit held Petitioners waived any standalone "subjective bad faith" argument below, and that the bankruptcy court's denial of stay relief rests independently on discretionary *Robbins* balancing factors—the Court will conclude that this case is an unsuitable vehicle to resolve the circuit split over the Chapter 11 good-faith standard.

4. **Question Presented & Conditional Merits Scope (Context-Only):**
   If the Court were to grant certiorari (`P = 0.14`), it would take the single Question Presented in the petition—whether a debtor's pursuit of bankruptcy via a divisional-merger ("Texas Two-Step") scheme lacks good faith constituting "cause" under 11 U.S.C. § 362(d)(1)—and would likely address whether the Fourth Circuit's conjunctive *Carolin* standard (requiring both objective futility and subjective bad faith) comports with the Bankruptcy Code's text and equitable structure under §§ 362(d)(1) and 1112(b).

5. **Summary Disposition Route Conditional on Grant (`summary-disposition-route` = 0.05):**
   Conditional on a grant, plenary merits review with full briefing and oral argument is far likelier than a summary disposition in the cert order (`summary-disposition-route` = 0.05). There is no intervening Supreme Court decision warranting a GVR, and the circuit split between the Fourth Circuit (*Carolin* / *DBMP*) and the Third Circuit (*LTL Management*) is a major doctrinal division that the Court would not resolve by summary reversal.

   Conditional on denial, the modal outcome (88%) is an unsigned denial order with no separate statement or dissent, matching the Court's clean denial orders in *Bestwall* (Nos. 23-675, 23-702, and 25-1013). However, there is a meaningful tail probability (`dissent-from-denial` = 0.12) that a Justice—most plausibly Justice Gorsuch (`0.045`), Justice Jackson (`0.030`), Justice Thomas (`0.025`), or Justice Sotomayor (`0.025`)—authors a statement respecting the denial of certiorari or a brief dissent noting the acknowledged circuit split over the Texas Two-Step and explaining that vehicle defects in this § 362(d)(1) appeal counsel awaiting a cleaner § 1112(b) dismissal case.
