The Supreme Court is unlikely to hear this appeal, with about a one-in-seven chance (14%) of granting review. Although the case raises an important disagreement among federal appeals courts over the "Texas Two-Step"—a controversial corporate restructuring tactic used here by building-products company CertainTeed to place its asbestos lawsuits into a separate subsidiary's bankruptcy—this specific lawsuit has serious procedural flaws that make it a poor test case. Rather than asking to dismiss the entire bankruptcy at the outset, two individual plaintiffs waited years into the bankruptcy to ask for a special exemption from the automatic pause on lawsuits (known as the "automatic stay"), and the lower court found they forfeited a key legal argument along the way. Even though the Supreme Court asked the company to file a written response after the company initially waived its right to respond, the Court will likely deny review once that response highlights these procedural obstacles.
