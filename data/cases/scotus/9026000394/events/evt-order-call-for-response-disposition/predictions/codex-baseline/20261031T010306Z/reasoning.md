## Reasons to move above the anchor

The petition presents a developed disagreement about good faith and financial distress, not merely an individualized damages dispute. The Third Circuit's LTL decision is the principal comparator. The petition describes the additional conflict over the Fourth Circuit's requirement of both subjective bad faith and objective futility, including contrary approaches in the Eighth and Eleventh Circuits. The appendix confirms that the Fourth Circuit applied that two-part requirement to section 362(d) and treated pursuit of an authorized section 524(g) process as supporting good faith (App. 13a–17a). I credit a real doctrinal divergence, while distinguishing it from a perfectly matched conflict on the same motion.

The published appellate decision includes Judge King's extensive dissent. The question also has substantial practical stakes: the majority itself describes roughly 60,000 pending claims and an uncapped funding agreement, rather than making the record's importance depend only on the petition's characterization (App. 4a–6a). Five amicus entries, including states and bankruptcy specialists, reinforce that this is not simply a private litigant's isolated grievance. The Court requested a response after DBMP waived one. That is affirmative interest, though it does not establish four votes for review.

## Why denial remains more likely

The majority stresses that the appeal concerns individual stay-relief motions, not dismissal of the Chapter 11 case, eligibility for section 524(g), or a direct challenge to the nondebtor preliminary injunction (App. 9a–10a). It also affirms under the Robbins balancing factors and emphasizes the evidentiary record and abuse-of-discretion posture (App. 10a–15a). Those are serious vehicle complications for a petition asking the Court to condemn the restructuring as such. A general disagreement about dismissal under section 1112(b), as in LTL, does not automatically resolve whether these claimants must obtain relief under section 362(d). The separate balancing discussion may complicate effective relief even if the Court revises the good-faith standard; I do not assume it is conclusively an independent ground.

The petition acknowledges recent unsuccessful attempts to obtain review of related Bestwall issues and distinguishes this narrower question (petition pp. 19–21). A targeted corpus lookup independently returned denial of Bestwall, No. 25-1013, on June 1, 2026, with one distribution. That is a different petition and legitimate pre-snapshot context, not this cell's outcome. I give the earlier denial modest negative weight, not precedential effect: its questions and vehicle differ. There is no provisioned linked lead case, and same-question.json contains no matches, so I do not assume a hold or an intervening-decision GVR.

Together these considerations support a meaningful chance of review but not an even chance. My largest uncertainty is whether the eventual response portrays the good-faith issue as cleanly dispositive or as entangled with the unchallenged balancing and limited relief sought. The record contains only the petitioners' cert-stage advocacy, so confidence in their characterization of a square split is appropriately discounted.
