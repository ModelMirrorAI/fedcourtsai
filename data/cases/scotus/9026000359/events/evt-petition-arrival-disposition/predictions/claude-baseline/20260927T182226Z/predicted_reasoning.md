# Forecast: what the Court will do with No. 26-359

**Rubicon Real Estate Holdings, LLC v. City of Pontiac** (CA6, No. 25-1631, published opinion of June 18, 2026, panel Griffin, Larsen, Readler). Paid petition docketed September 16, 2026; response due October 16, 2026. Arrival moment: the docket shows **zero distributions**, no CVSG, and no response yet — that is the moment's definition, and every claim below is stated from that state.

## Disposition

The petition will be **denied**. P(any grant, including GVR) = 0.01.

## Distribution and relists (from zero distributions)

- The petition will be distributed at least once (the `relist-increment` claim from a zero-distribution state): ~0.96. The only routes that avoid a first distribution are a Rule 46 dismissal on settlement or a withdrawal, and nothing in this record suggests either.
- Once distributed, it will be **considered and denied at its first conference, with no relist**. The City is likely to waive its response (a municipality facing a scattershot error-correction petition usually does), which would put the petition on a **November 2026** conference list; if the City instead files a brief in opposition, possibly after an extension, expect a December 2026 or January 2027 conference. Either way, one distribution, then an order-list denial.

## CVSG

No call for the views of the Solicitor General: ~0.005. No federal party, no federal statute or program at issue, and the medical-marijuana setting is one the federal government has no interest in weighing in on at the cert stage.

## Which question, if any

If the Court took anything, it would not be any of the five "questions" as written — none states a legal question. The only reformulable issue is whether **prolonged administrative inaction on a permit application counts as a deprivation for procedural-due-process purposes** (the petition itself notes the Court has not decided the point). But the petition presents no circuit split on it, and the record (deficiency letters, conditional approvals issued within about 21 months, a state-court declaratory judgment already obtained) makes the case a poor vehicle for it. The substantive-due-process "shock the conscience" point and the Takings "extraordinary delay" point are both fact-bound applications of settled law; the First Amendment retaliation point is a Monell final-policymaker question decided under Pembaur and Praprotnik with no claimed split.

## Summary disposition route (conditional on a grant)

Conditional on the Court granting at all, the likelier route is a **GVR** rather than plenary review: ~0.6. There is no plausible plenary vehicle in these five questions, so a grant would almost have to come from an intervening decision this Term touching permit-delay takings or municipal-liability doctrine, followed by a hold and a GVR. I know of no such pending merits case on the OT2026 docket, which is why the unconditional grant number is so low.

## Dissent from denial

No dissent or statement respecting denial: P(some Justice writes | denial) ~0.02. Property-rights statements do appear occasionally from Justices Thomas or Gorsuch, but on petitions that isolate a clean doctrinal question, which this one does not.
