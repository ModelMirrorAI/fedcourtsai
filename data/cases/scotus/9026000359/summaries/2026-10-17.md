## In brief

A real estate developer asks the Court to revive its lawsuit against a Michigan city over delayed permits for a marijuana-facility development.

## What happened

Rubicon Real Estate Holdings bought property in Pontiac, Michigan, intending to lease it to medical marijuana growers and processors after securing a zoning change. City officials, including the city clerk, delayed approving licenses for Rubicon's prospective tenants, partly over a dispute about whether such businesses could operate outside a designated zoning overlay. The tenants eventually withdrew, and the project collapsed. Rubicon, along with its principal Joseph Brown and his separate design firm, sued the city and the clerk, alleging due process, takings, equal protection, and First Amendment retaliation violations. The district court granted summary judgment to the city and clerk on all claims, and the Sixth Circuit affirmed.

## What the Court is being asked

Rubicon asks the Court to decide whether the lower courts applied the wrong standards in rejecting its claims: whether it had a protected property interest in operating marijuana facilities on its land, whether it was denied adequate procedural protections before losing that interest, whether the city's conduct was arbitrary enough to violate substantive due process, whether the permitting delays amounted to an uncompensated taking of its property, and whether the city can be held responsible for a mayor's alleged retaliation against Rubicon's principal for suing the city.

## Where it stands

The petition was filed, the city waived its right to respond, and the case was distributed for the Court's November 6, 2026 conference. No decision on whether to hear the case has yet been recorded.

## What each outcome would mean

Rubicon, as petitioner, is asking the Court to take up the case. If the Court grants review, it would hear full arguments and decide the legal questions itself, without yet determining which side is right. If the Court denies the petition, the Sixth Circuit's decision affirming judgment for the city and the clerk would stand, leaving Rubicon's claims dismissed. A denial would not mean the Court agrees with the lower court's reasoning, only that the case proceeds no further.
