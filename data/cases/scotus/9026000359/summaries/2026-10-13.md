## In brief

A real estate developer asks the Court to revive its lawsuit against a Michigan city over delayed marijuana-business permits that it says doomed its development project.

## What happened

Rubicon Real Estate Holdings bought property in Pontiac, Michigan, planning to lease it to medical marijuana growers and processors after securing a zoning change. City officials, including the city clerk, delayed approving licenses for Rubicon's prospective tenants, partly over disagreement about where such businesses could operate. The tenants eventually withdrew, and the project collapsed. Rubicon, along with its principal Joseph Brown and his separate design company, sued the city and the clerk, claiming violations of due process, equal protection, takings, and First Amendment rights. The district court granted summary judgment to the city and clerk on all claims, and the Sixth Circuit affirmed.

## What the Court is being asked

The petition asks the Court to review whether the appeals court wrongly rejected Rubicon's claims that it had a protected property interest in operating marijuana facilities on its rezoned land, that delays in processing permits violated due process, that the delays amounted to an uncompensated taking of property, and that city officials could be held responsible for pressuring a third party to cut ties with Brown's company after Rubicon sued the city.

## Where it stands

The petition was filed and the respondents waived their right to file a response. The docket does not yet show any scheduling for conference or further action.

## What each outcome would mean

Rubicon and the other petitioners are asking the Court to take up the case. If the Court grants the petition, it would hear the case and decide the legal questions itself, deciding nothing in advance about who should win. If the Court denies the petition, the Sixth Circuit's decision affirming judgment for the City of Pontiac and the city clerk would stand, leaving Rubicon's claims dismissed.
