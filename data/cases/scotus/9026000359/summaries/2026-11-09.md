## In brief

The Supreme Court declined to hear a real estate developer's bid to revive constitutional claims against a city over delayed marijuana-business permits.

## What happened

Rubicon Real Estate Holdings bought a Pontiac, Michigan property hoping to lease it to medical marijuana businesses. The city rezoned the property, but the city clerk delayed approving licenses for Rubicon's prospective tenants, who eventually walked away, collapsing the deal. Rubicon, along with its manager Joseph Brown and his separate design company, sued the city and the clerk, also claiming the city retaliated against Brown's design business after Rubicon sued. A federal district court granted summary judgment to the city and clerk on all claims, and the Sixth Circuit Court of Appeals affirmed, finding no constitutional violation.

## What the Court is being asked

The petition asks the Court to decide whether the lower court was wrong to reject the developer's claims that the city's delays violated its property rights, denied it fair procedures, amounted to arbitrary government conduct, took its property without compensation, and unlawfully retaliated against it for suing the city.

## Where it stands

The city waived its right to respond to the petition. The case was distributed for the Court's November 6, 2026 conference, and the Court denied the petition on November 9, 2026.

## What each outcome would mean

Rubicon and its co-plaintiffs, as petitioners, asked the Court to overturn the Sixth Circuit's ruling in favor of Pontiac and the city clerk. By denying the petition, the Court leaves the Sixth Circuit's decision in place, meaning the lower courts' dismissal of all the developer's claims stands and the case is over. The denial does not mean the Court agreed with the lower court's reasoning, only that it chose not to review the case further.
