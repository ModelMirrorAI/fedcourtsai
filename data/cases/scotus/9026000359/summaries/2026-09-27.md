## In brief

A property developer asks the Court to revive its lawsuit against the City of Pontiac, Michigan, over delays in approving medical marijuana business permits.

## What happened

Rubicon Real Estate Holdings bought property in Pontiac intending to lease it to medical marijuana businesses, after the city rezoned the land to allow that use. Rubicon's prospective tenants applied for permits, but the city clerk, Garland Doyle, delayed processing the applications and maintained the site was not properly zoned. The tenants eventually terminated their leases. Rubicon also says the city pressured an unrelated developer to cut ties with a design firm owned by Rubicon's principal, in retaliation for suing the city. A state court had ordered the city to comply with the rezoning agreement, but Rubicon says the damage was already done. The federal district court granted summary judgment to the city, and the Sixth Circuit affirmed.

## What the Court is being asked

Rubicon argues the lower courts wrongly rejected its claims that the city violated its property rights, denied it fair procedures before harming its interests, took arbitrary action against it, effectively took its property without compensation through prolonged delay, and retaliated against it for going to court. It asks the Court to decide whether these rulings conflict with existing legal principles governing such claims.

## Where it stands

The petition was filed on September 14, 2026, and a response from the city is due October 16, 2026. The Court has not yet acted on the petition.

## What each outcome would mean

Rubicon and the other petitioners are asking the Court to take up the case. If the Court grants the petition, it will hear full arguments and decide the legal questions itself, without presuming either side is right. If the Court denies the petition, the Sixth Circuit's ruling against Rubicon stands, meaning the city's win in the lower courts remains in place and Rubicon's claims stay dismissed. A denial would not mean the Court agrees the lower court was correct, only that the case ends there.
