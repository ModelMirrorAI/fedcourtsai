## What happened

Rubicon Real Estate Holdings bought property in Pontiac, Michigan, intending to lease it to medical marijuana businesses, after the city rezoned the land to allow that use. The petition says city officials, including the city clerk, later refused to process tenants' license applications, causing tenants to walk away and the project to collapse. Rubicon and a related design company sued the city and the clerk, raising constitutional claims. A federal district court granted the city summary judgment, meaning it ruled against Rubicon without a trial, and the Sixth Circuit Court of Appeals affirmed that ruling on June 18, 2026. Rubicon and its co-plaintiffs then asked the Supreme Court to review that decision.

## What the Court is being asked

The petition raises several issues together: whether Rubicon had a property right in using its land for the approved purpose, whether the city denied it fair procedures before that right was effectively taken away, whether the city's conduct was arbitrary enough to violate a different constitutional protection against unfair government action, whether the delay in processing licenses amounted to an uncompensated taking of property, and whether the city can be held responsible for a mayor's alleged pressure on a business partner in retaliation for Rubicon having sued the city.
