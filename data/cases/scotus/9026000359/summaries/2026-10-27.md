## In brief

A real estate developer asks the Court to revive its civil rights lawsuit against a Michigan city over delayed permits for a marijuana business development.

## What happened

Rubicon Real Estate Holdings bought property in Pontiac, Michigan, intending to lease it to medical marijuana growers and processors after securing zoning changes and permits. City officials, including the city clerk, delayed approving licenses for Rubicon's prospective tenants, partly over a dispute about whether the zoning allowed such uses outside a designated overlay district. The tenants eventually withdrew, and the project collapsed. Rubicon, its manager Joseph Brown, and Brown's separate design company sued the city and the clerk, alleging due process, takings, equal protection, and First Amendment retaliation violations. The district court granted summary judgment to the defendants on all claims, and the Sixth Circuit affirmed.

## What the Court is being asked

The petition asks the Court to review whether the lower courts were wrong to reject the claims that the city and its clerk violated Rubicon's property rights by delaying permit approvals without adequate process, whether that delay amounted to a government taking of property without compensation, and whether the city can be held responsible for a mayor's alleged retaliation against Brown's design company for Rubicon's earlier lawsuit against the city.

## Where it stands

The city and clerk waived their right to respond to the petition, and the petition was distributed for the Court's conference on November 6, 2026. The docket does not yet show any action taken at that conference.

## What each outcome would mean

Rubicon and the other petitioners are asking the Court to take up the case and reverse the Sixth Circuit's ruling, which favored the city and its clerk. If the Court grants the petition, it would agree to hear the case and decide these questions itself, without yet deciding who is right. If the Court denies the petition, the Sixth Circuit's decision affirming summary judgment for the city and clerk stands, and Rubicon's lawsuit remains dismissed.
