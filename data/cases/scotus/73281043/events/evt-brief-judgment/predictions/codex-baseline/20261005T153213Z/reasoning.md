## Why above that anchor

Texas has the stronger structural argument. Its brief, printed pp. 13–27, separates the statutory route for a new constitutional rule from the route for newly discovered facts and treats an existing rule's lack of likely success as different from its unavailability. Atkins existed before Johnson's first federal application. The prediction is that later diagnostic developments will not reset the statutory legal gateway. The parallel language in Section 2255(h)(2) reinforces a rule-centered reading, although the Court need not formally decide a federal prisoner's case here.

The respondent expressly agrees that the statutory modifier attaches to **rule**, not **claim** (respondent's brief, p. 12). Consequently, the last-antecedent argument alone cannot resolve the real disagreement. His stronger answer is relational availability: an existing rule is not practically usable when the claimant has no possible basis for relief. His reliance on Ross and Booth, his objection that substantive constitutional rules necessarily operate on facts, and his characterization of the DSM change as an external diagnostic framework rather than newly discovered personal facts make the case meaningfully closer than a simple request for equitable dispensation (pp. 12–21, 28–33). I nevertheless expect a majority to distinguish access to a legal rule from eligibility for relief under it, and to reject importing the administrative-remedies availability inquiry into this successive-petition gateway.

I verified the general analogue **Jones v. Hendrix, 599 U.S. 465, 477–78 (2023)** through CourtListener, including the majority's treatment of the enumerated successive-motion conditions and the six-Justice lineup. That supports, but does not control, this forecast: Jones concerned the federal saving clause, while Johnson argues that he satisfies an enumerated gateway itself. It would be incorrect to treat Jones as having already decided this precise statutory phrase. Its restrictive structural reasoning is why I move above the pooled merits anchor rather than simply repeating it.
