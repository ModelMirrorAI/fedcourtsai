# Predicted reasoning — Guerrero v. Johnson, No. 25-1003 (evt-brief-judgment)

Forecast of what the Court will do with the judgment below, made from the
briefed record (both merits briefs on file, argument set for November 4, 2026).

## Judgment class and vote

**Reversed.** The Court will reverse the Fifth Circuit's judgment affirming the
denial of the Director's motion to dismiss Johnson's successive petition, and
remand for further proceedings under the correct reading of
§ 2244(b)(2)(A). Expect **7–2**: Roberts, Thomas, Alito, Kagan, Gorsuch,
Kavanaugh, and Barrett in the majority; Sotomayor and Jackson in dissent. The
closest call on the lineup is Kagan, who joined a unanimous Court for Texas on
§ 2244(b) in *Rivers v. Guerrero* and has written the Court's leading
"available means capable of use" opinion in *Ross v. Blake*; the textual
concession in Johnson's own brief (that "previously unavailable" modifies
"rule") makes her joining the majority somewhat more likely than not. A
lineup one vote narrower (6–3) is the main alternative.

Not a scoreable claim, but for the record: the opinion is most likely assigned
to Barrett, Kavanaugh, or Thomas; Sotomayor is the likeliest dissenting author,
and a short Jackson concurrence or separate dissent on the capital context is
plausible.

## Which question the Court reaches

The Court takes the question presented as written and decides the meaning of
"a new rule of constitutional law, made retroactive … that was previously
unavailable." It will not reach whether Johnson is intellectually disabled,
whether equitable tolling was proper, or whether his claim could satisfy
§ 2244(b)(2)(B). The interlocutory posture will not stop it: the certified
question was answered below by applying *Cathey*, and the Court granted over
Johnson's vehicle objections.

## The ground the majority rests on

The majority will read "previously unavailable" as modifying "rule," and will
hold that a rule is available once this Court has announced it, whether or not
a particular prisoner's claim under it would then have succeeded. *Atkins*
(2002) predates Johnson's crime, his conviction, and his 2011 federal petition,
so it was not "previously unavailable" to him. The 2013 DSM-5 revision and the
2019 evaluations are new facts, and new-facts claims travel through
§ 2244(b)(2)(B), whose clear-and-convincing and innocence-of-the-offense
requirements the Fifth Circuit's "some possibility of merit" test would let a
petitioner bypass. Expect the opinion to lean on the structural reading of (A)
and (B) as separate channels (in the manner of *Jones v. Hendrix*), on the
parallel text of § 2255(h)(2), and on AEDPA's displacement of the pre-AEDPA
abuse-of-the-writ inquiry. The Eleventh Circuit's *In re Bowles* will be cited
with approval. The Court will distinguish *Ross v. Blake* on the ground that a
remedy's practical availability under the PLRA is a different question from
whether a legal rule has been announced.

## Breadth

Categorical, not case-bound: a rule that availability is a legal and temporal
question for every successive petition under (A) and (h)(2), with no
case-specific futility inquiry. The majority will reserve, probably in a
footnote, the scenario the Director's own brief concedes, where a new rule is
announced while a prior petition is still pending and amendment is
impracticable.

## Separate writings and procedural exits

A DIG is live but unlikely (roughly one in twenty): Johnson's party-presentation
argument that the Director reframed the question between the certified
question, the petition, and the merits brief has some force, but the core
question (what "previously unavailable" means) has been constant and the Court
granted knowing the posture. An equally divided Court is not in play (no
recusal). The dissent will argue that "available" carries its ordinary
practical meaning, that an *Atkins* claim foreclosed by then-governing
diagnostic criteria was not usable in any real sense, and that the majority's
rule leaves a possibly intellectually disabled prisoner facing execution with
no federal forum.

## Timing

Argued November 4, 2026; decision most likely between February and May 2027.
