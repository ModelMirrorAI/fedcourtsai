I adjust substantially above 69.8% because Texas obtained review of an interlocutory ruling that preserves a successive capital habeas claim, the text and structure favor Texas, and the six-Justice conservative bloc has previously read AEDPA's successive-review limits strictly.

The petition's strongest point is grammatical and structural. Section 2244(b)(2)(A) speaks of a “new rule of constitutional law ... that was previously unavailable”; Atkins was decided before Johnson's crime, conviction, and first federal petition. The later DSM-5 evidence goes to the factual viability of his Atkins claim. Congress separately dealt with new factual predicates in Section 2244(b)(2)(B), whose innocence requirement Johnson's sentencing-only claim cannot meet. That makes the Fifth Circuit's “some possibility of merit” gloss vulnerable as a means of moving a new-facts claim into the new-law gateway.

The opposition identifies real downside risk. It argues that “available” should be practical, that all relevant circuits use a feasibility inquiry and differ only in application, and that the case is interlocutory and presents a question different from the one certified. It also emphasizes that the DSM-5 arrived while Johnson's first federal case was pending and that then-existing Texas standards made an Atkins claim futile. Those points support some chance of affirmance or a DIG, but the Court granted review after the vehicle objections were squarely presented, reducing their force as disposition risks.

The provisioned petition was marked truncated at 100 pages, but its petition section, question presented, party arguments, and relevant lower-court reasoning were available; the truncation cuts off part of the appended record rather than the merits of the cert-stage briefing. The brief in opposition and questions-presented extract were complete.

For vote allocation, Jones v. Hendrix supplies the closest broad directional signal: Roberts, Thomas, Alito, Gorsuch, Kavanaugh, and Barrett joined a strict reading of AEDPA's limits, while Sotomayor, Kagan, and Jackson dissented. Bowe v. Here that method points toward Texas. I therefore forecast the Jones lineup, while recognizing that Kagan or Jackson could join a text-and-structure majority.
