# Forecast: Guerrero v. Johnson, No. 25-1003 (merits, moment: grant)

**Judgment class: reversed.** The Court will reverse the Fifth Circuit's
judgment and hold that Dexter Johnson's successive habeas application does not
satisfy 28 U.S.C. § 2244(b)(2)(A), because "previously unavailable" refers to
the legal availability of the new rule of constitutional law itself — and
Atkins v. Virginia (2002) was available to Johnson when he filed his initial
federal petition in 2011. The Court will repudiate the Fifth Circuit's In re
Cathey "some possibility of merit" gloss (adopted also by the Ninth Circuit in
Muñoz) and side with the Eleventh and Fourth Circuits: futility of a claim at
the time of an earlier petition does not make the rule it relies on
unavailable. Expect the opinion to lean on the reasoning of Jones v. Hendrix
(2023) — that circuit precedent foreclosing a claim does not make the remedy
unavailable — and Bousley v. United States (futility is not cause), and to note
that the identical language of § 2255(h)(2) carries the same meaning. An
outright reversal is likelier than a vacatur-and-remand: the certified
interlocutory question is purely legal, and answering it against Johnson
resolves the gatekeeping issue; at most the Court remands for dismissal of the
application.

**Vote lineup: 6–3.** Roberts, Thomas, Alito, Gorsuch, Kavanaugh, and Barrett
in the majority; Sotomayor, Kagan, and Jackson in dissent. This is the lineup
of Shinn v. Ramirez, Shoop v. Twyford, Brown v. Davenport, Jones v. Hendrix,
and Thornell v. Jones — the Court's recent AEDPA/capital-habeas cases have
been remarkably stable on exactly this axis, and this case presents the same
strict-text-versus-equitable-practicality divide. A 7–2 (Kagan joining a
narrow majority on plain-text grounds) is the most plausible alternative;
9–0 is unlikely because the dissenters will see the Cathey rule as the only
practical reading for prisoners whose claims were factually hopeless when
first available. Authorship: Thomas is the most likely majority writer (he
wrote Shinn and Jones v. Hendrix); Alito and Kavanaugh are next. Sotomayor is
the likely dissent author in a capital case. (Authorship and writing roles
resolve against nothing today; they are stated as context, not as the
scoreable part.)

**Question taken and question left.** The Court will decide the QP as granted
— the meaning of "previously unavailable" — and will not reach whether
Johnson is in fact intellectually disabled, whether Moore v. Texas is itself a
"new rule ... made retroactive," or any other element of § 2244(b)(2)(A). The
majority will treat the merits-at-the-time inquiry as irrelevant to
availability, categorically.

**Timing.** Cert was granted June 15, 2026 (OT2025 grant, argued in OT2026):
expect argument between October and December 2026 and a decision between
February and June 2027, most likely in the spring.

**Procedural exits.** A DIG is conceivable but unlikely (~3–4%): the
interlocutory posture the BIO pressed was known at the grant, the certified
question is cleanly presented, and no factual development can moot it. An
equally divided affirmance requires a recusal for which the record shows no
basis. A summary disposition will not occur — the case is set for briefing
and argument (the grant order carried no judgment).

**Breadth.** The holding will be categorical — availability of a rule is a
legal question, assessed rule-by-rule, without a case-specific merits or
evidence inquiry — and will control both § 2244(b)(2)(A) and § 2255(h)(2),
abrogating In re Cathey (CA5) and Muñoz (CA9).
