# Predicted Court Reasoning — *Guerrero v. Johnson* (No. 25-1003)

## Judgment and Vote Lineup

The Court will **reverse** the judgment of the United States Court of Appeals for the Fifth Circuit (`judgment: reversed`, `probability: 0.89`).

The expected vote lineup is **7–2** to reverse:
- **Majority (7):** Chief Justice Roberts and Justices Thomas, Alito, Kagan, Gorsuch, Kavanaugh, and Barrett.
- **Dissent (2):** Justices Sotomayor and Jackson.

A broader **8–1** or **9–0** reversal is a realistic secondary possibility if Justices Sotomayor and Jackson conclude that the plain text of 28 U.S.C. § 2244(b)(2)(A) cannot sustain the Fifth Circuit's "some possibility of merit" standard and instead concur or concur in the judgment while emphasizing alternative avenues (such as clemency, original habeas, or non-successive ripeness principles). Conversely, an affirmance or a dismissal as improvidently granted (DIG) is unlikely: no vehicle defect or jurisdictional impediment detained the Court at oral argument, and the certified interlocutory appeal under 28 U.S.C. § 1292(b) squarely presents the statutory question.

## Question Reached and Doctrinal Ground (`majority-ground`)

The Court will reach the single Question Presented and reverse on the plain text, grammar, and structure of 28 U.S.C. § 2244(b)(2)(A).

1. **Grammatical Referent of "Previously Unavailable":** Under ordinary rules of grammar and the rule of the last antecedent, the restrictive relative clause "that was previously unavailable" in § 2244(b)(2)(A) ("the applicant shows that the claim relies on a new rule of constitutional law, made retroactive to cases on collateral review by the Supreme Court, that was previously unavailable") modifies the nearest noun phrase—"a new rule of constitutional law, made retroactive to cases on collateral review by the Supreme Court"—not the more distant noun "claim" in the introductory clause. That reading is reinforced by the parallel provision for federal prisoners in 28 U.S.C. § 2255(h)(2), which authorizes a second or successive motion certified to contain "a new rule of constitutional law, made retroactive to cases on collateral review by the Supreme Court, that was previously unavailable" without using the word "claim" at all.
2. **Rejection of the "Some Possibility of Merit" / Futility Exception:** Because "previously unavailable" refers to the legal availability of the retroactive constitutional rule announced by the Supreme Court, a rule is available once the Supreme Court has announced it (and made it retroactive, where required, under *Tyler v. Cain*, 533 U.S. 656 (2001)) prior to the conclusion of the prisoner's initial federal habeas proceeding. The Fifth Circuit erred in *In re Cathey*, 857 F.3d 221 (5th Cir. 2017), and in the decision below by asking whether the prisoner's *claim* had "some possibility of merit" at the time of the initial petition in light of later-emerging clinical standards (the DSM-5) or subsequent non-retroactive decisions (*Hall v. Florida*, 572 U.S. 701 (2014); *Moore v. Texas*, 581 U.S. 1 (2017)).
3. **Statutory Structure of § 2244(b)(2) and *Teague* Principles:** The majority will explain that Congress divided second-or-successive claims into two distinct channels: § 2244(b)(2)(A) for new retroactive constitutional rules announced by the Supreme Court, and § 2244(b)(2)(B) for newly discovered factual predicates meeting a stringent clear-and-convincing actual-innocence standard. Allowing changes in diagnostic manuals (such as the 2013 publication of the DSM-5), newly obtained expert evaluations, or non-retroactive procedural decisions like *Moore* to re-trigger § 2244(b)(2)(A) under the banner of an older retroactive rule (*Atkins v. Virginia*, 536 U.S. 304 (2002)) collapses the statutory distinction between legal and factual developments and circumvents *Tyler v. Cain*. Moreover, because *Atkins* was decided in 2002—five years before Respondent's 2007 trial and nine years before his 2011 initial federal habeas petition—*Atkins* was neither "previously unavailable" nor a "new rule" as to Respondent under *Teague v. Lane*, 489 U.S. 288 (1989).

## Breadth of the Holding (`ground-breadth`)

The Court will issue a **categorical** statutory holding across § 2244(b)(2)(A): a constitutional rule announced by the Supreme Court before the conclusion of a prisoner's prior federal habeas petition was not "previously unavailable" within the meaning of § 2244(b)(2)(A), regardless of whether a claim invoking that rule would have lacked evidentiary support or failed under then-prevailing circuit or state precedent.

At the same time, the majority opinion will keep its holding within the bounds of § 2244(b)(2)(A) rather than sweeping more broadly than necessary:
- The Court will resolve the case primarily on the "previously unavailable" requirement (and the closely linked definition of a "new rule" relative to the petitioner's litigation timeline), without needing to unsettle how *Tyler v. Cain* applies when a genuinely new rule is announced after an initial petition but made retroactive later.
- The Court will decline to adjudicate whether an eighth-amendment execution challenge premised on a change in diagnostic standards or a prisoner's condition could ever be framed as a *non-successive* petition under *Panetti v. Quarterman*, 551 U.S. 930 (2007), and *Ford v. Wainwright*, 477 U.S. 399 (1986), noting that Respondent proceeded through the § 2244(b) second-or-successive authorization process and did not preserve a *Panetti* theory below.
