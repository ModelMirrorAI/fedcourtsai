# Forecast of the Court's decision

The Court will **reverse the Fifth Circuit and remand**, by **6–3**, with Roberts, Thomas, Alito, Gorsuch, Kavanaugh, and Barrett in the majority and Sotomayor, Kagan, and Jackson dissenting. The probability that the judgment is disturbed, counting a vacatur as well as reversal, is **94%**. Reversal is the modal formal judgment; vacatur and remand is the principal alternative.

The majority will hold that section 2244(b)(2)(A) concerns a **previously unavailable constitutional rule**, not merely a claim that lacked a prior chance of success. Atkins already supplied the constitutional prohibition before Johnson's first federal habeas application. A later change in diagnostic standards does not itself make that rule previously unavailable. The Court will reject the Fifth Circuit's requirement of a previous possibility of merit as the measure of availability and explain that newly developed factual support cannot avoid Congress's separate restrictions on fact-based successive claims.

That holding will extend beyond Johnson's particular diagnosis: for rules announced before an initial federal application, lack of facts sufficient to win will not make the rule unavailable under this gateway. The majority will **reserve the exact cutoff for a new rule announced while a first federal habeas application is pending**. It will not decide whether Johnson is intellectually disabled, whether he otherwise merits substantive habeas relief, or whether Moore independently qualifies as a retroactive new rule. It will not overrule Atkins or Panetti; the competency-at-execution ripeness setting will remain distinguishable from this claim about disability at the time of the offense.

The new-rule requirement provides reinforcing or alternative reasoning: a decision already governing before a prisoner's conviction became final is not a newly announced rule relative to that prisoner merely because a later medical development makes it more helpful. This route could attract Kagan to the judgment even if she rejects the majority's principal availability analysis; the committed modal lineup nevertheless places her in dissent.

**Thomas will deliver the opinion of the Court**, with Barrett the next most likely author. **Jackson will write the principal dissent**, joined by Sotomayor and Kagan in the modal forecast. That dissent will emphasize the practical meaning of availability, the incorporation of clinical standards into Atkins, and the loss of an effective first opportunity to present the claim. Sotomayor may also write separately on ripeness and the protected constitutional category; separate-writer probabilities allow more than one dissenting author. An additional majority-side concurrence is less likely than a single statutory opinion.

The Court will reject the request to dismiss the writ as improvidently granted. The challenged panel opinion expressly reaffirmed the availability test, so the interlocutory posture and earlier wording of the certified question will not prevent a merits resolution. No recusal-based equal division is forecast. A signed decision is expected during the remainder of the October 2026 Term, most plausibly between February and June 2027; that timing is contextual rather than a scored claim.
