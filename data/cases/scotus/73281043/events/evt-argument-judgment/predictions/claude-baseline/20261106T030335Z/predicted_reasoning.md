# Forecast: Guerrero v. Johnson, No. 25-1003 (argued Nov. 4, 2026)

**Judgment: reversed.** The Court reverses the Fifth Circuit's judgment, which affirmed the district court's refusal to dismiss Johnson's successive Atkins application. It remands with the result that the application must be dismissed under § 2244(b)(4).

**Vote: 6–3.** Roberts, Thomas, Alito, Gorsuch, Kavanaugh and Barrett are in the majority. Sotomayor, Kagan and Jackson dissent. The vote I am least sure of is Kagan's. At argument she accepted that "new rule" means new *as to the prisoner*, which is the petitioner's reading. But she pressed hard on an "Atkins II" theory: because Atkins borrows its standard from clinical criteria, a major DSM revision changes the legal rule itself. I expect her to join the dissent or write it, and I don't expect her to join the majority.

**Ground.** The majority reaches the question presented, "previously unavailable," and adopts the Eleventh and Fourth Circuits' reading. A rule is available once this Court has announced it and the prisoner could invoke it, whether or not a claim under it would have succeeded. The "some possibility of merit" test from *In re Cathey* is rejected as a futility exception the text does not support. The Court treats the DSM-5 as at most a new fact. Under § 2244(b)(2)(B)(ii) a new fact can open the gateway only if it goes to guilt of the offense, so it cannot help a sentence-only Atkins claim. The opinion probably also notes, or holds in the alternative, that Atkins is not a "new" rule as to Johnson, because it was decided years before his crime and conviction. Justice Kavanaugh invited that narrower route, and the United States urged the Court to address both prongs.

**Breadth.** The rule is general and covers § 2244 and § 2255(h)(2) alike. The opinion explicitly leaves open what happens when a new rule is announced while the first federal petition is pending. Justices Kavanaugh, Barrett and Gorsuch each probed that question, and both Texas and the SG said it need not be decided.

**Writers.** The November sitting's assignments are unknown. Thomas opened the questioning and has long written on AEDPA. Barrett asked how to write a narrow opinion. Kavanaugh and Alito are also plausible authors. I expect a principal dissent by Sotomayor or Jackson. Both pressed the Panetti/Ford analogy and the "one fair shot" reading of the statute, and a second dissent is possible. Kagan may write separately on the Atkins II theory. A short concurrence from Thomas or Gorsuch on the (b)(2)(B) "innocent of the death penalty" point is possible but not likely.

**Procedural exits.** A DIG is very unlikely. The case comes up on a § 1292(b) interlocutory appeal from the district court, not on the authorization order that § 2244(b)(3)(E) shields from certiorari, so jurisdiction is clean and nobody raised a vehicle problem at argument. No recusal is noted, so an equally divided Court is not in play.
