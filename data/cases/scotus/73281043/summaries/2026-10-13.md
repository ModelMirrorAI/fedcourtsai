## In brief

A Texas prison official asks the Court to decide whether a death row inmate's long-delayed intellectual-disability claim was properly allowed to proceed in federal court.

## What happened

Dexter Johnson was sentenced to death in 2007 for a murder committed in 2006. His trial expert testified his IQ was too high to qualify as intellectually disabled under then-current standards, so he did not raise a claim under Atkins v. Virginia, which bars executing intellectually disabled people. After a 2013 diagnostic manual revised how intellectual disability is assessed, Johnson sought to raise an Atkins claim for the first time, in a second federal habeas petition, shortly before a scheduled execution. The Fifth Circuit authorized the filing, reasoning the claim had been "previously unavailable." The district court agreed the claim could proceed, and the Fifth Circuit affirmed on an interlocutory appeal, prompting Texas's petition.

## What the Court is being asked

Federal law generally bars prisoners from filing a second federal habeas petition unless, among other exceptions, their claim relies on a new constitutional rule that courts have said applies retroactively and that was "previously unavailable" to them. The Court is asked whether a rule counts as previously unavailable only based on when the Supreme Court announced it, or whether it can also be considered unavailable if a claim based on it had no realistic chance of succeeding earlier due to the diagnostic standards in place at the time.

## Where it stands

The Court granted review, the case was argued on November 4, 2026, and it now awaits decision.

## What each outcome would mean

Texas, as the petitioner, is asking the Court to reverse the Fifth Circuit's decision and hold that Johnson's claim was not previously unavailable, which would mean AEDPA barred his second petition from proceeding. If the Court affirms, Johnson's Atkins claim would continue toward adjudication in the lower courts under the Fifth Circuit's approach. If the Court reverses, Johnson's successive petition would be barred, ending that avenue for his claim. The Court could also vacate and send the case back without deciding the question for either side.
