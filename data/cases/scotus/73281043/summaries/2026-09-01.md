## What happened

Dexter Johnson is a Texas death row inmate convicted of a 2006 murder. He did not raise a claim that he is intellectually disabled, and therefore ineligible for execution, in his first federal habeas petition. Years later, after diagnostic standards for intellectual disability changed, he sought to raise that claim in a second federal habeas petition. The Fifth Circuit allowed the claim to proceed, reasoning that the claim had no realistic chance of succeeding earlier, so it counted as "previously unavailable." On an interlocutory appeal (an appeal of one issue before the whole case is finished), the Fifth Circuit stood by that approach, and it declined to reconsider the case as a full court. Texas prison officials, represented by the state's prison director, ask the Supreme Court to review that ruling.

## What the Court is being asked

The law limits when a prisoner can bring a second habeas petition, generally allowing new claims only if they rely on a genuinely new constitutional rule that could not have been raised before. The Court is asked whether a claim counts as previously unavailable merely because it would have failed on the facts as they existed earlier, or whether it counts as unavailable only if the rule itself did not yet exist or could not have been invoked at all. Johnson answers that the lower court applied the same practical standard other courts use and reached the right result on unusual facts; he also argues the case is a poor vehicle because it comes up before a final ruling on his underlying claim.

## Where it stands

The Supreme Court granted the petition for review, and the case has been briefed and scheduled for argument on November 4, 2026. The government and several other parties have filed friend-of-the-court briefs supporting the petitioner.
