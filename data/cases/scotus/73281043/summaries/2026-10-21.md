## In brief

The Court heard Texas's challenge to a Fifth Circuit ruling that let a death-row prisoner pursue a second federal habeas petition raising a claim that intellectual disability bars his execution.

## What happened

Dexter Johnson was convicted of capital murder in Texas and sentenced to death. His first federal habeas petition did not raise a claim that he is intellectually disabled and therefore ineligible for execution under the Supreme Court's Atkins decision. Years later, after a revised psychiatric diagnostic manual changed how intellectual disability is assessed, Johnson sought authorization to file a second federal habeas petition raising such a claim. The Fifth Circuit authorized it, reasoning that the claim had been "previously unavailable" because it lacked any realistic chance of success under the older diagnostic standards. A federal district court agreed Johnson could proceed, and the Fifth Circuit affirmed that ruling on an interlocutory appeal, over several judges' dissent from rehearing en banc.

## What the Court is being asked

Federal law sharply limits when a second habeas petition can raise a new claim, allowing it mainly when the claim relies on a new constitutional rule that was not available earlier. The Court is asked whether a rule counts as previously unavailable when a claim based on it could have been raised in an earlier federal petition but would have failed at that time, or whether unavailability depends only on whether the Supreme Court had already announced the rule.

## Where it stands

The Court granted review, and the case was argued on November 4, 2026. It now awaits decision.

## What each outcome would mean

Texas, as petitioner, asked the Court to reverse the Fifth Circuit's ruling in Johnson's favor below. If the Court affirms, Johnson's interpretation prevails: his claim may proceed, and the case returns to the lower courts for further litigation over whether he is in fact intellectually disabled. If the Court reverses, Texas's position prevails: Johnson's second petition would be barred, cutting off this avenue for challenging his death sentence. If the Court vacates, the lower court's decision is set aside and the case returns for further proceedings without resolving which side is right.
