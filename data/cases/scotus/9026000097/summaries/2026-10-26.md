## In brief

Parents of children with disabilities ask the Supreme Court to revive their challenge to a Massachusetts rule limiting where private-school students can receive publicly funded special education services.

## What happened

Massachusetts gives all children with disabilities, including those in private schools, a right to special education services, but a state regulation says privately enrolled students can get publicly funded services only at a public school or other "neutral" site, not on their private school's campus. Parents of two children with disabilities say this rule forces them to choose between their children's services and their constitutional right to choose private schooling. A federal district court dismissed their lawsuit, and the First Circuit Court of Appeals affirmed, holding that a legal principle barring the government from penalizing people for exercising constitutional rights does not apply to the right to choose private school.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked whether a rule that prevents the government from pressuring people to give up a constitutional right by withholding a benefit also protects the right of parents to send their children to private school, or whether that protection is limited to other rights, such as religious freedom.

## Where it stands

The docket shows no action by the Court on the petition since it was distributed.

## What each outcome would mean

The Hellman and Harrison families, as petitioners, are asking the Court to reverse the First Circuit's ruling, which favored Massachusetts officials. If the Court grants the petition, it will hear the case and decide whether this legal principle protects the right to choose private schooling; a grant would not itself decide who wins. If the Court denies the petition, the First Circuit's decision stands, leaving the Massachusetts regulation in place and the families' claims dismissed.
