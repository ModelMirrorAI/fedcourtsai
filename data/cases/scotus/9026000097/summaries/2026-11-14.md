## In brief

Parents of private-school children with disabilities ask the Court to revive their challenge to a Massachusetts rule limiting where the state will deliver their children's special-education services.

## What happened

Massachusetts law entitles all children with disabilities, including those in private school, to publicly funded special-education services. But a state regulation requires that privately enrolled children receive those services at a public school or other neutral site, not on their private school's campus, a rule the state adopted to comply with a state constitutional ban on aiding private schools. Two families whose children attend private Jewish schools sued, arguing the rule unconstitutionally forces them to choose between their children's services and their right to choose private schooling. A federal district court dismissed the suit, and the First Circuit Court of Appeals affirmed, holding the regulation does not infringe that right.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked whether a rule against denying someone a government benefit because they have exercised a constitutional right applies to the right of parents to send their children to private school. The parents say the First Circuit wrongly treated that protection as limited to religious-liberty cases, while Massachusetts says the lower court correctly found no constitutional burden here at all.

## Where it stands

The petition was distributed for conference, after which the Court asked Massachusetts to respond; Massachusetts has now filed its brief opposing review, and the petition awaits the Court's decision on whether to hear the case.

## What each outcome would mean

The Hellman and Harrison families are asking the Court to take up and reverse the First Circuit's ruling, which left the Massachusetts regulation in place. If the Court grants review, it would hear full arguments on the question and decide whether the rule violates their constitutional rights, deciding nothing at this stage about who is right. If the Court denies review, the First Circuit's decision stands, leaving the regulation in effect and the families' lawsuit over.
