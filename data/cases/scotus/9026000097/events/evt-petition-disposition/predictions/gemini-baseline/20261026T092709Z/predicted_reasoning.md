## III. Doctrinal and Certiorari Analysis

Should the Court evaluate the petition for plenary review, three core certiorari criteria weigh heavily against a grant:

1. **Absence of a Genuine Circuit Split on the Question Presented**:
   The First Circuit's decision in *Hellman v. Craven*, 168 F.4th 255 (1st Cir. 2026), is consistent with the unanimous consensus of the federal courts of appeals that *Pierce v. Society of Sisters* and *Meyer v. Nebraska*, 262 U.S. 390 (1923), do not obligate states to subsidize private education or provide public special-education services on-site at private schools, and that neutral public-school enrollment conditions for state-funded special-education entitlements are subject to rational-basis review (e.g., *Gary S. v. Manchester School District*, 374 F.3d 15 (1st Cir. 2004); *Strout v. Albanese*, 178 F.3d 57 (1st Cir. 1999); *Swanson v. Guthrie Independent School District No. I-L*, 135 F.3d 694 (10th Cir. 1998)). Petitioners' claimed division of authority relies on pre-*Dobbs* abortion-funding and speech/takings unconstitutional-conditions decisions from other contexts rather than any appellate decision applying the unconstitutional conditions doctrine to invalidate a public-school enrollment requirement for state special-education services under *Pierce*.

2. **Mismatch Between *Carson*/*Espinoza*/*Trinity Lutheran* and Neutral Secular Conditions**:
   As Chief Judge Barron explained for the First Circuit panel, Massachusetts's Place Regulation (603 Mass. Code Regs. § 28.03(1)(b)(2)) applies neutrally to all private schools, religious and nonsectarian alike, and Petitioners intentionally brought no Free Exercise claim. In *Espinoza*, 591 U.S. at 487, and *Carson*, 596 U.S. at 779, the Court expressly reaffirmed that a state need not subsidize private education, holding only that once a state chooses to do so, it cannot disqualify schools solely because they are religious. Treating the mere withholding of in-kind public special-education services from private-school students as an unconstitutional penalty on the *Pierce* right would conflict with *Maher v. Roe*, 432 U.S. 464 (1977), *Harris v. McRae*, 448 U.S. 297 (1980), and *Rust v. Sullivan*, 500 U.S. 173 (1991), which distinguish between penalizing a constitutional right and declining to subsidize its exercise out of public funds.

3. **Procedural Posture (Waiver Without CFR)**:
   The most decisive institutional indicator is that Massachusetts waived its right to respond on September 24, 2026, and the petition was distributed for the October 23, 2026 conference without any call for a response. Absent a CFR, the petition will be denied.
