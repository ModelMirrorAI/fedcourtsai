# Forecast: Hellman v. Craven, No. 26-97 (cert petition)

**Disposition: denied (P(grant) ≈ 0.17).**

**Where the docket stands.** The petition was distributed once, for the
September 28, 2026 conference. Massachusetts had waived its response, but the
Court called for one on August 20, which took the petition off that
conference. Seven amicus filings came in at the cert stage, including one from
West Virginia, South Carolina and twelve other States. The response is due
November 10, 2026 (the Clerk granted part of the requested extension). No CVSG
has been called for.

- **Further distribution: near-certain (~0.96).** After the brief in
  opposition and the reply window, the petition will be distributed again,
  most likely for a conference in early December 2026. The only realistic way
  that fails is a withdrawal or settlement. I expect one or two distributions
  before a decision. If the Court relists after the first post-BIO
  conference, that would be the clearest signal of a grant.
- **CVSG: unlikely (~0.05).** The respondents are state officials and the
  benefit comes from state special-education law, so no federal program
  interest calls for the Solicitor General's views. The IDEA's provisions on
  parentally placed private-school children are context only, not the claim.
- **If granted:** the Court would take the single question as written:
  whether the unconstitutional-conditions doctrine protects the Pierce right to
  send one's child to private school. It would likely leave aside the
  preserved Privileges-or-Immunities and Equal Protection theories. The case
  would go to plenary review. A GVR or summary reversal is unlikely (~0.05
  conditional on a grant), since no intervening decision is pending that this
  case could be held for.
- **If denied:** a separate writing is plausible but less likely than not
  (~0.18). The petition sits where parental rights meet school-choice
  funding, and 14 States support it, which are the conditions under which
  Justice Alito, Justice Gorsuch or Justice Thomas has written. Justice Thomas
  might write separately to point to the Privileges-or-Immunities route
  rather than substantive due process.

**Why denial is still the likelier outcome.** The claimed split (one court
with the First Circuit against five applying the doctrine to the Pierce right)
rests largely on older and state-court decisions. The Eighth Circuit is
described as having only "flagged" the question. The petition also asks the
Court to extend a heightened-scrutiny framework to an unenumerated
substantive-due-process right, which some Justices who might otherwise be
sympathetic are wary of doing. And the respondents can recast the dispute as
the State choosing where to deliver services, a choice Maher and Harris treat
as within the government's power to decide what it funds. The call for a
response and the cert-stage amicus filings make a grant clearly more likely
than the base rate for this band, but not likely overall.
