# Forecast — Hellman v. Craven (No. 26-97), after the call for a response

**Docket state at my vantage (2026-08-21):** one distribution (for the 9/28/2026
conference), no relist, no CVSG. Massachusetts waived its response on Aug 13; the
Court called for a response on Aug 20 (due Sep 21, 2026). Seven amicus filings
are docketed by Aug 20, including a 14-state coalition led by West Virginia and
South Carolina.

- **Disposition:** denial is the likelier outcome, but this is a live grant
  candidate — roughly a one-in-five chance of plenary review.
- **Further distribution:** near certain. The 9/28 distribution is superseded by
  the response deadline; once the brief in opposition and any reply are in, the
  petition is redistributed (most likely for a late-October or November
  conference). I expect at least one relist beyond that first post-response
  conference if the Court is seriously considering it; a straight denial at the
  first post-response conference is the modal single path.
- **CVSG:** unlikely. The respondent is a state board; though the IDEA's
  private-school "equitable services" provisions frame the benefit, the
  question is a constitutional one between parents and a State, and the Court
  rarely invites the Solicitor General on such petitions.
- **Question taken if granted:** the single QP as written — whether the
  unconstitutional-conditions doctrine reaches the Pierce right to choose
  private schooling. The Court might reformulate toward whether denying a
  generally available benefit because a child attends private school burdens
  the parental right and triggers heightened scrutiny.
- **Route:** plenary review, not a summary disposition; no intervening decision
  supports a GVR, and the question is a doctrinal extension unsuited to summary
  reversal.
- **Separate writing on a denial:** possible. The issue sits next to the
  Espinoza / Carson line and the Court's recent parental-rights docket; if the
  Court denies, a dissent or statement from Justice Thomas, Alito or Gorsuch is
  the most plausible separate writing, though more likely none.

If granted, the likely ground is whether a State may condition a statutory
special-education entitlement on enrollment in public (rather than private)
school, with the Massachusetts anti-aid amendment's "any private school" sweep
as the justification tested.
