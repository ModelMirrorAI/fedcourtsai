### Downward Adjustments (-1.0%)
- **Weakness of the Alleged Circuit Split:** Petitioners (`petition.txt`, pp. 13–23) argue that the Courts of Appeals are split 3–2 on whether the unconstitutional conditions doctrine applies to substantive due process rights (contrasting the First Circuit here and in *Gary S. v. Manchester School District*, 374 F.3d 15 (1st Cir. 2004), and the Seventh Circuit in *St. Joan Antida High School Inc. v. Milwaukee Public School District*, 919 F.3d 1003 (7th Cir. 2019), against the Fifth, Sixth, and Ninth Circuits). However, as the First Circuit's opinion (`appendix.txt`, pp. 18a–27a) demonstrates, no circuit has held that the Fourteenth Amendment's *Pierce* right requires a state to provide publicly funded educational services on-site at a private school.
  - The Fifth Circuit case cited by Petitioners (*La. Debating & Literary Ass'n v. City of New Orleans*, 42 F.3d 1483 (5th Cir. 1995)) involved a city human-rights commission demanding private clubs surrender their First Amendment intimate-association privacy to keep municipal licenses; in the actual school context, the Fifth Circuit has agreed with the First and Seventh Circuits that *Pierce* does not invalidate rules favoring public education (*Cornerstone Christian Schs. v. Univ. Interscholastic League*, 563 F.3d 127, 138 n.12 (5th Cir. 2009), cited at `appendix.txt`, p. 26a).
  - The Sixth Circuit case (*RSWKL, LLC v. Christensen*, 143 F.4th 734 (6th Cir. 2025)) was a preliminary-injunction sovereign-immunity and property/takings dispute over lakeshore land condition demands.
  - The Ninth Circuit case (*Koala v. Khosla*, 931 F.3d 883 (9th Cir. 2019)) was a First Amendment Free Speech student-press subsidy case, while in the special-education context (*Loffman v. Cal. Dep't of Educ.*, 119 F.4th 1147 (9th Cir. 2024), distinguished at `appendix.txt`, p. 26a n.12), the Ninth Circuit rested on the Free Exercise Clause, not *Pierce*.
- **Direct Conflict with Longstanding Supreme Court Precedent:** In *Norwood v. Harrison*, 413 U.S. 455, 462 (1973), the Supreme Court squarely held that "[i]t is one thing to say that a State may not prohibit the maintenance of private schools [under *Pierce*] and quite another to say that such schools must, as a matter of equal rights, receive state aid." Similarly, *Maher v. Roe*, 432 U.S. 464, 477 (1977), and *Harris v. McRae*, 448 U.S. 297, 318 (1980), rejected the argument that *Pierce* requires states to subsidize private choices equally with public ones. While the Court has held in *Trinity Lutheran* (2017), *Espinoza* (2020), *Carson v. Makin* (2022), and *Mahmoud v. Taylor* (2025) that the **Free Exercise Clause** bars religious discrimination or indirect coercion against religious exercise, Petitioners brought **no Free Exercise claim**—even though the Harrison plaintiffs send their child to a Jewish day school—because Massachusetts's Place Regulation (`603 C.M.R. § 28.03(1)(e)(1)`) is religion-neutral and applies identically to all secular and religious private schools in order to comply with the Massachusetts Anti-Aid Amendment (`Mass. Const. amend. art. XVIII, § 2`). Extending the *Carson*/*Espinoza* non-discrimination rule from the Free Exercise Clause to a secular substantive due process right under *Pierce* would require overruling or drastically paring back *Norwood* and *Maher*—a major doctrinal leap the current Court has been reluctant to make under the rubric of substantive due process.
- **State-Law Entanglement:** Petitioners' unconstitutional-conditions theory depends entirely on the premise that Massachusetts statute (`M.G.L. c. 71B, § 3`) creates an individual entitlement to special-education services beyond the federal IDEA floor, and that the state agency's Place Regulation (`603 C.M.R. § 28.03(1)(e)(1)`) improperly conditions that state-created entitlement. Both lower courts rejected Petitioners' reading of the state statutory and regulatory scheme (`appendix.txt`, pp. 21a–23a, 29a, 46a–48a). The Court rarely grants certiorari to police how a state structures a voluntary state-law benefit that goes beyond federal IDEA requirements.

Combining these factors yields a final grant probability of **`0.04`** (4.0%), slightly above the 3.4% `baseline` band anchor to reflect the CFR and IJ representation, but heavily constrained by the absence of a genuine circuit split on the *Pierce* question and the settled *Norwood*/*Maher* line of precedent.
