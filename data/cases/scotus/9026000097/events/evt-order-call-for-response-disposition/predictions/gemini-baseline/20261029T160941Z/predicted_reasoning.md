# Forecast of the Court's Disposition and Procedural Path

## Procedural Trajectory: Further Distributions and CVSG

As of the August 21, 2026 cutoff snapshot, this petition has been distributed once (on August 19, 2026 for the September 28, 2026 Long Conference) after Respondents waived their right to respond, and the Court called for a response on August 20, 2026 (`distribution_count: 1`). Because a response was requested before the initial conference sat, the petition will be pulled from the September 28 list and redistributed for conference once the brief in opposition and reply are filed (`relist-increment: 0.99`). Beyond that post-response redistribution, the petition is unlikely to be relisted multiple times; the Court will most likely deny certiorari at the first conference following receipt of the response, or after at most one relist if a Justice prepares a separate writing.

The Court will not call for the views of the Solicitor General (`cvsg-increment: 0.01`). Although the Individuals with Disabilities Education Act (IDEA), 20 U.S.C. § 1412, forms part of the statutory backdrop, Petitioners do not assert a federal statutory claim under the IDEA and concede that the IDEA does not require on-site services for parentally placed private school students. The challenge exclusively targets a Massachusetts state regulation (`603 C.M.R. § 28.03(1)(e)(1)`) implementing a Massachusetts statute (`M.G.L. c. 71B, § 3`) and the Massachusetts Constitution's Anti-Aid Amendment (`Mass. Const. amend. art. XVIII, § 2`) under the Fourteenth Amendment. No federal agency interest or federal statutory ambiguity warrants a CVSG.

## Question Presented and Merits Posture (Conditional on a Grant)

If the Court were to grant certiorari, it would take the single question presented as framed in the petition: whether the unconstitutional conditions doctrine applies to the fundamental right recognized in *Pierce v. Society of Sisters*, 268 U.S. 510 (1925), to send one's child to a private school. The Court would not reformulate the question to reach the Fourteenth Amendment Privileges or Immunities Clause argument (which Petitioners raised below only for preservation and did not include in their Question Presented).

Conditional on a grant, the Court would set the case for plenary briefing and oral argument rather than disposing of it summarily in the cert order (`summary-disposition-route: 0.05`). There is no intervening Supreme Court precedent that the First Circuit overlooked—*Mahmoud v. Taylor*, 606 U.S. 522 (2025), and *Carson v. Makin*, 596 U.S. 767 (2022), were decided under the Free Exercise Clause, and the First Circuit explicitly addressed them—nor is the decision below a clear error under settled Fourteenth Amendment precedent that could support a summary reversal. If reached on plenary review, the central doctrinal question would be whether *Norwood v. Harrison*, 413 U.S. 455 (1973), and *Maher v. Roe*, 432 U.S. 464 (1977), foreclose applying the unconstitutional conditions framework to secular substantive due process claims seeking equal on-site delivery of state-funded educational services at private schools.

## Separate Writings on Denial

Should the Court deny certiorari, there is a modest probability (`dissent-from-denial: 0.12`) that one or more Justices will author a dissent from the denial of certiorari or a statement respecting the denial. Justice Thomas (`0.07`), Justice Alito (`0.05`), and Justice Gorsuch (`0.04`) are the likeliest authors of a separate writing, given their ongoing interest in parental rights under *Pierce* and *Meyer*, school choice, and the scope of the unconstitutional conditions doctrine (as well as Justice Thomas's longstanding interest in the Privileges or Immunities Clause noted in the opinions below). However, because the petition deliberately omits a Free Exercise claim even though one of the plaintiff families attends a religious private school, and because the underlying entitlement is a creature of Massachusetts state statutory law, the most likely outcome is an unsigned denial order without separate comment.
