Paramount Global* (No. 25-459), *National Basketball Ass'n v. Salazar* (No. 24-994), *Solomon v. Flipps Media, Inc.* (146 S. Ct. 885), or *Hughes v. National Football League* (146 S. Ct. 1790).

## 2. Disposition and Summary-Disposition Route

- **Primary Disposition Forecast (`disposition`: 0.04; `predicted_disposition`: `denied`)**: Once Respondent NBCUniversal files its brief in opposition, the Court will deny certiorari. The Court already denied certiorari twice within the past eleven months on the identical Second Circuit VPPA "ordinary person" standard applied to Meta Pixel transmissions (*Solomon v. Flipps Media, Inc.*, 146 S. Ct. 885 (Dec. 8, 2025), and *Hughes v. National Football League*, 146 S. Ct. 1790 (Mar. 9, 2026)), and the decision below is a non-precedential summary order that merely applied circuit law-of-the-panel rules. Moreover, because the courts below already held that Golden qualified as a "consumer" under Second Circuit precedent (*Salazar v. NBA*, 118 F.4th 533) and dismissed her complaint solely on the independent ground that no "personally identifiable information" was disclosed under *Solomon*, neither an affirmance nor a reversal in *Salazar v. Paramount Global* (No. 25-459) will undermine the Second Circuit's judgment.
- **Conditional Route if Granted (`summary-disposition-route`: 0.35)**: Conditional on the Court granting the petition (4% probability), there is roughly a 35% probability that the grant takes the form of a summary GVR (`gvr`) rather than plenary merits review (65%). A GVR could occur only if the Court's opinion in *Salazar v. Paramount Global* (No. 25-459) articulates a broad interpretive framework for the VPPA that five Justices decide the Second Circuit should consider in the first instance, whereas plenary review (65% conditional on grant) would be the path if four Justices specifically voted to resolve the 3–1 circuit split between the First Circuit's "reasonable foreseeability" test (*Yershov*) and the Second, Third, and Ninth Circuits' "ordinary person" test (*Solomon*, *Nickelodeon*, *Eichenberger*).

## 3. Conditional Question Presented and Merits Ground (If Granted)

- **Question Presented Taken**: If the Court were to grant plenary review, it would take the single Question Presented in the petition substantially as written: whether information is "personally identifiable information" under 18 U.S.C. § 2710(a)(3) when a video tape service provider knowingly discloses it to a third-party recipient that understands it to identify a specific person as having requested or obtained specific video materials, even if an "ordinary person" inspecting the transmitted code would not.
- **Conditional Merits Reasoning**: If granted for plenary review, the Court would likely focus on the statutory text of 18 U.S.C. § 2710(a)(3) ("includes information which identifies a person as having requested or obtained specific video materials or services") read alongside the scienter requirement in § 2710(b)(1) ("knowingly discloses, to any person, personally identifiable information"). Even on plenary review, however, Petitioner would face a substantial threshold obstacle if the Court rules in *Salazar v. Paramount Global* (No. 25-459) that a free email newsletter subscriber is not a "consumer" under § 2710(a)(1), which could lead to affirmance on that alternative ground or dismissal of the writ as improvidently granted.
