The Supreme Court is very likely (96% chance) to deny Sherhonda Golden's request to review her lawsuit against NBCUniversal under the Video Privacy Protection Act, a federal law that forbids video providers from sharing records of what videos a customer watches. Golden claims NBCUniversal violated this law when its Today.com website used tracking code to send her numeric Facebook ID and the titles of videos she watched to Meta, and the lower courts dismissed her case because an ordinary person looking at that computer code could not identify her. Although the Supreme Court asked NBCUniversal to file a written response to her petition, the Court already turned down two nearly identical appeals from the same appeals court inside the past year, and a separate video-privacy case currently before the Court (*Salazar v. Paramount Global*) deals with a different issue that cannot overturn the reason Golden lost below.
