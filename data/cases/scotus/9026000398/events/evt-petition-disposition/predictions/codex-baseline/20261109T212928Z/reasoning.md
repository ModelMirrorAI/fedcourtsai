## Question and vehicle

The question presented concerns whether a provider's knowledge that the actual recipient can identify the viewer suffices for personally identifiable information under 18 U.S.C. § 2710, even when an ordinary person could not interpret the transmitted code. The petition's introduction and Part I describe a First Circuit recipient-sensitive approach opposed to the Second, Third, and Ninth Circuits' ordinary-person approach. Its allegations connect a specific Facebook identifier and specific video information to Facebook's actual decoding ability. That is a coherent question with recurring commercial importance, not merely a request to correct the application of a settled rule. See provisioned petition pp. 1–3, 7–17 and QP.

Independent primary-source checks support a real doctrinal tension. Yershov v. Gannett, 820 F.3d 482, 486–87 (1st Cir. 2016), considers the recipient's identification capabilities and foreseeable linkage. Its GPS-plus-device-identifier facts nevertheless differ from the Facebook Pixel facts, so the claimed outcome-determinative split remains partly petitioner's advocacy. Solomon v. Flipps Media, 136 F.4th 41 (2d Cir. 2025), discusses the competing circuits and supplies the controlling approach applied below. Sources: CourtListener opinion IDs 3199261 and 10905600, especially the retrieved foreseeability passage and circuit discussion.

The attached appellate order, App. 1a–7a, makes the issue clean but not novel: it treats Golden's allegations as materially indistinguishable from Solomon and rejects the contention that general intervening textualist decisions displaced that precedent. The petition reports certiorari denials in Solomon on December 8, 2025 and Hughes on March 9, 2026 (petition p. 9). Those are preexisting decisions in other cases, not this petition's outcome. Although a denial is not a merits ruling, these recent refusals are substantial negative selection signals. The asserted lack of alternate dismissal grounds helps vehicle quality, but no opposition is yet due, so I cannot treat respondent as conceding that assertion.
