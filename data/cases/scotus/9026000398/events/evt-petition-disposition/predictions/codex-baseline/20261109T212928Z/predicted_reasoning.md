# Forecast of the Court's disposition

The Court will most likely deny certiorari without an explanatory opinion. The existing circuit disagreement gives the petition a plausible route to review, but the question largely reprises the ordinary-person issue in recently declined petitions. The Court will not treat general textualist decisions on unrelated statutes as compelling summary reversal of the judgment below.

The supplied docket contains **one distribution** and **no CVSG**. Because the Court requested a response after that distribution and extended its deadline to December 23, 2026, the petition will almost certainly be distributed at least once more. The central procedural forecast is one further distribution after the opposition and opportunity for reply, potentially followed by a hold or another distribution tied to Salazar. This is not a forecast that the November 6 conference already produced a substantive relist. The probability of any additional distribution is 99%.

The Court is unlikely to call for the Solicitor General's views: 1.5%. No invitation is expected before the ordinary response process concludes.

A hold for Salazar v. Paramount Global is a plausible intermediate step. Nonetheless, a ruling on consumer status will not necessarily resolve the different personally-identifiable-information issue presented here. Ultimate denial remains the modal disposition, including after a hold.

If the Court grants plenary review, it will likely take the petition's single question substantially as written, focusing on whether the provider's knowledge of the actual recipient's identifying capabilities defeats an ordinary-person limitation. Conditional on any grant, plenary briefing and argument are slightly more likely than disposition in the cert order: approximately 52.6% versus 47.4%. A summary route would principally be a GVR for reconsideration after Salazar, not an immediate merits reversal resolving the split.

If review is denied, no dissent or statement respecting denial is expected; the conditional chance of any such writing is 2.5%. No particular Justice is forecast to write. A denial would leave the Second Circuit's judgment in place without endorsing its statutory interpretation.
