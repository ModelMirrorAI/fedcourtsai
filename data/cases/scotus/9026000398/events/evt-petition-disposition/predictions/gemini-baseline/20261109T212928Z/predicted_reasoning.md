# Predicted Reasoning: *Sherhonda Golden v. NBCUniversal Media, LLC* (No. 26-398)

## Procedural Trajectory and Disposition Forecast

The docket currently records **one distribution** (on October 7, 2026, for the October 30, 2026 conference, following Respondent's October 5, 2026 waiver), which was overtaken by the Court's October 22, 2026 call for a response (extended to December 23, 2026).

1. **Further Distributions (`relist-increment: 0.95`):** Once Respondent NBCUniversal Media, LLC files its Brief in Opposition on or around December 23, 2026 and Petitioner files her reply, the petition will be distributed at least once more in January 2027. The Court will either deny the petition outright at that January 2027 conference or hold it briefly pending the merits decision in *Salazar v. Paramount Global*, No. 25-459 (argued October 14, 2026), before denying certiorari once *Salazar* issues. Expect one to two additional distributions recorded on the docket before final disposition.
2. **No Call for the Views of the Solicitor General (`cvsg-increment: 0.01`):** The Court will not invite the Solicitor General to file a brief. The dispute involves a private statutory damages action under the Video Privacy Protection Act (VPPA), 18 U.S.C. § 2710, and the Court already granted and heard oral argument in *Salazar v. Paramount Global* without seeking the Solicitor General's views at the cert stage.
3. **Ultimate Disposition (`predicted_disposition: "denied"`, `probability: 0.04`):** The Court will deny the petition for a writ of certiorari. The Court has already denied certiorari twice inside the last year on the exact Question Presented — whether Facebook Pixel transmissions of URLs and `c_user` cookies constitute "personally identifiable information" under 18 U.S.C. § 2710(a)(3) — first in the Second Circuit's lead published decision, *Solomon v. Flipps Media, Inc.*, 146 S. Ct. 885 (Dec. 8, 2025), and again in *Hughes v. National Football League*, 146 S. Ct. 1790 (Mar. 9, 2026), after *Salazar v. Paramount Global* had already been granted. Moreover, because the Second Circuit affirmed the dismissal of Petitioner's complaint solely on the independent ground that no "personally identifiable information" was disclosed under *Solomon* without reaching whether Petitioner qualified as a "consumer" under § 2710(a)(1), the Court's decision in *Salazar v. Paramount Global* on the "consumer" question will not disturb the adequate and independent ground supporting the judgment below.
4. **Summary Disposition vs. Plenary Review Conditional on a Grant (`summary-disposition-route: 0.62`):** Conditional on the unlikely event that the Court grants review, a summary grant, vacatur, and remand (GVR) in light of *Salazar v. Paramount Global* is the likelier route (`0.62`), occurring only if the *Salazar* opinion addresses the statutory structure and definitions of 18 U.S.C. § 2710(a) broadly enough to implicate the Second Circuit's construction of § 2710(a)(3). If the Court instead granted plenary review (`0.38` conditional share), it would take the single Question Presented as framed in the petition — whether information is "personally identifiable information" under 18 U.S.C. § 2710(a)(3) when the video tape service provider knows the recipient understands it to identify a specific person's video-viewing history, even if an "ordinary person" could not decipher the coded disclosure.
5. **Separate Writings on Denial (`dissent-from-denial: 0.02`):** Upon denial of certiorari, no Justice is expected to author a dissent or statement respecting the denial, matching the summary denials without noted dissents in both *Solomon v. Flipps Media* and *Hughes v. National Football League*.
