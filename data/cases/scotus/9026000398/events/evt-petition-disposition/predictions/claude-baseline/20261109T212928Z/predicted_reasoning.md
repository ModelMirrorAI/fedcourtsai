# Forecast — No. 26-398

The docket shows one distribution, for the Nov. 6, 2026 conference. That distribution is superseded by the Court's Oct. 22 request for a response, now due Dec. 23, 2026. After the brief in opposition comes in, expect the petition to be redistributed for a January 2027 conference.

From there, the most likely path is one of two:
1. **Denial at the first post-response conference.** The Court already denied the same "ordinary person" PII question in *Solomon v. Flipps Media* and *Hughes v. NFL*.
2. **A hold for Salazar v. Paramount Global (No. 25-459).** Under this path the petition is rescheduled repeatedly until Salazar issues, probably in spring 2027. It is then most likely denied, or GVR'd if Salazar's reading of the VPPA's definitions bears on "personally identifiable information".

I do not expect a CVSG, since this is a private dispute with no federal party.

If the Court grants plenary review, it would take the question as written: whether a provider's knowledge that the recipient (Facebook) can identify the viewer makes the information PII, regardless of what an "ordinary person" would understand. Conditional on any grant, a summary GVR after Salazar is roughly as likely as plenary review.

On denial, I expect no separate writing.
