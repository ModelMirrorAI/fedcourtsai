## In brief

A consumer asks the Court to revive her privacy lawsuit against NBCUniversal over video-watching data shared with Facebook.

## What happened

Sherhonda Golden sued NBCUniversal, claiming it violated a federal video privacy law by sharing her Facebook ID and video-viewing history with Facebook through tracking code on Today.com, without her consent. The district court dismissed her claim, and the Second Circuit affirmed, applying its own prior ruling that such information counts as protected only if an "ordinary person" could decipher it, not just a tech company like Facebook. Golden argues NBCUniversal knew Facebook would understand the disclosure, and that other appeals courts apply a different, broader test that would have let her claim proceed.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked whether a company that shares coded information knowing the recipient can identify a person's video-watching history has broken the law, even if an average person looking at the same code could not figure out what it means.

## Where it stands

The petition was distributed for the Court's November 6, 2026 conference; the docket does not yet show a decision on it. NBCUniversal initially waived its right to respond but was then asked to respond, and its deadline to do so has since been extended to December 23, 2026.

## What each outcome would mean

Golden is asking the Court to take up her case and overturn the Second Circuit's ruling against her. If the Court grants review, it would hear full arguments and decide which legal test applies, without yet deciding who wins. If it denies review, the Second Circuit's decision dismissing Golden's claim stands, and NBCUniversal's win in the lower courts remains in place.
