## Why move substantially above the anchor?

First, the petition seeks certiorari **before judgment**, but not in isolation. Its opening procedural account and opposition footnote 1 identify companion direct appeals, Allen v. Milligan, No. 26-117, and Allen v. Singleton, No. 26-118, arising from overlapping district-court proceedings. The petition reports that the Eleventh Circuit stayed Caster's appeal on June 25 pending those cases. The opposition expressly requests a matching grant in Caster if the Court reaches the merits in the direct appeals, including if it summarily affirms. Accordingly, P(grant) is not identical to P(Alabama wins). Companion treatment makes the absence of a circuit judgment less disabling than for an ordinary before-judgment petition.

The same-question list reports exact QP-text matches to 26-117 and 26-118, both pending as of its cutoff. This is corroboration of the filings' companion account, not independent proof of their docket posture: I did not inspect the companion dockets. There is no staged `related/` snapshot and no frozen Vide link on which to base a stronger claim.

Second, the petition's introduction and printed page 9, and the reply's printed page 1, describe the Court's June 2 stay as resting on substantive disagreement with the renewed injunction after Louisiana v. Callais. The opposition at printed pages 8–9 agrees that the stay permitted the 2023 plan's use in the 2026 election, while contesting the State's account of the merits. A prior merits-inflected intervention in the very litigation is more informative than a generic circuit-split allegation. It makes enforcement of the Court's already-announced framework through summary action plausible. I treat the parties' characterization of the stay as advocacy; I did not independently retrieve the stay opinion.

Third, Alabama explicitly seeks summary reversal rather than another full round of merits argument. Its theory is that the district court rejected lawful districting criteria, failed to separate racial from partisan polarization, and used legal disagreement as evidence of discriminatory intent. Alexander v. South Carolina State Conference of the NAACP, 602 U.S. 1 (2024), independently retrieved through CourtListener, supplies general context for the good-faith and race-versus-politics arguments. It does not itself resolve this Section 2 dispute or erase the distinction between racial gerrymandering and intentional vote dilution.

## Why retain a substantial non-grant probability?

The opposition's strongest objection is jurisdictional: the challenged preliminary injunction concerns the 2026 election and has already been stayed, while the district court reserved later elections for further proceedings. Its printed pages 9–10 argue that reversing now affords no effective relief. The reply at printed pages 1–3 responds that election administration and certification continue into November or December, part of the injunction survives until a replacement enactment, and the liability findings have continuing effects. On November 9, the November 3 voting date has passed; the reply's earlier request for action in October or November cannot be assumed to eliminate mootness now. I do not assume certification is complete or retrieve election results.

Even if review becomes moot, Alabama alternatively requests vacatur under Munsingwear, which would count as a grant-family outcome under this contract. That supports the GVR allocation rather than converting every jurisdictional problem into denial. But vacatur is not automatic, and the Court can also leave further merits litigation to the district court and circuit.

The opposition also contests the substance: it says the maps were drawn without impermissible racial predominance, Gulf Coast and incumbent-protection rationales were pretextual, and a substantial trial record supports intentional discrimination. Those contentions weaken any claim that summary reversal is routine. A stay is not a final merits adjudication, repeated distribution can precede a denial with writings, and an already-remanded case can require fact-sensitive reconsideration rather than outright reversal. These are the principal checks on the upward adjustment.
