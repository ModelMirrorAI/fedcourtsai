# Forecast of the Court's action

The Court will most likely grant this petition and summarily reverse the renewed injunction, handling Caster alongside the parallel Milligan and Singleton appeals. This is a forecast of the petition's disposition, not a claim that the earlier emergency stay already decided it. P(any grant) is 70%; summary reversal is the modal individual outcome at 45%.

The snapshot records three distributions: September 30, November 2, and November 9, 2026. I expect **one or two more distributions**, followed by a disposition in the coming weeks rather than a new full briefing-and-argument cycle. The probability of at least one additional distribution is 70%. The Court will not call for the Solicitor General's views; the probability of a new invitation is 1%.

Conditional on a grant, a disposition in the cert order itself is **90% likely**, versus 10% for plenary consideration. Summary reversal is more likely than a further grant-vacate-remand order because the State argues that the Court's existing post-Callais directions already resolve the legal errors. A vacatur and remand, including a mootness-based vacatur, is the principal alternative grant route. A simple denial remains a substantial alternative if the Court declines before-judgment review or concludes that no useful relief remains after the election.

If the Court grants to address the substance, it will most likely focus on **QP1 and QP4 together**: whether plaintiffs' alternatives must respect the State's permissible nonracial districting criteria, and whether refusal to draw an additional race-based district supports the intentional-discrimination finding. QP2's distinction between race and politics will support that analysis. I do not expect a separate new holding on QP3's entire totality-of-circumstances inquiry. Any plenary question would likely be narrowed to those issues rather than take all four questions as an unrestricted invitation to revisit the entire Voting Rights Act framework.

On the modal summary-reversal branch, the Court's explanation will identify a failure to apply its governing framework to legitimate districting priorities and legislative good faith, rather than announce that all intentional-discrimination claims are foreclosed. The existence and duration of continuing injunctive relief will be the threshold issue if mootness is addressed.

If review is denied, I expect **no noted dissent or statement**; the conditional probability of some such notation or writing is 17%. Thomas or Alito would be plausible authors on that less likely branch. On a summary reversal, a dissent by Sotomayor or Jackson, potentially joined by other Justices, is appreciably more likely than a dissent from denial. These author possibilities are reflected in the independent `order-writers` probabilities, not in a forecast of disclosed cert votes.
