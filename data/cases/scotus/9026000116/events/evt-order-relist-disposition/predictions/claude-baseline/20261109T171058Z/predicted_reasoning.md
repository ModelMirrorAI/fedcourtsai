# Forecast — Allen v. Caster, No. 26-116 (cert before judgment, CA11)

**State forecast from.** The docket shows three distributions (10/16, 11/6, 11/13 conferences): the petition has been relisted twice, in lockstep with the companion direct appeals Allen v. Milligan (26-117) and Allen v. Singleton (26-118), which carry the identical distribution history. No CVSG.

**Disposition.** The Court will act on Caster together with Milligan and Singleton, and the most likely result is a grant that disposes of the case in the order itself: certiorari before judgment granted and the May 2026 preliminary injunction summarily reversed, following the reasoning of the Court's own June 2026 stay opinion (Allen v. Milligan, 608 U.S. 511). The next most likely grant route is a Munsingwear vacatur, granting cert, vacating the injunction, and remanding with instructions to dismiss as moot, because the injunction was framed around the now-completed 2026 elections. Plenary review (noting probable jurisdiction in the appeals and granting Caster to consolidate) is a distant third. The least likely outcome is a denial: the Court would dismiss the Milligan/Singleton appeals as moot without vacatur and leave Caster to the Eleventh Circuit.

**Further relists.** About even odds that the petitions are distributed again after November 13. Two relists already suggest someone is writing, whether a per curiam or a dissent, and a per curiam summary reversal with a dissent often takes more than one more conference.

**CVSG.** None. The United States is not a party whose views the Court needs, and the Court has already engaged this dispute on the merits three times.

**Question taken, if granted.** On the summary route the Court would not frame a question. It would rest on Callais and the stay opinion: plaintiffs' illustrative maps must satisfy the State's legitimate districting goals (QP 1), and racially polarized voting must be separated from partisanship (QP 2). If the case is set for argument, expect QPs 1–2 as written, with QP 4 (the Fourteenth Amendment claim) as the vehicle for the intentional-discrimination finding.

**Separate writing.** A summary reversal will very likely draw a dissent from Justices Sotomayor, Kagan and/or Jackson, most likely Jackson or Sotomayor writing. If the Court denies instead, expect a statement or dissent, most plausibly from Justice Thomas or Justice Alito, objecting to leaving the liability findings unvacated.
