- **Further relists (`relist-increment`: 0.55):** Expect one to two additional relists before the order issues. Whether measured against the `distribution_count: 2` frozen in `context.json` (which the November 9 distribution for the November 13 conference already exceeds) or forward from the November 13, 2026 conference, a coordinated summary order across all three Alabama redistricting dockets—likely accompanied by a per curiam explanation or separate dissenting/concurring writings—commonly takes one to two further conferences to circulate and finalize.
- **Call for the Views of the Solicitor General (`cvsg-increment`: 0.01):** The Court will not issue a CVSG. The Solicitor General already participated as amicus curiae supporting Alabama's stay application in this posture (`No. 25A1316`), and the Court does not invite the Solicitor General's views on an interlocutory post-remand redistricting preliminary injunction that is paired with direct appeals under 28 U.S.C. § 1253.

## Disposition Route and Questions Presented

- **Primary disposition (`predicted_disposition`: `gvr`; `disposition`: 0.72; `summary-disposition-route`: 0.85 conditional on grant):** The Court is very likely to dispose of *Caster* in the cert order itself rather than setting an interlocutory preliminary injunction for plenary merits briefing and oral argument. Specifically, the likeliest outcome is a **grant of certiorari before judgment, vacatur of the May 26, 2026 preliminary injunction order, and remand (`gvr`)**—either for further proceedings consistent with *Louisiana v. Callais*, 146 S. Ct. 1131 (2026), and the Court's published stay opinion in *Allen v. Milligan*, 608 U.S. 511 (2026), or as moot under *United States v. Munsingwear, Inc.*, 340 U.S. 36 (1950), now that the November 3, 2026 general election has been conducted under the 2023 Plan pursuant to the Court's June 2, 2026 stay. A **summary reversal** on the merits in the cert order is the main alternative summary route.
- **Scope of review if granted:** If the Court grants plenary review or issues a merits summary disposition, it will address the single question presented in *Caster*—whether the district court erred in preliminarily enjoining Alabama's 2023 congressional redistricting plan under Section 2 of the Voting Rights Act in light of *Louisiana v. Callais*—in consolidated or tandem proceedings with the first question presented in *Milligan* (`26-117`) and *Singleton* (`26-118`).
- **Conditional merits ground:** If the Court reaches the merits (either by summary disposition or after plenary review), the majority will rest on *Louisiana v. Callais*, 146 S. Ct. 1131 (2026), and *Allen v. Milligan*, 608 U.S. 511 (2026), holding that the district court failed to apply the presumption of legislative good faith to Alabama's nonracial districting priorities (preserving the Gulf Coast community of interest in Mobile and Baldwin Counties and protecting incumbents) under the first *Gingles* precondition and failed to require evidence disentangling partisan polarization from racial-bloc voting under the second and third *Gingles* preconditions.

## Separate Writings (`dissent-from-denial`: 0.22; `order-writers`)

- **On a summary grant/vacatur or summary reversal:** Justices Sotomayor, Jackson, and Kagan are the likeliest authors of a dissenting opinion or statement defending the district court's post-*Callais* factual findings, while Justices Thomas, Alito, or Kavanaugh may author a brief concurrence addressing why vacatur or summary reversal is warranted.
- **Conditional on a denial (`dissent-from-denial`: 0.22):** If the Court denies certiorari before judgment in *Caster*—which would most plausibly occur only as a procedural housekeeping choice while vacating the preliminary injunction in the direct appeals (*Milligan* and *Singleton*) and leaving the single-judge *Caster* appeal to be resolved by the Eleventh Circuit once its stay lifts—most Justices would not write separately on the *Caster* denial itself, though Justice Thomas or Justice Alito could note a dissent or statement if the denial left any part of the May 26, 2026 order undisturbed.
