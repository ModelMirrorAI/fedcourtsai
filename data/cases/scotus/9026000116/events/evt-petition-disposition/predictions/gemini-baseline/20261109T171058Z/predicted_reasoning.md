# Forecast of Disposition: *Wes Allen, Alabama Secretary of State, et al. v. Marcus Caster, et al.* (No. 26-116)

## I. Predicted Disposition and Route

The Court will **grant** the petition for a writ of certiorari before judgment in No. 26-116 alongside its action on the companion direct appeals from the three-judge district court in *Allen v. Milligan* (No. 26-117) and *Allen v. Singleton* (No. 26-118).

### 1. Summary Disposition in the Cert Order vs. Plenary Review
Conditional on granting certiorari before judgment, the likelier route (**65%** conditional probability) is a **summary disposition in the cert order itself**—either vacating the May 26, 2026 preliminary injunction and remanding for further proceedings (under *United States v. Munsingwear, Inc.*, 340 U.S. 36 (1950), now that the November 3, 2026 general election has been conducted under the 2023 Plan pursuant to this Court's June 2, 2026 stay, or in light of *Louisiana v. Callais*, 608 U.S. ___ (2026)) or summarily reversing the preliminary injunction on the grounds already set forth in this Court's June 2, 2026 *per curiam* stay opinion (*Allen v. Milligan*, 608 U.S. ___, 146 S. Ct. 1196 (2026)).

Because the district court's May 26, 2026 order was an interlocutory preliminary injunction entered specifically to govern the 2026 congressional election while final judgment proceedings on remand from this Court's May 11, 2026 vacatur (*Allen v. Caster*, 146 S. Ct. 1131 (2026)) remained pending, and because the 2026 election cycle has now passed, full plenary briefing and oral argument on the preliminary injunction is less likely (**35%** conditional probability) than a summary vacatur or reversal that clears the preliminary injunction and returns the case to the Northern District of Alabama for entry of final judgment.

### 2. If Plenary Review Is Granted: Questions Presented Taken
If the Court instead sets No. 26-116 down for plenary briefing and argument consolidated with Nos. 26-117 and 26-118, it will grant on Question 1 of the Petition—whether the 2023 Plan likely violates § 2 of the Voting Rights Act under the updated *Gingles* framework announced in *Louisiana v. Callais*, 608 U.S. ___ (2026) (specifically whether plaintiffs' illustrative maps perform "just as well" on all of Alabama's legitimate nonracial districting criteria, including keeping the Gulf Coast counties together and avoiding pairing incumbents, and whether plaintiffs proved racial bloc voting controlling for partisan affiliation). Question 2 (whether the preliminary injunction abused the district court's equitable discretion under *Purcell*) is moot now that the 2026 election has occurred.

## II. Conference Trajectory, Relists, and CVSG

- **Further Relists (`relist-increment` = 0.25):** Having already been distributed three times (for the October 30, November 6, and November 13, 2026 conferences, bridging the November 3, 2026 general election), the petition is likely to resolve from the November 13, 2026 conference, with a modest (25%) chance of one or more additional relists if a *per curiam* order or separate writing is still circulating.
- **Call for the Views of the Solicitor General (`cvsg-increment` = 0.005):** The Court will not issue a CVSG. The Solicitor General already participated as amicus curiae at the stay stage in June 2026, and the procedural posture of this state redistricting dispute does not warrant an invitation brief.

## III. Separate Writings on the Order

- **Dissent from Denial (`dissent-from-denial` = 0.05):** If the Court denies the petition for certiorari before judgment in *Caster* (most likely in tandem with dismissing or vacating the *Milligan* and *Singleton* preliminary injunction appeals as moot following the 2026 election), no Justice is likely to note a dissent from denial because Respondents urged denial on mootness grounds and Petitioners requested *Munsingwear* vacatur as a fallback.
- **Order Writers (`order-writers`):** If the Court summarily reverses the preliminary injunction on the merits rather than vacating it under *Munsingwear* or granting plenary review, Justice Sotomayor (joined by Justices Kagan and Jackson) is the likeliest author of a dissenting statement from the summary disposition, mirroring her dissents from the May 11, 2026 vacatur and the June 2, 2026 stay order.
