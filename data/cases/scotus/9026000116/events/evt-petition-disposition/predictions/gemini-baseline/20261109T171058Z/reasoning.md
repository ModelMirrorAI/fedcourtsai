# Reasoning: *Wes Allen, Alabama Secretary of State, et al. v. Marcus Caster, et al.* (No. 26-116)

## I. Procedural Posture and Companion Dockets

This petition for a writ of certiorari before judgment arises out of the fourth Supreme Court trip in the long-running litigation over Alabama's post-2020 congressional redistricting. Three parallel suits in the Northern District of Alabama challenge the state's congressional map:
1. *Milligan v. Allen*, No. 2:21-cv-1530-AMM (three-judge district court: Judges Marcus, Manasco, and Moorer), bringing statutory Voting Rights Act § 2 and constitutional Fourteenth Amendment intentional-discrimination claims;
2. *Singleton v. Allen*, No. 2:21-cv-1291-AMM (same three-judge district court), bringing constitutional claims; and
3. *Caster v. Allen*, No. 2:21-cv-1536-AMM (single-judge district court: Judge Manasco sitting alone), bringing only a statutory § 2 claim.

Because *Milligan* and *Singleton* were heard by a three-judge district court under 28 U.S.C. § 2284, direct appeals from their orders lie to the Supreme Court under 28 U.S.C. § 1253. Because *Caster* was heard by Judge Manasco sitting alone, appeals go to the Eleventh Circuit, requiring Alabama to file a petition for certiorari before judgment under 28 U.S.C. § 1254(1) and S. Ct. Rule 11 to keep the three cases consolidated or aligned before this Court.

In the first round (*Allen v. Milligan*, 599 U.S. 1 (2023)), the Court granted certiorari before judgment in *Caster* (No. 21-1086) alongside the direct appeal in *Milligan* (No. 21-1087) and affirmed the preliminary injunction against the 2021 map. In the second round, after the district court entered a permanent injunction against Alabama's 2023 Plan on May 8, 2025 (*Singleton v. Allen*, 782 F. Supp. 3d 1092 (N.D. Ala. 2025)), Alabama filed direct appeals in *Milligan* and *Singleton* and a petition for certiorari before judgment in *Caster* (No. 25-243). Following this Court's decision in *Louisiana v. Callais*, 608 U.S. ___ (Apr. 29, 2026) (No. 24-109), the Court on May 11, 2026 granted certiorari before judgment in *Caster* (No. 25-243), vacated the judgment, and remanded for further consideration in light of *Callais* (*Allen v. Caster*, 146 S. Ct. 1131 (2026)).

Hours after the May 11, 2026 vacatur, plaintiffs moved for a temporary restraining order and preliminary injunction. On May 26, 2026, the three-judge court in *Milligan* and *Singleton* (App. 1a–115a) and Judge Manasco sitting alone in *Caster* (App. 661a–668a) entered preliminary injunctions again barring the Secretary of State from using the 2023 Plan and ordering the use of the 2023 Special Master Plan for the 2026 congressional elections. Notice of appeal to the Eleventh Circuit was filed in *Caster* (No. 26-11828), and notices of direct appeal to this Court were filed in *Milligan* and *Singleton*.

On June 2, 2026, this Court granted Alabama's emergency stay applications in all three cases (*Allen v. Milligan*, Nos. 25A1314, 25A1315, and 25A1316, 608 U.S. ___, 146 S. Ct. 1196 (2026) (per curiam)), over a 17-page dissent by Justice Sotomayor joined by Justices Kagan and Jackson. On June 25, 2026, the Eleventh Circuit stayed the *Caster* appeal (No. 26-11828) pending this Court's decision in *Milligan* and *Singleton* (App. 675a–676a).

On July 24, 2026, Alabama docketed the jurisdictional statements in *Milligan* (No. 26-117) and *Singleton* (No. 26-118) alongside this petition for certiorari before judgment in *Caster* (No. 26-116). All three cases were distributed together for the October 30, November 6, and November 13, 2026 conferences (`distribution_count: 3`).

## II. The Mootness / Summary Disposition Dynamic vs. Plenary Review

A critical development occurred between the June 2, 2026 stay order and the November 13, 2026 conference: the 2026 election cycle has now concluded (with the August 11, 2026 special primary and the November 3, 2026 general election conducted under the 2023 Plan pursuant to this Court's stay), and final judgment has still not been entered on remand in the Northern District of Alabama.

Both parties focus heavily on the procedural posture:
- **Petitioners (Alabama)** argue that the preliminary injunction was entered in clear defiance of *Callais* and this Court's May 11 vacatur. Petitioners ask the Court to **summarily reverse** the May 26, 2026 preliminary injunction (or, in the alternative, note probable jurisdiction in *Milligan*/*Singleton* and grant certiorari before judgment in *Caster*). In their September 30, 2026 Reply Brief, Petitioners also respond to Respondents' mootness argument by contending that the preliminary injunction is not limited on its face to the 2026 elections (citing App. 4a, which enjoined conducting "any elections" with the 2023 Plan and ordered use of the Special Master Plan in the "2026 congressional election") and that the dispute is capable of repetition yet evading review, while adding at the conclusion of their Reply Brief (Reply 9) that "[a]t minimum, vacatur is warranted under *United States v. Munsingwear, Inc.*, 340 U.S. 36, 39 (1950)."
- **Respondents (Caster Plaintiffs)** argue in their September 18, 2026 Brief in Opposition that the appeal of the May 26, 2026 preliminary injunction is **moot** because the preliminary injunction sought only temporary relief for the 2026 election cycle pending final judgment on remand, and this Court's June 2, 2026 stay ensured that the 2026 elections are conducted under the 2023 Plan. Respondents point to this Court's recent disposition in *Robinson v. Ardoin*, 144 S. Ct. 2710 (2024), where after staying a preliminary injunction in a § 2 redistricting case and holding it alongside *Milligan*, the Court dismissed the writ of certiorari before judgment as improvidently granted once the preliminary injunction no longer had practical effect, allowing the district court to proceed to final judgment on the merits. Respondents therefore urge the Court to deny the petition for certiorari before judgment in *Caster* (or dismiss/vacate as moot) so the district court can enter a final judgment on remand applying *Callais*.

### Evaluating How the Court Will Dispose of No. 26-116

1. **Base Rate Anchor (`cert_anchor`):**
   From `context.json`, this case sits in the `high` salience band (`distribution_count: 3`, no CVSG), where the 10-Term pooled risk-set grant-family base rate is **34.84%** (`0.3484455958549223`).

2. **Why `granted` (`actual_granted = 1`) is significantly more likely than the 34.8% base rate (~65%):**
   Under the pipeline's outcome rules (`docs/outcome-decomposition.md`), `actual_granted = 1` covers any disposition in the grant family: plenary `granted`, `granted-in-part`, `gvr` (grant, vacate, and remand, including *Munsingwear* vacatur), and `summary-reversal` (which the SCOTUS docket and corpus record as `granted` with a same-day merits judgment, or `gvr`).
   Here, there are three main paths the Court may take across the trio of Nos. 26-116, 26-117, and 26-118 after two relists (sitting at `distribution_count = 3` for the November 13, 2026 conference):

   - **Path A: Grant Certiorari Before Judgment and Summarily Reverse or Vacate (including *Munsingwear* GVR) (~42% unconditional probability; ~65% of all grants).**
     Why have the three cases been relisted twice (from Oct. 30 to Nov. 6, and from Nov. 6 to Nov. 13)? First, under the Court's modern practice, the Court routinely relists at least once before granting plenary review, issuing a summary reversal, or issuing a contested GVR/opinion. Second, the Oct. 30 conference was five days *before* the November 3, 2026 general election, and the Nov. 6 conference was three days *after* the general election. Once the November 3, 2026 election took place, any argument over whether the preliminary injunction could still affect the 2026 election cycle evaporated.
     Furthermore, in the June 2, 2026 stay opinion (*Allen v. Milligan*, 146 S. Ct. 1196), six Justices joined a detailed 4-page *per curiam* opinion holding that the district court's May 26, 2026 preliminary injunction departed from *Callais* on multiple fronts:
     - First, under *Callais*, a § 2 plaintiff's illustrative map "must meet *all* the State's legitimate districting objectives" "just as well" as the State's map, including keeping together the Gulf Coast community of interest (Mobile and Baldwin Counties) and avoiding the pairing of incumbents. Yet the district court found a § 2 violation even though the plaintiffs' alternative maps did not perform just as well on those criteria.
     - Second, the district court failed to follow *Callais*'s instruction that Gingles preconditions 2 and 3 require an analysis controlling for party affiliation showing racial bloc voting that cannot be explained by partisan affiliation.
     - Third, on intentional discrimination, the district court failed to heed *Alexander*'s presumption of legislative good faith by treating legal disagreement with the earlier remedial order as discriminatory animus.
     Because the 6-Justice majority already wrote a merits analysis explaining why the May 26, 2026 preliminary injunction is unlawful under *Callais*, and because the 2026 election is now over while the district court still must enter final judgment on remand from the May 11 vacatur, the Court has a very strong incentive either to:
     (a) Grant certiorari before judgment in *Caster* (alongside noting jurisdiction/summarily disposing of *Milligan* and *Singleton*) and **summarily vacate or reverse** the preliminary injunction (either on the merits per the June 2 *per curiam* or under *Munsingwear* now that the 2026 election has passed), clearing the preliminary injunction off the books so the district court can proceed to final judgment on remand; or
     (b) Issue a **GVR** (vacating the preliminary injunction as moot under *Munsingwear* after the November 3, 2026 election, or vacating and remanding for final judgment proceedings).
     Note that in the corpus, when the Court grants certiorari and summarily vacates or reverses in the cert order (as it did in No. 25-243, *Allen v. Caster*, on May 11, 2026), the docket records `Certiorari before judgment granted. Judgment vacated...` which resolves to `actual_granted = 1` (either `granted` with `summary-merits` or `gvr`) and `summary-disposition-route = 1`.

   - **Path B: Grant Certiorari Before Judgment for Plenary Review alongside Noting Probable Jurisdiction in Nos. 26-117 & 26-118 (~23% unconditional probability; ~35% of all grants).**
     Because *Milligan* (26-117) and *Singleton* (26-118) are mandatory direct appeals under 28 U.S.C. § 1253 from a three-judge district court's preliminary injunction, the Court cannot simply deny certiorari in *Milligan* and *Singleton*—it must either note probable jurisdiction, summarily dispose of them (reverse/vacate), or dismiss them as moot. If the Court notes probable jurisdiction in *Milligan* and *Singleton* for plenary briefing and oral argument, it is virtually certain to grant certiorari before judgment in *Caster* (26-116) to keep the consolidated remedial/liability record together, just as it did in OT2022 (No. 21-1086) and in the May 2026 vacatur (No. 25-243) and June 2026 stay (No. 25A1316). However, setting an interlocutory preliminary injunction down for full merits briefing and argument in spring 2027—after the 2026 election has already occurred and while final judgment proceedings on the exact same trial record remain pending in the district court—is procedurally awkward compared to a summary vacatur/reversal or mootness disposition.

   - **Path C: Deny Certiorari Before Judgment in *Caster* (26-116) (~35% unconditional probability).**
     Why might the Court **deny** certiorari before judgment in No. 26-116 (`actual_granted = 0`)?
     1. **Mootness of the 2026 Preliminary Injunction:** If the Court concludes that the May 26, 2026 preliminary injunction governed only the 2026 election cycle (which concluded on November 3, 2026), it may dismiss or vacate the direct appeals in *Milligan* (26-117) and *Singleton* (26-118) as moot (or vacate the three-judge order under *Munsingwear*) while simply **denying** the petition for certiorari before judgment in *Caster* (26-116), leaving the Eleventh Circuit (where Appeal No. 26-11828 is pending and stayed) and Judge Manasco to vacate the *Caster* preliminary injunction as moot and proceed to final judgment. Under Rule 11, certiorari before judgment is granted only upon a showing of imperative public importance requiring immediate settlement by this Court; if the Court does not grant plenary review in *Milligan*/*Singleton*, there is no imperative need to bypass the Eleventh Circuit in *Caster* when the Eleventh Circuit will automatically follow this Court's disposition of *Milligan*/*Singleton*.
     2. **Strictly Procedural Denial Pending Final Judgment:** Alternatively, even if the Court vacates the *Milligan*/*Singleton* preliminary injunctions on direct appeal, it occasionally denies a companion petition for certiorari before judgment when the court of appeals has held the appeal in abeyance and can dispose of it in light of the direct appeal. However, in the prior two rounds of this exact litigation (No. 21-1086 and No. 25-243), the Court granted certiorari before judgment in *Caster* rather than leaving it behind in the Eleventh Circuit, because the district court's liability opinion in *Caster* incorporates the three-judge opinion in *Milligan* verbatim (App. 667a–668a).

   Balancing these pathways:
   - Total probability of a grant-family disposition (`actual_granted = 1`): **0.65** (`65%`), comprising ~42% summary disposition in the cert order (summary reversal or *Munsingwear*/merits vacatur/GVR) and ~23% plenary grant consolidated with Nos. 26-117 and 26-118.
   - Total probability of denial (`actual_granted = 0`): **0.35** (`35%`).
   - For `predicted_disposition`, because `granted` vs `gvr` depends on whether the Court styles a cert-order disposition as a vacatur/remand (`gvr`) or as a summary reversal / plenary grant (`granted`), and both map to `granted = 1`, we select `"granted"` (which covers both plenary grants and summary reversals recorded on the docket as granted+reversed, and is the standard positive disposition label).

3. **`cvsg-increment` (`0.005`):** Near zero. The United States already participated as amicus curiae at the stay stage in June 2026, this is a fast-moving state redistricting dispute that the Court has already heard twice on the merits and twice on stay/vacatur, and the Court virtually never calls for the views of the Solicitor General on a petition for certiorari before judgment in a state redistricting case at its third conference.
4. **`summary-disposition-route` (`0.65`):** Conditional on a grant (`actual_granted = 1`), the probability that the grant disposes of the case in the cert order itself (either via a *Munsingwear* / *Callais* vacatur-and-remand `gvr` or a summary reversal `summary-merits`) rather than setting the preliminary injunction down for full merits briefing and oral argument. Given that the 2026 election has already occurred, final judgment proceedings are pending in the district court, and the Court already issued a 4-page *per curiam* merits analysis in the June 2, 2026 stay order, a summary disposition in the cert order is substantially more likely (~65% conditional on grant) than full plenary briefing and oral argument on an expired preliminary injunction (~35% conditional on grant).
5. **`dissent-from-denial` (`0.05`):** Conditional on a denial (`actual_granted = 0`), a denial here would almost certainly occur because the 2026 election is over and the preliminary injunction is moot (leaving final judgment proceedings to continue below). Thus, conditional on denial, a noted dissent from denial is unlikely (~5%).
6. **`order-writers`:** On the cert order, if any Justice writes separately (e.g., dissenting from a summary reversal or vacatur, or concurring in a mootness disposition), Justice Sotomayor (who authored the dissents in the May 11, 2026 vacatur and the June 2, 2026 stay, joined by Justices Kagan and Jackson) is by far the likeliest writer (`0.35`), followed by Justices Kagan and Jackson (`0.15` each), with smaller probabilities (`0.01`–`0.05`) for the conservative Justices concurring in or dissenting from the procedural disposition.
