## Why this case is substantially above the anchor

This is not an ordinary request to correct disputed trial findings. The petition, pp. 1–3 and 8–9, describes a previous grant before judgment and remand in light of Callais on May 11, 2026, followed by renewed injunctions and a June 2 Supreme Court stay. The petition and September 29 reply characterize the stay's reasons as rejecting the lower court's treatment of Alabama's nonracial goals and its application of the legislative-good-faith presumption. The opposition's statement and mootness argument acknowledge that the stay permits the State to use its 2023 map. Those pre-petition proceedings are evidence about the current petition, not its disposition.

I distinguish the existence of that acknowledged stay from petitioners' interpretation of its reasons: I did not obtain the standalone stay opinion or a verified vote lineup. Even with that discount, the Supreme Court's repeated involvement in this same litigation is much stronger evidence of likely corrective action than an unsupported assertion of conflict with precedent. A grant need not entail another full round of argument when the asserted error is failure to implement an already-issued remand. Petitioners expressly seek summary reversal, not a new doctrinal merits hearing.

The parallel appeals substantially reduce the practical obstacle presented by certiorari before judgment. `questions-presented.txt`, pp. ii–iii, identifies Milligan and Singleton as the same underlying litigation. The retained Eleventh Circuit order in `appendix.txt`, App. 675a–676a, actually stays Caster pending those Supreme Court proceedings. The corpus lookups for **26-117** and **26-118** confirm matching parties, district-court case numbers, three distributions, and the same November 13 conference; neither row records a disposition. These are the text-matched candidates in `same-question.json`, not formal Vide links: `context.json` has no Vide links. I rely on the filings and actual records, not on their 1.0 similarity scores, to establish the connection. The opposition, p. 3 n.1, itself asks the Court to grant this petition and act consistently if it reaches the companion appeals. That concession strengthens a grant route without conceding Alabama's merits position.

## Counterweights and principal uncertainty

Respondents' strongest procedural point is **mootness**, not lack of importance. Their opposition, pp. 9–10, says the injunction governs only the 2026 election, that the stay already gave Alabama the practical relief it wanted, and that future elections await additional proceedings. As of November 9, election day, November 3, has passed; I do not treat the reply's September request for a pre-election decision as still prospective. The reply, pp. 1–3, responds that canvassing and certification continue after election day, that part of the remedial order persists until a new legislative plan, and that the dispute and claimed collateral effects remain. These are competing legal characterizations, not established answers to the jurisdictional question. If mootness prevails, petitioners alternatively request Munsingwear vacatur, which would still count as a grant through a GVR; mootness therefore changes route as well as lowering grant probability.

On substance, the opposition, pp. 12–35, identifies allegedly race-neutral illustrative maps, intra-party evidence of racial polarization, factual findings of intentional discrimination, and clear-error review. It argues that the State's asserted partisan goals were post-hoc and that Gulf Coast and incumbent-protection criteria masked racial discrimination. Those are serious objections to summary correction on a complex trial record. The retained May 26 order in the petition, App. 2a–6a, confirms that the district court claimed to reconsider the evidence under Callais rather than simply refusing the remand. I do not treat allegations of defiance as findings.

The most important scope distinction is in the Caster order, retained at App. 108a–109a: **Caster alleges statutory Section 2 dilution only**, while the two companion actions also present constitutional claims. The four common questions are therefore not four equally independent issues for this petition. Corrective action here is most naturally tied to the statutory questions, not a freestanding resolution of the fourth, constitutional question. The absence of an Eleventh Circuit merits judgment is expected in a before-judgment petition, not a missing-opinion defect.

Balancing these considerations, I move from the 34.8% anchor to 82%, rather than to near-certainty. A coherent approximate outcome allocation is 52% summary reversal, 23.5% GVR, 6.5% plenary or other grant, 13% denial, and 5% non-grant dismissal or other termination. This yields the stated grant probability and approximately 92% cert-order disposition conditional on a grant. The summary-reversal label is not used interchangeably with a GVR: the former would decide the error now; the latter would vacate and send the case back, including on mootness grounds.

## Other claims and significance

The 0.58 additional-distribution probability reflects a petition already twice redistributed with linked appeals and a plausible written summary disposition, but also the possibility of action immediately after November 13. It is not a lookup from the terminal relist table. A new CVSG is only 0.01 because the Court has already addressed the litigation and has no evident need for a lengthy additional consultation. Conditional on denial, I assign 0.20 to a noted dissent or statement. The independent order-writer probabilities primarily allow a liberal Justice, most plausibly Kagan, to object to the predicted summary reversal; they are unconditional across disposition routes, not conditional on denial and not forecasts of who merely joins another's opinion. No current cert vote lineup is asserted.

The 0.88 significance score reflects congressional representation and the national consequences of applying the post-Callais standard to racial versus partisan districting objectives. It does not represent a second grant estimate. Much of the underlying doctrinal change is already described as having occurred, so this is not scored as a wholly new foundational holding.
