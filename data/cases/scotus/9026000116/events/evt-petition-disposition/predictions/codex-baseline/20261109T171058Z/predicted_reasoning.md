# Forecast of the Court's action

## Disposition and timing

The Court will most likely grant certiorari before judgment and **summarily reverse** the preliminary injunction in coordination with Allen v. Milligan, No. 26-117, and Allen v. Singleton, No. 26-118. The probability of any grant is **82%**. Conditional on a grant, the probability that the Court disposes of the case in the cert order rather than setting it for briefing and argument is **92%**. A GVR, including vacatur prompted by mootness, is the main alternative grant route; it is not the same prediction as a summary reversal.

The modal timing is late November or December 2026, following the November 13 conference and potentially further circulation of an order and separate writings. A hold for the companion appeals could extend consideration into early 2027. This is a timing forecast, not a claim that a particular order date is already scheduled.

## Further docket activity

The baseline already records **three distributions**, for the October 16, November 6, and November 13 conferences, and no CVSG. From that state, I assign **58%** to at least one additional distribution. The most likely path within that branch is one or two more distributions, not an extended sequence awaiting new briefing. There is a substantial alternative of action after the already-scheduled November 13 conference with no increment.

The Court will not call for the Solicitor General's views: the probability of a new CVSG is **1%**. The related cases and the Court's earlier intervention supply the likely route without another months-long consultation.

## Questions and conditional rationale

If the Court grants review, it will most likely address the first three questions together as an application of the post-Callais Section 2 standard: how illustrative maps must account for legitimate state criteria, how evidence distinguishes racial voting patterns from politics, and what supports an inference of discriminatory dilution. It will not need to resolve the fourth question as a separate constitutional claim in Caster, whose own district-court order identifies a statutory claim only. Constitutional reasoning may accompany action in the parallel appeals.

If the predicted summary reversal occurs, the likely ground is that the injunction cannot be sustained on an analysis that discounts permissible nonracial districting objectives and fails to separate partisan explanations from racial discrimination under the controlling standard. The likely action will correct the injunction rather than announce an entirely new nationwide test. If the Court instead concludes that election-specific relief is moot, the leading grant alternative is vacatur and remand, not a substantive reversal dressed as a GVR. If it chooses plenary review, it will likely coordinate briefing and argument with the companion appeals.

## Separate writings

Conditional on **denial**, the probability of a noted dissent or statement respecting denial is **20%**; the modal denial has none. A denial-related objection is more likely from a Justice favoring corrective intervention than from the anticipated objectors to a summary reversal.

Across all outcomes, Kagan is my leading candidate to author a separate writing, followed by Sotomayor and Jackson, principally in opposition to summary reversal. Their respective independent probabilities of writing on the dispositive order are 35%, 20%, and 18%. Other Justices have small independent writing probabilities as recorded in `prediction.json`. These are authorship forecasts, not predictions that every Justice joining a dissent writes one.
