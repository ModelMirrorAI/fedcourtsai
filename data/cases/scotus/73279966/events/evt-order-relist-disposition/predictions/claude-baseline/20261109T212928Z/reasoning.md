- `sg-invited-brief.txt`: the United States **recommends granting** ("the petition for a writ of certiorari should be granted"). It argues the decision below is wrong on intergovernmental immunity, because Washington exempts its own detainees from its minimum-wage law, and on preemption (8 U.S.C. 1555(d), $1/day). It says the decision conflicts with *CoreCivic v. It also notes that recent administrations have taken the same position.
- `lower-court-docket.txt` / SG brief: the Ninth Circuit denied rehearing en banc over dissents (Bumatay, J.; Collins, J.), and Judge Bennett dissented from the panel decision.
- The respondents' supplemental briefs argue that *Hencely v. Fluor* (2026) and *GEO Group v. Menocal* (2026) back the decision below. They also argue that Washington applies the MWA to its own private contract facilities, so the law does not discriminate, and that only Washington's law is relevantly different (per the government's earlier concession). The reply contests the split and the reading of *Hencely*.

Its posture suggests the Court is handling both together, possibly granting one and holding the other.

## Anchors and adjustments
- Band anchor (`cert_anchor`, high band, reached rate): **0.346**.
- Statpack CVSG cut (paid segment, n=164 resolved): granted 29.3% + GVR 5.5% ≈ 35%. Relist cut 2 / 3+ buckets: granted+GVR ≈ 41% / 37% (n=495 / 483). Both cuts include CVSGs where the SG recommended denial. No table splits by SG recommendation. In my judgment, a CVSG where the SG recommends granting and identifies an acknowledged circuit split is granted well over half the time.
- Adjust upward for: SG recommends granting; a published split with the Third Circuit; a federal-interest question about immigration detention, which this administration treats as a priority; a strong vehicle; en banc dissents; a relist right after the SG brief, which rules out a quick denial.
- Adjust downward for: this Court ruled against GEO/contractors twice last Term (*Menocal*, *Hencely*), and respondents argue those decisions resolve this case. Respondents also argue Washington's law is one of a kind, so the split has little practical effect. The relist could be preparing a dissent from denial rather than a grant. The Court might also pick 26-71 as its vehicle and hold this one; the relist suggests it is sorting out how the two cases fit together.
- Net: **0.72**. If granted, I expect plenary review (summary route 0.05). A GVR in light of *Hencely* is unlikely, because *Hencely* cuts toward affirmance rather than requiring reconsideration.
