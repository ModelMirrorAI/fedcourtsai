# Predicted reasoning — GEO Group v. Nwauzor (No. 25-828), CVSG-moment forecast

The snapshot shows one distribution (Conference of 5/14/2026) followed by the
CVSG order of May 18, 2026. I am forecasting from that state.

## Procedural path

- **The Solicitor General will file the invited brief between December 2026 and
  May 2027**, most likely in the December–January window that CVSGs issued in
  May typically target so the Court can act during the same Term. The brief
  will **support GEO and recommend a grant** (or at minimum endorse GEO's
  merits position): the petition credibly recounts that the last three
  administrations backed GEO's intergovernmental-immunity and preemption
  arguments in this and parallel litigation, the judgment forced ICE's
  contractor to suspend the congressionally mandated voluntary work program at
  the Tacoma facility, and the current administration's detention-expansion
  posture makes a federal brief adverse to its own detention contractor very
  unlikely.
- **The petition will be relisted further.** After the SG files, the petition
  will be redistributed at least once — this is near-mechanical for a CVSG'd
  petition — and I expect one to two additional distributions beyond the single
  one now recorded before the Court acts (claim: 0.97 on at least one more).
- **No further CVSG** can issue; the invitation is already on the docket (the
  claim is vacuous for this cell).
- **Disposition: grant of plenary review, most likely between February and
  June 2027.** If the SG files by January, a grant lands in time for argument
  in the fall of 2027 (OT2027).

## What the Court takes

If it grants, the Court takes the **question presented as written** — whether
the Supremacy Clause lets a state reclassify federal immigration detainees in
the federal voluntary work program as employees subject to state minimum-wage
law because a private contractor runs the federal facility. The QP already
bundles both doctrinal routes (intergovernmental immunity and obstacle
preemption); I do not expect a narrowing rewrite, though a reformulation that
splits the immunity and preemption questions into two numbered QPs is
plausible.

## Summary routes and separate writings

- **A GVR is unlikely (~5%)**: no intervening decision bears on the merits.
  *GEO Group v. Menocal* (decided February 25, 2026) resolved only the
  collateral-order appealability of a derivative-sovereign-immunity denial,
  not the Supremacy Clause merits, so it supplies no GVR vehicle.
- **Summary reversal is unlikely (~2%)**: the Ninth Circuit ruled after
  certified-question proceedings in the Washington Supreme Court and a full
  jury trial; the Court would want briefing and argument on a question with
  this structural sweep.
- **If denied** (against my expectation, and most plausibly after an SG deny
  recommendation on vehicle or splitlessness grounds), expect a **dissent from
  denial or statement respecting denial** — Alito and Thomas are likeliest, as
  the Justices most receptive to federal-contractor immunity claims and most
  critical of Ninth Circuit federalism rulings.

## Conditional merits note

If granted, the likelier ground of reversal is discriminatory-treatment
intergovernmental immunity — Washington exempts its own detainees from the
Minimum Wage Act while applying it to federal detainees — rather than the
broader obstacle-preemption theory, because it decides the case without a
sweeping holding about state wage regulation of federal contractors generally.
