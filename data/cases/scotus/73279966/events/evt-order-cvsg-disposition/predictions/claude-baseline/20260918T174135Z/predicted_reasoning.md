# Forecast: what the Court will do with No. 25-828, GEO Group v. Nwauzor

**Disposition: certiorari granted, with plenary briefing and argument.** P(grant) = 0.80.

## Relists and distributions

The snapshot shows **one distribution** (for the May 14, 2026 conference), followed by the
May 18, 2026 CVSG. The Solicitor General filed the United States' brief on September 15,
2026, recommending that the petition be granted. Expect the petition to be redistributed
for an October 2026 conference once the parties' supplemental briefs are in, so at least
one further distribution is near-certain. Expect **one or two** more distributions in
total: a grant either at the first post-CVSG conference or after a single relist. A longer
relist string is unlikely; the residual scenario is the Court deferring this petition to
consider it alongside GEO Group v. Ferguson, No. 26-71 (the Washington HB 1470 facility
regulation case, petition filed July 10, 2026), which would push the grant into December
2026 or January 2027 but still end in a grant.

## CVSG

Already on the docket (May 18, 2026), and already answered. No further CVSG will issue.

## Route, if granted

Plenary review, not a summary disposition. There is no intervening decision to GVR against,
and the question is a contested constitutional one decided after a full jury and bench
trial, with a seven-judge en banc dissent below and a circuit conflict the Solicitor General
endorses. A summary reversal is a remote possibility only.

## Question the Court would take

The petition's single question as written, or the Solicitor General's slightly tighter
reformulation, which foregrounds the discrimination feature (Washington exempts its own
detainees from the Minimum Wage Act). Either way the grant covers both intergovernmental
immunity and preemption; the Court would not split the two. Expect argument in the
February through April 2027 sittings and a decision by the end of June 2027.

## If granted: likely ground (conditional)

Reversal, most likely on the intergovernmental-immunity discrimination prong under
United States v. Washington (2022) and Dawson v. Steager (2019): a Washington law that
reaches the detainees at the only federal detention facility in the state while exempting
state and local detention facilities, in a state that bars itself from contracting with
private detention operators, is not evenhanded. The preemption holding is the less likely
ground, since GEO concedes no federal statute forbids paying more than $1 per day.

## If denied (conditional)

A denial over the Solicitor General's grant recommendation, on a question drawing seven
en banc dissenters, would likely draw a dissent from denial or a statement respecting
denial, most plausibly from Justice Alito, Justice Thomas, or Justice Gorsuch.
