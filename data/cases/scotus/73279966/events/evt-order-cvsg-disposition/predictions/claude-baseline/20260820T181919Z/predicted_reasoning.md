# Forecast — GEO Group, Inc. v. Nwauzor, No. 25-828 (CVSG moment)

Forecast made from the docket as of 2026-05-19: petition filed January 9,
2026, one distribution (Conference of 5/14/2026), CVSG issued May 18, 2026.

**Disposition.** The Court grants certiorari, more likely than not (p = 0.55),
and sets the case for plenary briefing and argument. The path there runs
through the Solicitor General: the United States has supported GEO's
intergovernmental-immunity and preemption position across three
administrations (it said so in the petition, and the current administration's
interest in ICE detention contracting is at its zenith), so I expect the SG's
brief to recommend a grant. If the SG instead recommends denial — most
plausibly on vehicle grounds, since the ICE contract required GEO to comply
with state and local labor laws and ICE told GEO there was "no maximum"
detainee pay — the Court very likely denies.

**Timing.** A CVSG issued in mid-May will not return before the summer recess.
Expect the SG's brief between roughly November 2026 and January 2027, a
redistribution within two to three weeks of its filing, and a disposition at
one of the January–March 2027 conferences. If granted, argument would land in
the fall of OT2027.

**Relists.** The petition sits at one distribution. Once the SG files, it will
be redistributed at least once — this is near-mechanical for a CVSG'd petition
(p = 0.96 on the relist increment) — and one or two further relists after the
SG brief would be unremarkable for a grant of this profile.

**CVSG.** Already on the docket (May 18, 2026); the increment claim is vacuous
for this cell. No second invitation happens (p = 0.02).

**Question taken.** If the Court grants, it takes the QP essentially as
written — whether the Supremacy Clause permits a state to impose its
minimum-wage law on federal immigration detainees in a federal voluntary work
program because a private contractor runs the facility. The QP already bundles
intergovernmental immunity and obstacle preemption; a grant might be styled
"limited to the intergovernmental-immunity question," since that is where the
2d/3d/4th-versus-9th Circuit split (Town of Windsor; CoreCivic v. Governor of
N.J.; the Fourth Circuit line) is cleanest, but I expect no reformulation.
(Registered context-only.)

**Summary disposition.** Unlikely (p = 0.05 conditional on a grant). No
intervening decision supports a GVR — GEO Group v. Menocal (decided February
25, 2026) resolved only a collateral-order appealability question, not these
merits — and the Court does not summarily reverse a fact-laden jury judgment
carrying a contested split; it takes it for argument.

**If denied.** A denial would most likely follow an SG recommendation to deny,
and in that world I still see a meaningful chance (p = 0.20) of a noted
dissent from denial or a statement respecting denial — Justice Thomas or
Justice Alito are the likeliest authors, given the federalism stakes and the
three-administration alignment behind the petitioner's position. Merits-shaped
expectations stay conditional: if granted, the likely ground of decision is
that Washington's scheme discriminates against the federal government and
those with whom it deals, per United States v. Washington, 596 U.S. 832
(2022), which reversed the Ninth Circuit on a neighboring Washington statute.
