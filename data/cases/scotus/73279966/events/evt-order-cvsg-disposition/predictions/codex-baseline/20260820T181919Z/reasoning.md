# Rationale

I assign a 0.48 probability to any grant-family disposition and therefore select denial as the single most likely disposition. The sal-v3 context places this paid petition in the `high` band after one distribution and an observed CVSG. Pooling the bracketed `reached` high-band rows in the committed statpack over Terms 2017-2024, strictly before this petition's OT2025, gives 439 grant-family outcomes among 1,074 weighted resolved petitions, or 40.9%. That is the primary anchor. As a shape check, the statpack's paid scored-segment CVSG cut reports 29.4% plenary grants and 5.5% GVRs (34.9% grant family), while the corresponding no-CVSG population is much lower. I do not add the CVSG cut mechanically to the band anchor because the high band already reflects the observed signal.

I adjust upward from the 40.9% anchor for unusually strong indicators of certworthiness. The Court itself requested the United States' views immediately after the first conference. The petition presents a single legal question, seven Ninth Circuit judges would have reheard the case, and the United States supported GEO's Supremacy Clause position through successive administrations below. The dispute also affects a federal immigration program and private federal contracting beyond the parties, and the petition is represented by experienced Supreme Court counsel.

I stop below 0.50 because both provisioned opposition briefs identify substantial obstacles. They argue that CoreCivic expressly aligned the Third Circuit's approach with the Ninth Circuit's and distinguished neutral burdens from operational bans, substantially weakening the claimed split. They also emphasize record facts that GEO controlled the work program, could pay more than one dollar per day, remained profitable at the state wage, and contracted to follow applicable state labor law. On preemption, the petition concedes that no federal law expressly caps GEO's payment, making this look partly like fact-bound error correction. The briefs' discussion of the Court's recent Menocal contractor-immunity decision is another reason not to assume that federal-contractor status alone will attract four votes. The expected Solicitor General position is the largest uncertainty and could move the grant probability sharply in either direction.

The 0.98 relist-increment probability reflects the CVSG posture rather than an ordinary first-conference relist hazard: absent withdrawal or another unusual termination, the petition must return to a conference after the Solicitor General's brief. The 0.01 CVSG-increment number refers to an additional post-prediction call; the existing May 18 invitation makes that claim vacuous for scoring and a second invitation is implausible. Conditional on a grant, I put only 0.06 on a cert-order summary route because neither side identifies an intervening authority calling for a GVR, and the legal and factual disagreements favor plenary treatment. Conditional on denial, I assign 0.14 to any noted dissent or statement; the issue and procedural history create some pressure for a writing, but silent denials remain the norm.

I used the complete questions presented, the snapshot, the main petition, the complete State opposition brief, and the available portion of the individual respondents' opposition. `documents.json` marks the petition and combined opposition text as truncated: the petition's merits argument and the State brief are complete, but their appendices and the later individual brief are cut off. That limits record-level checking but does not hide the principal cert-stage arguments. I made no live CourtListener or web lookup and did not inspect the current docket beyond the frozen record.
