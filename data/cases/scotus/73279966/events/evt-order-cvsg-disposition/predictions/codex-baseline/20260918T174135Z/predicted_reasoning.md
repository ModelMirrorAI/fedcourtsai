# Forecast

The Court will grant certiorari for plenary consideration of the Supremacy Clause question, rather than resolve the merits summarily. P(any grant) is **0.58**; denial remains a substantial alternative.

The baseline contains **one distribution**, for the May 14, 2026 conference, followed by the **May 18, 2026 CVSG**. From that state, I expect the Solicitor General's brief, supplemental party submissions, and one or two additional conference distributions before disposition. P(at least one additional distribution) is **0.96**. The first post-CVSG redistribution counts toward this increment even if it would not colloquially be called a relist. I expect a disposition in late 2026 or the first half of 2027, with considerable uncertainty from the government's briefing schedule. This timing is a forecast from the supplied record, not an assertion about developments since May.

A CVSG has already issued. I forecast no fresh invitation; the **0.00** CVSG-increment entry is vacuous for this moment and is not a prediction that the recorded invitation will disappear. I expect the United States to support review, emphasizing interference with its detention program and the protection of federal contractors.

If review is granted, I expect the Court to address the substance of GEO's single question: whether applying Washington's minimum-wage law to participants in the contractor-operated federal detention work program violates the Supremacy Clause. The focus will be intergovernmental immunity, particularly the boundary between permissible regulation of a contractor and interference with federal operations, with discriminatory treatment and preemption as related grounds. I do not forecast wholesale immunity for every federal contractor from ordinary state employment regulation.

Conditional on a grant, **0.10** is assigned to disposition in the cert order itself and **0.90** to plenary proceedings. The disputed comparison between public and private detention, the contract's state-law obligations, and the absence of an identified intervening controlling decision favor full briefing over a GVR or summary reversal. Conditional on plenary review, the likely substantive focus is whether this particular application of the wage law crosses the constitutional line, not whether federal contracting automatically extinguishes state regulatory power.

Conditional on denial, P(a noted dissent or statement respecting denial) is **0.20**. The modal denial is an unexplained order without separate writing; no individual Justice's vote or authorship is forecast.
