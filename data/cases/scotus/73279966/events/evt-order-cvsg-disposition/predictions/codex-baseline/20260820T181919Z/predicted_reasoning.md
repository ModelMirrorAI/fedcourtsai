# Forecast

The petition has been distributed once, for the May 14, 2026 conference, and the Court then invited the Solicitor General's views on May 18. The petition will almost certainly be distributed at least once more after the Solicitor General files; one post-brief distribution is most likely, with a meaningful chance of one additional relist before disposition. A second call for the Solicitor General's views is not expected because the existing invitation already supplies that input.

The most likely ultimate disposition is denial of certiorari, although grant and plenary review are nearly as likely. The Solicitor General is likely to support GEO's federal-interest concerns, but the Court may still conclude that the asserted circuit conflict is not clean: the respondent briefs argue that the other circuits use the same distinction between permissible incidental burdens on contractors and impermissible control of federal operations.

If the Court grants, it is likely to reformulate the question around whether applying a generally applicable state wage law to a federal detention contractor directly regulates or discriminates against federal operations. It would probably leave broader obstacle-preemption questions secondary. Plenary briefing and argument are much more likely than a summary disposition because there is no identified intervening decision that naturally supports a GVR, and the competing characterizations of Washington's law and GEO's contract make summary reversal unsuitable.

If certiorari is denied, the likeliest order is unexplained and carries no separate writing. There is a modest chance that one or more Justices issue a statement or dissent because the petition implicates federal immigration operations, attracted seven votes for rehearing below, and follows a CVSG. If plenary review is granted, the likely merits fault line will be whether the state law merely raises a contractor's costs or instead has the practical effect of controlling a federal program.
