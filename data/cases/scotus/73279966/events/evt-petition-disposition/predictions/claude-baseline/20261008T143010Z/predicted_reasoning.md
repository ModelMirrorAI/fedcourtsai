# Predicted reasoning — what the Court will do with No. 25-828

**Bottom line.** The Court grants certiorari and sets the case for plenary briefing and argument, most likely in an order issued after the November 6, 2026 conference or the one or two conferences that follow it. P(grant family) = 0.78.

**State the docket is forecast from.** Two distributions are on the record: April 28, 2026 for the May 14 conference (which produced the May 18 CVSG rather than a disposition), and October 7, 2026 for the November 6 conference, the first conference after the Solicitor General's September 15 brief and the respondents' September 29 supplemental briefs. A CVSG is already on the docket, so no further CVSG can issue; the `cvsg-increment` claim is vacuous and the 0.01 written there is a formality.

**Relists.** I expect at least one further distribution past the two shown (P = 0.60), and more likely one or two than many. Two reasons: the Court's modern habit of relisting once before a grant to check the vehicle, and the fact that GEO's companion petition against Washington's facility-regulation statute, No. 26-71, was rescheduled on October 6 and redistributed on October 7 for the same November 6 conference. Pairing the two petitions is the kind of thing that produces a held-over conference or a relist of one while the other is sorted out. If the Court instead acts at the first conference, the likelier form is a grant, not a denial.

**Which question the Court takes.** If it grants, the Court takes the petition's single Supremacy Clause question essentially as written, possibly in the SG's reformulation, which foregrounds the discrimination theory (Washington exempts its own detainees but not federal ones). I do not expect the Court to split the question into the respondents' three sub-questions. The most probable framing of the grant is on the intergovernmental-immunity discrimination prong with the preemption prong riding along; the Court may hold No. 26-71 for this case or grant and consolidate it, with a hold the likelier of the two.

**Route.** Plenary review, not a summary disposition (P(cert-order route | grant) = 0.05). There is no intervening decision to GVR in light of: Hencely v. Fluor (April 22, 2026) cuts for respondents, not petitioner, so a GVR would make no sense, and a contested constitutional question with a $37 million judgment and a certified state-law ruling is not summary-reversal material. Timing: a grant in November or December 2026 puts argument in the February through April 2027 sittings and a decision by late June 2027.

**If the Court denies.** A denial after the SG's grant recommendation and a seven-judge dissent from the Ninth Circuit's en banc denial would draw a written objection more often than not, but silent denials of SG-backed petitions are not rare: P(dissent or statement respecting denial | denial) = 0.45. The likeliest author is Justice Thomas or Justice Alito; the "from whom" half is context only.

**Conditional merits view (context only).** If granted, the likeliest ground is that applying the Minimum Wage Act to GEO discriminates against the federal government and its contractors under United States v. Washington (2022), because the state exempts residents of state, county and municipal detention institutions and not federal detainees; the "same immunity as the government" theory of direct regulation is the part Hencely makes hardest to win, and I would expect the Court to avoid resting on it. Reversal would be the probable judgment, but nothing on this cell resolves that.
