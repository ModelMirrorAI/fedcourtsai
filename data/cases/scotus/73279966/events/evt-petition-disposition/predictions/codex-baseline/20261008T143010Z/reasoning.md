## Case-specific adjustment

The strongest upward evidence beyond that anchor is **the Solicitor General's express recommendation to grant**, not merely the docket entry saying that a government brief exists. The United States' brief, printed pages 9–10 and 19–23, endorses review on intergovernmental immunity and preemption, argues conflict with the Third Circuit's CoreCivic decision, identifies operational consequences, and emphasizes final judgments and resolved state-law questions. The petition's QP and printed pages 17–23 frame a potentially consequential limit on states' regulation of federal contractors. The government brief also identifies seven judges favoring rehearing below. These considerations materially strengthen the prospect of four votes to hear the case, though they do not establish a clean split as fact.

There are substantial downward adjustments. Both provisioned BIO introductions argue that the supposed conflict concerns applications of a shared standard rather than incompatible legal rules. They emphasize the contract's state-law compliance requirement and the distinction between a reimbursement limit and a prohibition on higher compensation. More importantly, the Nwauzor supplemental brief, printed pages 1–4, says June 2026 detention standards change the prospective compensation rules, reducing the practical reach of a case over the earlier contract; it expressly does not claim formal mootness. It also contests the government's assertion that comparable state contractors are absent. Washington's supplement, printed pages 1–4 and 12–13, invokes Hencely against expansive contractor-immunity and preemption arguments and reinforces the state-specific vehicle objections. These remain attributed advocacy: I did not separately retrieve the revised standards, underlying contracts, or Hencely opinion. Together they prevent treating the favorable CVSG response as near-dispositive.

The resulting 0.68 is a judgmental adjustment from the 0.3495 anchor, not a fitted probability for favorable SG recommendations. The largest uncertainty is whether the Justices view the revised standards and disputed comparator facts as making this a poor vehicle despite federal support.
