# Forecast of the Court's action

The Court will most likely **grant certiorari for plenary review**. I assign 68% to any grant and expect a grant, if it occurs, to set the case for briefing and argument rather than immediately decide the merits.

The baseline contains **two distributions**: April 28 for the May 14 conference and October 7 for the November 6 conference. The Court has already called for the Solicitor General's views on May 18, and the United States has filed its response. From this state, I forecast **one further distribution** as the modal path before action, with **62% probability of at least one additional distribution**. A decision in November or December 2026 is the central timing forecast; timing is context, not a scored claim.

I forecast **no further CVSG**. The existing invitation has been answered; the required CVSG-increment probability is 0.0 and is vacuous for this already-CVSG record.

If review is granted, the Court will likely take the petition's core question: whether intergovernmental immunity or federal preemption prevents Washington from applying its minimum-wage law to detained workers in a privately operated federal immigration facility. The practical focus will be the treatment of federal contractors compared with other detention operators and the legal force of the federal compensation framework. I expect review of that question, not a free-standing reconsideration of the damages amount. The respondents' argument that revised detention standards reduce the dispute's prospective importance presents the principal alternative path to denial.

**Conditional on a grant, the probability of disposition in the cert order is 10%.** Plenary review is much more likely than summary reversal or a GVR: the opposing submissions dispute comparator facts, contract significance, and the reach of related precedent, rather than identifying an uncontested mechanical remand. Any merits holding remains conditional on review; I do not forecast a merits judgment here.

**Conditional on denial, the probability of any noted dissent or statement is 22%.** The modal denial would therefore be unexplained and without a separate writing. I make no individual-Justice prediction.
