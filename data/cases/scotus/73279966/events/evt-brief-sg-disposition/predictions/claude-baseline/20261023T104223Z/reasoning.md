# Reasoning — P(grant) = 0.80

**Inputs read.** Snapshot `record/snapshots/2026-09-15.json` (cutoff
2026-09-16, forward mode), `context.json` (band `high` under sal-v4,
distribution_count 1, CVSG 2026-05-18), and the provisioned documents: the
QP, the petition (truncated), the BIO (truncated; I read its split section),
the cert reply, and the SG's invited brief (29 pp., full text, not OCR).

**What the Solicitor General recommended.** Grant. The brief's opening and
conclusion both state "the petition for a writ of certiorari should be
granted." It argues the decision below is wrong on intergovernmental immunity
(discrimination: Washington exempts its own detainees) and on preemption;
that it conflicts with or is in tension with the Third Circuit's *CoreCivic v.
Governor of New Jersey* (2025) and older Second and Fourth Circuit decisions;
that the issue is exceptionally important to nationwide federal detention
contracting; and that the case is an "ideal vehicle" (final judgments after
trials, state-law question settled by the state supreme court on
certification, thorough opinions including Judge Bennett's panel dissent and
Judge Bumatay's dissent from denial of rehearing en banc).

**Anchors.** The statpack's CVSG cut (paid scored segment, all Terms pooled)
gives a grant family of ~34.8% (granted 29.3% + gvr 5.5%, n≈164 resolved).
The high-band bracketed `reached` rate over the prior Terms shown runs
~31–42% (OT2025 41.1%, n=73; OT2024 40.5%, n=116). Both are the population
*before* the SG weighs in; no committed table splits CVSG cases by the SG's
recommendation.

**Adjustment.** My judgement, not a committed figure: the Court follows an SG
grant recommendation in a large majority of CVSG cases (my recollection of
the literature is on the order of 70–80%), versus a low single-digit-to-teens
rate after a deny recommendation; averaging those across the pooled CVSG
population is consistent with the ~35% anchor. Case-specific factors push up
from the "SG says grant" conditional: a federal-interest question the United
States itself presses (the federal government's own detention operations), a
claimed conflict with the Third Circuit, two dissents below with an
en banc dissent joined by multiple judges, a ~$37M judgment and suspension of
the federal program, elite counsel (Clement), and an amicus brief at the
petition stage. Pushing down: the BIO's argument that *CoreCivic* applies the
same test and there is no true split (the Third Circuit struck a ban, the
Ninth upheld a wage law — different burdens), and the possibility the Court
prefers to wait for, or pair with, No. 26-71 (which would more likely delay
than defeat a grant). Net 0.80.
