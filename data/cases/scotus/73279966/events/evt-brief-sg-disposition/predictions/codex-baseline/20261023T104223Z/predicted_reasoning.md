# Forecast

The Court will grant plenary review of GEO's Supremacy Clause challenge to applying Washington's minimum-wage law to detainees participating in the voluntary work program at its federally contracted detention facility.

The provisioned docket shows **one conference distribution**, for May 14, 2026, followed by the May 18 CVSG and the September 15 invited brief. From that state, expect a post-SG conference distribution and probably one additional distribution before a grant: roughly one to two further distributions, with two my modal forecast. P(at least one further distribution) is **0.99**. The first ordinary post-SG distribution itself satisfies this claim; it need not represent a genuine relist after conference consideration. Expect disposition during fall 2026 or early 2027 rather than an extended hold. This describes the expected progression from the September 15 record, not an observation of the docket's position on the run date.

The Court has already obtained the Solicitor General's views. No new CVSG is expected; the increment claim is vacuous for this cell. The invited brief recommends a grant, not a hold, GVR, or denial.

If granted, expect a question substantially matching the SG's formulation: whether the Supremacy Clause permits a State to apply its minimum-wage statute to federal immigration detainees in privately operated detention facilities while exempting detainees in its own facilities. The principal focus will be intergovernmental immunity, particularly the comparator for discriminatory treatment and the distinction between regulating federal detention operations and imposing ordinary costs on a contractor. Preemption may remain an alternative ground rather than requiring an independent question.

Conditional on a grant, expect briefing and argument rather than disposition in the cert order: **P(summary route | grant) = 0.08**. The dispute concerns the reach of existing immunity doctrine and the characterization of this program, not an identified intervening decision that straightforwardly requires a GVR. If the Court reaches the merits, the more likely basis for relief is discriminatory treatment of federal detention operations, without a categorical exemption of all federal contractors from generally applicable labor laws.

If review is denied, expect no separate writing, although a dissent from denial or statement respecting denial is a meaningful minority possibility: **P(any such writing | denial) = 0.24**. No individual Justice's vote or authorship is forecast.
