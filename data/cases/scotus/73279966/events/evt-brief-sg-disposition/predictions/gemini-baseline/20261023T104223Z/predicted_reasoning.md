## Conditional Merits Outlook (If Granted)

If granted, the Court is likely to reverse the Ninth Circuit and hold that applying Washington's Minimum Wage Act to detainee participants in the Voluntary Work Program at the Northwest ICE Processing Center violates the Supremacy Clause:
1. **Intergovernmental Immunity (Discrimination):** Following *United States v. Washington*, 596 U.S. 832 (2022), and *Dawson v. Steager*, 586 U.S. 171 (2019), the Court is likely to agree with Judge Bennett's panel dissent and the Solicitor General that Washington's statute discriminates against the federal government and its contractor. Washington exempts inmates and residents of "state, county, or municipal" detention institutions (`Wash. Rev. Code § 49.46.010(4)(k)`) while prohibiting state-contracted private prisons (`Wash. Rev. Code § 72.68.110(1)`), leaving the federal contractor-operated NWIPC as the sole detention facility in the State required to pay detainees the state minimum wage ($17+ per hour).
2. **Intergovernmental Immunity (Direct Regulation) & Preemption:** The Court will also address whether classifying federal immigration detainees in a Congressionally authorized work program (`8 U.S.C. § 1231(g)(1)`, `8 U.S.C. § 1555(d)`) as private "employees" directly interferes with federal immigration detention operations or stands as an obstacle to the federal statutory and regulatory scheme (PBNDS § 5.8).
