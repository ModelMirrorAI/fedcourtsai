## What happened

The GEO Group runs a privately owned immigration detention facility in Tacoma, Washington, under contract with federal Immigration and Customs Enforcement. For years, GEO paid detainees who worked in the facility's kitchen, laundry, and cleaning operations $1 to $5 per day through a voluntary work program. Washington State and a class of detainees, led by Ugochukwu Nwauzor, sued GEO for violating the state's minimum wage law. After a jury trial, GEO was ordered to pay millions in back wages and unjust-enrichment damages, and courts required GEO to pay the state minimum wage going forward. The Ninth Circuit affirmed, and the full appeals court declined to rehear the case. GEO then asked the Supreme Court to step in.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked whether a state may require a private company holding federal immigration detainees to pay them the state's minimum wage, even though the company operates under a federal contract and the state exempts detainees held in its own government-run facilities from that same wage law. GEO argues this amounts to the state improperly regulating and discriminating against the federal government and its contractor, and that federal law overrides the state requirement. Washington and the detainees respond that the wage law applies neutrally to all private employers regardless of who they contract with, does not control any federal operation, and is not overridden by any federal statute.

## Where it stands

The petition has been fully briefed, including opposition briefs from Washington and the detainees and a reply from GEO, and the Court invited the Solicitor General to submit the federal government's views (a step called a CVSG); that brief has since been filed, but the docket does not yet show any further action by the Court on the petition.
