## In brief

GEO Group asks the Court to decide whether Washington can require it to pay detainees in its privately run immigration facility the state minimum wage.

## What happened

GEO Group operates a private immigration detention facility in Tacoma, Washington, under contract with federal immigration authorities. Detainees there worked in a voluntary program for $1 to $5 per day. Washington State and a class of detainees sued, arguing this violated the state's minimum wage law. A jury and judge ruled against GEO, awarding millions in damages, and the Ninth Circuit Court of Appeals affirmed, with one judge dissenting. The full Ninth Circuit declined to rehear the case. GEO then asked the Supreme Court to step in.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked whether a constitutional rule that shields the federal government from certain state laws also protects a private company that contracts with the federal government, so that Washington cannot apply its minimum wage law to detainees working at GEO's facility. The Court is also asked whether federal law, which caps reimbursement for this work program at $1 per day, overrides the state's minimum wage requirement.

## Where it stands

The petition has been distributed for conference more than once, and the Court asked the federal government's Solicitor General for its views, which were later filed. The case is now distributed again for a further conference, with no decision yet recorded.

## What each outcome would mean

GEO Group is asking the Court to hear the case. If the Court grants review, it will decide the legal questions after full briefing and argument, resolving nothing yet on its own. If it denies review, the Ninth Circuit's decision affirming the judgment against GEO Group stands, and GEO Group remains required to comply with the lower court's rulings in favor of Washington State and the detainee class. A grant would not indicate who is likely to prevail once the case is heard.
