## In brief

GEO Group asks the Court to decide whether Washington's minimum wage law can require it to pay immigrant detainees who work at its privately run federal detention facility.

## What happened

GEO Group operates a detention facility in Tacoma, Washington under contract with federal immigration authorities. Detainees there could work in a voluntary program, typically earning $1 to $5 per day. Washington State and a class of detainees sued, arguing this violated the state's minimum wage law. After trial, courts ordered GEO to pay back wages and unjust-enrichment damages, and enjoined further underpayment. The Ninth Circuit affirmed, rejecting GEO's arguments that the state law improperly reached federal operations or was preempted by federal law, though one judge dissented. The full Ninth Circuit declined to rehear the case, over several judges' dissents.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked whether a constitutional principle that shields the federal government from state interference also protects a private company that contracts with the federal government to run a detention facility, such that the state cannot require it to pay its state minimum wage to detainees working there, and whether federal law otherwise overrides the state wage requirement.

## Where it stands

The petition was distributed for conference, and the Court then invited the Solicitor General to file a brief giving the federal government's views; that brief has been filed, along with supplemental briefs from both sides. The docket shows no decision yet on whether the Court will hear the case.

## What each outcome would mean

GEO Group is asking the Court to take up the case. If the Court grants review, it would hear full arguments and decide the legal question itself, which would not by itself determine who wins. If the Court denies review, the Ninth Circuit's decision affirming the judgment against GEO Group would stand, leaving Washington and the detainee class with their wage award and the injunction in place. The Solicitor General's brief, filed at the Court's request, may address whether the case merits review but does not itself decide the outcome.
