## In brief

GEO Group asks the Court to review a ruling that it must pay Washington's minimum wage to immigration detainees working in its federally contracted detention facility.

## What happened

GEO Group operates a private immigration detention facility in Tacoma, Washington, under contract with Immigration and Customs Enforcement. Detainees who volunteered for a work program were paid $1 to $5 per day. Washington State and a class of detainees sued, arguing this violated the state's minimum wage law. After trial, courts awarded the detainees back pay and the state unjust-enrichment damages, and the Ninth Circuit affirmed, rejecting GEO's arguments that the state law improperly reached federal operations and was preempted by federal law. GEO asked the full Ninth Circuit to rehear the case, which it declined to do over several judges' dissents.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked whether a state can require a private company holding federal immigration detainees under government contract to pay those detainees the state minimum wage, even though the company's contract and federal policy set a lower rate, and even though the state exempts detainees in its own facilities from this same wage law. The question involves two ideas: whether this amounts to a state directly controlling federal operations or unfairly treating federal contractors worse than comparable state ones, and whether federal law and policy override the state wage law.

## Where it stands

The petition has been fully briefed and distributed for conference more than once. The Court invited the Solicitor General to weigh in, and the federal government filed a brief supporting GEO's request that the Court hear the case. The case was distributed again for a conference after that brief was filed, and the docket shows no decision yet on whether the Court will take the case.

## What each outcome would mean

GEO Group, which lost below, is asking the Court to hear the case. If the Court grants review, it will decide the legal questions itself after full briefing and argument, and the outcome could favor either side; a grant alone decides nothing. If the Court denies review, the Ninth Circuit's decision stands, meaning GEO Group must comply with Washington's minimum wage law for detainee workers and the damages awards in favor of the state and the detainee class remain in place.
