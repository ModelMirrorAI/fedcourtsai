## In brief

GEO Group asks the Court to overturn rulings that it must pay Washington's minimum wage to immigration detainees who work at its privately run detention facility.

## What happened

GEO Group runs a Tacoma, Washington detention facility under contract with federal immigration authorities, employing detainees in a work program that pays $1 to $5 per day. Washington State and a class of detainees sued, arguing this violated the state's minimum wage law. After a jury trial, GEO was ordered to pay millions in back wages and unjust enrichment, and was enjoined from continuing to underpay detainees. The Ninth Circuit, after getting guidance from the Washington Supreme Court on state-law questions, affirmed, rejecting GEO's arguments that the state law improperly intruded on federal authority. GEO then asked the Supreme Court to review that decision.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked whether a constitutional rule that shields federal government operations from state interference also protects a private company that contracts with the federal government to run a detention facility, when the state requires that company to pay its own minimum wage to detainees who work there. GEO argues federal law and its contract with immigration authorities should override the state wage law; Washington and the detainees argue the state law applies neutrally to any private employer and does not control federal operations.

## Where it stands

The petition was distributed for conference and, after that conference, the Court asked the Solicitor General for the federal government's views on whether to take the case. The government has since filed a brief, and both sides have filed supplemental briefs. The docket does not show any further action on the petition since those filings.

## What each outcome would mean

GEO Group asked the Court to take up the case. If the Court grants review, it will decide the legal question itself, and the case would proceed to full briefing and argument without either side yet prevailing. If the Court denies review, the Ninth Circuit's decision affirming the judgment against GEO stands, leaving Washington and the detainees the winners below and requiring GEO to comply with the state minimum wage law.
