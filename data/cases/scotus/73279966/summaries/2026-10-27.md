## In brief

GEO Group asks the Court to overturn rulings that it must pay Washington's minimum wage to immigration detainees who work in its privately run detention facility.

## What happened

GEO Group runs a private immigration detention facility in Tacoma, Washington, under contract with federal immigration authorities. Detainees who volunteer to work there were paid as little as $1 a day. Washington State and a class of detainees sued, arguing this violated the state's minimum wage law. After trial, courts ordered GEO to pay back wages and stopped it from continuing the practice without paying minimum wage. The Ninth Circuit affirmed, rejecting GEO's arguments that the Constitution's Supremacy Clause barred the state law from applying. GEO then asked the Supreme Court to review that decision.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked whether a state can require a private company that houses federal immigration detainees to pay those detainees the state's minimum wage for work they perform, even though the company operates under a federal contract and federal rules set a lower baseline payment. The dispute centers on whether applying the state wage law improperly interferes with or discriminates against federal operations, and whether federal law overrides the state requirement.

## Where it stands

The Court asked the Solicitor General to weigh in, and the federal government filed a brief supporting GEO and urging the Court to take the case. Both sides then filed supplemental briefs, and the petition has been distributed for a conference the docket shows is scheduled after the most recent filings.

## What each outcome would mean

GEO Group is asking the Court to take up the case and reverse the lower court's decision. If the Court grants review and ultimately sides with GEO, Washington's minimum wage law could not be applied to detainees working at GEO's facility, undoing the damages award and injunction against GEO. If the Court grants review and sides with the respondents, Washington and the detainees, the lower court's judgment requiring GEO to pay minimum wage would be upheld. If the Court denies review, the Ninth Circuit's decision favoring Washington and the detainees remains in effect, though a denial would not itself be a ruling on who is right.
