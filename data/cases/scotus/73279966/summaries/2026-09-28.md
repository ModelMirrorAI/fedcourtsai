## In brief

GEO Group asks the Court to overturn rulings that it must pay Washington's minimum wage to immigrant detainees who work at its privately run detention facility.

## What happened

GEO Group operates a privately owned immigration detention facility in Tacoma, Washington, under contract with Immigration and Customs Enforcement. Detainees there work in jobs like cooking and laundry through a voluntary work program, typically earning $1 to $5 per day. Washington State and a class of detainees sued, claiming this violated the state's minimum wage law. After a trial, a jury and judge ruled against GEO, awarding backpay and ordering GEO to stop underpaying. The Ninth Circuit, after getting input from the Washington Supreme Court on state law questions, affirmed. GEO then asked the full Ninth Circuit to rehear the case, which it declined, with several judges dissenting.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked whether a state can require a private company holding federal immigration detainees under a federal contract to pay those detainees the state's minimum wage, even though federal rules cap reimbursement for such work at $1 per day and the company treats the detainees as participants in a voluntary program rather than employees.

## Where it stands

The petition was distributed for conference, and the Court then invited the Solicitor General to file a brief giving the federal government's views; that brief has been filed, but the docket does not show any further action on the petition.

## What each outcome would mean

GEO Group is asking the Court to hear its case and overturn the Ninth Circuit's decision. If the Court grants review, it would hear full arguments and decide the legal question itself, without deciding in advance who is right. If it denies review, the Ninth Circuit's decision favoring Washington State and the detainees stands, and GEO must comply with the minimum wage ruling; a denial would not mean the Court agrees with that outcome, only that the case ends there.
