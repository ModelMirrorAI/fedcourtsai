## In brief

GEO Group asks the Court to decide whether Washington can require it to pay state minimum wage to immigrant detainees working at its federal detention facility.

## What happened

GEO Group operates a private immigration detention facility in Tacoma, Washington under contract with federal Immigration and Customs Enforcement. Detainees who worked in a voluntary program there were usually paid $1 to $5 per day. Washington State and a class of detainees sued, arguing this violated the state's minimum wage law. After a jury trial, GEO was ordered to pay millions in back wages and unjust enrichment, and was enjoined from continuing the practice. The Ninth Circuit, after getting input from the Washington Supreme Court on state-law questions, affirmed, rejecting GEO's arguments that the state law improperly interfered with federal operations or was preempted by federal law. The full Ninth Circuit declined to rehear the case, over several judges' dissents.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked whether a constitutional doctrine protecting the federal government from state interference blocks Washington from applying its minimum wage law to detainees held at a privately run federal immigration facility, where the state exempts detainees in its own government-run facilities from that same law. The question includes whether state law conflicts with federal rules governing detainee work programs.

## Where it stands

The petition has been fully briefed, including a brief from the Solicitor General, invited by the Court, supporting GEO Group's request that the case be heard. The case was distributed for conference again in November 2026, and the docket shows no decision yet on whether the Court will take the case.

## What each outcome would mean

GEO Group, the private contractor, is asking the Court to hear the case. If the Court grants the petition, it will hear full arguments and decide the legal question itself, without yet deciding who wins. If the Court denies the petition, the Ninth Circuit's decision stands, meaning Washington's minimum wage law would continue to apply to detainees at GEO's facility, and the damages awards to the state and the detainee class would remain in effect.
