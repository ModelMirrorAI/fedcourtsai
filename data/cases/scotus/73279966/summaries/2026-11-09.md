## In brief

GEO Group asks the Court to overturn rulings that it must pay Washington's minimum wage to immigrant detainees who work at its privately run federal detention facility.

## What happened

GEO Group operates a federal immigration detention facility in Tacoma, Washington, under contract with Immigration and Customs Enforcement. Detainees there could work in a voluntary program, usually earning $1 a day. Washington state and a class of detainees sued, arguing this violated the state's minimum wage law. After a jury trial and related proceedings, GEO lost, was ordered to pay back wages and turn over profits, and was enjoined from continuing the program without paying minimum wage. The Ninth Circuit affirmed, rejecting GEO's arguments that the federal Constitution shields it from the state law. GEO's request for rehearing by the full Ninth Circuit was denied, with several judges dissenting.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked whether a state can require a private company holding federal immigration detainees to pay them the state's minimum wage for work performed in a federal program, even though the state does not impose that same wage requirement on detainees held in its own government-run facilities, and even though federal law caps what the government will reimburse the company for such pay.

## What each outcome would mean

GEO Group is asking the Court to take up the case and reverse the Ninth Circuit, which ruled against it. If the Court grants review, it would hear full arguments and decide the legal questions itself; a grant alone would not determine who wins. If the Court denies review, the Ninth Circuit's decision stands, meaning GEO must comply with Washington's minimum wage law and the damages awards against it remain in place. A denial would not mean the Court agrees the lower court was right, only that the case ends there.
