## In brief

GEO Group asks the Court to review a ruling that it must pay Washington's minimum wage to detainees working at its privately run immigration facility.

## What happened

GEO Group runs a privately owned immigration detention facility in Tacoma, Washington, under contract with federal immigration authorities. Detainees there work in jobs like cooking and cleaning, generally paid $1 to $5 per day. Washington State and a class of detainees sued, arguing this violated the state's minimum wage law. After trial, courts ordered GEO to pay back wages and other sums, and the Ninth Circuit Court of Appeals affirmed, rejecting GEO's arguments that the state law improperly intruded on federal authority. GEO asked the full Ninth Circuit to reconsider, and it declined.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked whether a state can require a private company to pay its state minimum wage to detainees held at a federal immigration facility the company operates, when federal rules otherwise cap reimbursable pay at $1 a day and the state does not apply its wage law to detainees at its own government-run facilities.

## Where it stands

The petition has been fully briefed, including an amicus brief and supplemental briefs from both sides, and the Court asked the federal government for its views through the Solicitor General, who has since filed a brief. The case awaits the Court's decision on whether to hear it.

## What each outcome would mean

GEO Group is asking the Court to take up the case after losing in the lower courts. If the Court grants review, it will hear full arguments and decide the legal question itself, with no party assured of winning at that stage. If the Court denies review, the Ninth Circuit's decision stands, leaving in place the rulings that GEO must comply with Washington's minimum wage law and pay the damages awarded to the state and to the detainee class led by Nwauzor. A denial would not mean the Court agrees with that outcome, only that the lower court's decision remains final.
