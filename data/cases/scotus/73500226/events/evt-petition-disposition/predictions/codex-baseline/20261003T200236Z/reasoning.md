## Prior and adjustment

The governing anchor is the **bracketed reached-state rate** in the committed `metrics/statpack.md` sal-v4 table. Pooling every rendered Term strictly earlier than 2025 gives Terms 2017–2024. The corresponding `metrics/statpack.json` fields supply unrounded arithmetic: 89 grant-family outcomes over 392 weighted resolved reached-state petitions, or **22.704%**. I exclude the 2025 and 2026 rows. This is the state class's risk set, not the federal-petitioner rate merely because the United States is a respondent.

The modern whole-docket counts imply roughly 2.8% any-grant, and the Sixth Circuit cut is likewise around 2.8%; neither is the appropriate replacement for the selected state anchor. I also read the paid-segment relist and CVSG cuts. Their grant-family shares are approximately 1.7%, 13.3%, 40.9%, and 36.8% for terminal relist buckets 0, 1, 2, and 3+, and 6.3% without a CVSG versus 34.9% with one. These are terminal-state population descriptions, not transition probabilities from this case's one-distribution state. I do not multiply their apparent boosts into the state rate or mistake an SG party brief for a CVSG.

I raise the 22.7% anchor to **48% any grant** principally because this is now an acknowledged, post-remand circuit conflict over the constitutionality of a federal statute, with both respondent groups affirmatively asking the Court to resolve the enforcement question. The petition alone says the Fifth and Eighth Circuit remands were pending when it was filed. The later, pre-decision responses and reply establish a materially stronger setting: the Fifth Circuit again rejected the enforcement provisions, and the government and Authority filed petitions Nos. 26-201 and 26-199. I use that information as reported in the August and September filings, not as a claim to have independently checked those dockets today.

The principal reason not to forecast an overwhelming grant probability for **this petition** is vehicle competition. Both responses ask the Court to grant the Fifth Circuit petitions and hold Oklahoma. The federal response distinguishes the enforcement split from the rulemaking question, on which it reports agreement among the circuits; the Authority makes the same distinction. High probability of Supreme Court review of the shared issue is not high probability of plenary review of every petition presenting it. A hold followed by denial is a substantial outcome when the Sixth Circuit already sustained the law.

The reply supplies a serious counterweight: it identifies an appellate-finality objection in the Fifth Circuit case involving five claims allegedly left unresolved after a partial voluntary dismissal, and argues for taking Oklahoma alone or consolidating all the petitions. I treat this as a party's vehicle argument, not an adjudicated jurisdictional defect. It increases the chance this petition becomes a plenary vehicle or protective companion grant. The reply also accepts narrowing review to enforcement if necessary.

For the hold path, the federal response's supervision and facial-challenge arguments give a credible route to leaving the Sixth Circuit judgment intact. Petitioners' contrary distinction between private enforcement discretion and after-the-fact agency review is substantial, so a companion ruling requiring reconsideration here remains quite plausible. These are conditional merits assessments used to price eventual denial versus GVR; they are not forecasts that a denial will carry a majority opinion.

An approximate internally consistent allocation is 19.2% plenary grant, 28.8% summary grant-family disposition, 50% denial, and 2% other non-grant termination. Thus 48% any grant and 60% summary route conditional on grant describe the same belief. Denial is the modal terminal label and non-grant remains narrowly more likely in the binary. The figures are subjective estimates, not frequencies measured for this unusually specific companion posture.

## Procedural claims and significance

The **0.94 additional-distribution probability** is driven by the explicit rescheduling and expected coordination with the companion cases, not by a claim that the Court has already substantively relisted this petition. One or two further distribution entries are the central estimate, with a long hold a real possibility. **0.01 CVSG** reflects an existing federal-party brief addressing the exact issue. **0.60 conditional summary route** principally represents later GVRs after a companion ruling, rather than a belief that this difficult dispute is ready for immediate summary reversal. **0.10 conditional denial-writing probability** allows for constitutional concern but discounts a separate writing if the issue has been aired in a companion merits decision.

The **0.76 significance score** concerns the consequences of resolving private regulatory enforcement and national horseracing oversight, not grant likelihood. The respondents' discussion of other self-regulatory structures supports significance beyond a single industry; how far a ruling would actually extend remains uncertain. I omit cert votes rather than invent a publicly observable lineup.

The largest uncertainties are vehicle selection, whether the claimed Fifth Circuit finality problem matters, and how a companion ruling would map onto this judgment. The missing provisioned responses were recoverable, so the data-quality note is informational rather than blocking. No fresh companion docket or ruling was sought to resolve those uncertainties.
