# Forecast: Oklahoma v. United States, No. 25-1325 (evt-petition-disposition)

**State forecast from.** The docket shows one distribution (for the Conference of
October 9, 2026), followed on September 21 by a "Rescheduled" entry; no CVSG, two
amicus briefs at the cert stage, responses from both the federal respondents and the
Authority, and a reply. The petition has never been considered at conference.

**Relists.** The petition will be distributed again, almost certainly for a November
2026 conference, so that it is considered together with the four petitions from the
Fifth Circuit's June 11, 2026 decision in *National Horsemen's Benevolent &
Protective Ass'n v. Black* (Nos. 26-199 and 26-201, the Authority's and the FTC's
petitions, whose replies were filed September 30; and Nos. 26-332 and 26-335, the
Gulf Coast Racing and Texas petitions, with responses due October 13 and 14). Expect
the redistribution in late October, and one or two further relists of the cluster
before the Court acts on it, in the ordinary way for a grant. If the Court grants the
Fifth Circuit petitions and holds this one (the government's requested disposition),
this docket will then sit without further distributions until the merits decision in
*National Horsemen*, and will be distributed one more time for the clean-up
conference in late June or early July 2027.

**CVSG.** None. The Solicitor General is already a party as counsel for the federal
respondents and has filed a brief in opposition; a call for the views of the
Solicitor General is not a live possibility here.

**Which question, and by what route.** The Court will grant review of the
enforcement-provisions question this Term: the Fifth Circuit has held a federal
statute facially unconstitutional, the Sixth Circuit has twice upheld the same
provisions, and every party before the Court (petitioners, the federal respondents,
and the Authority) asks the Court to resolve that split now. The dispute is over the
vehicle. The federal respondents and the Authority ask the Court to grant Nos. 26-199
and 26-201 and hold this petition; petitioners ask the Court to grant this petition
alone or, at minimum, to grant and consolidate it with the Fifth Circuit cases,
pointing to a latent finality question in the Fifth Circuit appeal (an unresolved
Rule 41(a) partial-dismissal issue) that the Sixth Circuit case does not carry.

My forecast is that the Court takes the enforcement question through the Fifth
Circuit petitions and that this petition is more likely held than granted outright,
roughly 60/40 against a plenary grant here. If granted plenarily, the Court will
limit the grant to the enforcement question (the petition's question presented as
framed covers both rulemaking and enforcement; the rulemaking half has no circuit
conflict, both circuits having upheld it after *Consumers' Research*) and consolidate
this case with Nos. 26-199 and 26-201 for argument in February or March 2027. If
held, the disposition of this petition will track the merits judgment in *National
Horsemen*: a GVR in light of that decision if the Court holds the enforcement
provisions unconstitutional (I put that near even odds, slightly under), or a denial
if the Court upholds them, in either case at the end of June or early July 2027.

Summing the routes: about a 60% chance this petition ends in a grant of some kind,
split roughly 58% plenary (consolidated) to 42% GVR among the grant outcomes. An
outright denial before any merits decision is unlikely (under 10%); the realistic
denial path is a hold followed by denial after the Court upholds the Act.

**If granted on the merits.** Conditional on plenary review, the likely ground is
whether the FTC retains the "say-so" *Consumers' Research* requires over the
Authority's investigation, sanction, and civil-enforcement decisions, and whether a
facial challenge can succeed given the FTC's December 2025 enforcement-rule
modification. The rulemaking question stays out.

**Dissent from denial.** If the petition is denied after the Court upholds the Act in
*National Horsemen*, expect no separate writing on this docket; the merits dissenters
will have spoken in the lead case. A separate writing is plausible only in the
unlikely event of an outright denial of the entire cluster, which I do not expect.
