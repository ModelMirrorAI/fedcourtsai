## In brief

Oklahoma and other states ask the Court to strike down a federal law that lets a private corporation help regulate horseracing, after a lower court upheld it.

## What happened

Congress created the Horseracing Integrity and Safety Authority, a private nonprofit, to write and help enforce rules for thoroughbred racing, under oversight from the Federal Trade Commission. Oklahoma and other states, racing commissions, and horse-industry groups sued, arguing this setup hands government power to a private entity in violation of constitutional limits on delegating public authority. After back-and-forth through the courts, including a Supreme Court order sending the case back for another look following an unrelated ruling on private parties helping run federal programs, the Sixth Circuit again upheld the law, finding the FTC retains ultimate control over both rulemaking and enforcement. Oklahoma again asks the Supreme Court to intervene.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked whether a federal law may give a private organization the power to propose rules that bind an entire industry and to investigate, charge, and sanction rule-breakers, when a federal agency reviews those actions only after they occur or takes effect. The petitioners say the private entity effectively controls policy and enforcement; the government and the private entity respond that the agency keeps final authority and the arrangement is constitutional.

## Where it stands

The petition has been distributed for conference twice, most recently for December 4, 2026, after an earlier conference date passed with the case rescheduled rather than acted on; the docket shows no decision yet on whether the Court will hear the case. The federal government's brief asks the Court to instead take up a related case from the Fifth Circuit and hold this petition pending that case's outcome.

## What each outcome would mean

Oklahoma and the other states are asking the Court to take up their case and reverse the lower court's ruling, which upheld the law. If the Court grants the petition, it would agree to hear the case and decide the constitutional question itself; a later decision could then affirm the law as written, reverse it, or send the case back without deciding. If the Court denies the petition, the Sixth Circuit's ruling upholding the law stands, leaving the Horseracing Authority's structure in place, at least in the states covered by that court. The government's filings suggest the Court could instead act on related petitions from a different appeals court and hold this case until that is resolved.
