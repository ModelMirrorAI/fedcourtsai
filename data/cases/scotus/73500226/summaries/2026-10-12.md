## In brief

Oklahoma and other states ask the Court to strike down a federal law that lets a private corporation help regulate and enforce rules for horseracing nationwide.

## What happened

Congress created the Horseracing Integrity and Safety Authority, a private corporation, to write and enforce safety and anti-doping rules for the horseracing industry under the Federal Trade Commission's oversight. Oklahoma and other states, racing commissions, and horseracing businesses sued, arguing this arrangement unconstitutionally hands government power to a private entity and improperly pressures states. After back-and-forth litigation, including a trip to the Supreme Court and back, the Sixth Circuit again upheld the law, finding the FTC retains ultimate control over both rulemaking and enforcement. The Fifth Circuit, considering a similar challenge, reached the opposite conclusion on enforcement powers, creating a split among appeals courts.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked whether a law may give a private organization the power to propose industry rules and carry out enforcement actions like investigations, subpoenas, and lawsuits, when a federal agency reviews but does not originate those actions, or whether only the government itself may hold such power.

## Where it stands

The petition has been fully briefed, including responses from the federal government and the horseracing authority and a reply from Oklahoma. It was distributed for the Court's conference on October 9, 2026, and then rescheduled; the docket does not yet show what the Court decided.

## What each outcome would mean

Oklahoma and the other states asked the Court to take up the case. If the Court grants review, it will hear full arguments and decide whether the law's rulemaking and enforcement provisions are constitutional, without yet deciding who wins. If the Court denies review, the Sixth Circuit's decision upholding the law stands, leaving the horseracing authority's current powers in place, at least in that circuit. The federal government and the horseracing authority have asked the Court instead to hold this case while it resolves related petitions from a Fifth Circuit case addressing similar questions.
