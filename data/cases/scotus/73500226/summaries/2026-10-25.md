## In brief

Oklahoma and other states ask the Court to strike down a federal law that lets a private corporation regulate horseracing, saying it hands government power to a private entity.

## What happened

Congress created the Horseracing Integrity and Safety Authority, a private nonprofit, to write and enforce rules for thoroughbred racing under the Federal Trade Commission's oversight. Oklahoma, other states, and racing organizations sued, arguing this setup unconstitutionally gives governmental power to a private entity. After back-and-forth litigation, including a trip to the Supreme Court and back, the Sixth Circuit upheld the law, finding the FTC retains enough control over the Authority's rulemaking and enforcement. The Fifth Circuit, considering a similar challenge, reached the opposite conclusion on the enforcement provisions, creating a disagreement between circuits.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked whether a law may let a private organization propose and help enforce binding federal rules for an industry, so long as a federal agency reviews and can revise what the private group does, or whether this arrangement improperly puts government power in private hands.

## Where it stands

The petition has been briefed, including responses from the federal government and the Authority and a reply from Oklahoma. It was distributed for the Court's October 9, 2026 conference, then rescheduled; the docket does not yet show a decision on whether the Court will hear the case.

## What each outcome would mean

Oklahoma and the other states are asking the Court to overturn the Sixth Circuit's ruling, which upheld the law. If the Court grants review, it would hear full arguments on whether the law is constitutional, deciding nothing about the outcome at this stage; the federal government has suggested holding this petition while it pursues related cases from the Fifth Circuit on the same enforcement question. If the Court denies review, the Sixth Circuit's decision upholding the law stands, and the Authority and FTC continue operating under the current structure for Oklahoma and the other challengers.
