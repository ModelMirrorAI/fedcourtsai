# Forecast at the CVSG moment

The most likely disposition is denial of certiorari after the Solicitor General files the requested brief. The probability of any grant, including a summary grant, is **38%**. A denial would leave the interlocutory ruling in place without deciding whether the Tribes can prove their requested damages.

## Further proceedings

The snapshot records **one distribution**, entered September 9, 2026, for the September 28 conference. The Court called for the Solicitor General's views on **October 5, 2026**. That invitation has already occurred; it is not a future predicted event.

I expect the Court to await the government's brief and then distribute the petition again. The probability of at least one additional distribution is **96%**. My central forecast is **one to three additional distributions** before disposition, including the first post-CVSG distribution, rather than an immediate decision or a prolonged series of relists. This is a forecast of docket distributions, not necessarily a count of distinct conferences actually held.

The central timing forecast is a cert disposition in the first half of **2027**, after several months for the government to respond. Delay into the following Term remains plausible; the CVSG does not fix a briefing deadline in the provided record. The `cvsg-increment` probability is **0%** because this cell already begins with the invitation; that claim is vacuous for scoring.

## Question and route

If the Court grants review, I expect it to focus on whether CERCLA permits natural-resource damages for interim loss of uses with a cultural dimension, and what connection to injury to the resource is required. That is a more neutral formulation than assuming that the recovery necessarily compensates free-standing subjective distress. I do not expect this petition to reopen the separate question of liability for discharges originating in Canada.

Conditional on a grant, I assign **10%** to disposition in the cert order itself and **90%** to plenary review. A contested statutory boundary is more likely to receive briefing and argument than summary reversal, and the provided record identifies no intervening decision requiring a GVR. A government recommendation for reconsideration could still create a summary route.

Conditional on denial, I assign **12%** to a noted dissent or statement respecting denial. The central forecast is a routine denial without separate writing; I make no individual-Justice vote or authorship prediction.

## Expected basis

The competing considerations are substantial federal interests in the scope of natural-resource remedies, against an interlocutory decision whose consequences still depend on proof and valuation at trial. My central forecast is that the Court will obtain the government's assessment but ultimately leave the issue for a more developed record. If review is granted instead, the likely merits dispute is the boundary between compensable lost resource uses and personal cultural injury, not whether tribal cultural uses are inherently less valuable. That merits discussion is conditional, not a prediction that a merits opinion will necessarily issue.
