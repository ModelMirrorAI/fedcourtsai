## Why 38%, rather than the baseline or a confident grant

**Positive considerations.** The Court has affirmatively sought federal input despite the respondent's opposition to doing so. The government has potentially important interests both as a resource trustee and as a potentially responsible party. The petition presents one statutory question and identifies substantial exposure: its introduction describes more than $500 million in additional tribal claims, separate from joint restoration-related claims. That is the petition's characterization, not an award or independently verified valuation. Industry, Canadian mining interests, British Columbia, and two legal organizations have filed supporting amici. These features make the dispute more consequential than an isolated disagreement over an individual damages calculation. Sources: snapshot proceedings; petition pp. 1–5, 24–25, 30–36.

**The alleged split is materially contestable.** Teck describes a three-way conflict among the Ninth, Tenth, and D.C. Circuits. The opposition explains that the cited decisions permit interim lost-use recovery and did not decide whether culturally significant uses must be excluded. I checked narrow passages in the older primary opinions through CourtListener: *Ohio v. Department of the Interior*, 880 F.2d 432, 460, 464 (D.C. Cir. 1989), recognizes option and existence values and discusses the exclusion of purely private resources in ownership terms; *New Mexico v. General Electric Co.*, 467 F.3d 1223, 1245, 1250–51 n.39 (10th Cir. 2006), recognizes public interim loss-of-use damages while limiting the remedy to injury to the trustee's role. Those passages support the opposition's argument that there is not a clean opposite holding on tribal cultural uses. They do not establish that every valuation proposed here is lawful. The distinction lowers my cert estimate relative to a genuinely square circuit split. Sources: petition pp. 28–30; opposition pp. 14–18; CourtListener opinion IDs 7858699 and 168319, passages logged in `retrieval.md`.

**Vehicle and question-framing concerns remain substantial.** This is an interlocutory appeal, with no natural-resource damages award yet. The respondent argues that the Ninth Circuit decided only against a categorical exclusion of culturally connected losses, leaving causation, measurement, and evidentiary sufficiency for trial. The petition instead frames the decision as authorizing subjective loss independent of injury to a resource. That mismatch could let the government recommend denial without endorsing the petitioner's characterization of limitless liability. The opposition also identifies unresolved standing, limitations, and ripeness arguments and disputes whether the statutory restriction on use of recovered funds applies to tribes in the way Teck assumes. I treat these as asserted vehicle and textual complications, not as independently adjudicated barriers. Sources: opposition pp. 24–28, 31–34; petition pp. 34–36.

**The government is an important unresolved variable.** Both briefs report that the United States supported the Tribes before the Ninth Circuit in December 2024. The petition reports that a later request for time to address rehearing did not result in a brief. Neither fact establishes what the Solicitor General will recommend in response to the new invitation. I do not assume a reversal of position from a change in administration or treat the CVSG as already containing a grant recommendation. A recommendation for plenary review would substantially improve Teck's prospects; a recommendation emphasizing interlocutory posture would reinforce denial. Sources: petition pp. 24–25; opposition p. 32.

The net adjustment is therefore modestly above the roughly 35% anchor, to **38%**. National remedial stakes and the possibility of a significant government recommendation warrant some upward weight, but the split and vehicle objections prevent a majority-probability grant call. This is a judgmental update, not the output of a fitted numerical model; the CVSG itself is already reflected in the conditional base-rate context and is not counted twice as an independent multiplier.
