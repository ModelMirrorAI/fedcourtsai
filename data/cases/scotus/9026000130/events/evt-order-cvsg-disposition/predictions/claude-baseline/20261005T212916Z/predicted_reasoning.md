# Forecast: Teck Metals Ltd. v. Confederated Tribes of the Colville Reservation, No. 26-130

**Moment:** CVSG. The docket shows one distribution (for the September 28, 2026 conference) and, on October 5, 2026, an invitation to the Solicitor General to file a brief expressing the views of the United States.

## Disposition

The petition will most likely be **denied**, but it is a close call: I put the chance of a grant at **40%**. A grant, if it comes, will be a plenary grant on the question presented substantially as Teck framed it or as the Ninth Circuit itself framed it ("whether § 9607(a)(4)(C) authorizes damages for the interim lost uses of injured natural resources in a case where the diminished uses have a cultural dimension"), set for briefing and argument in October Term 2027. No summary disposition is in prospect: there is no intervening decision of this Court to GVR against, and the question is a contested one of statutory construction, not a per curiam candidate (summary-route probability conditional on grant: 4%).

## Further distributions

The petition **will be distributed again** (relist-increment: 96%). That is the mechanics of a CVSG rather than a signal: once the Solicitor General files, the Clerk redistributes the petition for a conference, and both sides typically file supplemental briefs. The only way the count does not rise is a settlement or withdrawal before the SG files, which after 22 years of this litigation and Teck's stated intention to withstand settlement pressure I put at a few percent. Expect at least one redistribution and quite possibly a relist or two after it, since CVSG petitions the Court is seriously weighing are often held over a conference.

## The Solicitor General

A CVSG is already on the docket, so the cvsg-increment claim is vacuous for this cell (I state 1% only because the set requires a number; a second invitation does not occur). What matters is the content and timing of the SG's brief. An October invitation usually produces a brief in the spring, most often between late April and late May 2027, so the Court will likely act on the petition in **May or June 2027**, with a decision at the final conferences of the Term. The United States' institutional position is split: as a trustee (Interior, NOAA) it relies on nonuse and cultural values in its own damage-assessment regulations and defended the Tribes as amicus in the court of appeals under the prior Administration; as a potentially responsible party at federal facilities it has strong reason to want damages cabined, and the current Administration chose not to file at the rehearing stage. I think the likelier brief recommends **denial** on vehicle grounds (interlocutory posture, methodology untested, the Ninth Circuit's holding narrower than the petition paints it) while possibly disagreeing with parts of the panel's reasoning, roughly 60/40 against a grant recommendation. If the SG recommends a grant, the Court will very probably grant; if the SG recommends denial, a grant becomes unlikely but not negligible given the amicus interest and the Court's own appetite for CERCLA-scope cases.

## Which question, if granted

The Court would take the question as a single one about the scope of natural-resource damages under § 9607(a)(4)(C) and (f)(1), possibly reformulated to track the Ninth Circuit's own framing (lost uses with a cultural dimension) rather than Teck's "felt loss of a cultural connection" framing, which the BIO plausibly says mischaracterizes the holding. If granted and reached, the likely ground would be textual: whether "damages for injury to, destruction of, or loss of natural resources" measured by "use value" reaches nonuse or cultural values, read against the restoration-focused use restriction in § 9607(f)(1).

## If denied

Expect a dissent from or statement respecting denial with probability about **20%**. The most plausible author is Justice Gorsuch (who has written separately on CERCLA's reach in Atlantic Richfield and on tribal interests), possibly joined by Justice Thomas or Justice Alito, framed around the "obscure provision" point and strict-liability exposure. More likely than not, the denial is silent.
