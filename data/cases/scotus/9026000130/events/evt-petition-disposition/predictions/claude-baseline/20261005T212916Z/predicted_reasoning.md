# Forecast: Teck Metals Ltd. v. Confederated Tribes of the Colville Reservation, No. 26-130

## State of the docket at prediction time

The petition was distributed once, for the Conference of September 28, 2026. At that
first look the Court did not deny; on October 5, 2026 it invited the Solicitor General
to file a brief expressing the views of the United States. So this forecast is made from
a one-distribution, CVSG-issued state: the petition is now parked until the SG files.

## What the Court will do

**Disposition.** P(any grant) = 0.40. The modal outcome is a denial after the SG files,
but this is close to a coin flip, and the SG's recommendation will decide it. The Court
will act on the petition in the second half of OT2026: the SG most likely files in the
spring (an October CVSG is usually answered by May so the Court can act before the
summer recess), with a June 2027 disposition; a December filing and a January 2027
disposition is the less likely fast track.

**Further distribution.** Yes, with near certainty: once the SG's brief arrives the
petition is redistributed for a conference and ordinarily considered at that one or the
next. Expect one to two further distributions beyond the single one on the docket. The
only way the count stays at one is a settlement-driven dismissal before the SG files,
which after 22 years of litigation is unlikely.

**CVSG.** Already on the docket (October 5, 2026). Nothing further to forecast on this
claim; the increment is vacuous.

**Which question, if granted.** The Court would take Teck's QP essentially as written,
possibly with a sharpening toward the Ninth Circuit's own formulation (whether
section 107(a)(4)(C) authorizes damages for interim lost uses of injured natural
resources where the lost uses have a cultural dimension). If granted, the likely
ground is textual: whether "use value" and "damages for ... loss of natural resources"
reach nonuse or existence-type values, read against the trustee use-restriction in
section 107(f)(1). A plenary grant would be set for argument in OT2027.

**Summary disposition.** Very unlikely (0.05 conditional on a grant). There is no
intervening decision to GVR in light of, and the question is a first-impression
statutory one the Court would want briefed and argued.

**Dissent from denial.** If the Court denies, expect a clean denial most of the time.
P(a dissent from or statement respecting denial | denial) = 0.15: a textualist Justice
could write briefly to flag the "miscellaneous provision" reading and the strict-liability
exposure, but the interlocutory posture and the absence of a split make a silent denial
the more likely form.

## Why the Court lands there

The case has everything that gets a CVSG and about half of what gets a grant. On the
grant side: an elite petitioner team (counsel of record is a former acting SG), five
cert-stage amici including a foreign sovereign (British Columbia), the U.S. Chamber, the
National Mining Association and the American Petroleum Institute, a published Ninth
Circuit opinion that rests its holding on a "Reports and studies" provision, and a
textualist Court that has recently policed CERCLA and the Clean Water Act for remedies
that are "tucked into" obscure provisions. The federal government's own exposure as a
potentially responsible party gives the SG a real institutional reason to favor limiting
the remedy.

On the denial side: there is no genuine circuit conflict (the Tenth and D.C. Circuit
decisions never addressed a cultural component, and the Ninth Circuit expressly aligned
itself with the D.C. Circuit); the posture is a 1292(b) interlocutory appeal with no
damages yet awarded, so the SG can comfortably say the "felt loss" complaint is really
a methodology and Daubert fight for remand; the Department of the Interior's own
regulations have recognized nonuse and cultural values since 1986, so the SG would have
to walk away from its own trustee regulations to support Teck; and the Court has
denied two prior Teck petitions in this same case, one after a CVSG in which the SG
recommended denial.
