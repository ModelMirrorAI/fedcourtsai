## Why 32%, rather than the anchor or ordinary denial odds

**The CVSG is substantial positive evidence, already incorporated in the anchor.** The Court has requested federal input despite the opposition's argument that the government's earlier appellate participation made another consultation unnecessary (BIO pp. 31–32). I do not add a second large CVSG bonus on top of a band that already reflects that signal. The statutory question implicates the federal government as trustee and potentially responsible party; the petition discusses both interests at pp. 24–25. Five amicus filings, including British Columbia and industry groups, support treating this as more than routine error correction.

**The competing descriptions of the holding weaken the claimed clean split.** Teck frames the question as compensation for subjective cultural disconnection, arguing that the Ninth Circuit diverges from the Tenth Circuit's restoration-focused rule and the D.C. Circuit's exclusion of purely private interests (petition pp. 28–30). The Tribes respond that neither comparator decided the cultural-use question, that all recognize interim lost-use damages, and that limits on private claims or privately owned resources do not decide a sovereign trustee's claim for injury to public natural resources (BIO pp. 14–17). On these materials, there is an arguable tension, but not a demonstrated square conflict over materially identical claims. I therefore discount Teck's three-way-split characterization rather than assume it is established.

**Interlocutory posture matters here for a concrete reason.** The appellate ruling, as described in both briefs, rejects categorical exclusion of culturally inflected natural-resource uses but leaves valuation and proof for trial. The BIO says no natural-resource damages have yet been awarded, and identifies standing, limitations, and ripeness arguments reserved for a later appeal (pp. 32–34). The petition's account of hundreds of millions of dollars in requested relief establishes potential stakes, not an adjudicated award. The possibility that proof fails or narrower grounds resolve the claims gives the Court a reason to wait. Teck's counterargument is meaningful: a certified statutory question may be reviewed now, and settlement pressure may prevent a later clean vehicle (petition pp. 34–36). I give that counterargument weight without treating certification as eliminating the unresolved issues.

**An additional statutory complication favors caution about this vehicle.** The BIO distinguishes the measure of damages from restrictions on spending recoveries, and argues that the spending restriction Teck invokes does not expressly name Tribes (pp. 22–24, 34). Teck disputes the implication through its statutory-history discussion and footnote at p. 7. I do not resolve that legal disagreement as fact. It creates another way the Court could find the petition's broad framing ill-suited to deciding a generally applicable rule here.

**The SG's position remains unknown.** The petition reports an earlier federal appellate brief supporting the Tribes and no subsequent rehearing brief after additional time was sought (pp. 24–25). Neither that earlier position nor the administration change establishes what the requested Supreme Court brief will recommend. A favorable recommendation could materially lift grant prospects; a vehicle-based recommendation to deny could sharply reduce them. My 32% integrates this uncertainty, leaving denial more likely but a grant quite plausible.

Overall, importance and demonstrated Court interest keep the forecast near the high-band anchor; the contested split and unfinished damages record move it modestly below that anchor. This is judgmental calibration, not a fitted statistical adjustment.

## Additional claims and significance

- **Further distribution: 0.97.** One distribution is already recorded. I expect another after the SG files, ordinarily followed by disposition and possibly one additional relist. The contract counts the next distribution even if it is best described procedurally as resumption after a CVSG rather than a conventional relist. The small complement allows withdrawal, settlement, or another nonstandard exit.
- **CVSG increment: 0.00.** The invitation is already recorded. There is no first CVSG left to forecast; the harness masks this claim as vacuous. This does not predict that the invited brief will fail to arrive.
- **Summary route conditional on grant: 0.07.** No intervening controlling decision is identified in the supplied materials as a GVR trigger. Resolving this contested statutory boundary would more naturally require plenary briefing and argument. The small residual includes an unforeseen intervening development or a summary correction after federal input. This is conditional on grant, not a 7% unconditional probability.
- **Dissent or statement conditional on denial: 0.08.** I expect an unsigned denial without a separate writing. The policy stakes make a statement possible, but the materials identify no particular Justice's public commitment to this question. I do not infer private cert votes from silence.
- **Big-case score: 0.66.** A merits ruling could affect natural-resource-damages valuation across federal, state, and tribal trustees and substantial industrial liabilities. Tribal cultural interests and cross-border participation broaden the stakes. It remains a specialized damages question without an actual award, rather than a general ruling on all tribal rights or all transboundary pollution liability.
