## In brief

A mining company asks the Court to review a ruling letting a tribe seek environmental damages that include harm to its cultural connection to a river.

## What happened

Teck Metals, a Canadian company, discharged mining waste into the Columbia River for decades, contaminating areas within the Colville Reservation. In long-running litigation, the Confederated Tribes of the Colville Reservation sought damages under a federal environmental cleanup law for injuries to natural resources, including compensation tied to the loss of the river's cultural uses for tribal members. The district court ruled that such cultural-use damages were not allowed and granted Teck partial summary judgment, but the Ninth Circuit reversed, holding the law does not categorically bar recovering damages for lost uses that have a cultural component. The Ninth Circuit denied rehearing.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked whether the federal law allows a tribe to recover damages for the loss of a cultural connection to natural resources, or whether such damages must be limited to the objective costs of restoring, replacing, or acquiring substitutes for the resource itself.

## Where it stands

The petition was distributed for conference, Teck then filed a reply, and the Court has since invited the Solicitor General to file a brief giving the federal government's views, so the case awaits that input before the Court decides whether to take it.

## What each outcome would mean

Teck Metals, as petitioner, asks the Court to take up the case and reverse. If the Court grants review and rules for Teck, it would narrow what damages the Tribes can seek, likely barring the cultural-loss claims. If the Court instead affirms, the Ninth Circuit's decision allowing such damages would stand, and the case would continue toward trial on the Tribes' claims. If the Court denies review, the Ninth Circuit's ruling in the Tribes' favor remains in place and the litigation proceeds in the lower courts. A vacate-and-remand would set that ruling aside without deciding the question either way.
