# Forecast: what the Court will do with No. 25-1246, Vasquez v. Guerrero

**Bottom line.** The Court will deny certiorari (P(any grant, including a GVR) = 0.14),
but not soon: the petition will be held and redistributed at least once more, and a
denial will very likely draw a separate writing.

## Relists and timing

The docket shows **one distribution**, for the September 28, 2026 long conference, and
no further entry as of the October 5 snapshot. Petitioner moved on August 14 to defer
consideration, and on September 17 told the Clerk that a companion petition, No. 26-353
(from the Fifth Circuit's August 28 ruling in No. 26-70008 on the protective Brady/Napue
petition), had been filed and asked that the two be considered together. The companion's
response is due October 16, 2026. I expect the Court to do what it ordinarily does with a
pair like this: take no action on the lead petition at the long conference and redistribute
both once No. 26-353 is ripe, so a **further distribution is very likely (0.85)**, probably
for a late-November or December conference, and a decision on both petitions together,
most likely before the end of January 2027 unless the State takes an extension on the
companion. A second redistribution after that is plausible if a Justice is writing.

## CVSG

None (0.02). This is a state capital habeas case between a Texas prisoner and the Texas
Attorney General; no federal interest is implicated, and the Court does not invite the
Solicitor General on AEDPA gateway questions of this kind.

## Which question, if any, the Court would take

If the Court grants plenary review it would take **Question 1 as written** (whether
§ 2244(b)(2)(B)(i)'s due-diligence requirement is measured by a reasonable applicant or
a reasonable attorney), probably narrowed to drop the "exceptions for egregious
misconduct" clause. Question 2 (a constitutional floor of one merits review of a
substantial IAC claim before execution) and Question 4 (deference to the local DA over
the AG) would not be granted on their own. Question 3 (whether late-surfacing Brady and
Napue claims are "second or successive") is the question Justice Sotomayor has flagged
twice, but on this docket it rides on an authorization denial that § 2244(b)(3)(E) shields;
it is better presented in No. 26-353, and if the Court is interested in it the grant would
come there, with this petition held and then disposed of in its wake.

## Summary disposition versus plenary review

Conditional on any grant, I put the **cert-order route at 0.5**. The two live grant paths
are about equally weighted: (a) a GVR, either in light of whatever the Court does with
No. 26-353 or a Newberry v. Texas-style GVR directing the Fifth Circuit to consider the
Nueces County DA's position that relief is warranted; and (b) plenary review of Question
1. A per curiam summary reversal on the merits is unlikely: the Fifth Circuit's alternative
holding on the innocence prong gives the Court no clean error to correct summarily.

## Dissent from denial

If the Court denies, I expect a separate writing with **probability 0.38**: a dissent from
denial or a statement respecting denial from Justice Sotomayor, likely joined by Justice
Jackson and possibly Justice Kagan, stressing the recanted toxicology testimony, the two
DAs' concessions, and the "illogical" treatment of Brady claims as successive. The record
request on August 11 is consistent with chambers preparing such a writing.

## Conditional merits view

If the Court granted Question 1, the likelier outcome is a narrow decision that the
statute's "applicant" diligence inquiry is factual and context-dependent, with counsel's
knowledge relevant but not automatically imputed, followed by a remand; I would not
expect the Court to adopt a categorical "rogue agent" carve-out. If the Court instead
granted No. 26-353 on the Panetti question, this petition would most likely be GVR'd
rather than argued alongside it.
