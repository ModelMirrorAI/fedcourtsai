## Case-specific adjustments

The petition's strongest review candidate is Question 1: whose diligence matters under section 2244(b)(2)(B)(i), including the treatment of serious attorney misconduct. It alleges conflicting circuit approaches and an internal inconsistency in toxicology evidence that counsel failed to investigate. The capital stakes, forensic dispute, and local prosecutors' support for relief make this more substantial than an ordinary error-correction petition. The August record request and September 28 receipt are additional, limited attention signals. These factors justify moving above the 5.12% anchor, but do not demonstrate four votes for review.

The central counterweight is an independent obstacle visible in the actual decision, not merely asserted by the respondent. The provisioned petition's Appendix A, pages 17a–19a, concludes that Vasquez also fails section 2244(b)(2)(B)(ii)'s innocence requirement. Its pages 11a–16a frame diligence in terms of representation and the available evidence; the opinion is unpublished. The BIO, pages 13–18 and 26–29, therefore offers serious answers both to the claimed clean split and to whether resolving that split would change the judgment. I do not decide the disputed forensic facts myself.

I retrieved the already-linked August 11 reply to avoid treating the BIO as unanswered. Reply pages 6–8 dispute the appellate court's authority to decide innocence without the intervening district-court assessment, challenge its treatment of other evidence, and contest waiver. This gives the petitioner a response, but still asks the Court to overcome several intertwined procedural and factual obstacles rather than resolve one clean statutory conflict.

Question 2 seeks a broader constitutional guarantee of review of an ineffective-assistance claim. Question 3 concerns later-discovered Brady/Napue claims, with preservation and first-gate jurisdiction objections discussed at BIO pages 29–32. Question 4 adds the dispute over who may speak for Texas and the effect of local prosecutors' positions. Those questions increase the potential importance without removing the current petition's vehicle problems.

The retrieved August 14 motion seeks coordination with another habeas appeal. The September 17 letter reports that a related cert petition, No. 26-353, was filed September 11 and asks the Court to consider it alongside this petition. These pre-snapshot filings make a coordination delay plausible; they do not establish consolidation, cure this petition's independent obstacles, or show that either Supreme Court petition has been granted. I did not retrieve either petition's disposition or subsequent docket history.
