# Predicted course: Vasquez v. Guerrero, No. 25-1246 (capital)

**Where the docket stands.** The petition has been distributed twice. The first distribution was for the 9/28/2026 long conference. On 10/7 it was distributed again for 11/6/2026, with no order on the petition in between. Petitioner's motion to defer consideration (filed 8/14; the State opposed it 8/24) is still undecided on the docket. The 9/17 letter asks the Court to consider the petition alongside a new petition, No. 26-353, which comes from the related Fifth Circuit COA proceeding on his Brady and Napue/Giglio claims. The record was requested on 8/11 and received on 9/28. No CVSG.

**Disposition: denied.** I expect the Court to deny, most likely together with No. 26-353 once that petition is ready for conference. I read the move to 11/6 as the Court lining 25-1246 up with its companion. It is not a sign of grant interest.

**Further relisting: likely (one or two more).** The State filed its BIO in 26-353 on 10/16. In the ordinary course that petition is distributed for a late-November conference. For the Court to take the two petitions together, 25-1246 has to be distributed again after 11/6. Even if the 26-353 timing lines up sooner, a capital denial that draws a separate writing usually needs extra conferences.

**CVSG: no.** A state prisoner is suing a state official over a habeas-procedure question. The Court has no reason to invite the Solicitor General.

**If granted, which question.** QP1, the "reasonable attorney" vs "reasonable applicant" diligence standard under §2244(b)(2)(B)(i), is the only one that looks certworthy. The Court might narrow it to the standard alone and drop the "egregious misconduct" exception. QP2 (constitutional avoidance), QP3 (whether Brady/Napue claims count as successive, which also has vehicle problems the BIO raises) and QP4 (deference to a district attorney's confession of error) would be left aside.

**Summary disposition: unlikely.** I see no intervening decision that would support a GVR. A summary reversal of a Fifth Circuit AEDPA ruling in a prisoner's favor would be unusual.

**Separate writing on denial: plausible, not likely.** Several features fit the Court's usual source of dissents from denial in capital cases: the toxicologists recanted, the DA's office that prosecuted him supports relief, and no court has reached the IATC claim on the merits. A statement or dissent from one or more Justices (Sotomayor and/or Jackson are the most likely authors) is a real possibility.
