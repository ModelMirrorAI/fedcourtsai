## In brief

A Texas death row inmate asks the Court to review a ruling that blocked his claim that trial lawyers missed a flaw in key evidence.

## What happened

Richard Vasquez was convicted of capital murder and sentenced to death for killing his girlfriend's four-year-old daughter. Years later, a newly hired toxicologist found an inconsistency in a trial report about cocaine in the child's system, evidence prosecutors had used to show intent. Vasquez sought to raise an ineffective-assistance claim based on this discovery in a new federal habeas petition, but under federal law such later petitions face strict limits. The district court and the Fifth Circuit Court of Appeals both ruled against him, finding he had not shown the diligence the law requires or proven his innocence. Vasquez now asks the Supreme Court to review that ruling.

## What the Court is being asked

The case asks how courts should judge whether a death row inmate acted diligently in uncovering facts for a late-filed claim: by what his lawyers reasonably could have discovered, or by what the inmate himself could have discovered, especially when he says his own lawyers failed him badly. It also raises whether blocking review of such a claim violates constitutional rights, whether certain evidence-suppression claims count as barred "repeat" claims, and whether courts must resolve disputes over which state official speaks for the state in these proceedings.
