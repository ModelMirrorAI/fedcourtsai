## In brief

A Texas death row inmate asks the Court to revive a successive habeas petition claiming his trial lawyers missed a flaw in cocaine evidence used against him.

## What happened

Richard Vasquez was convicted of capital murder and sentenced to death for killing his girlfriend's four-year-old daughter. Years later, a defense toxicologist found an inconsistency in trial evidence about cocaine in the child's blood. Vasquez sought permission to file a successive federal habeas petition raising an ineffective-assistance claim based on this discovery. The Fifth Circuit allowed him to proceed on that claim, but the district court and then the Fifth Circuit ruled against him, holding he had not been diligent in discovering the claim earlier and had not shown his innocence. Vasquez also raised claims about suppressed evidence and about which Texas official may represent the state in his case.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked to decide whether the diligence standard for raising this kind of late habeas claim should account for a lawyer's own role in missing the problem, and whether an exception applies when a petitioner says his own attorneys seriously failed him. The case also raises whether certain suppressed-evidence claims count as "successive" under the relevant habeas rules, and whether courts must resolve disputes over which state official may speak for the government in such proceedings.

## Where it stands

The petition has been distributed for conference twice, most recently for November 6, 2026, and the docket shows it was "rescheduled" after that distribution; no ruling on the petition has been recorded.

## What each outcome would mean

Vasquez, the petitioner, is asking the Court to reverse the Fifth Circuit's decision, which the state respondent, Eric Guerrero, defends. If the Court grants review, it would hear the case fully and decide the legal questions; a grant alone would not determine who wins. If the Court denies review, the Fifth Circuit's decision stands, leaving Vasquez's petition dismissed and his claims unreviewed on the merits. Vasquez has also separately asked the Court to defer deciding this petition while a related case proceeds; the docket does not show whether that request has been granted or denied.
