## In brief

A Texas death row inmate asks the Court to revive a habeas claim that his trial lawyers missed a flaw in key evidence against him.

## What happened

Richard Vasquez was convicted of capital murder and sentenced to death for killing his girlfriend's four-year-old daughter. Evidence that the child had cocaine in her system was used to help show he intended to kill her. Years later, a toxicologist hired by Vasquez's lawyers found an internal inconsistency in the toxicology report underlying that evidence. Vasquez sought to raise a new claim that his trial lawyers were constitutionally ineffective for not catching this flaw sooner. Federal law limits second-round habeas petitions unless an inmate shows he could not have discovered the claim's basis earlier through due diligence. The district court and the Fifth Circuit Court of Appeals both ruled that Vasquez failed to show such diligence, since the report's inconsistency was available at trial, and dismissed his petition.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked whether the diligence requirement for filing a second habeas petition should be judged by what a reasonable lawyer could have discovered, or instead by what the inmate himself could have discovered, especially where his own attorneys allegedly committed serious errors. It is also asked whether barring review of such a claim would violate constitutional trial rights, whether certain evidence-suppression and false-testimony claims count as a barred second petition, and whether courts must resolve disputes over which Texas official can speak for the state in these proceedings.

## Where it stands

## What each outcome would mean

Vasquez is asking the Court to take up his case.
