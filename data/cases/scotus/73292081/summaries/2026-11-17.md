## In brief

A Texas death row inmate asks the Supreme Court to revive his bid for federal habeas review of a flawed-evidence ineffective-assistance claim.

## What happened

Richard Vasquez was convicted of capital murder and sentenced to death for killing his girlfriend's four-year-old daughter. Years later, his lawyers found an inconsistency in the trial toxicology report concerning cocaine found in the victim's system, evidence used to help show intent to kill. Vasquez sought permission to file a successive federal habeas petition raising an ineffective-assistance claim based on this discovery. The court of appeals allowed him to proceed on that basis, but the district court dismissed the petition, finding Vasquez had not been diligent in uncovering the problem earlier, as federal law requires for successive petitions. The court of appeals affirmed, holding Vasquez failed to show either diligence or his innocence. Vasquez now asks the Supreme Court to review that ruling.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked whether, in deciding if a death-row inmate acted diligently in discovering new evidence for a follow-on habeas claim, courts should ask what the inmate's lawyers could reasonably have found, or instead what the inmate himself could have found, and whether an exception applies when the inmate's own lawyers are accused of serious misconduct. Vasquez also raises related questions about whether some types of claims are exempt from the diligence rule, and about which state officials may represent the state in such proceedings.

## Where it stands

The petition has been distributed for conference multiple times, most recently for a December 4, 2026 conference, and was rescheduled from an earlier conference date. The docket shows no decision yet on whether the Court will hear the case; a motion by Vasquez to defer consideration, opposed by the state, is also pending.

## What each outcome would mean

Vasquez, the inmate, is asking the Court to take up and ultimately reverse the lower court's ruling against him. If the Court denies review, the court of appeals' decision stands, and Vasquez's habeas claim remains dismissed as untimely under the successive-petition rules. If the Court grants review, it would hear full briefing and argument and decide the diligence question itself; a decision favoring Vasquez could allow his ineffective-assistance claim to go forward, while a decision favoring the state, Eric Guerrero, would leave the dismissal in place. The Court could also send the case back without resolving the question for either side.
