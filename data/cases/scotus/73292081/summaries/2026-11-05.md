## In brief

A Texas death row inmate asks the Court to revive his bid for federal court review of a claim that his trial lawyers were constitutionally ineffective.

## What happened

Richard Vasquez was convicted of capital murder and sentenced to death in Texas. Years later, his lawyers discovered an apparent inconsistency in the toxicology report used at trial to show cocaine in the victim's system. Vasquez sought permission to file a successive federal habeas petition raising an ineffective-assistance claim based on this discovery. The Fifth Circuit allowed him to proceed on that claim but ultimately ruled against him, holding he had not been diligent in discovering the problem earlier and had not shown, as the law also requires, that the new evidence would clearly establish his innocence. Vasquez asks the Supreme Court to review that ruling.

## What the Court is being asked

The case raises questions about a federal law that limits repeat habeas petitions by prisoners. One question is whether courts should measure a prisoner's diligence in finding new evidence by what the prisoner's own lawyers could reasonably have discovered, or by the prisoner's own personal awareness, and whether an exception applies when the prisoner's lawyers are accused of serious misconduct. Other questions concern whether certain claims of hidden evidence count as barred repeat claims, and what weight a federal court must give when local and state prosecutors disagree about whether a prisoner deserves relief.

## Where it stands

The petition has been distributed for conference multiple times, most recently for the December 4, 2026 conference; the docket shows no decision yet. A motion by Vasquez to defer consideration of the petition, tied to a related appeal, is also pending without a recorded ruling.

## What each outcome would mean

Vasquez, the petitioner, is asking the Court to reverse the Fifth Circuit's decision, which upheld the dismissal of his case. If the Court grants review, it would hear the case fully and decide the legal questions; that step alone would not determine who wins. If the Court denies review, the Fifth Circuit's ruling stands, leaving Vasquez's ineffective-assistance claim unreviewed on the merits, as Eric Guerrero, the prison official named as respondent, has urged.
