## In brief

The Court declined to hear a lawyer's challenge to his six-month suspension from practicing before a federal appeals court over fabricated legal citations in briefs.

## What happened

Attorney Mike Singh Sethi filed immigration-appeal briefs containing fake case citations and misquoted opinions, which the Ninth Circuit traced to unchecked use of generative artificial intelligence by a firm employee. After ordering Sethi and a colleague to explain why they should not be disciplined, the Ninth Circuit issued a published order suspending Sethi from practicing before it for six months, fining him, and imposing other requirements, partly based on findings that his later explanations to the court were themselves misleading. Sethi asked the Supreme Court to review that suspension.

## What the Court is being asked

The petition raised two questions: whether the appeals court could rely on conduct and statements made after the original disciplinary charges, without separately notifying Sethi that this later conduct itself could be punished, and whether the court could suspend him for failing to meet a specific disclosure requirement about how citation errors arose that the petition says was announced only in the final ruling, with no earlier rule or notice requiring it.

## Where it stands

The petition was distributed for the Court's November 6, 2026 conference, and the Court denied it on November 9, 2026.

## What each outcome would mean

Sethi, the attorney suspended by the Ninth Circuit, asked the Supreme Court to overturn that suspension. With the petition denied, the Ninth Circuit's order stands: the six-month suspension, fine, and related requirements remain in effect, and the Supreme Court will not review whether Sethi received adequate notice of the charges against him. The denial does not mean the Supreme Court agreed the suspension was proper, only that it will not consider the case further.
