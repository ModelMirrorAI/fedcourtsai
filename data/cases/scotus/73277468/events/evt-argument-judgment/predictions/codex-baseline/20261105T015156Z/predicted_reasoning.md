# Forecast of the Court's decision

## Judgment and lineup

The Court will **vacate the Eleventh Circuit's judgment and remand**, by **7–2**. Barrett will write for Roberts, Sotomayor, Kagan, Gorsuch, Kavanaugh, Jackson, and herself. Alito will dissent, joined by Thomas. Authorship and the dissenting lineup are lower-confidence predictions than the disturbed judgment; a broader consensus on the same remand remains a substantial alternative.

The remand will not decide that the district attorney's office is forever barred from asserting the personal-staff defense. Nor will it establish that Younge wins her discrimination claim. It will require renewed consideration of whether that defense may enter the case under the appropriate procedural standards.

## Majority ground

The majority will reject the Eleventh Circuit's rule that a defendant can avoid the amendment standards merely by introducing an unpleaded affirmative defense through summary judgment. **Rule 15(a)(2), including the Foman factors, supplies the standard; Rule 16(b)(4)'s good-cause requirement also governs when a scheduling deadline makes it applicable.** Lack of prejudice is important but does not displace the other relevant considerations.

The opinion will distinguish applying the proper standard from insisting on a particular paper filing. A district court can treat an appropriate summary-judgment submission as a request to amend, rather than invariably ordering a separate motion and amended answer first. That accommodation avoids an unnecessary formality without authorizing a separate judicially created no-prejudice exception. The petitioner's acceptance of constructive amendment will help the majority reconcile liberal amendment with adherence to the rules.

The Court will not accept the contention that an affirmative defense becomes exempt from this framework simply because it presents a legal argument on an already-developed record. Nor will a general harmless-error appeal suffice to replace the specific amendment inquiry. The majority need not resolve the full reach of Rule 61 or every exception permitting consideration of unpleaded matters.

## Breadth and reserved issues

The stated rule will extend **generally to affirmative defenses first asserted at summary judgment**, not just the Title VII personal-staff exemption or this plaintiff's discovery requests. Its breadth will be procedural and cross-cutting rather than a new rule about which workers Title VII protects.

The opinion will leave the lower courts to determine whether a binding amendment deadline applied here, whether Rule 16 therefore requires good cause, and whether relief under the applicable standards is warranted. It will not decide the precise content of good cause for every attorney mistake. It will proceed on the case's affirmative-defense premise without resolving whether the personal-staff exclusion must always be classified that way. It will also leave the ultimate employment-discrimination merits unresolved.

## Separate writing and alternatives

Alito's predicted dissent will emphasize the absence of concrete prejudice, the fully developed record, and the limited practical difference between the existing analysis and Rule 15. It may also question whether this case requires a Rule 16 inquiry at all. Thomas will join that dissent rather than necessarily write separately. A short concurrence accepting the judgment but emphasizing amendment flexibility is plausible, but not the central forecast.

Vacatur is more likely than a categorical reversal barring the defense because the contemplated remedy is application of the correct standards, with a possible identical ultimate result. Reversal and remand is the principal alternative judgment label. A DIG is a small vehicle risk, not the expected disposition, and nothing in the record supplies a basis for forecasting equal division.
