## Why the case moves above the anchor

The lower opinion expressly separates unpleaded defenses from amendments. Appendix footnote 8 says that lack of prejudice governs the former, while Rules 15 and 16 and good cause govern the latter. That makes the issue more than an uncertain reconstruction of what the district court implicitly decided. The petitioner challenges a real difference in legal standards, not simply an omitted piece of paper. Sources: petition appendix 13a–14a; petitioner's merits brief, statement and Part III.

The respondent has a substantial merits-oriented defense: discovery covered Younge's responsibilities, she had notice of the personal-staff issue, and the lower courts found no lost opportunity that would change the result. Its brief invokes Rule 61, the absence of an express automatic-waiver sanction, the 1966 change to Rule 12(h), and the distinction between new factual issues and legal arguments on a fixed record. The petitioner answers that amendments already provide a route to cure omissions, and that no-prejudice and purely-legal exceptions bypass the rules rather than implement them. Sources: respondent's merits brief, summary and Parts I–II; petitioner's merits reply, Parts II–III and Rule 61 discussion. I give real weight to the respondent's argument as an affirmance or harmlessness route, but it is weaker once the petitioner accepts a constructive amendment.

The decisive incremental evidence is the **argument's convergence on using the amendment standards without insisting on a separately filed motion**. Barrett repeatedly tested that accommodation with both advocates and expressly suggested sending the case back even if the Eleventh Circuit might ultimately reach the same result. Kagan pressed why the existing rule standards should not control rather than a standard that is sometimes stricter and sometimes looser. Gorsuch identified bad faith, dilatory conduct, and undue delay as considerations that a no-prejudice rule cannot exhaust. Jackson objected to the incentives created by allowing a different route around amendment. Sotomayor likewise reduced the issue to the full Foman inquiry. Those are substantive, mutually reinforcing signals across at least five Justices, not an inference from question totals. Source: `argument-transcript.txt`, petitioner's constructive-amendment exchanges and respondent's exchanges with those Justices.

Kavanaugh emphasized that good cause and prejudice ask different questions; I read that as a weaker lean toward enforcing the rule framework, not as an express commitment to the petitioner. Roberts asked essentially administrative questions, so his predicted vote rests mostly on the available narrow disposition and the lineup reference point. Thomas repeatedly asked what prejudice Younge actually suffered; Alito questioned the practical difference, the defense's classification, and whether a scheduling deadline existed. Those make them the most plausible dissenters, although either could join a narrow remand. A question is not a vote: the 7–2 point forecast is substantially more fragile than the majority-ground forecast.

The features corroborate the distribution of discussion, not its direction. Jackson's words were 363 during petitioner's presentation and 508 during respondent's; Alito's were 436 and 165. These figures come with **3/3 segments attributed, 145/146 judicial turns attributed to a segment, one judicial turn outside segments, zero unresolved Justice labels, and zero unrecognized label lines**. Overall petitioner-side judicial words exceed respondent-side words, 2,434 to 1,938, so a naive aggregate question-count heuristic would miss the actual convergence. The substantive reading, not either count, drives the prediction.

## Residual uncertainty and writings

My judgment-class allocation is vacated 0.68, reversed 0.21, affirmed-in-part-reversed-in-part 0.02, affirmed 0.07, DIG 0.02, and equal division 0.00. Thus disturbed outcomes sum to 0.91. Vacatur is most likely because the Court can correct the framework while leaving application of Rule 16, the content of any amendment request, and the eventual discrimination disposition for remand. An outright reversal of the legal rule could instead be labeled reversed. An affirmance remains possible if enough Justices find the existing findings sufficient under the proper standard. A DIG is possible if the narrowing at argument creates a vehicle objection, but several Justices appeared to regard that narrowing as a solution rather than a reason to exit. All nine Justices appear in the transcript and no recusal is provisioned.

No sitting-assignment data was consulted. Joining a dissent would not itself make Thomas a writer. The other writing probabilities remain low because the forecasted rule is an accommodating procedural compromise.
