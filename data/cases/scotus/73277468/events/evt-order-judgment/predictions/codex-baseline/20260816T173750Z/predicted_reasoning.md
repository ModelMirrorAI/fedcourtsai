# Forecast

The Court will likely vacate the Eleventh Circuit's judgment and remand by a 7-2 vote. Chief Justice Roberts and Justices Sotomayor, Kagan, Gorsuch, Kavanaugh, Barrett, and Jackson are likely to join the majority; Justices Thomas and Alito are likely to dissent. A dismissal as improvidently granted, an equally divided affirmance, or a recusal affecting the result is unlikely.

The majority will hold that Rule 8(c)(1)'s command to state affirmative defenses in a responsive pleading operates together with Rules 15(a)(2) and 16(b)(4). When a defendant omitted a defense and the scheduling-order deadline has passed, the defendant cannot avoid the requirements for amendment and good cause merely by placing the defense in a summary-judgment motion. Lack of prejudice may matter to leave to amend, but it is not an independent substitute for the amendment rules.

The Court will apply that rule generally to affirmative defenses first raised in dispositive motions, rather than limiting it to Title VII's personal-staff exemption. It will leave room for defenses that were fairly pleaded despite imperfect labeling and for timely or constructively permitted amendments. Because the lower courts did not apply the Rule 16 good-cause and Rule 15 amendment standards to this defense, the likely judgment is vacatur and remand rather than a final merits judgment for Younge.

The majority is unlikely to decide whether Younge ultimately falls within Title VII's personal-staff exemption or whether the District Attorney's Office could satisfy the amendment standards on remand. The dissent will likely emphasize fair notice, the absence of prejudice after extensive discovery, and the Federal Rules' preference for decisions on the merits rather than pleading technicalities.
