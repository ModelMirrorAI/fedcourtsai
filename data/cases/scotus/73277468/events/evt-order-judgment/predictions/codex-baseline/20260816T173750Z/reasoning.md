I adjusted upward from that baseline because the question presented is a clean, recurring conflict over the interaction of Rules 8(c), 15, and 16. The petition identifies several competing circuit approaches and has a strong textual and structural argument: Rule 8(c)(1) says a party "must" state an affirmative defense, Rule 15 supplies the route for changing a pleading, and Rule 16 requires good cause after a scheduling deadline. The Eleventh Circuit expressly said it treats unpleaded affirmative defenses differently from late amendments, making the asserted conflict unusually direct. The likely remedy is vacatur so the lower court can address amendment and good cause under the proper standard.

Those facts support fair notice and lack of prejudice. The Court's preference for resolving cases on the merits, along with Rule 8's failure to state an automatic-waiver consequence, could support affirmance or a narrower notice-based rule.
