# Forecast: Younge v. Fulton Judicial Circuit District Attorney's Office (No. 25-352)

**Event:** the judgment the Court enters after argument, forecast at the grant
moment (certiorari granted March 30, 2026; argument set for November 2, 2026).

## Judgment class

The Court will **disturb the judgment below** — P(disturbed) = **0.72** — and
the modal label is **`vacated`**: the Court holds the Eleventh Circuit's
*Hassan* prejudice-only standard inconsistent with Rules 8(c), 15, and 16, and
vacates and remands for the lower courts to apply the correct standard to the
DA's Office's late-raised personal-staff defense, rather than directing
judgment for Younge. Within my 0.72 of disturbance I put roughly 0.40 on
`vacated`, 0.28 on `reversed` (the Court applies its rule itself and holds the
defense forfeited on this record — counsel conceded he discovered the defense
only while drafting the summary-judgment motion, a year past the scheduling
order's amendment deadline), and 0.04 on the mixed label. The undisturbed
complement is almost entirely a straight **affirmance** (~0.24) — the Court
adopts the lenient five-circuit prejudice-only rule on facts that flatter it —
with a small residue (~0.03–0.04) for a DIG, live but unlikely: the
respondent's brief in opposition disputed the factual premise (arguing its
answer's "personal staff" admission pleaded the defense), and if the Court
comes to see the case as turning on that record dispute rather than the split,
a DIG is the exit. An equally divided affirmance is negligible; no recusal is
apparent.

## Vote lineup

**Unanimous or near-unanimous for vacatur: 9–0 is my modal lineup**, and I
predict all nine Justices in the majority. The Court's recent Federal Rules
cases (Dupree v. Younger, Fort Bend County v. Davis, Jones v. Bock) run
unanimous or lopsided, and the textual argument — Rule 8(c)(1) says "must"
plead "any" affirmative defense, and the summary-judgment end-run reads
Rules 15(a)(2) and 16(b)(4) out of the book — travels across the Court's
methodological camps. The likeliest defectors, if any, are one or two of the
pragmatist-leaning Justices (Alito or Kavanaugh) writing that the Rules'
harmless-error and function-over-form tradition (Rule 61, Foman, Parrish)
supports the prejudice test; an 8–1 or 7–2 with a dissent on those lines is
the main alternative to unanimity. Expect a single, fairly short majority
opinion; a concurrence sketching how district courts should handle late
defenses on remand (construe-as-motion-to-amend, per the Second Circuit's
Monahan) would not be surprising.

## Ground and breadth

If the Court disturbs, the ground will be **textual and structural**: Rule
8(c)'s mandatory pleading command cannot be evaded by the tactical choice to
skip amendment and move straight for summary judgment, because that maneuver
substitutes a bare prejudice inquiry for the stricter, reticulated standards
of Rule 15(a)(2) (leave of court, Foman factors including undue delay) and
Rule 16(b)(4) (good cause after a scheduling-order deadline). The likely
disposition of the parties' competing frames: the Court rejects the Eleventh
Circuit's *Hassan* line but also declines the D.C. Circuit's per se bar,
landing at or near the intermediate position — an unpleaded defense may be
entertained only on the amendment standards, so undue delay and diligence
matter independently of prejudice. The rule will be stated categorically and
trans-substantively, for all civil cases, not confined to Title VII; the
application — whether this defendant's year-late, admittedly inadvertent
raising survives good-cause scrutiny — goes back down. The Court will take the
question presented essentially **as written** (the respondent's merits brief
already litigates it on those terms), and will not reach the second-order
question whether the personal-staff exemption was in fact adequately pleaded
by the answer's admission — that is remand fodder.
