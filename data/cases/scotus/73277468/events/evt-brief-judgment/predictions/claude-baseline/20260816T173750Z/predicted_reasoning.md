# Predicted reasoning — Younge v. Fulton Judicial Circuit District Attorney's Office (No. 25-352), merits judgment, briefed moment

**Judgment class: `reversed`.** P(judgment below disturbed) = 0.78. Within the
disturbed mass I expect an outright reversal (~0.45) somewhat ahead of a
vacatur-and-remand (~0.30), with the mixed in-part label a remote residue
(~0.03). The undisturbed complement (~0.22) is almost entirely a straight
affirmance adopting the prejudice test; a DIG or an equally divided affirmance
is negligible — the QP is clean, the vehicle fully briefed, and no recusal is
apparent on the docket.

**Why the Court disturbs.** The Eleventh Circuit's *Hassan* rule lets a
defendant raise a never-pleaded affirmative defense by summary-judgment motion,
subject only to a plaintiff-borne prejudice showing, while the same circuit
bars the same defense if the defendant instead moves to amend after the Rule
16(b)(4) deadline without good cause. That internal asymmetry — the motion
route open while the amendment route is closed — is the petition's strongest
card, and the current Court's Federal Rules cases enforce rule text against
purpose-based glosses. Rule 8(c)(1) says "must ... any"; Rules 15(a)(2) and
16(b)(4) say "only." Multiple doctrinal paths lead to disturbance: a strict
amendment-required rule reverses outright; the intermediate diligence rule
(three circuits) vacates, because defense counsel admitted the defense first
came to mind while drafting the summary-judgment motion, a year past the
deadline, with no good-cause showing; even a retained-but-tightened prejudice
standard that credits the GERA forum-channeling consequences of the late
personal-staff assertion would vacate.

**Why it might not.** Respondent's structural case is genuinely strong: Rule
8(c) states no consequence for violation (*State Farm v. Rigsby* posture), Rule
12(h) expressly waives only four defenses (expressio unius), *Wood v. Milyard*
treats an unpleaded defense as correctable forfeiture, and *Foman*'s
anti-formalism is the Rules' founding rhetoric. Eight circuits allow the
motion route; affirmance would not be an outlier. That path carries my ~0.22.

**Vote lineup.** I predict a unanimous Court, 9–0 for petitioner: Roberts,
Thomas, Alito, Sotomayor, Kagan, Gorsuch, Kavanaugh, Barrett, Jackson all in
the majority. The Court's recent FRCP-interpretation decisions run unanimous
or near-unanimous, and the textualist and plaintiff-side instincts point the
same way here. The likeliest crack is one or two votes (Alito or Kavanaugh)
for a discretion-preserving affirmance or a concurrence in judgment resting on
the diligence rule rather than a per se amendment requirement.

**Ground and breadth** (the two graded semantic claims, stated structurally in
`prediction.json`): the majority rests on the text of Rules 8(c), 15, and 16
read together and rejects *Hassan*'s no-prejudice gloss as an atextual bypass
of the amendment rules; and it states a categorical, transsubstantive rule
rather than a fact-bound one, while leaving open Rule 15(b) constructive
amendment and declining to reach whether Younge was actually "personal staff"
under § 2000e(f) — that question goes back on remand.

**Separate writings** (context only — no artifact resolves authorship): a short
concurrence sketching when trial-by-consent or Rule 15(b) still lets a late
defense in is plausible; a dissent, if any, adopts respondent's
forfeiture-plus-prejudice frame. The majority opinion likely falls to one of
the Court's frequent civil-procedure authors (Barrett, Kagan, or Jackson).

**Timing** (unscored, stated for discipline): argued November 2, 2026; a
decision between late January and April 2027, on the early side if unanimous.
