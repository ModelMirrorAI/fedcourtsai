# Forecast of the Court's judgment

The Court is more likely than not to affirm the Eleventh Circuit, probably by a cross-ideological 6-3 vote. The majority will distinguish Rule 8(c)'s mandatory pleading duty from the consequence of violating it. It will reason that Rule 8 does not say an omitted affirmative defense is permanently waived, while Rules 12(h) and 71.1 expressly prescribe waiver in specified settings. Read with Rule 61's instruction to disregard errors that do not affect substantial rights, that contrast will support allowing an inadvertently omitted defense at summary judgment when the plaintiff suffered no prejudice from the delay.

The Court will reach the single question presented and hold that the DA's Office could assert the personal-staff defense on this record without first amending its answer. The majority will treat Rules 15 and 16 as governing amendments that inject new factual issues into a case, not every later legal argument based on a fully developed record. It will stress that Younge's own testimony established the material facts, that further discovery would not have altered the personal-staff analysis, and that the district court rejected the separate policymaking-appointee defense when its late assertion would have been prejudicial.

The holding will likely be narrower than a categorical license to raise any omitted defense. The majority will reserve deliberate waiver or sandbagging, defenses dependent on disputed facts, and cases in which earlier notice could have changed discovery or litigation choices. It will affirm rather than vacate because the lower courts' no-prejudice finding satisfies that rule and the underlying personal-staff determination is not independently at issue.

The forecast lineup is Chief Justice Roberts and Justices Thomas, Alito, Kagan, Kavanaugh, and Barrett in the majority, with Justices Sotomayor, Gorsuch, and Jackson dissenting. The dissent will argue that Rules 8, 15, and 16 form an integrated textual scheme: an omitted defense must be added through an amendment governed by ordinary leave and scheduling standards, and Rule 61 does not authorize a court to bypass those requirements in the first instance. A dismissal as improvidently granted or an equally divided Court is unlikely.
