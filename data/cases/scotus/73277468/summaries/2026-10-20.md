## In brief

Younge challenged a Title VII ruling against her after the Eleventh Circuit allowed her employer to raise an unpled defense at summary judgment.

## What happened

Jasmine Younge sued the Fulton County District Attorney's Office, claiming she was fired because of her pregnancy, in violation of Title VII. The office's answer did not plead a defense that Younge was exempt from Title VII as a member of the elected district attorney's "personal staff." Only after discovery closed did the office raise this defense, in a motion for summary judgment. The magistrate judge, district court, and Eleventh Circuit all allowed the defense despite it never being pled, finding Younge was not prejudiced by the delay, and ruled against her on the merits.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked whether a defendant that never included a particular defense in its answer, and never asked to add it, can still rely on that defense to win a motion for summary judgment, or whether rules requiring defenses to be pled—and limiting late changes to pleadings—bar that defense unless the defendant formally seeks to amend its answer.

## Where it stands

The Court granted the petition, the case was briefed and argued, and a reply brief was filed and distributed after argument. The docket shows no decision yet.

## What each outcome would mean

Younge, who lost in the lower courts, is asking the Court to reverse the Eleventh Circuit's decision. If the Court affirms, the Eleventh Circuit's approach stands, and the dismissal of Younge's pregnancy discrimination claim based on the personal-staff defense remains in place. If the Court reverses, the Eleventh Circuit's decision is undone, favoring Younge's position that unpled defenses cannot be used this way, and the case would likely return to the lower courts for further proceedings. If the Court vacates, the lower court's decision is set aside and the case sent back without deciding who should ultimately win.
