## In brief

Jasmine Younge asked the Supreme Court to decide whether a defendant must formally amend its answer before relying on an unpleaded defense at summary judgment.

## What happened

Jasmine Younge sued the Fulton Judicial Circuit District Attorney's Office under Title VII, claiming she was fired because she was pregnant. After discovery closed, the office argued for the first time, in a motion for summary judgment, that Younge was exempt from Title VII coverage because she was part of the elected district attorney's "personal staff." The office never amended its answer to plead this defense. The magistrate judge, district court, and the Eleventh Circuit Court of Appeals all allowed the defense to be considered, reasoning that Younge was not prejudiced by the delay, and ultimately ruled against Younge on the merits. Younge asked the Supreme Court to review that outcome, pointing to disagreement among appeals courts on the question.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked whether a defendant that never included a particular defense in its answer can still rely on that defense in a summary judgment motion, without going through the usual process for amending pleadings, so long as the delay caused the other side no unfair disadvantage. Younge argues the defense should be treated as lost unless the defendant formally amends its answer under the ordinary rules governing amendments. The district attorney's office argues that courts may excuse the omission and consider the defense anyway when the other side was not harmed by the delay.

## Where it stands

## What each outcome would mean

Younge, as the party who petitioned for review, asked the Court to overturn the lower court's approach. If the Court sides with Younge, the case would likely return to the lower courts under a rule requiring defendants to formally seek permission to amend their answers before raising a previously unpleaded defense, which could revive Younge's discrimination claim or at least require fresh consideration of whether the defense was properly raised.
