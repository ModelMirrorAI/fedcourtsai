## What happened

Jasmine Younge worked briefly in 2019 as a deputy chief of staff at the Fulton County (Georgia) District Attorney's Office before she was fired. She sued the office under Title VII, claiming pregnancy discrimination. Title VII does not cover an elected official's "personal staff," and the district attorney's office argued at summary judgment—without ever having stated this defense in its answer—that Younge fell into that category. The magistrate judge, district court, and the Eleventh Circuit Court of Appeals all agreed the office could raise this defense that late because Younge was not harmed by the delay, and all three ruled that Younge was indeed personal staff, so her suit was dismissed.

## What the Court is being asked

The case asks whether a defendant who never included a defense in its formal court answer can still rely on that defense later, when asking a judge to decide the case without a trial, so long as the delay caused no real harm to the other side. Younge argues that courts should instead require a defendant in that situation to formally ask to change its answer, which is governed by stricter rules, and that several appeals courts require exactly that while others do not. The district attorney's office answers that requiring formal amendment elevates procedural technicality over resolving cases correctly, especially when the underlying facts are undisputed.

## Where it stands

The Supreme Court granted review, and the case has been fully briefed and argued; it was set for argument on November 2, 2026, and now awaits decision.
