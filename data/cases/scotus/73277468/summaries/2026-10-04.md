## In brief

Younge asks the Court to decide whether an employer can rely on an affirmative defense never included in its answer when seeking summary judgment.

## What happened

Jasmine Younge sued the Fulton Judicial Circuit District Attorney's Office, Georgia, claiming she was fired because she was pregnant, in violation of Title VII. The office's answer did not raise the defense that Younge was exempt from Title VII coverage as a member of an elected official's "personal staff." Only after discovery closed did the office raise this defense, in a motion for summary judgment. The magistrate judge and district court allowed the defense, found no prejudice to Younge from the delay, and ruled for the office on the merits. The Eleventh Circuit affirmed, holding that an unpled affirmative defense may be raised at summary judgment so long as the plaintiff is not prejudiced.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked whether a defendant that never included an affirmative defense in its answer, and never sought to add it by amendment, can still rely on that defense to win summary judgment, or whether rules requiring defenses to be pleaded and requiring court approval to add new ones must be followed instead.

## Where it stands

The case was granted, briefed, and argued on November 2, 2026; it now awaits decision following the parties' merits briefs, amicus filings, and a reply brief distributed September 1, 2026.

## What each outcome would mean

Younge, as petitioner, asked the Court to take up this question after losing below. If the Court rules for Younge, the Eleventh Circuit's decision would be reversed or vacated, and the case could return to the lower courts under a rule requiring the office to have sought formal amendment of its answer, potentially reviving Younge's Title VII claim. If the Court rules for the Fulton Judicial Circuit District Attorney's Office, the lower courts' judgment in the office's favor would stand, confirming that a defendant may raise a previously unpled affirmative defense at summary judgment where the plaintiff shows no prejudice from the delay.
