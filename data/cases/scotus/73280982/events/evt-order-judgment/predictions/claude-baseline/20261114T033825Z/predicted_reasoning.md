# Forecast — Robinhood Markets, Inc. v. Sodha (No. 25-944)

**Judgment:** The Court will vacate the Ninth Circuit's judgment and remand. The judgment below is disturbed.

**Lineup:** 9–0. No dissent. At most one short concurrence.

**Question reached:** Only the reformulated question in the grant order: whether Section 11(a)'s misleading-omissions prong allows liability on a finding that omitted interim financial information was material, without an additional inquiry into whether the omission made an affirmative statement misleading. The Court will answer no. It will not reach Item 303 or the required-statements prong, which were left out of the grant and will proceed on remand regardless.

**Ground:** The text of 15 U.S.C. 77k(a) makes liability turn on a "material fact … necessary to make the statements therein not misleading," which states two requirements. The Court will extend Omnicare (Section 11 is not a general duty to disclose all material facts; misleadingness is judged against the statement in its full context, including hedges and disclaimers) and Macquarie (the parallel Rule 10b-5(b) language reaches half-truths, not pure omissions) to hold that a plaintiff must identify a particular statement in the registration statement and show how the omitted interim results made that statement misleading. It will reject the Ninth Circuit's holding that the duty to disclose "collapses into" materiality whenever a statement about one period is followed by a later undisclosed development.

**Breadth:** A categorical statement of the elements for every Section 11(a) misleading-omissions claim, paired with a refusal to go further. The Court will not decide whether Robinhood's registration statement was misleading, will not choose between the "classic materiality" test and the First Circuit's "extreme departure" test for interim results, and will note that statements of historical results do not by themselves imply anything about later periods, while leaving room for cases where an issuer presents past results as indicative of the future. Application goes back to the lower courts.

**Who writes:** Justice Kagan or Justice Sotomayor is the most likely author, given their opinions in Omnicare and Macquarie; Justice Barrett is the next most likely. If anyone writes separately it will be Justice Jackson or Justice Sotomayor (stressing that the remand leaves respondents free to show their half-truth theory) or Justice Thomas or Justice Gorsuch (on a side point).

**Procedural exits:** A dismissal as improvidently granted is live but unlikely (about 7%): respondents maintain the parties agree on the two-element rule and dispute only its application, and the Court dismissed two Ninth Circuit securities cases on similar grounds in 2024. The reformulated question was written to prevent that. No recusal is expected, so an equally divided Court is not a realistic outcome.

**Timing (unscored):** Argument in the February or March 2027 sitting; decision by late June 2027, more likely May or early June given expected unanimity.
