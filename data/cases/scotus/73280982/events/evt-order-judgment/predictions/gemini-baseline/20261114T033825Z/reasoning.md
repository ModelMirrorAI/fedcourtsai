## 3. Adjustments from the 70.5% Baseline to `P(judgment-disturbed) = 0.84`

### Upward drivers (+21 to +22 percentage points before vehicle discount)
1. **Unambiguous textual and precedential error below:** The Ninth Circuit panel majority (Judge M. Smith, joined by Judge Rakoff over Judge Rawlinson's dissent) stated that *"Robinhood had a duty to disclose the third quarter information if it was material,"* held in Footnote 7 that *Matrixx* and *Macquarie* do not govern Section 11(a) because Rule 10b-5 lacks a required-statements prong, and remanded solely for the district court to evaluate materiality under "normal materiality standards." That collapses Section 11(a)'s two separate requirements ("material fact" + "necessary to make the statements therein not misleading") into one, violating the surplusage canon and contradicting *Omnicare* (575 U.S. at 190–96), *Matrixx* (563 U.S. at 44), and *Macquarie* (601 U.S. at 263–65, which specifically pointed to § 11(a)'s required-statements prong to contrast pure omissions with half-truths).
2. **SG + SEC CVSG alignment and exact QP adoption:** Following the June 1, 2026 CVSG, the Solicitor General and SEC filed a strong brief explaining that the Ninth Circuit misstated the governing legal rule, deepened confusion across the First, Second, and Ninth Circuits, and should be vacated. On November 13, 2026, the Court adopted the Solicitor General's reformulated Question 1 verbatim and denied Question 2 as recommended.
3. **Cross-ideological consensus & Respondent's own legal concession:** Modern statutory securities-pleading cases at the Court (*Macquarie*, 9–0 per Sotomayor, J.; *Slack*, 9–0 per Gorsuch, J.; *Omnicare*, 9–0 in judgment per Kagan, J.; *Sripetch*, 9–0 per Gorsuch, J.) draw broad consensus rather than 6–3 ideological splits. Indeed, in their October 21, 2026 supplemental brief (pp. 1, 7), respondents—newly represented by Gupta Wessler—explicitly conceded that pure omissions are not actionable under Section 11(a)'s misleading-omissions prong and that a plaintiff must prove both materiality and statement-specific misleadingness.

### Downward adjustments / Undisturbed pathways (-8 percentage points -> `0.84`)
Rather than pushing `P(disturbed)` above `0.90`, two genuine undisturbed risks warrant keeping `0.16` mass on undisturbed outcomes:
1. **Elevated DIG risk (~`0.07`, over 3x the `2.1%` base rate):** In OT2024, the Court DIG'd *both* of its Ninth Circuit securities cases after oral argument—*Facebook, Inc. v. Amalgamated Bank*, 604 U.S. 4 (2024), and *NVIDIA Corp. v. E. Ohman J:or Fonder AB*, 604 U.S. 20 (2024)—when merits briefing exposed either petitioner overreach beyond the clean cert question or case-specific disputes over what the complaint/panel said. Respondents' supplemental brief already laid the groundwork for a DIG pitch: conceding the two-element textual standard while arguing there is "no adversity" on QP1 and urging a charitable reading of the Ninth Circuit's opinion as having implicitly assumed half-truth context. Moreover, if Robinhood's merits brief overreaches by pressing for a categorical rule insulating historical numbers and "might not continue" risk warnings from intra-quarter downturns (a tension respondents flagged at Resp. Supp. Br. 8–10), five Justices could conclude the parties are talking past the granted question and DIG the writ. Still, DIG is far from modal because—unlike *Facebook* and *NVIDIA*—the SG and SEC vetted this exact vehicle objection pre-grant, the Court granted certiorari *despite* respondents' supplemental brief, and leaving a published Ninth Circuit opinion with Footnote 7 on the books would misdirect district courts circuit-wide.
2. **Affirmance risk (~`0.085`):** Respondents will argue on the merits (a) that read alongside Robinhood's extensive prospectus statements about KPIs and Q3 seasonal headwinds, the Ninth Circuit's *Stadnick* "total mix" formulation was merely imprecise shorthand for contextual misleadingness, or (b) that the judgment vacating dismissal should be affirmed on the alternative ground that the complaint plausibly pleads half-truths under *Omnicare*. While the Court rarely affirms by auditing complex registration-statement allegations in the first instance ("court of review, not of first view"), this accounts for ~8–9% probability mass.
3. **Equally divided affirmance (~`0.005`):** Neither the CVSG order nor the grant order noted any recusal despite fifteen bank/broker-dealer underwriters among the petitioners, and an 8-Justice bench would still divide overwhelmingly for vacatur rather than 4–4.

## 4. Choice of Judgment Label: Why `vacated` Over `reversed`

Across all parsed merits cases in `OT2017–OT2025`, `reversed` (`252`) outnumbers `vacated` (`131`) roughly 2-to-1. Here, however, **`vacated` (~`0.69` unconditional) strongly dominates `reversed` (~`0.13`)** (with `affirmed-in-part-reversed-in-part` ~`0.02`):
- Every analogous Supreme Court securities decision correcting an appellate legal standard without deciding complaint sufficiency in the first instance entered **`vacated`**: *Macquarie* (601 U.S. at 266), *Slack* (598 U.S. at 770), *Omnicare* (575 U.S. at 196), and *Goldman Sachs* (594 U.S. at 127). Contrast *Hikma Pharmaceuticals USA Inc. v. Amarin Pharma, Inc.* (June 4, 2026), where the Federal Circuit had *reversed* dismissal of a single claim and the Supreme Court itself held on the pleadings that the complaint failed Rule 12(b)(6), entering `reversed`.
- Here, the Ninth Circuit's own judgment was `AFFIRMED in part, VACATED in part, and REMANDED`, and it also vacated dismissal on **Item 303 (Question 2, cert denied)**—meaning the case must return to the lower courts regardless.
- Most tellingly, the Solicitor General's brief (pp. 13–14) explicitly framed the relief as vacatur and remand (*"if this Court holds that the court of appeals misstated the applicable legal rule, vacates its judgment, and remands for application of the proper standard"*), and the granted QP asks only whether liability may rest on materiality *"without an additional inquiry to determine whether the omission rendered any affirmative statement misleading."*
