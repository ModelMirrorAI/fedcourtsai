# Predicted Merits Reasoning: *Robinhood Markets, Inc. v. Sodha* (No. 25-944)

## Judgment and Vote Lineup

The Supreme Court will **vacate** the judgment of the Ninth Circuit and remand for further proceedings (`vacated`), by a **9–0** vote in which all nine Justices (`Roberts`, `Thomas`, `Alito`, `Sotomayor`, `Kagan`, `Gorsuch`, `Kavanaugh`, `Barrett`, and `Jackson`) join the opinion of the Court.

Vacatur and remand, rather than outright reversal, follows from both the limited grant and the posture below. The Ninth Circuit affirmed the dismissal of respondents' Item 105 theory, vacated the dismissal of their Section 11(a) misleading-omissions theory, and separately vacated the dismissal of their Item 303 / required-statements theory—a second question on which the Supreme Court denied review. Because the Ninth Circuit instructed the district court on remand to evaluate only the materiality of Robinhood's intra-quarter July 2021 key-performance-indicator declines without conducting a statement-specific misleadingness inquiry, the Supreme Court will follow its consistent practice in *Macquarie Infrastructure Corp. v. Moab Partners, L. P.*, 601 U.S. 257 (2024), *Slack Technologies, LLC v. Pirani*, 598 U.S. 759 (2023), and *Omnicare, Inc. v. Laborers Dist. Council Constr. Industry Pension Fund*, 575 U.S. 175 (2015): correct the legal standard, vacate the judgment of the court of appeals, and remand for the lower courts to apply the proper standard in the first instance alongside the unreviewed Item 303 remand.

## Scope of Review and Doctrinal Ground (`majority-ground`)

The Court reaches only the single, reformulated Question Presented adopted verbatim from the Solicitor General's invitation brief, leaving Question 2 (Item 303's temporal scope and quantification of intra-quarter trends) untouched.

Relying on the plain text of 15 U.S.C. § 77k(a) and the canon against surplusage, the majority holds that Section 11(a)'s misleading-omissions clause imposes two distinct requirements—that an omitted fact be material and that its omission render an affirmative statement in the registration statement misleading—extending *Macquarie Infrastructure Corp. v. Moab Partners, L. P.*, 601 U.S. 257 (2024), and *Omnicare, Inc. v. Laborers Dist. Council Constr. Industry Pension Fund*, 575 U.S. 175 (2015), to foreclose pure-omission liability for undisclosed interim financial data while also rejecting the First Circuit's non-textual "extreme departure" test from *Shaw v. Digital Equipment Corp.*, 82 F.3d 1194 (1st Cir. 1996). Specifically, the Court explains that Section 11(a) pairs two distinct omission triggers—omitting a material fact "required to be stated therein" or omitting a material fact "necessary to make the statements therein not misleading." Treating materiality ("altering the total mix of information") as sufficient by itself under the second clause collapses the two requirements into one, renders the words "necessary to make the statements therein not misleading" superfluous, and circumvents Congress's decision to tie pure-omission duties to express statutory or SEC reporting mandates. The Court rejects Footnote 7 of the Ninth Circuit's opinion, holding that the presence of Section 11(a)'s adjacent required-statements prong reinforces rather than weakens *Macquarie*'s distinction between pure omissions and half-truths.

## Breadth of the Holding (`ground-breadth`)

The majority announces a categorical rule for all Section 11(a) misleading-omissions claims—requiring a statement-specific misleadingness inquiry alongside materiality whenever an issuer omits interim or other information—but confines its decision strictly to that legal standard, declining to adopt any special categorical safe harbor for intra-quarter financial fluctuations and leaving both the sufficiency of respondents' half-truth allegations against Robinhood's registration statement and the unreviewed Item 303 claim for the lower courts on remand. Just as the Court rejects the Ninth Circuit's collapse of misleadingness into materiality, it declines any invitation by petitioners to hold as a matter of law that historical financial results and forward-looking cautionary warnings ("might not continue") can never be rendered misleading by undisclosed intra-quarter metrics absent an express intra-quarter projection. Instead, the Court instructs that ordinary half-truth principles under *Omnicare* and *Macquarie* govern—requiring a plaintiff to identify particular affirmative statements in the registration statement and show how the omitted interim facts rendered what was said misleading in context—and leaves whether respondents' second amended complaint satisfies that standard for remand.

## Procedural Exit Risk (DIG or Recusal)

A dismissal of the writ as improvidently granted (DIG) is a live minority risk—not merely a theoretical one—given respondents' explicit concession in their supplemental brief that Section 11(a) does not reach pure omissions and their argument (modeled on *Facebook, Inc. v. Amalgamated Bank*, 604 U.S. 4 (2024), and *NVIDIA Corp. v. E. Ohman J:or Fonder AB*, 604 U.S. 20 (2024)) that the parties agree on the abstract legal standard and disagree only over how to read the Ninth Circuit's opinion or apply that standard to Robinhood's prospectus. However, the Court is unlikely to DIG the case because the Solicitor General and the SEC vetted that exact vehicle objection before the grant, the Court granted certiorari over respondents' supplemental brief on the Solicitor General's narrow formulation, and Footnote 7 plus the remand instruction of the published Ninth Circuit opinion explicitly state an erroneous materiality-only rule that would otherwise remain binding circuit precedent. An equally divided affirmance is virtually foreclosed: neither the CVSG order nor the grant order noted any recusal despite fifteen financial-institution underwriters among the petitioners, and the underlying textual question does not divide the Court 4–4.

## Expected Authorship and Separate Writings

**Majority author:** A unanimous, compact statutory securities opinion will most likely be assigned to **Justice Sotomayor** (who authored both *Macquarie* and *Matrixx Initiatives, Inc. v. Siracusano*, 563 U.S. 27 (2011)), **Justice Kagan** (who authored *Omnicare*), **Justice Barrett** (who authored *Goldman Sachs Group, Inc. v. Arkansas Teacher Retirement System*, 594 U.S. 113 (2021)), or **Justice Kavanaugh**, with **Justice Gorsuch** (*Slack*, *Sripetch*) and **Justice Jackson** (*Hikma*) also plausible depending on spring sitting workload balance.

**Separate writings:** Most likely no Justice writes separately, matching the pattern of recent unanimous securities and pleading decisions (*Macquarie*, *Slack*, *Sripetch*, *Hikma*). If a separate concurrence appears, the likeliest authors are **Justice Thomas**, **Justice Gorsuch**, or **Justice Alito** writing briefly to caution lower courts against treating accurate historical figures or standard risk disclosures as implied representations of current intra-quarter performance, or **Justice Jackson** / **Justice Sotomayor** writing briefly to emphasize that respondents' concrete half-truth allegations and unreviewed Item 303 claim remain fully open on remand.
