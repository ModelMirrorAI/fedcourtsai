## 2. Case-Specific Adjustments Driving `probability = 0.78`

Starting from the OT2025 `high`-band base rate of `41.1%` (and the pooled CVSG grant-family rate of `34.8%`), I adjust substantially upward to **0.78** (`granted = 1`, `predicted_disposition = "granted"`) based on the provisioned filings:

1. **Explicit SG & SEC Recommendation to Grant Question 1 (`sg-invited-brief.txt`)**: On October 7, 2026, Solicitor General D. John Sauer, joined by the General Counsel and Solicitor of the Securities and Exchange Commission, filed an invited brief urging the Court to grant certiorari on the first question presented (as reformulated). An agency-joined CVSG brief recommending a grant on a core federal statutory question is one of the strongest positive signals on the cert docket.
2. **Direct Tension with *Macquarie* (2024) and *Omnicare* (2015) Plus an Acknowledged Circuit Split**: In a published opinion (`154 F.4th 1019`) over a lengthy dissent by Judge Rawlinson, the Ninth Circuit held that whenever an alleged omission under Section 11(a) involves the relationship between a prior historical statement and a subsequent intra-quarter event, *"the inquiries as to [disclosure] duty and materiality coalesce"* and *"collaps[ed] the duty to disclose into materiality."* As both the petition and the SG detail, that holding directly collides with the statutory text (`"necessary to make the statements therein not misleading"`, 15 U.S.C. § 77k(a)), *Omnicare* (575 U.S. at 189–94), and *Macquarie* (601 U.S. at 264), and squarely splits from the Third, Tenth, and Eleventh Circuits (*Adams Golf*, *Chesapeake Energy*, *Oxford Asset Mgmt.*), which require separate materiality and misleadingness inquiries, as well as the First Circuit (*Shaw*, *Glassman*), which applies an "extreme departure" test that the Ninth Circuit explicitly rejected (`"Shaw . . . is not the law of this circuit"`).
3. **Clean Legal Framing vs. Recent Securities DIGs (*Facebook* and *NVIDIA*)**: In the BIO, respondents (represented by Scott+Scott and Deepak Gupta) lean heavily on the OT2024 dismissals as improvidently granted in *Facebook, Inc. v. Amalgamated Bank*, 604 U.S. 4 (2024), and *NVIDIA Corp. v. Ohman*, 604 U.S. 20 (2024), and argue that the parties do not disagree on QP1 as literally phrased. Both objections are well answered:
   - Unlike *Facebook* and *NVIDIA*, where factbound pleading disputes obscured the legal rule, here the Ninth Circuit explicitly stated: *"We hold only that the district court applied the wrong legal standard"* under Section 11(a) and *"express[ed] no view on how [respondents'] claims would fare under"* that standard, remanding for the district court to apply it in the first instance (Pet. App. 29a & n.6). That mirrors the clean interlocutory posture in *Macquarie*, 601 U.S. at 261–62.
   - The SG's proposed reformulation of QP1—asking whether liability can rest on a materiality determination *"without an additional inquiry to determine whether the omission rendered any affirmative statement misleading"*—directly forecloses respondents' attempt to concede the literal wording of QP1 while defending the Ninth Circuit's convergence rule.
4. **Advocate and Amicus Weight**: Petitioners are represented by Jeffrey B. Wall (with Cravath and Orrick for the underwriters) and supported by three cert-stage amicus briefs from major repeat players (Prof. Joseph A. Grundfest; SIFMA & the U.S. Chamber of Commerce; and Washington Legal Foundation).

### Why Not Higher Than 0.78? (Main Uncertainties and Downward Adjustments)
- **Split SG Recommendation (Grant QP1 / Deny QP2)**: The SG recommends denying review on Question 2 (the Item 303 required-statements claim) because the SEC is actively pursuing a rulemaking overhaul of Regulation S-K and because the Ninth Circuit made an unbriefed assumption equating Item 303's disclosure threshold with Section 11(a) materiality. Respondents can argue in their October 21, 2026 supplemental brief that granting QP1 alone will leave the Item 303 claim alive on remand regardless. While the SG directly addresses and rejects this vehicle objection (`sg-invited-brief.txt` at 23), splits between QP1 and QP2 occasionally cause the Court to pass on an interlocutory petition.
- **Post-*Facebook*/*NVIDIA* Caution**: Even with a clean legal holding below, some Justices may remain wary of Ninth Circuit securities petitions where the underlying offering documents contained extensive cautionary disclosures and preliminary estimates that the parties characterize differently.
- **Unprovisioned Post-CVSG Supplemental Briefs**: The snapshot (`2026-10-26.json`) records supplemental briefs filed by petitioners on October 20, 2026 and by respondents on October 21, 2026. Because `documents.json` only provisions `petition`, `questions-presented`, `brief-in-opposition`, `cert-reply`, and `sg-invited-brief` (fetched through October 18, 2026), I cannot read the text of those supplemental briefs directly, though their arguments are highly predictable from the SG's brief, the BIO, and the cert reply. ## 4. Rationale for `big_case_score = 0.62`

If decided on the merits, this case will be the Court's principal follow-on to *Omnicare* and *Macquarie* governing Section 11(a) liability for omitted intra-quarter financial data in IPO registration statements. Because roughly a quarter of U.S. IPOs occur in the Ninth Circuit and Section 11(a) imposes strict liability on issuers and underwriters, the decision has substantial financial-market and corporate-disclosure significance (`0.62`), while sitting below constitutional or nationwide public-law blockbusters.
