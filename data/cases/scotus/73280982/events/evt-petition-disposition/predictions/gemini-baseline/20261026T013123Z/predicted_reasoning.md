## Plenary Review vs. Summary Disposition

Conditional on a grant, the Court will almost certainly set the case for plenary briefing and oral argument rather than disposing of it summarily in the cert order (`summary-disposition-route = 0.04`). The Solicitor General explicitly recommends plenary review on Question 1 rather than a grant, vacate, and remand (GVR), and the Ninth Circuit panel majority already cited and distinguished *Macquarie Infrastructure Corp. v. Moab Partners, L.P.*, 601 U.S. 257 (2024), and *Omnicare, Inc. v. Laborers Dist. Council Constr. Indus. Pension Fund*, 575 U.S. 175 (2015), in footnote 3 of its opinion (`Pet. App. 22a n.3`).

If granted on the merits, the Court is likely to vacate the Ninth Circuit's judgment on the Section 11(a) misleading-omissions claim, holding that Section 11(a)'s misleading-omissions clause (`15 U.S.C. § 77k(a)`) requires a concrete showing that an omitted interim fact rendered a specific affirmative statement in the registration statement misleading in context, and that the duty-to-disclose inquiry does not automatically collapse into materiality whenever an offering document reports accurate historical results from a prior period and omits subsequent interim developments, while remanding for the lower courts to apply that standard to the specific statements and half-truth allegations in Plaintiffs' complaint.
