## Procedural Trajectory and Disposition

The docket currently records **two distributions**: an initial distribution on May 12, 2026 for the Conference of May 28, 2026 (which resulted in the June 1, 2026 CVSG order), and a second distribution on October 21, 2026 for the Conference of November 6, 2026 following the Solicitor General's October 7, 2026 invited amicus brief and the parties' October 20–21, 2026 supplemental briefs. Because a CVSG has already been called for and answered, no further CVSG will occur (`cvsg-increment` = 0.00).

The Court will likely **grant certiorari** (`probability` = 0.78, `predicted_disposition` = `granted`). Because the November 6, 2026 conference is the Court's first conference considering the petition with the Solicitor General's brief in hand—and because the Solicitor General recommends granting only the first question presented in a reformulated form while denying the second—there is a substantial chance (`relist-increment` = 0.45) that the Court relists the petition once more (for the November 13 or November 20, 2026 conference) to verify vehicle details and settle the wording of the limited/reformulated grant order, though a direct grant out of the November 6, 2026 conference so the case can be argued in the spring of OT2026 is slightly likelier (55%).

## Scope of Review (Question Presented)

When the Court grants review, it will follow the Solicitor General's recommendation to **grant review limited to the first question presented (as reformulated by the United States)** and deny review as to the second question presented concerning Item 303 of Regulation S-K. Specifically, the Court will take up whether an issuer can be found liable under the misleading-omissions prong of Section 11(a) of the Securities Act of 1933, 15 U.S.C. § 77k(a), based on a failure to disclose material interim financial information, without an additional inquiry into whether the omission rendered any affirmative statement in the registration statement misleading. Leaving Question 2 behind avoids entangling the Court in the SEC's ongoing rulemaking review of Regulation S-K and an unpreserved threshold issue regarding the interplay between Item 303's "reasonably likely" standard and Section 11(a) materiality.

## Summary Disposition vs. Plenary Review

Conditional on a grant, **plenary review with full merits briefing and oral argument is overwhelmingly likely** (`summary-disposition-route` = 0.04). Neither a GVR nor a summary reversal is probable: *Macquarie Infrastructure Corp. v. Moab Partners, L.P.*, 601 U.S. 257 (2024), was decided more than a year before the Ninth Circuit's decision and was expressly addressed below, no intervening Supreme Court merits case is pending on Section 11(a), and the Solicitor General seeks plenary review on a three-way circuit split rather than summary vacatur.

## Conditional Merits Outlook and Separate Writings on Denial

If granted on Question 1, the Court is likely to vacate or reverse the Ninth Circuit's holding on Section 11(a)'s misleading-omissions prong, holding—in line with *Omnicare, Inc. v. Laborers District Council Construction Industry Pension Fund*, 575 U.S. 175 (2015), and *Macquarie*, 601 U.S. 257—that Section 11(a)'s text requires separate inquiries into whether omitted intra-quarter information is material and whether its omission rendered a specific affirmative statement in the registration statement misleading in context.

In the less likely event that the Court denies certiorari despite the Solicitor General's recommendation, a silent denial is still the modal outcome, though there is a modest probability (`dissent-from-denial` = 0.12) that one or more Justices—most plausibly Justice Thomas, Justice Alito, or Justice Gorsuch—notes a dissent from or statement respecting the denial of certiorari to highlight the conflict between the Ninth Circuit's rule and *Macquarie*/*Omnicare*.
