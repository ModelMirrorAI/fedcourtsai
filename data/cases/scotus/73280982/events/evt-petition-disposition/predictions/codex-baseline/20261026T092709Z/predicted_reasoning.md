# Forecast

The Court will grant certiorari limited to the first question, reformulated along the lines proposed by the United States: whether materiality of omitted interim financial information suffices under Section 11(a)'s misleading-omissions prong, or whether a separate inquiry must establish that the omission made an affirmative statement misleading. It will leave the Item 303 question outside the grant.

The snapshot records two distributions: for May 28, 2026, before the CVSG, and for November 6, 2026, after the invited brief. These are not two completed ordinary relists; the second returns the petition to conference after the government's participation. From that two-distribution state, I expect one further distribution before a limited grant, most likely in November or December 2026. The probability of at least one additional distribution is 0.60; an immediate grant after the November 6 conference remains a substantial alternative.

The Court already called for the Solicitor General's views on June 1, 2026, and received the invited brief on October 7. No new CVSG is expected. The zero assigned to the CVSG increment reflects that completed step, not an absence of governmental interest; the harness can mask the already-fired claim.

Plenary briefing and argument will follow a grant. Conditional on any grant, a cert-order summary disposition has probability 0.04. The dispute concerns an allegedly recurring legal standard, and the government's proposed reformulation calls for adversarial merits consideration rather than a routine remand in light of a newly intervening decision.

If review is denied, I expect no published dissent or statement respecting denial; the conditional probability of any such writing is 0.08. Thomas is the most plausible individual writer in that low-probability branch, but no Justice is expected to write. The order-writer probabilities are unconditional individual probabilities, not a forecast of the merits opinion's author.

If the Court reaches the merits after granting review, the likely ground would require analysis of misleading effect distinct from materiality under the misleading-omissions prong. That would not establish a blanket exemption for interim financial information or dispose of the separate Item 303 theory.
