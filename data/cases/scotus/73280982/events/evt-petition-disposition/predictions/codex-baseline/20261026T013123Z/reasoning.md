# Rationale for the prediction

## Information set

This is a cert-stage petition prediction in forward mode. I used the provisioned `record/snapshots/2026-10-26.json` and `record/context.json`: docket 25-944, Robinhood Markets, Inc. v. Sodha, from the Ninth Circuit. The frozen context is `high`, `sal-v4`, docket-number Term 2025, two distributions, and a June 1, 2026 CVSG. There is no cutoff. Although the event is the ordinary petition-disposition event, its supplied baseline includes the invited government brief; I forecast from that actual information set, not an imagined first-distribution record. No own-case disposition was sought or encountered, and I do not independently know it.

I read the questions in the opening of `petition.txt` (printed p. I), the petition's introduction, the opposition's introduction and vehicle discussion (printed pp. 1-5, 30-32), the reply's competing account of the Ninth Circuit's rule and vehicle (printed pp. 1-6, 12-13), and the relevant discussion and conclusion of `sg-invited-brief.txt` (printed pp. 9-19, 22-24). The separate `questions-presented.txt` is a bad extraction: it contains the petition's importance discussion around printed pp. 31-33, not the questions. I recovered the actual questions from the petition itself and checked them against the government's presentation. This defect is flagged.

`documents.json` lists the opposition, reply, petition, QP extract, and invited SG brief as text-available and not OCR-derived. The petition is truncated and contains some appendix material; the other substantive filings listed are not marked truncated. The October 20 petitioner supplemental brief and October 21 respondent supplemental brief appear on the snapshot but were not provisioned. I do not infer either one's contents or assume the respondents failed to answer the government. Their absence limits confidence and is disclosed in flags and tooling feedback. Contact details in the inputs play no part in the forecast.

## Base rate and adjustment

The committed statpack's `sal-v4` table matches the context. I pooled every shown Term strictly before this petition's docket-number Term 2025: 2017-2024. Using the `high` band's bracketed reached rates and weighted resolved denominators from `metrics/statpack.json` gives 314 grant-family outcomes over 898 weighted resolved petitions, or 34.97%. This includes grants and GVRs together; no cross-Term distinction between those labels is assumed. Terms 2025 and 2026 are excluded. These are the supplied committed pack's historical estimates, not a refreshed census or a claim about current remote-corpus freshness; I made no live corpus lookup.

For scale, the pack's modern-cert disposition table gives 1,250 grant-family outcomes among 44,686 resolved petitions, about 2.8%, but this is not an appropriate anchor for a paid high-band petition. The paid-segment CVSG cut is approximately 34.8% grant-family among 164 resolved petitions, versus approximately 6.2% without a CVSG. Those all-Term cuts are descriptive cross-checks, not strictly-prior baselines. Most importantly, neither published cut conditions on an invited recommendation to grant.

I raise the 34.97% high-band anchor to **80%** because the SG expressly recommends granting the first question and supplies a reformulation directed at the respondents' strongest objection. The government's discussion identifies a disputed legal distinction between materiality and misleading effect, endorses broader regulatory importance, and argues that the interlocutory posture does not prevent review. Its conclusion seeks review only of QP1, reformulated to emphasize an additional misleadingness inquiry. This is stronger evidence than the mere existence of a CVSG, and the government's securities-regulatory perspective matters to this particular question. The size of the uplift is judgment, not an estimated SG-recommendation-conditioned frequency; the committed pack supplies no such frequency.

The main reason not to go above 80% is vehicle quality. The opposition argues that both sides agree with the first question as originally worded, that the Ninth Circuit merely applied settled standards to particular allegations, and that prior pleading-stage securities grants have exposed illusory legal disputes. It also stresses remand before final judgment. Moreover, an Item 303 required-statements claim can continue even if petitioners prevail on misleading omissions. The SG acknowledges that surviving theory but regards guidance on QP1 as independently important. Its proposed additional-inquiry formulation helps answer the lack-of-adversity objection, but does not eliminate the Court's discretion to wait for a cleaner vehicle. I accordingly forecast a limited-question grant, not review of both questions.

The legal comparisons to Macquarie and Omnicare here are the arguments developed in the provisioned filings, especially the SG brief; they are not a claim that Rule 10b-5 and every branch of Section 11 are identical. I attempted general-precedent-only web checks, which returned no usable content. I rely on the supplied materials rather than treating those attempts as independent verification.

## Other claims and stakes

The 42% additional-distribution probability reflects a live post-SG petition where reformulating QP1 and debating the independent regulatory theory may take another conference. The two already observed distributions are not two ordinary relists: the first led to the CVSG and the second is the scheduled post-brief consideration. The paid-segment terminal relist buckets in the pack show grant-family shares of about 1.7% at zero relists, 13.4% at one, 40.6% at two, and 36.8% at three or more. These terminal categories cannot be read as forward transition probabilities from today's state, so the 42% is not a lookup from that table.

`cvsg-increment = 0` states no further call is expected; the harness should mask this claim because a CVSG is already recorded. `summary-disposition-route = 0.04` is conditional on a grant. The petition and reply invoke existing precedent rather than identify a new post-judgment controlling decision requiring an immediate GVR; the SG asks for plenary examination of QP1, making a summary route a small residual possibility. `dissent-from-denial = 0.12` is conditional on denial. The order-writer figures are much smaller unconditional probabilities, covering writings on the final petition order across all routes; they are not cert votes and do not imply that anyone will necessarily write. The aggregate chance of a writing on the modal grant is particularly low.

The stakes score of 0.55 reflects a nationally applicable securities-disclosure issue with material implications for issuers, investors, and IPO litigation, moderated by its technical scope and the likely exclusion of the broader Item 303 question. It does not encode likelihood of a grant. No per-Justice cert votes are supplied because the record gives little basis for a useful lineup prediction.
