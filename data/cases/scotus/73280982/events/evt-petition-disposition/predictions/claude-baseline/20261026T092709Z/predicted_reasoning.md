# Predicted course — Robinhood Markets, Inc. v. Sodha (No. 25-944)

**Disposition: grant, most likely limited to Question 1 (as reformulated by the
Solicitor General).** The docket shows two distributions (for the 5/28/2026
conference, at which the Court called for the views of the Solicitor General on
6/1/2026, and for the 11/6/2026 conference after the SG's brief and both sides'
supplemental briefs). The SG recommends granting Question 1 — whether an issuer
can be liable under Section 11(a)'s misleading-omissions prong for omitting
material interim financial information without a separate finding that the
omission rendered an affirmative statement misleading — and denying Question 2
(Item 303 / required-statements prong).

- **Relists:** I expect at least one further distribution (the Court's practice
  is to relist a petition at least once before granting), so the most likely path
  is relist after 11/6 and a grant in mid/late November 2026 or at an early
  December conference. A grant directly from the 11/6 conference is the main
  alternative.
- **CVSG:** already called (6/1/2026); no further CVSG is possible.
- **Which question:** Question 1, likely in the SG's reformulated wording or a
  close variant; Question 2 left behind, as the SG urged. A grant of both
  questions as written is possible but less likely.
- **Route:** plenary review, not a summary disposition — there is no intervening
  decision to GVR on, and the SG asks for merits review of a contested
  doctrinal question (the Ninth Circuit's departure from the Second Circuit's
  *Stadnick* framing and the First Circuit's "extreme departure" test, and its
  fit with *Omnicare* and *Macquarie*).
- **If granted, likely ground:** whether Section 11(a)'s omissions clause, like
  Rule 10b-5(b) after *Macquarie*, requires that an omission render some
  affirmative statement misleading — with the Court likely to hold that
  materiality alone is insufficient.
- **If denied:** no separate writing is the expected shape; a short statement
  (most plausibly from a Justice skeptical of the interlocutory posture or
  eager to reach the securities question) is a minor possibility.
