## Adjustment
The decisive fact is the SG's grant recommendation, which the committed cuts do not split out. In my general knowledge of practice, the Court grants roughly three-quarters or more of CVSG petitions where the SG recommends a grant. The relist adds to that. The Court routinely relists a petition at least once before granting plenary review, and a relist right after the first post-SG-brief conference fits a grant being prepared, possibly with the QP reformulated as the SG proposed. A circuit conflict, a published decision, the SEC's support, and repeat SCOTUS counsel (Gibson Dunn) all point the same way.

Things that pull me down:
- The Court sometimes declines even when the SG supports a grant.
- The relist could instead be for a per curiam or a dissent from denial.
- The case is interlocutory: a reversal of a dismissal, with a remand for further proceedings.
- Under the SG's own view, the Item 303 claim survives regardless.

I land at 0.84, well above the 0.346 anchor. The main uncertainty is whether the interlocutory posture or a vehicle concern raised in the BIO leads to a denial. I did not weigh the BIO in detail.
