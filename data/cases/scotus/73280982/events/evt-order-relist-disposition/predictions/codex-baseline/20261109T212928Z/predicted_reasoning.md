# Forecast of the Court's action

The Court will most likely grant review limited to the first question presented, in the Solicitor General's proposed formulation or a substantively equivalent version: whether liability under Section 11(a)'s misleading-omissions prong can follow from materiality of omitted interim financial information without an additional inquiry into whether an affirmative statement was misleading. It will most likely decline the separate Item 303 question.

The snapshot records **three distributions**, including the November 9 redistribution for the November 13, 2026 conference. The modal path is a limited grant after that scheduled conference, with **no further distribution**. There is a **42% chance of at least one additional distribution beyond those three**; if that happens, one or two additional distributions are the expected range rather than an extended hold.

The Court has already called for the Solicitor General's views on June 1, and the invited brief is on file. No new CVSG is forecast; the increment claim is vacuous because the invitation predates this prediction.

Conditional on a grant, **96% favors plenary review** rather than resolution in the cert order. The Court will likely seek briefing and argument on the distinct roles of materiality and misleadingness, rather than summarily reverse or remand in light of an intervening decision. A ruling for Robinhood on that question would clarify the standard without necessarily disposing of the separate required-disclosure theory; that consequence is conditional, not an unconditional merits forecast.

If the Court instead denies review, no separate writing is the modal forecast. The probability of any dissent or statement respecting denial, conditional on denial, is **16%**. Thomas or Alito is a more plausible writer than any individual colleague, but neither is predicted to write as a likely event. The expected cert order carries no majority merits opinion.
