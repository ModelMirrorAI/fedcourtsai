## Filings and counterarguments

The questions-presented file distinguishes Section 11(a)'s misleading-omissions prong from its required-statements prong, the latter tied here to Item 303. The petition's introduction and reasons for review, and the cert reply's Part I, argue that the Ninth Circuit made materiality alone sufficient for interim financial information. The BIO, particularly pp. 2–4, 12–24, and 30–32, disputes that characterization, stresses the connection to affirmative disclosures, contests the claimed split, and invokes the interlocutory posture. These are genuine obstacles rather than formalities.

The strongest new substantive evidence is `sg-invited-brief.txt`, pp. 9–19 and 23–24: the United States recommends **granting the first question only**, reformulated to ask whether a finding that interim information is material can suffice without an additional misleadingness inquiry. It regards that question as important, disputed across circuits, and reviewable in this vehicle. It rejects review of the second question as framed; a different problem with the Item 303 analysis was not adequately presented. Its recommendation is neither a GVR nor a hold.

The combined supplemental briefs sharpen the risk. Robinhood accepts the proposed reformulation and continues to seek both questions. Respondents argue that the two inquiries merely converge on these facts, so the reformulation does not create a real controversy. They also stress that the separate Item 303 theory will survive even if Robinhood wins on question one. I discount the grant probability materially for those objections: the case returns to ongoing litigation, and a narrow ruling will not necessarily terminate the suit. Nonetheless, the SG expressly addresses those points and endorses review notwithstanding the surviving theory. That makes a limited grant substantially more likely than either denial or acceptance of both questions.

I checked `decision-below.txt` against the caption, Ninth Circuit number 24-1036, and August 29, 2025 judgment. Its misleading-omissions discussion expressly acknowledges the distinction on which respondents rely, but then describes circumstances in which the inquiries coalesce; its First/Second Circuit comparison makes the disagreement more than a bare allegation in the petition. The provisioned lower-court docket records a published decision and denial of rehearing en banc, without a noted rehearing dissent. That is not a claim that the panel was unanimous: the SG describes a divided panel. The petition and decision-below files are marked truncated in `documents.json`; the QP, BIO, reply, SG brief, and combined supplement are not. My review used selected relevant sections, not an assumption that truncated materials were complete.

## Precedent and remaining claims

The corpus lookup of Macquarie, No. 22-1165, confirmed an earlier plenary grant in a closely related securities-disclosure dispute. The CourtListener search and opinion snippets independently confirmed its treatment of Section 11 in **Macquarie Infrastructure Corp. v. Moab Partners, L.P., 601 U.S. 257, 264–265 (2024)**. It distinguishes Rule 10b-5 from Section 11's separate required-disclosure liability. Accordingly, Macquarie supports attention to the misleading-omissions question but does not automatically eliminate the Item 303 theory or make summary reversal inevitable. The Omnicare corpus row lacked useful disposition and text fields, so it supplied no additional quantitative prior. Briefs' other citations are party/SG representations, not separately verified findings.

The **0.42 further-distribution probability** is an increment beyond the three already observed. A prompt limited grant after the scheduled conference is the largest single path; another one or two redistributions remain plausible while the Court settles the question's scope. It would be misleading to treat the earlier CVSG-related distribution as evidence of several unsuccessful post-SG conferences. The existing CVSG makes `cvsg-increment` vacuous; 0 is the administrative forecast, not a claim that no invitation occurred.

Conditional on any grant, I put only **0.04 on disposition in the cert order**. There is no identified new controlling decision requiring a GVR, and the SG seeks an adversarial resolution of a disputed standard. Conditional on denial, I assign **0.16 to a dissent or statement**; the modal forecast remains no writing. Per-Justice order-writing probabilities are unconditional, low, and not grant votes. They allow a modest concentration in Thomas and Alito without predicting a particular author's participation as likely. The significance score, 0.62, reflects nationwide offering-document and litigation consequences, not the probability of review.
