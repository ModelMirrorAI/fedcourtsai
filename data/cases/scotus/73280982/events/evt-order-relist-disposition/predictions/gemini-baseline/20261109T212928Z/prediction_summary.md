The Supreme Court is moderately likely—at about a three-in-five chance (62%)—to grant Robinhood's petition for certiorari, meaning it will agree to hear the appeal. The case asks whether a company going public can be sued under federal securities law for leaving out mid-quarter financial numbers whenever an investor later claims those numbers were important, even if nothing the company actually said was misleading. After asking the U.S. Solicitor General (the federal government's top Supreme Court lawyer) for advice, the government told the Court that the Ninth Circuit Court of Appeals got this important rule wrong and recommended that the Justices take up that question, and the Court has now held the case over ("relisted" it) for a second conference—a classic sign that a grant is likely.
