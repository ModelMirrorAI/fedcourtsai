## Case-Specific Signal Analysis

### 1. The Solicitor General's Primary Recommendation to Grant Question 1
In `sg-invited-brief.txt`, the Solicitor General and the SEC General Counsel strongly agree with Petitioners on the merits and certworthiness of Question 1:
- **Conflict with *Macquarie* and Statutory Text**: Under Section 11(a) of the Securities Act of 1933, 15 U.S.C. § 77k(a), an omission not required to be stated by an SEC regulation is actionable only if it is "necessary to make the statements therein not misleading." In *Macquarie Infrastructure Corp. v. Moab Partners, L.P.*, 601 U.S. 257, 263–265 (2024), the Court unanimously held that pure omissions are not actionable and that an omission violates the misleading-omissions clause only when it renders an affirmative statement misleading (a half-truth). The Ninth Circuit panel majority, however, held that "where the disclosure duty arises from the combination of a prior statement and a subsequent event, ... the inquiries as to duty and materiality coalesce," and instructed the district court on remand that if Plaintiffs adequately alleged the omitted intra-quarter KPIs and revenue figures were material, "then they have adequately alleged that Robinhood had a duty to disclose the omitted information." (Pet. App. 27a–28a, 32a–33a). As the SG explains, that legal standard erases the statutory misleadingness element.
- **Circuit Split and Market Impact**: The SG confirms that the Ninth Circuit's rule conflicts with the First, Third, Fifth, Tenth, and Eleventh Circuits (and misreads the Second Circuit's decisions in *Time Warner* and *Stadnick*). Moreover, the SG and SEC warn that the Ninth Circuit's approach undermines the SEC's periodic reporting regime (45 days after quarter-end for Form 10-Q under 17 C.F.R. § 249.308a(a)(2) and 135 days under Regulation S-X Rule 3-12) by compelling issuers conducting offerings in the Ninth Circuit—and potentially periodic filers under Rule 10b-5—to disclose unaudited intra-quarter metrics whenever they might later be deemed material.

### 2. The Post-CVSG Relist at the November 6, 2026 Conference
The Solicitor General's brief concluded with a bifurcated recommendation: "The petition for a writ of certiorari should be granted, limited to the first question presented, or, in the alternative, denied." Had the Court been persuaded by Respondents' BIO, Respondents' October 21 supplemental brief, or the SG's alternative vehicle reservation to simply deny review, the standard course at the November 6, 2026 conference would have been to deny certiorari on the November 9, 2026 order list. Instead, the Court relisted the petition for the November 13, 2026 conference. Under modern Roberts Court practice, a first relist immediately following the post-CVSG conference is a strong indicator that at least four Justices are inclined to grant review (and are using the relist week to verify the record and settle whether to grant Question 1 alone or both Questions 1 and 2).

### 3. Clean Threshold Legal Holding vs. *Facebook* / *NVIDIA* Pleading Concerns
My principal uncertainty—and the reason `probability` is `0.62` rather than `0.80+`—stems from the two vehicle reservations noted by the SG and pressed by Respondents (represented at the supplemental stage by Gupta Wessler LLP alongside Scott+Scott):
1. **The *Facebook* / *NVIDIA* Caution**: In OT2024, the Court dismissed *Facebook, Inc. v. Amalgamated Bank*, 604 U.S. 4 (2024), and *NVIDIA Corp. v. E. Ohman J:OR Fonder AB*, 604 U.S. 20 (2024), as improvidently granted after merits briefing and argument bogged down in fact-bound disputes over complaint allegations. Respondents argue that if certiorari is granted, they will defend the Ninth Circuit's judgment on the ground that their complaint actually pleaded concrete half-truths (pointing to Robinhood's preliminary Q2 2021 revenue estimates and risk warnings). However, as Petitioners demonstrate in their supplemental brief, the Ninth Circuit here *expressly declined* to pass on how the facts fared under any legal test and instead announced a pure legal rule for remand—the exact posture in which the Court granted and decided *Macquarie*, 601 U.S. at 262, 266 & n.2, and *Omnicare, Inc. v. Laborers District Council Construction Industry Pension Fund*, 575 U.S. 175, 194–195 (2015).
2. **The Independent Item 303 Remand (Question 2)**: The SG opposed review of Question 2, concluding that the Ninth Circuit's Item 303 holdings (rejecting a rigid two-month durational floor for "trends," requiring consideration of "events" and "uncertainties," and requiring quantification where reasonably practicable) track the regulatory text and create no circuit conflict. If the Court follows the SG and grants only Question 1, the Ninth Circuit's remand on Item 303 will remain undisturbed, so reversing on Question 1 will not by itself terminate the lawsuit. While that interlocutory feature creates roughly a 38% risk that the Court ultimately opts for the SG's alternative denial path, Section 11 claims are litigated and priced statement-by-statement and theory-by-theory, and leaving a published Ninth Circuit rule that openly contradicts *Macquarie* intact is something the Court is unlikely to accept after investing in a CVSG and a post-CVSG relist.
