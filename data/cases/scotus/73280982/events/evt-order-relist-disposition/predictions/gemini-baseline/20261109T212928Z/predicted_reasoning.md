## Scope of Review if Granted

The Court is likely to **grant certiorari limited to the first question presented**—whether Section 11(a) of the Securities Act of 1933, 15 U.S.C. § 77k(a), permits holding an issuer liable for omitting material intra-quarter financial data without showing that the omission rendered an affirmative statement in the registration statement misleading—while declining to review the second question presented concerning Item 303 of Regulation S-K, 17 C.F.R. § 229.303. Narrowing the grant to Question 1 follows the primary recommendation of the Solicitor General and the Securities and Exchange Commission, isolates the clean legal conflict between the Ninth Circuit's "coalescence" rule and *Macquarie Infrastructure Corp. v. Moab Partners, L.P.*, 601 U.S. 257 (2024), and avoids fact-intensive disputes over how the 2020 regulatory amendments to Item 303 apply to Robinhood's specific Q2/Q3 2021 revenue components.

## Plenary Review vs. Summary Disposition (`summary-disposition-route: 0.08`)

Conditional on a grant, the Court will almost certainly set the case for **plenary briefing and oral argument** rather than disposing of it summarily in the certiorari order (`summary-disposition-route: 0.08`). Although the Ninth Circuit's collapse of the misleadingness requirement into materiality is in sharp tension with *Macquarie*, the panel majority cited *Macquarie* in a footnote and purported to synthesize circuit precedent (*Time Warner*, *Stadnick*, and *Shaw*) on how Section 11(a) applies to intra-quarter developments following historic performance highs, and the Solicitor General recommended plenary review on Question 1 rather than summary reversal. A summary vacatur or GVR in light of *Macquarie* is a minor secondary possibility (~8% conditional on a grant).

## Conditional Merits Outlook

If certiorari is granted on Question 1, the Court is likely to vacate the Ninth Circuit's Section 11(a) misleading-omissions holding and remand for application of the statutory half-truth standard. The Court's reasoning will likely track the textual framework of *Omnicare, Inc. v. Laborers District Council Construction Industry Pension Fund*, 575 U.S. 175 (2015), and *Macquarie*, 601 U.S. at 263–265:
- Section 11(a)'s second clause imposes liability only when a registration statement omits a material fact "necessary to make the statements therein not misleading." 15 U.S.C. § 77k(a).
- Materiality and misleadingness are distinct statutory elements; collapsing the duty to disclose into materiality whenever an omission involves events subsequent to a prior reported period impermissibly converts Section 11(a)'s half-truth prohibition into a general duty to disclose all material intra-quarter developments ahead of SEC periodic reporting deadlines.
- The Court will likely leave for remand the question whether any specific affirmative statements in Robinhood's registration statement (such as its preliminary Q2 2021 estimates or descriptions of revenue drivers) were rendered misleading by the alleged omissions.

## Separate Writings on the Disposition Order (`dissent-from-denial: 0.18`)

If the Court grants certiorari, no separate writing will accompany the grant order. Conditional on the Court instead following the Solicitor General's alternative suggestion and **denying** certiorari due to the interlocutory posture and the unreviewed Item 303 remand, there is an estimated **18% probability** (`dissent-from-denial: 0.18`) that one or more Justices—most plausibly Justice Thomas, Justice Gorsuch, Justice Alito, or Justice Kavanaugh—will author a dissent from or statement respecting the denial of certiorari emphasizing that the Ninth Circuit's "coalescence" rule cannot be squared with *Macquarie* and warning lower courts not to equate materiality with a duty to disclose under Section 11(a).
