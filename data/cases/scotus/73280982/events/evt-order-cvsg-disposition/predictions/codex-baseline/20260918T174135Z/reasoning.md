## Prior and adjustment

The matching `sal-v4` high-band **reached** pool is the anchor. Pooling every displayed Term strictly preceding 2025, namely 2017-2024, gives **314 grant-family outcomes / 898 weighted resolved petitions = 34.9666%**. I calculated this from the unrounded `prefix_est_grant_rate` and `prefix_weighted_resolved` fields in `metrics/statpack.json`, rather than averaging rounded percentages. Terms 2025 and 2026 are excluded. The committed pack's latest modifying commit is dated September 14, 2026; that is artifact vintage, not a verified newest corpus pull or snapshot date. No live corpus freshness claim is made.

The paid-segment CVSG cut provides a useful conditioning cross-check: 163 resolved petitions, with 29.4% ordinary grants and 5.5% GVRs, approximately **34.9% any grant**. This pooled cut includes the current Term and is used descriptively, not as a replacement for the strictly-prior-Term anchor. The relist cuts show substantial differences across terminal distribution histories, but those terminal buckets do not estimate a forward hazard from this cell. In particular, treating this as an ordinary once-distributed petition would discard the affirmative CVSG signal. Conversely, adding a large CVSG boost on top of the high-band anchor would double count attention already represented there.

I increase the approximately 35% anchor to **44%**, a judgmental nine-percentage-point adjustment, not a fitted coefficient. The principal positive evidence is the combination of a concrete legal distinction, genuinely broad economic application, and a majority opinion whose own language makes the petition's criticism more than an invented characterization. The appendix expressly adopts the Second Circuit's approach instead of the First Circuit's extreme-departure test and connects the disclosure-duty inquiry with materiality (petition appendix 21a-28a). Petition pages 14-24 and 31-37 explain the claimed doctrinal conflict and compliance consequences. Three docketed amicus filings, including securities-industry participants and a former SEC commissioner, support issue salience, though I did not read their arguments. The June 1 invitation establishes that the Court wants government input; it does not establish what that input will recommend.

Important counterweights keep denial the modal outcome. The opposition argues that the prospectus allegedly selected favorable interim information while withholding adverse information and described risks that had already materialized (BIO pages 1-4). The majority's included discussion likewise identifies several potentially misleading statements and limits its reasoning to this context (appendix 22a-24a). Thus there is a real dispute over whether the question presented accurately captures the holding. The First/Second Circuit divergence is clearer than a showing that Robinhood's preferred formulation would change this case's result; the opposition emphasizes that petitioners do not actually ask the Court to adopt the First Circuit's test. Its Item 303 discussion challenges the existence of a clean conflict over a categorical duration rule. Finally, the court remanded without deciding the ultimate sufficiency of the allegations, so further proceedings could clarify or eliminate the supposed conflict (BIO pages 30-32; petition pages 36-37). Interlocutory posture is a prudential disadvantage, not a jurisdictional bar.

Macquarie Infrastructure Corp. v. Moab Partners, L.P., 601 U.S. 257, 263-265 (2024), independently confirms the distinction underlying the first question: Rule 10b-5(b) addresses misleading half-truths, while Section 11 separately reaches certain required disclosures. The official opinion expressly leaves the half-truth application beyond its narrow holding unresolved. That supports taking the petition seriously but does not establish that this Section 11/Item 303 case is controlled wholesale by Macquarie. My largest unresolved variable is the government's assessment of the regulatory consequences and vehicle; I do not infer an SG recommendation from the invitation.

## Other probabilities and stakes

The 96% further-distribution estimate reflects an expected return to conference after the SG responds, rather than extrapolation from terminal relist buckets. A small residual allows withdrawal or another termination before renewed distribution. The existing CVSG makes its increment claim vacuous; zero is not a forecast that the June 1 invitation did not occur. The conditional summary-route estimate is 12%, allowing a limited summary correction while favoring plenary treatment if the Court takes the case. The conditional 12% denial-writing estimate recognizes the dispute's salience without projecting public individual votes from a CVSG. These auxiliary numbers are judgmental and have no claimed fitted baseline.

The **0.64 stakes score** reflects potentially nationwide effects on IPO disclosure practice, underwriter exposure, and securities litigation. It is not the 44% grant estimate. The issues are important commercial-law questions but technically bounded; I do not adopt either side's broadest claims about the survival of the IPO market.
