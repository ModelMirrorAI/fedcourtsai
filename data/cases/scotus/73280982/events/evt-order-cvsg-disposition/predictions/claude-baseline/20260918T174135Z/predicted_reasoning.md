# Forecast: Robinhood Markets, Inc. v. Sodha, No. 25-944 (CVSG moment)

**Where the docket stands.** Distributed once, for the Conference of May 28, 2026. At that
first conference the Court did not relist; it called for the views of the Solicitor General
on June 1, 2026. No relist has occurred and no SG brief was on the docket at the baseline.

**Further distributions.** The petition will be distributed at least once more, and almost
certainly exactly one more time as a scheduled matter: once the Solicitor General files the
invited brief, the Clerk redistributes the petition for the next available conference, and the
parties may file supplemental briefs responding to the SG. I expect that redistribution rather
than a string of relists. A CVSG issued in June is typically answered in late November or
December so that the petition can be considered at a January conference; a later filing (as
late as May 2027) would push disposition toward the end of OT2026. If the Court is inclined to
grant, I expect it to act at the first conference after the SG brief or, at most, after one
relist.

**Call for the SG's views.** Already on the docket (June 1, 2026); no second CVSG will issue.

**Disposition.** Slightly more likely than not to be granted. The Court's own signal is strong:
it invited the SG at the first conference, without pausing to relist, which is what the Court
does when it already regards the petition as a serious candidate and wants the government's
view before committing. I expect the Solicitor General, on behalf of an SEC that is itself
reviewing Regulation S-K and that has never mandated intra-quarter reporting, to tell the
Court that the Ninth Circuit read Section 11(a)'s misleading-omissions clause and Item 303's
"known trends" language too broadly. The uncertain half is whether the SG then recommends a
grant or says the decision is wrong but the interlocutory, remand posture makes this a poor
vehicle. The Court follows the SG's recommendation in the large majority of CVSG cases, so the
disposition will largely track that brief.

**If granted, which question.** The Court would most likely take both questions essentially as
written, because they describe one holding from two angles: Question 1 (whether a material
omission of interim data is actionable under the misleading-omissions prong without an
inquiry into whether a specific statement was rendered misleading) is the cleaner vehicle
after Macquarie Infrastructure v. Moab Partners, and I expect the Court to grant on it first;
Question 2 (whether Item 303 requires disclosure of interim financial data before the
quarterly reporting deadline) follows because the Item 303 holding is the required-statements
route to the same liability. A grant limited to Question 1 alone is the likelier narrowing if
the SG counsels a narrower grant. The case would be set for briefing and argument, most
plausibly in the OT2026 spring sitting if granted in January.

**Summary disposition.** Unlikely. No intervening decision of this Court bears on the split, so
there is nothing to GVR in light of, and the Court does not summarily reverse published
securities-pleading decisions with a dissent below.

**If denied.** Expect a bare denial. A written dissent from denial is possible but not likely;
if one appears it would come from Justice Thomas, Justice Alito, or Justice Gorsuch, noting
the Ninth Circuit's outsized role in securities litigation and the Court's recent inability
to reach the merits in NVIDIA and Facebook.
