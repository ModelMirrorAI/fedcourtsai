# Forecast: Robinhood Markets, Inc. v. Sodha, No. 25-944 (CVSG moment)

The docket as of the 2026-06-02 snapshot shows **one distribution** (Conference
of 5/28/2026) and a **CVSG entered 6/1/2026** — the Court invited the Solicitor
General's views at its first look at the petition.

**Disposition.** More likely granted than not among the plausible worlds where
the Court acts, but the single most likely single outcome across all worlds is
still close to a coin flip: I put **P(any grant) at 0.45** and name `granted`
as the modal disposition. The petition presents two clean, recurrent questions
on Section 11(a) omissions liability for interim financial data — the
misleading-omissions prong (with claimed tension against *Macquarie
Infrastructure v. Moab Partners*) and Item 303's reach — an area the Court has
tried and failed to settle before (*Leidos* was granted on the Item 303 split
and evaporated by settlement). The business-side mobilization (SIFMA and the
Chamber, the Washington Legal Foundation, and Joseph Grundfest as amici at the
petition stage), a former acting Solicitor General as counsel of record, and a
dissent below all mark this as a serious candidate. The main paths to denial:
the SG recommends denial on the interlocutory-posture objection the BIO presses
(the Ninth Circuit reversed a dismissal and remanded — no final judgment, no
developed record), or the securities class action settles before the SG files,
mooting the petition (a real hazard in this class of case, and exactly how
*Leidos* died).

**Relist / further distributions.** Effectively certain: the mechanics of a
CVSG guarantee at least one more distribution once the SG's brief arrives.
From the one distribution shown, I forecast **the petition is distributed
again with probability 0.96** — the residual is settlement/dismissal before
the SG files. Expect two to three further distributions in total (one when the
SG brief lands, plausibly a relist after).

**CVSG.** Already on the docket (6/1/2026) — nothing further to forecast; the
increment claim is vacuous for this cell.

**Timing.** The SG's brief will likely arrive between December 2026 and May
2027 (CVSG responses off the Term's rhythm typically land near the winter
holidays or in the May "grant window"); disposition of the petition most
likely falls in OT2026, i.e., between January and June 2027.

**Which question, if taken.** Most likely both QPs as written — they travel
together, and QP1 (misleading-omissions/materiality-only) is the cleaner
split-plus-*Macquarie* hook; if the Court trims, it takes QP1 alone and leaves
the Item 303 question behind, as Item 303's "known trends" inquiry is the more
factbound half.

**Summary disposition.** Unlikely: **P(cert-order disposition | grant) =
0.08**. No intervening decision of this Court bears on either QP, so there is
nothing to GVR in light of; a grant here means plenary briefing and argument.

**Dissent from denial.** If the Court denies — most plausibly following an SG
recommendation to deny on vehicle grounds — I expect it to deny quietly:
**P(some noted dissent or statement respecting denial | denial) = 0.10**.
Business-law denials rarely draw separate writings, though a short statement
noting the issue's importance (in the vein of a Justice flagging a recurring
securities question for a better vehicle) is conceivable.

**If granted** (conditional context, not an unconditional claim): the likely
ground of decision would be that Section 11(a)'s misleading-omissions clause
requires that an omission render an affirmative statement misleading — not
materiality alone — reversing or vacating the Ninth Circuit.
