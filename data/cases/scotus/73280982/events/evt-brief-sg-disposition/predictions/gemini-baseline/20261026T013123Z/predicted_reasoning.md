# Predicted Court Disposition and Procedural Trajectory

## Disposition and Questions Presented

The Court will **grant** the petition for a writ of certiorari limited to the First Question Presented, adopting the Solicitor General's proposed reformulation (or substantially similar language):

> Whether an issuer can be found liable under the misleading-omissions prong of Section 11(a) of the Securities Act of 1933, 15 U.S.C. 77a *et seq.*, based on a determination that the issuer failed to disclose interim financial information that is material within the meaning of Section 11(a), without an additional inquiry to determine whether the omission rendered any affirmative statement misleading.

## Summary Disposition vs. Plenary Review

Conditional on a grant, the Court will set the case for full merits briefing and oral argument rather than disposing of it summarily in the cert order (`summary-disposition-route` = 0.02). No intervening Supreme Court precedent postdates the Ninth Circuit's August 2025 decision (*Macquarie Infrastructure Corp. v. Moab Partners, L.P.*, 601 U.S. 257 (2024), was already decided and discussed below), and the Solicitor General recommends plenary review on a reformulated question rather than summary vacatur or reversal.

If granted, on the merits the Court is likely to vacate or reverse the Ninth Circuit's judgment on the Section 11(a) misleading-omissions claim, holding—consistent with *Omnicare, Inc. v. Laborers District Council Construction Industry Pension Fund*, 575 U.S. 175 (2015), and *Macquarie*, 601 U.S. 257 (2024)—that materiality and misleadingness are two separate statutory elements under 15 U.S.C. 77k(a), and that omitting material intra-quarter financial data does not violate the misleading-omissions prong unless the omission renders specific affirmative statements in the registration statement misleading when read in light of surrounding context and disclaimers.
