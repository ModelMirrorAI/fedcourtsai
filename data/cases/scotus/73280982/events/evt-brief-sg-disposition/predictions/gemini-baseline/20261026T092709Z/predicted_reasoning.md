# Predicted Reasoning: *Robinhood Markets, Inc., et al. v. Vinod Sodha, et al.* (No. 25-944)

## Disposition and Procedural Path

The Court will grant the petition for a writ of certiorari, limited to a reformulated version of the first question presented concerning Section 11(a)'s misleading-omissions prong, and set the case for plenary briefing and oral argument.

- **Distributions and Further Relists (`relist-increment`):** The snapshot as of October 7, 2026, records **one** distribution so far (`DISTRIBUTED for Conference of 5/28/2026`), which resulted in the June 1, 2026 invitation to the Solicitor General. Now that the Solicitor General's invited brief has been filed on October 7, 2026, the petition will be distributed at least once more for an upcoming conference after the supplemental briefing window closes, and likely distributed once or twice in total (an increment of one to two additional distributions beyond the single pre-CVSG distribution) as the Court acts on the Solicitor General's recommendation and frames the reformulated question presented.
- **CVSG (`cvsg-increment`):** The Court already called for the views of the Solicitor General on June 1, 2026, and the United States filed its invited brief on October 7, 2026. No further CVSG will occur.
- **Scope of Grant and Question Presented:** The Court will follow the Solicitor General's recommendation to grant review limited to Question 1, reformulated along the lines proposed by the United States: whether an issuer can be found liable under the misleading-omissions prong of Section 11(a) of the Securities Act of 1933, 15 U.S.C. 77k(a), based on a determination that the issuer failed to disclose interim financial information that is material within the meaning of Section 11(a), without an additional inquiry to determine whether the omission rendered any affirmative statement misleading. The Court will deny review of Question 2 concerning Item 303 of Regulation S-K, both because the lower-court disagreement over Item 303's "trend" and quantification standards is less cleanly presented and because the SEC is actively reviewing Regulation S-K on its regulatory agenda.
- **Summary Disposition Route (`summary-disposition-route`):** Conditional on a grant, the Court is overwhelmingly likely to set the case for plenary merits briefing and oral argument rather than disposing of it summarily in the cert order via a GVR or summary reversal. There is no intervening Supreme Court decision post-dating the Ninth Circuit's August 2025 opinion that would trigger a GVR (*Macquarie Infrastructure Corp. v. Moab Partners, L.P.*, 601 U.S. 257 (2024), was already decided and discussed below), and the Solicitor General urges plenary review on a reformulated question rather than summary action.
- **Separate Writings on the Cert Order (`dissent-from-denial` / `order-writers`):** An opinion accompanying the cert order is unlikely. If the Court grants review on Question 1 as expected, the order will issue without separate writing. In the unlikely event the Court denies the petition over the Solicitor General's recommendation (potentially out of concern over the interlocutory posture or memories of the *Facebook* and *NVIDIA* DIGs), there is a modest chance (~8%) that one of the Justices attentive to securities litigation and statutory text (such as Justice Thomas, Justice Alito, or Justice Gorsuch) would note a dissent from denial or a statement respecting denial.

## Conditional Merits Outlook

If granted on the first question presented, the Court is likely to vacate or reverse the Ninth Circuit's judgment. Building on *Omnicare, Inc. v. Laborers District Council Construction Industry Pension Fund*, 575 U.S. 175 (2015), and *Macquarie Infrastructure Corp. v. Moab Partners, L.P.*, 601 U.S. 257 (2024), the Court is likely to hold that Section 11(a)'s misleading-omissions clause ("omitted to state a material fact ... necessary to make the statements therein not misleading") requires two distinct inquiries—materiality of the omitted fact and whether its omission rendered an affirmative statement in the registration statement misleading in context—and that a court of appeals errs by collapsing the duty-to-disclose inquiry into materiality alone for interim financial data.
