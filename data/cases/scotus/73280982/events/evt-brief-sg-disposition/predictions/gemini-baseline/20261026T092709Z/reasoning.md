## Case-Specific Adjustments (`probability = 0.78`)

Starting from the `high`-band / unconditional CVSG baseline (~35%–41%), the decisive update at `evt-brief-sg-disposition` is the content of `sg-invited-brief.txt`, filed October 7, 2026 by the Solicitor General and joined by the General Counsel and Solicitor of the SEC:

### Strong Upward Factors
1. **Explicit SG + SEC Recommendation to Grant Question 1:** The United States unambiguously recommends that the petition be granted on the first question presented (reformulated to highlight whether Section 11(a)'s misleading-omissions prong permits a finding of liability based on materiality of omitted interim financial data without an additional inquiry into whether the omission rendered any affirmative statement misleading). Having the SEC sign on alongside DOJ in a major securities case removes any doubt about the federal regulatory interest.
2. **Clean Legal Framing and Alignment with Recent Precedent (*Macquarie* and *Omnicare*):** The Ninth Circuit explicitly stated that where omissions involve the relationship between a prior statement concerning a particular time period and a subsequent event, "the inquiries as to duty and materiality coalesce," and that "by collapsing the duty to disclose into materiality," it was rejecting the First Circuit's *Shaw* test and instructing the district court to determine only whether the omitted information was material. Both Petitioners and the Solicitor General cogently show that this rule is in direct tension with *Omnicare* (575 U.S. at 189–190) and *Macquarie* (601 U.S. at 264) and conflicts with decisions of the First, Third, Tenth, and Eleventh Circuits.
3. **Top-Tier Supreme Court Advocacy and Amicus Support:** Petitioners are represented by Jeffrey B. Wall (Sullivan & Cromwell on the petition; Gibson Dunn on the reply) alongside Cravath and Orrick, and are supported at the cert stage by three prominent amici (former SEC Commissioner Joseph A. Grundfest, SIFMA/U.S. Chamber of Commerce, and Washington Legal Foundation).
4. **SG's Proposed Reformulation Neutralizes Respondents' Primary Vehicle Objection:** Respondents argued in the BIO that everyone agrees the answer to Petitioners' Question 1 as literally framed is "no," and that the dispute is merely factbound. The SG directly addresses this objection by proposing a crisp reformulation ("without an additional inquiry to determine whether the omission rendered any affirmative statement misleading") that isolates the pure legal issue on which the Ninth Circuit's remand order turned.

### Moderating / Downward Factors (Why 0.78 Rather Than 0.90+)
1. **Recent Securities-Case DIGs (*Facebook* and *NVIDIA*):** In OT2024, the Court dismissed two Ninth Circuit pleading-stage securities cases as improvidently granted (*Facebook, Inc. v. Amalgamated Bank*, 604 U.S. 4 (2024), and *NVIDIA Corp. v. E. Ohman J:or Fonder AB*, 604 U.S. 20 (2024)). Respondents lean heavily on those DIGs to warn the Justices that this case could similarly turn into a factbound dispute over Robinhood's specific Q1/Q2 2021 disclosures once briefed on the merits. While the SG explains why the Ninth Circuit's opinion here turned on a threshold legal standard (just as in *Macquarie*), some Justices may remain cautious about taking another interlocutory Ninth Circuit securities-pleading case.
2. **Split Recommendation (Grant QP 1 / Deny QP 2) and Remaining Item 303 Claim:** Because the SG recommends denying review on Question 2 (Item 303), respondents' Section 11(a) required-statements claim premised on Item 303 would remain live on remand regardless of the outcome on Question 1 (though the SG notes at pp. 22–23 that the Ninth Circuit also erred in assuming an Item 303 violation automatically satisfies Section 11(a) materiality). The SG explicitly explains why this does not detract from the importance or suitability of Question 1 (p. 23), but it is a modest vehicle wrinkle compared to a case where a single legal question disposes of the entire complaint.

Weighing the strong positive signal of an SG/SEC grant recommendation on a clean statutory question against the modest headwinds from recent securities DIGs and the split QP recommendation yields **`probability = 0.78`** (`granted = 1`, `predicted_disposition = "granted"`).
