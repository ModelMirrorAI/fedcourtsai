# Forecast of the Court's action

The Court will grant certiorari limited to Question 1, reformulated along the lines proposed by the Solicitor General, and set the case for plenary briefing and argument. It will decline to add the broader Item 303 question. This is a grant in part as to the questions presented, and counts as a grant on the binary disposition axis.

The provisioned docket shows one conference distribution, followed by the June 1 CVSG and the October 7 invited brief. Expect one further distribution to put the now-briefed petition before the Court, with zero or one additional true relist after that conference. Another distribution is substantially more likely than a genuine repeated-consideration relist. No new CVSG is expected; the Court already has the views it requested.

The question taken will ask whether a plaintiff alleging misleading omissions under Section 11(a) can establish liability from omitted interim financial information's materiality without an additional inquiry into whether an affirmative statement was made misleading. The added word and concept of an additional inquiry will preserve the actual disagreement between the parties rather than ask an abstract question both answer identically.

Plenary review is more likely than either a GVR or summary reversal, conditional on a grant. The dispute calls for clarifying a statutory liability standard, not mechanically applying a newly intervening decision. If granted, the likely legal ground will distinguish materiality from misleadingness while preserving the separate required-statements prong; that is a conditional merits expectation, not a forecast of a merits opinion accompanying the cert order. Item 303 issues and whether the actual disclosures were misleading can remain for further proceedings.

If review is denied instead, expect an unsigned denial without a dissent or statement respecting denial. No particular Justice's separate writing is the modal forecast. If there is a writing, Thomas or Gorsuch is a somewhat more plausible candidate than most colleagues for discussing the asserted statutory split, but all individual writing probabilities remain low.
