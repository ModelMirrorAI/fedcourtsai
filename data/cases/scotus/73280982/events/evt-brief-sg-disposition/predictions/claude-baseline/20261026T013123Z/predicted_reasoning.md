# Predicted course — Robinhood Markets, Inc. v. Sodha (No. 25-944)

**State forecast from:** one distribution (conference of May 28, 2026), CVSG
issued June 1, 2026, and the Solicitor General's invited brief filed October 7,
2026. No relist beyond that first distribution appears on the record.

**Disposition: certiorari granted, limited to Question 1 (likely as
reformulated by the United States).** The Solicitor General recommends a grant
on the first question (the Section 11(a) misleading-omissions prong — whether
materiality of omitted interim financial data suffices without a separate
inquiry into whether the omission made an affirmative statement misleading) and
a denial on the second (Item 303 / required-statements prong). I expect the
Court to follow that split: a grant limited to Question 1, with the Court either
adopting the government's reformulated wording or directing the parties to brief
it. A grant of both questions is unlikely; a full denial is the main
alternative.

**Further distribution:** the petition will be distributed again now that the
SG's brief is in — most likely for a November or early-December 2026
conference after the parties' supplemental briefs. A grant may come after one
or two relists rather than at the first post-CVSG conference, but I expect
disposition before the end of the calendar year, putting a granted case on the
OT2026 argument calendar for spring 2027.

**CVSG:** already on the docket; nothing further to forecast.

**Summary disposition:** not expected. There is no intervening decision to GVR
in light of, and the SG asks for plenary review, not a summary reversal.

**If denied:** a separate writing is unlikely but possible — a statement or
dissent from a Justice who sees the Ninth Circuit's materiality/misleadingness
merger as squarely wrong (Thomas, Alito or Gorsuch the likeliest), possibly
noting the interlocutory posture as the reason to wait.

**If granted (conditional):** the Court would take the reformulated Question 1
and likely hold, along the lines of Omnicare and Macquarie, that materiality
and misleading effect are distinct elements of a Section 11(a) omissions claim,
vacating and remanding.
