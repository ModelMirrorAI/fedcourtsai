## In brief

Robinhood asks the Court to review a Ninth Circuit ruling that revived shareholder claims over its 2021 IPO disclosures.

## What happened

Investors sued Robinhood and its IPO underwriters, claiming the company's offering documents misleadingly omitted interim second-quarter 2021 financial data showing a business slowdown, in violation of securities-law disclosure rules. A district court dismissed the suit, applying a rule that such data need only be disclosed if it showed an "extraordinary" departure from historical results. The Ninth Circuit reversed, holding that ordinary materiality standards govern instead, and sent the case back for further review. Robinhood asked the Supreme Court to hear the case, and the respondent shareholders oppose review.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked whether a company can be held liable for leaving out material interim financial data without a separate finding that the omission made some specific statement in its offering documents misleading, and whether a regulation requiring disclosure of known business trends and uncertainties required the company to disclose and quantify that same interim data before its regular reporting deadline.

## Where it stands

The petition has been fully briefed, including an invited brief from the Solicitor General recommending the Court grant review of a reformulated version of the first question while denying the second, and both sides have filed supplemental briefs responding to that recommendation. The docket does not yet show the Court's decision on the petition.

## What each outcome would mean

Robinhood, the company that lost before the Ninth Circuit, is asking the Court to take up the case and reverse. If the Court grants review and later rules for Robinhood, the stricter disclosure standard would apply and could make it harder for the shareholders to pursue their claims. If the Court grants review and rules for the shareholders, the Ninth Circuit's materiality-based standard would stand confirmed. If the Court denies review, the Ninth Circuit's ruling remains in place and the shareholders' case returns to the district court to proceed under that standard.
