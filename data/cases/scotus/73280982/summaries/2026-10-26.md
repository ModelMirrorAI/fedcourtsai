## In brief

Robinhood asks the Court to review a Ninth Circuit ruling that revived shareholder claims over disclosures in Robinhood's 2021 IPO documents.

## What happened

Investors sued Robinhood and its IPO underwriters, claiming the company's offering documents misled them by omitting interim second-quarter 2021 financial data showing a business slowdown after early-2021 trading spikes tied to "meme stocks" and Dogecoin. The district court dismissed the claims, applying a standard requiring the omitted data to reflect an "extraordinary" departure from historical results. The Ninth Circuit reversed in part, holding that the district court used the wrong legal standard for evaluating both a securities-law provision on misleading omissions and a related disclosure regulation, and sent the case back for further review. Robinhood then asked the Supreme Court to take up the case.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked two questions. First, whether a company can be held liable for omitting interim financial data simply because the information was significant to investors, without separately asking whether leaving it out made some other statement the company made misleading. Second, whether a disclosure regulation requires companies to disclose and measure interim financial trends before their regular quarterly reporting deadlines.

## Where it stands

The petition has been fully briefed, including amicus filings and a reply, and the Court asked the Solicitor General for the federal government's views. The government's brief, filed in October 2026, recommended that the Court grant review of the first question (reformulated) but not the second; supplemental briefs from both sides followed, and the case was then distributed for a conference in November 2026. The docket shows no decision yet on whether the Court will hear the case.

## What each outcome would mean

Robinhood and the other petitioners are asking the Court to take up the case and reverse the Ninth Circuit's decision. If the Court denies review, the Ninth Circuit's ruling stands, and the investors' lawsuit continues in the lower courts under the standard that court set. If the Court grants review, it would decide the legal question itself; a ruling for Robinhood would make the omission claims harder to sustain, while a ruling for the investors, Sodha and others, would leave the Ninth Circuit's approach in place. The government has suggested the Court could grant review on a reworded version of the first question while leaving the second question undecided.
