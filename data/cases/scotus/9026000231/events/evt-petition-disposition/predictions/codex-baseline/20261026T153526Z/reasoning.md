## Anchor and adjustments

The appropriate committed anchor is the sal-v4 **baseline reached** rate for private petitioners, not the terminal baseline rate, the federal-petitioner column, or the whole docket's mix. Delaine is the petitioner; having the United States as respondent does not make him a federal petitioner. I pooled every rendered Term strictly before 2026: 2017–2025. Using metrics/statpack.json's baseline `prefix_weighted_resolved` and `prefix_est_grant_rate`, the denominator is 12,912 denial-reweighted resolved equivalents and the estimated grant-family numerator is 659, yielding **5.1038%**. These are the committed statpack's population estimates, not a fresh corpus query or a claim about today's corpus. The own-Term 2026 row is excluded. The numerator includes GVRs as required by the any-grant target.

I adjust substantially upward to **30%** for three case-specific reasons. First, a response request after a federal waiver shows an affirmative interest that the frozen baseline band does not capture. Second, the petition presents a recurring, outcome-determinative federal statutory question, with claimed disagreement across several circuits rather than a request to reconsider an individual factual finding. Third, the petition identifies later published Ninth Circuit authority, Lopez, alongside en banc Gomez and the Tenth Circuit's Sjodin, and a recent Eleventh Circuit decision, Sheely, taking the opposing approach. These descriptions come from the provisioned petition, pp. 11–17, not independent retrieval of those decisions. The NACDL amicus and experienced counsel reinforce the presentation quality, but neither independently establishes a certworthy conflict.

The upward adjustment is restrained because the split is not as clean as the petition's formulation suggests. The Sixth Circuit's actual reasoning distinguishes intent to apply force from intent to cause injury; it reads Gomez's California offense as lacking even intentional or knowing application of force, whereas Florida felony battery requires intentional touching or striking plus serious injury. Its closest expressly contrary example, Vega, is unpublished (appendix pp. 19a–25a). The petition's discussion of Lopez strengthens the conflict argument, but even that discussion acknowledges the Ninth Circuit's reluctance to resolve the full intent-to-harm debate. Different state-law elements and the distinction between intending violent force and intending a particular injury leave room for the Court to regard the conflict as less mature or less square than advertised. Borden's fractured rationale also gives the government a substantive answer rather than making correction automatic.

The vehicle itself is reasonably strong. The issue was raised below and resolved, the plea preserved the ACCA challenge, and the enhancement depends on three predicates. Although the panel upheld the other two, eliminating felony battery would still remove the necessary third predicate; those rulings are not an independent basis to preserve the enhancement. The imposed 188 months also exceeds the panel's stated unenhanced 180-month statutory maximum, so the record does not establish harmlessness on a same-sentence theory (appendix pp. 4a–5a, 19a–25a, 84a–85a). The separate debate about the timing of Florida aggravated-assault interpretations is not the petition's question and is not a vehicle obstacle to reaching the battery issue. Rehearing denial adds no dissenting signal. Denial remains more likely than grant because a substantial legal issue, a response request, and strong representation still do not establish that four Justices will take this particular vehicle.

## Other probabilities and stakes

The statpack's paid-segment terminal relist buckets show grant-family shares of about 1.8%, 13.7%, 41.2%, and 37.4% for zero, one, two, and at least three relists. The no-CVSG and CVSG buckets show approximately 6.4% and 34.8% grant-family shares, respectively. These pooled terminal-state cuts are shape checks, **not transition hazards**, and I do not use them as probabilities of this petition's next distribution or future CVSG. In particular, the 97% additional-distribution estimate rests on the outstanding requested response after the sole recorded distribution; it does not elevate the case to a frozen higher band or imply a 97% chance of substantive reconsideration. A normal federal response request is not a CVSG.

The 8% summary-route estimate is conditional on a grant and reflects the absence of an identified intervening controlling decision, while retaining a small allowance for a later GVR, concession, or summary correction. The 10% denial-writing estimate is conditional on denial; most of its mass is on disagreement over applying Borden, not a prediction that the Court will explain an ordinary denial. Neither these probabilities nor the per-Justice writing probabilities are claimed as measured rates for a matched empirical cohort. I omit cert votes because the record does not support confident individual lineups.

The stakes score of 0.61 reflects a recurring nationwide sentencing rule with large consequences for prison terms and related elements clauses. It is not the probability of review. The principal uncertainty is whether the government's response exposes meaningful differences among the state offenses or instead concedes a genuinely square conflict. That missing advocacy is why I stop well below an even-money grant forecast despite the favorable presentation and response request.
