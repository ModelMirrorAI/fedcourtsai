# Predicted course — Delaine v. United States, No. 26-231

**State forecast from.** The docket shows one distribution (for the 9/28/2026
conference), made after the United States waived its response. The Court then
called for a response on Sep 4, so the petition left that conference
undecided. The response is now due Nov 4, 2026, after a 30-day extension. No
CVSG is possible: the United States is the respondent.

**Further distribution.** The petition will almost certainly be distributed
again once the government's brief in opposition and any reply are in, most
likely for a conference in late November or December 2026. I expect a
decision at that conference or after one or two relists. A relist after the
BIO would itself be a real grant signal.

**CVSG.** None. The Solicitor General is already counsel for the respondent.

**Disposition.** Denial is more likely than not, but a plenary grant is a live
possibility, well above the docket's ordinary rate. The circuits are
acknowledged to be split since *Borden*: the Ninth (en banc *Gomez*, *Vega*,
*Lopez*) and Tenth (*Sjodin*) against the Sixth (below), Seventh and
Eleventh. The split is outcome-determinative here: the Florida felony battery
predicate is needed for the third ACCA strike. Expect the government to oppose.
It will probably concede some tension in the circuits but argue three things:
the Ninth and Tenth Circuit decisions concern different statutes (California
assault, Idaho battery) rather than Florida's; any shallow conflict could
resolve itself; and the decision below is right under *Delligatti*'s
"force that causes injury is violent force" reasoning.

**If granted.** The Court would take the QP as written: whether an offense
requiring an intentional act but no mens rea as to the resulting injury is an
ACCA "violent felony". It would grant for plenary review and argument, not by
summary disposition. No intervening decision supports a GVR.

**If denied.** Most likely without separate writing. A statement respecting
denial is possible from Justice Jackson, Justice Sotomayor or Justice Gorsuch,
who have written on ACCA's harshness and its lenity questions.
