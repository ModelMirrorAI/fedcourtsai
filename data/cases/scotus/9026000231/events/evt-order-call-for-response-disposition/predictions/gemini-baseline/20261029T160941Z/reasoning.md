## 3. Case-Specific Adjustments
Starting from a post-CFR conditional prior of ~12%–15%, I evaluate the upward and downward factors in the record:

### Upward Factors
1. **Top Supreme Court Counsel and Amicus Support:** Although Delaine proceeded CJA/Federal Defender below, the certiorari petition is filed by Lisa S. Blatt of Williams & Connolly LLP—one of the most successful Supreme Court advocates in the country—and the corpus record (`scotus/9026000231`) indicates amicus participation by the National Association of Criminal Defense Lawyers (NACDL).
2. **Published Opinion Addressing Two Recurring ACCA Doctrinal Questions:** Judge Murphy's published opinion for the Sixth Circuit (174 F.4th 474) thoroughly engages both questions presented:
   - **QP 1:** Whether an offense that requires an intentional act of physical contact, but no mens rea as to the resulting physical pain or injury (Fla. Stat. § 784.041(1) felony battery), satisfies ACCA's elements clause after *Borden v. United States*, 593 U.S. 420 (2021). Judge Murphy explicitly notes the unresolved *Marks* question left open in *Delligatti v. United States*, 604 U.S. 423, 434 n.5 (2025), between Justice Kagan's four-Justice plurality and Justice Thomas's concurrence.
   - **QP 2:** Whether a sentencing court applying the categorical approach must evaluate state law as it stood at the time of the state conviction (*McNeill* / *Brown*) when a subsequent state supreme court decision (*Somers v. United States*, 355 So. 3d 887 (Fla. 2022)) clarifies/departs from prior intermediate appellate decisions (*United States v. Anderson*, 99 F.4th 1106 (7th Cir. 2024) vs. *Somers*, 66 F.4th 890 (11th Cir. 2023) and the Sixth Circuit here).
3. **Outcome-Determinative Stakes for Petitioner:** Delaine's ACCA enhancement rested on exactly three predicates (2009 Florida felony battery, 2012 Florida aggravated assault, and 2019 Ohio domestic violence). Because ACCA requires three qualifying violent felonies, invalidating *either* the 2009 Florida felony battery (QP 1) *or* the 2012 Florida aggravated assault (QP 2) would eliminate his 15-year mandatory minimum.

### Downward Factors
1. **Weak / Illusory Split on Question 1:** While Petitioner frames a conflict between the Sixth, Seventh, and Eleventh Circuits (*Delaine*, *Douglas*, *Vail-Bailon*) and the Ninth Circuit (*United States v. Lopez*, 2026 WL 2045622 (9th Cir. July 17, 2026); *United States v. Gomez*, 165 F.4th 1199 (9th Cir. 2026) (en banc)), Judge Murphy persuasively explains (App. 20a–25a) why those decisions do not actually conflict on a statute like Florida felony battery. In *Gomez* (and *Lopez*, which followed *Gomez* on Cal. Penal Code § 245(a)(1)), California assault with a deadly weapon does not require an intentional or knowing application of physical force to another person at all (it covers an intentional act knowing of facts that would lead a reasonable person to realize battery will directly and probably result). By contrast, Florida felony battery under Fla. Stat. § 784.041(1) requires that the defendant "[a]ctually and intentionally touch[] or strike[] another person against the will of the other," which directly satisfies *Borden*'s requirement that the defendant consciously direct force at the person of another, and satisfies *Johnson* / *Delligatti* because it actually causes great bodily harm (`Delligatti`, 604 U.S. at 432: "any force that actually causes injury" qualifies as violent force). Moreover, the Court already denied the government's or defendant's petition in *Gomez* (No. 25-7193) on May 18, 2026, and denied certiorari in *Somers* (No. 23-5828) on the Eleventh Circuit's identical holding.
2. **Independent State-Law Alternative Holding on Question 2:** On QP 2, the Sixth Circuit expressly held that "[u]ltimately, though, we need not enter this debate to decide this case. Even if we must ignore changes in judicial interpretation that postdate a defendant's prior conviction, the Florida Supreme Court did not change Florida law in *Somers*." (App. 14a). Judge Murphy detailed at length (App. 14a–18a) why all five Florida Districts required intentional threats prior to 2012 and why the Seventh Circuit in *Anderson* misread two Fifth DCA cases (*Kelly* and *LaValley*) that were superseded by later Fifth DCA decisions (*Dobosh*, *Denard*, *Cambell*) before Delaine's 2012 conviction. The Solicitor General's BIO will highlight this alternative holding as a textbook vehicle barrier on QP 2, and note that this Court already denied certiorari on the *Anderson*/*Somers* Florida aggravated assault split in *Sheely v. United States* (and *Somers*).

### Synthesis (`probability = 0.14`)
Balancing the strong advocate signal (Lisa Blatt), the CFR after waiver, and the clean statutory framing of QP 1 against the fact that every circuit to consider Florida felony battery (or an equivalent intentional-touching + resulting-injury statute) has held it satisfies ACCA's elements clause, and the alternative holding blocking QP 2, I set `probability` (`disposition`) at **0.14** (`granted = 0`, `predicted_disposition = "denied"`).
