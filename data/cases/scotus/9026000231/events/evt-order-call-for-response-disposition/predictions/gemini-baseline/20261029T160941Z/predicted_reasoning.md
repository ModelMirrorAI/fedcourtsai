## Disposition and Route Forecast
The Court will most likely **deny** the petition for a writ of certiorari (`predicted_disposition: denied`, `probability: 0.14`).

1. **Question Presented 1 (Mens Rea as to Resulting Injury under ACCA's Elements Clause):** If the Court were to grant certiorari, it would likely limit review to Question 1—whether an offense that requires an intentional act of physical contact, but no mens rea as to the resulting physical pain or injury (here, Florida felony battery under Fla. Stat. § 784.041(1)), qualifies as a "violent felony" under 18 U.S.C. § 924(e)(2)(B)(i)—or grant both questions as written. However, plenary review is unlikely because the Sixth Circuit's holding aligns with the Eleventh and Seventh Circuits (*Vail-Bailon*, *Douglas*) and the Court recently denied certiorari in *United States v. Gomez*, No. 25-7193 (May 18, 2026). The alleged split with the Ninth Circuit (*United States v. Lopez*, 2026 WL 2045622, and *Gomez*) turns on state-specific california assault statutes (where the actor need not intend or know that physical contact will occur at all, such as driving near someone), whereas Florida felony battery requires an actual and intentional touching or striking of another person against their will.
2. **Question Presented 2 (Retrospective vs. Clarifying State Judicial Decisions under *McNeill* and *Brown*):** The Court is very unlikely to grant Question 2 in this vehicle because the Sixth Circuit explicitly rested on an independent, state-law alternative ground (App. 14a–18a): even assuming sentencing courts must ignore post-conviction judicial changes in state law, the Florida Supreme Court's decision in *Somers v. United States*, 355 So. 3d 887 (Fla. 2022), did not change Florida law, and as of 2012 there was no "realistic probability" that Florida courts applied aggravated assault to reckless threats. Furthermore, if Delaine loses on either Question 1 or Question 2, or wait-and-see vehicle issues arise because he must prevail on **both** Questions 1 and 2 to reduce his ACCA predicate count below three (having dropped his challenge to his third predicate, 2019 Ohio domestic violence, in the Supreme Court), the vehicle is encumbered: reversing on only one question leaves him with two valid predicates if the other stands, wait—under ACCA (18 U.S.C. § 924(e)(1)), three predicates are required! Thus, knocking out *either* the 2009 Florida felony battery (QP 1) *or* the 2012 Florida aggravated assault (QP 2) would reduce his qualifying predicates from three to two and invalidate his ACCA enhancement. Even so, the Court rarely grants two distinct questions in a single petition when Question 2 has a factbound state-law alternative holding and Question 1 lacks a square conflict on an intentional-touching statute.

## Summary Disposition and Separate Writings
Conditional on a grant, the Court would almost certainly set the case for plenary briefing and oral argument rather than disposing of it summarily in the cert order (`summary-disposition-route` = 0.05), as no pending Supreme Court merits case currently controls either question to trigger a GVR, and summary reversal on a contested ACCA elements-clause question is virtually unheard of.

Conditional on a denial, there is a modest probability (`dissent-from-denial` = 0.08) that a Justice—most plausibly Justice Thomas, Justice Gorsuch, Justice Sotomayor, or Justice Jackson—authors a brief statement respecting the denial of certiorari or a dissent from denial highlighting either the unresolved *Marks* status of *Borden v. United States*, 593 U.S. 420 (2021), or the tension between the Sixth/Eleventh Circuits and the Fourth/Fifth/Seventh/Eighth Circuits over whether *McNeill* and *Brown* apply to intervening state judicial decisions.
