# Predicted course — Delaine v. United States, No. 26-231

**Disposition: denied (P(grant) ≈ 0.20).** After the Solicitor General files the
response the Court requested on September 4, 2026 (due October 5), I expect the
Court to deny the petition at a fall conference, most likely in November or
December 2026. A grant is a real possibility (about one in five).

**Distributions.** The docket shows one distribution, for the conference of
September 28, 2026. The response request took the petition off that conference.
Once the brief in opposition and any reply are in, it will almost certainly be
distributed again. I expect one further distribution and a decision at that
conference. If the petition is relisted one or more times after that, the chance
of a grant rises sharply.

**CVSG.** None. The United States is the respondent, so the Court will not ask
for the Solicitor General's views.

**What the government's response will likely do.** I am not inferring the
response's content, only the usual pattern. The government argued for the
Sixth Circuit's rule below and conceded below that the circuits disagree. It
will probably acknowledge some tension but argue that the split is shallow or
still developing (the Ninth Circuit said it had not resolved the debate), that
few statutes are affected, or that the Court should wait. That kind of
opposition usually produces a denial.

**If granted.** The Court would take the question as written: whether Borden's
greater-than-recklessness mens rea must attach to the resulting pain or injury,
or only to the volitional act of contact (here, Florida felony battery). The
Court would set the case for plenary review rather than decide it summarily.
A GVR is unlikely because no intervening decision bears on the question.

**If denied.** I expect no noted dissent. A short statement or dissent is
possible, most plausibly from Justice Jackson or Justice Gorsuch, given their
records on ACCA and the rule of lenity. More likely, someone would note that the
issue may deserve review in a better-developed split.
