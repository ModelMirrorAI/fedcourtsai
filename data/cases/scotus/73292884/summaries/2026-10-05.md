## In brief

The Court declined to hear a nonprofit's challenge to New Mexico's campaign-finance law requiring disclosure of donors funding ads that mention candidates shortly before elections.

## What happened

Rio Grande Foundation, a nonprofit issue-advocacy group, wanted to mail a legislative scorecard naming candidates close to an election but held off because New Mexico's Campaign Reporting Act requires groups to disclose large donors behind such ads. Rio Grande sued New Mexico's Secretary of State, arguing the disclosure rules violated its First Amendment rights. A federal district court upheld the law, and the Tenth Circuit Court of Appeals affirmed, with one judge dissenting. The full Tenth Circuit declined to rehear the case. Rio Grande then asked the Supreme Court to take up the dispute.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court was asked whether the government's interest in informing voters justifies requiring donor disclosure from groups that only mention a candidate without explicitly urging a vote for or against them, whether such disclosure rules can be properly limited in scope without requiring donors to specifically earmark their money for that purpose, and whether these rules should face the toughest level of constitutional review rather than a somewhat less demanding standard.

## Where it stands

The petition was distributed for conference twice, and the Court requested a response from New Mexico after initially considering it. On October 5, 2026, the Court denied the petition.

## What each outcome would mean

Rio Grande Foundation asked the Court to take up the case. With the petition denied, the Tenth Circuit's ruling upholding New Mexico's donor disclosure requirements stands, and Rio Grande Foundation remains subject to those rules if it distributes the ads at issue. The denial does not mean the Supreme Court agreed the lower court was correct; it simply leaves that decision in place without the Supreme Court deciding the constitutional questions Rio Grande Foundation raised.
