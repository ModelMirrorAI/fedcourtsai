## In brief

A nonprofit advocacy group asks the Court to strike down New Mexico's campaign-finance donor-disclosure law as applied to its issue-advocacy mailings.

## What happened

Rio Grande Foundation, a nonprofit that publishes a legislator scorecard called the Freedom Index, sued New Mexico's Secretary of State, arguing that the state's Campaign Reporting Act unconstitutionally forces it to disclose donors when it mails materials mentioning candidates close to an election, even without expressly urging votes for or against them. A federal district court upheld the law, and a divided Tenth Circuit panel affirmed, finding the disclosure requirement served the state's interest in informing voters and was adequately tailored. One judge dissented, arguing the law swept too broadly. The full Tenth Circuit declined to rehear the case.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked to decide whether a state's interest in informing voters justifies requiring donor disclosure from groups that mention candidates in their materials but do not explicitly urge for or against their election, whether such disclosure rules can be properly limited in scope without requiring donors to earmark their contributions for that purpose, and whether rules like this one should face the toughest level of constitutional review rather than the more flexible standard courts have applied to disclosure laws.

## Where it stands

The petition has been distributed for conference twice, most recently for the September 28, 2026 conference, after full briefing including a brief in opposition, a reply, and several amicus briefs. The docket does not yet show any decision on the petition.

## What each outcome would mean

Rio Grande Foundation is asking the Court to hear the case. If the Court grants the petition, it will hear full arguments and decide whether New Mexico's disclosure law is constitutional as applied to this kind of speech, without yet deciding who wins. If the Court denies the petition, the Tenth Circuit's ruling upholding the law stands, and New Mexico's disclosure requirements continue to apply to Rio Grande Foundation's mailings as before.
