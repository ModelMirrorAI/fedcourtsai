## What happened

Rio Grande Foundation, a New Mexico advocacy group, wanted to mail a "Freedom Index" scoring state legislators but held back because doing so would trigger donor-disclosure requirements under New Mexico's Campaign Reporting Act for ads that mention a candidate close to an election. The group sued New Mexico's Secretary of State, arguing the disclosure rule violates the First Amendment. A federal district court upheld the law, and a divided Tenth Circuit panel agreed, though one judge concurred reluctantly and another dissented, arguing the rule was not properly tailored. The full Tenth Circuit declined to rehear the case, with two judges dissenting from that denial.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked whether a state's interest in informing voters is enough to justify requiring donor disclosure from groups that only discuss issues and candidates without directly urging people to vote for or against them; whether such disclosure rules are properly limited in scope when they apply to groups that aren't primarily focused on electing candidates, absent a system tying specific donations to specific ads; and whether these disclosure rules should face the toughest level of judicial review rather than the somewhat less demanding review courts have applied to disclosure laws generally.

## Where it stands

The petition and response have been filed, including a brief in opposition arguing the case is a poor vehicle for these issues, along with several amicus briefs and a reply from the petitioner. The case was distributed for conference in June 2026, and after further briefing was distributed again for a conference on September 28, 2026, where the Court will decide whether to take it up.
