# Predicted reasoning — Rio Grande Foundation v. Toulouse Oliver, No. 25-1248

**Forecast state.** The docket shows **two distributions**: the first for the
June 4, 2026 conference (after the Secretary waived), which produced a
*Response Requested* order on May 26, 2026 rather than a decision, and the
second, after the brief in opposition and reply, for the **September 28, 2026
long conference**. No conference has yet considered the petition on a full
record. No CVSG is on the docket. Four amicus briefs support the petitioner.

**Disposition.** The Court will most likely **deny** the petition (P(grant)
0.14). The Court has shown sustained appetite for donor-disclosure questions
since *Americans for Prosperity Foundation v. Bonta*, and the response request
means at least one chambers wants a closer look. But the petition claims no
circuit split, the Tenth Circuit construed §(3)(c) to reach only ads
reasonably read as advocacy and suggested the Freedom Index is the functional
equivalent of express advocacy anyway, the challenge is facial on a record the
panel found thin on chill, and the "major purpose" question was arguably not
pressed below. The Court will treat these as vehicle defects and wait for a
cleaner case.

**Relists.** From the two distributions shown, expect the petition to be
**distributed at least once more with probability about one-third**. If the
Court is inclined to grant it will relist at least once to check the vehicle;
if it denies, a relist is likelier than for the ordinary petition because a
dissent from denial by Justice Thomas is plausible and takes time to write.
The modal path is still a denial announced in the October 5, 2026 order list
with no further distribution.

**CVSG.** No call for the Solicitor General's views (about 0.04). No federal
party, no federal statute at issue, and the federal government's own
electioneering-communication disclosure regime gives the SG no reason to
volunteer against the State.

**Which question, if granted.** The Court would take QP 1 and QP 2 together,
reformulated as whether a state may require donor disclosure for
electioneering communications that are neither express advocacy nor its
functional equivalent and, if so, what exacting scrutiny's narrow-tailoring
step requires of general-fund donor disclosure. QP 3 (strict scrutiny for
disclosure) would be left behind: *AFPF* settled exacting scrutiny five years
ago and the Court will not revisit it in a case that loses under either
standard on the State's reading.

**Summary disposition.** Conditional on a grant, a summary route is unlikely
(about 0.05). No intervening decision favors the petitioner: *NRSC v. FEC*
(June 30, 2026), as the BIO characterizes it, speaks approvingly of disclosure,
so a GVR "in light of" it would cut the wrong way. A grant here means plenary
review.

**Dissent from denial.** Conditional on a denial, expect a noted dissent or a
statement respecting denial with probability about 0.20, most plausibly from
Justice Thomas, who has long argued that compelled disclosure is subject to
strict scrutiny and who has flagged the express-advocacy line since *Citizens
United*. Justice Gorsuch is the second most likely writer. The modal outcome is
a silent denial.

**If granted, conditionally.** The likely ground would be *AFPF*'s narrow-
tailoring requirement applied to general-fund donor disclosure, along the lines
of Judge Eid's dissent, rather than a holding that disclosure may never reach
issue advocacy. Reversal would be the likelier judgment, and it would be
narrow: a holding about the fit between an informational interest and
non-earmarked general-fund donors, not a repudiation of *Citizens United*'s
disclosure paragraph.
