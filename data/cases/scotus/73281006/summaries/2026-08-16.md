## What happened

Daniel Grand, an Orthodox Jew living in University Heights, Ohio, invited neighbors to pray with him at his home. After a neighbor complained, the city's law director sent Grand a cease-and-desist letter warning that using his home as a "place of religious assembly" without a special permit violated zoning rules. Grand applied for the permit but withdrew his application before the city's planning commission finished considering it, saying he no longer wanted to operate a "house of worship" as the ordinance defined it. He later sued the city and several officials, raising First Amendment, RLUIPA, and other claims. The district court ruled most claims were not ready for court review because there was no final city decision and no real harm, and the Sixth Circuit unanimously agreed.

## What the Court is being asked

The case asks whether courts should treat claims like Grand's differently depending on their subject. One rule, used in property and zoning disputes, normally requires a final decision from local authorities before a lawsuit can proceed. Another rule, used for free-speech and religion claims generally, allows a lawsuit as soon as someone shows a credible government threat discouraged them from exercising their rights, even without a final decision or formal enforcement. Grand's petition argues the second, more lenient rule should have applied to his claims about being deterred from holding prayer gatherings; the city argues the first rule was correctly applied because Grand never obtained, or waited for, a final ruling and was not otherwise harmed.
