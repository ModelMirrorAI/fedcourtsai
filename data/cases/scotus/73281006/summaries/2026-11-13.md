## In brief

Daniel Grand asked the Supreme Court to decide whether a zoning finality rule for property-takings claims also blocks religious-liberty lawsuits over local permit disputes.

## What happened

Daniel Grand, an Orthodox Jew living in University Heights, Ohio, invited neighbors to pray with him at his home. The city sent a cease-and-desist letter, saying he needed a special-use permit to use his house as a place of religious assembly. Grand applied for the permit, faced a contentious public hearing, and then withdrew his application, saying he did not intend to operate a formal house of worship. He sued the city and officials, raising First Amendment, equal-protection, due-process and federal land-use law claims, along with allegations of surveillance and harassment. The district court dismissed most claims as not ripe because no final zoning decision had been reached; the Sixth Circuit affirmed.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked whether a rule developed for property-takings cases—requiring a final decision from local zoning authorities before a lawsuit can proceed—also applies to claims that government threats chilled someone's religious exercise, worship, and assembly, even when those claims arise in a zoning dispute.

## Where it stands

The Court granted the petition, and the case was briefed and argued; it is scheduled for oral argument on December 9, 2026, and awaits decision.

## What each outcome would mean

Grand, who brought this case, is asking the Court to reverse the lower court's ruling that his claims were not ripe for review. If the Court sides with Grand, his claims could go forward in the lower courts without his having to obtain a final zoning determination first. If the Court sides with the city, the finality requirement would continue to apply, and Grand's claims would remain barred unless he pursues further proceedings before local zoning authorities.
