# Forecast of the Court's judgment

The Court will vacate the Sixth Circuit's judgment and remand for claim-specific consideration of justiciability. The point forecast is a unanimous judgment, with Barrett the most likely individual author of a signed opinion. The Court will act after the argument scheduled for December 9, 2026, most likely during the first half of 2027 and by the end of the Term.

The controlling distinction will be between an injury caused by the ultimate disposition of a land-use application and an injury already inflicted during the government's response to religious exercise. A final local decision can be necessary to know whether a proposed use will actually be denied. But it cannot be a categorical prerequisite to adjudicating a claim that officials have already imposed an objectively credible threat, coerced a permit application, or subjected a person to discriminatory treatment causing an independently cognizable injury. The Court will reject an automatic land-use finality barrier, not declare all asserted chilling effects sufficient without an objective injury inquiry.

The majority will take the narrower route substantially advanced by the United States: ordinary Article III requirements govern, and the need for a final permit determination depends on the particular injury asserted. It will not adopt the full breadth of petitioner's proposed takings-only limitation on finality, overrule Williamson County wholesale, or decide that the City violated the First Amendment or RLUIPA on the underlying facts. The outcome will concern access to adjudication, not entitlement to a permit, damages, or immunity from neutral zoning rules.

Grand's move makes prospective relief a serious threshold issue. The Court will distinguish that problem from the alleged completed injuries underlying the pleaded damages demand, and leave the lower courts to resolve the scope and preservation of surviving claims and any remaining mootness questions. The forecast does not assume that pleading damages conclusively defeats respondents' forfeiture argument. If that argument succeeds and no claim survives, dismissal as improvidently granted or an undisturbed judgment becomes a genuine alternative. A vacatur solely for mootness would still disturb the judgment, but would not fulfill the doctrinal-ground forecast above.

The narrow injury-based rule can attract Justices across the Court without agreement on broader religious-liberty doctrine. No dissent is the point forecast. If a Justice writes separately, Thomas and Jackson are the leading individual candidates, with Gorsuch also plausible: a concurrence could address the status of prudential ripeness, the boundaries of the Court's ruling, or the need to resolve preservation and mootness. Those possibilities do not predict that any particular Justice will write with greater than 50% probability. Authorship is diffuse; Barrett's 18% is a plurality of the author distribution, not a confident assignment prediction.
