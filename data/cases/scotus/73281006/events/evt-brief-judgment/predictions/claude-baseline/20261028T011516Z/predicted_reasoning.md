# Predicted reasoning — Grand v. City of University Heights (No. 25-965), briefed moment

**Judgment class: vacated (and remanded).** The Court will set aside the Sixth Circuit's judgment that Grand's as-applied claims were unripe under *Williamson County*. A reversal label is the main alternative within "disturbed"; a DIG is the live procedural exit (see below), and an outright affirmance is the least likely result.

**Vote lineup: lopsided, most likely 9-0 or 8-1 on the judgment.** The Solicitor General supports the petitioner, the ripeness rule the Sixth Circuit applied is categorical, and the Court's recent justiciability cases treat ripeness and standing as one Article III injury inquiry. Any dissent is more likely to rest on mootness or forfeiture — Grand sold his home and left the city after the cert-stage reply, and the respondents say he never pressed damages below — than on defending a land-use-specific finality rule.

**Question reached.** The Court answers the granted question about chilling-effect injuries versus *Williamson County* finality. It does not adopt the petitioner's broader reformulation in his merits brief, which the respondents call out. The majority's line will be that *Williamson County* finality is ordinary Article III injury applied to a claim whose injury is a denied land use. Claims resting on injuries independent of any permit decision are tested under ordinary standing and chilling doctrine. Here that means the past chill from the cease-and-desist demand and the officials' threats, the permit fee and legal costs, and the alleged discriminatory treatment.

**Breadth.** The ground will be narrow and the case remanded. The Court will not decide for itself whether the prospective claims were ripe, whether they are now moot after the move and the mayor's departure, or whether the damages theory was preserved. The United States expressly invites that remand. *Williamson County*'s role for takings claims and for challenges to a permit outcome stays untouched.

**Procedural exit.** The Court ordered a 4,000-word sur-reply on the effect of the move. That order shows the Justices want the move briefed, which signals they mean to decide the case rather than drop it. The complaint's request for compensatory damages (J.A. 96, as the respondents concede) keeps a live controversy. Still, the move, the preservation fight and the reformulated question make a DIG a real possibility, around 8%.

**Who writes.** The case will be argued December 9, 2026. Roberts wrote *Knick*, which overruled *Williamson County*'s state-litigation prong, so he or a Justice he assigns for continuity is a natural author. Gorsuch and Barrett are also plausible authors. Expect separate writings from Thomas and/or Gorsuch, urging that *Williamson County* finality be confined further or abandoned outside takings. Alito may write on religious-liberty themes. Jackson may concur in the judgment or dissent on mootness or forfeiture grounds.
