# Predicted Court Reasoning: *Daniel Grand v. City of University Heights, Ohio, et al.* (No. 25-965)

## I. Predicted Judgment Class and Vote Lineup

- **Judgment Class:** `vacated` (the judgment of the Sixth Circuit is vacated and the case is remanded for further proceedings consistent with the Court's opinion).
- **Vote Lineup:** **9–0** unanimous judgment disturbing the Sixth Circuit's decision, with all nine Justices (`Roberts`, `Thomas`, `Alito`, `Sotomayor`, `Kagan`, `Gorsuch`, `Kavanaugh`, `Barrett`, and `Jackson`) voting in the `majority` to vacate and remand.

## II. Question Reached and Doctrinal Ground (`majority-ground`)

The Court will reach the single Question Presented and hold that *Williamson County Regional Planning Commission v. Hamilton Bank of Johnson City*, 473 U.S. 172 (1985), does not impose a categorical administrative-finality barrier that displaces ordinary Article III standing and *Abbott Laboratories v. Gardner*, 387 U.S. 136 (1967), ripeness principles when a plaintiff brings First Amendment or Religious Land Use and Institutionalized Persons Act (RLUIPA) claims alleging completed chilling of religious exercise or religious discrimination.

Specifically, the majority will ground its holding in three doctrinal pillars:
1. **Confinement of *Williamson County* to Its Substantive Regulatory-Takings Rationale:** Building on *Pakdel v. City & County of San Francisco*, 594 U.S. 474 (2021), *Knick v. Township of Scott*, 588 U.S. 180 (2019), and last Term's unanimous decision in *First Choice Women's Resource Centers, Inc. v. Davenport*, 146 S. Ct. 1114, 1125–28 (2026), the Court will explain that *Williamson County*'s finality rule was "compelled by the very nature of the inquiry required by the Just Compensation Clause," 473 U.S. at 190—where a court cannot know whether a regulation "goes too far" in diminishing property value until the zoning authority reaches a final decision on permitted development. By contrast, a First Amendment chilling injury or an Equal Protection / Free Exercise / RLUIPA discrimination injury occurs the moment a credible threat of enforcement deters protected religious exercise or subjects a claimant to unequal or retaliatory burdens.
2. **Statutory Fidelity Under 42 U.S.C. § 1983 and RLUIPA:** Neither § 1983 nor RLUIPA requires administrative exhaustion, and RLUIPA expressly directs that "[s]tanding to assert a claim or defense under this section shall be governed by the general rules of standing under article III of the Constitution," 42 U.S.C. § 2000cc-2(a), and that the statute be construed broadly in favor of religious exercise, § 2000cc-3(g). Under *Lexmark International, Inc. v. Static Control Components, Inc.*, 572 U.S. 118, 125–28 (2014), federal courts may not superimpose a prudential land-use finality hurdle to decline jurisdiction over claims that present a concrete, ripe Article III injury.
3. **Completed Past Harms and Retrospective Damages:** Following *Uzuegbunam v. Preczewski*, 592 U.S. 279 (2021), and the Solicitor General's framework (echoing *Temple B'Nai Zion, Inc. v. City of Sunny Isles Beach*, 727 F.3d 1349 (11th Cir. 2013), and *Roman Catholic Bishop of Springfield v. City of Springfield*, 724 F.3d 78 (1st Cir. 2013)), the Court will hold that Grand's claims seeking retrospective nominal and compensatory damages for past chilled Sabbath prayer gatherings and past discriminatory subjection to the Special Use Permit process cannot be rendered unripe by his subsequent withdrawal of the permit application, because no future zoning decision by the Planning Commission could undo or remedy already-completed constitutional or statutory harms.

## III. Breadth of the Ground (`ground-breadth`)

Rather than holding that municipal finality is never relevant to any land-use-related RLUIPA or constitutional claim, the Court will adopt the Solicitor General's **claim-specific approach** (`ground-breadth`):
- Where a plaintiff alleges a *completed past injury*—such as past chilling of home prayer gatherings caused by a cease-and-desist order and mayoral threats of prosecution, or discriminatory imposition of a commercial house-of-worship permitting regime on a small religious gathering—the claim is immediately ripe under Article III without awaiting a final permit decision.
- Where, however, a claim seeks prospective relief challenging the *substantive application of a land-use restriction to a proposed land use* (for example, whether a zoning code's substantive terms unlawfully burden a proposed facility), whether the municipality has actually committed to applying the restriction to that use remains relevant to fitness under *Abbott Laboratories* and RLUIPA's statutory text ("impose or implement a land use regulation").
- Because the Sixth Circuit and District Court applied a blanket *Williamson County* finality bar to dismiss all twelve counts for lack of subject-matter jurisdiction without distinguishing completed chilling and discrimination claims (or retrospective damages claims) from prospective land-use claims, and without evaluating how Petitioner's recent move from University Heights or the City's summary-judgment stipulation affects prospective relief under mootness/voluntary-cessation doctrine, the Court will vacate and remand for the lower courts to conduct a claim-by-claim analysis in the first instance.

## IV. Procedural Exit Assessment (DIG / Mootness)

Although Petitioner notified the Court on September 18, 2026 of his move out of the City of University Heights (and the Court permitted a 4,000-word sur-reply from Respondents on that issue), a dismissal as improvidently granted (DIG) is unlikely. Under *Uzuegbunam*, 592 U.S. at 285–93, Petitioner's live claims for retrospective compensatory and nominal damages under § 1983 and RLUIPA prevent the case as a whole from becoming moot, even if his move out of the City moots his claims for prospective injunctive and declaratory relief. Rather than dismissing the writ, the Court will decide the threshold ripeness question on which certiorari was granted and leave the mootness of prospective relief and any preservation/merits defenses for remand.

## V. Opinion Authorship and Separate Writings

- **Majority Author (`majority-author`):** Justice **Gorsuch** (`0.25`) is the modal author, having written *First Choice Women's Resource Centers, Inc. v. Davenport*, 146 S. Ct. 1114 (2026), which expressly flagged this issue last Term (146 S. Ct. at 1128), and having consistently authored or led the Court's opinions on RLUIPA and Article III justiciability. Chief Justice **Roberts** (`0.15`), Justice **Barrett** (`0.14`), and Justice **Kavanaugh** (`0.13`) are the next likeliest authors if the opinion is assigned to craft a measured institutional consensus around the Solicitor General's claim-by-claim framework.
- **Separate Writings (`separate-writers`):**
  - **Thomas (`0.28`) / Alito (`0.22`) / Gorsuch (`0.20`):** Likely to author or join a concurrence emphasizing that prudential ripeness doctrine as a whole lacks an Article III foundation (building on *Susan B. Anthony List*, 573 U.S. at 167, and Justice Thomas's concurrence in *First Choice*) or stressing the pervasive weaponization of municipal zoning codes against minority religious exercise and home prayer groups.
  - **Jackson (`0.26`) / Sotomayor (`0.22`):** Most likely to author a concurrence (or partial dissent) underscoring the narrow scope of the remand—highlighting that Grand's initial email announcing the inauguration of a named *shul* created genuine ambiguity about the nature of his land use, and emphasizing that lower courts on remand remain free to find prospective claims moot (given Grand's move and the City's stipulation) or specific theories forfeited or meritless on summary judgment.
