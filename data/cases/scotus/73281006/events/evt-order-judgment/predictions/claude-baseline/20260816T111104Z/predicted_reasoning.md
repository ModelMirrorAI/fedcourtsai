The question presented — whether the First Amendment's chilling-effect
doctrine is displaced by *Williamson County*'s land-use finality requirement
when government threats chilled religious exercise — is a justiciability
question. The Court will hold Grand's First Amendment and RLUIPA claims ripe:
the cease-and-desist letter, the mayor's warnings, police patrols, and a
prosecutor's inspection add up to a credible threat of enforcement, and under
the *Susan B. Anthony List v. Driehaus* pre-enforcement framework a plaintiff
facing such a threat need not first pursue a permit process to final decision.
The Court will reverse (or vacate) and remand for the lower courts to reach
the merits of the RLUIPA and First Amendment claims; it will not decide those
merits itself.

## Vote lineup

**9–0.** Justiciability reversals of this shape run unanimous or lopsided
(*SBA List* was 9–0; *Pakdel*, per curiam), and the religious-liberty valence
here drew a cross-ideological amicus coalition (Agudath Israel, USCCB, the
Islam & Religious Freedom Action Team, ACLJ, Manhattan Institute). The most
plausible defection is one or two of Sotomayor, Kagan, or Jackson concurring
in the judgment or dissenting on the ground that Grand's withdrawal of his own
permit application distinguishes the chilling-effect cases; I put a unanimous
disturbance well above an 8–1 or 7–2 split, and no lineup in which the
affirmance side wins. Expect separate writings: a concurrence from Gorsuch or
Thomas urging that *Williamson County* finality has no place outside takings
law (authorship and writings are unscored; stated for completeness).

## Ground and breadth

The majority will rest on chilling-effect/credible-threat ripeness (*SBA
List*, *Steffel*), holding that *Williamson County*'s final-decision rule does
not bar First Amendment and RLUIPA claims premised on enforcement threats —
confining finality to claims that turn on how a regulation is finally applied
(takings-style claims) rather than overruling it. The rule will be stated
categorically for the class of pre-enforcement religious-exercise challenges,
not as a fact-bound holding about this letter. The Court will take the QP as
written; there is no second QP to leave behind.

## Procedural path and timing (unscored)

Argument in the winter sittings (most likely January–March 2027, given the
extended briefing schedule), decision by late June 2027. A DIG is the live

finality-exception argument below, and the Sixth Circuit deemed the vagueness

objections with the reply in hand, so I price it low (0.04). No further
