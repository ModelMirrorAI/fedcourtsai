# Forecast

The Court will reverse the Sixth Circuit and remand. The most likely judgment is unanimous. A one- or two-Justice dissent focused on whether the City's acts amounted to a credible threat, rather than on the governing legal distinction, is the leading alternative.

The Court will answer the sole question presented substantially as written. It will hold that a plaintiff who stops protected religious exercise in response to a credible government command has suffered a complete First Amendment chilling injury. The ordinary pre-enforcement rule does not require that plaintiff to violate the command, incur a citation, or finish a discretionary land-use process before obtaining judicial review. The Court will treat the law director's cease-and-desist letter, Grand's cancellation of prayer, the mayor's reiteration of the restriction, and the City's patrol and investigative activity as enough to require application of that doctrine rather than a mechanical *Williamson County* finality rule.

The opinion will be narrower than a general abolition of land-use finality. It will distinguish constitutional claims based on an already-completed chill from as-applied RLUIPA or property-use claims whose resolution still depends on an authoritative zoning interpretation. The Court will leave the ordinance's validity, forfeiture questions not encompassed by the granted question, and claim-by-claim application for remand. It is unlikely to hold that every cease-and-desist letter is final agency action or that every subjective chill is actionable.

A separate opinion, if any, will emphasize that Grand withdrew his permit application before the Planning Commission or Board of Zoning Appeals ruled, disclaimed an intent to operate an ordinance-defined house of worship, received no citation, and remained free to seek an authoritative interpretation. It will characterize any continuing chill as subjective and the dispute as factually contingent.

A dismissal as improvidently granted or an equally divided affirmance is unlikely. The judgment should issue during the October 2026 Term, most likely by June 2027.
