# Forecast: what the Court will do with No. 25-1098, O'Donnell v. City of Chicago

**Bottom line.** The petition is most likely denied (P(any grant) = 0.16). The grant mass that
remains is overwhelmingly a hold-and-GVR trailing the two police-power takings petitions set for the
same September 28, 2026 conference — *Hadley v. City of South Bend* (No. 25-1158, CA7) and
*Pena v. City of Los Angeles* (No. 25-1163, CA9) — not a plenary grant of this facial challenge.

## Relists and holds

The docket shows **two distributions**: April 28 (for the May 14 conference, pulled when a response
was requested on April 30) and July 15 (for the September 28 long conference). No true relist has
happened yet; the second distribution is the post-response redistribution.

I forecast **at least one further distribution with probability 0.6**. The mechanism is the
companion structure, not this petition's own merit: Hadley and Pena are distributed for the same
conference, each carrying five amicus briefs, and the Court almost never grants a case cold at the
long conference — a grant of either would typically follow a relist, and this petition (whose CA7
opinion applies *Hadley* by name) would be relisted or held alongside it. If the Court holds this
petition for a granted Hadley/Pena, the eventual redistribution for the disposing conference (mid-2027)
is itself a further distribution. The no-further-distribution branch is a clean denial of all three on
the October 5, 2026 order list, which I put near 0.35–0.4.

## CVSG

**No CVSG here (0.03).** The challenged measure is a municipal ordinance with no federal program at
stake. If the Court wanted the Solicitor General's views on a police-power exception to the Takings
Clause it would invite them in *Hadley* or *Pena*, the lead vehicles, and simply hold this one.

## Which question the Court would take, if any

If the Court reaches this case at all, it takes **QP 2 reformulated** — whether there is a
police-power / law-enforcement exception to the Takings Clause — which is the Hadley/Pena question,
and never QP 1 as written ("is this practice an unconstitutional taking?"), because the complaint is
an exclusively facial challenge under *Keystone Bituminous* and the record has no as-applied facts
about surplus proceeds. The *Tyler v. Hennepin County* surplus theory the petition leads with is
not what the Court would grant on; the Court's own recent tax-sale cases (*Tyler*, *Pung v. Isabella
County*, June 23, 2026) sit in the taxing-power line the Seventh Circuit distinguished, and the
petition concedes there is no circuit conflict on impound-for-unpaid-tickets (*Tate v. D.C.* agrees
with CA7).

## Summary disposition versus plenary review

**Conditional on any grant, a summary route is the likely form (0.8).** Concretely: the Court grants
*Hadley* and/or *Pena*, holds this petition, and after reversing or vacating below in 2027 GVRs this
case for reconsideration in light of that decision, because the Seventh Circuit's opinion here rests
expressly on *Hadley*'s "classic example of police power" principle. A plenary grant of this petition,
alone or consolidated, is a residual ~0.03: the facial-only posture, the absence of a split, and the
strong *Bennis* forfeiture line make it a poor lead vehicle, and the BIO makes that case well.

## Dissent or statement respecting denial

**Conditional on denial, a separate writing on this docket is unlikely (0.10).** The Justices most
likely to write on the police-power exception (Sotomayor and Gorsuch wrote in *Baker v. City of
McKinney* in 2024 that the issue should percolate; Thomas and Gorsuch write on forfeiture) would
attach any statement to *Hadley* or *Pena*, the sympathetic wrong-house-raid vehicles, not to a
facial challenge to a parking-ticket impound ordinance. A one-line "see statement in No. 25-1158"
cross-reference is the most plausible form a writing here would take.

## Timing

If denied outright: October 5, 2026 order list. If relisted with the companions: a disposition
within one to three further conferences in October–November 2026. If held: no action until
Hadley/Pena are decided, then a GVR or denial in roughly June 2027.
