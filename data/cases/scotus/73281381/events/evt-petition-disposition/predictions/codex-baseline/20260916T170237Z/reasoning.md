## Why 10% rather than the 17.24% anchor

The strongest positive docket signal is the April 30 request for a response after respondents initially waived. That is actual Court attention, and the petition raises a substantial property-rights question. The petition's QPs and pages 13-20 argue that Tyler's protection of surplus value extends beyond tax collection and that Bennis does not authorize confiscating unrelated vehicles simply because an owner owes civil ticket debt. Those are intelligible competing characterizations, not merely a request to reweigh evidence.

However, the two distribution entries are not persuasive evidence of two substantive conference examinations. The first scheduled conference was followed by a request for a response due after that conference; the second distribution followed the opposition and reply. The observable path is consistent with re-circulation after response completion, not an unexplained post-conference relist. This reduces the evidentiary force of the elevated classification without changing the classification itself.

The main downward adjustment is vehicle quality. The opposition, pages 7-8 and 11-15, says the complaint exclusively advanced a facial challenge to the whole enforcement ordinance, not an as-applied taking, due-process claim, or excessive-fines claim. It identifies applications involving no sale, no surplus, or return of a vehicle. The petition acknowledges the facial ruling below but primarily argues from retained surplus and particular vehicles. On those materials, the Court could view this as a poor vehicle to decide the broad constitutional boundary even if some applications are troubling. This is my assessment of the competing filings, not an independent holding that the City's facial-challenge argument must prevail.

There are also material factual differences between the parties' descriptions. The QP broadly describes impoundment and sale; the opposition at page 4 says O'Donnell's vehicle was sold for scrap and Goree's was relinquished to a lienholder. The petition at pages 11-12 instead emphasizes the outstanding debt and the co-signor's liability. I cannot assume a demonstrated surplus in each plaintiff's case, nor adopt the petition's account of inability to pay as an undisputed record fact; the opposition contests that allegation in footnote 5.

The petition does not demonstrate a clean contrary appellate holding on the precise traffic-debt forfeiture issue. The opposition at pages 17-18 relies on Tate as aligned authority; the petition's broader police-power citations and historical surplus argument are not by themselves a square split. The opposition at pages 9-10 also points to Hadley as a different vehicle for the more general law-enforcement takings question. I use only that pre-decision description, not any independently retrieved status or result of Hadley. Together these considerations justify moving below the selected-population anchor to 10%, while the requested response and serious Tyler/Bennis issue keep the estimate above an undifferentiated low-interest petition.
