## In brief

Vehicle owners ask the Court to revive their claim that Chicago's practice of impounding and selling cars over unpaid tickets violates the Constitution's limits on government takings of property.

## What happened

Ryan O'Donnell and Michael Goree had vehicles impounded and sold by Chicago after accumulating unpaid parking tickets, under a city ordinance allowing impoundment and disposal of vehicles belonging to owners with multiple unpaid fines. They sued, arguing the city's process, including keeping sale proceeds without returning any surplus, amounts to an unconstitutional taking of property. The district court dismissed the suit, and the Seventh Circuit affirmed, holding the ordinance is an exercise of the city's power to punish lawbreakers rather than a taking.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked whether a city ordinance that lets a local government immobilize, impound, and ultimately sell vehicles to enforce unpaid traffic fines amounts to taking private property without paying for it, and whether there are any constitutional limits on a government's power to confiscate property this way when acting under its general authority to enforce laws.

## Where it stands

The petition has been distributed for conference twice, most recently for the October 9, 2026 conference, after both respondents waived their right to respond, the Court then requested a response, and both sides completed briefing, including a reply from the petitioners.

## What each outcome would mean

O'Donnell and Goree, as petitioners, are asking the Court to take up their case. If the Court grants the petition, it will hear full arguments and decide whether Chicago's ordinance violates constitutional property protections, without presuming either side correct beforehand. If the Court denies the petition, the Seventh Circuit's ruling against the vehicle owners stands, leaving Chicago's impoundment and sale practice in place as currently upheld.
