## In brief

The Court declined to hear Chicago drivers' challenge to a city ordinance letting it tow, impound, and sell vehicles over unpaid parking tickets without compensation.

## What happened

Ryan O'Donnell and Michael Goree had their vehicles impounded and disposed of after accumulating unpaid Chicago parking tickets, under a city ordinance allowing immobilization, towing, and eventual sale of vehicles belonging to owners with multiple unpaid fines. They sued the City and its towing contractor, URT United Road Towing, arguing the ordinance amounts to an unconstitutional taking of property because the City keeps sale proceeds without crediting the debt or returning any surplus. The district court dismissed the claims, and the Seventh Circuit affirmed, holding the ordinance is a punitive exercise of police power rather than a taking.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked whether a city's practice of seizing and selling vehicles to enforce unpaid traffic fines, while keeping all proceeds without crediting the debt or refunding any surplus, violates the constitutional requirement that government pay for property it takes. A related question is whether there is any limit on a local government's power to confiscate property while enforcing its laws.

## Where it stands

The petition was distributed for multiple conferences between May and October 2026. On October 19, 2026, the Court denied the petition, with a statement by Justice Sotomayor, joined by Justice Gorsuch, respecting that denial.

## What each outcome would mean

O'Donnell and Goree, as petitioners, asked the Court to take up their case. The denial leaves the Seventh Circuit's ruling in place, meaning the City's ordinance stands and the drivers' lawsuit remains dismissed. A denial does not mean the Court agreed the ordinance is constitutional; it simply leaves the lower court's judgment undisturbed. Had the Court instead granted the petition, it would have scheduled the case for full briefing and argument to decide the constitutional question itself, without yet determining which side would prevail.
