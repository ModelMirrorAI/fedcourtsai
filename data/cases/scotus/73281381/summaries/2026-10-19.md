## In brief

The Supreme Court declined to hear Chicago drivers' challenge to the city's practice of impounding and selling vehicles over unpaid parking tickets without compensation.

## What happened

Ryan O'Donnell and Michael Goree each had their vehicles towed, impounded, and sold by the City of Chicago after accumulating unpaid parking tickets, under a city ordinance allowing escalating enforcement including vehicle disposal. Neither received payment or credit toward their debts from the sales. They sued the City and URT United Road Towing, the city's towing contractor, arguing the ordinance amounts to an unconstitutional taking of property. A federal district court dismissed the claims, and the Seventh Circuit Court of Appeals affirmed, holding that the city's vehicle forfeiture scheme is an exercise of police power to enforce traffic laws, not a taking.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked whether a city violates constitutional protection against uncompensated property seizures when it impounds and sells vehicles to enforce unpaid traffic fines, keeping all proceeds without crediting the debt or returning any surplus value, and whether there is any limit on a local government's power to confiscate property this way while enforcing its own laws.

## Where it stands

The petition was distributed for conference multiple times, with the Court asking for a response from the City after initial waivers. The Court denied the petition, with a statement by Justice Sotomayor, joined by Justice Gorsuch, respecting that denial.

## What each outcome would mean

O'Donnell and Goree asked the Court to overturn the Seventh Circuit's ruling in favor of Chicago and URT. The denial leaves the Seventh Circuit's decision in place, meaning the city's practice of impounding and selling vehicles without compensating owners for surplus value remains in effect, and O'Donnell and Goree's lawsuit stays dismissed. The denial does not mean the Court agreed the lower court was correct; it simply leaves that ruling standing.
