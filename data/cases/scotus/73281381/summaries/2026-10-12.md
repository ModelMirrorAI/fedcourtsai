## In brief

Two Chicago drivers ask the Supreme Court to revive their claim that the City's practice of towing, impounding, and selling vehicles over unpaid parking debt amounts to an unconstitutional taking.

## What happened

Ryan O'Donnell and Michael Goree each had vehicles towed, impounded, and disposed of by the City of Chicago after accumulating unpaid parking ticket debt, under a city ordinance allowing escalating enforcement against vehicle owners who fail to pay. Neither received compensation or debt credit for the lost vehicles. They sued the City and its towing contractor, URT United Road Towing, arguing the ordinance is unconstitutional on its face. A federal district court dismissed the case, and the Seventh Circuit Court of Appeals affirmed, holding that the city's vehicle forfeiture process is an exercise of police power to enforce traffic laws rather than a taking requiring compensation.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked to decide whether a city's practice of seizing and disposing of vehicles to enforce unpaid traffic fines, without paying the owner for the vehicle's value or crediting it against the debt, violates the constitutional rule against taking private property for public use without compensation. A related question is whether there is any limit on a local government's power to confiscate property this way when acting under its general authority to enforce laws, rather than through formal condemnation.

## Where it stands

The petition has been distributed for conference more than once, most recently for the October 9, 2026 conference, after full briefing including a response and reply. The docket does not yet show a decision on whether the Court will hear the case.

## What each outcome would mean

O'Donnell and Goree, as petitioners, are asking the Court to take up their case. If the Court grants review, it would hear full arguments and decide whether the city's vehicle forfeiture practice violates the takings rule, without yet determining who wins. If the Court denies review, the Seventh Circuit's ruling stands, leaving in place the dismissal of the drivers' claims and the city's practice as currently structured.
