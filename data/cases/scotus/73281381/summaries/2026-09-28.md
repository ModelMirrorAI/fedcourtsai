## In brief

Vehicle owners ask the Court to revive their claim that Chicago's practice of impounding and selling cars over unpaid parking tickets amounts to an unconstitutional taking.

## What happened

Ryan O'Donnell and Michael Goree had vehicles impounded and sold by Chicago under an ordinance letting the City immobilize, tow, and eventually dispose of vehicles registered to owners with multiple unpaid traffic and parking judgments. They sued, arguing the City keeps sale proceeds without repaying any surplus above the debt owed, and that this violates the Constitution's protection against government takings of property. The district court dismissed the claims, and the Seventh Circuit affirmed, holding that the ordinance is an exercise of the City's power to punish violations of its traffic laws, not a taking requiring compensation.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked whether a city's practice of seizing and selling vehicles to enforce unpaid traffic fines, keeping any proceeds beyond the debt owed, crosses a line into taking private property without paying for it. It is also asked more broadly whether there is any limit on a local government's power to confiscate property when enforcing its laws.

## Where it stands

The petition has been distributed for conference twice, most recently for the September 28, 2026 conference, after full briefing including a brief in opposition and a reply. The docket does not yet record any action from either conference.

## What each outcome would mean

O'Donnell and Goree, as petitioners, are asking the Court to take up their case. If the Court grants the petition, it would hear the case and decide whether the ordinance's forfeiture and sale process violates the takings clause, without yet deciding who is right. If the Court denies the petition, the Seventh Circuit's ruling against O'Donnell and Goree stands, leaving Chicago's ordinance in place and their claims dismissed.
