## In brief

A bankruptcy litigant's emergency request to pause a federal district judge's upcoming ruling while he pursued recusal arguments was denied by the Chief Justice.

## What happened

Paul Schultz, a debtor in a long-running bankruptcy dispute, sought to have the federal district judge handling an appeal involving trustee H. Jason Gold removed from the case, arguing the judge's impartiality was reasonably in question. After the judge denied his recusal motion, Schultz asked the Fourth Circuit for mandamus relief, which was denied, and he then filed a petition for rehearing there. Fearing the district judge would issue a merits ruling before the rehearing petition was resolved, Schultz asked the Supreme Court to step in.

## What the Court is being asked

Schultz asked the Circuit Justice to temporarily block the district judge from issuing a final ruling in the underlying appeal until the Fourth Circuit finished considering whether that judge should be removed from the case for appearing partial. He argued that without such a pause, the question of whether the judge could keep handling the case would become moot once she ruled.

## Where it stands

The application for a stay was submitted to the Chief Justice on November 2, 2026, and denied on November 11, 2026.

## What each outcome would mean

Schultz, the applicant, asked the Court to pause the district judge's handling of the underlying appeal while recusal proceedings continued in the Fourth Circuit. Denying the stay, which is what happened, means no pause was ordered: the district judge remains free to rule on the appeal while Schultz's rehearing petition proceeds separately in the Fourth Circuit. The denial does not decide whether recusal was warranted or resolve the underlying bankruptcy dispute between Schultz and Gold; it only means the Supreme Court did not grant the temporary relief Schultz requested.
