## In brief

A bankruptcy debtor asks the Chief Justice to pause a federal appeals judge from ruling until a recusal dispute is resolved.

## What happened

Paul Schultz, a bankruptcy debtor, has long disputed whether a creditor called Clear Sky Financial properly established its claim against his estate, and separately has pursued claims against H. Jason Gold, a bankruptcy trustee, over how the estate was administered. Schultz sought recusal of the district judge handling his appeal in the Gold matter, citing concerns about fairness and notice. The judge denied recusal, and the Fourth Circuit denied Schultz's request for mandamus relief. Schultz then filed a rehearing petition and an emergency motion in the Fourth Circuit asking it to block further rulings until that petition is decided, and asks the Supreme Court to step in while the Fourth Circuit considers those requests.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked whether it should temporarily block the district judge from issuing a final ruling in the appeal while a rehearing request over her recusal remains pending before the Fourth Circuit, so that the recusal question can be resolved before the judge's decision takes effect.

## Where it stands

This is an emergency application filed with the Chief Justice, who handles emergency matters from the Fourth Circuit. The docket shows only the application's submission; it does not yet record any ruling on it.

## What each outcome would mean

Schultz, the applicant, is asking the Court to pause proceedings. If the Court grants the stay, the district judge would be blocked from issuing a final ruling in the underlying appeal until the Fourth Circuit acts on Schultz's pending rehearing and preservation requests, leaving the recusal question to be resolved first. If the Court denies the stay, the district judge remains free to rule on the appeal while the rehearing petition proceeds separately in the Fourth Circuit. Either way, the order would be temporary and would not resolve the recusal dispute or the underlying bankruptcy appeal itself.
