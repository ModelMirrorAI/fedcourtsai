# Forecast of the Court's handling

The Chief Justice will most likely deny the application for a stay in a short order, without an explanatory opinion. The probability of any grant, including partial relief, is 6%, conditional on the application not being dismissed or denied as moot. A nonmoot denial is the modal disposition.

The starting record contains one submission entry dated November 2, 2026, with no response request, no full-Court referral, and no amicus entries. From that state:

- A response will probably not be requested; the probability of a new request is 10%.
- The application will probably not be referred to the full Court; the probability of a new referral is 25%. Referral, if it occurs, need not imply relief.
- No further amicus participation is expected; the probability that the counted amicus total rises above zero is 1.5%. This includes qualifying submitted briefs as well as Latin-form amicus entries, without double-counting the same submitted-and-accepted brief.

The modal timing forecast is disposition within about two weeks of the November 2 submission, approximately by November 16, 2026. This is a forecast from the submission record, not a statement that the matter remains pending on any later date. If a response is requested, resolution is more likely shortly after the response deadline, potentially several weeks after submission.

No separate writing is expected. The independent probabilities of writing on the disposition order are Roberts 0.3%, Thomas 0.5%, Alito 0.4%, Sotomayor 0.3%, Kagan 0.3%, Gorsuch 0.4%, Kavanaugh 0.3%, Barrett 0.3%, and Jackson 0.3%.

If the Court explains a denial, the expected rationale is failure to establish the demanding prerequisites for emergency relief rather than a definitive ruling on the underlying bankruptcy dispute. That doctrinal expectation is conditional: the forecast does not attribute any particular argument or defect to the unavailable application.
