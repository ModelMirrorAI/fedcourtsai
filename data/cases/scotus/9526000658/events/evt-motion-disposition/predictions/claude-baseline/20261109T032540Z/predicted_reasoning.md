# Forecast — 26A658, Paul Schultz v. H. Jason Gold (stay application)

**State at my vantage:** one docket entry — the application for a stay, submitted
to the Chief Justice on Nov 2, 2026 (docketed Nov 6). No response requested, not
referred to the Court, zero amicus entries.

- **Disposition:** the Chief Justice, acting as Circuit Justice, denies the
  application alone, without calling for a response, most likely within about one
  to three weeks of docketing (mid-to-late November 2026). P(relief granted in
  whole or part) ≈ 2%.
- **Response requested:** very unlikely (≈4%). Requests for a response go to
  applications with a plausible prospect of relief; a pro se stay in a
  bankruptcy dispute with a case trustee rarely draws one.
- **Referral to the full Court:** ≈15%. The realistic path is not the Chief
  Justice referring it at first instance but the applicant renewing a denied
  application to another Justice, who then refers it to the Court (which then
  denies). Self-represented applicants do this with some regularity, and the
  timing could stretch into December if that happens.
- **Amicus:** essentially none (≈1%).
- **Writings:** no Justice writes on the disposing order; a one-line denial.

Conditionally, on the merits standard: a stay requires a reasonable probability
of certiorari, a fair prospect of reversal and irreparable harm. A personal
bankruptcy dispute with no identified circuit split and an intermediate
bankruptcy-appeal posture is a poor candidate for certiorari, so the first
prong is unlikely to be met.
