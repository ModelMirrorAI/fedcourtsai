## Conditional Merits and Stay Standard Assessment

To obtain a stay pending the filing and disposition of a petition for a writ of certiorari (or mandamus), an applicant must show (1) a reasonable probability that four Justices will consider the issue sufficiently meritorious to grant certiorari, (2) a fair prospect that a majority of the Court will vote to reverse the judgment below, and (3) a likelihood that irreparable harm will result from the denial of a stay. *Hollingsworth v. Perry*, 558 U.S. 183, 190 (2010) (per curiam).

The application fails at the threshold on both cert-worthiness and likelihood of reversal. Below, in *In re: Paul Schultz*, No. 26-2404 (4th Cir. Oct. 22, 2026) (unpublished per curiam), the Fourth Circuit denied Applicant Paul Schultz's petition for a writ of mandamus seeking to disqualify the district judge (Judge Leonie M. Brinkema) in his pending bankruptcy appeal (*Schultz v. Gold*, No. 1:26-cv-02804, E.D. Va., appealing orders in Adv. No. 26-01022, Bankr. E.D. Va.), to vacate the district court's order denying recusal, and to reassign his bankruptcy appeal to a district judge from another district, and denied his stay motions as moot. Because mandamus is a drastic remedy reserved for extraordinary circumstances where the petitioner has a clear and indisputable right to relief and no other adequate means to attain it (*Cheney v. United States District Court*, 542 U.S. 367, 380 (2004)), and because adverse judicial rulings in an ongoing bankruptcy proceeding do not ground mandatory recusal or Supreme Court intervention, there is no reasonable probability that four Justices would vote to grant review or that five Justices would vote to reverse.
