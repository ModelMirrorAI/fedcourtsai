## What happened

Arizona passed two 2022 laws meant to confirm that voters are U.S. citizens: one added citizenship-verification steps for removing people from voter rolls, the other required documentary proof of citizenship for some registrants. Nonprofit groups, including Promise Arizona, sued, arguing one law was passed for discriminatory reasons. After a trial, a federal district court found no discriminatory purpose. The Ninth Circuit Court of Appeals disagreed in part: it ruled that Promise Arizona had standing to sue even though it had not identified any specific member who was harmed, and it set aside the finding of no discriminatory intent, sending that question back for another look. Arizona and its Attorney General, along with other parties, separately asked the Supreme Court to review these rulings.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked whether a group can sue over a law by claiming some unnamed members might be affected, without identifying anyone actually harmed. It is also asked whether the appeals court, which is supposed to accept a trial judge's factual findings unless clearly wrong, instead improperly reexamined the evidence itself and reached its own conclusion that the law was discriminatory. Related petitions from other parties raise additional questions about voter-roll removal procedures, citizenship-documentation requirements, and mail-voting rules under federal election law, which the federal government's brief addresses separately.

## Where it stands

The petition has been fully briefed, including a reply from Arizona and a brief from the United States recommending that the Court deny review of this particular petition while granting review in a related case. The case has been distributed for the Court's conferences in June 2026, where the justices will decide whether to take it up.
