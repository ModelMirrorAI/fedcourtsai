## In brief

Arizona asks the Court to review a ruling that revived a discrimination claim against its voter-roll law, arguing the challenger lacked standing to sue.

## What happened

Arizona passed laws in 2022 to verify voters' citizenship, including a program removing noncitizens from voter rolls. Nonprofit groups, including Promise Arizona, sued, claiming the roll-removal law was enacted with discriminatory intent. After a trial, a federal district court found no discriminatory purpose. The Ninth Circuit Court of Appeals vacated that finding and sent the discrimination question back for reconsideration, after first ruling that Promise Arizona had standing to sue even though it had not identified any specific member harmed by the law. Arizona, joined by its Attorney General, petitioned for review, as did other parties in related cases.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked two things: first, whether an organization can sue in federal court based only on the possibility that some unidentified members might be harmed, without identifying any actual injured member; and second, whether the appeals court improperly reexamined and reweighed the trial court's factual findings on discriminatory intent, rather than simply checking them for obvious error.

## Where it stands

The petition has been distributed for conference twice, and the federal government, as respondent, filed a brief opposing it while supporting a related petition raising overlapping questions. Arizona filed a reply defending its petition, and the parties later filed supplemental briefs; the docket shows no decision yet on whether the Court will hear the case.

## What each outcome would mean

Arizona and its Attorney General, as petitioners, are asking the Court to overturn the Ninth Circuit's decision. If the Court grants review and ultimately rules for Arizona, the standing rule could be tightened and the discrimination claim against the roll-removal law could be cut off or sent back on different terms. If the Court denies review, the Ninth Circuit's decision stands, and the discrimination claim returns to the district court for further proceedings, as that court already ordered. The federal government's brief supports denial of Arizona's petition specifically, while favoring review of a different petition raising overlapping issues.
