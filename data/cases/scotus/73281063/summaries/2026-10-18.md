## In brief

Arizona asks the Court to review a ruling reviving a discrimination claim against its voter-roll law, challenging the plaintiff's standing and the appeals court's evidence review.

## What happened

After Arizona passed laws in 2022 to check voter rolls for noncitizens and require proof of citizenship, nonprofit groups Promise Arizona and Southwest Voter Registration Education Project sued, claiming the voter-roll law was passed with discriminatory intent. After a trial, the district court found no discriminatory purpose. A Ninth Circuit panel reversed that finding, ruling that Promise Arizona had standing to sue even though it had not identified any specific member harmed by the law, and that the trial court had not properly weighed all the evidence together. Judge Bumatay dissented, as did eleven judges when the full court declined to rehear the case.

## What the Court is being asked

Arizona asks the Court to decide two things: first, whether a group can sue in federal court based only on the possibility that some unnamed members might be harmed, without identifying anyone actually affected; and second, whether the appeals court overstepped its role by second-guessing the trial court's factual findings on discriminatory intent, rather than only checking them for clear error.

## Where it stands

The petition has been fully briefed, including a reply from Arizona and a brief from the United States recommending the Court deny this petition (while supporting a related one). The case was distributed for conferences in June 2026, and the docket shows no action since; a supplemental brief from Arizona was later filed in October 2026.

## What each outcome would mean

Arizona, as petitioner, is asking the Court to take up the case. If the Court grants review, it would hear the case and decide the standing and evidence-review questions itself, without yet determining who wins. If the Court denies review, the Ninth Circuit's decision reviving the discrimination claim stands, and the case would continue with further proceedings in the lower courts on whether the voter-roll law was discriminatory.
