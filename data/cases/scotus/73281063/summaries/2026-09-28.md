## In brief

Arizona asks the Court to review a Ninth Circuit ruling that revived a discrimination challenge to Arizona's noncitizen voter-roll law.

## What happened

Arizona passed laws in 2022 to verify voters' citizenship, including a program to remove noncitizens from voter rolls. Nonprofit groups Promise Arizona and Southwest Voter Registration Education Project sued, claiming the roll-removal law was passed with a discriminatory purpose. After a trial, a federal district court found no discriminatory purpose. A Ninth Circuit panel vacated that finding, ruling that Promise Arizona could sue on behalf of unnamed members who "may" be affected, and that the district court had improperly failed to weigh the evidence as a whole. Judge Bumatay dissented, and eleven judges dissented from rehearing the case with the full Ninth Circuit. Arizona and other parties then petitioned the Supreme Court.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked whether a group can sue on behalf of its members when it cannot identify any specific member who has been or will be harmed, only that some unidentified members might be affected. The Court is also asked whether the appeals court overstepped its role by second-guessing the trial court's factual finding of no discriminatory intent, rather than simply checking that finding for clear error.

## Where it stands

The petition has been distributed for conference twice, most recently for June 25, 2026, after the federal government filed a brief recommending the Court deny this petition while granting a related one filed by the Republican National Committee. The docket shows no action recorded since that distribution.

## What each outcome would mean

Arizona and its Attorney General, joined by state legislators, are asking the Court to take up the case; Promise Arizona and other groups won the revival of their claim below. If the Court grants review, it would hear the case in full and decide the standing and evidence-review questions, without yet deciding who wins. If it denies review, the Ninth Circuit's decision reviving the discrimination claim stands, and the case returns to the district court for further proceedings on that claim.
