## What happened

Arizona passed two 2022 laws meant to confirm that voters are United States citizens: one requiring proof of citizenship on the state registration form and for removing noncitizens from voter rolls, another restricting mail voting for certain registrants. Two nonprofits, Promise Arizona and Southwest Voter Registration Education Project, sued, arguing the voter-roll law was passed with a discriminatory purpose against Latino voters. After a trial, a federal district court found no discriminatory purpose. A Ninth Circuit panel disagreed, ruling that one nonprofit had standing to sue even without naming a member who was actually harmed, and that the trial court had not properly weighed all the evidence of discriminatory intent. Judge Bumatay dissented, as did eleven judges when the full court declined to rehear the case. Arizona and its Attorney General, joined by related petitions from Arizona legislators and the Republican National Committee, are asking the Supreme Court to review this ruling.

## What the Court is being asked

The case raises two questions. First, whether a group can sue in federal court on behalf of its members when it cannot identify any specific member who was actually or imminently harmed by the law, only that some unnamed members "may" be affected. Second, whether the appeals court exceeded its role by second-guessing the trial court's factual findings on discriminatory intent, rather than only checking those findings for clear error.

## Where it stands

The petition was distributed for conference twice, most recently June 25, 2026, after the government (as a respondent in related, consolidated cases) filed a brief opposing review and Arizona filed a reply; the docket shows no action recorded since that conference.
