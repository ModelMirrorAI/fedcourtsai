## In brief

Arizona asks the Court to overturn a Ninth Circuit ruling that revived a discrimination claim against a state law requiring citizenship checks for voter rolls.

## What happened

Arizona passed laws in 2022 requiring proof of citizenship to register and letting officials remove noncitizens from voter rolls. Two nonprofits, Promise Arizona and Southwest Voter Registration Education Project, sued, claiming the voter-roll law was enacted with discriminatory intent against Latino voters. After a trial, the district court found no discriminatory purpose. A Ninth Circuit panel reversed that finding and sent the case back for further review, over a dissent; the full Ninth Circuit later declined to rehear the case, with eleven judges dissenting. Arizona and its Attorney General then asked the Supreme Court to review that reversal. Two related petitions by other parties raise overlapping and additional questions about the same underlying case.

## What the Court is being asked

Arizona raises two questions. First, whether a group can sue on behalf of its members by claiming that an unknown number of unnamed members "may" be harmed, without identifying anyone actually affected. Second, whether the appeals court improperly substituted its own assessment of the evidence for the trial court's factual finding that the law was not enacted for a discriminatory reason, rather than limiting itself to checking for clear mistakes.

## Where it stands

The petition was distributed for conference twice, after full briefing including a brief from the federal government opposing Arizona's petition while supporting a related one, and several amicus briefs. The Court has not yet acted on the petition, and the petitioners have since filed a supplemental brief; no ruling on whether to hear the case appears in the docket.

## What each outcome would mean

Arizona and its Attorney General, as petitioners, are asking the Court to take up the case. If the Court grants review, it would hear full arguments and decide the standing and evidence-review questions itself, deciding nothing yet about who wins. If the Court denies review, the Ninth Circuit's decision reviving the discrimination claim stands, and the case returns to the district court for further proceedings on whether the voter-roll law was discriminatory, a result favoring Promise Arizona and the other challengers.
