The docket currently records one distribution (distributed on November 24, 2026, for the Conference of December 11, 2026) and no call for the views of the Solicitor General (CVSG). The petition sits on the December 11 Conference list alongside two companion Fifth Circuit Ten Commandments petitions: *Nathan v. Alamo Heights Independent School District*, No. 26-257, and *Brumley v. Roake*, No. 25-1360.

The Court will ultimately deny certiorari in *Dier*, though the petition is slightly more likely than not to record at least one additional distribution after December 11, 2026. If the Court gives close consideration to granting *Nathan* (or circulates a separate writing on the package before denying all three petitions), *Dier* will be relisted into January 2027—typically one to two additional conferences—alongside *Nathan* and *Brumley*. Alternatively, if the Court grants *Nathan* alone and holds *Dier* pending a merits decision in *Nathan*, *Dier* will be redistributed once more at a post-merits cleanup conference in June 2027 before being disposed of (denied if *Nathan* is affirmed on the merits, or GVR'd if *Nathan* is reversed). Only if the Court denies the entire three-case package outright out of the December 11 Conference will *Dier* resolve without another distribution.

The Court will not call for the views of the Solicitor General. The petition presents pure First and Fourteenth Amendment questions concerning a state classroom-display statute and implicates no federal statutory scheme, agency administration, or unique federal sovereign interest.

Conditional on the Court granting review in *Dier*, plenary review and a summary disposition (specifically, a post-hold grant, vacate, and remand) are nearly evenly balanced routes, with plenary review slightly likelier. An immediate summary per curiam reversal on the cert papers is exceedingly unlikely because neither the district court nor the Fifth Circuit adjudicated the Establishment Clause merits in this case. If the Court grants plenary review—most plausibly by pairing *Dier* with *Nathan* under Louisiana's conditional acquiescence to ensure an aggrieved petitioner and vigorous state representation on Louisiana's context-discretion statute—it would take both Question Presented 1 (pre-enforcement ripeness) and Question Presented 2 (the Establishment Clause facial challenge), likely consolidated or argued in tandem with *Nathan*, and reach the interplay between *Stone v. Graham*, *Van Orden v. Perry*, and *Kennedy v. Bremerton School District*. Conversely, if the Court grants *Nathan* alone, holds *Dier*, and later holds in *Nathan* that mandatory classroom Ten Commandments displays are facially unconstitutional, it will dispose of *Dier* by a summary GVR in light of *Nathan*.

Conditional on a denial of certiorari, the order disposing of *Dier* will most likely carry no noted dissent or separate statement. Any dissent from denial or statement respecting denial protesting the en banc Fifth Circuit's treatment of *Stone v. Graham* is substantially more likely to be filed solely on the *Nathan* docket (No. 26-257), where the Fifth Circuit actually reached the merits. There remains a modest chance that Justice Sotomayor or Justice Jackson (potentially joined by Justice Kagan) authors a dissent or statement captioned across the paired dockets, or that Justice Gorsuch, Justice Thomas, or Justice Kavanaugh writes briefly respecting the procedural posture.
