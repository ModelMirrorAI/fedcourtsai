### 2. Upward adjustments from the 5.2% / 2.7% baselines
Despite sitting in the `baseline` band with zero amicus briefs on its own docket, *Dier* (`26-256`) is not an isolated summary-affirmance petition. It is part of a synchronized three-case package scheduled for the **December 11, 2026 Conference** alongside *Nathan v. Alamo Heights Independent School District*, No. 26-257 (challenging Texas S.B. 10), and *Brumley v. Roake*, No. 25-1360 (Louisiana's conditional petition on H.B. 71, where the Court requested a response on July 28, 2026, and rescheduled from the December 4 Conference to align all three cases on December 11). Three structural features push `P(grant)` up to **0.14**:

1. **Vertical *stare decisis* pressure in *Nathan* (`26-257`)**: In *Nathan*, 173 F.4th 576 (5th Cir. 2026) (en banc), a closely divided en banc Fifth Circuit upheld Texas's public-school classroom Ten Commandments mandate on the merits and treated *Stone v. Graham*, 449 U.S. 39 (1980) (per curiam), as effectively abrogated by *Kennedy v. Bremerton School District*, 597 U.S. 507 (2022). Under *Rodriguez de Quijas v. Shearson/American Express, Inc.*, 490 U.S. 477, 484 (1989), and *Agostini v. Felton*, 521 U.S. 203, 237 (1997), lower courts lack authority to declare directly applicable Supreme Court precedent overruled. Backed by repeat Supreme Court counsel and three substantial scholar/advocacy amicus briefs, *Nathan* has roughly a ~32% chance of drawing four votes to grant.
2. **Louisiana's conditional acquiescence plus the Texas Attorney General transition**: Six days before filing the *Nathan* and *Dier* BIOs on November 10, 2026, Texas voters elected Democrat Nathan Johnson as Attorney General (taking office in January 2027). Both Texas's BIO in *Nathan* and Louisiana's BIO in *Dier* (`BIO 1, 7`) highlight that the incoming Texas Attorney General has announced he will not defend S.B. 10, potentially complicating adversarial presentation in *Nathan*. Meanwhile, Louisiana's Solicitor General vigorously defends H.B. 71 and **explicitly conditionally acquiesced in *Dier*** (`BIO 7`) should the Court grant *Nathan*. Moreover, Louisiana's own petition in *Brumley v. Roake* (`25-1360`) faces a serious prevailing-party / interlocutory obstacle under 28 U.S.C. § 1254(1) and Article III because Louisiana *won* vacatur of the preliminary injunction below, whereas Dier lost a final judgment and presents both ripeness (QP1) and the Establishment Clause merits (QP2). Conditional on *Nathan* being granted (~0.32), there is roughly a ~20% chance (~6.4 percentage points unconditionally) that the Court grants *Dier* alongside *Nathan* to keep a clean, adversarial Louisiana vehicle before the Court.
3. **The hold-and-GVR pathway**: Even if the Court grants *Nathan* alone (leaving Alamo Heights ISD and its co-respondent school districts to defend S.B. 10 through district-retained counsel) and **holds** *Dier*, a merits reversal in *Nathan* holding classroom Ten Commandments mandates facially unconstitutional would undercut the sole premise of *Roake*/*Dier*'s ripeness ruling—that classroom Ten Commandments displays are not categorically invalid and turn on local implementation context (`App. 16a–18a`). Because Dier is the losing party below, a plaintiff victory in *Nathan* would ordinarily produce a post-merits **GVR** in *Dier* (~6.5 percentage points unconditionally; plus ~1.5 percentage points for standalone plenary/GVR possibilities, summing to ~14.4% -> `0.14`).

### 3. Why denial (~86%) remains strongly favored
Two layers of headwinds keep `P(grant)` well below parity:

- **First, ~68% probability the Court denies the entire package this Term**:
  - *No circuit split and active percolation*: No other circuit has yet ruled on the new wave of classroom Ten Commandments laws. Appeals challenging Arkansas's Act 573 (*Stinson v. State of Arkansas*, Nos. 25-2713 & 26-1722) are actively pending in the Eighth Circuit, and H.B. 71 posters went up in Louisiana classrooms in August 2026 (`BIO 3–4`), allowing concrete post-implementation records (including *Roake* on remand) to develop without the facial pre-enforcement difficulties flagged in *Moody v. NetChoice, LLC*, 603 U.S. 707 (2024).
  - *Cross-cutting cert incentives and vehicle friction*: Six conservative Justices may see little urgency to disturb Fifth Circuit outcomes upholding Texas's law and vacating the injunction against Louisiana's while Eighth Circuit percolation is underway—especially after Texas's outgoing SG warned of mid-briefing representation upheaval in *Nathan*. Conversely, the three liberal Justices face classic defensive-denial risk (risking a 6–3 national precedent formally overruling *Stone v. Graham*) and cannot grant without a fourth vote.
- **Second, severe vehicle defects specific to *Dier* (`26-256`)**:
  - *Unaddressed Article III standing, traceability, and sovereign-immunity objections*: Dier sued only Governor Landry, AG Murrill, Superintendent Brumley, and state board members—**not** the local governing authority of Benjamin Franklin High School (`App. 5a–7a`). As Judge Guidry emphasized (`App. 19a–20a`), La. R.S. § 17:2124(B)(1) commands `"each public school governing authority"`—not teachers or state-level officials—to display the Commandments. Defendants raised standing and sovereign immunity under Rule 12(b)(1) below (`App. 7a`), which the district court bypassed by dismissing on ripeness.
  - *Adult employee plaintiff vs. captive schoolchildren*: Unlike *Stone*, *Roake*, and *Nathan* (brought by minor students under compulsory attendance laws and their parents), Dier is an adult public-school teacher whose classroom exposure and workplace duties implicate public-employee doctrine, which Judge Guidry held made his ripeness showing *weaker* than the *Roake* parents' (`App. 19a–20a`). Notably, the *Roake* respondents' BIO (`No. 25-1360`) actively urged the Court *against* using *Dier* as a vehicle for this exact reason.
  - *Thin petition, summary-affirmance posture, and post-implementation staleness*: Dier's 11-page petition failed to cite *Stone v. Graham* at all (citing only four cases total before adding *Stone* in the reply brief), drew zero amicus support, seeks review of a two-sentence summary affirmance procured on Dier's own unopposed motion after reversing his district-court position (`BIO 2–3`), and challenges a pre-implementation dismissal *without prejudice* (`App. 21a–22a`) even though displays are now up in Louisiana schools. And even if *Dier* is held pending a *Nathan* grant, *Dier* will still be **denied** at cleanup if the conservative majority affirms *Nathan* on the merits.
