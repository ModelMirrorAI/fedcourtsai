## In brief

A Louisiana teacher asks the Court to revive his lawsuit challenging a state law requiring Ten Commandments displays in every public school classroom.

## What happened

Louisiana's H.B. 71 requires every public school classroom to display a specific version of the Ten Commandments, with set minimum size and an accompanying state-written statement about their history in education. Christopher Dier, a public high school teacher, sued, arguing the law violates the Establishment Clause, the Free Exercise Clause, and the Free Speech Clause of the First Amendment. The district court dismissed his suit, holding his challenge was not yet ready for decision (not "ripe"), relying on a Fifth Circuit decision in a related case, Roake v. Brumley, involving parents and students. The Fifth Circuit summarily affirmed that dismissal. A related Fifth Circuit decision, Nathan v. Alamo Heights, upheld a similar Texas law on the merits.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked whether Dier's challenge was ready for a court to decide, given that the law leaves some implementation details to local school officials, and whether the law's mandated classroom displays violate the constitutional separation of church and state.

## Where it stands

The petition was distributed for the Court's conference of December 11, 2026, after the respondents filed their brief opposing review and the petitioner filed a reply. The docket shows no action since that conference.

## What each outcome would mean

Dier, who lost in the lower courts, is asking the Court to take up his case and reverse. If the Court grants review, it would hear full arguments on whether his claim was ripe and whether the law is constitutional, deciding nothing yet. If it denies review, the Fifth Circuit's ruling against Dier stands, leaving his suit dismissed. Louisiana's brief states it opposes review unless the Court also takes up the companion Texas case, Nathan, in which case it would not oppose.
