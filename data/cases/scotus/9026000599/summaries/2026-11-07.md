## What happened

The National Trust for Historic Preservation sued the National Park Service and other federal officials to stop construction of a new East Wing, after the existing structure was demolished in late 2025. A district court preliminarily enjoined above-ground construction, while allowing below-ground and safety-related work to continue. The D.C. Circuit affirmed that injunction. The government then asked the Supreme Court to pause the injunction while it sought review, and the Court granted that stay in August 2026, finding the government likely to succeed on the question of whether the Trust had legal standing to sue. The government has now filed a petition asking the Court to take up the case.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is being asked whether the lower court was wrong to block the ballroom construction. That question includes whether the organization suing had a sufficient legal stake to bring the case at all, whether federal law authorized the project, and whether the harms to the government from pausing construction outweighed the harm to the organization's member who objected to the building's design.

## Where it stands

## What each outcome would mean

The National Park Service and other federal petitioners are asking the Court to reverse the lower court's ruling. If the Court grants the petition, it would hear full arguments and decide the legal questions itself; granting review alone would not decide who wins. If the Court denies the petition, the D.C.
