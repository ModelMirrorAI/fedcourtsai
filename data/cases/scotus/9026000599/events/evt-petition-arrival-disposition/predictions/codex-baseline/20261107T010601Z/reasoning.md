## Why this petition is exceptional

The stay majority found the government likely to prevail on Article III standing and found the balance of harms strongly in its favor. Its order expressly terminates the stay if certiorari is denied (App. 9a). Denial without another procedural solution would therefore restore an injunction that the same majority has just found unsupported. A prior stay is provisional, not a promise of certiorari, but a reasoned five-Justice disposition on both standing and equitable relief is far stronger evidence than the caption or political prominence alone.

The November 5 petition asks for summary reversal, alternatively a GVR, and only alternatively plenary review (petition pp. 2–3, 10–12, 26). The retained August opinion supplies a ready explanation on standing and equities without resolving the disputed statutory authorization. I consequently assign **0.90 conditional on a grant** to disposition in the cert order itself. Summary reversal is the modal label; a GVR relying on the earlier opinion remains a substantial alternative. The 0.94 grant probability includes both routes and does not imply a 94% probability of summary reversal specifically.

There is a serious contrary argument, not merely an offended observer caricature. The matching D.C. Circuit decision, dated August 7 in No. 26-5123, describes regular visits to President's Park, professional study of the White House, and loss of a particular historic view (opinion pp. 30–41). Roberts's stay dissent treats that as concrete aesthetic injury and disputes statutory authorization (App. 10a–16a). Those materials are why I do not treat an emergency ruling as a final adjudication. Nonetheless, the five-Justice stay majority considered and rejected this very standing distinction on the provisional record. That makes the narrow standing/equities route more likely than reopening every statutory question.

The lower-court record matches the petition's court, docket, date, parties, and injunction. It identifies Judges Millett and Garcia as the majority and Judge Rao as dissenting. The provisioned docket summary reports no rehearing petition found in its six reviewed postjudgment entries; I do not turn that limited search into an assertion about unserved proceedings. The petition is complete in its advocacy section but its appendix is selectively truncated; `decision-below.txt` is also truncated. I rely on the retained stay opinions and the readable lower-court standing discussion, not on omitted pages. No brief in opposition is provisioned or yet shown filed, so there is no response whose substance I can weigh. The same-question file has no matches above its threshold; that does not prove there is no related litigation or circuit disagreement.

## Procedural claims, uncertainty, and stakes

At zero distributions, the literal `relist-increment` claim means **at least one future distribution**, including the first one. I assign 0.995, not a probability of a second conference. My central trajectory is two or three total distributions: initial consideration followed by roughly one or two genuine relists while a summary order and separate writing are prepared. A CVSG is exceptionally unlikely (0.001) because the Solicitor General already presents the federal government's position as petitioner's counsel.

The paid-segment terminal relist cut has 10,204 resolved petitions in bucket 0, 2,532 in bucket 1, 495 in bucket 2, and 482 in bucket 3+. Their combined grant-family percentages are approximately 1.8%, 13.5%, 41.2%, and 37.1%, respectively. These describe where petitions ended, not the forward hazard from this case's zero-distribution state. The CVSG cut has 164 resolved invited-view cases (34.8% grant family) versus 13,549 without one (6.4%); it is not an instruction to invite the government to advise on its own petition. The terminal amicus buckets contain 11,109 resolved cases with zero briefs, 1,158 with one, and 1,446 with two or more. No current amicus evidence justifies importing the higher-support buckets; filings can still accrue after arrival.

The 0.35 dissent-from-denial forecast is conditional on the unlikely denial branch. The separate `order-writers` probabilities are unconditional and cover writings on grants as well: Roberts's 0.60 chiefly reflects a possible dissent from summary relief, not a 60% chance of dissent from denial. A Justice joining his writing is not counted as an author. I do not predict a cert-stage majority-opinion author.

The remaining 6% nongrant mass allows for provisional views changing, further procedural developments, and construction or remedial changes producing dismissal or denial rather than the expected grant. Plenary treatment is also possible because the standing dispute is substantive and the injunction interlocutory. These uncertainties limit confidence in the exact route more than in whether the Court intervenes.

The **0.90 stakes score** concerns the allocation of authority over White House construction, congressional spending/property powers, and aesthetic standing. It is not a second grant probability and does not assume a sweeping statutory holding.
