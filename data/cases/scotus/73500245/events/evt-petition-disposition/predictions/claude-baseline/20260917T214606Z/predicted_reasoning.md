## CVSG

No call for the views of the Solicitor General. The dispute is between a private attorney and a state bar over a state fee-dispute statute; no federal program, statute, or agency interest is implicated, and the Court does not invite the SG on a petition of this kind.

## Which question, if granted

The petition presents one question: whether the Alabama Supreme Court's reading of Ala. Code § 34-3-62, and its application to the petitioner, violated Fourteenth Amendment procedural due process (a fair-notice theory drawn from *Marks v. United States*). If the Court took anything, it would have to reformulate the question, because as written it asks the Court to review a state court's interpretation of a state statute, which is not a federal question. The only federal question available is whether due process barred discipline for conduct the petitioner claims the statute told him was safe. I forecast the Court takes none of it.

## Summary disposition versus plenary review

Conditional on any grant, a summary route is likelier than plenary review: there is no circuit or state-court split to resolve and no question of general importance, so the only realistic grant path is a summary per curiam correcting a clear constitutional error, or a GVR should an intervening decision on fair-notice due process in professional discipline arrive between now and disposition. Neither is expected; the conditional claim is stated because the harness declares it.

## Dissent from denial

None. No Justice writes on this denial. The petition raises no issue any current Justice has flagged as needing the Court's attention, no amici filed, and the respondent did not even respond.
