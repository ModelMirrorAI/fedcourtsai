## In brief

The Supreme Court declined to review an Alabama lawyer's challenge to his law license suspension over a fee dispute.

## What happened

Jerry Blevins, an Alabama attorney, had a fee dispute with a client in 2017 and filed a declaratory judgment action under a state law governing disputed attorney fees. The circuit court resolved the fee dispute in 2018. In 2022 the Alabama State Bar brought disciplinary charges against Blevins over the same fee dispute. A disciplinary board found him guilty of failing to communicate and charging an excessive fee, and suspended his law license for six months. Blevins argued that because he had relied on the state fee-dispute law, disciplining him violated his right to fair process. The Alabama Supreme Court rejected that argument and upheld the discipline, then denied rehearing.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court was asked whether the Alabama Supreme Court's reading of the state fee-dispute statute, and the way that reading was applied to Blevins, deprived him of fair notice and a fair process before his law license was suspended, as the Constitution's guarantee of due process requires.

## Where it stands

The Alabama State Bar waived its right to respond to the petition. The Court distributed the case for its September 28, 2026 conference, and on October 5, 2026, the Court denied the petition.

## What each outcome would mean

Blevins, as petitioner, asked the Court to take up his case and overturn the Alabama Supreme Court's ruling. Denial, which is what occurred, leaves the Alabama Supreme Court's decision in place: Blevins's six-month suspension and public reprimand stand, and the Alabama State Bar's position prevails. The denial does not mean the Supreme Court agreed that the lower court's interpretation of the statute was correct; it simply leaves that ruling undisturbed without deciding the constitutional question Blevins raised.
