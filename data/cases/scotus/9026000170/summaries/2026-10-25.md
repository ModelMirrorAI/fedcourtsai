## In brief

FedEx asks the Court to decide whether a law barring appeals of patent review decisions also blocks review of a dispute over an unnamed interested party.

## What happened

Qualcomm filed petitions at the Patent Office challenging FedEx's patent, without naming Roambee, a company Qualcomm had discussed investing in, as a party with a stake in the case. FedEx argued the omission violated a rule requiring petitions to name all such parties. The Patent Office's board declined to resolve the dispute, reasoning it would not affect the outcome, and later found FedEx's patent claims invalid. FedEx appealed, but the Federal Circuit ruled that a different law, which makes the board's decision to take up a case final and unappealable, also barred review of this dispute. The Federal Circuit did, separately, send part of the case back over an unrelated error.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked whether the law making the Patent Office's decision to take up a patent challenge unappealable also prevents courts from reviewing a claim that the Office skipped a required step in identifying all interested parties before proceeding, even though the Office later admitted it should have resolved that question.

## Where it stands

The petition has been distributed for conference, and the Court asked for a response, which Qualcomm filed opposing review; the government, also a respondent, waived its right to respond. The Solicitor General separately requested, and was granted, an extension of time to respond, to October 28, 2026. The docket shows no decision yet on whether the Court will hear the case.

## What each outcome would mean

FedEx, which lost in the lower court, is asking the Court to take up the case. If the Court grants review, it will decide the legal question itself, without yet indicating who wins. If it denies review, the Federal Circuit's decision stands, meaning FedEx's challenge to how the Patent Office handled the ownership-interest dispute remains unreviewed and Qualcomm's win at the Patent Office is unaffected.
