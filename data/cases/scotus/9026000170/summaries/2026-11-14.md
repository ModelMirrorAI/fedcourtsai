## In brief

FedEx asks the Court to decide whether a law shielding patent agency decisions from court review also bars review of a skipped statutory check.

## What happened

Qualcomm filed a patent office challenge against a FedEx patent, without naming a company called Roambee as a co-interested party, even though Qualcomm had ties to Roambee. FedEx argued the omission violated a legal requirement, but the patent board declined to resolve the dispute, reasoning it would not affect the case's outcome, and later ruled the challenged patent claims invalid. FedEx appealed, and the Federal Circuit held that a different law barring review of decisions on whether to open these proceedings also blocked review of this argument. The agency itself later agreed its approach had been wrong, but still argued courts could not review it.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked whether the law making the patent agency's decision to open a review "final and unreviewable" also blocks a court from reviewing a claim that the agency skipped a required check on who has a stake in the case, where the agency itself now says it should have made that determination.

## Where it stands

The petition has been fully briefed, including responses from Qualcomm and the federal government and a reply from FedEx; the case was distributed for a conference and the government was asked to respond. The docket shows no decision yet on whether the Court will hear the case.

## What each outcome would mean

FedEx, which lost in the lower court, is asking the Court to take up the case. If the Court grants review, it would hear full arguments and decide whether this kind of challenge can be reviewed by courts at all; a grant would not itself decide who wins. If the Court denies review, the Federal Circuit's ruling stands, leaving in place its conclusion that FedEx's argument cannot be reviewed and leaving Qualcomm's win intact.
