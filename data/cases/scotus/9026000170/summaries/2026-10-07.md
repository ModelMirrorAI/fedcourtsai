## In brief

FedEx asks the Court to decide whether a law barring appeals of patent review decisions also blocks review of a disputed disclosure requirement.

## What happened

Qualcomm filed petitions at the Patent Office challenging a FedEx patent. FedEx argued Qualcomm's petition should have named Roambee, a company Qualcomm had invested in, as a real party in interest, which the law requires petitions to disclose. The Patent Office declined to resolve that dispute, instituted review anyway, and later found FedEx's patent claims unpatentable. FedEx appealed, but the Federal Circuit ruled that a separate law makes the Patent Office's institution-related decisions unappealable, and held this barred review of the real-party dispute too. The agency, while now agreeing FedEx's legal argument was correct, maintained that review was still barred.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked whether the law making the Patent Office's decision to start a patent review final and unappealable also prevents courts from reviewing the Office's refusal to decide a related, required disclosure question before proceeding, when the agency itself now agrees it misapplied the law.

## Where it stands

The petition was distributed for the Court's September 28, 2026 conference; the Court then requested a response, which Qualcomm filed along with an amicus brief from VLSI Technology, and the response deadline was later extended to October 28, 2026. The docket shows no decision yet on whether the Court will hear the case.

## What each outcome would mean

FedEx, which lost in the lower court, is asking the Court to take up the case. If the Court grants review, it will decide the legal question itself, without yet determining who wins. If it denies review, the Federal Circuit's ruling stands, leaving Qualcomm's position intact and FedEx unable to pursue its real-party-in-interest challenge in that court. A denial would not mean the Court agrees the lower court was right, only that the decision remains in place.
