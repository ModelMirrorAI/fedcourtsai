## In brief

FedEx asks the Court to review whether a law barring appeals of patent-office institution decisions also blocks review of disputes over unnamed interested parties.

## What happened

FedEx owns a patent that Qualcomm challenged through inter partes review, a Patent Office process for reconsidering an issued patent's validity. FedEx argued Qualcomm's petition should have named another company, Roambee, as an interested party, which could affect later estoppel rules. The Patent Office Board, applying a policy it has since dropped, declined to resolve that dispute and went on to find most of FedEx's patent claims unpatentable. FedEx appealed, but the Federal Circuit ruled that a federal law making institution decisions "final and nonappealable" also barred review of this dispute, though it sent back a separate obviousness issue on unrelated grounds.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked whether the law shielding the Patent Office's decision to begin a patent review from court review also prevents courts from examining the Office's refusal to decide whether a petition properly identified all interested parties, a requirement separate from the decision to institute review itself.

## Where it stands

The petition has been distributed for conference more than once, most recently for December 4, 2026, after full briefing including responses from both Qualcomm and the federal government and a reply from FedEx. The docket does not yet show any decision on the petition.

## What each outcome would mean

FedEx, which lost this issue at the Federal Circuit, is asking the Court to take up and reverse that ruling. If the Court grants review, it would hear full arguments and decide whether such disputes can be reviewed by courts, deciding the issue for neither side in advance. If the Court denies review, the Federal Circuit's ruling stands, leaving Qualcomm's institution of the patent review intact and FedEx unable to pursue its challenge over the unnamed party in this case.
