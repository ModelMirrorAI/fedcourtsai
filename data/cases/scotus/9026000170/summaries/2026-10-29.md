## In brief

FedEx asks the Court to decide whether a law blocking appeals of patent review decisions also blocks review of a separate legal requirement it claims was ignored.

## What happened

Qualcomm filed a petition with the Patent Office to challenge a FedEx patent, without naming a company called Roambee as a co-interested party, even though FedEx was suing Roambee over the same patent. FedEx argued Roambee should have been named and that the case should not proceed. The Patent Office, applying a policy it has since dropped, allowed the case to go forward without resolving that dispute, and ultimately ruled the patent's challenged claims invalid. FedEx appealed, but the Federal Circuit held that a law making institution decisions "final and nonappealable" also barred review of this naming dispute, though it sent back a narrower issue involving three patent claims.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked whether the law that bars appeals of the Patent Office's decision to open a patent review also bars appeals of a claim that the Office skipped a required check on who has an interest in the case, where that check was never actually performed.

## Where it stands

The petition has been fully briefed: both Qualcomm and the federal government filed briefs opposing review, and FedEx's reply, if any, is not reflected in the docket. The case has not yet been distributed for a new conference following the completion of briefing on October 28, 2026.

## What each outcome would mean

FedEx, which lost in the Federal Circuit, is asking the Court to take up the case and reverse that ruling. If the Court grants review, it would hear full arguments on whether this kind of claim can be appealed at all, without yet deciding who is right. If the Court denies review, the Federal Circuit's decision stands, meaning FedEx's challenge remains barred and the Patent Office's unpatentability ruling against FedEx's claims stays in place, while the unrelated three-claim remand proceeds separately.
