## In brief

FedEx asks the Court to decide whether a patent dispute involving Qualcomm can be reviewed in court despite a law that limits appeals of patent review decisions.

## What happened

Qualcomm filed petitions at the Patent Office challenging FedEx's patent, without naming a company called Roambee as a real party in interest, even though FedEx was separately suing Roambee over the same patent. FedEx argued Qualcomm should have named Roambee and that the Patent Office was wrong not to resolve the dispute before proceeding. The Patent Office instituted review anyway and later found FedEx's patent claims unpatentable. FedEx appealed to the Federal Circuit, which ruled that a federal law limiting review of decisions to start patent review proceedings barred it from hearing FedEx's challenge. The Federal Circuit did send part of the case back on an unrelated point.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked whether a law making the Patent Office's decision to start a patent review final and unreviewable also blocks courts from reviewing a related claim: that the Office wrongly refused to decide whether the company challenging the patent had identified everyone with a stake in the case, as another part of the same law requires.

## Where it stands

The petition has been distributed for conference, and the Court asked for a response from Qualcomm, which has since filed a brief opposing review. The Solicitor General, representing the United States, waived its right to respond and was later granted more time; the docket does not yet show the Court's decision on whether to hear the case.

## What each outcome would mean

FedEx, which lost at the Federal Circuit, is asking the Court to hear the case. If the Court grants review, it would hear full arguments and decide whether FedEx's challenge can be reviewed in court, without yet deciding who is right. If the Court denies review, the Federal Circuit's ruling stands, leaving in place its conclusion that FedEx's challenge is barred from review and that the Patent Office's unpatentability finding against FedEx's claims remains undisturbed on this issue.
