# Forecast of the Court's action

The Court will deny the petition after obtaining the requested response, without a separate writing. The response request is a reason to inspect the petition's distinction between refusing to decide a statutory prerequisite and deciding that prerequisite incorrectly, but it will not produce four votes for review.

The snapshot records one distribution, on August 26, 2026, for the September 28 conference, followed by the August 28 response request. I expect one additional distribution after response processing, with zero or one genuine post-consideration relists before disposition. The probability of any additional distribution is 0.98; that includes a rescheduling necessitated by the response, not just a substantive relist. The likely timetable is an autumn 2026 order after briefing, with extensions capable of pushing it later. I do not forecast a separate call for the Solicitor General's views: the United States is already a respondent and can give its position in the requested briefing. P(new CVSG) is 0.005.

If the Court grants, it will most likely take the petition's single question substantially as presented: whether section 314(d) bars review of the Patent Office's refusal to determine compliance with section 312(a)(2)'s real-party-in-interest disclosure requirement. The issue would be the availability of judicial review, not a Supreme Court finding that Roambee actually was a real party in interest. Conditional on a grant, plenary briefing and argument are more likely than a summary order; P(summary disposition | grant) is 0.20. The supplied record identifies no intervening Supreme Court decision requiring a routine grant-vacate-remand order.

Conditional on denial, P(a noted dissent or statement respecting denial) is 0.06. No writing is the central forecast; Gorsuch, followed by Thomas, is the most plausible writer in the small separate-writing tail because the petition emphasizes judicial control of agency authority. These are authorship possibilities, not predictions of the Court's unreported certiorari votes. If review is granted, the likely contested ground is whether the disclosure obligation is inseparable from the unreviewable institution decision or instead enforceable as a limit on how the proceeding is conducted.
