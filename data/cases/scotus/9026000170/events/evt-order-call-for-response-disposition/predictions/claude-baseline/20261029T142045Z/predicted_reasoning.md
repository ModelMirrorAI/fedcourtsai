# Forecast — Federal Express Corp. v. Qualcomm Inc., No. 26-170

**Call: certiorari denied.**

**Posture forecast from.** One distribution (for the 9/28/2026 conference), no
relist, no CVSG; both respondents (Qualcomm and the United States) waived, and
the Court called for a response on Aug 28, 2026, due Sep 28. The call pulls the
petition off that conference.

- **Further distribution.** Near certain: once the responses are in (or the
  time runs out, after any extension the SG takes), the petition is distributed
  again for a later conference — likely November or December 2026. Expect it to
  be decided at that next conference without a relist; a relist past it is
  unlikely.
- **CVSG.** None. The United States is a respondent and will file its own
  brief in opposition, so there is nothing to invite.
- **Expected response.** The Director concedes § 312(a)(2) was violated but
  maintains § 314(d) bars review; the government and Qualcomm will argue the
  question is controlled by *Thryv v. Click-To-Call* (the § 315(b) time bar is
  "closely related" to institution and unreviewable), that the Office has
  already changed its prospective policy, and that the issue therefore has
  little continuing importance. That combination — a precedential Federal
  Circuit rule with no possible split and an agency fix going forward — is the
  standard recipe for denial.
- **If granted.** The Court would take the single QP as written: whether
  § 314(d) bars review of the Board's refusal to resolve a real-party-in-interest
  dispute under § 312(a)(2), framed around whether *Cuozzo*'s "shenanigans" /
  statutory-limits carve-out and *SAS* reach a § 312 requirement after *Thryv*.
  Plenary review would be the route; a summary disposition is unlikely
  because no intervening decision bears on the question.
- **Separate writing.** Most likely none. If anyone writes, the likeliest is
  Justice Gorsuch, who dissented in *Thryv* and wrote in *Cuozzo* and *SAS* on
  the limits of § 314(d).
