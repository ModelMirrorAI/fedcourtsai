The Court will deny the petition for a writ of certiorari after the briefs in opposition are filed.

**Further distributions and relists.** The docket at the call-for-response moment shows a single distribution (on August 26, 2026, for the Long Conference of September 28, 2026), followed two days later by the Court's request for a response due September 28, 2026. Because the call for a response resets the briefing schedule after both respondents (the United States and Qualcomm Incorporated) initially waived their right to respond, the petition will be redistributed at least once more once the responses arrive (or after any extensions). After that post-response distribution, the petition is unlikely to draw multiple conference relists, though a single relist to allow chambers to examine the interplay between 35 U.S.C. § 312(a)(2), § 314(d), and the USPTO Director's intervening policy reversal is a live possibility before denial.

**Call for the Views of the Solicitor General (CVSG).** The Court will not issue a CVSG. The Director of the United States Patent and Trademark Office intervened in the Federal Circuit appeal and the United States is already a named respondent represented by the Solicitor General on the Supreme Court docket. Because the Court's August 28, 2026 order calling for a response already requires or invites the federal respondent's brief in opposition directly as a party, a separate CVSG order will not issue.

**Question presented if granted.** If the Court were to grant certiorari, it would take the single question presented in the petition as framed: whether 35 U.S.C. § 314(d) bars judicial review of the Patent Office's refusal to determine whether an inter partes review petition identifies all real parties in interest under 35 U.S.C. § 312(a)(2). Conditionally on a grant, the merits dispute would center on whether a complete refusal to adjudicate a disputed § 312(a)(2) real-party-in-interest objection is an ultra vires agency action reviewable under *SAS Institute Inc. v. Iancu*, 584 U.S. 357 (2018), or an unreviewable precondition to institution governed by *Cuozzo Speed Technologies, LLC v. Lee*, 579 U.S. 261 (2016), and *Thryv, Inc. v. Click-To-Call Technologies, LP*, 590 U.S. 45 (2020).

**Summary disposition vs. plenary review.** Conditional on a grant, the Court is strongly expected to set the case for plenary briefing and oral argument rather than disposing of it summarily in the cert order. The Federal Circuit's precedential holding rests on its reading of *Cuozzo* and *Thryv*, and the Solicitor General continues to defend the § 314(d) appealability bar even after the USPTO Director disavowed the Board's underlying practice prospectively. There is no intervening Supreme Court decision that would trigger a routine grant, vacate, and remand (GVR), and summary reversal over the opposition of both Qualcomm and the Solicitor General on a statutory patent-reviewability question is unlikely.

**Dissents or statements respecting denial.** Upon denial of certiorari, the order will most likely issue without any noted dissent or separate statement. However, there is a modest chance (around 7%) that Justice Gorsuch—potentially joined by Justice Thomas, Justice Alito, or Justice Sotomayor, given the alignments in *SAS Institute* and *Thryv*—writes a brief dissent from denial or statement respecting denial highlighting the tension between § 314(d)'s institution bar and an agency's admitted refusal to enforce a mandatory statutory prerequisite under § 312(a)(2).
