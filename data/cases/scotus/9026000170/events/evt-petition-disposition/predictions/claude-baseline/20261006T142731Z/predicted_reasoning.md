# Forecast: what the Court will do with No. 26-170, Federal Express Corp. v. Qualcomm Inc., et al.

**Disposition: the petition will be denied.** P(any grant) = 0.08.

**Relists.** The docket shows **one distribution** (Aug 26, 2026, for the Sept 28 conference). The Court then called for a response (Aug 28) after both respondents had waived, and on Sept 29 extended the Solicitor General's time to respond to Oct 28, 2026, "for all respondents." The petition will therefore be **redistributed at least once more** after the government's brief in opposition and FedEx's reply land: expect a new distribution in mid-to-late November for a conference in the first half of December 2026. I expect the petition to be decided at that conference or after at most one further relist; two or more additional relists beyond the redistribution are unlikely (roughly one in five). The only route to no further distribution is a dismissal under Rule 46 (settlement), which I put at a few percent.

**CVSG.** None, because none is possible in the ordinary sense: the United States (the PTO Director) is already a respondent and has been directed to file a response. The Director's position will arrive as a brief in opposition by Oct 28, not as an invited amicus brief.

**The government's response.** The Solicitor General will oppose certiorari. The Director has conceded since the Federal Circuit argument that the Board should have decided the real-party-in-interest (RPI) dispute, but has consistently maintained that § 314(d) bars review of that failure, and the SG opposed the closely related Dolby v. Unified Patents petition (No. 25-1011) this spring. Expect the BIO to argue (i) that Cuozzo and Thryv squarely cover a § 312(a)(2) challenge raised in opposition to institution, (ii) that the rescinded Board policy makes the question non-recurring, and (iii) that FedEx's relief would only unwind institution. A confession of error with a suggestion of vacatur is a low-probability variant (under one in ten).

**Which question the Court would take, if it takes one.** The QP as written is a single clean reviewability question, and the Court would take it essentially as framed, perhaps tightened to "whether § 314(d) bars review of the Board's refusal to decide a § 312(a)(2) real-party-in-interest challenge before issuing a final written decision." The merits of who is an RPI would not be before the Court.

**Summary disposition versus plenary review.** Conditional on a grant, plenary review is the likelier route (about 70%): the question is the scope of § 314(d), which the Court has taken three times (Cuozzo, SAS, Thryv) and always with argument. The summary route (about 30% of grants) runs through the government: if the SG's response concedes that the Federal Circuit's "boils down to institution" reasoning is overbroad, a GVR for reconsideration in light of the government's position becomes plausible. No intervening decision of this Court supplies a GVR vehicle on its own.

**If granted, the likely ground.** A grant would signal a majority ready to confine Cuozzo and Thryv to challenges that were actually decided at institution, holding that an agency's refusal to make a determination the statute makes a condition of considering the petition is reviewable under Cuozzo's "outside its statutory limits" reservation. The Director's concession of statutory error would be the opinion's center of gravity.

**Dissent from or statement respecting denial.** Conditional on a denial, I expect no separate writing most of the time (about 88%). The one Justice likely to write is Justice Gorsuch, who dissented from the reviewability holding in Thryv and would be drawn to an agency "admits it violated the statute, yet no court may say so" framing; a short statement respecting the denial noting that the Office has since changed its policy is the likelier form if anything is written.
