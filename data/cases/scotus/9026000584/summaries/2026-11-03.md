## In brief

Virginia's DMV commissioner asks the Court to review a ruling that treats personalized license plate messages as private speech protected by the First Amendment.

## What happened

Curtis Whateley got a Virginia personalized license plate reading "FTP&ATF," meaning "Fuck the Police & Alcohol, Tobacco, and Firearms." After a complaint, the DMV recalled the plate as vulgar and potentially promoting violence. Whateley sued, claiming the recall violated his free speech rights. The district court dismissed his suit, ruling that personalized plates are government speech, which is not subject to ordinary First Amendment limits. The Fourth Circuit disagreed, holding that the personalized character combinations are private speech, and sent the case back for further proceedings on whether the recall was constitutional under the rules that apply to private speech on government property.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked whether personalized messages that drivers choose for their license plates count as the government's own speech, which the state can freely control, or as private speech by the driver, which the government may regulate only within First Amendment limits. The petition says courts around the country disagree on this question.

## Where it stands

The commissioner filed a petition asking the Court to take up the case on October 29, 2026. The respondent's response is due December 3, 2026, and the Court has not yet acted on the petition.

## What each outcome would mean

Virginia's commissioner, who lost at the Fourth Circuit, is asking the Court to take the case and reverse that ruling. If the Court grants the petition, it would hear full arguments and decide whether Virginia's personalized plates are government speech, deciding nothing yet. If it denies the petition, the Fourth Circuit's ruling stands, meaning Whateley's claim proceeds in the lower court under private-speech rules, and Virginia's recall of his plate would face First Amendment scrutiny.
