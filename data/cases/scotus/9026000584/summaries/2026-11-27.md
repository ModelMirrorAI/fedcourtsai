## In brief

Virginia's DMV commissioner asks the Court to review a ruling that personalized license plate messages are private speech protected by the First Amendment.

## What happened

Curtis Whateley requested a Virginia vanity plate reading "FTP&ATF," meaning "Fuck the Police & Alcohol, Tobacco, and Firearms." The DMV issued it, then recalled it after a complaint, citing guidelines against vulgar or violence-condoning messages. Whateley sued the DMV Commissioner, arguing the recall violated his First Amendment rights. The district court dismissed his suit, ruling that personalized plate messages are government speech, which is not subject to ordinary free speech limits. The Fourth Circuit reversed, holding that the character combinations on Virginia's personalized plates are private speech, and sent the case back for further proceedings on whether the DMV's restrictions are constitutional under rules governing government-controlled forums for private speech.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked whether a state can limit what drivers put on personalized license plates without running afoul of free speech protections, or whether those personalized character combinations count instead as the government's own speech, which the government may control as it wishes.

## Where it stands

The petition was distributed for the Court's December 11, 2026 conference. The respondent waived his right to file a response. No action on the petition has yet been recorded.

## What each outcome would mean

Virginia's DMV Commissioner is asking the Court to take up the case and reverse the Fourth Circuit's ruling. If the Court grants the petition, it would hear the case and decide whether personalized plate messages are government or private speech, without that grant deciding who wins. If the Court denies the petition, the Fourth Circuit's ruling that Whateley's plate message is private speech stands, and his case returns to the lower courts to determine whether Virginia's restrictions meet First Amendment standards.
