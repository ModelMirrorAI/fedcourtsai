# Forecast — No. 26-584, Jack v. Whateley

The petition has been distributed once (for the December 11, 2026 conference) on a waived response. The Court will deny certiorari.

- **Path.** Most likely the petition is denied outright on the order list following the December 11 conference (about 60%). The principal alternative (roughly one in three) is that a Justice calls for a response, the ACLU of Virginia files a brief in opposition in early 2027 stressing the interlocutory posture and the pending Seventh Circuit case, the petition is redistributed once, and it is then denied in the February–April 2027 window. A true relist after full briefing is unlikely; more than one further distribution is improbable.
- **CVSG.** None. No federal interest is implicated.
- **Question presented.** If the Court took the case, it would take the single question essentially as written, narrowed in substance to whether the owner-selected character combinations on personalized plates are government speech under *Walker* and *Shurtleff*. If granted, the more likely result is affirmance of the Fourth Circuit's private-speech holding.
- **Summary disposition.** Not the likely route. There is no intervening decision to support a GVR and the published, unanimous opinion below is not a summary-reversal candidate.
- **Separate writing.** A denial will issue without any noted dissent or statement. If one appeared, it would most plausibly come from Justice Thomas or Justice Alito.
- **Why denial.** The Fourth Circuit's judgment is interlocutory (remanded for forum analysis), the Court denied the same question in *Gilliam v. Gerregano* (No. 25-107) in December 2025, the Seventh Circuit is about to weigh in, and no amicus supports the State.
