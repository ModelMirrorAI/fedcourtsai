# Predicted Court Action: *Jack v. Whateley*, No. 26-584

The Court will deny the petition for a writ of certiorari.

## Conference Trajectory and Further Distributions

The petition has been distributed once so far — on November 24, 2026, for the December 11, 2026 Conference — following respondent's November 20, 2026 waiver of the right to respond. The slightly likelier procedural path is an outright denial on the December 14, 2026 order list without further action. There is, however, a substantial secondary chance that the Court calls for a response before acting, given a State Solicitor General petition presenting an acknowledged split between a published Fourth Circuit opinion and two state courts of last resort (*Commissioner of Indiana Bureau of Motor Vehicles v. Vawter* and *Gilliam v. Gerregano*). If the Court calls for a response, the petition will be distributed once more (or twice at most if briefly relisted) after the brief in opposition arrives in early 2027, and the Court will then deny review once the interlocutory Rule 12(b)(6) posture and ongoing Seventh Circuit litigation (*M J Nichols Co. v. Boardman*) are placed squarely before the Conference.

## Views of the Solicitor General (CVSG)

The Court will not call for the views of the Solicitor General. Personalized license-plate issuance and recall under state motor-vehicle statutes (`Va. Code §§ 46.2-711, 46.2-725, 46.2-726`) and the First and Fourteenth Amendments implicate no federal statute, federal agency program, or federal sovereign interest.

## Question Presented and Route if Granted

If the Court were to grant certiorari, it would take the petition's single Question Presented as written — whether personalized alphanumeric combinations displayed on state-issued license plates constitute government speech exempt from First Amendment Free Speech Clause scrutiny — and leave untouched the unaddressed forum-classification and viewpoint-neutrality questions that the Fourth Circuit remanded to the district court.

Conditional on a grant, the Court will set the case for plenary briefing and oral argument rather than disposing of it summarily in the certiorari order. No pending Supreme Court merits case addresses the government-speech doctrine, leaving no basis for a hold and grant-vacate-remand (GVR) order unless the dispute became moot under *Munsingwear*, and the Fourth Circuit's unanimous, thoroughly reasoned opinion applying *Matal v. Tam* and *Shurtleff v. City of Boston* leaves no plausible basis for summary reversal. If granted on the merits, the Court would likely affirm the Fourth Circuit and hold that driver-coined personalized character combinations, subject only to negative anti-offensiveness screening across more than 930,000 Virginia vanity plates, constitute private speech rather than government expression.

## Separate Writings on Denial

Expect the denial order to issue without a noted dissent or statement respecting the denial of certiorari, just as the Court denied certiorari without separate writing last Term on the identical question in *Gilliam v. Gerregano*, No. 25-107. In the unlikely event a Justice writes separately on the order, the likeliest form is a short statement respecting the denial of certiorari (most plausibly authored by Justice Thomas, Justice Alito, or Justice Gorsuch) noting the conflict between the Fourth Circuit and the Indiana and Tennessee Supreme Courts while explaining that the interlocutory remand from a Rule 12(b)(6) dismissal counsels waiting for a final judgment.
