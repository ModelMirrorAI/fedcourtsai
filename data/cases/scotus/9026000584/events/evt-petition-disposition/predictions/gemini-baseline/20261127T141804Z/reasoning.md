## 2. Adjustments From the `0.2352` `state`-Band Anchor to `0.12`

Starting from the `0.2352` `state`-band `reached` anchor, one strong upward factor competes against three weighty downward factors:

1. **Upward — Genuine, acknowledged split between a federal circuit and two state high courts (+):**
   The Fourth Circuit expressly parted ways with the Indiana Supreme Court (*Commissioner of Indiana BMV v. Vawter*, 45 N.E.3d 1200 (Ind. 2015)) and the Tennessee Supreme Court (*Gilliam v. Gerregano*, 709 S.W.3d 456 (Tenn. 2025)) on a pure federal constitutional question: whether personalized alphanumeric combinations on state-issued license plates are government speech under *Walker v. Texas Division, Sons of Confederate Veterans, Inc.*, 576 U.S. 200 (2015), or private speech under *Matal v. Tam*, 582 U.S. 218 (2017), and *Shurtleff v. City of Boston*, 596 U.S. 243 (2022). As the first post-*Walker* federal court of appeals decision on vanity plates, *Whateley* converts what had been uniform post-*Walker* state-high-court authority (alongside split federal district courts) into a square federal-circuit/state-supreme-court split, pressed by a State Solicitor General defending a program covering more than 930,000 plates. On a clean final judgment with an enjoined program, this split would push P(grant) well above the `0.235` `state` anchor.

2. **Downward — Severe interlocutory Rule 12(b)(6) vehicle defect (--):**
   The Eastern District of Virginia dismissed Whateley's complaint at the Rule 12(b)(6) pleading stage solely on government-speech grounds, never reaching forum classification or whether recalling Whateley's `FJB LGB` plate under Virginia's anti-offensiveness guideline was viewpoint-neutral. The Fourth Circuit expressly declined to reach those alternative merits defenses and vacated and remanded for further proceedings (`decision-below.txt`, slip op. 27–28). No injunction bars Virginia from administering its vanity-plate program while litigation proceeds on remand, no factual record beyond the pleadings exists, and Virginia can seek certiorari from a final judgment if it ultimately loses on remand. The Court routinely denies interlocutory Rule 12(b)(6) remands even in the presence of a split.

3. **Downward — Recent unremarked denial in *Gilliam v. Gerregano* and active Seventh Circuit percolation (--):**
   Corpus retrieval (`scotus/73274790`) and CourtListener checks confirm that less than twelve months ago, on December 8, 2025, the Court denied certiorari without a single noted dissent or statement in *Gilliam v. Gerregano*, No. 25-107 — a case presenting the *exact* same question from a **final** Tennessee Supreme Court judgment, litigated by experienced Supreme Court counsel (John J. Bursch) and supported by **five** cert-stage amicus briefs (including FIRE, Simon Tam / Institute for Free Speech, ACLJ, WILL / Buckeye Institute, and Protect the First Foundation). Furthermore, CourtListener confirms that the Seventh Circuit heard oral argument on September 17, 2026 in *M J Nichols Co. v. Boardman*, No. 26-1043 (7th Cir.), which remains pending (`dateTerminated: null`) and will soon supply a second post-*Walker* federal circuit opinion on state vanity plates (and from a summary-judgment record rather than a Rule 12(b)(6) dismissal). Waiting for *Nichols* and for final judgments is the classic institutional course.

4. **Downward — Inverted error-correction / merits alignment (-):**
   In *Matal v. Tam* (8–0), Justice Alito warned for the Court that *Walker* "marks the outer bounds of the government-speech doctrine," and in *Shurtleff* (9–0), Justice Alito (joined by Justices Thomas and Gorsuch) urged an even stricter limit on government-speech defenses. Here, Judge Harris's unanimous opinion held that a motorist's political slogan (`FJB LGB`) on a vanity plate is *protected private speech*, aligning closely with *Matal* and *Shurtleff*. Consequently, the Justices typically most receptive to State SG petitions challenging progressive appellate rulings have scant doctrinal appetite to reach down interlocutorily to *reverse* a ruling protecting political speech from anti-"offensiveness" censorship, while Justices more sympathetic to *Walker* have little reason to take an interlocutory Rule 12(b)(6) case when forum analysis remains open on remand. Respondent's rapid waiver reflects justified confidence in these vehicle and merits headwinds.

Weighing these together moves my forecast from `0.2352` down to **`0.12`** — well above the general paid-petition baseline because of the real CA4/TN-IN split and State SG posture, but nearly half the `state`-band `reached` rate and slightly below the `0.1364` zero-amicus `state` arm.
