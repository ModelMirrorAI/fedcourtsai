# Forecast of the Court's action

The Court will most likely deny the petition without a separate writing. The concrete conflict over personalized license-plate characters will not necessarily overcome the unfinished posture: the Fourth Circuit has required further First Amendment analysis, but Virginia may still prevail on remand without having its restrictions invalidated.

The baseline records **zero distributions** and no CVSG. From that state, the petition is 99% likely to receive at least one conference distribution. That initial distribution satisfies the declared `relist-increment` claim; it is not itself a relist. The modal path is a first distribution followed by denial. The chance of a repeat distribution after the first is approximately 44%; if the Court pursues review, one or two additional distributions are more plausible than an immediate grant or a very long series. Timing depends on the response and any extensions: initial conference consideration is more likely in early 2027 than before the December 3, 2026 response deadline.

A call for the Solicitor General's views is unlikely, at 3.5%. If one occurs, it will most naturally follow initial conference consideration in 2027. The government/private-speech distinction does not ordinarily require executive-branch technical input to the extent that a federal regulatory or foreign-relations dispute might.

If review is granted, the Court will most likely take a narrowed version of the question: whether personalized alphanumeric combinations chosen by motorists on Virginia-issued license plates constitute government speech for First Amendment purposes. It is less likely to take the petition's broad formulation as an invitation to decide the constitutionality of every restriction on individual expression on state-owned plates. The forum classification and validity of the particular recall are not questions the Fourth Circuit finally resolved.

Conditional on any grant, plenary briefing and argument are the expected route; summary disposition has a 9% probability. There is no identified intervening decision supplying an obvious GVR instruction, and reconciling the identification function with the private message is a substantive doctrinal task. If plenary review is granted, the likely doctrinal focus will be Walker's application to registration characters, alongside the distinction between regulating private expression and communicating a government message. This is conditional reasoning, not a forecast that an opinion will issue despite the expected denial.

Conditional on denial, there is an 8% chance of a dissent or statement respecting denial; no writing is the clear modal prediction. Thomas, Alito, or Gorsuch are somewhat more plausible individual writers than most colleagues, but no particular author is expected, and the order-writing distribution does not forecast cert votes.
