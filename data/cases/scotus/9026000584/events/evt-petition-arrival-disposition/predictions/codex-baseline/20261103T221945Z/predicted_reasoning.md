# Forecast of the Court's disposition

The Court will **deny the petition**, most likely without a separate writing. The disagreement over personalized plate messages makes review a serious possibility, but the unresolved proceedings after remand will make this petition less attractive than a final judgment resolving whether Virginia's restrictions actually violate the First Amendment.

The record begins with **zero distributions and no CVSG**. The petition will receive an ordinary first conference distribution after the response process, most likely in January–March 2027, allowing for a response extension. The modal path is denial at that first consideration, without a true postconference relist. I assign 98% to at least one distribution beyond today's zero; that is the contract's `relist-increment`, not a prediction that the Court has already held a conference. There is approximately a 42% chance of a later relist beyond the initial distribution; on that path, one or two additional distributions are more likely than a long sequence. A hold awaiting another vehicle is possible but not the principal forecast. I expect the petition to be disposed of by the end of June 2027 rather than held over, absent a new development.

The Court will **not call for the Solicitor General's views**; I assign a 4% chance of a new CVSG, most plausibly after the first conference during winter or spring 2027. This state-license-plate constitutional dispute does not present an identified federal administrative interest requiring that extra round of briefing.

**If review is granted**, the Court will take a narrower formulation of the petition's sole question: whether owner-selected alphanumeric messages on Virginia's personalized license plates are government speech or private speech subject to First Amendment forum analysis. It will not take the broad question to mean that all limits on private plate expression are forbidden, nor decide unrelated facial-vagueness or due-process claims abandoned on appeal. The merits inquiry would address Walker's scope and the distinction between the state's vehicle-identification function and the driver's personal message; it would not simply assume that one speaker's message eliminates the other's.

Conditional on a grant, **plenary briefing and argument are strongly favored**: the probability of a GVR or other disposition riding the cert order is 7%. No intervening controlling decision is identified in the record, and the lower courts' reasoned disagreement makes summary reversal an unlikely substitute for resolving the open doctrinal question.

Conditional on denial, there is a **10% chance of a noted dissent or statement respecting denial**, but the prediction is no such writing. If one Justice does write, Thomas is my leading individual candidate, with Alito and Gorsuch next; those are low-probability alternatives rather than an expected signed order. No denial would itself resolve whether Virginia may ultimately recall Whateley's plate under the forum rules left for remand.
