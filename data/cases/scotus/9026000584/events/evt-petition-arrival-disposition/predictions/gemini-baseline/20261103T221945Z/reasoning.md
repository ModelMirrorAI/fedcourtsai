1. **Interlocutory Remand Posture Is a Serious Vehicle Defect:**

The Fourth Circuit vacated and remanded specifically so the district court can determine in the first instance what kind of forum Virginia's vanity-plate program creates and whether the recall of `"FTP&ATF"` (on profanity/vulgarity and violence grounds) survives forum scrutiny (`decision-below.txt`, Slip Op. 27–28 & n.11). Crucially, as the Fourth Circuit pointed out (citing *Mitchell*, 148 A.3d at 328–39, where Maryland won on remand because banning `"MIERDA"` was held reasonable and viewpoint-neutral in a nonpublic forum), **Virginia may still prevail on remand**. Even if Virginia loses on remand, awaiting final judgment would allow the Supreme Court to consider both the threshold government-speech question and the closely intertwined question of whether states may prohibit profane or vulgar combinations in a nonpublic forum after *Matal* and *Brunetti* in a single, concrete record. The Court routinely denies interlocutory petitions where the petitioner may prevail on remand or where further proceedings will sharpen the constitutional issues.

The Justices have strong institutional incentives to allow the Seventh Circuit to rule in *Nichols* (and allow the remand in *Whateley* to conclude) rather than granting an interlocutory petition now.

Moreover, Chief Justice Roberts and Justices Thomas and Alito dissented in *Walker* itself (576 U.S. at 221–36), arguing that even specialty plate designs are perceived by the public as private speech. Thus, the Justices most sympathetic to free-speech claims are doctrinally skeptical of Virginia's government-speech theory, while Justices who agree with Judge Harris's unanimous panel opinion (joined by Judges Agee and Keenan) have little reason to disturb an interlocutory remand.
