## 3. Case-Specific Adjustments from the Anchor

### Upward Pressures (+0.05 from the generic `state` arrival baseline)

1. **Square, Acknowledged Split on a Question Expressly Reserved in *Walker*:**
   - In *Walker v. Texas Division, Sons of Confederate Veterans, Inc.*, 576 U.S. 200, 204 (2015), the Supreme Court held that state specialty license plate designs are government speech but explicitly stated that it was "not [concerned] with the personalization program" (vanity plates).
   - Both the Fourth Circuit opinion (184 F.4th at 377–78, App. 14a–15a) and the district court opinion (App. 45a) acknowledge an entrenched divide among state courts of last resort and federal courts over how *Walker*'s three-factor test applies to personalized alphanumeric combinations on license plates:
     - **Government speech:** The Tennessee Supreme Court in *Gilliam v. Gerregano*, 737 S.W.3d 258 (Tenn. 2025), and the Indiana Supreme Court in *Commissioner of Indiana Bureau of Motor Vehicles v. Vawter*, 45 N.E.3d 1200, 1204–05 (Ind. 2015), held that personalized alphanumeric combinations on license plates are government speech immune from First Amendment scrutiny.
     - **Private speech:** The Fourth Circuit below (*Whateley*, 184 F.4th 368) and the Maryland Court of Appeals (*Mitchell v. Maryland Motor Vehicle Administration*, 148 A.3d 319 (Md. 2016))—along with at least six federal district courts—hold that personalized vanity plate combinations are private speech subject to First Amendment forum analysis.
   - Notably, when the *Gilliam* plaintiff petitioned for certiorari last Term (No. 25-107), Tennessee opposed certiorari by arguing that no federal court of appeals had yet addressed the question post-*Walker* and specifically cited *Whateley* as pending in the Fourth Circuit (Pet. 12–13). The Fourth Circuit's published decision now supplies the federal appellate conflict with two state courts of last resort, and creates direct geographic friction between Virginia/Maryland and neighboring Tennessee.
2. **State Sovereign Petitioner Challenging Restriction on a Major State Program:**
