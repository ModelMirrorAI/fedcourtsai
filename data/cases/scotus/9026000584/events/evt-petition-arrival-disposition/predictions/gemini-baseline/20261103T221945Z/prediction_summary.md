We predict the Supreme Court will deny Virginia's request to review this case, with about an 18% chance (roughly one in five) of taking it up. The case asks whether custom letter-and-number combinations on personalized "vanity" license plates—here, `"FTP&ATF"`—count as the state's own government speech or the driver's private speech protected by the First Amendment. Although state courts and federal appeals courts are divided on that question, the Fourth Circuit merely sent the case back to the trial court to decide whether Virginia can still ban vulgar plates under standard free-speech rules, and the Supreme Court usually waits until a case is completely finished before stepping in.
