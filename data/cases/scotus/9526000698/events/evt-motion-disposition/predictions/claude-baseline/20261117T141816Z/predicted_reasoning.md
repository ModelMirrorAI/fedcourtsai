# Forecast — 26A698, Simpkins v. Tennessee (application for injunctive relief)

The record at prediction time shows the application submitted to the Chief Justice, no response requested, no referral to the Court, and zero amicus entries.

- **Disposition.** The application will be denied. The most likely form is a one-line denial by the Chief Justice acting alone, with no explanation. No administrative stay will issue, and the Court will not enjoin the foreclosure sale, the state prosecution or the garnishment, nor enter any merits judgment.
- **Response.** No response will be called for from Tennessee or any other respondent.
- **Referral.** More likely than not the matter ends with the Chief Justice's denial. There is roughly a one-in-four chance the applicants renew the application to another Justice under Rule 22.4, in which case it will be referred to the full Court, distributed for a conference, and denied by the Court without noted dissent.
- **Amici.** None will be filed.
- **Timing.** A single-Justice denial within about one to two weeks of submission (by roughly late November 2026). If renewed and referred, a denial by the Court at a conference in December 2026 or January 2027.
- **Writings.** No Justice will write on the order.
- If the Court were to act favorably at all (which I do not expect), it would be a brief administrative stay limited to the foreclosure sale, not any of the merits relief requested.
