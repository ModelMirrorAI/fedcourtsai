## In brief

The Court denied a couple's emergency request to block a foreclosure sale of their Tennessee home and halt related state proceedings.

## What happened

David and Sally Simpkins, representing themselves, faced a non-judicial foreclosure sale of their Spring Hill, Tennessee home scheduled for November 17, 2026. They also faced state criminal charges and a homeowners association collection action, and had pursued federal civil rights litigation in the District of Columbia over these matters. After the D.C. Circuit Court of Appeals ruled against them, they asked the Supreme Court to step in directly. Their application says warrants were issued before supporting affidavits were filed, that the criminal case is time-barred, that a judge handling their case was also a defendant in their federal lawsuit, and that a wage levy was improperly taken from a non-debtor spouse's earnings.

## What the Court is being asked

The applicants ask the Court to immediately stop the foreclosure sale and related state court actions while they pursue further review, and to rule now that various state proceedings against them are invalid because of missed deadlines, a judge's alleged conflict of interest, and claimed excessive force by police.

## Where it stands

The Chief Justice denied the application on November 17, 2026, the day of the scheduled foreclosure sale.

## What each outcome would mean

The Simpkinses, as applicants, asked the Court to pause the foreclosure sale and related state proceedings. The denial leaves those proceedings and the scheduled sale in effect, meaning the foreclosure could go forward and the state criminal and civil cases the applicants challenged are not blocked by this ruling. The denial does not decide the underlying legal questions the applicants raised; it only declines to grant emergency relief at this stage, and the case may continue in the lower courts.
