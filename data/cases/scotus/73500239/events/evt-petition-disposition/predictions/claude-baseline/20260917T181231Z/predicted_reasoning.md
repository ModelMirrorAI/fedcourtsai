# Forecast: Pesta v. Bloomberg, No. 25-1337 (evt-petition-disposition)

**Disposition.** The Court will deny certiorari at or shortly after the September 28, 2026 conference. P(any grant) = 0.02.

**Relists.** The docket shows **one** distribution (July 1, 2026, for the Conference of 9/28/2026) and no relist. I forecast from that state. The most likely course is a single conference and a denial on the October 5, 2026 order list. The realistic paths to another distribution are (a) a call for a response after the long conference, which would redistribute the petition once the brief in opposition arrives, and (b) a one-conference relist while a Justice considers a statement respecting denial. Together I put a further distribution at about 12%. If a response is called for, expect one to two further distributions and still a denial.

**CVSG.** No call for the views of the Solicitor General. The dispute is between a former professor and officials of a state university; the federal interest (NIH data-access rules) is peripheral and no federal party is before the Court. P ≈ 0.01.

**Which question, if any.** If the Court took anything, it would not take the questions as written. QP 1 (whether the *Sullivan* actual-malice showing is a threshold the government must clear before *Pickering* balancing) is the only one framed as a legal rule, and the petition concedes the circuit disagreement it cites (*Johnson v. Multnomah County*) is "not quite posed as it is here." QP 2 (the *Garcetti* academic-freedom reservation) is the question with genuine interest at the Court, but the Sixth Circuit did not reach the district court's alternative *Garcetti* holding, so it is not squarely presented. QP 3 is a request for error correction on the balance. A grant would come only after a called-for response and would most plausibly be reformulated around the *Garcetti* reservation.

**Summary disposition versus plenary review.** Conditional on a grant, I put the cert-order route (a GVR or a summary disposition) at about 35%. No intervening decision of this Court that I can identify bears on the *Pickering*/*Garcetti* framework as applied here, so an ordinary GVR has nothing to ride on; but on a vehicle this fact-bound, a grant that happens at all is disproportionately likely to be a GVR in light of some decision handed down between now and disposition rather than plenary review of these questions.

**Separate writing on denial.** Conditional on a denial, about 5% that a Justice notes a dissent or files a statement respecting denial. Justice Thomas has written respecting denial on the *Pickering*/*Garcetti* framework before (the petition cites *MacRae v. Matthews*), and the academic-freedom reservation in *Garcetti* is a live interest for at least one or two Justices. Against that, the decision below turns on causation (the professor was fired for research misconduct, not for the content of his paper), which gives a Justice little doctrinal purchase for a statement, and the opinion is unpublished. If a statement issues, expect it from Justice Thomas, possibly joined by Justice Alito, and expect it to concern the *Garcetti* academic-freedom question rather than the *Sullivan* malice theory.

**Conditional merits.** If granted on QP 2, the likely ground would be that academic scholarship by a public-university professor falls outside *Garcetti*'s official-duties rule and is analyzed under *Pickering*; the *Sullivan* malice threshold theory of QP 1 would not be adopted.
