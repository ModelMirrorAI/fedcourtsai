## What happened

Bryan Pesta, a tenured professor at Cleveland State University, published a study on genetics and cognitive ability using restricted federal data. After critics complained, the National Institutes of Health found he had used data outside the scope of one of his approved requests. CSU then disciplined him and, following a misconduct investigation, stripped his tenure and fired him. Pesta sued CSU's president and several officials, claiming his firing was retaliation for his speech in violation of the First Amendment. The district court ruled for the university, finding Pesta was fired for research misconduct rather than the content of his paper, and the Sixth Circuit Court of Appeals affirmed. The full appeals court declined to rehear the case.

## What the Court is being asked

The case involves the standard courts use when a government employee, like a public university professor, claims they were punished for their speech. The petition argues that before weighing the employee's speech interests against the employer's interests, the government should first have to show the employee's speech involved deliberate or reckless falsehood; and that this same approach should resolve an unsettled question about how much protection academic speech gets. The petition also argues that, however the general legal standard is defined, the lower courts here misapplied it to Pesta's case by not properly weighing his study's value against the university's justifications for firing him.
