## In brief

A fired university professor asked the Supreme Court to revive his free-speech lawsuit against Cleveland State University officials; the Court declined.

## What happened

Bryan Pesta, a tenured professor at Cleveland State University, published a study using restricted federal research data. After other researchers complained, the National Institutes of Health found he had used the data outside the scope of his approval, and the university disciplined him and then stripped his tenure and fired him, citing research misconduct. Pesta sued university officials, claiming the real reason was retaliation for the content of his research, in violation of his free-speech rights. The district court ruled for the officials, finding Pesta was fired for misconduct, not for his speech. The Sixth Circuit Court of Appeals affirmed that decision, and rehearing was denied.

## What the Court is being asked

Pesta's petition asks the Court to clarify the legal test courts use to decide when a public employee's firing for speech-related reasons violates the First Amendment. He argues the Court should require the university to first show his statements were knowingly or recklessly false before weighing his free-speech interest against the university's interests, a standard he says the Court's earlier cases have applied inconsistently. He also argues the lower courts misapplied that balancing test to the facts of his case.
