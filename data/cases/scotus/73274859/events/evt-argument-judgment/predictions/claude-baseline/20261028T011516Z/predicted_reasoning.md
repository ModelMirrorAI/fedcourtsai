# Predicted reasoning — Suncor Energy (U.S.A.) Inc. v. County Commissioners of Boulder County, No. 25-170

## Judgment class
**Reversed.** The Court reverses the Colorado Supreme Court's judgment (which let
Boulder's state-law climate claims proceed past the motion to dismiss) and remands.
A vacate-and-remand under a Clean Air Act preemption framework is the main
alternative disturbing outcome. Both procedural exits are live with an eight-member
bench: a 4–4 affirmance by an equally divided Court (Justice Alito no longer
participates, per the Clerk's letter of September 28, 2026), and a dismissal for
want of jurisdiction on the added question (whether this is a final judgment under
28 U.S.C. § 1257 / *Atlantic Richfield*). I put those two together at roughly a
quarter to a third.

## Jurisdiction (the added question)
The Court finds jurisdiction. *Atlantic Richfield* treats a state supreme court's
self-contained original proceeding as final, and the Colorado Rule 21 proceeding
(an order to show cause, then discharged) fits that well enough for a majority.
Expect Justices Sotomayor and Jackson to say the Court lacks jurisdiction or should
not decide the case piecemeal, and possibly Justice Thomas to write separately on
§ 1257 finality.

## Vote lineup (8 participating)
- **Majority (6):** Roberts, Thomas, Gorsuch, Kavanaugh, Barrett, Kagan.
- **Dissent (2):** Sotomayor, Jackson.
- **Recused:** Alito.

My least certain placements are Kagan and Gorsuch. Kagan was hostile to the
petitioners' constitutional theories, but she told respondents' counsel the Clean
Air Act was "the more natural and simpler route" to the petitioners' result. That
reads as an openness to statutory preemption, so she may join a narrower ground or
concur in the judgment. Gorsuch pressed the SG on why the Clean Air Act would leave
no cause of action at all when EPA disclaims authority. He may concur only in the
judgment, or he could be the fifth vote that fails to appear (5–3 or a 4–4 split).

## Ground and breadth
The majority rests on the line of interstate-pollution precedents that Justice
Kavanaugh framed as a "wall of precedent" (Milwaukee I, Milwaukee II, *Ouellette*,
*AEP*). Interstate air pollution has always been governed by federal law. The Clean
Air Act displaced federal common law but saved only claims under the law of the
source State, as *Ouellette* read the parallel Clean Water Act saving clause.
Colorado is the affected State, so its law cannot supply the rule of decision.
Justice Barrett's "enclave of federal law" framing fits this ground. Labeling the
claims as deception or failure to warn does not change their gravamen, because the
harm runs entirely through global emissions.

The rule is categorical: affected-State law cannot reach out-of-state and global
emissions. That disposes of the similarly pleaded municipal suits. The opinion
leaves open whether a source-State-law suit against in-state emitters survives. It
does not adopt the broad freestanding constitutional extraterritoriality principle
that the petitioners and the SG led with, since several Justices (the Chief Justice,
Kagan and Kavanaugh) showed little appetite for it.

## Who writes
- **Opinion of the Court:** most likely Kavanaugh (who framed the precedential
  route at argument), the Chief Justice (a Term-opening case of this size), or
  Barrett. This is the October sitting's first case, so no assignment pattern from
  the sitting is visible yet.
- **Separate writings:** a principal dissent from Jackson or Sotomayor, covering
  jurisdiction and piecemeal review and defending state tort law. A Thomas
  concurrence on jurisdiction or on the federal-common-law premise. Possibly a
  Gorsuch concurrence in the judgment, more skeptical of implied or common-law
  preemption. Possibly Kagan writing to rest on the Clean Air Act alone.
