## Why 0.77

My judgment-class allocation is reversed **0.64**, vacated **0.10**, affirmed-in-part-reversed-in-part **0.03**, affirmed **0.10**, dismissed-as-improvidently-granted/jurisdictional dismissal route **0.07**, and affirmed-by-an-equally-divided-court **0.06**. The latter dismissal bucket is an uncertainty allocation to a no-merits exit, not a claim that every jurisdictional dismissal uses the DIG wording. Disturbed outcomes sum to **0.77**, exactly the top-level probability and `judgment-disturbed` claim. The chosen judgment is `reversed`; `granted=1` records that merits-stage binary, not a new cert grant.

The argument moves me moderately above the historical anchor. Kavanaugh repeatedly frames the pollution precedents as a federal baseline with source-state exceptions. Barrett's discussion of a federal enclave, preexisting state authority, and the apparent equivalence between interstate emissions regulation and tort liability supports a confined reversal. Roberts' concern about repeated climate suits and the producer/emitter distinction points the same way, despite his resistance to treating every cross-border tort as exclusively federal. Gorsuch's questioning is more skeptical of the government's explanation than of every possible route to reversal. Thomas' questions about jurisdiction and downstream retailers are weaker directional evidence. None is a promised vote.

The provisioned argument features count **163 Justice turns in petitioner-aligned segments and 69 in respondent-aligned segments**. Coverage is **232 attributed of 233 Justice turns**, with one turn outside segments, all **4/4 segments attributed**, no unresolved Justice turns, and no unrecognized label lines. Those are speaker-turn counts, not hostility scores, independent questions, or votes; petitioner-aligned counts include the United States and rebuttal. I do not convert the imbalance into a directional probability. The content of Barrett's exchanges, for example, is materially more informative than the number of interruptions.

There are substantial reasons not to go to 0.90. Respondents persuasively distinguish statutory displacement from automatic constitutional foreclosure and emphasize that EPA regulates emitters rather than all upstream marketing. AEP expressly left the state-law preemption question open; the CourtListener predecessor-opinion checks confirmed that limitation. Ouellette concerns a statutory source-state/affected-state distinction, not categorical constitutional immunity. Its extension from a point-source nuisance to producer deception and worldwide damages requires additional reasoning. Jackson presses precisely the undeveloped question whether Colorado can be a source State as well as an injured State. The Colorado opinion decided a motion-to-dismiss defense, not ultimate liability or causation.

The Court itself added jurisdiction, making the finality objection serious. Respondents say Rule 21 operated as interlocutory appellate review, that other defenses remain, and that the fourth Cox category does not fit. Sotomayor's argument questions press those issues and the possibility of certification. Atlantic Richfield gives petitioners a concrete response, but not an automatic win. Alito's recusal removes one possible reversal vote and makes a 4–4 result possible. These risks sit in the undisturbed complement, not in an artificially redefined disturbance probability.
