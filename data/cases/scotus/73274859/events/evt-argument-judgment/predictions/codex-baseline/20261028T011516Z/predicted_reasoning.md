# Forecast of the Court's judgment

The Court will **reverse** the Colorado Supreme Court's judgment permitting Boulder's claims to proceed over the federal preemption defense and remand for proceedings consistent with that ruling. The modal lineup is **5–3**: Roberts, Thomas, Gorsuch, Kavanaugh, and Barrett in the majority; Sotomayor, Kagan, and Jackson dissenting. Alito will take no part, consistent with the September 28 clerk's letter. Reversal does not mean this Court awards damages or resolves every alternative defense.

## Jurisdiction

The Court will find the Colorado Rule 21 proceeding sufficiently self-contained under Atlantic Richfield Co. v. Christian to satisfy 28 U.S.C. § 1257(a), and will find a concrete adverse stake sufficient for Article III. It will not need to overrule Cox Broadcasting. The dispute over original proceedings versus interlocutory review will generate disagreement, but will not prevent a merits judgment.

## Majority ground

The decisive ground will be Clean Air Act preemption: Colorado may not use tort damages assessed for worldwide greenhouse-gas emissions to substitute an injured State's standards for the federal and source-state allocation of emissions regulation. The Court will draw on American Electric Power Co. v. Connecticut and International Paper Co. v. Ouellette. The opinion will treat the practical basis of liability, rather than the designation of the defendants as upstream producers or the complaint's marketing allegations, as controlling for these aggregate climate-damages claims.

The Court will distinguish displacement of federal common law from preemption of state law rather than treating AEP as already having decided every state-law claim. Federalism and the interstate-pollution precedents will inform the statutory construction. The Court will not need an independent, sweeping constitutional rule immunizing all out-of-state commercial conduct, nor a free-standing foreign-affairs holding, to reach its judgment.

## Breadth

The rule will reach the category of injured-state claims seeking compensation for aggregate interstate and international emissions, not every lawsuit involving fossil fuels. It will leave ordinary deception claims that do not depend on worldwide climate-damages liability and source-state claims outside the categorical bar. Those reservations will not preserve Boulder's present worldwide-damages theory merely because some defendants also operate in Colorado. The opinion will not decide EPA's current regulatory dispute or the correctness of every allegation of climate causation.

## Writings and alternatives

Kavanaugh is the modal majority author, with Roberts and Barrett close alternatives: his argument repeatedly organized the pollution precedents around a federal baseline and source-state exceptions. Sotomayor is the modal dissenting author, emphasizing jurisdictional finality and the limits of preemption. Gorsuch or Thomas may write separately about the source of the federal rule, and Jackson may emphasize pleading-stage assumptions and the distinction between emissions and misleading sales. These are forecasts of authorship, not predictions that every dissenter writes.

Kagan is the least secure placement in the modal lineup. Her statutory obstacle-preemption questions could produce a narrower **6–2** judgment instead. If one of the five predicted majority Justices disagrees on jurisdiction or preemption, Alito's nonparticipation makes an equally divided affirmance realistic. A jurisdictional dismissal is also live, not merely a theoretical possibility; both leave the judgment below undisturbed.
