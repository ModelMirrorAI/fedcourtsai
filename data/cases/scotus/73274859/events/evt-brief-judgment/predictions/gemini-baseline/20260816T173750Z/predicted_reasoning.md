Expect the Court to reverse the Colorado Supreme Court. The majority will likely hold that state-law nuisance, trespass, and related tort claims seeking damages for injuries caused by interstate and international greenhouse gas emissions are preempted by federal law. The Court will likely reason that regulating emissions is inherently a federal issue that requires balancing environmental protection with energy needs and economic growth—a balance that must be struck uniformly at the federal level, not through a patchwork of state-court damages awards. The 6-3 conservative majority will likely view the Clean Air Act and federal common law as displacing the state-law claims, echoing the Second Circuit's reasoning in *City of New York v. Chevron* and extending the principles from *AEP v. Connecticut*.

Justices Sotomayor, Kagan, and Jackson are expected to dissent, arguing that the Clean Air Act preserves state-law remedies and does not expressly preempt state tort claims, pointing to traditional principles of federalism and the presumption against preemption.
