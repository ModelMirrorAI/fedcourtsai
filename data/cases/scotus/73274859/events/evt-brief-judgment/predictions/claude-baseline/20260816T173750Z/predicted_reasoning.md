# Forecast: Suncor Energy (U.S.A.) Inc. v. County Commissioners of Boulder County, No. 25-170 (merits, briefed moment)

## Judgment class

The Court will **reverse** the Colorado Supreme Court's judgment (2025 CO 21),
holding that federal law precludes Boulder's state-law claims for injuries
attributed to interstate and international greenhouse-gas emissions. I put
P(judgment disturbed) at **0.77**.

The path there runs through the Court's own added question. It will first hold
that it **has jurisdiction**: statutory jurisdiction under the fourth
*Cox Broadcasting* exception (the federal preclusion issue is finally decided
below, reversal ends the litigation as pleaded, and forcing the defendants
through a full state trial would erode the federal policy at stake), and
Article III jurisdiction on the *ASARCO v. Kadish* line. A dismissal for want
of jurisdiction is the main alternative branch (~15%): respondents' brief leads
with it and the interlocutory posture is genuinely awkward under §1257, but a
Court that added the question in the same order that granted an interlocutory
petition — after relisting it through six distributions — is far more likely
clearing its path than laying a trap for itself. A DIG is possible but
unlikely (~3%); an outright affirmance ~5%.

Expected majority ground, stated fully in the semantic block: structural
preclusion via the federal-common-law-of-interstate-pollution line
(*Milwaukee*, *AEP v. Connecticut*, extending *Ouellette*'s exclusion of
affected-state law), rather than implied Clean Air Act preemption — the
petitioners and the Solicitor General have led with the structural theory, and
it avoids the CAA's saving clauses. A narrower CAA-preemption reversal is the
likelier fallback if Kavanaugh or Barrett balk at constitutionalizing the rule.

## Vote lineup

**6–3** along the familiar line: Roberts, Thomas, Alito, Gorsuch, Kavanaugh,
and Barrett in the majority; Sotomayor, Kagan, and Jackson in dissent. Alito
did not recuse at the grant (no "took no part" notation, unlike the 2023
petition), so a full nine-member bench sits and an equally divided affirmance
is off the table absent a later recusal. Gorsuch is the least certain majority
vote — his anti-preemption federalism instincts (*National Pork*, his implied-
preemption skepticism) could push him toward respondents or toward the
jurisdictional exit — but on climate-suit preclusion I expect him with the
majority. Kagan is the least certain dissenter; a narrow, jurisdiction-heavy
majority opinion could attract her concurrence in the judgment. Authorship is
unscored: my guess is Kavanaugh or the Chief Justice, with Sotomayor writing
the principal dissent.

## Timing and process

Argued **October 5, 2026** (already calendared, first argument of OT2026), with
divided argument likely granted to the Solicitor General (motion filed July 28,
2026, unopposed in substance — the SG filed in support of petitioners at both
stages). A decision in this posture — high salience, likely divided — lands
late in the Term: expect the judgment **May–June 2027**. Separate writings are
near-certain: at least one concurrence on the ground's scope (possibly Thomas
on federal common law's legitimacy, or Gorsuch limiting the holding to
non-source-state law) and a full-throated principal dissent on federalism and
the historical pedigree of transboundary nuisance claims.
