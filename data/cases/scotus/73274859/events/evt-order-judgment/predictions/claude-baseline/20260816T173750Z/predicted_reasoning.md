# Predicted reasoning — Suncor Energy (U.S.A.) Inc. v. County Commissioners of Boulder County (No. 25-170)

**Forecast: the Court reverses the Colorado Supreme Court, roughly 6–3, holding
that federal law precludes Boulder's state-law claims for injuries attributed to
interstate and international greenhouse-gas emissions. P(judgment below
disturbed) = 0.73.**

## Judgment class

The Court will enter **reversed**. The Colorado Supreme Court (2025 CO 21) held
Boulder's tort claims neither constitutionally precluded nor preempted by the
Clean Air Act; the majority will reject that holding on the constitutional-
structure ground pressed by petitioners and the United States: injuries from
out-of-state and foreign emissions are governed by federal law under the
Milwaukee–Ouellette–AEP line, and the Clean Air Act's displacement of federal
common law does not hand the field to the states. Outright reversal is more
likely than vacatur — the question is legal and binary, and the Court took the
case to settle the nationwide wave of climate-damages suits — but a
vacate-and-remand articulating the federal rule and remanding for application
(e.g., to sort emissions-based claims from any severable deception theory) is
the secondary disturbed route.

## The added jurisdictional question

The grant order directed briefing on "whether this Court has statutory and
Article III jurisdiction to hear this case" — the Colorado judgment is
interlocutory (it affirmed the denial of a motion to dismiss), so § 1257
finality turns on Cox Broadcasting's fourth exception, and respondents lead
their merits brief with the argument that petitioners' injury is too
speculative for Article III under ASARCO. I forecast the Court **finds
jurisdiction** and reaches the merits: it granted with the finality objection
squarely presented in the brief in opposition, and a majority inclined to duck
had the cheaper option of denying. The realistic alternative — a dismissal for
want of jurisdiction (or a DIG once the finality problem looks worse after
argument), leaving the judgment below standing — is the main downside scenario
and carries about 0.15 of my probability mass. Expect the eventual majority to
apply Cox's fourth category (federal preemption issue finally decided below,
reversal would end the suit, and postponing review would seriously erode the
federal policy asserted), with one or more separate writings questioning that
category's pedigree.

## Vote lineup

**6–3.** Roberts, Thomas, Alito, Gorsuch, Kavanaugh, and Barrett to reverse;
Sotomayor, Kagan, and Jackson in dissent. Roberts is the likeliest author of a
structural-federalism opinion of this magnitude; expect a Gorsuch concurrence
(possibly joined by Thomas) writing separately on the limits of implied
preemption while accepting the inherently-federal-field rationale, and a
Sotomayor dissent on both jurisdiction and the merits. Two lineup risks: Alito
did not participate in this case's prior petition (No. 21-1550, 2023), so a
recusal here would make it 5–3; and Thomas or Gorsuch could balk at
constitutional preemption without a congressional command, though I expect the
foreign-affairs and interstate-structure framing to hold them.

## Timing and the rest of the calendar

Argued October 5, 2026 (first day of OT2026 argument). A decision of this
salience will land late: expect the opinion between April and the end of June
2027. The Solicitor General's motion for divided argument will be granted, as
is routine for the United States as amicus supporting petitioners.

## Conditional merits shape

The opinion will decide the preemption question on constitutional structure /
federal-common-law grounds rather than Clean Air Act obstacle preemption, and
will not reach the dormant foreign-affairs theory as an independent holding.
The rule will be categorical as to emissions-based climate claims (see the
`ground-breadth` semantic claim) while disclaiming any view on purely
intrastate pollution suits or misrepresentation claims not predicated on
cross-border emissions. Should the Court instead affirm (~0.10), expect a
narrow opinion holding that after AEP's displacement holding no federal common
law remains to preempt, leaving CAA preemption for final-judgment review.
