# Forecast of the Court's judgment

The Court will dismiss the writ as improvidently granted on jurisdictional grounds, leaving the Colorado Supreme Court's judgment in place without deciding the climate-preemption question. The likely vote is 6–2, with Justice Alito recused: Chief Justice Roberts and Justices Sotomayor, Kagan, Gorsuch, Barrett, and Jackson will favor dismissal; Justices Thomas and Kavanaugh will dissent and would reach the merits.

The controlling ground will be statutory finality. The Colorado Supreme Court's Rule 21 decision affirmed the denial of dismissal and returned the case for further proceedings; it did not terminate the litigation. The Court will conclude that the fourth *Cox Broadcasting* exception does not authorize immediate review because a federal-law ruling for the energy companies would not necessarily eliminate every deception, in-state-conduct, or source-state-law theory and delayed review does not itself erode federal policy. The opinion may reinforce that conclusion with Article III concerns about reviewing a state interlocutory ruling that did not finally alter the companies' tangible legal rights.

The holding will be narrow to this procedural posture. It will not decide whether the Clean Air Act, displaced federal common law, constitutional structure, or foreign-affairs principles preclude climate-damages claims after a final state judgment. A dissent will argue that the completed state resolution of the federal defense, the national uniformity interest, and *Cox* permit review now; it will likely add that state law cannot regulate injuries attributed to cumulative interstate and international emissions.

If the Court rejects the jurisdictional exit, the likelier merits result is disturbance rather than affirmance, probably through a narrow vacatur requiring attention to source-state law and the distinction between emissions-based and independently deceptive conduct. That alternative is substantial but is not the predicted judgment.
