# Rationale

I assign a 0.42 probability that the judgment below will be disturbed and predict a dismissal as improvidently granted. The registered merits anchor is 359 disturbed judgments among 515 parsed judgments (69.7%) across the available grant Terms 2017–2024, all strictly before this case's 2025 grant Term. Those rows cover 515 of 557 granted cases and report 57 pool-guard exclusions. That is a sound starting point for an ordinary granted case, but this case has an unusually strong procedural off-ramp.

The grant order itself added whether the Court has statutory and Article III jurisdiction. The Colorado Supreme Court reviewed the denial of a motion to dismiss in a Rule 21 proceeding and remanded for further litigation. Respondents therefore have substantial arguments that there is no final judgment under 28 U.S.C. 1257, that the fourth *Cox Broadcasting* exception does not apply because other theories could survive even a federal-law reversal, and that the state ruling did not finally alter tangible legal rights within *ASARCO*. The analogous jurisdictional complications discussed in the cert-stage brief make a DIG materially more likely than in the merits baseline.

If the Court reaches the substantive question, I would favor disturbance. Petitioners connect *American Electric Power*, *Ouellette*, and the former federal common law of interstate pollution to a uniform federal rule and argue that Boulder cannot avoid that rule by pleading worldwide emissions injuries as state-law marketing and production torts. Respondents have meaningful answers: displacement ordinarily returns the analysis to congressional intent; their deception claims target conduct rather than emissions controls; and source-state law or narrower claims could survive. Even a petitioner victory may therefore be a vacatur for narrower claim-by-claim or choice-of-law analysis rather than a categorical reversal. My 0.42 is approximately the product of a substantial chance that the Court clears both jurisdictional hurdles and a strong conditional chance of petitioner success on some ground.

The predicted lineup treats Justice Alito's recusal in the earlier related Suncor petition as a strong, but uncertain, indicator of renewed recusal. I expect the three Democratic appointees to prefer the jurisdictional exit, with Chief Justice Roberts, Justice Gorsuch, and Justice Barrett more likely than Justices Thomas and Kavanaugh to join it. Justice Kavanaugh's recorded dissent from denial in the earlier Suncor petition makes him the clearest vote to reach the merits. The principal uncertainty is whether the grant, despite the added question, reflects a majority already prepared to sustain jurisdiction.

This is a grant-moment forecast. I used the petition, questions presented, the completed November 2025 cert-stage brief in opposition, the grant order, and the committed statpack.
