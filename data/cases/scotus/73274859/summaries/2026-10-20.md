## In brief

Energy companies asked the Supreme Court to decide whether federal law bars Boulder's state-law lawsuit seeking damages for climate-change harms allegedly caused by the companies' fossil fuel production and marketing.

## What happened

Boulder County and the City of Boulder sued Suncor Energy and Exxon Mobil in Colorado state court, claiming the companies' production, marketing, and sale of fossil fuels contributed to climate change, causing local harms like wildfires, floods, and drought, and that the companies deceived the public about these risks. They sought damages, not an injunction against emissions. The trial court refused to dismiss the case, rejecting the companies' argument that federal law barred the claims. The Colorado Supreme Court agreed with the trial court, holding that the Clean Air Act displaced any applicable federal common law and did not preempt Colorado's tort claims. Two justices dissented, arguing that federal law still barred the suit.

## What the Court is being asked

The companies argue that the Constitution's structure, separate from any federal statute, prevents any single state from using its own law to address harms from pollution or climate effects that cross state and international borders — areas they say must be governed by federal law alone, with Congress's enactment of the Clean Air Act not automatically opening the door to state lawsuits unless Congress said so. Boulder counters that ordinary preemption rules apply once Congress legislates in this area, and that nothing in the Clean Air Act or the Constitution stops Colorado from using its own tort law to address in-state harms. The Court also asked the parties to brief whether it has authority to hear the case at all at this stage.

## Where it stands

The Court granted review and also directed the parties to address its own jurisdiction. The case has been fully briefed and argued, with the argument held on October 5, 2026, and the case now awaits decision.

## What each outcome would mean

Suncor and Exxon Mobil, as petitioners, asked the Court to step in and shut down Boulder's lawsuit. If the Court rules for the companies, it would hold that federal law bars Boulder's claims, ending this suit and similar suits elsewhere on the same legal theory. If the Court rules for Boulder, the case would return to Colorado courts, where Boulder's lawsuit could continue toward further proceedings on the merits. The Court could also decide it lacks jurisdiction to hear the case at this stage, which would send the case back without resolving the underlying legal question for either side.
