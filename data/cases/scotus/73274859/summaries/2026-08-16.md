## In brief

Energy companies asked the Supreme Court to decide whether federal law blocks Colorado local governments' state-law lawsuits over climate-change harms from fossil fuel production and marketing.

## What happened

Boulder County and the City of Boulder sued Suncor Energy and Exxon Mobil in Colorado state court, claiming the companies' production, marketing, and sale of fossil fuels contributed to climate change and caused local harms, and seeking damages under state nuisance, trespass, and related claims. The trial court refused to dismiss the suit, and the Colorado Supreme Court affirmed, holding that federal law did not bar the claims. The energy companies then asked the U.S. Supreme Court to review that decision, arguing it conflicts with a federal appeals court ruling and with the companies' view of the Constitution's structure.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked whether federal law, including the Clean Air Act and the Constitution's structure, prevents these local governments from pursuing damages under state law for harms tied to interstate and international greenhouse-gas emissions. The Court also directed the parties to address whether it has authority under the relevant federal statute and under the Constitution to hear this case at all, given that the Colorado Supreme Court's ruling did not finally end the lawsuit.

## Where it stands

The Court granted the petition and added the jurisdictional question on its own. The case has been fully briefed on the merits, numerous outside groups have filed briefs, and oral argument is scheduled for October 5, 2026.

## What each outcome would mean

Suncor and Exxon, as petitioners, asked the Court to rule that federal law bars Boulder's claims. If the Court affirms the Colorado Supreme Court, Boulder's lawsuit continues in state court under state law. If the Court reverses, it would hold that federal law precludes the claims, a result favoring the companies. If the Court instead finds it lacks jurisdiction to hear the case at this stage, the case would return to Colorado courts without a ruling on the preemption question. The Court could also vacate and send the case back without deciding the issue for either side.
