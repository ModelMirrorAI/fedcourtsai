## What happened

Boulder County and the City of Boulder sued Suncor Energy and Exxon Mobil in Colorado state court, seeking damages for local harms they attribute to climate change, arguing the companies deceived the public about fossil fuels' role in climate change and knowingly contributed to it through decades of production and sales. The energy companies argued that federal law bars these state-law claims. A trial court rejected that defense, and the Colorado Supreme Court, reviewing the issue on an interlocutory (mid-case, not final) basis, agreed with the county and city, over a dissent urging the Supreme Court to intervene. The energy companies then asked the Supreme Court to review that decision before the underlying lawsuit is finished.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked whether federal law—either a federal statute regulating air pollution or the structure of the Constitution itself—stops local governments from using state law to sue fossil fuel companies for damages tied to interstate and global greenhouse-gas emissions. The Court also directed the parties to address a threshold question: whether the Court even has authority to hear the case at this stage, given that the Colorado Supreme Court's ruling did not end the lawsuit but only decided one issue along the way.

## Where it stands

The Court granted the petition and added the jurisdictional question for briefing and argument. Merits briefing and numerous amicus (friend-of-the-court) briefs have been filed, and the case has been set for oral argument on October 5, 2026.
