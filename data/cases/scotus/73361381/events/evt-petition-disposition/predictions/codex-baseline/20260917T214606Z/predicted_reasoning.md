The September 16 snapshot records one distribution, entered July 1 for that conference, and no CVSG. I predict zero additional distributions and no call for the Solicitor General's views. The question presented concerns whether due process permits application of a shortened limitations period through an uncodified session-law transition provision. The petition's concession that the transition affords a reasonable time to sue will leave its challenge focused on the distinction between published session law and codified law. The Court will not treat the asserted disagreement over the Ohio provision as a sufficient reason to take this federal notice question. The expected denial will state no merits rationale and will not adjudicate the constitutionality of the Ohio provision.

If review is granted, the Court will most likely take the petition's single question, focused on whether omission of the transition provision from the codified section makes otherwise enacted and published law constitutionally inadequate notice. Plenary briefing and argument are more likely than a summary disposition: P(summary route | any grant) is 0.12. No intervening controlling decision or companion proceeding supporting a GVR is identified in the materials consulted. Conditionally on plenary review, the likely ground would distinguish legislative publication and a reasonable compliance period from individualized notice of an adjudication.

Conditionally on denial, the probability of any noted dissent or statement respecting denial is 0.008. I expect none and do not forecast a particular Justice's private cert vote or an opinion author.
