# Forecast: what the Court will do with No. 25-1291

**Disposition.** The petition will be denied at or shortly after the
September 28, 2026 conference, by an unsigned order in the October 5, 2026
order list (or the first list after the conference). No response will be
called for.

**Relists.** The docket shows one distribution (July 1, 2026, for the
September 28 conference) and has never been relisted. I expect no further
distribution: the petition is a pro se paid filing to which the respondent
filed neither a brief in opposition nor a waiver, and nothing in it invites a
second look. The main residual routes to another distribution are a
call for a response (which I put well under one in ten) or a housekeeping
reschedule off the long conference.

**CVSG.** None. The dispute is a private insurance-contract limitations
question under Ohio law with no federal statute, agency, or federal interest
implicated, so the Solicitor General's views will not be sought.

**Which question, if taken.** The petition's single question asks whether a
State may rely on an uncodified session-law provision to apply a shortened
limitations period to accrued claims consistent with due-process notice. If
the Court took anything it would have to reformulate around whether
publication of a session law is constitutionally adequate notice of a
limitations change, but I expect it to take nothing: the Ohio Tenth District
decided the case on state statutory construction and the Ohio Constitution's
Retroactivity Clause and never passed on a federal due-process claim, which
leaves the federal question unpreserved for purposes of 28 U.S.C. § 1257.

**Summary disposition.** Not the likely route in any scenario. No intervening
decision of this Court bears on the question, so a GVR has nothing to key on;
a summary reversal is implausible on a theory that runs against Texaco v.
Short's statement that a legislature generally need do no more than enact and
publish a law. Conditional on the improbable grant, I still put roughly a
quarter on a cert-order disposition, reflecting the grant family's general
GVR share rather than anything case-specific.

**Dissent from denial.** None expected. No Justice has shown interest in the
notice-by-publication theory in this form, the petitioner is pro se, and the
case presents no recurring or high-stakes issue. A statement respecting denial
is very unlikely.
