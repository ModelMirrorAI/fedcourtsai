## 2. Substantive Certworthiness: A Real 1–7 Split Whose Only Path Up Is Defense Petitions

Petitioners present a single statutory question: whether "appropriate relief" for a defined-contribution-plan participant under ERISA § 502(a)(2), 29 U.S.C. § 1132(a)(2), includes monetary recovery to other participants' individual retirement accounts.

1. **The circuit conflict is genuine and acknowledged.** Between 2023 and February 2026, seven circuits invalidated defined-contribution plan arbitration provisions that barred participants from recovering plan-wide losses extending beyond their own individual accounts, holding that ERISA §§ 502(a)(2) and 409(a) authorize an individual participant to recover all losses to the plan and that barring such relief violates the FAA's "effective vindication" exception: *Harrison v. Envision Management Holding, Inc.*, 59 F.4th 1090 (10th Cir. 2023); *Henry v. Wilmington Trust NA*, 72 F.4th 499 (3d Cir. 2023); *Cedeno v. Sasson*, 100 F.4th 386 (2d Cir. 2024); *Parker v. Tenneco, Inc.*, 114 F.4th 786 (6th Cir. 2024); *Platt v. Sodexo, S.A.*, 148 F.4th 709 (9th Cir. 2025); *Williams v. Shapiro*, 161 F.4th 1313 (11th Cir. 2025); and *Parrott v. International Bancshares Corp.*, 167 F.4th 728 (5th Cir. 2026) (with the Ninth Circuit below following *Platt*). Then, on March 10, 2026, a unanimous Fourth Circuit panel (Judge Richardson, joined by Judges Niemeyer and Agee) broke ranks in *Trauernicht v. Genworth Financial Inc.*, 169 F.4th 459 (4th Cir. 2026), holding—in reversing a Rule 23(b)(1) class-certification order—that under *Massachusetts Mutual Life Insurance Co. v. Russell*, 473 U.S. 134 (1985), *LaRue v. DeWolff, Boberg & Associates, Inc.*, 552 U.S. 248 (2008), and *Thole v. U.S. Bank N.A.*, 590 U.S. 538 (2020), a defined-contribution participant suing individually under § 502(a)(2) can recover only losses affecting his own account. *Trauernicht* expressly noted its disagreement with the arbitration circuits, and the Fourth Circuit denied rehearing en banc on June 4–5, 2026 without a poll.
2. **Strategic asymmetry in petitioning.** CourtListener appellate and district dockets (`ca4` No. 24-1880; `vaed` No. 3:22-cv-00532) show that the *Trauernicht* plaintiffs let their September 2, 2026 certiorari deadline expire without petitioning, and that *Cedeno*, *Parker*, *Platt*, and *Parrott* are all actively litigating in district court with no companion SCOTUS petition pending. Because ERISA's broad venue statute, 29 U.S.C. § 1132(e)(2), lets plaintiffs sue outside the Fourth Circuit while preventing multi-state plan sponsors from administering uniform terms, the plaintiffs' bar has every incentive to preserve its 7–1 advantage rather than seek review from the Fourth Circuit. *Capital Group v. Pover* is thus the first post-*Trauernicht* vehicle presenting the conflict.
3. **Doctrinal salience on the current Court.** The intersection of FAA arbitration enforcement (*American Express Co. v. Italian Colors Restaurant*, 570 U.S. 228 (2013); *Viking River Cruises, Inc. v. Moriana*, 596 U.S. 639 (2022)) and individualized ERISA standing/remedies (*Thole*; Chief Justice Roberts's concurrence and Justice Thomas's concurrence in the judgment in *LaRue*) naturally attracts attention from at least several conservative chambers, explaining the quick CFR.

## 3. Counterweights: Why Denial Remains Three-to-One Likely

Despite the real split and strong representation, three substantial headwinds keep the grant probability down at 25%:

1. **Posture mismatch between *Trauernicht* (Rule 23) and *Pover* (FAA + non-severability blow-up).** *Trauernicht* did not involve an arbitration clause or the effective-vindication doctrine; it held only that defined-contribution plaintiffs could not invoke Rule 23(b)(1) mandatory class certification on the theory that an individual plaintiff already recovers for everyone's accounts without Rule 23(b)(3)'s predominance and opt-out protections. In *Trauernicht*, class-wide recovery remained available if plaintiffs satisfied Rule 23(b)(3). Here, by contrast, Capital Group's Plan Amendment No. 1 (`App. 72a–77a`) combines a class/representative waiver (`§ 19.3(b)`) with an individual-account remedy cap and a non-severability "blow-up" clause (`§ 19.8`) voiding arbitration if those limits fail. Adopting *Trauernicht*'s statutory rule in *an arbitration-waiver case* would mean employers can foreclose both class actions *and* plan-level fiduciary remedies (such as recovery of plan-wide excessive investment-management fees or disgorgement of fiduciary profits beyond one worker's pro rata share). Respondent's upcoming BIO will highlight that *Trauernicht* expressly arose in a Rule 23 posture and never blessed prospective waivers of plan-level remedies.
2. **Idiosyncratic contract-construction issues that divided the panel below.** Although the Ninth Circuit majority resolved threshold delegation and contract construction against Capital Group (`App. 7a–17a`)—making its statutory § 502(a)(2) holding (`App. 17a–23a`) outcome-determinative if reversed—Judge VanDyke's dissent (`App. 24a–39a`) did *not* endorse *Trauernicht* or dispute *Platt*. Instead, Judge VanDyke dissented solely on delegation and on an unusual savings sentence at the end of Capital Group's `§ 19.8` ("the requirements described above shall remain required with the minimum change necessary to allow the arbitration requirement to be permissible under ERISA," `App. 76a`). Petitioners dropped both contract arguments at the cert stage, leaving a record in which the dissenting judge below thought the arbitration clause did not even bar the disputed remedies—a classic BIO argument that the case is a messy, plan-specific vehicle and that Petitioners also skipped questioning the judge-made "effective vindication" step itself.
3. **Defensive-denial risk and cross-ideological circuit alignment on ERISA's text.** Section 409(a) makes a breaching fiduciary liable "to make good *to such plan* any losses *to the plan*," and *LaRue*'s seven-Justice majority reiterated that § 502(a)(2) remedies "plan injuries," not "individual injuries distinct from plan injuries," 552 U.S. at 256. Notably, panels on the Fifth Circuit (*Parrott*), Sixth Circuit (*Parker*), and Eleventh Circuit (*Williams*) joined the Second, Third, Ninth, and Tenth Circuits in reading §§ 502(a)(2) and 409(a) to authorize plan-wide recovery. Faced with seven circuits—including conservative panels—reading the statutory text against Petitioners, conservative Justices who favor arbitration enforcement cannot be confident of five votes to reverse on pure ERISA text, creating substantial incentive either to deny outright and let the post-*Trauernicht* split mature in an FAA case from the Fourth, First, Seventh, Eighth, or D.C. Circuits, or to seek the Solicitor General's views first.
