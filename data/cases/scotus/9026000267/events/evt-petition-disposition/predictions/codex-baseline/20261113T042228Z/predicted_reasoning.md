# Forecast of the Court's action

The Court will deny the petition after considering the requested response, most likely without a separate writing. The petition presents an important disagreement over retirement-plan remedies, but its arbitration posture and the contractual and equitable-relief issues make it an imperfect vehicle for resolving the narrower monetary-recovery question. The modal denial will not announce a view on which circuit has interpreted ERISA correctly.

The snapshot records **one distribution** and no CVSG. I expect **one further distribution** after the opposition, presently due December 2, 2026, and any reply, followed by disposition in early 2027. The probability of at least one additional distribution is **97%**. That includes renewed distribution after a called-for response, not necessarily a true merits-driven relist. On a grant path, one or two additional conference distributions are plausible before review is allowed; a long sequence is not the central forecast.

The Court will probably not call for the Solicitor General's views. I assign **12%** to a new CVSG, most plausibly after the Court considers the completed response papers in early 2027. Such an invitation would defer the ultimate decision while the government prepares its brief.

If the Court grants review, I expect it to take the petition's single statutory question substantially as presented: whether ERISA § 502(a)(2) permits a defined-contribution-plan participant to obtain monetary recovery for other participants' individual retirement accounts. I do not expect a grant framed primarily around whether particular investment funds performed poorly or around the forfeited delegation argument.

Conditional on any grant, plenary briefing and argument are much more likely than a grant accompanied by immediate disposition: **4%** for the latter route. There is no identified intervening decision making remand the obvious course. If plenary review is granted, the remedial scope question will require distinguishing permission to recover account-level losses from a prohibition on broader plan recovery; a merits outcome is not predicted unconditionally here.

Conditional on denial, I assign **5%** to a noted dissent or statement respecting denial. The expected order has none. If a Justice writes, Thomas, Alito, Gorsuch, or Kavanaugh is relatively more plausible than the other Justices, but no author is a likely outcome.
