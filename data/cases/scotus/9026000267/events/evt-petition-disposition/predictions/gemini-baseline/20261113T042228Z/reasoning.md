Parker*, No. 24-559 (denied Jan. 13, 2025) (alongside *Envision Management Holding, Inc. v. Harrison*, 144 S. Ct. 285 (2023)). At the time of those denials, **every** published circuit decision (3d, 6th, 7th, 10th, and 2d) agreed that arbitration provisions barring plan-wide remedies under ERISA §§ 502(a)(2) and 409(a) were unenforceable under the FAA's effective-vindication doctrine.
   On April 20, 2026, however, a unanimous Fourth Circuit panel (Niemeyer, J., joined by Agee and Quattlebaum, JJ.) broke ranks in *Trauernicht v. Genworth Financial Inc.*, 169 F.4th 459 (4th Cir. 2026) (read in full via CourtListener), holding that under *Russell*, *LaRue*, *Thole*, and *Trump v. CASA, Inc.*, 606 U.S. 831 (2025), a defined-contribution-plan participant suing under § 502(a)(2) is restricted to recovering losses to her own individual account (paid through the Plan) and cannot recover for other participants' accounts outside Rule 23. Both the Fourth Circuit (*Trauernicht*, 169 F.4th at 472) and the Ninth Circuit below (App. 20a–21a) explicitly acknowledged the square conflict—now 8–1, with the Ninth Circuit (*Platt* and *Pover*) joining seven sister circuits (2d, 3d, 5th in *Parrott*, 6th, 7th, 10th, and 11th in *Williams*) against the Fourth Circuit. Moreover, the Fourth Circuit denied rehearing en banc in *Trauernicht* on Sept. 16, 2026, locking in the split.

## 4. Downward Adjustments: Severe Vehicle and Merits Obstacles in `appendix.txt`
Close inspection of the Ninth Circuit's majority opinion, Judge VanDyke's dissent, the District Court's ruling, and the Plan's arbitration amendment (App. 1a–74a) reveals **four substantial vehicle problems** that Respondent has not yet briefed (because her BIO is due Dec. 2, 2026) and that keep `P(grant)` well below 50%:

1. **Unchallenged Part B.1.i Contract-Construction Holding (*Platt* / "Representative Basis")**:
   Unlike the arbitration clauses in *Trauernicht* (169 F.4th at 463–64), *Cedeno*, *Smith*, *Harrison*, *Henry*, *Parker*, and *Parrott*—which expressly carved out and permitted § 502(a)(2) claims on behalf of the Plan seeking losses to the *claimant's own individual account* while barring remedies benefiting *other* participants—Capital Group's January 2020 amendment (Section 15.6(c), App. 73a) contains **no individual-account carveout**. It flatly commands that a participant `"must bring any dispute in arbitration on an individual basis only, and not on a class, collective or representative basis"`.
   In **Part B.1.i** (App. 19a–20a), the Ninth Circuit held first that § 502(a)(2) fiduciary-breach claims are *always* `"representative"` in the principal-agent sense (because recovery goes to the Plan under *LaRue* and *Munro*), and second—following *Platt v. Sodexo, S.A.*, 148 F.4th 709, 715, 721 (9th Cir. 2025)—that Capital Group's ban on `"class, collective or representative"` claims prohibits bringing **any** claim in a representative capacity on the Plan's behalf. Tellingly, Judge VanDyke dissented **solely** on Part B.1.i's contract interpretation (and forfeited delegation), while expressly **agreeing** with the majority on Part B.1.ii (the § 502(a)(2) plan-wide relief issue, App. 24a–25a). Yet Capital Group petitioned **only** on Part B.1.ii! Respondent's BIO will powerfully argue that even if the Court adopted *Trauernicht* and confined § 502(a)(2) recovery to Pover's own account, an individual-account § 502(a)(2) suit is still brought in a representative capacity on behalf of the Plan (*LaRue*, 552 U.S. at 253, 256; Pet. 27), which the Ninth Circuit's unchallenged Part B.1.i contract ruling holds is barred by *this* clause and routed to federal court by its non-severability provision. While Petitioners can invoke the clause's `"minimum change necessary"` savings sentence (App. 73a) and Pet. 34's argument about downstream district-court scope, this contract-drafting idiosyncrasy makes *Pover* a markedly dirtier vehicle than a standard *Cedeno*/*Trauernicht* clause.
2. **Putative Rule 23 Class Action Below**: Petitioners' secondary vehicle argument (Pet. 32–34)—that the Ninth Circuit's ruling allows Pover to pursue other participants' account losses in federal court *without* satisfying Rule 23—runs into the record fact that Pover's First Amended Complaint **was** filed as a putative Rule 23 class action (App. 40a, 42a, citing FAC ¶ 8).
3. **Non-Monetary Plan-Wide Equitable Relief Omitted from the QP**: Unlike *Trauernicht*, Pover's complaint explicitly seeks **removal of the breaching fiduciaries** and **reformation of the Plan** under § 409(a) (App. 18a, 43a), and the District Court held independently that the arbitration waiver was invalid because it barred plan-wide removal of fiduciaries (App. 61a–63a). Because Petitioners' QP addresses only *"monetary recovery to other participants' individual retirement accounts,"* reversing on the QP would still leave unresolved whether Pover's prayer for fiduciary removal independently triggers effective vindication and triggers the non-severability poison pill.
4. **Unresolved Threshold Formation Defense & Interlocutory Posture**: Both lower courts declined to reach Pover's threshold contract-formation objection that Capital Group's Committee unilaterally added the arbitration clause in January 2020 without notice or consent from Pover (a participant since 1992) (App. 10a, 49a). In addition, even on the statutory merits, conservative textualists face real tension with 29 U.S.C. § 1109(a)'s text (`"make good to such plan any losses to the plan"`) and Justice Thomas's concurrence in *LaRue*, 552 U.S. at 261–62 (joined by Justice Scalia), which emphasized that 401(k) assets allocated to individual accounts remain Plan trust assets so §§ 409(a) and 502(a)(2) `[o]n their face ... permit recovery of all plan losses`.
