## In brief

An asset-management company asks the Court to decide how much money a retirement-plan participant may recover on the plan's behalf when suing over mismanaged investments.

## What happened

Cathy Pover, a former Capital Group employee, sued the company and plan fiduciaries, alleging they kept underperforming investment funds in the retirement plan to collect fees, breaching duties owed under a federal pension law. Capital Group asked the court to send the dispute to arbitration under a plan provision barring group or representative claims. The district court refused, and the Ninth Circuit affirmed, holding that provision unenforceable because it would block Pover from pursuing the plan-wide recovery the law allows. A dissenting judge disagreed with that reading and would also have let an arbitrator decide the arbitration question itself.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked whether a plan participant who sues over fiduciary mismanagement of a retirement plan with individual accounts may seek money for losses in other participants' accounts, or only for losses in her own account.

## Where it stands

Capital Group petitioned for review in August 2026; Pover waived her right to respond, but the Court then asked for a response, which after an extension is due December 2, 2026. The petition has been distributed for one conference so far, and amici have filed briefs supporting review.

## What each outcome would mean

Capital Group, which lost in the lower court, is asking the Court to take up and reverse that ruling. If the Court grants review, it would hear the case and decide the legal question; a grant alone would not decide who wins. If it denies review, the Ninth Circuit's decision favoring Pover's broader view of recoverable relief stands, and the case continues in the lower courts under that rule. A denial would not mean the Court agreed with that outcome, only that the lower court's ruling remains in place.
