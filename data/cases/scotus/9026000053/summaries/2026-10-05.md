## In brief

Insurers ask the Court to take up a dispute over whether state or federal law decides if they can force a hurricane-damage lawsuit into arbitration.

## What happened

One Lakeside Plaza sued a group of insurers in Louisiana over denied hurricane damage claims. Two foreign insurers were dismissed from the case with prejudice; the remaining domestic insurers tried to compel arbitration, relying on an international treaty governing arbitration agreements. The district court refused, and the Fifth Circuit affirmed, holding that Louisiana law, which bars arbitration clauses in insurance policies, controlled, rather than federal common law. The insurers argue this conflicts with rulings from four other circuits. The petition asks the Court to hold this case until it resolves a companion petition, Indian Harbor Insurance Co. v. Town of Vinton, raising the identical issue, and then decide this case the same way.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked to decide whether state law or federal common law governs when a company that did not sign an arbitration agreement can nonetheless force arbitration under principles of fairness, in cases involving the international treaty on enforcing arbitration agreements. One Lakeside responds that the insurers each have separate contracts with it, that no foreign party is actually involved, and that the treaty does not apply at all, so no such question needs resolving here.

## Where it stands

The petition was distributed for the Court's September 28, 2026 conference, after which the docket shows the case was rescheduled. No decision on whether to hear the case has been recorded.

## What each outcome would mean

Indian Harbor Insurance Co. and the other insurers, as petitioners, are asking the Court to act. Granting review would mean the Court hears the case and decides which body of law controls; it would not by itself decide who wins. Denying review would leave the Fifth Circuit's ruling in place, meaning Louisiana law continues to bar the insurers from compelling arbitration, and One Lakeside's lawsuit proceeds in court. The Court could also hold the petition until it rules on the related Town of Vinton case and then dispose of this one consistently, without separately deciding this case's facts.
