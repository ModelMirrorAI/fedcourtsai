## In brief

Insurers ask the Court to take up, alongside a related case, whether state or federal law governs non-signatories' use of arbitration against a policyholder.

## What happened

One Lakeside Plaza, a Louisiana company, sued eight domestic insurers and two foreign insurers over denied hurricane damage claims. One Lakeside dropped the foreign insurers from the suit. The domestic insurers asked the court to compel arbitration, relying on an arbitration clause and seeking to invoke it through the foreign insurers' agreements under a doctrine called equitable estoppel, which lets a non-signatory enforce or be bound by another party's arbitration agreement in some circumstances. The district court refused, citing a Louisiana law barring arbitration clauses in insurance policies. The Fifth Circuit affirmed, following its recent decision in a similar case called Town of Vinton, finding no meaningful difference between the two.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked whether, for arbitration agreements covered by an international treaty on enforcing arbitration awards, federal or state law decides when a company that did not sign the arbitration agreement can still use it to force arbitration. The insurers say this question is already before the Court in the Town of Vinton case and ask that this petition be held and then decided the same way.

## Where it stands

The petition has been distributed for conference twice, most recently for November 6, 2026, after a reply brief from the insurers was filed; the docket shows no decision yet on whether the Court will hear the case.

## What each outcome would mean

The insurers, as petitioners, are asking the Court to overturn the Fifth Circuit's ruling, which favored One Lakeside. If the Court grants the petition, it would hear the case and decide the legal question itself; a grant would not by itself decide who wins. If the Court denies the petition, the Fifth Circuit's ruling against arbitration stands, leaving One Lakeside free to pursue its claims in court rather than arbitration. The insurers have also asked the Court to hold this case until it resolves the related Town of Vinton petition and then rule consistently, rather than deciding the two separately.
