## What happened

One Lakeside Plaza, a Louisiana property owner, sued its hurricane-damage insurers, eight U.S.-based companies plus two foreign ones, after the insurers allegedly failed to properly pay claims. One Lakeside dropped the foreign insurers from the suit and pursued only the domestic ones. Those insurers tried to force arbitration, arguing they could rely on an arbitration clause tied to the foreign insurers even though they were not signatories to that agreement. The lower courts disagreed, applying Louisiana law, which generally bars arbitration clauses in insurance policies, and ruled that the domestic insurers could not use fairness-based ("equitable estoppel") principles to compel arbitration. The Fifth Circuit affirmed in a brief opinion relying on its recent ruling in a similar case, Town of Vinton, and denied a rehearing request.

## What the Court is being asked

The insurers ask the Court to decide whether federal rules or state rules control when a company that did not sign an arbitration agreement can still force arbitration under fairness principles, in cases involving an international arbitration treaty. The petition does not ask the Court to resolve that question here; instead it asks the Court to hold this case until it acts on a companion case, Town of Vinton, raising the identical issue, and then handle this one the same way. One Lakeside responds that the insurers are separate domestic companies under their own contracts, that Louisiana law bars insurance arbitration clauses regardless, and that the international treaty does not apply because no foreign party remains in the case.

## Where it stands

The petition and the brief opposing it have both been filed, and the case awaits the Court's action, including a decision on the insurers' request to hold this case pending the outcome of the related Town of Vinton case.
