## In brief

A group of insurers asks the Court to hold their case until it decides a related dispute over whether federal or state law governs arbitration disputes with insurers.

## What happened

One Lakeside Plaza sued eight domestic insurers and two foreign insurers in Louisiana state court over denied hurricane damage claims. One Lakeside then dismissed the foreign insurers with prejudice. The domestic insurers, removing the case to federal court, asked the court to compel arbitration, arguing they could enforce an arbitration clause tied to the foreign insurers' involvement. The district court refused, and the Fifth Circuit affirmed, relying on its recent decision in a similar case, Town of Vinton, which held that Louisiana law—not federal law—governs whether a nonsignatory insurer can use equitable estoppel to compel arbitration in these circumstances. One Lakeside opposes further review, arguing the insurers' contract structure means there is no arbitration agreement to enforce.

## What the Court is being asked

The insurers are not really asking the Court to decide the merits now. They ask the Court to hold this petition until it rules on a separate, already-pending petition raising the identical question, then resolve this case the same way. The underlying question is whether federal or state law determines when a company that never signed an arbitration agreement can nonetheless force a dispute into arbitration under the treaty governing international arbitration awards. One Lakeside argues the case does not even present that question properly, because the insurers each have separate contracts with no arbitration clause that survives Louisiana law.

## Where it stands

The petition has been filed, and One Lakeside filed a brief opposing review on August 12, 2026. The docket shows no order yet on the petition or on the request to hold it pending the related case.

## What each outcome would mean

The insurers, as petitioners, asked the Court to act. If the Court grants review and later rules for the insurers in the related case, this case could be sent back for reconsideration under a federal-law standard, which the insurers argue would let them compel arbitration. If the Court denies review or rules against the insurers' position, the Fifth Circuit's decision stands, leaving One Lakeside's lawsuit against the domestic insurers in court rather than arbitration. Denial would not itself decide who is right on the underlying legal question.
