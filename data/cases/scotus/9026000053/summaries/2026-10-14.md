## In brief

Insurance companies ask the Court to hold their case until it resolves the same legal question in a related case.

## What happened

After two hurricanes damaged its property, One Lakeside Plaza sued its insurers, including Indian Harbor Insurance Co. and several others, claiming they failed to pay covered losses. The insurers asked courts to compel arbitration under a policy clause and under a treaty covering international arbitration agreements, arguing that even though most of the insurers were domestic, they could enforce arbitration through a legal doctrine allowing non-signatories to an agreement to invoke it. A federal district court denied arbitration, applying a Louisiana law that bars arbitration clauses in insurance policies. The Fifth Circuit affirmed, relying on its recent decision in a similar case, Town of Vinton, and later denied rehearing.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked whether federal court-made rules or state law should decide when a company that did not sign an arbitration agreement can still force arbitration under the treaty, in a case the insurers say raises the identical question already pending before the Court in Town of Vinton. The insurers argue this question has split the circuits, with most courts applying federal rules and the Fifth Circuit applying state law; One Lakeside disputes that the split exists and defends the Fifth Circuit's approach.

## Where it stands

The petition has been distributed for the Court's conference of September 28, 2026, after full briefing including a response and reply; a docket entry dated August 27, 2026 shows the case was rescheduled, but no entry yet records the Court's action on the petition.

## What each outcome would mean

Indian Harbor Insurance Co. and the other insurer-petitioners are asking the Court to take up the case. If the Court grants the petition, it would hear the case fully and decide which body of law governs; the insurers have also asked the Court to simply hold this petition until it decides the related Town of Vinton case and then handle this one the same way. If the Court denies the petition, the Fifth Circuit's decision stands, leaving in place the ruling that state law governs and that One Lakeside cannot be forced into arbitration.
