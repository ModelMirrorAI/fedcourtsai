## In brief

Insurers ask the Court to take up their dispute with a Louisiana property owner over who decides whether they can force arbitration under an international arbitration treaty.

## What happened

After Hurricanes Laura and Delta damaged its property in 2020, One Lakeside Plaza sued its insurers, including Indian Harbor Insurance and several other domestic companies, for failing to pay its claim. The insurers asked the district court to compel arbitration under a policy clause and under an international treaty governing arbitration agreements, arguing they could enforce arbitration clauses found in contracts with two foreign insurers (since dismissed from the case) even though they themselves were not parties to those foreign contracts. The district court refused, holding that Louisiana law bars arbitration clauses in insurance policies. The Fifth Circuit affirmed, relying on its recent ruling in a similar case, Town of Vinton.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked to decide whether federal rules or state law should determine when a company that did not sign an arbitration agreement can still rely on it to force arbitration, in cases governed by an international arbitration treaty. Indian Harbor says this question is identical to one already pending before the Court in Town of Vinton and asks the Court to hold this case until it rules there.

## Where it stands

The petition has been distributed for conference twice, most recently for a conference after the snapshot date, and the docket shows no decision yet on whether the Court will hear the case.

## What each outcome would mean

Indian Harbor and the other insurers are asking the Court to take up the case. If the Court grants review, it would hear the case in full and decide the legal question, without deciding in advance who wins. If it denies review, the Fifth Circuit's decision applying Louisiana law stands, leaving in place the ruling that blocked the insurers from compelling arbitration, a result favoring One Lakeside. The insurers have also asked the Court to simply hold this case until it rules on the related Town of Vinton case and then resolve this one the same way.
