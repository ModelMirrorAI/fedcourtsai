# Forecast of the Court's action

The Court will ultimately deny this petition. The principal alternative is a hold followed by a grant, vacatur, and remand in light of a decision in Indian Harbor Insurance Co. v. Town of Vinton, No. 25-1383, rather than independent plenary consideration of this case. The probability of any grant, including a GVR, is 25%.

The snapshot records **one distribution**, entered August 26, 2026 for the September 28 conference, followed by **Rescheduled** on August 27. It records no second distribution and no CVSG. I expect one additional distribution before disposition, with two additional distributions a plausible alternative. P(at least one additional distribution) is 80%. The next distribution would most naturally implement the rescheduling; it would not by itself demonstrate that the Court had substantively considered and relisted the petition. A longer hold awaiting the lead case is possible, but I do not forecast repeated substantive relists as the central path.

I expect no call for the Solicitor General's views on this docket: P(a new CVSG here) is 2.5%. Although the Convention supplies a federal treaty interest, this petition presents itself as a follower, and any broader investigation is more likely to center on the lead petition. An invitation on another docket would not satisfy this cell's CVSG claim.

If the Court reaches the question, it will focus on whether federal common law or state law governs nonsignatory equitable estoppel under the New York Convention, as framed in the petition. I do not expect it to take a free-standing question about the correctness of the hurricane-loss adjustment or insurance damages. The likely grant route is a GVR applying an intervening lead-case ruling to the Fifth Circuit's derivative affirmance. Conditional on any grant, I assign an 88% probability to a cert-order disposition rather than plenary review. A direct summary reversal is much less likely than a GVR because the choice-of-law question and the contractual defenses require more than correction of an obvious disregard of an existing holding.

If review is denied, I expect an ordinary order without a dissent or statement respecting denial; P(any such separate writing | denial) is 3%. I do not predict a named Justice or a private cert vote. Denial would leave the lower-court judgment standing without supplying a Supreme Court merits rationale.
