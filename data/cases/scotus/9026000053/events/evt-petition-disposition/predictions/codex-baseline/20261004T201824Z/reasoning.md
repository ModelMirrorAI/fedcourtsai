## Why 25% rather than 5%

The questions presented expressly ask for a hold and treatment consistent with Town of Vinton. The petition describes the decision here as an unpublished, one-paragraph application of that lead decision, and says the lead case is the better plenary vehicle (petition, printed pp. 1–3, 7–9, 16–18). That substantially changes the route distribution: lack of suitability for separate argument does not eliminate the chance of a grant, because a follower can receive a GVR. Conversely, a requested hold is advocacy, not evidence that the Court has agreed to hold the case.

The underlying question has more substance than an ordinary insurance error-correction petition. Petitioners identify an asserted First/Second/Fourth/Ninth-versus-Fifth Circuit conflict over federal versus state equitable-estoppel law under the Convention (petition, pp. 8–16). I treat the breadth and outcome-determinacy of that conflict as contested rather than independently established. A targeted primary-source check confirmed that GE Energy Power Conversion France SAS, Corp. v. Outokumpu Stainless USA, LLC, 590 U.S. 432, 445 (2020), left both the availability of estoppel on its facts and the governing body of law for remand; it did not decide that federal common law must govern. Source: CourtListener lead opinion 9889180, concluding remand discussion. Thus this petition identifies an open question rather than merely demanding enforcement of an already announced answer.

The opposition supplies substantial vehicle and merits resistance. It stresses separate insurance contracts, the dismissal with prejudice of the foreign insurers, and the insured's reliance on its own contracts with domestic defendants rather than on the foreign contracts whose arbitration provisions the defendants seek to invoke (BIO, pp. 1–5, 20–27). It also argues that service-of-suit endorsements supersede arbitration provisions (BIO, pp. 3 and 12–19). Those are respondent's arguments, not assumed independent holdings. They nonetheless create a plausible route to denying review or leaving the judgment intact even if the abstract choice-of-law issue interests the Court. The petition answers that concerted misconduct and the Convention warrant estoppel, but I cannot assume that adopting federal law necessarily produces arbitration in this particular record.

Together, a potentially consequential conflict, a direct link to a lead petition, and the possibility of derivative relief warrant a substantial increase above the approximately 5% prior. The disputed factual fit, unpublished follower posture, and uncertainty about whether the lead case will receive review keep the forecast well below 50%. My **25%** is a judgmental forecast of any grant, not a fitted estimate and not a forecast that this case will itself receive argument. The 88% conditional summary-route probability assigns about 22 percentage points of total probability to cert-order relief and 3 points to other grant routes.
