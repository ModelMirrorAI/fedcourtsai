# Forecast: Indian Harbor Insurance Co. v. One Lakeside Plaza, L.L.C. (No. 26-53)

**Bottom line.** The Court will treat this petition as a hold for *Indian Harbor Insurance Co. v. Town of Vinton* (No. 25-1383), which presents the identical question, and will dispose of it only after Vinton is resolved. Most likely the petition is **denied**, either because Vinton is denied or because Vinton is granted and the Fifth Circuit is affirmed. The grant path is a **GVR** in light of a Vinton merits decision favorable to the insurers. P(any grant) = 0.20.

## Relists and distributions

The docket shows **one distribution** (for the 9/28/2026 conference, entered Aug 26) followed the next day by **"Rescheduled."** That reschedule is not a merits signal about this petition; it tracks Vinton, where the Court called for a response on Jul 27 (due Aug 26, reply filed Sep 9) so that Vinton could not be considered at the long conference without redistribution. Expect this petition to be **redistributed at least once more**, in tandem with Vinton, for an October or November 2026 conference. If Vinton is granted, this petition will then sit undistributed as a hold for the duration of the merits case and be redistributed once more after the Vinton decision (roughly June 2027). If Vinton is denied, both are denied on the same order list, likely by December 2026 after one or two conferences. So: at least one further distribution with near certainty; two or more is the modal path if Vinton is granted.

## CVSG

No CVSG in **this** case. A CVSG is plausible in Vinton itself (the question is one of treaty implementation, and GE Energy had a CVSG), but the Court invites the Solicitor General on the lead petition, not on a companion it is holding. A CVSG in Vinton would push this petition's disposition into late 2027 without touching this docket.

## Which question the Court would take

None in this case directly. The Court would decide the question in Vinton as framed there: whether federal common law or state law governs whether a nonsignatory may invoke equitable estoppel to enforce an arbitration agreement falling under the New York Convention. This petition would not be consolidated; the Fifth Circuit's disposition here is a one-paragraph unpublished affirmance that adds nothing to the Vinton vehicle, and petitioners themselves ask only for a hold.

## Summary disposition versus plenary review

Conditional on any grant, the route is almost certainly a **GVR** "for further consideration in light of" the Vinton decision. A plenary grant of this petition, or a consolidated grant alongside Vinton, is a small residual.

## Dissent from denial

If denied, expect **no separate writing** on this docket. If Vinton is denied with a dissent or statement respecting denial, that writing would attach to Vinton; a companion hold denied the same day ordinarily draws nothing of its own.

## If the Court reaches the merits in Vinton

Conditionally: a reversal would rest on the Convention's and Chapter 2's uniformity interest supplying a federal rule of decision for nonsignatory enforcement, as the First, Second, Fourth, and Ninth Circuits hold; an affirmance would rest on Arthur Andersen plus 9 U.S.C. § 208's residual incorporation of Chapter 1's state-law approach to who is bound. Either outcome flows down to this petition as GVR or denial respectively.
