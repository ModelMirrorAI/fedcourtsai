# Forecast — Indian Harbor Insurance Co. v. One Lakeside Plaza, L.L.C. (No. 26-53)

This petition is an explicit companion to the pending petition in *Indian
Harbor Insurance Co. v. Town of Vinton, Louisiana*, No. 25-1383: it presents
the identical question (whether federal common law or state law governs
equitable estoppel enforcement of New York Convention arbitration agreements
by nonsignatories, the question *GE Energy* expressly reserved) and asks the
Court only to hold it and dispose of it consistently with *Vinton*. Everything
below keys on that structure.

**Distribution and hold.** The record shows zero distributions — the arrival
state this cell forecasts from. The BIO was filed August 12, 2026, so the
petition is fully briefed; expect it to be distributed for the late-September
2026 long conference. From there the Court will treat it as *Vinton*'s
shadow: no independent action while *Vinton* is undecided.

**Relists.** Expect at least one distribution with near-certainty (0.97) —
only a settlement or withdrawal before conference prevents it. The likely
shape is one initial distribution, then a hold with no repeated relisting; if
*Vinton* is granted, one redistribution after *Vinton* is decided (roughly
June 2027), for about two distributions total. This is a hold pattern, not
the serial-relist pattern that signals an impending plenary grant.

**CVSG.** None expected here (0.01). This is a private commercial insurance
dispute; if the Court wanted the Solicitor General's views on the Convention
question it would call for them in *Vinton*, the lead vehicle, not in this
tag-along.

**Which question presented.** The Court will not take this petition's QP
independently: the decision below is a one-paragraph unpublished per curiam
that simply applies *Town of Vinton*, and the petition itself says *Vinton*
is the better vehicle. If plenary review of the question ever happens, it
happens in *Vinton* (or by consolidation, which I put at only a couple of
percent).

**Summary disposition.** Conditional on any grant here, the route is
overwhelmingly a GVR in light of the Court's disposition of *Vinton* (0.93) —
exactly the *Monsanto Co. v. Salas/Johnson* companion pattern of June 30,
2026 that the petition itself invokes. A plenary grant of this docket is a
remote possibility only.

**Disposition and timing.** Most likely outcome: **denied** (~0.78) — either
because *Vinton* is denied (in which case this petition is denied within
weeks, likely October–November 2026), or because *Vinton* is granted and the
Fifth Circuit is affirmed. The grant-family outcome (~0.20) is almost
entirely the GVR path: *Vinton* granted (I put that near 30%), the Fifth
Circuit's judgment disturbed (~75% conditional on grant, given the 4–1 split
runs against it), and this case then GVR'd (~95% conditional on that), which
would land June–July 2027.

**Dissent from denial.** Unlikely on this docket (0.04). If a Justice writes
on a denial of the Convention question, the writing attaches to *Vinton*; a
companion denial rarely draws its own separate writing.
