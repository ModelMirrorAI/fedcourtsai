# Predicted reasoning — Renteria v. New Mexico Office of the Superintendent of Insurance, No. 25-113

Forecast from the CVSG moment: the docket as of the 2025-10-15 snapshot shows the
petition filed July 28, 2025, respondents' waiver, a response requested by the
Court on August 14, a brief in opposition, one cert-stage amicus (Alliance of
Health Care Sharing Ministries), a reply, two distributions (conferences of
9/29/2025 and 10/10/2025), and the October 14, 2025 order inviting the Solicitor
General to file a brief expressing the views of the United States.

## What the Court will do with the petition

**The disposition will wait on the Solicitor General, and it will be a close
call that I put modestly on the deny side (P(any grant) = 0.42).** The CVSG most
naturally targets QP4 — whether the ACA's recognition of health care sharing
ministries preempts New Mexico's order that Gospel Light stop operating or
register as an insurer — since that is where the United States' own statutory
interest lies. If the SG recommends a grant on any question, expect a grant; if
the SG flags the vehicle (interlocutory posture, no factfinding, preservation
gaps the Tenth Circuit itself noted) and recommends denial, expect the Court to
deny, likely with a separate writing. I expect the SG's brief to lean
petitioner-friendly on the merits of the free-exercise and preemption questions
but to be candid about the vehicle; a "deny, or hold for a better vehicle"
recommendation is nearly as likely as a straight grant recommendation.

**Timing.** CVSG briefs invited in October typically arrive the following May
through December. Expect the SG's brief around May–October 2026, redistribution
within two to three weeks of filing, and a disposition either at the end of
OT2025 (June 2026) or, more likely, in the fall 2026 conferences early in
OT2026.

## Relists

The petition sits at two distributions (one relist) with the CVSG intervening.
Mechanically, a CVSG'd petition is redistributed once the SG files, so I expect
**at least one and probably two to three further distributions** — near
certainty (0.96) on at least one more, discounted only for the small chance the
case settles, is withdrawn, or is dismissed before the SG files.

## CVSG

A CVSG is already on the docket (October 14, 2025); the increment claim is
vacuous for this cell. A second invitation is a rarity I put at 0.02.

## Which question the Court would take (context-only)

If it grants, I expect the Court to take **QP4 (ACA preemption)** and either
QP1 or QP2 (the Smith neutrality / general-applicability standards), likely
reformulated into a single question about whether a law's secular exemptions
must be considered in the neutrality and general-applicability analysis. QP3
(whether hostile statements alone establish a violation) is the weakest and
most fact-bound; I expect it to be left behind.

## Summary disposition route

**Unlikely (0.10 conditional on a grant).** A post-CVSG grant is almost always
plenary — the Court does not invite the SG's views to then GVR. The residual
probability covers a GVR in light of an intervening free-exercise decision
(the Court's docket carries other Smith-adjacent cases whose resolution could
supply one).

## If denied

A denial of a CVSG'd religious-liberty petition pressing Smith-standard
questions has a real chance of drawing a separate writing — I put **0.35 on a
dissent from, or statement respecting, denial**. Justices Thomas, Alito, and
Gorsuch have repeatedly written on Smith's framework at the cert stage; a
denial grounded on the interlocutory posture is the kind most likely to draw a
statement noting the issue's importance while accepting the vehicle problem.
