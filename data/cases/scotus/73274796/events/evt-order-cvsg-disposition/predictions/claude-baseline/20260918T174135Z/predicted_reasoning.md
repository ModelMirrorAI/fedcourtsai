# Forecast: what the Court will do with No. 25-113 (Renteria v. New Mexico OSI)

**State forecast from.** The provisioned snapshot (2025-10-15) shows two distributions (Conferences of 9/29/2025 and 10/10/2025), a response requested after the State waived, one amicus brief (Alliance of Health Care Sharing Ministries), a reply, and the CVSG order of 10/14/2025. Forward retrieval (permitted on this cell) adds the docket as it stands on 2026-09-18: the Solicitor General filed on 2026-05-26 recommending that the petition be **held** pending *St. Mary Catholic Parish v. Roy*, No. 25-581 (cert. granted 2026-04-20, from the Tenth Circuit), and that the preemption question be denied; petitioners' supplemental brief (2026-06-09) asked for a grant now or, in the alternative, a hold; the petition was distributed for the 2026-06-25 Conference and has no entry since. No disposition exists.

**Disposition: GVR, probability of any grant 0.70.** The Court has already done what the SG asked: the petition was not acted on at the 2026-06-25 Conference and shows no relist entry, which is the shape of a held petition. It will stay held until *St. Mary Catholic Parish* is decided, argued in the fall of 2026 and decided by the end of June 2027. The Court has ruled for the religious claimant in essentially every Free Exercise merits case of the last decade (*Trinity Lutheran*, *Masterpiece Cakeshop*, *Espinoza*, *Fulton*, *Tandon*, *Carson*, *Kennedy*, *Catholic Charities*, *Mahmoud*), and it took *St. Mary* at the religious petitioners' request from a Tenth Circuit loss; I put reversal there at about 0.85. If *St. Mary* reverses on its first question (whether general applicability requires categorical exemptions for identical secular conduct), the Tenth Circuit's *Renteria* holding that labor-union and fraternal-society exemptions do not undermine general applicability because those bodies serve a different purpose is squarely implicated, and the SG has said so on the record. The modal outcome is therefore a **grant, vacate, and remand** in light of *St. Mary Catholic Parish*, on the first order list after that decision (late June or early July 2027, or the October 2027 Long Conference cleanup if *St. Mary* comes down on the last day).

**Paths to a non-grant (0.30).** (a) *St. Mary* affirms or is decided narrowly on its second question (the *Carson v. Makin* status-based-exclusion theory), leaving the general-applicability standard untouched; the Court then denies *Renteria* rather than granting plenary review of a preliminary-injunction ruling whose regulated entity, Gospel Light, is not a petitioner. (b) A denial even after a favorable *St. Mary* if the Court treats the Tenth Circuit's neutrality holding as an independent ground. (c) Mootness or dismissal if the New Mexico state-court appeal or a business decision by Gospel Light ends the dispute, a small share.

**Relists.** The docket showed two distributions; it has already been distributed a third time (2026-06-25). The held petition will be distributed at least once more when the Court disposes of it after *St. Mary*, so the relist-increment claim is effectively certain.

**CVSG.** Already on the docket (2025-10-14); the increment claim is vacuous here. The brief is in.

**Route.** Conditional on a grant, the cert-order route (GVR) dominates: about 0.93. Plenary review is a small residual, most likely only if the Court wants a companion vehicle on the "identical purposes" comparability test or on QP 3 (official hostility as a standalone violation).

**Which question, if plenary.** The Court would take QP 2 (general applicability and comparability of secular exemptions), probably reframed as the SG framed it, and could pair it with QP 1 on animus; it would leave QP 3 behind and would not take QP 4 (ACA preemption), which the SG says presents no split, rests on an unresolved state-law question, and is partly unpreserved.

**Dissent from denial.** If the petition is denied, about 0.25 that a Justice writes. The record's "GoFundMe" and "scripture" remarks by state counsel are the kind of material that draws a statement from Justice Alito or Justice Thomas, but a hold-then-deny after *St. Mary* usually goes quietly, and a denial most likely means *St. Mary* went the State's way, in which case a dissent from denial is more likely than in an ordinary quiet denial.

**Merits, conditional on plenary review.** The Court would hold that exemptions undermining the State's interest in a similar way defeat general applicability regardless of the exempted body's purpose, reverse the denial of the preliminary injunction on the Free Exercise claim, and not reach preemption.
