# Forecast of the Court's action

From the October 14, 2025 CVSG vantage, the most likely ultimate disposition is **denial of certiorari**, with **44% probability of any grant**, including a GVR or other summary grant route.

The record already shows **two distributions** and the invitation to the Solicitor General. The Court will await the government's submission and then return the petition to conference. I expect one or two additional distribution entries before disposition; the probability of at least one is **97%**. This forecast concerns distribution entries, not a claim that the two existing scheduled conferences both considered the petition substantively. No new CVSG is expected: that step has already occurred.

Measured from the CVSG date, the expected process lasts several months, with a central disposition window in spring through fall 2026. This is a forecast from the frozen record, not a statement that the case remains pending on the run date, and timing is not one of the scored claims.

If the Court denies, the likely procedural explanation is that the preliminary-injunction record and disputed comparator facts make this an unsuitable vehicle to resolve a broader free-exercise issue. Denial will most likely be unexplained; the probability of any noted dissent or statement respecting denial, conditional on denial, is **22%**. I do not predict a public individual cert-vote lineup.

If the Court grants plenary review, the most likely focus is **question two**, formulated around whether comparability turns on risks to the government's regulatory interest rather than the organizations' primary purposes, potentially accompanied by question one's neutrality standard. The Court is less likely to take all four questions as written. The preemption question is a meaningful alternative focus because the government's views may illuminate federal HCSM recognition, but a grant need not reach the petition's broad claim that the ACA disables state regulation.

Conditional on a grant, **plenary briefing and argument are more likely than a disposition in the cert order itself**: the summary-route probability is **25%**. Within that minority branch, a GVR for reconsideration in light of Catholic Charities is more plausible than an immediate, comprehensive summary reversal of the insurance-regulation ruling. If plenary merits review follows, the likely corrective ground would be a risk-based comparator analysis, leaving application to the developed record below rather than announcing categorical immunity for health care sharing ministries.
