## Anchor and adjustments

The primary anchor is the committed statpack's `sal-v4` high-band bracketed reached rate. Pooling all displayed strictly prior Terms, 2017–2024, using the exact JSON rates and weighted denominators gives 314 grant-equivalents / 898 weighted resolved petitions = 34.9666%. This excludes Terms 2025 and 2026. The context and table versions match. These are denial-reweighted counts from the supplied pack, not a newly queried or independently freshness-verified corpus.

The paid-segment CVSG cut provides a closely related descriptive check: 163 resolved petitions, 29.4% ordinary grants plus 5.5% GVRs, or approximately 34.9% any grant. That whole-pack cut is not a separately prior-Term-filtered estimator, so it does not replace the primary anchor. The relist cut shows much higher grant-family shares at terminal counts two and three-plus than at zero or one, but those are terminal buckets, not forward hazards. I do not multiply these overlapping signals or treat them as independent evidence.

I raise the grant probability to **0.44**, leaving denial the likelier disposition, for the following reasons:

- **The legal concern is concrete.** The petition's first two questions challenge an animus-centered neutrality inquiry and a comparator inquiry that distinguishes organizations by their purposes instead of the risks their conduct poses. The petition develops these arguments at pp. 18–27. The panel itself recites the risk-based test but then relies on whether medical payments are primary or ancillary activities (appendix pp. 21a–24a). That juxtaposition, together with a panel dissent, provides a meaningful correction argument rather than merely a broad complaint about burdens on religion.
- **The Court has requested more than ordinary briefing.** The response request followed by a CVSG supports serious interest. I regard the federal statutory and tax-status questions as a plausible reason for asking the United States, not as proof that four Justices favor review. The CVSG signal is largely already in the anchor; the adjustment is for this record's substantive features, not for counting the invitation twice.
- **Catholic Charities adds a limited correction route.** The June 5, 2025 decision rejected a religious-exemption regime that distinguished theological choices and required strict scrutiny. It predates this snapshot and postdates the February 2025 panel decision. It lends support to petitioners' objection to an exclusively animus-based approach, but does not itself decide the comparability of an insurance business and an incidental benefit society. Respondents make that distinction expressly (BIO pp. 16–17). A GVR is plausible, not mechanically required.
- **Vehicle problems keep the estimate below one-half.** This is an appeal from denial of preliminary relief. Respondents dispute whether there is evidence of comparable secular entities engaging in the same conduct (BIO pp. 3–4, 14–16), and the panel notes the evidentiary gap at appendix p. 23a n.9. The record also contains administrative findings of enforceable coverage contracts. A Court accepting those distinctions can view the petition as a fact-specific application of existing rules rather than a clean disagreement over legal standards.
- **The alleged splits and preemption theory are contested.** The petition describes divergent neutrality approaches, but the BIO argues that the circuits consider both text and motive and disputes an outcome-determinative conflict (pp. 19–20). On preemption, the petition emphasizes federal HCSM recognition and nationwide sharing (pp. 30–36); respondents answer that federal exemption from the individual mandate does not immunize contractual insurance activity from state regulation (pp. 23–26). The panel also declined one religion-based membership argument as unpreserved (appendix p. 27a n.11). I do not treat that limited forfeiture as forfeiture of the entire preemption claim, but it narrows the available vehicle.

The combined upward adjustment is approximately nine percentage points, not an empirically fitted coefficient. The principal unresolved variable is what the Solicitor General will recommend and how the government will characterize the relationship between federal recognition and state regulation. No SG brief is in the provisioned record, and I have not retrieved one.
