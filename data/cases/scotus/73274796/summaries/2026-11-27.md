## In brief

Members of a health care sharing ministry ask the Court to revive their First Amendment and preemption challenge to New Mexico's order shutting down their ministry's operations in the state.

## What happened

Breanna Renteria and Laura Smith belong to Gospel Light, a Christian health care sharing ministry recognized under the Affordable Care Act. New Mexico's insurance regulator ordered Gospel Light to stop operating in the state unless it registered and complied with state insurance law, after finding it was acting as an unlicensed insurer. Renteria, Smith, and Gospel Light sued, seeking a preliminary injunction on free exercise and federal preemption grounds. A federal district court denied the injunction, and the Tenth Circuit affirmed, over a dissent, finding the state law neutral and generally applicable and not preempted.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked to decide how strictly courts must assess whether a state law treats religious organizations less favorably than similar secular ones before applying heightened scrutiny, and whether a state's enforcement action against the ministry conflicts with, and is therefore barred by, federal provisions recognizing such ministries.

## Where it stands

The Solicitor General, invited to weigh in, recommended that the Court hold the petition pending its decision in a different case raising an overlapping free exercise question, and that it not take up the preemption issue. The petitioners filed a supplemental brief urging the Court to grant review now rather than hold the case. The docket shows no decision yet.

## What each outcome would mean

Renteria and Smith, the petitioners, are asking the Court to reverse the Tenth Circuit's ruling, which favored the New Mexico insurance office. If the Court grants review and later rules for petitioners, New Mexico's enforcement action against the ministry could be struck down or sent back for reconsideration under a different legal standard. If it rules for New Mexico, the order requiring the ministry to comply with state insurance law or cease operating would stand. If the Court instead denies the petition, or holds it pending the related case and the outcome does not help petitioners, the Tenth Circuit's decision upholding the state's action remains in effect.
