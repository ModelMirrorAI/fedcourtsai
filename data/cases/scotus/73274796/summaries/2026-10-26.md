## In brief

Two members of a Christian health-cost-sharing ministry ask the Court to revive their claim that New Mexico's insurance rules violate their religious-exercise rights and are preempted by federal law.

## What happened

Breanna Renteria and Laura Smith belong to Gospel Light, a health care sharing ministry (HCSM) whose members share medical costs according to shared religious beliefs instead of buying insurance. New Mexico's insurance regulator ordered Gospel Light to stop operating in the state unless it complied with state insurance law, finding it was acting as an unlicensed insurer. Renteria and Smith sued, seeking to block enforcement, arguing the action violated their free-exercise rights and was preempted by federal health-care law. The district court denied a preliminary injunction, and the Tenth Circuit affirmed over a dissent, finding the state law neutral and generally applicable and not preempted.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked whether a state law counts as neutral and evenhanded toward religion only if challengers prove officials acted with subjective hostility, or whether exemptions favoring secular organizations over religious ones can themselves show the law is not evenhanded; and separately, whether federal law recognizing these sharing ministries overrides the state's attempt to require them to follow state insurance regulations.

## Where it stands

The petition was distributed for conferences in September and October 2025 without recorded action, after which the Court invited the Solicitor General to file a brief. The government's brief, filed in May 2026, recommended holding the case pending a decision in a related case and said the preemption question alone did not warrant review. Petitioners then filed a supplemental brief urging the Court to grant or decide the case now. The case was distributed again for a June 2026 conference, and the docket shows no decision yet on the petition.

## What each outcome would mean

Renteria and Smith, the petitioners, are asking the Court to overturn the Tenth Circuit's ruling, which left in place New Mexico's enforcement action against their ministry. If the Court grants the petition, it would hear full arguments and decide the free-exercise and preemption questions; if it denies the petition, the Tenth Circuit's ruling favoring the state regulator stands, and New Mexico's order remains in effect. The Court could also hold the petition until it decides a related case on similar free-exercise questions, which would not resolve this case's outcome but would delay action on it.
