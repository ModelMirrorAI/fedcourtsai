## In brief

Two members of a Christian health-sharing ministry ask the Court to revive their challenge to New Mexico's order that their ministry comply with state insurance law or stop operating.

## What happened

Breanna Renteria and Laura Smith belong to Gospel Light, a health care sharing ministry (HCSM) that lets members pool money for medical costs consistent with their religious beliefs, an arrangement recognized under the Affordable Care Act. New Mexico's insurance regulator found Gospel Light was acting as an unlicensed insurance company and ordered it to stop operating in the state or comply with state insurance law. Petitioners sought a preliminary injunction, arguing this violated their free exercise rights and that federal law preempted the state's action. The district court denied the injunction, and the Tenth Circuit affirmed, over a dissent, finding petitioners unlikely to succeed on either claim.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked whether the lower court used too strict a test for deciding if the state's insurance regulation was neutral toward religion and applied generally to everyone, rather than singling out religious conduct, and separately whether federal law protecting health care sharing ministries bars New Mexico from forcing one to choose between operating as such a ministry or complying with state insurance law.

## Where it stands

The petition was distributed for conference twice, after which the Court invited the Solicitor General to file a brief giving the federal government's views; that brief has now been filed, recommending the petition be held pending a related case, and the matter was distributed again for a further conference.

## What each outcome would mean

Renteria and Smith are asking the Court to reverse the Tenth Circuit's ruling against them. If the Court grants review and later rules for them, the case would go back for reconsideration under a different legal standard; if it rules for New Mexico's insurance office, the Tenth Circuit's decision allowing enforcement would stand. If the Court denies review, the Tenth Circuit's decision against petitioners remains in place. The government's brief also suggests the Court could instead hold the case until it decides a related pending case before acting further.
