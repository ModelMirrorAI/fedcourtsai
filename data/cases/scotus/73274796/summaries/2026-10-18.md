## In brief

Two members of a Christian healthcare-sharing ministry ask the Court to revive their challenge to New Mexico's order that the ministry comply with state insurance law or shut down.

## What happened

Breanna Renteria and Laura Smith belong to Gospel Light, a health care sharing ministry (HCSM) whose members pool money for medical costs consistent with their religious beliefs. New Mexico's insurance regulator found Gospel Light was acting as an unlicensed insurance company and ordered it to stop operating in the state unless it complied with state insurance law. Renteria and Smith sued, arguing this violated their religious-exercise rights and was blocked by federal law recognizing HCSMs. A federal district court denied a preliminary injunction, and the Tenth Circuit affirmed, with one judge dissenting. New Mexico's insurance office opposes further review.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked to decide whether the state insurance rules applied to Gospel Light count as neutral and generally applicable, so that no heightened justification was needed to enforce them against a religious organization, and whether the state's enforcement conflicts with and is barred by federal laws that created and protect health care sharing ministries.

## Where it stands

After the petition, opposition, and reply were filed, the Court invited the Solicitor General to submit the federal government's views. The government's brief, filed in May 2026, recommended holding the case pending the Court's decision in a separate case raising related questions, and said the preemption question alone does not warrant review. The case has been distributed for conference following a supplemental brief from the petitioners, and the docket shows no decision yet on the petition.

## What each outcome would mean

Renteria and Smith, as petitioners, are asking the Court to take up their case. If the Court grants review, it would hear full arguments on the neutrality and preemption questions and decide them, deciding nothing in advance for either side. If the Court denies review, the Tenth Circuit's decision upholding New Mexico's enforcement action against Gospel Light stands. The government has suggested a third path: holding the case until a related case is decided, then acting accordingly, which would also leave the outcome undetermined for now.
