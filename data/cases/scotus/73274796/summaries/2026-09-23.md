## What happened

Breanna Renteria and Laura Smith belong to Gospel Light, a health care sharing ministry (HCSM) whose members pool money for medical costs based on shared religious beliefs rather than buying insurance. New Mexico's insurance regulator found that Gospel Light was operating like an insurance company without meeting state licensing requirements and ordered it to stop operating in New Mexico or comply with state insurance law. The members sought a court order blocking enforcement while the case proceeded, arguing this violated their religious freedom and conflicted with federal health care law. A federal district court and the Tenth Circuit Court of Appeals both declined to block the order, finding the members were unlikely to win their claims.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked to decide how strictly courts should judge whether a state regulation counts as neutral toward religion and applies generally to everyone, including whether a plaintiff must prove officials were deliberately hostile to religion, or whether it is enough that a law treats comparable secular and religious organizations differently. The case also raises whether statements by government officials that seem dismissive of religious beliefs can, by themselves, violate religious freedom protections. Finally, the Court is asked whether a federal law creating tax and insurance-mandate exemptions for these ministries prevents a state from requiring them to follow state insurance rules.
