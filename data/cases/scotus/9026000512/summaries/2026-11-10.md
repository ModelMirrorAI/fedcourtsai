## In brief

A doctor convicted of fraud and sex offenses after a second trial asks the Court to take up his claim that the retrial violated double jeopardy protections.

## What happened

Muhammad Saeed, a physician, was tried on charges including fraudulent schemes, sexual abuse and assault, and prescription-fraud counts. A jury acquitted him of the prescription-fraud counts but could not reach a verdict on the remaining counts. At a second trial on those remaining counts, Saeed argued the state improperly relied on the same prescription-practice evidence and theories the first jury had rejected. He was convicted on all remaining counts. Before sentencing, he asked the trial court to vacate the judgment on double jeopardy grounds; the court denied the motion. The Arizona Court of Appeals dismissed his appeal for lack of jurisdiction, reasoning no sentence had yet been entered, and the Arizona Supreme Court denied review.

## What the Court is being asked

The petition asks the Court to decide whether a defendant who has been found guilty by a jury, but not yet sentenced, can immediately seek federal court review of a claim that the prosecution improperly reused issues an earlier jury had already resolved in his favor, rather than having to wait until after sentencing and a full appeal. It also asks whether a conviction obtained that way can be treated as invalid even before any sentence is imposed.
