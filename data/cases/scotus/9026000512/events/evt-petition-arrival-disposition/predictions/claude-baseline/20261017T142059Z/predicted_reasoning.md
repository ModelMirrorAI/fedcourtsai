# Forecast — Saeed v. Arizona, No. 26-512 (arrival moment)

**Disposition: denied.** The Court will deny certiorari at the petition's first
conference, most likely after the State waives or files a short brief in
opposition.

- **Distributions.** The docket shows zero distributions (petition filed Aug 5,
  2026, docketed Oct 16, 2026; response due Nov 16, 2026). I expect the petition
  to be distributed once for conference — roughly late November or December 2026,
  depending on whether Arizona waives or responds — and **not relisted**. One
  distribution, then denial.
- **CVSG.** None. The respondent is a State, the questions concern a state
  criminal prosecution, and no federal program or federal party interest is
  implicated; the Court will not call for the Solicitor General's views.
- **Which question, if granted.** If the Court took anything it would be a
  reformulated version of QP 1 — whether the Double Jeopardy Clause (via *Abney*)
  requires a post-verdict, pre-sentence avenue for immediate review of an
  *Ashe* collateral-estoppel claim — not QP 1 as written, which asks about
  federal habeas under 28 U.S.C. § 2241, a remedy no Arizona court could grant on
  this judgment. QP 2 (whether judgment must be entered on a void conviction)
  largely turns on Arizona's Rules of Criminal Procedure and would be left behind.
- **Summary route.** A summary disposition is unlikely in absolute terms; no
  intervening decision of this Court bears on the question. Conditional on any
  grant at all, though, a summary vacatur (e.g., a GVR or a per curiam addressing
  the interlocutory posture) is a non-trivial share of the mass — roughly one in
  four.
- **Dissent from denial.** I expect no noted dissent and no statement respecting
  the denial.
