# Forecast of the Court's action

The Court will grant the application to stay the Fourth Circuit's October 7, 2026 mandamus judgment pending the certiorari process. I assign an unqualified substantive grant probability of **0.78**. This forecasts the stay application, not certiorari itself, the ultimate interpretation of the Communications Act, or the temporary administrative stay mentioned in the opposition. An order expressly granting in part and denying in part belongs on the ungranted side of this forecast.

The Chief Justice will refer the application to the full Court, which will resolve it by order rather than leave the final decision to the Circuit Justice alone. The probability of a recorded referral after the frozen, not-yet-referred state is **0.96**. The Court will preserve the agency process and existing advertising arrangements rather than permit the accelerated mandamus deadline to take effect while it considers further review. Any explanation accompanying a grant will emphasize the demanding standard for mandamus, the agency's ongoing proceedings, and the practical instability of renewed intervention so close to the election. It will not need to decide the underlying statutory entitlement to discounted advertising.

The frozen record already shows the Chief Justice's October 8 response request and the October 10 response. I expect no additional response call; the required response-requested increment is stated as **0.00**, with the already-fired rung left to the harness's vacuity mask. The record contains one submitted amicus brief, which counts even without a later acceptance entry. I expect no further distinct qualifying amicus entry or submission before resolution, with **0.20** probability that the count rises above one; conditional on a rise, one additional filer is the most likely shape.

I expect a substantive order within approximately one week of this October 14 forecast, most likely October 14–21, 2026, and before the November 3 election. Timing is an unscored forecast, not a statement that the case has remained pending since the snapshot. The principal alternative is denial on the narrow ground that requiring an FCC decision does not itself change advertising rates and therefore does not inflict the injury addressed by the earlier stay. I do not forecast a merits opinion or assign individual Justice votes.
