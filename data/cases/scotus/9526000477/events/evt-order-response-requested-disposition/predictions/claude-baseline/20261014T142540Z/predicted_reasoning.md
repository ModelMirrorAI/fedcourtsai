# Forecast — 26A477, NRCC v. Brown (stay pending certiorari)

**Disposition: the stay will be granted (unqualified), P ≈ 0.72.** The Court
already stayed the same Fourth Circuit panel's judgment in this same dispute
(No. 26A274, Sept. 4, 2026), holding the court of appeals "likely lacked
statutory jurisdiction" while the candidates' application for FCC review was
pending, and finding irreparable harm to the party committees in the run-up to
the midterms. The new judgment — mandamus ordering the full Commission to
decide that same application by noon Oct. 9, while an expedited comment cycle
(comments Oct. 12, replies Oct. 19) is open — reaches the same end through a
different writ. I expect the Court to treat its Brown I stay as controlling
(cf. *Trump v. Boyle*) and stay the mandamus judgment and mandate pending
certiorari, most likely by a full-Court order with noted dissents from some or
all of the Justices who would have denied Brown I.

The main ways this does not resolve as an unqualified grant:
- **Mootness / dismissal** — if no administrative stay issued before the Oct. 9
  deadline and the Commission ruled on the application, the stay could be
  dismissed or denied as moot, or withdrawn by applicants.
- **Partial relief** — e.g. staying only the deadline while leaving the writ
  in place, which the resolver reads as a denial.
- An outright denial on the view that a mandamus to *decide* (not to rule a
  particular way) inflicts no irreparable harm on the committees.

**Ladder from here.** My record shows only the application (submitted to the
Chief Justice as Circuit Justice, Oct. 8): no response request, no referral, no
amicus briefs on the snapshot. The event itself says the Court has called for a
response, so I expect the response-request rung to show as fired at
resolution (≈0.95). I expect the Chief to **refer the application to the full
Court** (≈0.80), as is customary for a contested, politically salient stay that
follows a full-Court ruling in the same case. Further **amicus** filings are
possible but not likely on the compressed timetable (≈0.35) — broadcaster
associations or other party committees are the plausible filers.

**Timing (unscored).** An administrative stay likely issued on or about Oct.
8–9 alongside the response call; responses due within roughly a week; final
disposition in the second half of October, well before Election Day.

**If granted**, the likely ground restates Brown I: the Fourth Circuit cannot
issue mandamus in aid of a jurisdiction it likely lacks while an application
for review remains pending, and the equities in the weeks before the election
favor the status quo.
