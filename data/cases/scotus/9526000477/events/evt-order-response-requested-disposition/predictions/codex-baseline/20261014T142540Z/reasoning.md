## Why not near certainty

The strongest counterargument is a genuine procedural distinction, not simply resistance to the earlier stay. The October 7 order requires the Commission to decide; it does not prescribe its decision or directly vacate its guidance. The majority, App. 30–34, relies on a duty to pass upon the application, more than five months of delay, and the risk that an election will make review ineffective. Wynn, App. 35–37, expressly argues that completing agency review satisfies rather than evades the prerequisite identified by the Supreme Court. Those arguments weaken both the applicants' categorical no-jurisdiction theory and the asserted immediacy of harm from the mandamus order itself. The agency could decide without changing advertising rates, and later judicial relief would require its own justification. The status-quo characterization is contested in the supplied opinions.

I therefore leave meaningful probability for denial, narrower relief scored as denial, or a procedural termination. Missing opposition text and unobserved developments after October 8 further limit confidence. The application seeks action before noon on October 9, a deadline already past when this prediction is written on October 14. I do not treat it as an upcoming deadline or infer either compliance, a temporary stay, mootness, or a final disposition from its passage.
