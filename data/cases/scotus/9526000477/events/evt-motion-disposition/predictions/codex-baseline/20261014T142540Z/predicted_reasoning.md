# Forecast of the Court's action

The Court will grant the application and stay the Fourth Circuit's October 7 mandamus order requiring an FCC decision by October 9 at noon. The probability of an unqualified grant is **84%**. The forecast concerns the disposing order, not merely a temporary administrative hold.

From the October 8 arrival record, which shows no requested response, no referral, and no amici, I forecast:

- **A response will be requested on an expedited schedule:** 96% probability of a response-request increment.
- **The Chief Justice will refer the application to the full Court:** 94% probability of a referral increment.
- **The amicus count will remain zero:** 35% probability that it rises above zero, counting qualifying submitted briefs and docket entries under the declared rule. One or two new entries are plausible if the Court allows several days for consideration.

Relative to the October 8 submission, I expect immediate handling of the October 9 deadline, potentially by an administrative stay, followed by a substantive disposition within approximately one week. This is an arrival-relative forecast; it does not assert that those dates are future as of the writing date. No timing claim is included in the scored claim block.

The expected intervention will preserve the practical protection of the earlier September 4 stay while the FCC completes its process. If the Court explains the grant, its likely emphasis will be the extraordinary character of mandamus, the disputed basis for forcing an immediate agency decision during ongoing consideration, and disruption to political advertising close to the election. It need not decide the ultimate legality of the lowest-unit-charge interpretation.

The principal alternative is denial on the narrower ground that compelling a decision is different from dictating its substance or reinstating the previously stayed merits judgment. My modal forecast rejects that distinction as sufficient to justify this particular compressed deadline, while leaving the FCC's eventual duty to decide intact. I expect a short order rather than a comprehensive merits opinion; any doctrinal explanation above is conditional on an explanation accompanying the interim ruling.
