## In brief

The United States asks the Court to review a ruling that blocked it from stepping in as defendant in E. Jean Carroll's defamation lawsuit against President Trump.

## What happened

E. Jean Carroll sued Donald Trump for statements he made in 2019 denying her assault allegations. The Attorney General initially certified that Trump's statements fell within the scope of his federal employment, a certification that under a federal law called the Westfall Act can substitute the United States as the defendant in place of an employee. That certification was later withdrawn, the case went to trial, and a jury awarded Carroll $83.3 million. After Trump returned to office, a new Attorney General issued a fresh certification while the case was on appeal. The Second Circuit refused to allow substitution, ruling the request came too late, was waived, and should be denied on fairness grounds.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked whether the appeals court wrongly read the Westfall Act as requiring the government's certification that an employee's conduct was within the scope of federal employment to happen before trial, when the government argues the law only imposes that deadline for removing a case from state to federal court, not for later substituting the United States as defendant once the case is already in federal court.

## Where it stands

The petition was filed in July 2026. The response deadline was extended twice, and the respondent was given permission to file a combined brief opposing both this petition and a related one. The case has not yet been distributed for a conference.

## What each outcome would mean

The United States is asking the Court to reverse the lower court's decision. If the Court takes the case and ultimately agrees with the government, the United States could be substituted as the defendant, which would likely end Carroll's defamation claim because federal law does not allow suits against the government for defamation. If the Court denies the petition or affirms the ruling below, the Second Circuit's decision stands, leaving Trump personally as the defendant and the jury's verdict for Carroll in place. A denial would not mean the Court agreed with the lower court's reasoning, only that the case proceeds no further at the Supreme Court.
