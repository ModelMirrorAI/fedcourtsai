## In brief

The United States asks the Court to review a ruling that blocked the government from substituting itself for President Trump as the defendant in E. Jean Carroll's defamation lawsuit.

## What happened

Carroll sued Trump for defamation over statements he made denying her assault allegations while he was President. The Justice Department initially certified that Trump's statements fell within the scope of his federal employment, which would substitute the United States as the defendant under the Westfall Act, a law that can shield federal employees from certain personal lawsuits. A later Attorney General withdrew that certification, the case went to trial, and a jury awarded Carroll $83.3 million. After Trump was reelected, a new Attorney General tried to recertify the statements as within his official duties. The Second Circuit denied that request, ruling the attempt came too late and had been waived.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked whether the appeals court wrongly read the law governing when the government may certify that an official's conduct was job-related, specifically whether that law stops the Attorney General from making this certification after a trial has already happened in a case that started in state court.

## Where it stands

The petition has been fully briefed, including an amicus brief and a brief in opposition the respondent was permitted to expand in length. It is awaiting the Court's decision on whether to grant review; no conference action is yet reflected in the docket.

## What each outcome would mean

The United States, joined by Trump, is asking the Court to take up the case and reverse the Second Circuit's decision. If the Court grants review and later sides with the government, the United States could be substituted as the defendant, which would likely end Carroll's defamation claim because federal law does not allow such suits against the government for defamation. If the Court denies review, the Second Circuit's ruling stands, leaving Trump personally liable for the jury's award and the case closed to further substitution efforts.
