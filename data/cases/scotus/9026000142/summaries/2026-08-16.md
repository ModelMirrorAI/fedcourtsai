## In brief

The federal government asks the Court to review a ruling that blocked it from replacing President Trump as the defendant in E. Jean Carroll's defamation lawsuit.

## What happened

Carroll sued Trump for statements he made in 2019 denying her assault allegations. Under a federal law called the Westfall Act, the Attorney General can certify that a federal employee's challenged conduct was part of the person's official duties, which lets the government take the employee's place as defendant. An earlier Attorney General certified Trump's statements this way, then a later one withdrew that certification, and the case went to trial without the government substituted in; a jury awarded Carroll $83.3 million. After Trump's later reelection, a new Attorney General recertified the statements as official conduct. The Second Circuit Court of Appeals denied the government's motion to be substituted in, ruling the recertification came too late and was barred by the statute, waived, and unfair to Carroll at that stage. Rehearing was denied.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked whether the appeals court wrongly read the Westfall Act as barring the government from certifying, after a trial has already happened, that a federal employee's earlier conduct was part of his official duties.

## Where it stands

The government filed its petition for certiorari on July 28, 2026, and Carroll's response is due August 31, 2026. The docket shows no further action yet.

## What each outcome would mean

The United States, as petitioner, is asking the Court to take up the case. If the Court grants review, it would hear full arguments and decide whether the recertification was valid, without yet deciding who wins. If the Court denies review, the Second Circuit's ruling stands, leaving the government unable to substitute itself as defendant and leaving Carroll's jury verdict against Trump intact under the lower court's decision.
