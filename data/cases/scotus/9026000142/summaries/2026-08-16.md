## What happened

E. Jean Carroll sued Donald Trump for defamation over statements he made in 2019 denying her assault allegations. The government initially certified that Trump was acting within the scope of his federal employment, which would substitute the United States as the defendant, but the Attorney General later withdrew that certification, and the case proceeded to trial, where a jury awarded Carroll $83.3 million. While Trump's appeal was pending, a new Attorney General recertified the statements as within the scope of his office and asked to substitute the United States as defendant. The Second Circuit denied that request, and rehearing en banc was later denied over dissents.

## What the Court is being asked

The government asks the Court to decide whether a law that lets the Attorney General shield federal employees from certain lawsuits by certifying that their actions were part of their official duties allows a later Attorney General to issue that certification after a trial has already happened, when an earlier certification had been withdrawn before trial. The government argues the law sets no such after-trial cutoff once a case is in federal court, while the lower court read the law as requiring any such certification to come before trial.
