## In brief

The federal government asked the Supreme Court to hear its bid to substitute itself for President Trump as defendant in E. Jean Carroll's defamation lawsuit.

## What happened

E. Jean Carroll sued Donald Trump for defamation over statements he made in 2019 denying her sexual assault allegations. A jury eventually awarded her $83.3 million. Years earlier, the Justice Department had certified that Trump's statements fell within the scope of his job, which would let the government take his place as defendant under a law called the Westfall Act. The department later withdrew that certification, so the case proceeded against Trump personally. After trial and appeal, a new Attorney General tried to recertify and substitute the government back in. The Second Circuit Court of Appeals rejected that move, ruling it came too late and was barred by the statute, by waiver, and by fairness to Carroll.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked to decide whether the appeals court wrongly read the Westfall Act as barring the government from certifying, after a trial has concluded, that an employee's conduct was within the scope of his federal job.

## Where it stands

The petition was filed in July 2026. Carroll filed a brief opposing review on October 30, 2026, after receiving an extension and permission to file a combined response to this petition and a related one. The docket shows no further action since then.

## What each outcome would mean

The United States, as petitioner, is asking the Court to overturn the lower court's decision, which left Trump as the defendant and let Carroll's jury award stand. If the Court grants review, it would hear full arguments and decide whether the government may recertify Trump's conduct as official after trial; granting review decides nothing by itself about who wins. If the Court denies review, the Second Circuit's ruling stands, Trump remains the defendant, and Carroll's judgment is unaffected by this petition.
