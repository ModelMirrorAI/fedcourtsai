## In brief

The United States asks the Court to revive a Westfall Act defense that would substitute the government for President Trump in E. Jean Carroll's defamation lawsuit.

## What happened

E. Jean Carroll sued Donald Trump for statements he made denying her sexual assault allegations. Attorney General Barr initially certified that Trump's statements fell within the scope of his presidential duties, which would substitute the United States as defendant under the Westfall Act. Attorney General Garland later withdrew that certification. The case went to trial, and a jury awarded Carroll $83.3 million. After Trump returned to office, Attorney General Bondi tried to recertify the statements as official conduct, but the Second Circuit ruled this attempt came too late, citing three independent reasons including the statute's text and litigation fairness. The full appeals court declined to rehear the case.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked whether the appeals court wrongly read the law governing substitution of the government as a defendant for certain employee conduct to bar the government from certifying that conduct as official after a trial has already occurred, when the statute's express deadline for certification applies only to removing a case from state court before trial.

## What each outcome would mean

The United States, as petitioner, asks the Court to overturn the lower court's ruling. If the Court grants the petition, it would hear full briefing and argument on whether the government can recertify the conduct as official after trial, a ruling that could allow the United States to replace Trump as defendant and end Carroll's defamation claim under sovereign immunity rules. If the Court denies the petition, the Second Circuit's ruling stands, leaving the jury verdict against Trump in place and the case concluded on that point.
