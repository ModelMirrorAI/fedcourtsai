# Predicted reasoning — United States v. E. Jean Carroll, No. 26-142 (evt-petition-arrival-disposition)

Forecast made at the arrival moment: the docket shows the petition filed July 28,
2026 and docketed July 30, 2026, with a response due August 31, 2026 — zero
distributions, no conference, and no BIO yet. Everything below forecasts forward
from that state.

## Disposition

**The Court grants plenary review (P ≈ 0.65).** The Acting Solicitor General is
the petitioner; the question — whether the Westfall Act bars the Attorney
General's posttrial recertification of a President's conduct — carries a dissent
below (Judge Menashi), a rehearing-stage statement (Judge Chin), and the framing
this Court has proven most receptive to since *Trump v. United States*: personal
liability that "will chill the exercise of executive power by future
Presidents." The petition also offers the Court a statutory route that avoids
the presidential-immunity waiver question raised in the President's separate
companion petition, which is classic grant bait for a Court that wants to
resolve the controversy narrowly. Against that, the petition claims no circuit
split — its "warrants review" section is pure importance — so a denial that
leaves the Second Circuit's judgment standing is a live outcome, not a tail.

## Relists and timing

Expect the respondent to seek and receive at least one extension of the BIO
deadline into September–October 2026, first distribution in **November or
December 2026**, and **one to two relists** before disposition — high-profile
grants are routinely relisted at least once for a clean record, and this
petition will likely be coordinated with the President's companion petition so
the two are considered at the same conference. Disposition of the petition by
**January–February 2027**; if granted, argument in **spring 2027** and a
decision by the end of OT2026 (June 2027).

## CVSG

**None (P ≈ 0.01).** The United States is the petitioner; the Court does not
call for the views of a party, so a CVSG is effectively impossible here.

## Question taken, if taken

The QP **as written** — the Westfall Act timing question. The Court would not
reformulate to reach the underlying scope-of-office merits (which turn on D.C.
respondeat-superior law the D.C. Court of Appeals construed in 2023), and it
would leave the presidential-immunity waiver question to the companion petition,
most likely granting this one and holding or granting the other.

## Summary disposition

**Unlikely (P ≈ 0.05 conditional on a grant).** No intervening decision exists
to GVR on, and a summary reversal of a reasoned, divided court of appeals
ruling on a novel statutory question of this profile would be out of character;
if the Court moves, it sets the case for full briefing and argument.

## If denied

A fair chance (**P ≈ 0.30**) that a denial draws a noted dissent or a statement
respecting denial — most plausibly from Justice Thomas, Justice Alito, or
Justice Gorsuch — given the SG's separation-of-powers framing and the sitting
President's personal stake. Most denials, even of SG petitions, remain silent,
which keeps this below even odds.
