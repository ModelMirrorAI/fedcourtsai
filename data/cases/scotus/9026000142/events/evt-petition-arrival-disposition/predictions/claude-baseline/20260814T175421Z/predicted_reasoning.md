# Forecast: United States v. E. Jean Carroll, No. 26-142

Forecast at arrival. The docket shows **zero distributions** — the petition was
docketed July 30, 2026 with a response due August 31, 2026 — so every claim
below is forecast from that pre-conference state.

## Path to disposition

- **Distribution.** Carroll will file a brief in opposition rather than waive;
  expect one extension of the response date into late September or October.
  First distribution around **late October to mid-November 2026**, for a
  November or early-December conference.
- **Relists.** Expect **one to two relists** before action — the Court's
  now-routine practice of relisting once before granting a petition it intends
  to take, plus the extra care a case implicating the sitting President's
  personal liability invites. I do not expect the long serial-relist pattern of
  a petition the Court is trying to avoid; this one will be resolved within two
  or three conferences of first distribution, i.e. **disposition by roughly
  January 2027**, in time for April 2027 argument if granted.
- **No CVSG.** The United States is the petitioner; the Solicitor General's
  views are already on the table. A CVSG is effectively impossible here.

## Disposition

**Grant (plenary), probability 0.72.** The Court takes the question presented
essentially **as written** — whether the Westfall Act barred the Attorney
General's post-trial recertification — likely reframed only lightly. The
constitutional-avoidance framing (a statutory route that disposes of the whole
suit without reaching presidential immunity) is built for this Court and I
expect it to work. Watch for the Court to **consider this petition alongside
the President's companion petition** from the same judgment on
presidential-immunity waiver (the petition says one is being filed): the
likeliest package is a grant here with the companion **held** pending
disposition, rather than a consolidated grant on the constitutional question.

- **Summary disposition is unlikely.** No intervening decision supports a GVR,
  and a per curiam reversal on a contested statutory question the en banc
  Second Circuit divided over would be a poor fit; if the Court acts, it takes
  the case for argument.
- **If denied**, expect the denial to draw a **dissent from denial or a
  statement** — Justice Thomas and/or Justice Alito are the likeliest authors,
  on the theme Judge Menashi's en banc dissent set out (the Act's text has one
  timing rule, and it governs removal only).
- **If granted**, the likely judgment is **reversal**, with the Court holding
  the Westfall Act imposes no post-removal timing bar on recertification and
  remanding for review of the certification's merits (scope of employment under
  D.C. law) rather than terminating the suit itself in the first instance.
