# Rationale

I assign a 0.74 probability to any grant and predict a plenary grant. The committed `sal-v3` statpack is the main anchor. Pooling the `federal` band's grant-family counts over the strictly prior Terms shown (OT2017 through OT2025) gives 143 grants or GVRs among 201 resolved federal petitions, or 71.14%. This is the correct arrival-time population because the United States is the petitioner and the frozen context places the case in the `federal` band; the zero-distribution state is not the terminal relist-zero population.

I adjust modestly above that anchor. The petition presents a textual federal question about whether Section 2679(d)(2)'s words “before trial” limit only certification for removal or also bar later recertification and substitution after timely removal. Review is supported by the United States' institutional position, the sitting President's asserted exposure to an $83.3 million judgment, a published Second Circuit ruling, and an en banc dissent joined in relevant part by three judges. The petition also offers the Court a statutory route that could avoid broader presidential-immunity questions.

The adjustment is small because the vehicle has serious weaknesses. The petition identifies no square conflict over the unusual certify-decertify-recertify sequence. The lower court relied on statutory timing, waiver, and equitable finality, and even a government victory on timing would leave a fact-intensive scope-of-employment inquiry. Those features make denial plausible and make full review much more likely than a cert-order disposition. The strictly prior paid-petition statpack gives a 34.76% cert-order share among grant-family outcomes (300 of 863), but I reduce the conditional route probability to 0.12 because this dispute lacks an intervening decision suitable for a GVR and its alternate grounds call for plenary treatment.

The `relist-increment` probability is 0.98 because the frozen arrival state has zero distributions: the claim asks whether the distribution count will rise at all, not whether a petition already considered at conference will be relisted again. A paid, properly docketed federal petition of this kind is overwhelmingly likely to receive an initial distribution. The forecast document separately predicts one actual relist after that first conference. The CVSG probability is 0.01 because asking for the federal government's views would be redundant when the United States itself filed the petition. I place the denial-writing probability at 0.38; no published baseline exists, but the en banc dissent and separation-of-powers stakes make a separate writing materially more likely than in an ordinary denial.

The snapshot, questions presented, and petition were available. `documents.json` marks the 157-page petition extraction as truncated, and no brief in opposition was provisioned because the response was not yet due at this arrival moment. The forecast therefore reflects a one-sided record, and the principal uncertainty is whether the opposition can turn the alternate grounds and the government's changes of position into a decisive vehicle objection. A general CourtListener search for preexisting Westfall Act timing authorities was attempted, but the MCP service returned HTTP 429, so I relied on the provisioned record and committed base-rate materials without a direct REST fallback.
