## 2. Case-Specific Adjustments to `P(grant) = 0.38`

### Downward Forces (driving the forecast from ~0.58 down toward 0.38)
1. **Zero circuit conflict and zero recurring footprint (-0.15)**: Neither the Acting Solicitor General (Solicitor General Sauer is recused) nor Judge Menashi's en banc dissent identifies a single conflict among the circuits on whether 28 U.S.C. § 2679(d)(2) permits posttrial recertification in an action removed from state court. Indeed, respondents note without contradiction on the post-enactment record that in nearly 38 years under the Westfall Act, DOJ has never previously withdrawn certification before trial in a removed case, stood by through a civil jury verdict and full appellate briefing, and then sought to recertify on appeal. Nor does the posture threaten future Presidents or federal officers whose certifications are withdrawn by a successor administration, because 28 U.S.C. § 2679(d)(3) expressly empowers any employee to petition the district court *before trial* for judicial certification, with immediate collateral-order review under *Osborn v. Haley*, 549 U.S. 225, 238–39 (2007).
2. **Severe vehicle and waiver obstacles across three alternative holdings (-0.12)**: The Second Circuit rested its denial of the April 2025 substitution motion on three stated grounds: (a) statutory untimeliness under § 2679(d)(2) and (d)(3); (b) explicit waiver after DOJ requested time on remand to reconsider Barr's 2020 certification, Judge Kaplan set a July 13, 2023 deadline for any new or amended certification or substitution motion, and DOJ formally notified the court on July 11, 2023 (in a detailed 2,500-word analysis following *Trump v. Carroll*, 292 A.3d 220 (D.C. 2023), and the *Carroll II* verdict) that it declined to certify; and (c) equitable/finality bars against unwinding years of litigation and an $83.3M Seventh Amendment jury verdict via a two-sentence post-briefing recertification. Even if textualist Justices question reading "before trial" in § 2679(d)(2) into post-removal substitution, institutionalist Justices (particularly Chief Justice Roberts and Justices Barrett and Kavanaugh) are typically reluctant to override Rule 16 scheduling orders and intentional pretrial disclaimers to undo a completed jury trial.
3. **Non-dispositive remand under *Lamagno* (-0.05)**: The SG pitches `26-142` as a clean statutory constitutional-avoidance off-ramp to spare the Court from deciding presidential-immunity waiver in No. `26-141`. Yet under *Gutierrez de Martinez v. Lamagno*, 515 U.S. 417, 434 (1995), an Attorney General's certification is conclusive only for removal, *not* for substitution. Reversing the Second Circuit's timeliness bar would merely remand the case for contested, fact-intensive scope-of-employment litigation under D.C. *respondeat superior* law (where the D.C. Court of Appeals already held in *Trump v. Carroll*, 292 A.3d at 240, that subjective motivation is central and unresolved as a matter of law, and where Carroll also preserves her threshold contention that the President is not an "employee of the government" under 28 U.S.C. § 2671).
4. **Revealed Court behavior in *Carroll II* (`No. 25-573`) (-0.04)**: Corpus lookup of `25-573` shows that the Court distributed *Carroll II* 16 times (`distribution_count = 16`) before denying certiorari without noted dissent on June 29, 2026 (`146 S. Ct. 2789`) and denying rehearing on August 17, 2026. While *Carroll II* lacked an SG petition and concerned pre- and post-presidential conduct, it demonstrates strong resistance on this Court to intervening in the *Carroll* litigation.

### Countervailing Upward Forces (+0.16 over what vehicle flaws alone would imply)
1. **Sitting President + $83.3M judgment for in-office White House press statements + Rule of Four**: Unlike *Carroll II* ($5M for a 1996 assault and 2022 post-presidency statements), *Carroll I* imposes $83.3 million (~$100M with interest) in personal liability on the sitting President for June 2019 statements issued via the White House Press Office and on the White House lawn. United States*, 603 U.S. 593, 629 (2024) (highlighting presidential public communications as part of official responsibilities), only **four Justices** are needed to grant review.
2. **Serious statutory text hook and 3-judge en banc dissent including Chief Judge Livingston**: The SG's textual contrast between § 2679(d)(1) (no "before trial" limit in federal-commenced suits), § 2679(d)(2) ("before trial" attached to removal, omitted in the substitution sentence), § 2679(d)(3) ("before trial" attached to employee petitions), and Pub.
