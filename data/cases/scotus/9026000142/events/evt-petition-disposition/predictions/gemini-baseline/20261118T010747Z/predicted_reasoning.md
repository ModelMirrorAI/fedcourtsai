At least six Justices will decline plenary or summary intervention because the petition presents no circuit conflict, arises from a one-off post-judgment posture in which the Department of Justice affirmatively withdrew its Westfall Act certification before a District Court scheduling deadline in July 2023 while the individual defendant declined to invoke his statutory pretrial remedy under 28 U.S.C. § 2679(d)(3), and would not terminate the litigation even if reversed because scope-of-employment certification remains subject to contested, fact-intensive *de novo* judicial review under District of Columbia law pursuant to *Gutierrez de Martinez v. If the Court instead grants review, plenary briefing and oral argument will be far likelier than a summary disposition or grant-vacate-remand (GVR) order. Although the Acting Solicitor General's reply brief (echoing No. 26-141) requests in the alternative a GVR in light of *Trump v. United States*, 603 U.S. 593 (2024), *Trump v. United States* was decided in July 2024 — more than a year before the Second Circuit's panel decisions and nearly two years before en banc denial — and does not address Westfall Act certification timing under 28 U.S.C. § 2679(d)(2). Conditional on a plenary grant, the Court would take the petition's sole Westfall Act Question Presented as written (likely consolidated or argued in tandem with No. 26-141) and would focus on whether § 2679(d)(2)'s "at any time before trial" clause restricts only removal from state court rather than post-removal substitution, leaving ultimate District of Columbia scope-of-employment factfinding for remand.

Conditional on denial of certiorari, an unnoted denial order is the likeliest result, as occurred in *Carroll II* (*Trump v. Carroll*, No. 25-573). There is nevertheless a substantial chance (~28%) that one or more Justices note a dissent from, or file a statement respecting, the denial of certiorari — most likely authored by Justice Thomas or Justice Alito (potentially joined by Justice Gorsuch) emphasizing the text of 28 U.S.C. § 2679(d)(2) and the Presidency's institutional interests echoed in Judge Menashi's dissent from the denial of rehearing en banc.
