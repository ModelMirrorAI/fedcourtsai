# Forecast — United States v. Carroll, No. 26-142

The docket shows one distribution, for the conference of December 4, 2026. The Court will not dispose of the petition at that conference. It will be rescheduled or relisted at least once, and probably several times, alongside the President's own petition in No. 26-141, as happened with the earlier Carroll petition (No. 25-573).

The Court will ultimately deny certiorari. The question presented arises from a sequence that has not occurred before and is unlikely to recur (certification, withdrawal, and post-trial recertification across three administrations), there is no conflict among the circuits, the Second Circuit gave alternative grounds, and a reversal would only send the case back for a fact-bound scope-of-employment inquiry.

There will be no call for the views of the Solicitor General; the United States is the petitioner.

If the Court does grant, it will take the Westfall Act question as the government wrote it, most likely together with the waiver-of-presidential-immunity question in No. 26-141, and set the cases for argument rather than decide them summarily. In that event the likely ground is textual: the "before trial" limit in 28 U.S.C. 2679(d)(2) governs removal and not substitution, with a remand for the scope-of-employment question. A summary reversal or a later GVR is possible but less likely than plenary review.

If the Court denies, the denial will most likely come without any noted dissent or statement. If anyone writes, expect Justice Thomas or Justice Alito, addressing the Second Circuit's reading of the Westfall Act or the treatment of presidential immunity as waivable.
