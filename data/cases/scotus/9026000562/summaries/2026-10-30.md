## In brief

Two self-represented former tenants ask the Court to review South Carolina's refusal to let them use electronic court filing in their landlord dispute.

## What happened

James McNeil and Meaghan Poyer, representing themselves, sued their former landlord and property managers in South Carolina state court over a security deposit, lease issues, and related claims. Because South Carolina's electronic filing system is limited to licensed attorneys, they had to file paper documents by hand, which they say were scanned into lower-quality, non-searchable images that degraded photo evidence central to their privacy claims. The trial court eventually dismissed their case entirely as a sanction for what it found to be abusive and frivolous filings. The South Carolina Supreme Court separately declined to order the trial court to rule faster on their motions. McNeil and Poyer then petitioned the U.S. Supreme Court, naming the circuit court as respondent.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked whether it violates constitutional guarantees of fair treatment and equal legal process for a state court system to let only lawyers file documents electronically, while requiring people representing themselves to file paper copies that come out lower-quality once scanned into the court record.

## Where it stands

The petition was filed on August 19, 2026, and docketed on October 28, 2026. The respondent's response is due November 27, 2026; the Court has not yet acted on the petition.

## What each outcome would mean

McNeil and Poyer, as petitioners, are asking the Court to take up their case. If the Court grants review, it would hear full arguments and decide whether the electronic-filing rule violates their constitutional rights, without yet deciding who wins. If the Court denies review, the South Carolina Supreme Court's decision not to intervene in the filing dispute stands, and the underlying case dismissal remains as decided by the South Carolina courts, leaving McNeil and Poyer without further recourse in this proceeding.
