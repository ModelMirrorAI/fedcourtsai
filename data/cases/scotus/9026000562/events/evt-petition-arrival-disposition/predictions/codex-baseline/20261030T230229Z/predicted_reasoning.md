# Forecast of the Court's treatment

The Court will deny the petition without an explanatory opinion or separate writing. The May 21 state order declining original mandamus jurisdiction will remain undisturbed; denial will not decide whether attorney-only electronic filing is constitutional.

The baseline shows **zero distributions**. The petition will receive one ordinary conference distribution, then be denied without a further distribution. P(at least one distribution from this zero-distribution state) is **0.99**, the event's `relist-increment` claim. A second distribution, including a reschedule, has only about a **2%** chance; the central forecast is **zero actual relists after initial distribution**.

The Court will not call for the Solicitor General's views. P(a new CVSG) is **0.001**. No invitation date is forecast.

If the Court grants review, it will most likely take the single question in a narrowed form: whether unequal filing procedures that materially impair preservation of a self-represented litigant's evidence deny due process or equal protection. It would not need to decide an unrestricted constitutional right to electronic filing, the merits of the landlord dispute, or every alleged instance of scheduling disparity. Conditional merits consideration would focus on meaningful access and evidence preservation rather than treating lawyer and nonlawyer filing methods as necessarily identical.

Conditional on any grant, ordinary merits briefing and argument is the likelier route (**85%**), versus **15%** for a GVR or another disposition in the cert order. There is no identified intervening decision or companion grant that makes a summary remand the central forecast.

Conditional on denial, P(a noted dissent or statement respecting denial) is **0.006**. No Justice is predicted to write. Among the very small individual writing probabilities, Sotomayor and Jackson have the largest, followed by Gorsuch; these are not predictions of recorded votes.
