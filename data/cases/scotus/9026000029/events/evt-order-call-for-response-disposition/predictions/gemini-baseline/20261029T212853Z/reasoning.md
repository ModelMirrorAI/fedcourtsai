### A. Arguments Favoring Certiorari or Summary Reversal (Upward Adjustments)
1. **Substantial Doctrinal Tension with *Daimler*, *BNSF*, *Bristol-Myers Squibb*, and *Ford Motor*:**
   - **Question Presented 1 (General Jurisdiction):** Both Mead Johnson & Company, LLC and Mead Johnson Nutrition Company are incorporated in Delaware and relocated their headquarters ("nerve center") from Illinois to Evansville, Indiana in April 2022—before these 51 complaints were filed in late 2022 and 2023. The Illinois Appellate Court held (`App. 20a–30a`) that Petitioners were nonetheless "essentially at home" in Illinois under the "exceptional case" footnote of *Daimler AG v. Bauman*, 571 U.S. 117, 139 n.19 (2014), because they had been headquartered in Illinois for over a decade before April 2022, leased part of one floor in a Chicago building with ~35–38 remote/hybrid employees (including six executives), maintained bank accounts with JPMorgan in Illinois, and had previously filed suits in Illinois courts. Treating a corporate headquarters move coupled with a retained satellite office of three dozen employees as a *Perkins*-style "surrogate principal place of business" stretches *Daimler* and *BNSF Railway Co. v. Tyrrell*, 581 U.S. 402 (2017), well past their recognized bounds.
   - **Question Presented 2 (Specific Jurisdiction):** The 51 respondents reside in 22 other States, and their infants were born, fed Petitioners' preterm infant formula (`Enfamil`), injured (developing necrotizing enterocolitis, or NEC), and treated entirely outside Illinois (`Pet. 8–9; App. 2a–4a`). In *Bristol-Myers Squibb Co. v. Superior Court*, 582 U.S. 255 (2017), the Court squarely held that a state court lacks specific jurisdiction over non-residents' claims for out-of-state injuries even when the defendant conducts extensive research, marketing, or distribution in the forum State. In *Ford Motor Co. v. Montana Eighth Judicial District Court*, 592 U.S. 351 (2021), the Court emphasized that the plaintiffs were forum residents injured in the forum State. Here, the Fifth District invoked *Ford*'s "relate to" prong to sustain specific jurisdiction over non-resident, out-of-state injury claims based on clinical trials at Rush University, national marketing roles of Illinois employees, and proximity to an Indiana warehouse (`App. 30a–38a`).
2. **Finality Under *Cox Broadcasting* (`28 U.S.C. § 1257(a)`):**
   - As Petitioners note (`Pet. 3–4`), a state-court judgment finally rejecting a federal due-process challenge to personal jurisdiction is reviewable under the fourth category of *Cox Broadcasting Corp. v. Cohn*, 420 U.S. 469, 482–83 (1975), where the only remaining proceedings are merits trials and a serious federal question would otherwise be eroded by forcing the defendant to litigate to final judgment.
3. **High-Stakes Mass-Tort Context:**
   - The NEC preterm formula litigation involves hundreds of lawsuits across the N.D. Ill. MDL (*In re Abbott Laboratories, et al., Preterm Infant Nutrition Products Liability Litigation*, MDL No. 3026; see *Abdullah v. Mead Johnson & Co.*, 7th Cir. July 20, 2026) and Illinois state courts in St. Clair and Madison Counties (including a $60M verdict in *Watson v. Mead Johnson*, 2026 IL App (5th) 240936, reversed for a new trial in June 2026). The participation of the American Academy of Pediatrics warning of threats to neonatal intensive care nutrition products gives the petition unusual policy weight for a personal-jurisdiction dispute.

### B. Vehicle & Doctrinal Obstacles Favoring Denial (Downward Adjustments)
1. **Error-Correction Posture Rather Than a Genuine Split of Authority:**
   - The Illinois Appellate Court did not repudiate *Daimler*, *BNSF*, *Bristol-Myers Squibb*, or *Ford Motor*, nor did it announce a categorical rule of law that conflicts with another state high court or federal court of appeals. Rather, it recited the governing Supreme Court standards verbatim and applied them to a unique, transitional set of facts: a corporation that was undisputedly headquartered in Illinois for more than a decade during the period when many of the challenged product labeling, marketing, and clinical-study decisions took place, and that moved its official nerve center to Indiana only months before the suits were filed while leaving several C-suite and marketing executives working out of its Chicago office.
2. **Dual Independent Holdings Below (General AND Specific Jurisdiction) Create a Severe Vehicle Problem:**
   - Because the Fifth District affirmed personal jurisdiction on **both** general jurisdiction (QP 1) and specific jurisdiction (QP 2), Petitioners must win on **both** questions to disturb the judgment below.
   - Even if several Justices view the Fifth District's general-jurisdiction analysis (QP 1) as a clear misapplication of *Daimler*'s exceptional-case footnote, reaching that question cannot change the outcome unless the Court also reverses the specific-jurisdiction holding (QP 2).
   - And on QP 2, Respondents' forthcoming brief in opposition (written by Ashley Keller) will have a forceful vehicle argument: unlike in *Bristol-Myers Squibb* (where BMS was headquartered in New York and New Jersey, not California), Mead Johnson's actual principal place of business ("nerve center") was in Glenview, Illinois during the years when many of the 51 infants were born and fed the formula and when the national warnings and marketing strategies challenged in the complaints were formulated. Whether a state court may exercise specific jurisdiction over claims arising from corporate decisions made while the company was headquartered in that State—or whether individual plaintiffs among the 51 were born before vs. after the April 2022 relocation—was decided on a paper-only record without an evidentiary hearing (`App. 8a–9a`), making this an awkward vehicle for a clean constitutional rule.
3. **Interlocutory Posture & Reluctance to Police Fact-Bound State Applications:**
   - While *Cox Broadcasting* permits review when the Court grants certiorari to resolve a major doctrinal question (as in *Bristol-Myers*, *Ford*, and *Mallory*), the Court routinely denies interlocutory state-court personal-jurisdiction petitions where the dispute turns on the fact-specific application of settled standards rather than a recurring statutory or doctrinal split.
