## 2. Disposition Route and Dissent from Denial

- **Disposition:** The Court will **deny the petition for a writ of certiorari** (`predicted_disposition: "denied"`). Once Respondents' brief in opposition is filed, the Court will conclude that the decision of the Appellate Court of Illinois, Fifth District (*Toles v. Mead Johnson & Co., LLC*, 2025 IL App (5th) 231205) turns on a fact-bound record involving Mead Johnson's April 2022 corporate headquarters transition from Illinois to Indiana and pre-relocation Illinois headquarters conduct, rather than a clean legal split over Fourteenth Amendment personal jurisdiction. Because the lower court relied on independent holdings of both general jurisdiction and specific jurisdiction—and because the specific-jurisdiction record across the 51 consolidated plaintiffs is entangled with corporate labeling, clinical-study, and marketing decisions made while Mead Johnson was still headquartered in Illinois—the Court will view the case as an unsuitable interlocutory vehicle.
- **Summary Disposition vs. Plenary Review (If Granted):** Conditional on the Court granting review (`P(grant) = 0.16`), plenary briefing and argument is the likelier route (`65%` of grants), while a summary disposition in the cert order itself accounts for `35%` of grants (`summary-disposition-route: 0.35`). Specifically, a summary GVR is unlikely because no intervening Supreme Court decision has altered the Fourteenth Amendment framework since the Fifth District ruled, but Petitioners explicitly request summary reversal in the alternative, and if four or more Justices decide to intervene solely to correct the Fifth District's overbroad application of *Daimler* and *Bristol-Myers Squibb*, a per curiam summary reversal is a live possibility.
- **Separate Writing on Denial:** Should the Court deny certiorari as predicted, we expect **no noted dissent or separate statement** (`dissent-from-denial: 0.06`, i.e., a 94% probability of a clean summary denial order). If a separate writing were to appear on the denial order, it would most likely be a brief dissent or statement from Justice Thomas, Justice Alito, or Justice Gorsuch criticizing the Fifth District's expansion of *Daimler*'s "exceptional case" footnote and *Ford Motor*'s "relate to" standard.
