## Why materially above baseline

The Court affirmatively requested a response after distribution. This is evidence of interest, though it requires much less agreement than granting review. The four amicus submissions independently indicate that the dispute is not merely a single plaintiff's factual complaint. More importantly, the appended appellate opinion itself supplies substantial tension with the Court's jurisdiction precedents, rather than leaving that tension solely as the petitioner's characterization.

On general jurisdiction, appendix paragraphs 18 and 21–23 rely on historical headquarters and continued Illinois operations after the asserted relocation. The temporal question is important: former headquarters should not automatically become perpetual all-purpose jurisdiction. On specific jurisdiction, paragraphs 38–40 acknowledge that the challenged infants did not consume the products, become ill, or receive treatment in Illinois, but emphasize Illinois marketing of the same products. The retrieved Ford Motor opinion, 592 U.S. 351 (2021), involved resident plaintiffs injured in the forum; its discussion of Bristol-Myers distinguishes the absence of a suit-specific connection. The appellate opinion's reasoning therefore presents a plausible error-correction target even without a developed split between appellate courts.

## Why denial remains substantially likelier

First, this is an interlocutory state-court ruling, not a final liability judgment. The petition invokes 28 U.S.C. § 1257(a) but its jurisdiction section does not develop an exception to finality. Cox Broadcasting Corp. v. Cohn, 420 U.S. 469 (1975), recognizes limited routes to review with further state proceedings pending; it does not make the state supreme court's refusal of discretionary review sufficient by itself. Whether an exception permits immediate review here is unresolved on this record. I treat that as a substantial vehicle risk, not as a definitive jurisdictional bar.

Second, the appendix complicates the petition's assertion that the company unquestionably left Illinois. Paragraphs 17–22 describe disputed corporate representations, a 2021 principal-place-of-business filing, a public headquarters designation, and continuing national marketing functions in Illinois. These facts do not necessarily satisfy Daimler's demanding standard, but they make a clean legal-only summary reversal less certain. There are also two alternative jurisdictional grounds to address. The court's forfeiture discussion concerning the separate reasonableness argument adds briefing complexity; I do not treat it as a forfeiture of the entire minimum-contacts challenge.

Third, the petition predominantly seeks correction of an asserted misapplication of existing law. It cites a contrary federal district-court result, Harden, but that is not itself a mature appellate split, and the Illinois court distinguishes that record. The petition's assertions about product necessity, litigation volume, settlement pressure, and hostile forums are advocacy, not findings that I independently verified. The federal constitutional issues matter, but this petition does not ask the Court to resolve the underlying medical evidence or product liability.
