# Predicted course — Mead Johnson & Co. v. Greear, No. 26-29

**State forecast from.** Paid petition from the Appellate Court of Illinois, Fifth
District (Illinois Supreme Court denied leave Mar. 25, 2026). Distributed once (for
the 9/28/2026 conference); respondents had not responded, and on Sept. 1, 2026 the
Court **requested a response** (due Oct. 1). No CVSG. Four amicus filings on the
record (Atlantic Legal Foundation/FDCC; Chamber of Commerce; DRI/Illinois Defense
Counsel by motion; American Academy of Pediatrics).

**Disposition.** Denied is the single likeliest outcome, but this petition is a
genuine contender: about a 27% chance of some grant (plenary grant or summary
reversal). The call for a response is the Court's first sign of attention; the
petition presents a facially sharp tension with *Bristol-Myers Squibb v. Superior
Court* (2017) — nonresident plaintiffs, nonresident injuries, jurisdiction resting
on the defendant's general in-state marketing — and with *Daimler*/*BNSF v. Tyrrell*
on "at home" general jurisdiction premised on "dozens" of in-state employees.

**Relists.** Once the response (and any reply) is in, the petition will be
distributed again — near-certain. I expect at least one relist before disposition
if the Court is weighing a summary reversal (it typically relists such cases
several times); a straight denial would come at the first post-response
conference, likely in November–December 2026.

**CVSG.** Not expected: no federal party or federal statute is involved, and the
Court rarely seeks the SG's views in a private due-process jurisdiction dispute.

**Which question, if granted.** Most likely QP 1 as a reformulated, cleaner
question — whether general jurisdiction is measured at the time suit is filed
(versus the time of the underlying conduct) for a corporation that moved its
principal place of business — since that is the genuinely open doctrinal point.
QP 2 (specific jurisdiction) is the stronger summary-reversal hook, because the
lower court's statement that no authority requires the plaintiff to reside or be
injured in the forum is hard to square with *BMS*.

**Route if granted.** Mostly plenary review (~70%); a summary per curiam
reversal on the specific-jurisdiction holding is a real minority route (~30%),
which the petition expressly requests. A GVR is unlikely — no intervening
decision bears on the question.

**If denied.** Expect no separate writing most likely; a short dissent or
statement (Alito or Gorsuch/Thomas the plausible authors) is a low-probability
outcome. If the Court summarily reverses, a dissent from the summary disposition
(e.g., Sotomayor or Jackson, objecting to resolving it without briefing) is
possible.
