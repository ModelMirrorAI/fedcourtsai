The Supreme Court is very likely to deny Mead Johnson's petition for review, with only about a 1 in 40 (2.5%) chance of a grant. Mead Johnson asks the Court to decide whether an Illinois state court violated constitutional due process—the fairness rules limiting where a defendant can be sued—by allowing 51 out-of-state families to sue the company in Illinois over preterm infant formula injuries that occurred in other states. Although the Illinois appellate court used an unusually broad theory of jurisdiction, the plaintiffs waived their right to respond and the lower court relied on case-specific factual findings that Mead Johnson still held itself out as headquartered in Illinois in sworn government filings and contracted to package products there, making this an unattractive vehicle for the Supreme Court to review.
