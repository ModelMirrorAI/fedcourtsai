## 3. Case-Specific Adjustments from the Provisioned Record

Starting from the **4.45%** pooled prior-Term `baseline` (`reached`) anchor, I adjust downward to **2.5% (`0.025`)** based on the interplay between the doctrinal attractiveness of the petition and its substantial procedural and vehicle obstacles:

### Upward Factors (Why P(grant) is above the ~1.4% zero-relist terminal floor)
1. **Aggressive lower-court personal-jurisdiction holdings in a high-stakes mass tort:** The Appellate Court of Illinois, Fifth District (*Toles v. Mead Johnson & Co.*, 2025 IL App (5th) 231205; App. 1a–64a) affirmed the St. Clair County Circuit Court's exercise of both general and specific personal jurisdiction over Mead Johnson as to 51 non-resident plaintiffs from 22 States whose preterm infants were born, fed Enfamil cow's-milk formula or fortifier products, and developed necrotizing enterocolitis (NEC) entirely outside Illinois:
   - On **general jurisdiction (QP 1)**, the Fifth District held that because Mead Johnson's corporate headquarters was in Illinois from May 2009 through the end of 2018, Mead Johnson "was not a nonresident defendant during this period" and therefore remained subject to general jurisdiction in lawsuits filed in 2021–2023 for the 38 plaintiff infants born between May 2009 and 2018 (App. 10a–13a). It also held that Mead Johnson remained "essentially at home" in Illinois for 15 post-2018 plaintiffs because its corporate relocation to Indiana was "not complete" (App. 12a–13a).
   - On **specific jurisdiction (QP 2)**, the Fifth District held that all non-resident plaintiffs' claims "arose out of or related to" Mead Johnson's Illinois contacts under *Ford Motor Co. v. Montana Eighth Judicial District Court*, 592 U.S. 351 (2021), because Mead Johnson continuously marketed and sold preterm infant formula in Illinois, held the Illinois WIC contract, conducted clinical studies and R&D in Illinois, and contracted with Catalent Pharma Solutions in Woodstock, Illinois to package Liquid Human Milk Fortifier (App. 17a–21a, 81a–88a)—even though none of the 51 non-resident infants ingested formula in Illinois or suffered injury there. That holding is in sharp tension with *Bristol-Myers Squibb Co. v. Superior Court*, 582 U.S. 255 (2017), and the Illinois Supreme Court's own precedent in *Rios v. Bayer Corp.*, 2020 IL 125020.
2. **Sophisticated counsel and institutional amicus support:** Petitioners are represented by experienced Supreme Court litigators at Hogan Lovells (Sean Marotta, Catherine Stetson) and Covington & Burling, and are supported by an amicus brief from the Chamber of Commerce of the United States, the American Tort Reform Association, and the Illinois Chamber of Commerce.

### Downward Factors (Why P(grant) is below the 4.45% `baseline` reached anchor)
1. **Waiver of response with no Call for Response (CFR) prior to distribution:** Respondents (represented by Ashley Keller of Keller Postman LLC) filed a waiver of their right to respond on October 9, 2026, and the petition was distributed on October 28, 2026 for the November 13, 2026 conference without a CFR having been issued. While a Justice can still request a response before or at conference, the vast majority of petitions distributed on a waiver without a pre-conference CFR are denied at their first conference.
2. **Overlapping alternative holdings and severe vehicle entanglement on General Jurisdiction (QP 1):**
   - For 53 of the non-resident plaintiffs (all except the 11 born before May 2009), the judgment below rests independently on *both* general and specific jurisdiction. Reversing as to those plaintiffs requires reaching and reversing on *both* QPs.
   - Crucially, the state courts' general-jurisdiction ruling did not rest purely on the abstract legal proposition that a former principal place of business at the time of injury suffices for general jurisdiction years later. Rather, the circuit court and appellate court made extensive factual findings (App. 10a, 12a–13a, 69a–74a) that Mead Johnson's transition to Indiana was "not complete in substance" when the suits began because: (a) on March 7, 2021, Mead Johnson's Vice President and General Counsel affirmed "under penalties of perjury" to the Illinois Secretary of State that its principal place of business was Chicago, Illinois; (b) Mead Johnson's website and state corporate filings through July 2022 continued to list Chicago as its global headquarters; (c) in federal court pleadings in November 2020 (*Clean Label Project Foundation*, D.D.C.) and August–September 2021 (*Robinson*, S.D. Ind.), Mead Johnson affirmatively represented that its principal place of business was in Illinois and denied that it was based in Indiana; and (d) key national hospital-marketing and commercial-training directors remained based in Illinois. That record of sworn representations and judicial filings makes QP 1 a poor, fact-bound vehicle for addressing historical general jurisdiction.
3. **Fact-bound alternative links on Specific Jurisdiction (QP 2) and intermediate state-court posture:** On specific jurisdiction, the lower courts relied not merely on nationwide sales and former headquarters, but also on clinical trials conducted at Illinois hospitals and a 15-year contract with Catalent Pharma Solutions in Woodstock, Illinois to manufacture, test, and package Mead Johnson's Liquid Human Milk Fortifier (App. 14a n.3, 81a–88a). Moreover, the decision below is an interlocutory ruling of a single intermediate state appellate district (the Illinois Supreme Court denied discretionary leave to appeal on March 25, 2026, App. 94a, and having already applied *Bristol-Myers Squibb* faithfully in *Rios v. Bayer Corp.*, 2020 IL 125020, creates no state high-court split). In addition, Mead Johnson remains a defendant in the same coordinated St. Clair County proceeding on claims brought by Illinois residents (*Toles*, *Pagan*), complicating any argument under the fourth category of *Cox Broadcasting Corp. v. Cohn*, 420 U.S. 469 (1975), that reversal would terminate the coordinated state-court litigation against Mead Johnson.

## 4. Rationale for Secondary Claims and Distributions

- **`relist-increment` (`0.11`):** Because respondents waived their response, the petition will be distributed a second time only if the Court calls for a response (or reschedules/relists at the November 13 conference). Weighing the Chamber amicus brief and the aggressive reasoning of the Fifth District against the waiver and fact-bound record, I estimate an 11% probability that the Court calls for a response or otherwise distributes the petition again.
- **`cvsg-increment` (`0.005`):** Personal-jurisdiction disputes in private state-law tort litigation almost never draw a CVSG unless foreign affairs, federal statutory preemption, or sovereign immunity is implicated.
- **`summary-disposition-route` (`0.15`):** Conditional on a grant, full merits review (~85%) is much likelier than a summary disposition (~15%), as there is no pending Supreme Court personal-jurisdiction case for a GVR hold and the dual-ground, fact-heavy record is ill-suited to summary per curiam reversal.
- **`dissent-from-denial` (`0.015`) and `order-writers`:** Conditional on denial, a separate writing is unlikely (~1.5%), especially if denied without a CFR. Should a Justice write separately (most plausibly if a response is called for and cert is subsequently denied), Justices Thomas (`0.006`), Alito (`0.005`), and Gorsuch (`0.005`) have the highest small individual probabilities given their past interest in personal-jurisdiction and mass-tort due process limits.
