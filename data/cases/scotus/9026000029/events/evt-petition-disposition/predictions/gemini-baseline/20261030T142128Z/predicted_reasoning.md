- **Further distributions / relists (`relist-increment`: 0.11):** The Court is most likely to deny the petition on the first distribution following the November 13, 2026 conference without calling for a response or relisting. The petition presents pure Fourteenth Amendment Due Process personal-jurisdiction questions arising out of state-court product-liability tort suits between private parties, implicating no federal statute, treaty, agency program administration, or federal sovereign interest.

## Scope of Review and Questions Presented (If Granted)

If the Court were to call for a response and ultimately grant certiorari, it would have to grant review on **both Question Presented 1 (general jurisdiction) and Question Presented 2 (specific jurisdiction)** as written, rather than taking either question in isolation:

- For 53 of the non-resident plaintiff infants (the 38 born between May 2009 and the end of 2018 while Mead Johnson was headquartered in Illinois, plus the 15 born after 2018), the Illinois Appellate Court relied on *both* general personal jurisdiction and specific personal jurisdiction as independent bases to affirm the denial of Mead Johnson's motion to dismiss. For the remaining 11 non-resident plaintiff infants born prior to May 2009, the appellate court relied solely on specific personal jurisdiction.
- Consequently, taking Question 1 alone would not disturb the judgment below as to any plaintiff unless the Court also reversed on Question 2; taking Question 2 alone would resolve only the 11 pre-May 2009 claims while leaving the 53 post-May 2009 claims standing on the state court's alternative general-jurisdiction holding.
- If granted on the merits, the Court would likely reverse and hold (1) under *Daimler AG v. Bauman*, 571 U.S. 117 (2014), and *Hertz Corp. v. Friend*, 559 U.S. 77 (2010), that general personal jurisdiction depends on whether the defendant corporation is "essentially at home" in the forum State at the time suit is filed rather than when the underlying injury occurred years earlier, and that residual sales-management personnel do not render a former headquarters state a continuing corporate home; and (2) under *Bristol-Myers Squibb Co. v. Superior Court*, 582 U.S. 255 (2017), and *Ford Motor Co. v. Montana Eighth Judicial District Court*, 592 U.S. 351 (2021), that specific personal jurisdiction does not extend to claims of out-of-state plaintiffs whose infants were born, ingested the product, and suffered injury entirely outside the forum State merely because the manufacturer marketed the same product in the forum, conducted earlier clinical studies there, or contracted with an in-state packager for one product line.

## Summary Disposition vs. Plenary Review

- **Summary disposition route (`summary-disposition-route`: 0.15 conditional on grant):** Conditional on granting review (after calling for a response), the Court is substantially more likely (~85%) to set the case for plenary briefing and oral argument than to issue a summary reversal or GVR (~15%). No pending Supreme Court case presents an overlapping personal-jurisdiction question that would trigger a holding-pattern GVR, and the presence of alternative, fact-intensive state-court findings regarding Mead Johnson's post-2018 corporate registrations and in-state contract manufacturing would make a summary per curiam reversal less likely than full merits review.

## Separate Writings on Denial

- **Dissent from or statement respecting denial (`dissent-from-denial`: 0.015 conditional on denial):** The Court will most likely issue a routine, unsigned denial order with no noted dissents or separate statements. In the unlikely event a separate writing accompanies a denial (primarily if the Court first calls for a response and then denies certiorari), Justices Thomas, Alito, or Gorsuch are the likeliest authors of a brief dissent from denial or statement respecting denial highlighting the tension between the Fifth District's ruling and *Daimler* / *Bristol-Myers Squibb*.
