# Certiorari Prediction: *Mead Johnson & Company, LLC, et al. v. Greear, et al.*, No. 26-29

## I. Overview and Procedural Posture

Petitioners Mead Johnson & Company, LLC and Mead Johnson Nutrition Company (collectively, "Mead Johnson") manufacture specialized cow's-milk-based infant nutrition products (`Enfamil Human Milk Fortifier`, `Enfamil Premature`, and `Enfamil EnfaCare`) used for premature and very-low-birthweight infants in neonatal intensive care units (NICUs). Respondents in this petition are plaintiffs representing 51 infants from 22 States outside Illinois who developed necrotizing enterocolitis (NEC) after being fed preterm formula or fortifier products in out-of-state hospitals. Respondents filed product-liability actions in the Circuit Court of St. Clair County, Illinois—often naming both Mead Johnson and Illinois-headquartered Abbott Laboratories—which were coordinated for pretrial and discovery purposes under *Toles v. Mead Johnson & Co.*, No. 21-L-981.

Mead Johnson moved to dismiss the claims of the non-Illinois plaintiffs for lack of personal jurisdiction. Following extensive jurisdictional discovery and an evidentiary hearing, the St. Clair County Circuit Court denied the motions on October 30, 2023, holding that Mead Johnson was subject to both general and specific personal jurisdiction in Illinois (Pet. App. 65a–93a). On interlocutory appeal under Illinois Supreme Court Rule 306(a)(3), the Appellate Court of Illinois, Fifth District, affirmed on both grounds in a published opinion, *Toles v. Mead Johnson & Co.*, 2025 IL App (5th) 231205 (Dec. 11, 2025) (Pet. App. 1a–64a). The Illinois Supreme Court denied Mead Johnson's petition for leave to appeal on March 25, 2026 (Pet. App. 94a).

Mead Johnson filed this petition for a writ of certiorari on July 6, 2026 (docketed July 8, 2026), presenting two questions:
1. Whether a state court can assert general personal jurisdiction over corporate defendants when those defendants have previously relocated their principal place of business from the State.
2. Whether the state court erred in asserting specific jurisdiction over out-of-state defendants where the State has no connection to any plaintiff or to any plaintiff's alleged injury.

## III. Legal Merits vs. Vehicle and Finality Obstacles

### A. Why the Petition Prompted a Call for Response
On the face of the Fifth District's opinion, Petitioners identify two conspicuous doctrinal flaws under Fourteenth Amendment Due Process precedent:
1. **Specific Jurisdiction Under *Bristol-Myers Squibb* and *Ford Motor***: In affirming specific jurisdiction over non-Illinois plaintiffs whose infants were born, fed formula, and injured entirely outside Illinois, the Fifth District did not cite *Bristol-Myers Squibb Co. v. Superior Court*, 582 U.S. 255 (2017) (*BMS*), and stated that "Mead Johnson cites no authority for the proposition that a plaintiff must reside or be injured in a forum state for the state to assert personal jurisdiction" (Pet. App. 20a). It reasoned that specific jurisdiction existed because Mead Johnson had marketed the same preterm formula products in Illinois continuously since 2000 and held the exclusive Illinois WIC contract (Pet. App. 18a–21a). That reasoning directly conflicts with *BMS*, 582 U.S. at 264–65, and *Ford Motor Co. v. Montana Eighth Judicial District Court*, 592 U.S. 351, 362 n.3, 369–70 (2021), which held that in-state sales and marketing of the same product to other persons do not support specific jurisdiction over nonresident plaintiffs injured outside the forum.
2. **Temporal Scope of General Jurisdiction Under *Daimler***: Relying on pre-*Daimler* state appellate precedent (*Riemer v. KSL Recreation Corp.*, 348 Ill. App. 3d 26, 35 (2004)), the Fifth District stated that general jurisdiction examines a corporation's contacts over a continuous period beginning "approximately when the claims arose and extend[ing] to when suit was filed," and held that because Mead Johnson's corporate headquarters was in Chicago from May 2009 through 2018 (when 38 of the infants were born), Mead Johnson "was not a nonresident defendant during this period" (Pet. App. 10a–13a, 23a). Assessing general "at home" jurisdiction based on where a defendant was headquartered years before suit was filed is in serious tension with *Daimler AG v. Bauman*, 571 U.S. 117 (2014), and *Goodyear Dunlop Tires Operations, S.A. v. Brown*, 564 U.S. 915 (2011).

### B. Why Certiorari (or Summary Reversal) Will Likely Be Denied After the Brief in Opposition
Despite those facial errors in the Fifth District's opinion, Respondents will have compelling vehicle, record, and finality arguments in their forthcoming Brief in Opposition that make plenary review or summary reversal unlikely:

1. **Dual Independent Grounds (Both General and Specific Jurisdiction Must Fall) and Fact-Bound Alternative Holdings on General Jurisdiction**:
   Because personal jurisdiction requires *either* general *or* specific jurisdiction, the Supreme Court cannot change the judgment below unless it reverses *both* holdings. Crucially, the lower courts did not rest general jurisdiction solely on Mead Johnson's historical 2009–2018 Chicago headquarters. After an evidentiary hearing, the circuit court made an explicit factual finding—affirmed by the Fifth District under the manifest-weight-of-the-evidence standard—that **Mead Johnson had not completed relocating its headquarters from Illinois to Indiana when these suits were filed in 2021–2022** (Pet. App. 10a, 12a–13a, 70a–74a). Specifically, the record showed:
   - On March 7, 2021, Mead Johnson's Vice President and General Counsel of Nutrition affirmed to the Illinois Secretary of State "under penalties of perjury" that Mead Johnson's principal place of business was Chicago, Illinois (Pet. App. 10a, 72a).
   - In federal court pleadings in November 2020 (*Clean Label Project*), August 2021 (*Sanchez*), and September 2021 (*Robinson v. Mead Johnson Nutrition Co.*, No. 3:21-cv-132 (S.D. Ind.)—where Mead Johnson *affirmatively denied* in its Answer that it was based in Indiana), Mead Johnson represented to federal courts that its principal place of business was in Illinois (Pet. App. 72a–73a).
   - Mead Johnson's website continued to identify Chicago as its Global Headquarters when the litigation began, its public corporate filings in multiple states listed Illinois as its principal place of business through July 2022, and it retained its Chicago office and national marketing/training leadership in Illinois (Pet. App. 10a, 12a–13a, 70a–72a).

   Thus, Question 1's premise—that Mead Johnson had "previously relocated [its] principal place of business from the State"—depends on overturning record-specific factual findings grounded in Mead Johnson's own sworn 2021 representations to courts and regulators. And if general jurisdiction stands on that fact-bound ground (or even raises a disputed factual question), Question 2 on specific jurisdiction becomes advisory because it cannot alter the judgment.

2. **Extensive In-State Product Development, Clinical Trials, and Contract Manufacturing Distinguishing *BMS***:
   Even as to specific jurisdiction, Respondents will point out that unlike *BMS*—where the defendant "did not develop Plavix in California, did not create a marketing strategy for Plavix in California, and did not manufacture, label, package, or work on the regulatory approval of the product in California," 582 U.S. at 259—the trial court made extensive findings connecting the design, clinical testing, regulatory approval, national marketing strategy, and manufacturing of Mead Johnson's preterm fortifiers to Illinois (Pet. App. 14a & n.3, 81a–88a):
   - Mead Johnson was undisputedly headquartered in Illinois from May 2009 through at least 2018, the exact window when 38 of the infants were born and when national warnings and marketing strategies were directed from Chicago.
   - Mead Johnson contracted from 2007 onward with **Catalent Pharma Solutions in Woodstock, Illinois** to manufacture, inspect, package, and label its Liquid Human Milk Fortifier (Pet. App. 14a n.3, 83a, 87a–88a).
   - Mead Johnson conducted its key clinical trials in Illinois hospitals (including the Illinois DHA and ARA Study, the Illinois Powdered Fortifier Study at Lutheran General Hospital in Park Ridge, and the 2011 Illinois Liquid Fortifier Study in Park Ridge and Oak Lawn), submitted that Illinois research to the FDA, and required its national sales force to distribute the resulting *Pediatrics* article to NICU physicians nationwide to refute concerns that its fortifier increased the risk of NEC (Pet. App. 81a–87a).

   Even if the Fifth District's broad stream-of-commerce reasoning was flawed, these record facts make this case a poor vehicle for addressing pure out-of-state specific jurisdiction.

3. **Interlocutory Posture and Lack of Conflict in the Illinois Supreme Court**:
   While the Court has occasionally treated state-court personal-jurisdiction rulings as final under the fourth category of *Cox Broadcasting Corp. v. Cohn*, 420 U.S. 469, 482–83 (1975), the decision here comes in an interlocutory posture from a single intermediate state appellate panel after the Illinois Supreme Court denied discretionary leave to appeal under Rule 315. Unlike *BMS*, *Ford Motor*, *BNSF*, and *Mallory*—all of which reviewed decisions of state courts of last resort—the Illinois Supreme Court has faithfully enforced *Daimler* and *BMS* in *Aspen American Insurance Co. v. Interstate Warehousing, Inc.*, 2017 IL 121281, and *Rios v. Bayer Corp.*, 2020 IL 125020. The dispute below is an intermediate appellate court's fact-laden effort to distinguish *Rios* and *Aspen* based on Mead Johnson's decade-long Illinois headquarters, sworn 2021 corporate filings, and Illinois R&D/co-manufacturing ties.

## IV. Conclusion and Probability Distribution

Weighing the positive signals (CFR, four amicus briefs, elite counsel, and loose language in the Fifth District's opinion) against the formidable vehicle obstacles (dual independent holdings below, sworn 2021 corporate filings contradicting Mead Johnson's relocation claim, extensive Illinois headquarters/R&D/Catalent manufacturing ties during 2009–2018, and interlocutory posture from an intermediate state court), I forecast that certiorari will be **denied** with probability **0.89**, **granted** for plenary review with probability **0.07**, resolved by **summary-reversal** with probability **0.03**, **gvr** at **0.005**, **dismissed** at **0.003**, and **withdrawn** at **0.002**.
