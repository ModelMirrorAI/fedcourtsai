# Predicted course — Mead Johnson & Co. v. Greear, No. 26-29

**Docket state I forecast from.** Distributed once (for the 9/28/2026 long
conference). Before that conference the Court **requested a response**; the
respondents' time was extended to October 30, 2026. No CVSG. Four amicus
filings at the cert stage (Atlantic Legal Foundation; U.S. Chamber of Commerce;
DRI by motion; American Academy of Pediatrics).

**Further distributions.** Near-certain. Once the brief in opposition is in
(due Oct 30) and the reply window runs, the petition will be distributed again,
most likely for a conference in late November or December 2026. I expect at
least one further distribution and a meaningful chance (~25%) of one or more
relists beyond that, since the petition's real route to success is the Court
holding it over to consider a summary reversal or a grant.

**CVSG.** Very unlikely: private parties on both sides, state-court personal
jurisdiction under the Due Process Clause, no federal statute or federal
programme in play.

**Disposition.** Most likely **denied**. The likeliest non-denial is a plenary
grant on QP 2 (specific jurisdiction); a **summary reversal** is a real
alternative because the Illinois Appellate Court found specific jurisdiction
over non-resident plaintiffs whose infants were not fed, injured or treated in
Illinois without citing *Bristol-Myers Squibb* — the posture the Court
described in *Ford Motor* as forum shopping. The denial case rests on the
interlocutory posture (a Rule 306 appeal from denial of a motion to dismiss,
with the Illinois Supreme Court declining leave — the BIO will likely press
§1257 finality), the fact that the general-jurisdiction holding gives an
independent ground for a subset of plaintiffs, and the "and/or Abbott"
pleadings that keep the cases in state court regardless.

**Question taken, if granted.** QP 2 as written (specific jurisdiction where no
plaintiff or injury is connected to the forum), possibly with QP 1 (whether
general jurisdiction is measured at filing rather than at the time of conduct)
— QP 1 is the more genuinely open question and the one a plenary grant would
add. If granted, the likely ground is that *BMS* controls and that marketing to
other Illinois hospitals does not make these claims "relate to" forum contacts.

**Summary route.** Conditional on any grant, ~30% that it comes as a summary
reversal (per curiam) on the specific-jurisdiction holding rather than
briefing and argument.

**Separate writing on a denial.** Unlikely (~10%). If anyone writes, Alito
(author of *BMS*) or Thomas/Gorsuch (who have written on personal-jurisdiction
first principles) are the candidates.
