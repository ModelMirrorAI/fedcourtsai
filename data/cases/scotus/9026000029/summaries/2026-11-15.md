## In brief

Mead Johnson asks the Court to review an Illinois ruling that state courts there may hear out-of-state infant-formula injury claims against it.

## What happened

Dozens of plaintiffs from across the country, whose infants allegedly developed a serious intestinal illness after being fed Mead Johnson's and Abbott's preterm formula products, sued in St. Clair County, Illinois. Mead Johnson argued Illinois courts lacked personal jurisdiction over it because the company had moved its headquarters to Indiana before most suits were filed and because the plaintiffs and their injuries had no connection to Illinois. The trial court disagreed, and the Appellate Court of Illinois, Fifth District, affirmed on interlocutory appeal, finding both general and specific jurisdiction. The Illinois Supreme Court denied further review.

## What the Court is being asked

The case raises two questions about when a state's courts may exercise power over an out-of-state company. The first is whether a company can still be treated as "at home" in a state, and so subject to suit there on any claim, years after it moved its main offices elsewhere. The second is whether a state can hear claims brought by out-of-state residents over injuries that occurred outside the state, based on the company's broader business activities within the state.

## Where it stands

The petition has been fully briefed, including an opposition brief and a reply, and was distributed for the Court's conference after earlier being distributed once before and having a response requested. The docket shows no decision yet on whether the Court will take the case.

## What each outcome would mean

Mead Johnson, which lost in the lower courts, is asking the Court to hear the case and reverse. If the Court grants review and later rules for Mead Johnson, the lawsuits by out-of-state plaintiffs would be dismissed from Illinois courts, likely requiring them to refile elsewhere. If it rules for the plaintiffs (Greear and others), the case returns to the Illinois trial court for further litigation there. If the Court denies review, the Illinois appellate ruling stands, and the lawsuits continue in Illinois courts.
