## In brief

Mead Johnson asks the Court to review an Illinois ruling that its contacts with the state let courts there hear out-of-state infants' injury claims.

## What happened

Dozens of families sued Mead Johnson and Abbott Laboratories in Illinois state court, claiming infant formula and fortifier products caused their premature infants to develop a serious intestinal disease. Many plaintiffs do not live in Illinois and say their infants were never treated there. The trial court denied Mead Johnson's motion to dismiss for lack of personal jurisdiction, finding both that Illinois courts have general authority over Mead Johnson because of its past and ongoing Illinois presence, and specific authority because the claims relate to Mead Johnson's Illinois activities. The Illinois Appellate Court affirmed, and the Illinois Supreme Court declined further review.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked whether Illinois courts may treat a company as subject to suit on any claim there, based on the state once having been the company's main base of operations, even years after the company says it relocated. It is also asked whether Illinois courts may hear claims by out-of-state residents over injuries that occurred outside Illinois, based on the company's marketing and business activities within the state.

## Where it stands

The petition has been fully briefed, including an opposition brief and several amicus briefs, and was distributed for the Court's September 28, 2026 conference. The docket does not show the Court has yet acted on it since that conference.

## What each outcome would mean

Mead Johnson, which lost in the lower courts, is asking the Court to take up the case and reverse. If the Court denies review, the Illinois appellate ruling stands, letting these lawsuits, including those by out-of-state families, continue in Illinois courts. If the Court grants review, it will hear full arguments and decide the jurisdictional questions itself, deciding nothing yet about who is right. An eventual decision could affirm the Illinois ruling, reverse it in Mead Johnson's favor, or vacate it and send the case back for further proceedings without resolving the issue for either side.
