## In brief

Baby formula makers ask the Court to review whether Illinois courts may hear out-of-state families' injury claims against them.

## What happened

Dozens of families from across the country sued Mead Johnson and another company in an Illinois state court, claiming the companies' cow's-milk-based formula and fortifier products caused their premature infants to develop a serious intestinal disease. None of the plaintiffs involved in this petition lived in Illinois or say their infants were harmed there. Mead Johnson argued Illinois courts had no power over it because it had moved its headquarters out of Illinois before the suits were filed. The trial court disagreed, and the Illinois Appellate Court affirmed, finding Illinois courts could hear the claims based on Mead Johnson's past and ongoing contacts with the state. The Illinois Supreme Court declined further review.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked whether courts in a state where a company no longer has its main offices can still hear claims against it from people who live elsewhere and were not injured there, based on the company's past presence or its general business activity in that state.

## Where it stands

The petition has been distributed for conference twice, most recently for December 4, 2026, after full briefing including an opposition brief and a reply; the docket shows no action taken since that distribution.

## What each outcome would mean

Mead Johnson, which lost in the Illinois Appellate Court, asks the Court to take up and reverse that ruling. If the Court grants the petition, it would hear full arguments and decide the jurisdictional questions itself, deciding nothing yet about who is right. If it denies the petition, the Illinois Appellate Court's decision stands, meaning the lawsuits by the out-of-state families against Mead Johnson may proceed in Illinois courts. Either way, the underlying lawsuits over the infants' injuries continue in the lower courts regardless of what happens with this petition.
