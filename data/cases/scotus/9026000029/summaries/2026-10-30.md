## In brief

Infant formula makers ask the Court to decide whether Illinois courts may hear lawsuits by out-of-state families alleging no connection to Illinois.

## What happened

Dozens of families whose premature infants allegedly developed a serious intestinal illness after using cow's-milk-based formula sued Mead Johnson and another formula maker in St. Clair County, Illinois. Many plaintiffs do not live in Illinois and say their infants were never treated there. Mead Johnson asked the trial court to dismiss the out-of-state claims, arguing Illinois courts had no power over it for those claims. The trial court disagreed, and the Illinois Appellate Court affirmed, finding Illinois courts could hear the claims both because Mead Johnson was once headquartered in Illinois and because it kept selling and marketing products there. The Illinois Supreme Court declined further review.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked whether a company that moved its headquarters out of a state years before a lawsuit was filed can still be sued there on claims unconnected to that state, and whether a state can hear claims by out-of-state plaintiffs over out-of-state injuries just because the company sold or marketed its products in that state generally.

## Where it stands

The petition has been distributed for conference, and the Court asked for a response, which was later granted an extension until October 30, 2026. The docket shows no decision yet on whether the Court will hear the case.

## What each outcome would mean

Mead Johnson, the petitioner, is asking the Court to overturn the Illinois courts' rulings. If the Court grants review and later rules for Mead Johnson, the out-of-state families' claims could be dismissed from Illinois courts, forcing them to refile elsewhere. If it rules for the families, Illinois courts could keep hearing these claims. If the Court denies review, the Illinois Appellate Court's decision allowing the suits to proceed in Illinois stands, and the litigation continues there.
