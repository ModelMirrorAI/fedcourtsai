# Forecast: Merritt v. Texas Farm Bureau, No. 26-6 (cert)

**Disposition.** The Court will deny the petition at or shortly after the
October 9, 2026 conference, without any separate writing.

**Relists.** The docket shows one distribution (for the 10/9/2026 conference)
and no relist. I expect no further distribution: the petition will be disposed
of on the order list following that conference. The residual chance of one
more distribution is a reschedule or an administrative carry-over rather than a
substantive relist; there is no pending merits case on the FLSA knowledge
requirement that this petition could be held for, and nothing in the record
suggests a Justice preparing a statement respecting denial.

**CVSG.** None. No federal party is involved, the petition identifies no
circuit conflict, and the Department of Labor's position on the "suffer or
permit" knowledge rule is not in dispute among the circuits. The Court has no
reason to ask the Solicitor General for views.

**Question presented, if taken.** Conditional on the very unlikely grant, the
Court would take the QP as written: whether an employer who permits an
employee to work unlimited hours "employs" the employee for all overtime
worked under 29 U.S.C. §§ 203(g) and 207 without a showing of actual or
constructive knowledge of the overtime. The waiver issue the BIO raises would
make a narrowed or reformulated question unlikely; the Court would either take
the textual question whole or not at all.

**Summary disposition.** Not the likelier route. There is no intervening
decision of this Court on the FLSA knowledge requirement to GVR in light of,
and a summary reversal of a rule applied by every circuit to have considered it
is not plausible. If granted at all, the case would be set for briefing and
argument; I place the cert-order route at a low conditional probability only
for an intervening decision I cannot foresee.

**Dissent from denial.** None expected. The textual argument ("suffer or
permit" says nothing about knowledge) is the kind that could in principle draw
a textualist Justice's interest, but the uniform circuit rule, the jury
verdict posture, the unchallenged "deliberately prevented knowledge"
instruction, and the petitioner's six-figure commission income make this a
poor occasion for a statement. A denial will be a bare line on the order list.
