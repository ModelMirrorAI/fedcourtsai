## Why 2%, rather than 5%

The petition offers an intelligible statutory distinction: permission for unrestricted work allegedly satisfies the FLSA's employment definition without additional proof of knowledge, unlike prohibited or unreported overtime. It also argues that failure to keep time records should not reward an employer. Those arguments support a nonzero chance of review and broader significance beyond the individual claimant. Sources: questions-presented.txt; petition.txt, printed pp. 3–9.

The largest downward adjustment is the lack of a demonstrated circuit conflict. The petition's argument seeks correction of the Fifth Circuit's statutory reading rather than identifying a contrary appellate holding on materially comparable facts. The BIO, printed pp. 7–12, supplies authorities from numerous circuits for a knowledge requirement. I treat that as an adversary's supported argument, not as an independently verified finding that every possible inter-circuit distinction has disappeared.

Vehicle concerns independently reduce the probability. The appended opinion, pp. 2a–7a, affirms after a jury verdict and rejects both the no-knowledge theory and the constructive-knowledge challenge. Footnote 2 expressly leaves waiver undecided because the panel rejects the argument on the merits. The BIO, pp. 12–18, argues that the no-knowledge theory was not preserved, that the verdict can rest on deliberate prevention of knowledge, and that deferential review complicates relief. Preservation is therefore a live contention, not a lower-court holding of waiver. The record also leaves conditional cross-appeal issues concerning classification and exemptions unresolved. These are reasons this vehicle is unattractive even if the Court finds the general statutory question interesting.

The claimant's substantial commission income does not itself negate FLSA rights. Nor do I adopt the opposition's assertion that the question could affect only him: unrestricted scheduling and misclassification can recur. Those considerations keep the estimate above a near-zero dismissal of the petition. On balance I reduce the approximately 5% prior to **2%**, with denial the categorical forecast.
