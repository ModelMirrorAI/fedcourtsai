## In brief

A former insurance agency manager asks the Court to revive his bid for unpaid overtime after a jury found his employer did not know he worked extra hours.

## What happened

Jerry Merritt worked as an agency manager for Texas Farm Bureau and related companies, classified as an independent contractor with full control over his own schedule. He later sued under the Fair Labor Standards Act, arguing he was really an employee owed overtime pay. The trial court agreed he was an employee and had worked 816 hours of overtime, leaving a single question for trial: whether the companies knew, or should have known, he was working those extra hours. A jury said no, and the trial court entered judgment for the companies. The Fifth Circuit unanimously affirmed, rejecting Merritt's argument that letting him work unlimited hours was itself enough to make the companies responsible for overtime pay regardless of their knowledge.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked whether an employer that allows an employee to work as many hours as he wants has thereby taken on responsibility for his overtime under federal wage law, or whether the employee must separately prove the employer actually knew, or should have known, he was working those extra hours.

## Where it stands

The respondents filed a brief opposing review, and the petition was distributed for the Court's October 9, 2026 conference. The docket does not yet show any action taken at or after that conference.

## What each outcome would mean

Merritt, who lost in the lower courts, is asking the Court to hear his case. If the Court grants review, it will consider the overtime-knowledge question in full and decide it on the merits, deciding nothing at this stage. If it denies review, the Fifth Circuit's decision affirming the jury verdict for the companies stands, and Merritt's case ends without further proceedings. A denial would not mean the Court agrees the lower court was right, only that the case will not be heard.
