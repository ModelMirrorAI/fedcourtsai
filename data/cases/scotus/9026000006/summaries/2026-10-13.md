## In brief

The Court declined to hear a former insurance agency manager's bid to revive his overtime pay lawsuit after a jury ruled against him.

## What happened

Jerry Merritt worked as an agency manager for Texas Farm Bureau and related companies, classified as an independent contractor with full control over his own schedule. After he retired, he sued under the Fair Labor Standards Act, claiming he was really an employee owed overtime pay. A trial court agreed he was an employee and had worked 816 hours of overtime, but left it to a jury to decide whether the companies knew about that overtime. The jury found they did not, and ruled against Merritt. The trial court and a unanimous panel of the Fifth Circuit Court of Appeals upheld that verdict.

## What the Court is being asked

The case asks whether a worker who is given free rein to work as many hours as he wants must still separately prove his employer knew he was working overtime, in order to collect overtime pay, or whether giving that freedom alone counts as allowing the overtime work.

## Where it stands

The petition was distributed for the Court's October 9, 2026 conference, and on October 13, 2026, the Court denied it.

## What each outcome would mean

Merritt, who asked the Court to take the case, sought to overturn the jury verdict and the Fifth Circuit's ruling against him. By denying the petition, the Court leaves the Fifth Circuit's decision in place: the jury's verdict for Texas Farm Bureau and the other companies stands, and Merritt does not receive the overtime pay he sought. The denial does not mean the Court agreed the lower court was right; it simply leaves that ruling as the final word in this case.
