# Forecast: what the Court will do with No. 25-1242, Hughes v. Locure

**Disposition.** The petition will be **denied**, most likely on the order list following the September 28, 2026 long conference or, if relisted, within one or two further conferences. P(any grant) = 0.06.

**Relists.** The docket shows **one distribution** (July 15, 2026, for the conference of September 28, 2026) and no relist. I expect the petition to be decided at that conference or the next: P(at least one further distribution) = 0.22. The facts are egregious enough, and the dissent below prominent enough, that a Justice may hold the case for a possible statement respecting denial, which is the main route to a relist here. A long string of relists would signal a summary reversal or a dissent being drafted, and I do not expect that.

**CVSG.** No call for the views of the Solicitor General. The suit is a private section 1983 action against a deceased county deputy's estate; no federal program, statute, or federal party is implicated. P = 0.02.

**Which question, if taken.** If the Court took the case it would not adopt the petition's two-part question as written. It would reformulate to a single question: whether the Eleventh Circuit erred in holding that an off-duty officer's intoxicated, unlit, 70-mph driving in a 25-mph zone, causing a death, did not violate clearly established substantive due-process law under County of Sacramento v. Lewis. The second half of the petition's question (whether the decision "creates a circuit split") is argument, not a question the Court would grant on.

**Summary disposition versus plenary review.** Conditional on a grant, a summary disposition is somewhat more likely than plenary review: P(summary route | grant) = 0.45. The plaintiff-side qualified-immunity grants of recent Terms have overwhelmingly come as per curiam summary reversals on obvious-clarity grounds (Taylor v. Riojas is the model) rather than argued cases, and there is no intervening decision that would drive a GVR. Plenary review would require the Court to want to define "intentional misuse of a vehicle" under Lewis footnote 13 as a matter of substantive due process, which the current Court has little appetite to do. I put the conditional below one-half because the interlocutory posture, the unresolved color-of-law question flagged by Chief Judge Pryor, and the preservation dispute raised in the brief in opposition make this a poor candidate for a clean per curiam.

**Dissent from denial.** Conditional on denial, P(some Justice notes a dissent or writes a statement respecting denial) = 0.10. The likeliest author is Justice Sotomayor, possibly joined by Justice Jackson, on the theme that qualified immunity has been stretched to shield conduct no reasonable officer could think lawful. Justice Gorsuch wrote Browder as a circuit judge, but a separate writing from him is less likely than from the Court's left. Most petitions of this shape are denied without comment, and I expect that here.

**Merits, conditionally.** If the Court did grant and decide the case, the likelier outcome would be a reversal on the narrow obvious-clarity ground (that no case law is needed to tell an officer that drunk, unlit, triple-the-limit driving on personal business violates a private person's right to bodily integrity), leaving the broader Lewis footnote 13 question and the Parratt state-remedy question untouched.
