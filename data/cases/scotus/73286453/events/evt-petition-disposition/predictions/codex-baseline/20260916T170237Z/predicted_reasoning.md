# Forecast of the Court's action

The Court will deny certiorari, most likely without a separate writing, after the conference scheduled for September 28, 2026. This forecasts a refusal of review, not an endorsement of the Eleventh Circuit's reasoning or a new merits holding.

The baseline records **one distribution**, on July 15, for that conference: no completed relist and no CVSG. The modal forecast is **zero additional distributions**. I assign an 18% probability to at least one further distribution; if the petition receives additional consideration, one further distribution is more likely than a prolonged series. I assign a 0.5% probability to a new CVSG and expect none.

Conditional on review being granted, the Court will more likely set the case for briefing and argument than dispose of it summarily: P(summary route | any grant) = 15%. The likely question would focus on whether the alleged non-emergency, intoxicated, unlit driving violated a clearly established substantive-due-process right, including whether obvious clarity can overcome qualified immunity despite the Eleventh Circuit's prior vehicle cases. A narrowed formulation is more likely than adoption of the petition's argumentative second question asserting a conflict. No identified intervening decision supplies a particular GVR ground.

Conditional on denial, P(a noted dissent or statement respecting denial) = 9%. The modal forecast remains no writing. The divided decision below and the comparison with Browder make a statement possible, but I do not forecast an individual Justice's vote or authorship.

The expected denial leaves unresolved the alleged inter-circuit tension. The petition's shift toward a precedent-based clearly-established-law argument, together with the separate unresolved state-action issue, will make this a less attractive vehicle than a case squarely presenting the constitutional standard without those complications.
