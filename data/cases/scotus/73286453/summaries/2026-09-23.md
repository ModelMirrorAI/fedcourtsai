## What happened

Deputy Sheriff Darian Locure, driving a patrol truck while intoxicated, without headlights, at roughly 70 mph in a 25-mph zone in Tuskegee, Alabama, collided with a car carrying Edwin Dewayne Moss, who died. Moss's estate, represented by Denise Hughes, sued Locure under federal civil rights law, claiming this violated Moss's right to bodily integrity. A federal district court let the case proceed, but the Eleventh Circuit Court of Appeals reversed, holding that Locure was protected by qualified immunity, a doctrine shielding officials unless they violated rights that were "clearly established" at the time. One judge dissented. Hughes, now representing Moss's estate, asks the Supreme Court to review that ruling; Locure's estate (through administrator Monique Locure) opposes review.

## What the Court is being asked

The case asks whether, at the time of the crash, it was already clearly established that a police officer can be held legally responsible for seriously injuring someone through intentional or reckless misuse of a patrol vehicle, outside of a chase or emergency. Hughes argues the Eleventh Circuit's ruling conflicts with a past Supreme Court decision and with how other appeals courts have handled similar cases, creating inconsistent rules nationwide. Locure's estate responds that Hughes raised this specific argument for the first time before the Supreme Court rather than in the lower courts, that no real conflict among courts exists, and that Hughes has not shown a constitutional violation at all, given that state wrongful-death law offers a separate remedy.

## Where it stands

The petition has been fully briefed, including a response from Locure's estate and a reply from Hughes, and the case was distributed for the Court's conference on September 28, 2026, where the Justices will decide whether to take up the case.
