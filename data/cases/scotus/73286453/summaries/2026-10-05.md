## In brief

The Court declined to hear a sheriff's deputy's bid to keep qualified immunity in a lawsuit over a fatal drunk-driving crash.

## What happened

Deputy Darian Locure, driving his patrol truck home while intoxicated and without headlights, struck a car in Tuskegee, Alabama, at more than 70 miles per hour in a 25-mph zone, killing passenger Edwin Dewayne Moss. Moss's estate, represented by Denise Hughes, sued Locure's estate under federal civil rights law, claiming he violated Moss's constitutional right to life. The district court let the claim proceed, but the Eleventh Circuit reversed, ruling that Locure was protected by qualified immunity, a doctrine shielding officials from being sued unless they violated rights that were clearly established at the time. One judge on that panel dissented, arguing the violation was obvious.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked whether, based on an earlier Supreme Court ruling about police vehicle use and decisions from other federal appeals courts, it was already clearly established in 2021 that an officer could be sued for intentionally misusing a vehicle in a way that seriously hurt someone. The petition also asks whether the appeals court's ruling conflicts with how other circuits have decided similar cases.

## Where it stands

The petition was distributed for the Court's conference on September 28, 2026, after full briefing including a response and reply. The Court denied the petition on October 5, 2026.

## What each outcome would mean

Hughes, representing Moss's estate, asked the Court to take up the case after losing at the Eleventh Circuit, which had ruled in favor of Locure's estate on qualified immunity grounds. By denying the petition, the Court leaves that ruling in place, meaning Locure's estate cannot be sued under federal civil rights law over Moss's death, though the record notes Hughes may still pursue separate state-law wrongful-death claims. The denial does not mean the Court agreed with the Eleventh Circuit's reasoning; it simply leaves that decision as the final word in this case.
