## In brief

The Court declined to hear a bid to revive a civil rights lawsuit against a sheriff's deputy's estate over a fatal off-duty crash.

## What happened

Denise Hughes, representing the estate of Edwin Dewayne Moss, sued Deputy Darian Locure's estate after Locure, allegedly intoxicated and driving without headlights at nearly three times the speed limit while off duty, struck and killed Moss in a 2021 crash. A federal district court allowed the civil rights claim to proceed, rejecting the estate's qualified immunity defense, a legal protection that shields officials from suits unless they violated clearly established rights. The Eleventh Circuit reversed, holding that existing case law left it unclear that Locure's conduct violated a constitutional right, so qualified immunity applied. One judge dissented, arguing the violation was obvious.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked whether it was already clearly established, through its own prior rulings and those of other appeals courts, that an officer can be held liable for a constitutional violation when the officer seriously injures someone through intentional misuse of a government vehicle, and whether the appeals court's contrary conclusion conflicts with that precedent and with how other circuits have ruled on similar facts.

## Where it stands

The petition was distributed for the Court's conference on September 28, 2026, after both sides filed briefs. The Court denied the petition on October 5, 2026.

## What each outcome would mean

Hughes, representing Moss's estate, asked the Court to take up the case and reverse the Eleventh Circuit's ruling for Locure's estate. The denial leaves the Eleventh Circuit's decision in place, meaning Locure's estate keeps the qualified immunity protection it won, and the civil rights claim over Moss's death does not go forward, though the docket does not indicate whether other claims in the case remain active.
