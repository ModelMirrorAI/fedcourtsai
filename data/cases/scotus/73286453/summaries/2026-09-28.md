## In brief

Hughes asks the Court to revive a lawsuit against a deputy's estate after the appeals court found the deputy had qualified immunity in a fatal drunk-driving crash.

## What happened

Off-duty deputy sheriff Darian Locure, intoxicated and driving without headlights at nearly three times the speed limit, crashed into a car and killed passenger Edwin Moss. Moss's estate, represented by Denise Hughes, sued under a federal civil-rights law, claiming a violation of Moss's constitutional right to bodily integrity. A federal district court let the claim proceed, but the Eleventh Circuit reversed, holding that Locure was protected by qualified immunity, a doctrine shielding officials from such suits unless they violated rights that were "clearly established" at the time. One judge dissented. Locure's estate opposes further review.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked whether it was already clearly established, at the time of the crash, that an officer can be held liable for intentionally misusing a police vehicle in a way that injures someone, even outside a chase or emergency. Hughes also asks whether the Eleventh Circuit's ruling conflicts with how other appeals courts have applied the Supreme Court's guidance in this area, a conflict that would matter to whether the Court should step in.

## Where it stands

## What each outcome would mean

Hughes, the estate representative, is asking the Court to take up the case. If the Court grants review, it would hear full arguments and decide whether the right at issue was clearly established, deciding nothing about the outcome at this stage. If the Court denies review, the Eleventh Circuit's ruling stands, meaning Locure's estate keeps qualified immunity and the federal civil-rights claim stays dismissed, though the estate could still pursue separate state-law claims.
