## In brief

A restaurant owner asks the Court to revive his lawsuit claiming police arrested him repeatedly to punish him for criticizing the police chief.

## What happened

Fred Rahdar and Kobra Ghorbani owned a restaurant in Friendswood, Texas. After Rahdar publicly criticized the local police chief over the restaurant's treatment during the COVID-19 pandemic, putting up a billboard and starting a petition calling for the chief's removal, Rahdar says city police began a multi-year campaign of surveillance, harassment and arrests against him, culminating in an arrest under a rarely-used misdemeanor statute. He sued the city and several officers, claiming the arrests were retaliation for his speech. A federal district court granted summary judgment against him, and the Fifth Circuit Court of Appeals affirmed in an unpublished opinion. The Fifth Circuit also denied rehearing before the full court.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked to clarify what kind of evidence a person can use to show that police arrested him in retaliation for his speech, even when the police had a valid legal basis for the arrest. Rahdar argues that a recent Supreme Court decision allows any objective evidence—such as arrest-record patterns or officer testimony—to support such a claim, and that the lower court applied too narrow a reading of that decision, conflicting with how another appeals court has approached the same issue.

## Where it stands

The respondents waived their right to file a response to the petition. The docket shows no indication yet that the Court has acted on the petition itself.

## What each outcome would mean

Rahdar and Ghorbani are asking the Court to take up the case. If the Court grants the petition, it would hear the case fully and decide what evidence can support this type of retaliation claim, deciding nothing yet about who prevails. If the Court denies the petition, the Fifth Circuit's ruling against Rahdar and Ghorbani stands, leaving the lower courts' judgment in favor of the city and officers in place, without the Court ruling on whether that judgment was correct.
