## In brief

The Supreme Court declined to review a pub owner's claim that police arrested him in retaliation for criticizing Friendswood's police chief.

## What happened

Fred Rahdar and Kobra Ghorbani ran a pub in Friendswood, Texas. After Rahdar publicly criticized the police chief, officers arrested him several times, including once in 2021 for blocking a police vehicle with his truck in a parking lot. Rahdar sued the city and several officers, claiming false arrest and retaliation for his speech. A federal district court ruled against him, finding the officers had probable cause for the arrest and that his evidence did not fit a narrow exception allowing retaliation claims despite probable cause. The Fifth Circuit affirmed, and then denied rehearing.

## What the Court is being asked

The case asked whether a recent Supreme Court ruling allows a broader range of evidence to show that police usually do not arrest people for certain conduct, even when officers had a valid legal reason for the specific arrest in question, as part of proving the arrest was really retaliation for protected speech.

## Where it stands

The petition was distributed for the Court's conference on November 6, 2026, and denied on November 9, 2026.

## What each outcome would mean

Rahdar and Ghorbani, who lost in the lower courts, asked the Court to take up the case. By denying the petition, the Court leaves the Fifth Circuit's ruling in place, meaning the lawsuit against Friendswood and the officers remains dismissed. The denial does not mean the Court agreed with the lower court's reasoning; it simply leaves that decision as the final word in this case.
