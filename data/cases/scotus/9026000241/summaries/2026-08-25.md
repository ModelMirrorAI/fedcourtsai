## What happened

Fred Rahdar and Kobra Ghorbani owned a restaurant, Friends Pub, in Friendswood, Texas. Rahdar kept the business open during the COVID-19 pandemic and later publicly criticized the local police chief, including through a billboard and a petition calling for the chief's removal. Rahdar and Ghorbani say that in response, city officials and police officers spent years harassing and surveilling them, made complaints against their restaurant, and arrested Rahdar multiple times, including once for allegedly obstructing a parking lot. A federal district court ruled against Rahdar and Ghorbani, and the Fifth Circuit Court of Appeals affirmed that decision in an unpublished opinion. The Fifth Circuit later denied a request to have the full court reconsider the case.

## What the Court is being asked

The case involves people who claim they were arrested in retaliation for criticizing the police, even though officers may have had a legally valid reason (probable cause) to arrest them anyway. A prior Supreme Court decision addressed what kind of evidence someone in that situation can use to show the arrest was really about retaliation rather than the legal violation. The petitioners argue that the lower court read that prior decision too narrowly, refusing to credit evidence they say shows retaliation, such as arrest records and officer testimony that the law in question is essentially never enforced. They ask the Court to clarify what kinds of evidence courts must consider.
