## In brief

A restaurant owner asks the Court to revive his claim that police arrested him repeatedly in retaliation for criticizing the police chief.

## What happened

Fred Rahdar and Kobra Ghorbani owned a restaurant in Friendswood, Texas. Rahdar publicly criticized the police chief, including with a billboard and a petition calling for his removal. Rahdar says the city and police officers responded with years of surveillance, harassment, and five arrests, including one arrest under a passageway-obstruction statute the petition says had never before been used this way. Rahdar sued, claiming the arrests were retaliation for his speech and violated his Fourth Amendment protection against unreasonable arrest. A federal district court in Texas ruled against him, and the Fifth Circuit Court of Appeals affirmed in an unpublished opinion. The Fifth Circuit also denied rehearing by the full court.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked whether, when someone claims police arrested them to retaliate for their speech even though the police had probable cause, the courts must let them use any objective evidence to support that claim, or whether courts may treat some kinds of evidence, such as officer testimony about what they would have done, as insufficient. The petition argues the Fifth Circuit's approach conflicts with a recent Supreme Court decision on this question and with how another appeals court has applied it.

## Where it stands

The petition for certiorari was filed July 13, 2026, and the case was docketed August 25, 2026. The respondents' response is due September 24, 2026; the docket does not yet show the Court taking any action on the petition.

## What each outcome would mean

Rahdar and Ghorbani are asking the Court to take up the case. If the Court grants the petition, it will hear full arguments and decide how courts should evaluate this kind of evidence in retaliatory-arrest cases, without yet deciding who wins. If the Court denies the petition, the Fifth Circuit's decision affirming judgment against Rahdar and Ghorbani stands, and their claims do not proceed further in this case. A denial would not mean the Supreme Court agrees with the Fifth Circuit's reasoning, only that the lower court's ruling remains in place.
