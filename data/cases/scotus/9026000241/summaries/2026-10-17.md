## In brief

A pub owner asks the Court to revive his claim that police arrested him in retaliation for criticizing them, after lower courts found the arrest was backed by probable cause.

## What happened

Fred Rahdar and Kobra Ghorbani owned a pub in Friendswood, Texas. Rahdar publicly criticized the police chief and alleges officers retaliated with surveillance, repeated bar checks, and several arrests. This case centers on a February 2021 arrest after Rahdar blocked a police vehicle with his truck. A federal district court granted summary judgment to the city and officers, finding officers had probable cause and that Rahdar failed to show an exception allowing a retaliation claim despite probable cause. The Fifth Circuit affirmed, and later denied rehearing.

## What the Court is being asked

The case concerns a First Amendment retaliatory-arrest claim. Courts have said that if police had probable cause for an arrest, that normally defeats a claim that the arrest was really retaliation for protected speech, unless the person can show, with objective evidence, that police typically do not arrest others for the same conduct. The Court is asked whether a 2024 ruling on this exception allows any kind of objective evidence to overcome a finding of probable cause, or whether the evidence Rahdar offered was insufficient as the lower courts held.

## Where it stands

The petition was filed, the respondents waived their right to respond, and the case was distributed for the Court's November 6, 2026 conference. The docket shows no action since that distribution.

## What each outcome would mean

Rahdar and Ghorbani, as petitioners, are asking the Court to take up the case. If the Court grants the petition, it would hear full arguments and decide the evidence question itself, deciding nothing yet about who wins. If it denies the petition, the Fifth Circuit's ruling stands, leaving in place the judgment against Rahdar and Ghorbani and the dismissal of their retaliatory-arrest claim.
