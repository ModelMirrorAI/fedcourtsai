## In brief

A pub owner asks the Court to revive his claim that police arrested him in retaliation for criticizing their department, despite having probable cause for the arrest.

## What happened

Fred Rahdar and Kobra Ghorbani owned a pub in Friendswood, Texas. After Rahdar publicly criticized the police chief, he was arrested several times, including once in February 2021 for blocking a police vehicle with his truck. He sued the city and several officers, claiming false arrest and retaliation for his speech. The district court granted summary judgment to the officers, finding they had probable cause to arrest Rahdar and that he had not shown the narrow circumstances needed to pursue a retaliation claim despite that probable cause. The Fifth Circuit affirmed, and later denied rehearing.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked to clarify what kinds of evidence a person can use to pursue a retaliation claim against police even when the officers had a valid legal reason to make the arrest. Rahdar's petition argues the lower court read a recent Supreme Court decision on this question too narrowly, rejecting his evidence that officers rarely enforce the law he was arrested under, and that this conflicts with how another federal appeals court has treated similar evidence.

## Where it stands

The petition was filed in July 2026, and the respondents waived their right to file a response. The case was distributed for the Court's November 6, 2026 conference, where the justices can decide whether to take it.

## What each outcome would mean

Rahdar is asking the Court to take up his case. If the Court grants the petition, it would hear full arguments and decide the question itself, which would not by itself determine who wins. If it denies the petition, the Fifth Circuit's ruling against Rahdar and Ghorbani stands, leaving intact the dismissal of their claims against the city and officers, though a denial would not mean the Court has ruled the lower court was correct.
