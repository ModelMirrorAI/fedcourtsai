# Forecast — Rahdar v. City of Friendswood, No. 26-241 (arrival)

Forecast made at docketing (docketed August 25, 2026; response due September 24,
2026). The docket shows **zero distributions** — that is the state every claim
below is forecast from.

**Disposition.** The Court denies certiorari, most likely at or shortly after
its first conference on the petition. The petition asks the Court to review an
unpublished Fifth Circuit affirmance of summary judgment on a retaliatory-arrest
claim, arguing the panel misapplied *Gonzalez v. Trevino*, 602 U.S. 653 (2024).
That is error-correction: *Gonzalez* predates the panel decision, the opinion
below is non-precedential, and the claimed conflict with the Eighth Circuit
(*Murphy v. Schmitt*, 143 F.4th 914 (8th Cir. 2025)) runs from one published
decision to an unpublished one — not the kind of entrenched split the Court
waits for. Expect a denial without comment in fall 2026.

**Distribution and relists.** The petition will be distributed for conference
in the ordinary course once the brief in opposition is filed or waived —
expect a first distribution for a late-October or November 2026 conference.
I do not expect a relist: the likeliest path is denial at the first conference,
with at most one further distribution if the case is paired with another
*Nieves*/*Gonzalez* petition.

**CVSG.** None. No federal party, no federal statute at issue, no United States
interest implicated in a §1983 suit against a Texas municipality and its
officers. A CVSG here would be extraordinary.

**Which question, if taken.** The QP as written — whether *Gonzalez*
"authorizes the use of any objective evidence to defeat probable cause" —
conflates the *Nieves* exception's evidentiary standard with defeating probable
cause itself, and the Court would not take it in that form. If the Court acts,
it reformulates toward the cleaner percolating question: what counts as
"objective evidence" that similarly situated persons are not arrested, and
whether officers' own testimony about their charging practices qualifies.

**Summary disposition.** Conditional on the Court acting favorably at all, a
cert-order disposition is a substantial share of the probability — likelier a
GVR than a plenary grant, but only if an intervening decision materializes
(e.g., the Court decides another retaliatory-arrest case this Term). As of
docketing no such intervening decision exists, since *Gonzalez* was already on
the books when the panel ruled; that is a reason the whole grant-family number
is low, and it makes plenary review the less likely half of an already unlikely
event.

**Dissent from denial.** Unlikely. The *Gonzalez* separate writings (Alito,
Jackson, Thomas) show interest in this doctrine, but an unpublished, fact-bound
vehicle with a muddled QP is not where a Justice stakes out a statement. Small
residual chance of a short statement respecting denial noting the objective-
evidence question is recurring.
