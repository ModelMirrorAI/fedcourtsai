# Rationale

I assign a 1.8% probability of any grant and predict denial. The sal-v3 arrival anchor is the pooled, strictly prior-Term bracketed `reached` rate for the baseline band: 6.52% over a denial-reweighted denominator of 13,163 paid petitions from Terms 2017 through 2025. This is the weakest band's risk set and therefore the whole paid arrival segment, not the terminal baseline-band rate. The committed Fifth Circuit cut is also modest, with a 3.8% grant-family rate in the modern discretionary-cert slice, but it is descriptive rather than my primary arrival anchor.

I adjust sharply below the arrival anchor because the petition challenges an unpublished decision through a fact-intensive summary-judgment record. Its asserted conflict with *Murphy v. Schmitt* is weak: the Eighth Circuit decided *Murphy* at the pleading stage and said the alleged officer statements suggested that objective evidence could be obtained in discovery; it expressly said those statements could not themselves serve as the objective evidence required at summary judgment and made the inquiry context-dependent. The petition does not squarely address that procedural distinction. Its question presented also describes objective evidence as "defeat[ing] probable cause," while *Nieves* and *Gonzalez* describe a narrow exception under which probable cause does not bar the retaliation claim. Those features make this look more like error correction than a clean vehicle for resolving a genuine circuit conflict.

There is some upside. The petition identifies twelve years of arrest records, testimony from six officers, and an allegedly singular use of the obstruction statute; the Supreme Court recently corrected an unduly cramped treatment of comparator evidence in *Gonzalez* and remanded *Murphy* for reconsideration. Those points keep the probability above a near-zero frivolous-petition assessment, but they do not overcome the unpublished, record-specific posture. No brief in opposition was provisioned at this arrival moment, so I could not test the petition's characterization against the respondents' account.

The frozen distribution count is zero. My 98.2% relist-increment probability therefore means that the petition will be distributed at least once after arrival, not that it will be relisted after its first conference; my modal path is one distribution and denial. I assign only 0.4% to a later CVSG because the federal interest is general civil-rights doctrine rather than an implicated federal program. Conditional on a grant, I put 62% on a cert-order disposition because any intervention is more plausibly a summary application of recent precedent than plenary review. Conditional on denial, I put 2.5% on a noted dissent or statement: the retaliation allegations have some salience, but the vehicle is unlikely to attract a separate writing.

I used the complete 25-page petition and extracted questions presented listed in `documents.json`. CourtListener supplied the cited Eighth Circuit opinion. Searches for the unpublished Fifth Circuit opinion returned no result, so the lower-court posture is assessed from the petition and the provisioned snapshot rather than the opinion itself.
