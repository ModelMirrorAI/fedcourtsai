## Why below the reached-band prior

The decisive vehicle problem is in the appendix, not the petition's characterization. At appendix 6a, the Fifth Circuit holds that the argument based on Lieutenant Price's testimony that he would first have asked Rahdar to move, and the argument treating employee Jazmine Lowery as a comparator, were raised for the first time on appeal. The panel expressly treats those arguments as forfeited. The petition asserts preservation in general terms but does not meaningfully overcome that holding. I do not extend this forfeiture finding to every item of arrest-history evidence; it is nevertheless a major obstacle to resolving the question the petition emphasizes.

The district court did not simply overlook Gonzalez. Appendix 27a-31a discusses it, the twelve-year arrest-history evidence, and officers' lack of recollection of similar arrests. It distinguishes the alleged conduct as deliberately blocking an officer's vehicle and notes three other arrests under the statute. Its reliance on narrow readings and on a concurrence is open to criticism, but the record presents an evidentiary application dispute rather than an express demand for identical named comparators. The appellate account of video evidence at 5a is also materially less favorable than the petition's narrative. I have not watched the video and rely only on the competing written descriptions.

The asserted circuit conflict is weaker after reading Murphy itself. Mason Murphy v. Michael Schmitt, No. 22-1726 (8th Cir. July 9, 2025), slip opinion 4-5 and footnote 3, allowed a pleading-stage claim to proceed. Officer statements supported the plausibility of allegations that objective evidence would exist; the majority expressly did not deem those statements independently sufficient objective evidence. Rahdar's retaliation claim, by contrast, reached summary judgment, and the appellate court imposed a preservation barrier. This is not a demonstrated square conflict on identical evidence and posture. The reporter lookup for the petition's Murphy citation returned no result; the case-name lookup supplied the actual appellate opinion, which is the authority I used.

There remains a genuine reason for a nonzero grant probability: the petition implicates the effectiveness of Gonzalez's protection against an unduly restrictive comparator requirement. Gonzalez v. Trevino, 602 U.S. 653, 658-59 (2024), rejects a requirement for virtually identical identifiable comparators and permits objective evidence of differential enforcement. The provisioned allegations of repeated retaliation and arrest-history evidence warrant attention. But that authority predates the decisions below, the Fifth Circuit opinion is unpublished, and both preservation and factual distinctions make this a poor vehicle for broad clarification. The separate bar-check false-arrest claim also rests on a pleading deficiency rather than the cert question about the retaliation exception (appendix 3a-4a).
