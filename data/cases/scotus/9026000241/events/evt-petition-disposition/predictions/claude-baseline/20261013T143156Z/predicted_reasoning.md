# Forecast: Rahdar v. City of Friendswood (No. 26-241)

**Denied at the November 6, 2026 conference, with no further distribution.**

- **Relists.** The docket shows one distribution (Oct 7, 2026, for the
  Conference of 11/6/2026). It has never been relisted. The respondents waived
  their right to respond, and I do not expect the Court to call for a response.
  Expect a straight denial on the order list after that first conference, with
  zero further distributions. The only realistic route to a second
  distribution is a call for a response (or a reschedule), and I put that low.
- **CVSG.** None. This is a private section 1983 damages suit against a Texas
  city and its officers. No federal interest calls for the Solicitor General's
  views, and no CVSG will issue on a petition whose response was waived.
- **Question presented, if taken.** The petition's single QP asks whether
  *Gonzalez v. Trevino* (2024) lets any objective evidence satisfy the *Nieves*
  exception to the probable-cause bar. If the Court took the issue at all, it
  would reformulate it into a neutral question about what evidence satisfies
  the *Nieves* exception. It would not take it as written, because the QP is
  framed as a reading of a per curiam.
- **Summary disposition.** Unlikely in any world. There is no intervening
  decision to GVR on: *Gonzalez* predates the Fifth Circuit's March 2026
  opinion, and that panel applied *Nieves* and *Gonzalez*. The panel held the
  petitioners' *Nieves*-exception evidence forfeited because it was raised
  for the first time on appeal. That defeats any summary reversal on the
  merits question. If a grant somehow happened, it would most likely be a
  summary vacatur in the style of *Gonzalez*, not plenary review.
- **Dissent from denial.** None expected. Some Justices have written on
  retaliatory arrest before, but the forfeiture holding makes this a poor
  candidate for a separate writing.
