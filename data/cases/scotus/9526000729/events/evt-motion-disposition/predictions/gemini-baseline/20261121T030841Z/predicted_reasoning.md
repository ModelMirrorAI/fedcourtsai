## Application of the Stay Standard

To obtain a stay of a state-court order or judgment pending Supreme Court review under 28 U.S.C. § 2101(f) and *Hollingsworth v. Perry*, 558 U.S. 183, 190 (2010), an applicant must establish (1) a threshold jurisdictional footing under 28 U.S.C. § 1257(a) — a final judgment or decree rendered by the highest court of a State in which a decision could be had, turning on a preserved federal question rather than an adequate and independent state-law ground — together with (2) a reasonable probability that four Justices will consider the issue sufficiently meritorious to grant certiorari, (3) a fair prospect that a majority of the Court will vote to reverse the judgment below, and (4) a likelihood that irreparable harm will result from the denial of a stay, balanced against the equities (*Nken v. Holder*, 556 U.S. 418, 434 (2009)).

This application fails on each threshold and merits prong:

1. **Jurisdictional and finality barriers (§ 1257(a) and state domestic-relations law).** The proceeding below in the Supreme Judicial Court of Maine (`And-26-291`) arises out of ongoing Androscoggin County divorce and domestic-relations litigation between Ian Buenaventura and Leslie Buot, following an earlier interlocutory Law Court appeal (`And-25-246`) in which Buenaventura sought anti-SLAPP dismissal of the divorce action and challenged Maine's irreconcilable-differences divorce standard. State domestic-relations, procedural-sealing, anti-SLAPP, and interlocutory-appeal rulings rest overwhelmingly on non-final or independent state-law grounds outside § 1257(a) and implicate core areas of state family-law administration in which the Supreme Court almost never intervenes.
2. **Certworthiness (four votes) and likelihood of reversal (five votes).** A *pro se* challenge to state divorce, custody, or anti-SLAPP orders presents no genuine conflict among state courts of last resort or federal courts of appeals on a substantial federal question. Four Justices will not vote to grant certiorari, and five Justices will not vote to disturb the Maine Supreme Judicial Court's action in `And-26-291`.
3. **Irreparable harm and equitable balance.** Routine progression or enforcement of state family-court orders does not constitute cognizable federal irreparable injury warranting emergency intervention from Washington, whereas staying state domestic-relations proceedings imposes immediate prejudice on the opposing spouse and disrupts state judicial administration.
