# Forecast — 26A512, Scanlan v. Coalition for Open Democracy (stay application)

**State of the record I forecast from (snapshot 2026-10-15):** application for a stay
of a D.N.H. preliminary injunction submitted to Justice Jackson (Circuit Justice, CA1)
on Oct 15, 2026; a response requested by Justice Jackson the same day, due Oct 19.
Not referred to the full Court; no amicus entries; no administrative stay entered.

**Disposition: denied (more likely than not), P(unqualified grant) = 0.42.**
The applicants ask the Court to stay an injunction that bars the Secretary of State from
enforcing his guidance under HB 323 and requires revised guidance telling officials they
*may consider* student IDs alongside other evidence of identity. The statute's removal of
student IDs as per se proof is left in force. I expect the full Court to deny, quite
possibly over a noted dissent by two or three Justices (Alito, Thomas, Gorsuch) invoking
Purcell. Why the denial is likelier than the grant: the injunction is narrow and touches
guidance, not the statute; by the State's own footnote the Secretary has already sent the
revised guidance, so a stay would itself be a second late change; and the State's slip on
FRAP 8 cost it a week. The grant path is real: a federal court rewriting state election
administration 32 days out on its own reading of state law, a circuit dissent (Judge
Dunlap), and the Court's recent Purcell stays in this Term's own election cases. A partial
or limited stay (e.g., only the order to reissue guidance) would resolve as denied under
the resolver's denial-first rule.

**Referral: likely (0.8).** A contested election-law stay with a requested response is
normally referred by the Circuit Justice to the full Court rather than decided in chambers.

**Further response request: n/a** — one has already been called for (the claim is vacuous;
stated at 0.05).

**Amicus: possible but under even (0.3).** The window is days, but election-law groups and
national party committees do file in pre-election stay applications.

**Timing:** disposed of by order within roughly a week of the Oct 19 response — before
the Nov 3 election, most likely Oct 21–28.
