## Why adjust upward, and why stop at 70%

The application presents a concrete election-administration dispute rather than an undifferentiated emergency request. Its introduction and statement describe an October 2 injunction, separately entered October 5, requiring revised guidance concerning the evidentiary use of student identification before the November 3 general election. Its Purcell argument describes months of official training and public guidance followed by a court-ordered change close to voting. If that account of implementation burdens is accurate, preserving the previously administered procedures is a strong basis for interim intervention. The reported 2–1 First Circuit stay denial also indicates a genuine dispute over the need for relief, although the dissent is not itself proof of likely Supreme Court success.

I independently read **Purcell v. Gonzalez, 549 U.S. 1, 4–6 (2006)** through CourtListener, opinion 9435151. It supports attention to election proximity, conflicting instructions, voter confusion, and the distinction between immediate electoral stability and ultimate constitutional validity. It does not establish an automatic rule that a state always wins an election-related stay.

The strongest downward consideration is the injunction's narrowness. Even the application acknowledges that the district court left the statutory exclusion of student IDs as automatically sufficient identification intact. The order instead addresses their consideration with other evidence under alternative verification provisions. A limited clarification of existing law can be less disruptive than the State's description suggests, and a new stay can itself reverse guidance already communicated. The application's footnote acknowledges initial efforts to comply. Two evidentiary hearings and the appellate majority's refusal to stay the order also counsel against simply accepting the applicant's account of hardship and legal error.

Only the application is provisioned: the manifest lists a complete, non-OCR-derived 18-page filing, not its separately linked appendix, the full district-court reasoning, or opposition text. The latter materials were not read, and my account of their substance is necessarily filtered through the applicant's advocacy. I therefore leave meaningful probability on denial and on narrower relief that would not resolve as an unqualified grant. The application's two differently captioned citations to docket 26A274 are unverified and are not independent support for my forecast; the discrepancy is recorded in flags.json.

At least one further amicus entry is plausible (**0.40**) given the public stakes, but the short response schedule and imminent election favor a compact proceeding. These are subjective forecasts, not statpack-conditioned estimates. The response request has already occurred: its increment entry is **0.0**, retained to complete the declared set and understood to be vacuous for scoring, not a prediction that the existing request disappears.

The **0.59 significance score** reflects statewide voting access and election administration close to an election, independently of my grant probability. Temporary relief in a single State is less consequential than a final nationwide constitutional ruling.
