# Forecast

The Court will grant the substantive stay application, suspending the district court's October 2 and October 5 injunction orders while the appeal proceeds. The central ground will be avoiding a late federal-court change to election administration, not a final decision that excluding student identification is constitutional. The likely disposing entry is a short, unqualified grant rather than a mixed grant-and-denial. P(unqualified grant) is **0.64**; a denial is the principal alternative. An administrative stay alone would not establish that the substantive application succeeds.

At the provisioned arrival vantage, the only proceeding is the October 15 submission to Justice Jackson. No response has been requested, no referral is recorded, and the amicus count is zero. From that state:

- Justice Jackson or the Court will request a response on an expedited schedule: **0.94** probability of a new response request.
- The application will be referred to the full Court rather than finally resolved by the Circuit Justice alone: **0.92** probability of a new referral.
- At least one additional amicus-counting entry will appear: **0.60** probability of a rise above zero. This includes a submitted amicus brief, not just an accepted Latin-form entry.

Expect resolution before the November 3 election, most likely in the latter half of October, roughly one to two weeks after submission. A temporary administrative stay may bridge consideration of the response, but the principal forecast concerns the substantive stay. The Court is unlikely to resolve the entire constitutional challenge or issue a full merits opinion on this emergency application.

The reason for intervention will be that officials have already trained and communicated under the challenged guidance, and a late change risks inconsistent administration. The denial alternative turns on treating the injunction as a manageable clarification of existing statutory discretion, rather than a new election rule. If that characterization prevails, the Court will leave the injunction in place rather than decide whether student IDs should generally be accepted.
