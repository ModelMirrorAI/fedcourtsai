# Forecast — Scanlan v. Coalition for Open Democracy (26A512), stay application

**Record at prediction time:** one entry — the application for a stay (with a
request for an immediate administrative stay), submitted to Justice Jackson as
Circuit Justice for the First Circuit on October 15, 2026. No response called
for, no referral, zero amicus entries.

**Disposition: denied (P(unqualified grant) = 0.33).** I expect the full Court
to deny the stay, most likely with a noted dissent from two or three Justices
who would grant under Purcell. The injunction does not block HB 323 itself; it
requires revised guidance saying officials *may consider* student IDs alongside
other evidence. The State has already pushed out compliant guidance, so a stay
less than three weeks before November 3 would itself be a late change of the
operative rule — Purcell cuts both ways here — and the dispute turns on a
federal court's reading of state law, which the Court may prefer to leave alone
on an emergency posture. The live path to a grant is the Court's recent
pattern (cited in the application: *People Not Politicians v. Onder*) of staying
late federal-court election injunctions almost categorically; that path is real
and is why this number sits well above the base rate.

**Response requested: very likely (0.88).** An election application from a
State asking for an administrative stay will draw a call for a response from
Justice Jackson, on a short deadline, within days.

**Referral to the full Court: likely (0.80).** Election-law stay applications
from a State are routinely referred rather than denied by the Circuit Justice
alone; a Circuit Justice denial in chambers is the main alternative.

**Amicus: modest chance of at least one entry (0.40).** National party
committees or voting-rights groups sometimes file in Purcell applications, but
the very compressed timetable and narrow issue cut against it.

**Timing:** disposed before the November 3 election, most likely within about
one to two weeks of the application (late October), by unsigned order. An
administrative stay is unlikely.
