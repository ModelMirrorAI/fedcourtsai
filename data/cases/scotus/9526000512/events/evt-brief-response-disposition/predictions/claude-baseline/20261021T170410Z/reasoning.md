# Reasoning: P(unqualified grant) = 0.36

**Anchor.** The statpack's interim section (*The interim docket (applications)*, a pack whose caption treats the rate as the scored base rate) gives a pool for application-Term 2026. It covers the strictly-prior Terms within ten (2016–2025), but only 2023–2025 carry parsed substantive rows: 18 + 16 + 17 = 51 granted out of 127 + 194 + 226 = 547 resolved, which is **9.3%**. That clears the 50-resolved floor. The earlier Terms are entirely unparsed, so the pool rests on three Terms only.

**Adjustments upward, from 9% to the mid-30s:**
- **Escalation.** A response was requested. The pack's cohort is unconditioned on that rung, and on that cohort fewer than a fifth of substantive applications draw a request.
- **The applicant is a State** seeking to stay a federal injunction on election administration 19 days before a general election. That is the Purcell posture this Court has been most willing to grant, and both briefs cite 2026 per curiam Purcell rulings (People Not Politicians v. Onder, NRCC v. Brown) that went the applicants' way, as both sides describe them.
- **A divided panel.** Judge Dunlap would have granted the stay.
- **An organized-party amicus** (the RNC) supports the stay.

**Adjustments downward:**
- **What is enjoined.** Read from the provisioned documents, the injunction leaves HB 323 intact and reaches only an executive directive the State did not defend on its own terms.
- **Unchallenged findings.** The application challenges neither the constitutional holdings nor the district court's findings on burden, timing and confusion. Its merits argument is a state-law reading plus a Pullman/McKesson point that, according to the response, was raised for the first time in this Court.
- **The guidance is already in place.** The Secretary issued revised guidance immediately, so a stay would reverse the rules a second time.
- **No fair prospect of cert.** The response argues persuasively that the State shows no reasonable probability of certiorari on an interlocutory state-law question.

These cut against the usual Purcell pull. Net, I land below even. I am not confident: this Court's Purcell grants have often come on weaker equities for the respondent, so 0.36 reflects real uncertainty, not a strong lean.

**Other claims.**
- **Referral (0.75).** Prior judgment about how Circuit Justices handle a State's election-stay application once a response is in hand.
- **Amicus increment (0.30).** Only one filing so far, and the window before disposition is short.
- **Response-requested increment.** Already fired, so the harness masks it.

- `application.txt`: 18 pages, read fully.
- `application-response.txt`: 44 pages. I read the first ~30k characters (tables, introduction, statement, start of the stay proceedings) and did not read the argument sections in full.
- `context.json`: band null (normal for interim), response_requested true, referred false, amicus 1.

**Retrieval.** None beyond the provisioned inputs and the committed statpack. My reading of the 2026 Purcell orders comes only from how the briefs characterize them.
