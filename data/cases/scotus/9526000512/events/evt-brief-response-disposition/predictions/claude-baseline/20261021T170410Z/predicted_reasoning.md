# Forecast: Scanlan v. Coalition for Open Democracy (26A512), disposition after the response was filed

**Disposition: denied, P(unqualified grant of the stay) = 0.36.**

## Where the ladder stands
The record I was given (snapshot 2026-10-19):
- The application was submitted to Justice Jackson on Oct 15, 2026.
- Justice Jackson requested a response the same day, due Oct 19. That rung has already fired.
- The application has **not** been referred to the full Court.
- **One** amicus entry is on the docket: the RNC's brief supporting the stay, submitted Oct 16.
- The respondent filed its opposition on Oct 19.

## Claims
- **Disposition.** I expect the stay to be denied. The order will most likely come from the full Court, possibly with noted dissents from Justices who would have granted it. I expect it within roughly a week of the response, and in any event before the Nov 3 general election. An unqualified grant is a real minority outcome (0.36). If it comes, I expect it as a short unsigned order, perhaps with noted or written dissents.
- **Referral to the full Court (0.75).** A State asking to stay a federal injunction on election administration weeks before a general election is the kind of application a Circuit Justice usually sends to the full Court rather than deciding alone. That is especially likely given the Court's recent per curiam Purcell rulings, which both sides cite. The remaining mass covers an in-chambers denial by Justice Jackson.
- **Further amicus entries (0.30).** There is little time before disposition and so far there has been only one filing. A brief from a voting-rights group or a student coalition supporting the respondent is plausible but not the likeliest path.
- **Another response request (0.05).** This claim is vacuous: the request is already on the docket.

## Why denial is likelier, and what would flip it
- **Narrow injunction.** No statute is enjoined. HB 323 stands, and the injunction reaches only the Secretary's April directive barring student IDs under the "any other evidence" safety valves.
- **The State's merits argument is thin.** It is a disagreement over how to read state law, and it does not challenge the district court's Anderson-Burdick holding or its independent Twenty-Sixth Amendment holding.
- **The status quo has already moved.** Revised guidance was issued in early October. A stay would arguably be the late change, which turns Purcell's logic against the applicant.
- **What would flip it.** This Court's majority has granted Purcell stays readily, including against relief directed at executive guidance. The 2-1 First Circuit split and the five months of training and public messaging give the applicants a strong late-change narrative. If the majority treats any federal-court-ordered change to election-official instructions inside about 30 days as presumptively stayable, the stay is granted.
