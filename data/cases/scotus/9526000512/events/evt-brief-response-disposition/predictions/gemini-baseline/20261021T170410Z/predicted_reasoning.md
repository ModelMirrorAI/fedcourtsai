## Conditional Doctrinal and Procedural Rationale

- **If the Court denies the stay (the 65% modal path):** The denial will likely issue as a standard unsigned order without a majority opinion, or accompanied at most by a brief concurrence emphasizing the certworthiness requirement under *Hollingsworth v. Perry*, 558 U.S. 183, 190 (2010), and *Does 1–3 v. Mills*, 142 S. Ct. 17, 18 (2021) (Kavanaugh, J., concurring). Specifically:
  1. **Absence of a Certworthy Federal Question or Challenged Merits Ground:** The district court *denied* preliminary injunctive relief against New Hampshire's statutory removal of student IDs from the list of per se valid voter IDs (HB 323 / RSA 659:13, II(a)), and enjoined only the Secretary of State's April 23, 2026 press release and single-sentence manual instruction categorically barring checklist supervisors from considering a student ID together with other evidence under the statutory "any other evidence" safety valves (RSA 654:12, I(d)(4) and RSA 659:13, I(c)(3)). In their stay application to this Court, Applicants do not brief or contest either the district court's *Anderson-Burdick* First/Fourteenth Amendment holding or its independent Twenty-Sixth Amendment holding; they argue only that the federal district court misread New Hampshire statutes and should have abstained or certified under *Railroad Commission of Texas v. Pullman Co.*—arguments Applicants never raised prior to the injunction and contradicted in their own pre-injunction stipulations below.
  2. **Inverted *Purcell* Equities Post-Compliance:** Immediately after the October 2, 2026 preliminary injunction, the Secretary of State updated the official state website and issued revised guidance to local election officials stating that checklist supervisors may consider a student ID along with other available evidence under the safety valves. Because that revised guidance has been the public status quo in New Hampshire for more than two weeks (including during an intervening October 6 municipal election) and affects neither ballot printing nor absentee voting, staying the injunction now would require the Secretary to reverse course for a second time less than two weeks before the November 3 election, creating the very voter confusion *Purcell* seeks to prevent (cf. *Republican National Committee v. Common Cause Rhode Island*, 141 S. Ct. 206 (2020)).

- **If the Court grants the stay (the 35% alternative path):** A grant would rest almost exclusively on a strict application of *Purcell v. Gonzalez*, 549 U.S. 1 (2006) (per curiam), and *People Not Politicians v. Onder*, 609 U.S. ___ (Sept. 25, 2026) (per curiam). Under that rationale, a majority would conclude that Respondent's 130-day delay after the April 2026 enactment of HB 323 and the April Directive—waiting until August 11, 2026, to file suit—and the district court's entry of an injunction on October 2, 2026 (between the September 8 state primary and the November 3 general election, 32 days before Election Day) improperly altered state election guidance on the eve of an election where the state-law interplay between HB 323, SB 223, and the statutory safety valves is "not entirely clearcut."
