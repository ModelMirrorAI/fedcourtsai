## Adjustments for this application

I raise the estimate substantially above 9.32% because this is a State's contested election application, the Circuit Justice affirmatively requested a response, an institutional political amicus supports relief, and the application reports a divided appellate stay decision. Its strongest argument is not the abstract validity of voter ID: it is that the October 2 injunction displaced guidance used for months, with the November 3 election approaching. The application, especially printed pages 8–12 and footnote 1 on page 14, explains why a revised email might not undo training, posters, and public outreach. Compliance with an injunction does not by itself eliminate the State's election-stability objection.

General legal context supports taking that concern seriously. In Merrill v. Milligan, Nos. 21A375 and 21A376 (February 7, 2022), Justice Kavanaugh's concurrence treated election proximity and implementation burdens as reasons to stay an injunction, while recognizing circumstances in which sufficiently clear merits, serious harm, prompt litigation, and feasible implementation permit relief. This is general precedent retrieved from the Court's official PDF, not this application's history. It prevents treating the response's narrow-statutory-scope argument as automatically dispositive.

I nevertheless keep grant probability below one half because the response identifies concrete obstacles not met by the application's short merits discussion. Printed pages 6–11 and 17–24 distinguish preserving HB 323's automatic-ID list from forbidding student IDs as any evidence under the safety valves. They attribute the injunction to independent federal constitutional grounds and a failure to offer a directive-specific justification. The application itself acknowledges that HB 323 was not enjoined, but does not substantively rebut those federal holdings. Its state-law disagreement and newly proposed state-court route do not clearly establish a fair prospect of reversal of every supporting ground.

The response's printed pages 24–33 also identify a limited implementation burden and revised guidance already issued shortly after the injunction. The State's own footnote acknowledges prompt compliance, although it disputes whether communication has been effective. This makes the status-quo issue genuinely two-sided: a stay now could itself change what officials and affected voters have recently been told. The response presents those facts persuasively enough to make denial the modal result, without erasing the substantial Purcell risk. The 0.41 is a judgmental synthesis, not a fitted conditional frequency.
