## In brief

New Hampshire election officials asked the Supreme Court to pause a lower court order requiring revised guidance on student IDs before the general election, and the Court granted the pause.

## What happened

New Hampshire's HB 323 removed student IDs from the list of identification a voter can use to get a ballot, but left in place separate "safety valve" provisions allowing election officials to consider other evidence of identity. A group called the Coalition for Open Democracy sued, arguing that the Secretary of State's guidance went further than the law itself by telling officials not to consider student IDs at all. A federal district court agreed in part, enjoining that guidance weeks before the November election and ordering the Secretary to issue revised instructions. The First Circuit twice declined to pause that order while the state's appeal continued.

## What the Court is being asked

The state asked the Court to pause the lower court's order while appeals continue, arguing that courts should not change election rules close to an election and that the district court misread state law. The Coalition answered that no statute was enjoined, only an executive directive that went beyond the law, and that the injunction simply held officials to the state's own description of its rules.

## Where it stands

The Court granted the stay application on October 22, 2026, pausing the district court's October 2 and October 5 orders while the appeal proceeds in the First Circuit and while any further Supreme Court review is sought. Justice Jackson dissented.

## What each outcome would mean

The state election officials sought this pause, and the Coalition for Open Democracy opposed it. The grant means the district court's injunction is paused: the Secretary's earlier guidance excluding student IDs remains in effect for now, rather than the revised guidance the district court had ordered. The pause is temporary and does not decide who is right; it lasts until the appeal is resolved or, if the state seeks Supreme Court review after that, until that review ends. Had the application been denied, the district court's injunction would have stayed in effect while the appeal continued.
