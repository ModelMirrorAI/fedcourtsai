## In brief

Gun owners ask the Court to strike down Maryland's broad set of laws banning firearms in places such as parks, transit, museums, and bars.

## What happened

After the Supreme Court's Bruen decision led Maryland to loosen its handgun-carry permitting, the state expanded its list of "sensitive places" where carrying is banned, covering mass transit, parks and forests, healthcare facilities, museums, stadiums, bars, and demonstrations, among others. Gun owners and gun-rights groups sued, claiming these bans violate the Second Amendment. The district court upheld most restrictions but struck down a few. A divided Fourth Circuit panel upheld nearly all the location bans, while striking down a rule presumptively barring carry on private property open to the public. Maryland separately petitioned on that private-property ruling.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked whether Maryland's many restrictions on carrying firearms in places open to the public are consistent with the constitutional right to keep and bear arms, and more specifically what historical principle should determine which locations the government may declare off-limits to carrying.

## Where it stands

The petition has been distributed for conference multiple times, most recently for October 16, 2026, with earlier conferences in September and October also rescheduled; the docket shows no decision yet on whether the Court will hear the case.

## What each outcome would mean

Novotny and the other gun owners, who lost most of their challenge below, are asking the Court to take up the case and reverse. If the Court grants review, it would hear full arguments on which places Maryland may bar guns from, deciding nothing yet about who wins. If it denies review, the Fourth Circuit's ruling upholding most of Maryland's restrictions stays in place, leaving those bans enforceable. A grant could also lead to vacating the lower ruling and sending the case back for reconsideration without deciding the merits either way.
