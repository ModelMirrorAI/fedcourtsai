## In brief

Gun owners and gun rights groups ask the Court to strike down Maryland laws barring firearms in places like transit, parks, and bars.

## What happened

Maryland expanded restrictions on carrying firearms in specific public locations, including mass transit, parks, healthcare facilities, places of amusement, and venues serving alcohol. Two groups of gun owners and gun rights organizations sued, arguing these restrictions violate the Second Amendment. A federal district court upheld most restrictions but struck down a rule requiring permission to carry on private property open to the public. A divided Fourth Circuit panel affirmed most of the restrictions, agreeing the private-property rule was unconstitutional but upholding nearly all the location-based bans, while one judge would have struck down most of them.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked whether laws banning firearm carrying in numerous places the public visits—transit, parks, healthcare facilities, entertainment venues, and places serving alcohol—are consistent with the right to keep and bear arms, given this nation's history of regulating firearms.

## Where it stands

The petition has been distributed for conference multiple times, most recently for November 13, 2026, after full briefing including a response and reply; the docket does not yet show any action taken on it.

## What each outcome would mean

Novotny and the other gun owners and organizations, who lost most of their challenge below, are asking the Court to take up the case and reverse. If the Court grants review, it would hear full arguments on whether Maryland's restrictions are constitutional, deciding nothing yet about who prevails. If it denies review, the Fourth Circuit's decision upholding most of Maryland's restrictions stands, leaving those laws in effect, while the lower court's separate ruling striking down the private-property consent rule remains undisturbed either way.
