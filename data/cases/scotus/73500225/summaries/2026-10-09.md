## In brief

Gun owners and gun-rights groups ask the Court to strike down Maryland's restrictions on carrying firearms in places like parks, transit, and healthcare facilities.

## What happened

Maryland enacted laws barring public carrying of firearms in numerous locations, including government buildings, mass transit, schools, healthcare facilities, parks, entertainment venues, and places serving alcohol. Gun owners and advocacy groups sued, arguing these restrictions violate the Second Amendment. The district court upheld most restrictions but struck down a rule requiring consent to carry on private property open to the public. A divided Fourth Circuit panel affirmed most of Maryland's restrictions, though one judge would have invalidated most of them. Both sides appealed; Maryland separately petitioned on the private-property ruling, but later withdrew that petition.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked whether a government's historical power to ban guns in a narrow set of traditionally sensitive locations extends to the broad range of places Maryland has restricted, and what principles should guide courts in deciding which locations qualify as sensitive enough to justify such bans.

## Where it stands

The petition has been distributed for conference twice, most recently for October 9, 2026, with the docket showing no action recorded since and the case "rescheduled" after each distribution.

## What each outcome would mean

Novotny and the other gun owners and groups, as petitioners, are asking the Court to take up the case. If the Court grants review, it would hear full arguments and decide whether Maryland's restrictions are constitutional, deciding nothing yet about who wins. If the Court denies review, the Fourth Circuit's decision upholding most of Maryland's restrictions stands, leaving those laws in effect.
