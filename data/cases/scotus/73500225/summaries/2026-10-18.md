## In brief

Gun owners ask the Supreme Court to strike down Maryland's bans on carrying firearms in places like parks, transit, hospitals, and bars.

## What happened

Maryland expanded its list of "sensitive places" where carrying a gun is banned, even for permit holders, after a Supreme Court ruling made it easier to get carry permits. Gun owners and gun-rights groups sued, arguing the bans violate the right to bear arms. A federal district court upheld most restrictions but struck down a rule requiring permission to carry on private property open to the public. A divided Fourth Circuit panel upheld nearly all the location bans, with one judge partly dissenting. The gun owners then asked the Supreme Court to review the parts of the ruling against them.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked whether a state may ban carrying firearms in numerous places the public commonly visits — including parks, mass transit, healthcare facilities, places of amusement, and establishments serving alcohol — consistent with the right to keep and bear arms, or whether such bans sweep too broadly to be justified by historical tradition.

## Where it stands

The petition has been distributed for conference multiple times, most recently for October 16, 2026, after earlier conferences in September and October were followed by rescheduling; the docket does not show any ruling yet on whether the Court will hear the case.

## What each outcome would mean

The gun owners and gun-rights groups, as petitioners, are asking the Court to take up the case. If the Court grants review, it would hear full arguments and decide whether Maryland's location bans are constitutional, without yet deciding who wins. If the Court denies review, the Fourth Circuit's decision upholding most of Maryland's restrictions stands, leaving those bans in place. A grant does not mean the petitioners will win; a denial does not mean Maryland's law was correct, only that the lower court's ruling remains in effect.
