## In brief

Gun owners and gun-rights groups ask the Court to strike down Maryland's bans on carrying firearms in places like parks, transit, and bars.

## What happened

After the Supreme Court's 2022 Bruen decision eased Maryland's permit requirements, the state enacted new laws barring permit holders from carrying guns in numerous places, including government buildings, mass transit, schools, health care facilities, parks and forests, entertainment venues, and places serving alcohol. Gun owners and gun-rights organizations sued, claiming these restrictions violate the Second Amendment. A federal district court upheld most restrictions but struck down a rule requiring permission to carry on private property open to the public. A divided Fourth Circuit panel largely affirmed, upholding nearly all the location-based bans while agreeing the private-property rule was unconstitutional.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked whether Maryland's many restrictions on carrying firearms in places open to the public are consistent with the constitutional right to bear arms, and whether the reasoning lower courts have used to uphold such location-based gun restrictions properly follows the historical-tradition test the Court has set out in earlier rulings.

## Where it stands

The petition has been distributed for conference twice, most recently for October 9, 2026, with the docket showing the case was rescheduled rather than acted on at either conference. Briefing is complete, including a reply from the petitioners referencing the Court's recent decision in a related case, Wolford v. Lopez.

## What each outcome would mean

Novotny and the other gun owners and groups, as petitioners, are asking the Court to take up the case. If the Court grants review, it would hear full arguments and decide whether Maryland's restrictions are constitutional, without yet determining who wins. If it denies review, the Fourth Circuit's decision upholding most of Maryland's restrictions stands, leaving those laws in effect. The petitioners have also asked, as an alternative to granting review, that the Court send the case back to the lower court for reconsideration in light of Wolford without deciding the question itself.
