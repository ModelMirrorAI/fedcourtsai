## In brief

Gun owners and gun-rights groups ask the Court to strike down Maryland's bans on carrying firearms in many public locations as violating the Second Amendment.

## What happened

Maryland enacted and long maintained laws barring the public carrying of firearms at numerous locations, including government buildings, mass transit, schools, healthcare facilities, parks and forests, places of amusement, and venues serving alcohol. Gun owners and several gun-rights organizations sued, claiming these restrictions violate the Second and Fourteenth Amendments. The district court upheld most restrictions but struck down a few. A divided Fourth Circuit panel upheld nearly all of Maryland's restrictions, ruling they fit within a historical tradition of regulating firearms in "sensitive places," while unanimously striking down a rule presumptively barring carrying on private property open to the public. One judge dissented in part, arguing the majority read the relevant history too loosely.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked to decide whether laws prohibiting people from carrying firearms in numerous places open to the public are consistent with the right to keep and bear arms, including what historical principle should determine which locations the government may declare off-limits to carrying firearms, and whether lower courts applying that principle are too divided or too lenient toward government restrictions to leave the question alone for now.

## Where it stands

The petition has been distributed for conference more than once, including a conference held before this snapshot date; the docket does not record any grant, denial, or other action following that conference. A further conference is scheduled, and the case remains awaiter a decision on whether the Court will hear it.

## What each outcome would mean

Novotny and the other gun owners and organizations, as petitioners, are asking the Court to take up the case. If the Court grants review, it would hear full arguments and decide whether Maryland's restrictions are constitutional, without yet deciding who wins. If the Court denies review, the Fourth Circuit's decision upholding most of Maryland's restrictions stands, leaving those laws in effect, while the lower court's separate ruling against the private-property rule, which Maryland is not contesting here, is unaffected either way.
