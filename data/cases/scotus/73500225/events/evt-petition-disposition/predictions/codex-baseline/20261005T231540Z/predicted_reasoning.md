# Forecast

The Court will most likely deny this petition, leaving the Fourth Circuit's judgment intact without resolving the constitutional merits. I assign a 38% probability to some grant, including a grant-vacate-remand order.

The snapshot records two distributions: September 2 for the September 28 conference, followed by a September 24 rescheduling, and October 5 for the October 9 conference. This is not evidence that two conferences have considered the petition. From that recorded count of two, I narrowly expect no further distribution (52%); the probability of at least one additional distribution is 48%. If the petition is distributed again, one or two further distributions are more likely than a long sequence. I expect no call for the Solicitor General's views (1% probability of a new CVSG).

Conditional on a grant, a disposition in the cert order itself is more likely than plenary briefing and argument (60% versus 40%). The principal summary route is a GVR for consideration of Wolford's intervening guidance, not a summary reversal adjudicating the full collection of Maryland restrictions. The opposition describes Wolford as deciding the private-property consent rule; whether its reasoning also requires reconsideration of these distinct sensitive-place restrictions is the important uncertainty. A GVR would not itself establish that Maryland's challenged restrictions are unconstitutional.

If the Court instead grants plenary review, I expect a narrower formulation of the petition's broad question: what historical analogues justify treating ordinary locations open to the public as sensitive places, particularly healthcare facilities or public transit. I do not expect an undifferentiated ruling on every Maryland location or a categorical requirement that all sensitive places provide armed security. This question-selection forecast is conditional, not a forecast that a merits opinion will issue.

Conditional on denial, I expect no noted dissent or statement respecting denial (78%; some such writing 22%). I make no Justice-specific vote or authorship forecast.
