## 2. Disposition and Scope of Any Grant

- **Disposition (`disposition`: 0.22; `predicted_disposition`: `denied`):** The Court will most likely deny the petition for a writ of certiorari. Although the Fourth Circuit divided 2–1 on several sensitive-place categories, its bottom-line holdings upholding firearm restrictions in state parks and forests, mass transit, bars and restaurants serving alcohol, health care facilities, museums, and entertainment venues align with the Second, Third, Seventh, and Ninth Circuits (*Antonyuk v. James*, 120 F.4th 941 (2d Cir. 2024); *Koons v. Attorney General New Jersey*, 156 F.4th 213 (3d Cir. 2025); *Schoenthal v. Raoul*, 150 F.4th 898 (7th Cir. 2025), *cert. denied*, No. 25-541 (Apr. 6, 2026); *Wolford v. Lopez*, 116 F.4th 959 (9th Cir. 2024), *rev'd in part on other grounds*, 609 U.S. ___, 146 S. Ct. 2032 (2026)). Moreover, three of the nine categories bundled into Petitioners' single omnibus Question Presented (government buildings, school grounds, and health care facilities) were upheld unanimously 3–0 below.
- **Scope if granted (context-only):** If the Court were to grant certiorari, it would likely reformulate and narrow the Question Presented to isolate one or two specific disputed categories—most plausibly public parks, mass transit, or establishments serving alcohol—along with the methodological question of whether mid-to-late-19th-century laws can establish a historical tradition under *New York State Rifle & Pistol Ass'n v. Bruen*, 597 U.S. 1 (2022), while excluding government buildings, school grounds, and health care facilities. If granted on the merits, a majority would likely hold that mid-to-late-19th-century regulations and private-carrier rules cannot by themselves sustain categorical firearm bans in open public spaces or public transit absent Founding-era analogues.
- **Summary disposition route (`summary-disposition-route`: 0.12 conditional on grant):** Conditional on a grant, plenary review is far likelier than a summary disposition in the certiorari order itself. *Wolford v. Lopez* was decided in June 2026 (before the Brief in Opposition was filed) and addressed only the default private-property rule, which the Fourth Circuit already invalidated. The only plausible summary-disposition route would be holding the petition for the Court's OT2026 Second Amendment cases (*Viramontes v. Cook County*, No. 25-238, and *Grant v. Higgins*, No. 25-566) and entering a GVR if those decisions clarify the 1791-versus-1868 historical-timeframe question.

- **Dissent from denial (`dissent-from-denial`: 0.55 conditional on denial):** Conditional on a denial of certiorari, it is slightly more likely than not that at least one Justice will author a dissent from (or statement respecting) the denial of certiorari. Justice Thomas is the likeliest author (`0.38`), potentially joined by or writing alongside Justice Alito (`0.08`) or Justice Gorsuch (`0.08`), criticizing the Fourth Circuit majority's reliance on late-19th-century statutes, municipal ordinances, and private railroad rules to designate broad swaths of public life—including state parks, forests, mass transit, and restaurants serving alcohol—as "sensitive places" off-limits to licensed concealed carry.
