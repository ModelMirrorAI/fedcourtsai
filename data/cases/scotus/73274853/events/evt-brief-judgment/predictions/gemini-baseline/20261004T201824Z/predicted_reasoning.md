The Court is likely to reverse the Eighth Circuit's decision, finding that state substantive law—rather than a uniform federal standard set by the Fifth Amendment floor—governs the measure of just compensation in private condemnations under the Natural Gas Act.

The majority is expected to rule that when Congress categorically delegates the federal eminent domain power to private parties under 15 U.S.C. § 717f(h), it does not preempt state substantive land-valuation rules unless Congress indicates a clear intent to do so. The Court will likely rest on the ordinary tools of statutory interpretation and federalism principles, reasoning that displacing state property law requires a clear statement, and that the historical practice under the NGA pointed to state law. The Court will likely establish a broad rule that state law governs compensation in private condemnations under the NGA.

A 6-3 lineup is probable, with the conservative wing (Roberts, Thomas, Alito, Gorsuch, Kavanaugh, Barrett) in the majority, emphasizing property rights and federalism, bringing the Eighth Circuit back in line with the Third, Fifth, Sixth, and Eleventh Circuits. The liberal wing (Sotomayor, Kagan, Jackson) may dissent, reasoning that the delegation of federal eminent domain power carries with it the federal measure of compensation (the Fifth Amendment), and that adopting patchwork state compensation laws undermines the NGA's goal of facilitating interstate natural gas infrastructure. A DIG (dismissal as improvidently granted) is unlikely given the clear, mature circuit split.
