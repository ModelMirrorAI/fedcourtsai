# Forecast: what the Court will do in Hoffmann v. WBI Energy Transmission, No. 25-159

**Judgment class: `affirmed`.** The Court will hold that the Fifth Amendment, not
state law, supplies the measure of compensation when a certificate holder condemns
under 15 U.S.C. § 717f(h), and will affirm the Eighth Circuit's vacatur of the
attorney-fee award. P(judgment below disturbed) = 0.44, so this is a lean, not a
confident call; the complement is mostly a reversal (or a reverse-and-remand on the
open North Dakota-law question), with a dismissal as improvidently granted a small
residual (about 0.03) rather than a live exit.

**Vote lineup: 7–2.** Majority: Roberts, Alito, Sotomayor, Kagan, Kavanaugh, Barrett,
Jackson. Dissent: Gorsuch, joined by Thomas. The two most likely defectors from the
majority are Barrett (whose PennEast dissent resisted treating a private delegatee as
the sovereign for every purpose) and Jackson (landowner-versus-pipeline sympathies);
the two most likely to rejoin it are Thomas and Gorsuch if the opinion is written as
an American-Rule case about fee-shifting rather than a federal-common-law case. A
6–3 affirmance is the next most likely shape. Authorship is not scored; the most
likely author of a majority of this shape is the Chief Justice or Justice Kavanaugh,
and the dissent is Gorsuch's.

**Which question the Court reaches.** The Court decides the question presented as the
United States reframed it — whether the Fifth Amendment, as opposed to state law,
determines the measure of compensation in a § 717f(h) condemnation — rather than
narrowing to the fee question. Respondent's vehicle objection (that North Dakota's
fee statute is a discretionary fee-shifting rule and not part of state "just
compensation") is noted and left unresolved as unnecessary to the judgment; the SG's
cert-stage brief invited exactly that course.

**Ground of decision.** The majority's chain: § 717f(h) delegates the federal
government's own eminent-domain power (PennEast: a "categorical" delegation); when
that power is exercised, the measure of compensation is the Fifth Amendment's (Kohl,
Miller, Bodcaw), and state compensation rules do not apply of their own force to a
federal taking; Congress adopts a state measure only expressly, as it did in the
General Bridge Act of 1946 a year before the 1947 NGA amendment, and the amendment's
"practice and procedure" conformity clause shows Congress borrowing state law for
procedure alone; so statutory silence leaves no gap for Kimbell Foods gap-filling,
and the Third, Fifth, Sixth, and Eleventh Circuit decisions applying that framework
are disapproved. Attorney's fees are excluded on the independent footing that the
American Rule governs a federal cause of action absent express statutory
authorization (Alyeska; Lackey v. Stinnie). The 1947 purpose — ending dependence on
state-law eminent-domain regimes that made a federal certificate "an illusory right
to build" — is used as confirmation, not as the ground.

**Breadth.** Categorical, not fees-specific: the federal standard governs every
§ 717f(h) condemnation, whether the state rule concerns valuation methods or
litigation costs, and whether the action proceeds in federal or state court. The
Court will not confine the holding to North Dakota's statute.

**Separate writings.** Gorsuch dissents on Rules-of-Decision-Act and federalism
grounds: the Fifth Amendment is a floor on government, not a ceiling on what a
private condemnor must pay; a private company, unlike the United States, is
ordinarily subject to state law; nothing in § 717f(h) displaces it; and the Court is
in substance fashioning federal common law of the kind Rodriguez v. FDIC rejected,
against forty years of unanimous circuit practice that produced no obstruction of
pipeline construction. Thomas joins. A short concurrence from Kavanaugh or Barrett
flagging that Congress can adopt state measures expressly is possible but not
forecast. No plurality fracture: the majority rationale and result travel together.

**Procedural exits.** A DIG is unlikely (about 0.03): both courts below treated the
state-versus-federal question as outcome-determinative, the SG called the vehicle
suitable, and the respondent's state-law point can be handled by assumption. An
equally divided Court is not in play; no recusal is indicated.

**Timing.** Argued Monday, 9 November 2026 (divided argument with the SG likely
granted). Decision expected between late February and early May 2027, with the
modal window March–April; this is a short, two-sided statutory case that should not
wait for the June rush.
