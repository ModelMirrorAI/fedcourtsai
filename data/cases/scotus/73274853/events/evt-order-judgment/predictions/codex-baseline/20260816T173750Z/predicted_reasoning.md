# Forecast

The Court will affirm the Eighth Circuit, most likely by a 6-3 vote. Chief Justice Roberts and Justices Alito, Sotomayor, Kagan, Kavanaugh, and Jackson will vote to affirm; Justices Thomas, Gorsuch, and Barrett will vote to reverse. A dismissal as improvidently granted or an equally divided affirmance is unlikely.

The majority will answer the granted question broadly. It will hold that a certificate holder exercising eminent domain under 15 U.S.C. § 717f(h) exercises the federal eminent-domain power that Congress delegated categorically. The Fifth Amendment therefore supplies the default measure of just compensation. Because Congress did not incorporate state substantive compensation rules or authorize fees in these circumstances, North Dakota law cannot add the landowners' litigation expenses to the award.

The Court will reject the argument that statutory silence creates a gap for judicial incorporation of state law under *United States v. Kimbell Foods*. It will reason that the constitutional compensation rule already fills the field and that § 717f(h)'s express reference to state practice and procedure does not incorporate state substantive law. It will also rely on Congress's express treatment of enhanced compensation and fee awards in other statutes as evidence that a greater award requires legislative direction.

The dissent will emphasize that private delegations of eminent-domain authority are strictly construed, that property and compensation rules traditionally come from state law, and that *PennEast Pipeline Co. v. New Jersey* decided the scope of the taking power rather than its compensation measure. It will favor the state-law rule followed by the Third, Fifth, Sixth, and Eleventh Circuits.

The likely judgment is a straightforward affirmance rather than a vacatur and remand. The majority's ground will extend beyond North Dakota attorney fees to the governing measure of compensation in private Natural Gas Act condemnations nationwide, while recognizing that Congress remains free to prescribe more generous compensation expressly.
