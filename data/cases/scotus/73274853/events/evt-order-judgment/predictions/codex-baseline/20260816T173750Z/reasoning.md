# Rationale

I assign a 0.34 probability that the judgment below will be disturbed and predict an affirmance. The committed merits statpack provides the starting point: across the available grant Terms strictly before the case's 2025 grant Term (2017-2024), 359 of 515 parsed judgments were disturbed, a pooled rate of 69.7%. The sample clears the 30-judgment floor and excludes cert-order dispositions, although recent-Term coverage remains affected by pending cases.

The case-specific evidence warrants a large downward adjustment from that general merits rate. Most importantly, the Solicitor General's pre-grant CVSG brief expressly recommended that the Court grant review and affirm. It argued that *PennEast Pipeline Co. v. New Jersey* described § 717f(h)'s delegation as categorical, that the Fifth Amendment supplies the compensation measure without a common-law gap, and that Congress must expressly adopt state law or enhanced compensation. The Court granted after receiving that recommendation. That sequence is unusually strong evidence that the Court took the case to resolve the acknowledged split on the federal-law side, not merely to correct the outlier Eighth Circuit.

The respondent also has a narrower route through the American Rule. North Dakota's provision can be characterized as discretionary fee shifting rather than part of just compensation, and federal statutes authorize condemnation fees expressly in limited circumstances not present here. Even if the Court does not embrace every part of the Eighth Circuit's reasoning, that feature makes affirmance easier.

I retain a substantial 0.34 disturbance probability because the Eighth Circuit rejected the rule of four other circuits. The petition's strongest arguments are that *Kimbell Foods* favors state law, property rules are a traditional state concern, private delegations should be construed narrowly, and *PennEast* concerned the scope of condemnation rather than compensation. The cert grant could therefore be a vehicle for reversing a newly created outlier. A reversal might also take the form of a vacatur and remand over whether North Dakota's fees are actually compensation, making the precise judgment class less certain than the binary forecast.

The predicted vote split follows the unusual alignment in *PennEast* while accounting for the different issue here. Roberts, Alito, Sotomayor, and Kavanaugh are the strongest affirmance votes from that decision's categorical-delegation reasoning; Jackson is likely to join them, and Kagan is the marginal sixth vote based on the no-gap and American Rule arguments. Thomas, Gorsuch, and Barrett are the most plausible dissenters on limited-delegation, federalism, and property-rights grounds. Individual votes are less certain than the judgment.

This is a forward prediction. The provisioned record included the petition, brief in opposition, questions presented, current docket snapshot, and grant-moment context.
