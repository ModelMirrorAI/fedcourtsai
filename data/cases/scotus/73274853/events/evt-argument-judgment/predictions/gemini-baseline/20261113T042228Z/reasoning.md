## Why I Move Sharply Down from the 70.4% Disturbed Baseline to `0.09`

Three reinforcing layers of case-specific evidence move `P(disturbed)` from the `0.704` base rate down to **`0.09`**:

### 1. Selection Posture and Solicitor General Alignment ("Grant to Affirm")

The Court's 70.4% historical disturbed rate reflects ordinary certiorari selection: the Court predominantly grants review to reverse rulings it views as dubious. Here, `sg-invited-brief.txt` and `merits-amicus-united-states.txt` show the exact opposite selection mechanism. Four circuits (the Third, Fifth, Sixth, and Eleventh) had held under *United States v. Kimbell Foods, Inc.* that state law governs compensation in private Natural Gas Act (`15 U.S.C. § 717f(h)`) condemnations, whereas a unanimous, cross-ideological Eighth Circuit panel (Judges Shepherd, Kelly, and Stras, with no rehearing petition filed) broke away after *PennEast Pipeline Co. v. New Jersey*, 594 U.S. 482 (2021), holding that federal Fifth Amendment law governs and vacating the Hoffmanns' attorney-fee award. In his invited brief (`sg-invited-brief.txt`), the Solicitor General explicitly urged the Court to **grant certiorari and affirm the Eighth Circuit** because the Eleventh Circuit (*Sabal Trail*) had refused to revisit its pre-*PennEast* precedent, leaving a 4–1 split that only this Court could clean up. On the merits, the United States (`merits-amicus-united-states.txt`) filed in **support of Respondent WBI Energy Transmission, Inc.** and participated in divided argument on Respondent's side. Our forecast follows the United States' position closely, both because the SG's merits support for a respondent is empirically a powerful predictor of affirmance and because the SG's doctrinal synthesis harmonized seamlessly with the bench's own view at argument.

### 2. Doctrinal Asymmetry in the Merits Briefing

On the merits, Petitioners (`merits-brief-petitioner.txt`, `merits-reply-petitioner.txt`) largely subordinated the *Kimbell Foods* federal-common-law rationale on which the four-circuit majority had relied, arguing instead that North Dakota condemnation law applies of its own force unless preempted (invoking *Hencely v. Fluor Corp.* from OT2025) and that *United States v. Miller* and *United States v. Bodcaw Co.* rest solely on federal sovereign immunity rather than on the nature of the federal eminent-domain power. Respondent (`merits-brief-respondent.txt`) and the Solicitor General countered with a tight statutory and constitutional chain: (a) *PennEast* established that § 717f(h) delegates the federal eminent-domain power itself; (b) *Kohl*, *Monongahela*, *Miller*, *93.970 Acres*, and *Bodcaw* treat the Fifth Amendment market-value measure (excluding attorney fees) as an incident of federal condemnation authority rather than an immunity defense; and (c) Congress expressly incorporated state law for "practice and procedure" in § 717f(h) and for compensation in the 1946 General Bridge Act (`33 U.S.C. § 532`), enacted one year earlier, while omitting any state-compensation directive from the 1947 NGA amendment. Moreover, because the Eighth Circuit already vacated the fee award outright, agreeing with Respondent produces a clean `affirmed` judgment rather than a `vacated` remand.

## Uncertainties and Where to Discount

My `0.09` disturbed probability preserves a disciplined tail rather than pricing affirmance at ~98%: post-argument surprises occasionally arise during drafting if several textualist/federalism-minded Justices revisit the pre-1947 Federal Power Act history (*Feltz*), *United States v. Jones* (1883), or *Kimbell Foods* incorporation, or if a latent North Dakota state-law wrinkle surfaces (~1.5% DIG risk, which counts as undisturbed). A reader should discount the unanimity call slightly more than the judgment call: an 8–1 split (with Gorsuch or Thomas dissenting) is substantially likelier (~20%–25%) than a 5-vote flip of the judgment.
