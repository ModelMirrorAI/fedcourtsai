# Predicted judgment and opinion

## Judgment and lineup

The Court will **affirm the Eighth Circuit**, leaving in place that court's vacatur of the attorney-fee award against WBI. The predicted vote is **9–0**: Roberts, Thomas, Alito, Sotomayor, Kagan, Gorsuch, Kavanaugh, Barrett, and Jackson join the opinion of the Court. The Court will decide the argued merits rather than dismiss the writ or divide equally.

**Barrett is the single most likely author**, although the assignment forecast is much less certain than the judgment forecast. The point forecast is a signed Barrett opinion without a separate writing. If there is a concurrence, the leading candidates are Gorsuch, Jackson, or Thomas; its likely focus is preserving the distinction between federal compensation standards and the immunities of the sovereign itself, or explaining the limits of borrowing state law.

## Majority ground

The Court will hold that **Section 717f(h)'s delegation of federal eminent-domain power carries the federal Fifth Amendment measure of compensation rather than incorporating state compensation law**. It will read the statute's silence as leaving that established federal rule in place, not as creating a gap that requires Kimbell Foods borrowing. North Dakota therefore cannot add attorney fees to the compensation owed in this condemnation.

The opinion will connect PennEast's understanding of the delegated power with the federal compensation precedents, distinguish the definition of property interests from the measure of payment for a federal taking, and reject the premise that sovereign immunity alone accounts for the federal default. Congress can authorize greater compensation or adopt state standards, but did not do so here. The existing reference to state practice and procedure will not become an incorporation of state substantive compensation rules.

The Court will not hold that the Fifth Amendment forbids legislatures from providing more than market-value compensation. Its reasoning will instead identify the standard Congress selected for these statutory condemnations. Nor will the decision confer every immunity of the United States on a private pipeline company.

## Breadth

The majority will announce **a rule for the measure of compensation in private condemnations under the Natural Gas Act generally**, rather than an attorney-fee-only exception or a rule restricted to these owners. That rule reaches state-law enhancements to compensation under this statute, not merely this particular North Dakota award. The Court will not purport to displace ordinary state property definitions, decide every feature of the Federal Power Act, or immunize private federal delegees from generally applicable state law in unrelated settings.

The Court will resolve the statutory choice-of-law question. It will have no need to decide WBI's alternative contention about the proper characterization of attorney fees under North Dakota law, because the federal compensation rule makes that state-law classification immaterial to this award.
