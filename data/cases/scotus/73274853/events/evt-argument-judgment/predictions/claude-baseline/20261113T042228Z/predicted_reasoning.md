# Forecast: Hoffmann v. WBI Energy Transmission, No. 25-159

**Judgment: affirmed.** The Court will affirm the Eighth Circuit and hold that just compensation in a private condemnation under the Natural Gas Act is measured by federal law (the Fifth Amendment standard), not by state law. North Dakota's attorney-fee rule therefore does not apply.

**Lineup: 8-1, with a 7-2 or unanimous result each plausible.** Roberts, Sotomayor, Kagan, Kavanaugh, Barrett and Jackson form the core of the majority. Alito and Thomas join. Gorsuch is the likeliest dissenter, on the ground that the identical Federal Power Act language was read to use state compensation law before 1947 and that state courts kept using state law afterward; if there is a second dissenter it is Thomas, on the theory that a private delegee does not inherit the sovereign's exemption from state law.

**Ground.** The majority will reason that Section 7(h) delegates the federal eminent domain power itself (PennEast), that compensation is inseparable from that power, and that the default for federal condemnations is the federal measure unless Congress says otherwise (Miller, 93.97 Acres). It will point to the textual contrast: Section 7(h) adopts state "practice and procedure" expressly and is silent on compensation, while the General Bridge Act of 1946 expressly adopted state compensation law one year earlier. It will say there is no statutory gap, so Kimbell Foods borrowing is not the mode of decision, and may add that a uniform federal measure serves the Act's purpose of freeing interstate pipelines from state eminent domain law. The sovereign-immunity explanation the petitioners offered for the federal-condemnor cases will be rejected: those cases rest on the nature of the power, not on immunity.

**Breadth.** A categorical rule for Natural Gas Act condemnations, reaching all state compensation enhancements, not just fees. The opinion will not purport to decide other delegation statutes.

**Who writes.** The opinion of the Court most likely comes from Roberts (author of PennEast, who called this an easy case), Kavanaugh or Jackson, with Sotomayor, Barrett and Kagan close behind. Gorsuch is the most likely separate writer (a dissent). A short Thomas writing is possible. No procedural exit is live: no recusal, no vehicle problem surfaced at argument, so a DIG or an equally divided Court is very unlikely.

**Timing.** Decision likely between January and April 2027.
