## Why I moved so far below it
The argument was one-sided, and I read it as a strong lean, though a question is not a vote.

- Roberts pressed the petitioners' counsel repeatedly with a hypothetical (the government condemns, pays no fees, and resells to the pipeline) and said he did not see why this was not an easy case. He returned to it with the government's lawyer in a friendly way.
- Sotomayor cited Monongahela and PennEast against separating the taking power from compensation.
- Jackson argued at length that federal law is the default for federal condemnations (93.97 Acres, Miller) and that the General Bridge Act contrast cuts against the petitioners. She also raised purpose frustration.
- Kavanaugh endorsed the Bridge Act contrast, said Miller, Bodcaw and PennEast together are a problem for the petitioners, and raised consumer energy costs.
- Barrett joined the Bridge Act point and probed which theory the petitioners rely on. Kagan said the ordinary meaning of eminent domain includes compensation and tested the petitioners with a treble-damages hypothetical.
- The respondent and the government drew few and mostly mild questions. `argument-features.json` agrees: 90 Justice turns (2,483 words) during the petitioners' time against 16 turns (494 words) for the respondent's side, with 106 of 107 Justice turns attributed. Counts measure attention, not direction, but here the content matches.

Points for the petitioners: Gorsuch confirmed that the Federal Power Act language is identical and asked the respondent to explain the state decisions applying state law; Kagan and Barrett asked why so many circuits went the other way; Thomas asked both the respondent and the government to answer the sovereign-immunity argument directly. The 4-1 circuit split in the petitioners' favor is a real reason for caution, and the Court grants mostly to reverse. I weigh those less than six Justices' stated difficulties with the petitioners' core premise.

I leave 0.12 for a reversal or vacatur, including a narrow remand route (for example, on the practice-and-procedure clause that Sotomayor and Gorsuch raised, although counsel disclaimed it).

## Votes

Thomas is uncertain: his questions gave the petitioners' immunity theory a hearing. Gorsuch is my one predicted dissent, at perhaps 55%. I did not use the per-Justice statpack section beyond noting that it gives no case-specific signal; the transcript drove the lineup.

## Authorship
The case was argued in the November sitting and I know nothing of that sitting's assignments, so I stayed fairly close to flat among the likely majority and shaded up Roberts (PennEast author), Kavanaugh and Jackson for their engagement.
