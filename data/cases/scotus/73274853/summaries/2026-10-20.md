## In brief

Property owners and a pipeline company dispute whether state or federal law sets the compensation owed when a private natural-gas company condemns land under the Natural Gas Act's eminent-domain provision.

## What happened

WBI Energy, a natural-gas pipeline company, condemned easements across ranchland owned by the Hoffmanns and others in North Dakota under the Natural Gas Act, which lets certificate holders use eminent domain to build pipelines. The parties settled on the value of the land but disputed whether WBI also owed the landowners' attorney's fees. The district court, applying North Dakota law, awarded over $383,000 in fees and costs. The Eighth Circuit reversed, holding that federal law, not state law, governs compensation in these private condemnations, and that federal law does not allow attorney's fees. Other circuits had reached the opposite conclusion.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked whether, when a private pipeline company exercises the government's eminent-domain power under the Natural Gas Act, the amount landowners must be paid is governed by the compensation rules of the state where the land sits or by a uniform federal standard tied to the Constitution's minimum requirement.

## Where it stands

The Court granted certiorari, received briefing including an invited brief from the Solicitor General supporting review, and heard oral argument on November 9, 2026. The case now awaits decision.

## What each outcome would mean

The Hoffmanns and the other landowners, as petitioners, are asking the Court to reverse the Eighth Circuit and rule that state law governs compensation. If the Court rules for the landowners, state compensation rules—including North Dakota's allowance of attorney's fees—would apply in these condemnations, and the case would likely return to the lower courts to apply that standard. If the Court rules for WBI Energy, the Eighth Circuit's judgment would stand, meaning only the federal constitutional minimum applies and the landowners cannot recover their attorney's fees. A decision to vacate and send the case back would leave the compensation question undecided for either side.
