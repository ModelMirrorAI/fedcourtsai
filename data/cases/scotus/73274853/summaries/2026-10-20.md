## In brief

The Court heard a dispute over whether state or federal law sets the compensation a private pipeline company must pay landowners when it condemns land under the Natural Gas Act.

## What happened

WBI Energy, a pipeline company, condemned easements across land owned by the Hoffmann family and others in North Dakota to build a natural-gas pipeline. After settling on the land's value, the landowners sought to recover attorney's fees and costs, which North Dakota law allows in condemnation cases. WBI argued federal law governs instead, and federal law does not allow such fees. The district court sided with the landowners, applying North Dakota law. The Eighth Circuit reversed, holding that federal law alone governs compensation in these condemnations, and that it does not include attorney's fees. The Eighth Circuit acknowledged its ruling conflicted with decisions from four other appeals courts holding that state law applies.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked whether, when a private company exercises the federal government's power of eminent domain under the Natural Gas Act, the compensation owed to landowners is governed by federal constitutional minimums or by the (sometimes more generous) rules of the state where the land sits.

## Where it stands

The Court granted certiorari, received briefing from both sides and from the United States as amicus curiae, and heard oral argument on November 9, 2026. The case now awaits a decision on the merits.

## What each outcome would mean

The landowners, Hoffmann and others, are asking the Court to reverse the Eighth Circuit's decision, while WBI Energy and the federal government (appearing as amicus) are asking it to be affirmed. If the Court rules for the landowners, state law would govern compensation in these condemnations, meaning North Dakota's rule allowing recovery of attorney's fees and costs would apply, and similar state-law protections would apply in other states. If the Court rules for WBI Energy, federal law alone would set the compensation standard nationwide, and landowners could not recover fees or other state-law benefits beyond the federal constitutional minimum.
