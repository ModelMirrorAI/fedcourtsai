## In brief

The Court will decide whether federal or state law sets the compensation a pipeline company must pay landowners when it condemns land under the Natural Gas Act.

## What happened

WBI Energy, a natural gas company, condemned pipeline easements across land owned by the Hoffmann family and others in North Dakota, using eminent domain power delegated by the Natural Gas Act. After the parties settled on the land's value, the landowners sought attorney's fees and expenses, which North Dakota law allows in condemnation cases. The district court agreed that North Dakota law controlled and awarded over $383,000 in fees. The Eighth Circuit reversed, holding that federal law, not state law, governs compensation in these cases, and that attorney's fees are not available under the federal standard. The landowners asked the Supreme Court to review that ruling, pointing to conflicting decisions from other appeals courts.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked whether, when a private company exercises the federal government's eminent domain power under the Natural Gas Act, the law of the state where the land sits should determine how much compensation the landowner receives, or whether a uniform federal standard applies instead. That question determines whether the landowners here can recover their attorney's fees as part of their compensation.

## Where it stands

The Court granted review, received briefing from both sides and the federal government, heard oral argument on November 9, 2026, and the case now awaits decision.

## What each outcome would mean

The landowners, Hoffmann and the other petitioners, are asking the Court to reverse the Eighth Circuit and rule that state law governs compensation. If the Court rules for the landowners, it would restore the district court's award of fees to them and establish that state compensation rules, including fee awards, apply in these condemnation cases nationwide. If the Court rules for WBI Energy instead, the Eighth Circuit's decision would stand, meaning only the federal constitutional minimum applies and the landowners' fee award would be eliminated. The Court could also send the case back without resolving the question for either side.
