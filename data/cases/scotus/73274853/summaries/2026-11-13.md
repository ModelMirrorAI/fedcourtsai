## In brief

Landowners whose property was condemned for a natural gas pipeline ask the Court to decide whether state or federal law sets the compensation owed in such takings.

## What happened

WBI Energy Transmission, a certificate holder under the Natural Gas Act, condemned pipeline easements across ranchland owned by Leonard Hoffmann and other McKenzie County, North Dakota landowners. After the parties settled on the easements' value, the landowners sought attorney's fees and expenses, arguing North Dakota law entitles condemnees to such costs as part of just compensation. The district court agreed and awarded over $383,000. The Eighth Circuit reversed, holding that federal law, not state law, measures compensation in private condemnations under the Act, and that the Fifth Amendment does not require payment of attorney's fees. The Eighth Circuit acknowledged its decision conflicted with rulings from four other circuits that had applied state compensation law in similar cases.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked whether, when a private company condemns land under a federal law that lets it exercise eminent domain but says nothing about how to calculate compensation, courts must look to the law of the state where the land sits (which may provide more generous compensation, including attorney's fees) or whether a uniform federal standard limited to the property's fair market value applies instead.

## Where it stands

The Court granted certiorari, received briefing including from the United States as amicus curiae supporting the pipeline company, heard oral argument on November 9, 2026, and the case now awaits decision.

## What each outcome would mean

The landowners, who lost below, are asking the Court to reverse the Eighth Circuit and hold that state law governs compensation, which would let them seek attorney's fees and other costs North Dakota law may allow. WBI Energy, which won below, argues for affirming the Eighth Circuit's rule that only the federal constitutional minimum—fair market value of the property, without attorney's fees—applies nationwide. The United States, participating in support of WBI, urges the same result. A ruling for the landowners would mean state compensation rules, including fee-shifting statutes, apply in these condemnations; a ruling for WBI would establish a single federal compensation standard for all such pipeline takings.
