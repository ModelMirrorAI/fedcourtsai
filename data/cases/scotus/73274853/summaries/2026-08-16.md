## What happened

WBI Energy Transmission, a natural gas company, used its federal certificate to condemn easements across ranchland in North Dakota owned by Leonard Hoffmann and other family members. After the parties settled on the value of the land itself, they still disputed whether the landowners could recover their attorneys' fees. The landowners argued North Dakota law, which allows such fee awards, should apply; WBI argued federal law, which generally does not allow them, should control. A federal district court sided with the landowners and awarded over $383,000 in fees, but the Eighth Circuit Court of Appeals reversed, holding that federal law governs and fees are unavailable. The landowners say this conflicts with rulings in four other circuits.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked whether, when a private pipeline company takes land under its delegated federal condemnation power, the payment owed to the landowner should be measured using the law of the state where the land sits, or instead only the minimum required under federal constitutional standards. The dispute here centers specifically on whether that landowner-favorable state-law approach extends to letting landowners recover their attorneys' fees in these condemnation cases.

## Where it stands
