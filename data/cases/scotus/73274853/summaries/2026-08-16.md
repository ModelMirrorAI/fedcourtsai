## In brief

Landowners ask the Court to decide whether state or federal law sets compensation, including attorney's fees, when a pipeline company condemns land under the Natural Gas Act.

## What happened

WBI Energy Transmission, a pipeline company, condemned an easement across ranchland owned by Leonard Hoffmann and others in North Dakota, using eminent domain authority granted under the Natural Gas Act. After settling the property's value, the landowners sought attorney's fees under North Dakota law. The district court awarded fees, applying state law. The Eighth Circuit reversed, holding that federal law, not state law, governs compensation in these condemnations, and that federal law does not allow fee awards. The landowners say this conflicts with rulings in four other appeals courts.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked whether landowners whose property is taken by a pipeline company exercising delegated federal condemnation power are entitled to compensation as measured by the state's rules, including fee-shifting laws, or only to the minimum compensation the Constitution requires, without fees.

## Where it stands

The case was distributed for several conferences, and the Court invited the Solicitor General to weigh in, who later filed a brief. The Court granted the petition, set the case for argument on November 9, 2026, and briefing is underway; the case has not yet been argued or decided.

## What each outcome would mean

Hoffmann and the other landowners petitioned after losing in the Eighth Circuit. If the Court rules for the landowners, it would hold that state law, including fee-shifting rules like North Dakota's, governs compensation in these condemnations, letting them pursue fees. If the Court rules for WBI Energy Transmission, the Eighth Circuit's decision applying only federal compensation rules, which exclude attorney's fees, would stand. The Court could also send the case back without deciding the question either way.
