## In brief

Landowners whose property was taken for a natural-gas pipeline ask the Supreme Court to decide whether state or federal law sets the compensation a pipeline company must pay.

## What happened

WBI Energy Transmission, a pipeline company holding federal approval to build a natural-gas pipeline, condemned easements across ranchland in North Dakota owned by Leonard Hoffmann and others. After the parties settled on the land's value, the landowners sought attorney's fees and costs, which North Dakota law allows in condemnation cases. A district court agreed, applying state law. The Eighth Circuit reversed, holding that federal law alone governs compensation in these pipeline condemnations and that it does not include attorney's fees, splitting from four other appeals courts that had applied state compensation rules in similar cases.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked whether, when a pipeline company uses the government's delegated power to take land for an interstate gas project, the payment owed to the landowner is set by federal rules alone or by the rules of the state where the land sits, which in some states allow more than the federal minimum, including attorney's fees.

## Where it stands

The Court granted cert, and the case has been argued and briefed on the merits; it was set for argument on November 9, 2026, and now awaits decision.

## What each outcome would mean

Hoffmann and the other landowners petitioned the Court after losing in the Eighth Circuit. If the Court rules for Hoffmann, the landowners could recover compensation measured by North Dakota law, including the attorney's fees they sought, and the Eighth Circuit's ruling would not stand as the final word. If the Court rules for WBI, the Eighth Circuit's decision applying only the federal minimum compensation standard, which excludes attorney's fees, would be affirmed. The Court could also send the case back without deciding the question either way.
