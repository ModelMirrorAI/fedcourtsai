A San Francisco homeowner asks the Court to hear her claim that city code-enforcement inspections of her property violated her Fourth Amendment rights.

## What happened

Mihai Emberton, representing herself, sued the City and County of San Francisco over code-enforcement actions involving a fence and other structures on her property, arguing the inspections amounted to unconstitutional searches. A federal district court ruled against her on the pleadings. She then pursued related claims in California state court; the San Francisco Superior Court sustained the city's demurrer (a ruling that her complaint failed to state a valid claim) without leave to amend. The California Court of Appeal denied her petition, noting it also appeared to violate court formatting rules. The California Supreme Court denied review. She now asks the U.S. Supreme Court to take up the case.

## What the Court is being asked

The petition asks the Court to clarify how courts should decide when a government inspection counts as a "search" requiring constitutional protections: whether that depends on whether the property or information was visible to the public or located in a particular place, or instead depends on whether the government interfered with the owner's right to keep others out, regardless of visibility or location.

## Where it stands

The petition was distributed for the Court's conference scheduled for November 6, 2026. No action on it has yet been recorded.

## What each outcome would mean

Emberton, as petitioner, is asking the Court to take up her case. If the Court grants the petition, it would agree to hear full arguments and decide the Fourth Amendment question itself, which would not by itself determine who wins. If the Court denies the petition, the California courts' rulings against Emberton remain in place, and her case ends without the Supreme Court deciding the underlying constitutional question.
