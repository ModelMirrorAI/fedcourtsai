## In brief

A San Francisco property owner asks the Court to revive her Fourth Amendment challenge to city code-enforcement inspections of her property.

## What happened

Mihal Emberton, representing herself, sued the City and County of San Francisco over code-enforcement actions involving a fence and other structures on her property, after a neighbor's complaint led building, planning, and public works officials to investigate. The trial court sustained the city's demurrer (a motion arguing the complaint fails to state a valid claim) against her amended complaint. The California Court of Appeal denied her petition challenging that result, noting her filing also exceeded the court's word-count and formatting rules. The California Supreme Court denied review. She now asks the U.S. Supreme Court to take the case.

## What the Court is being asked

The petition asks the Court to decide how courts should determine whether a government inspection of property counts as a search requiring constitutional protections. One question asks whether that determination turns on whether the area inspected was visible from outside, or instead on whether officials interfered with the owner's right to keep others out. The other asks whether the fact that an inspection happened can depend on where it occurred or what kind of evidence was collected, or whether that fact stands on its own regardless of location or evidence type.

## Where it stands

The petition was distributed for the Court's November 6, 2026 conference. The docket does not yet show any action taken on it.

## What each outcome would mean

Emberton, the petitioner, is asking the Court to hear her case and overturn the rulings against her. If the Court grants the petition, it would hear full arguments and decide the constitutional questions about government property inspections, without deciding in advance who wins. If the Court denies the petition, the California courts' rulings against Emberton stand, and her lawsuit remains unsuccessful; a denial would not mean the Court agrees with those rulings, only that it leaves them in place.
