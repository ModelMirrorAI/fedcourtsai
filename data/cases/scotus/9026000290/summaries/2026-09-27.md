## What happened

Mihai Emberton, a San Francisco property owner, disputes how the city's code enforcement agencies handled inspections of her property after a neighbor complained about noise. Emberton says these inspections were unconstitutional searches conducted without a warrant, consent, or any way to challenge them, even though her property was safe and code-compliant, and that the process led to permit demands, fees, and other enforcement actions. She sued the city; a trial court ruled against her, and the California Court of Appeal and California Supreme Court both declined to take up her case further. She now asks the U.S. Supreme Court to review the matter herself, without a lawyer.

## What the Court is being asked

The case asks the Court to clarify what counts as a government search of private property for constitutional purposes. One question is whether a search's effect on someone's privacy depends on whether the property was visible from outside, or instead on whether the government interfered with the owner's right to keep others out. The other question is whether the fact that a search happened can be erased by where the search took place or what kind of information or property was taken, or whether a search counts as a search no matter its location or the type of evidence collected.
