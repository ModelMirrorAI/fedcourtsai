# Forecast: Emberton v. Superior Court of California, San Francisco (No. 26-290)

**Disposition: denied**, on the paper record, without argument and without any separate writing.

## Procedural path

The docket shows zero distributions; this cell is forecast at docketing. The response is due October 5, 2026. The City and County of San Francisco (the real party in interest) will almost certainly waive its right to respond rather than file a brief in opposition, so the petition will be distributed once, for a late-October or early-November 2026 conference, and denied in the order list following that conference — most likely by mid-November 2026. Expect exactly one distribution and no relist: there is nothing here that would hold a Justice's attention for a second conference.

## Call for the views of the Solicitor General

No. The dispute is between a private homeowner and a municipal code-enforcement program under state law; no federal statute or federal program's interest is implicated, and the United States has no stake in how San Francisco enforces its fence and permit rules.

## Which question, if any, the Court would take

None as written. Both questions presented ask the Court to choose between two lines of its own Fourth Amendment precedent (Ciraolo, Dow, Marshall v. Barlow's against Katz, Kyllo, Horton, Boyd) — a framing that posits a conflict among the Court's own cases rather than a split among lower courts. Underneath is a demurrer sustained to a seventh amended complaint alleging that code inspectors' observation of a front-yard fence from the street was a "search." If the Court were ever to revisit plain-view observation of curtilage from a public vantage point, it would do so in a counseled criminal or § 1983 case with a developed record and a reasoned lower-court opinion, not from a one-line California Court of Appeal writ denial.

## Summary disposition

Not in play. There is no intervening decision of this Court that bears on the questions, so a GVR has nothing to key on, and a summary reversal is not a realistic route from an unreasoned state writ denial. Conditional on a grant of any kind — itself very unlikely — the cert-order route (a GVR) would be somewhat likelier than plenary review, because nothing about this record recommends it as an argued vehicle; that is the conditional stated in the claims block.

## Dissent or statement respecting denial

None expected. No Justice has signalled interest in re-examining the plain-view / open-fields line in the municipal-inspection context, and the petition's framing gives none a doctrinal hook.

## If granted (conditional)

Only if the Court, contrary to the forecast above, took the case would the merits matter. In that case it would reformulate the question around whether a code inspector's visual observation of an unenclosed front yard from a public sidewalk is a Fourth Amendment search, and would answer no under Ciraolo and Jardines's public-vantage principle, affirming.
