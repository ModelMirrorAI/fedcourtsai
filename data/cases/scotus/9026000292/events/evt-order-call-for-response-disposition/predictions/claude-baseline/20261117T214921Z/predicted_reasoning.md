# Forecast: Havlish v. Taliban, No. 26-292 (response-requested moment)

**State forecast from.** The docket shows two distributions (conferences of 11/6 and 11/13/2026), a CVSG issued 16 November 2026 with Justice Kagan recused, and a response requested on 17 November 2026 (due 17 December 2026). The companions, Nos. 26-293 (Ashton) and 26-294 (Owens), carry the same sequence.

**Further distributions.** The petition will be distributed again, almost certainly: once after the Solicitor General files the invited brief. That brief will most likely arrive in May 2027, possibly as late as the following autumn or December, and the Court will act on the petition within a few weeks of the redistribution. I expect one further distribution, with a second (a true relist) only if the Court is heading toward a grant or a writing accompanies a denial.

**CVSG.** Already on the docket; no second invitation will issue.

**Disposition.** Denial is the single most likely outcome, at about 64%, with a grant at about 36%. The Court will follow the Solicitor General's recommendation. I expect the United States more likely than not to recommend denial, defending the Executive's control over blocked sovereign assets and resisting a reading of TRIA that lets a post-blocking seizure of a foreign central bank by an unrecognized regime convert that bank's reserves into executable property. The three petitions will be disposed of together.

**Question presented, if granted.** The Court would take the petition's single question as written (whether TRIA execution depends on the owner's status as a terrorist party or its agency or instrumentality at the time of blocking), most likely granting 26-292 as the lead and granting or holding 26-293 and 26-294. If the Solicitor General reframes around central-bank immunity or recognition, the Court could add or substitute that framing.

**Summary route.** Unlikely. No intervening decision bears on the question, so a GVR has no hook, and a summary reversal of a published Second Circuit decision on a statutory question with foreign-relations stakes is implausible. If granted, the case is set for argument, with Justice Kagan not participating.

**Separate writings on a denial.** Probably none. If any, a short dissent or statement from Justice Thomas, Alito or Gorsuch on the text of TRIA is the likeliest form.
