The Supreme Court is slightly more likely to decline this appeal than to hear it: the chance of review is about 45 in 100. Families of terrorism victims and insurers seek access to frozen Afghan central-bank funds. They argue that the appeals court wrongly limited when those funds can be seized. Lower-court disagreement and billions of dollars at stake make review plausible. The Court has already requested the federal government's views, so a decision will probably take several months while it considers that advice and the requested response.
