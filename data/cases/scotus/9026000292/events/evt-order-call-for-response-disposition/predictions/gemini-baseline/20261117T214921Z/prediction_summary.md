The Supreme Court will likely deny review in this appeal, with a 38 percent—nearly two in five—chance of taking the case. Thousands of September 11 victims seek $3.5 billion in frozen Afghan central-bank reserves after a divided appeals court ruled that federal terrorism law requires the bank to have been Taliban-controlled when the funds were first frozen. Although the Court invited the Solicitor General (the federal government's top appellate lawyer) to weigh in and requested a response, the government will likely urge the Court to stay out for foreign-policy reasons, and Justice Kagan's recusal leaves eight Justices.
