## 3. Adjustments From the Anchor (`0.350 -> 0.38`)

### Upward Factors (+10 percentage points)

1. **Textualist vulnerability of the Second Circuit's holding and sharp en banc dissents (+5 pp):** Section 201(a) of TRIA authorizes execution on `"the blocked assets of that terrorist party (including the blocked assets of any agency or instrumentality of that terrorist party)"` wherever a plaintiff `"has obtained a judgment against a terrorist party,"` and § 201(d)(2)(A) defines `"blocked asset"` simply as `"any asset seized or frozen by the United States"` under TWEA or IEEPA. Judge Cabranes's panel majority held that the owner must have been a terrorist agency or instrumentality *at the moment the assets were blocked* (August 15, 2021), defeating turnover because DAB had not yet fallen under Taliban control that morning. As Judge Sullivan's panel dissent and four-judge en banc dissent (joined by Chief Judge Livingston and Judges Bianco and Park, 170 F.4th 100) argue, that temporal limitation appears nowhere in § 201(a)'s text and creates an anomalous safe harbor whenever a terrorist regime captures an entity whose assets were blocked immediately beforehand or during regime collapse. Notably, Judge Nardini's concurrence in the denial of rehearing en banc (joined by Judge Lohier) barely defended the panel's statutory reading on the merits, relying instead on perceived non-recurrence and factual doubts about Taliban control of DAB. Given this Court's consistent rejection of uncodified glosses on sovereign-immunity, terrorism, and sanctions texts (e.g., *Turkiye Halk Bankasi*, *Rubin*, *Havana Docks*), the statutory issue has strong appeal to textualist Justices.
2. **Extraordinary stakes, 31-State amicus support, and lead-vehicle status among the trio (+3 pp):** Over $3.5 billion in blocked assets and compensation for thousands of 9/11 victims make this a major dispute, reinforced by a 31-State amicus brief filed specifically in `26-292`. Among the three companions, `26-292` is the cleanest vehicle on the TRIA question: `26-294` (*Owens*) suffers from threshold pre-judgment attachment barriers under § 201(a)'s `"has obtained a judgment"` text, while `26-292` comprises post-judgment creditors whose claims failed below *solely* on the Second Circuit's blocking-date rule.
3. **Paired CVSG + immediate Call for Response (+2 pp):** While calling for a response after respondents let the October 5 deadline pass is standard housekeeping alongside a CVSG (the Court will not grant without offering FRBNY/respondents a chance to respond), entering the CFR the day after the CVSG ensures party opposition briefing is on hand well before the SG files, signaling organized preparation for a potential grant rather than a cursory look.

### Downward Factors (-7 percentage points)

1. **High likelihood the Solicitor General recommends denial to protect Executive foreign-affairs and central-bank equities (-4 pp):** In the district court and the Second Circuit, the United States opposed turning over DAB's $3.5 billion reserves to private TRIA creditors, emphasizing that the United States does not recognize the Taliban as the government of Afghanistan and views DAB's reserves as sovereign central-bank property of the Afghan State/people. Across administrations, the Executive Branch (State, Treasury/OFAC, and DOJ) strongly guards presidential leverage over IEEPA-blocked foreign central-bank reserves, foreign-state central-bank immunity (28 U.S.C. § 1611(b)(1)), and the President's exclusive recognition power (*Zivotofsky v. Kerry*). Even allowing ~22% probability that Solicitor General Sauer breaks with prior Executive opposition (or urges review to clarify TRIA/recognition doctrine), the modal (~78%) CVSG recommendation is **deny**—and historically the Court follows an SG denial recommendation roughly three-quarters to four-fifths of the time, even in salient cases.
2. **Distinguishability of the claimed circuit split and alternative grounds supporting affirmance (-2 pp):** Reading *Stansell III* (45 F.4th at 1349–50) and *Bennett* (825 F.3d at 955–61) directly shows that the alleged circuit split is not factually square. In *Stansell III*, the garnishees were *already* OFAC-designated FARC frontmen when blocked and argued they had *severed* ties before the garnishment writs issued—the exact converse of *Havlish*, where a recognized sovereign's central bank was blocked as Kabul fell. Moreover, SDNY ruled against petitioners on the independent constitutional ground that deeming DAB a Taliban instrumentality infringes the President's exclusive recognition power (a ground Judge Menashi endorsed in his separate en banc dissent), and Judge Nardini flagged unresolved factual questions whether DAB actually functions as a Taliban instrumentality. Those alternative obstacles create substantial risk that reversing on TRIA timing would not alter the bottom-line denial of turnover.
3. **Justice Kagan's statutory recusal (-1 pp):** Because Justice Kagan is recused under 28 U.S.C. § 455(b)(3), granting `26-292` requires **4 of 8** participating Justices (50% rather than 44.4%) and exposes the Court to a potential 4–4 split across overlapping statutory, central-bank-immunity, and *Zivotofsky* issues (whereas the unrecused companion `26-294` is a flawed pre-judgment vehicle).

Combining the 35.0% `high`-band anchor with +10 pp for textualist appeal, en banc dissents, stakes, and lead-vehicle posture, and -7 pp for expected SG opposition, split distinguishability / alternative grounds, and an 8-Justice bench yields **`P(grant) = 0.38`** (`granted = 0`, `predicted_disposition = "denied"`).
