# Predicted Court Trajectory and Disposition

The Court will **deny** the petition for a writ of certiorari after receiving the requested response and the invited brief of the Solicitor General (`predicted_disposition: denied`).

## Procedural Path Ahead

- **Further distributions (`relist-increment: 0.99`):** The docket currently records **two** distributions (distributed October 21, 2026 for the November 6 Conference, and relisted November 9, 2026 for the November 13 Conference), followed by the November 16, 2026 CVSG order and the November 17, 2026 call for a response (due December 17, 2026). Barring a unilateral Rule 46 dismissal, the petition is virtually certain to be distributed again once both the respondent/garnishee brief in opposition (likely from Garnishee-Interested-Party Federal Reserve Bank of New York, after an extension into early 2027) and the Solicitor General's invited brief arrive. Expect the Solicitor General to file in late April or May 2027, followed by **one to two post-CVSG distributions** in late May/June 2027 (or, if the SG files after the May cutoff, at the OT2027 Long Conference and one October relist) before final disposition.
- **Solicitor General invitation (`cvsg-increment: 0.01`):** The Court already invited the Solicitor General to express the views of the United States on November 16, 2026, alongside companions *Ashton v. The Taliban* (No. 26-293) and *Owens v. The Taliban* (No. 26-294). No further CVSG order will issue. The Solicitor General is expected to recommend **denial**, emphasizing that: (1) Executive Branch foreign-policy and sanctions equities counsel against private TRIA execution on $3.5 billion in blocked central-bank reserves of Da Afghanistan Bank (DAB); (2) the Eleventh Circuit's decision in *Stansell III* and the Ninth Circuit's decision in *Bennett* did not confront the capture of a recognized foreign state's central bank by an unrecognized terrorist group; and (3) alternative constitutional and statutory obstacles—specifically the President's exclusive recognition power under *Zivotofsky* and central-bank immunity under 28 U.S.C. § 1611(b)(1)—would complicate or defeat petitioners' recovery even if the Second Circuit's "at-the-time-of-blocking" gloss on TRIA § 201(a) were rejected.
- **Justice Kagan's non-participation:** Justice Kagan will remain recused under 28 U.S.C. § 455(b)(3) (prior government employment) at disposition, leaving an eight-Justice Court in which four of eight participating Justices would be required to grant review.

## Conditional Grant Route and Question Presented

- **Question presented if granted:** If the Court grants certiorari over (or with) the Solicitor General's views, it will take `26-292`'s single statutory question presented as written—whether a plaintiff's right to execute on blocked assets under TRIA § 201(a) depends on whether the assets' owner was a terrorist party or its agency or instrumentality at the time the assets were blocked—likely consolidating `26-292` with companion `26-293` (*Ashton*) or adding the *Zivotofsky* recognition-clause question presented in `26-293` so the Court can reach a dispositive merits judgment, while holding `26-294` (*Owens*, which carries threshold pre-judgment defects). Conditional on a plenary grant, a textualist majority would likely reject the Second Circuit's uncodified time-of-blocking requirement as inconsistent with TRIA § 201(a)'s present-tense text, while remanding (or dividing closely on) whether treating DAB as a Taliban instrumentality either fails on the record or intrudes on the President's exclusive recognition power.
- **Summary disposition conditional on grant (`summary-disposition-route: 0.12`):** If the Court grants, plenary briefing and oral argument are far likelier than summary disposition (~88% vs. ~12%). Summary reversal is practically foreclosed given the $3.5 billion stakes, foreign-affairs sensitivity, and divided appellate opinions. The primary path to a summary cert-order disposition is a **GVR** (including a *Munsingwear* vacatur) if the Executive Branch modifies the blocking or licensing status of the remaining $3.5 billion DAB reserves while the CVSG is pending, or a hold-and-GVR if the Court were instead to grant only `26-294` to avoid an eight-Justice bench.

## Separate Writings at Disposition

- **Dissent or statement respecting denial (`dissent-from-denial: 0.10`):** Most likely the denial order issues without separate opinion, with Justice Kagan noted as taking no part. However, given the forceful four-judge Second Circuit en banc dissent (Judge Sullivan, joined by Chief Judge Livingston and Judges Bianco and Park) criticizing the panel's reading of TRIA § 201(a), there is roughly a 1-in-10 chance conditional on denial that **Justice Gorsuch**, **Justice Thomas**, or **Justice Alito** authors a brief dissent from or statement respecting the denial of certiorari questioning the Second Circuit's atextual timing rule while noting the case's vehicle or foreign-relations complications.
