# Predicted Court Reasoning and Procedural Trajectory: *Fiona Havlish, et al. v. The Taliban, et al.* (No. 26-292)

## Procedural Trajectory from the First Relist

The docket currently records **two conference distributions** (`distribution_count = 2`): the petition was first distributed on October 21, 2026 for the November 6, 2026 Conference, and following the October 30, 2026 filing of petitioners' motion for leave to file a supplemental brief (and parallel supplemental filings in *vide* companion Nos. 26-293 and 26-294), the Court relisted all three petitions on November 9, 2026 for the November 13, 2026 Conference.

1. **Further Relists (`relist-increment`: 0.30)**:
   The most likely outcome (~70%) is that the Court disposes of the petition at the November 13, 2026 Conference—granting the motion for leave to file the supplemental brief and denying the petition for a writ of certiorari on the November 16, 2026 order list without any further distribution. If the petition is distributed again past the November 13 Conference (~30%), expect either one to two additional relists while a Justice circulates a separate statement or dissent from the denial of certiorari, or one future distribution cycle following a Call for the Views of the Solicitor General (CVSG).

2. **Call for the Views of the Solicitor General (`cvsg-increment`: 0.16)**:
   No CVSG appears on the docket. The Court is unlikely to invite the Solicitor General's views (~84% no CVSG), because the United States already participated extensively as *amicus curiae* in both the Southern District of New York and the Second Circuit opposing turnover of Da Afghanistan Bank's (DAB's) $3.5 billion in blocked reserves, and its foreign-policy and statutory positions are fully developed in the record below. If the Court does issue a CVSG (~16%), it would do so on the November 16 or November 23, 2026 order list, and the Solicitor General would later file an *amicus* brief recommending that certiorari be denied.

3. **Disposition and Question Presented (`disposition`: 0.13; `summary-disposition-route`: 0.08)**:
   The Court will most likely **deny** the petition for a writ of certiorari (alongside *vide* companion petitions Nos. 26-293 and 26-294).
   - **If granted**, the Court would grant plenary review (`summary-disposition-route`: 0.08) rather than issuing a summary reversal or GVR, as no intervening Supreme Court precedent bears on the Second Circuit's holding and the $3.5 billion foreign-sovereign-assets dispute is unsuitable for summary disposition on default without an appearing respondent.
   - **If granted**, the Court would take the single Question Presented in No. 26-292 as written—whether, under § 201(a) of the Terrorism Risk Insurance Act of 2002 (TRIA), 28 U.S.C. § 1610 note, a judgment creditor's right to execute on blocked assets depends on whether the assets' owner was a terrorist party or its agency or instrumentality at the time the assets were blocked—likely consolidating No. 26-292 with *Ashton* (No. 26-293) and potentially pairing or holding *Owens* (No. 26-294) on the Foreign Sovereign Immunities Act (FSIA) threshold questions, while appointing an *amicus curiae* (and hearing from the Solicitor General) to defend the Second Circuit's judgment.
   - **If granted on the merits**, the Court would likely confront not only the textual flaw in the Second Circuit's time-of-blocking rule (which has no basis in TRIA § 201(a)'s present-tense phrase "the blocked assets of that terrorist party (including the blocked assets of any agency or instrumentality of that terrorist party)"), but also the formidable alternative grounds raised by the District Court and the United States below: namely, that DAB's central-bank reserves remain the sovereign property of the recognized State of Afghanistan rather than assets "of" the Taliban, and that treating Afghanistan's central bank as an agency or instrumentality of the unrecognized Taliban regime would conflict with the President's exclusive recognition authority under *Zivotofsky v. Kerry*, 576 U.S. 1 (2015).

4. **Separate Writings on Denial (`dissent-from-denial`: 0.22)**:
   Should the Court deny certiorari, the baseline outcome (~78%) is an unadorned denial order. However, given the four-judge dissent from the denial of rehearing en banc below by Judges Sullivan and Menashi (joined by Judges Nardini and Park), there is a meaningful possibility (~22%) that one or more Justices—most likely Justice Gorsuch, Justice Thomas, or Justice Alito—will author or join a dissent from, or statement respecting, the denial of certiorari criticizing the Second Circuit's extratextual "time of blocking" limitation on TRIA § 201(a).
