## Legal question and vehicle

The provisioned `questions-presented.txt` and petition, printed pp. 2–6, 13–20 and 28–31, frame the issue as whether TRIA execution requires agency-or-instrumentality status **when assets were blocked**, rather than when execution is sought. The petition reports $3.5 billion in disputed assets, a panel dissent by Judge Sullivan, and five judges dissenting from denial of en banc rehearing. Those features support both the upward grant adjustment and the separate 0.83 stakes score; the stakes score is not a grant probability.

The retrieved August 26, 2025 Second Circuit opinion matches the provisioned judgment date and appellate docket 23-258. Its printed pp. 48–55 confirm that the panel distinguished TRIA from the FSIA, treated prior timing language as noncontrolling, selected the blocking date, and affirmed because DAB did not satisfy the instrumentality test on that date. This is a dispositive legal ground, not merely an advisory passage. The majority also held TRIA could overcome both jurisdictional and execution immunity. Accordingly, I do not treat the district court's immunity rationale as an unreviewed, independently sufficient holding of the appellate court for these judgment creditors. Other recognition, control, and execution-law questions may nevertheless remain if petitioners win on timing.

The petition's split argument has real substance but deserves a discount. In the retrieved Eleventh Circuit opinion, *Keith Stansell v. UBS Financial Services, Inc.*, August 23, 2022, No. 20-11736, printed pp. 14–17, the court rejected a requirement that the third party remain an instrumentality when execution is sought, recognizing past involvement and questioning the Second Circuit's time-of-filing language. That is meaningful temporal disagreement, but former affiliation in a FARC garnishment case is not precisely later acquisition of control over an already-blocked central bank. In *Bennett v. Islamic Republic of Iran*, 825 F.3d 949, 963–64 (9th Cir. 2016), the retrieved ownership section asks whether the blocked assets belong to Bank Melli under state property law. It does not expressly decide when an entity must become an instrumentality. I therefore do not adopt the petition's claimed three-way split as three indistinguishable holdings. I rely on Bennett's TRIA ownership discussion, not its separate FSIA analysis that the petition identifies as abrogated on other grounds.

The official `Vide` companions in `record/related/related.json` are Nos. 26-293 and 26-294. Their staged snapshots concern the same August 26 judgment and show initial distribution for November 6, without a grant or CVSG. They confirm coordinated litigation, not an independent lead case already warranting a hold. The absence of a second distribution in these companion snapshots is not proof of different outcomes. `same-question.json` supplies no matches above its threshold; that is not proof that no related legal controversy exists, and it supplies no hold rationale.

## Other probabilities and limitations

A CVSG is materially plausible at **30%** because sanctions, Afghan sovereign property, and executive recognition interests make the government's views valuable. No invited brief or current Supreme Court position of the United States is in the record. Most of my remaining probability still favors resolving without a CVSG. The **47%** additional-distribution probability incorporates both a possible government consultation and further consideration of the divided decision; the terminal relist table alone cannot estimate it.

Conditional on a grant, I put only **10%** on a cert-order disposition: resolving the contested statutory rule ordinarily calls for merits briefing, and I identified no controlling intervening decision requiring a GVR. Conditional on denial, I assign **22%** to some statement or dissent, reflecting the forceful lower-court disagreement but still expecting an unsigned denial. Per-Justice order-writer probabilities are unconditional marginal probabilities across all dispositions, not a distribution over cert votes and not probabilities conditional on denial. Their aggregate permits overlap and is consistent with the conditional existence claim.

`documents.json` marks the petition text nonempty but truncated around its appended materials; the main petition through its conclusion is available. No BIO, cert reply, supplemental-brief text, or amicus text was staged. In particular the October 30 motion to file a supplemental brief supplies no basis to guess what it says.
