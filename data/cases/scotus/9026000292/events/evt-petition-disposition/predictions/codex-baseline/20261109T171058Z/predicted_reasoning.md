# Forecast of the Court's action

The most likely final disposition is **denial of certiorari**, leaving the Second Circuit's judgment in place without a Supreme Court merits ruling. The denial would not announce approval of the lower court's statutory interpretation.

The baseline already contains **two distributions and one relist**: conferences of November 6 and November 13, 2026. I narrowly favor **no further distribution**, with a **47%** chance of at least one more. If there is another, one or two additional conference listings are more likely than a prolonged series, unless the Court first solicits the government's views. These increments begin after the November 9 entry; they do not count that existing relist again.

I forecast **no CVSG** as the more likely route, but assign a substantial **30%** probability to one. If called, it is most likely at one of the next several conferences, during late 2026 or early 2027, followed by a later redistribution after the government's submission. The consultation would address the federal interests in sanctions, central-bank reserves, and the statutory execution framework; I do not predict the government's recommendation from this record.

**If review is granted**, the Court is most likely to take the petition's single TRIA question essentially as written: whether a judgment creditor's right to execute on blocked assets depends on the asset owner's terrorist-party or instrumentality status at the time of blocking. Review need not encompass the companion petitioners' distinct prejudgment-attachment and state-recognition questions. The likely merits focus would be the timing rule in TRIA's text, rather than a direct order immediately distributing all disputed funds.

Conditional on any grant, **90%** of the probability favors a route requiring subsequent merits consideration rather than disposition in the cert order. The remaining **10%** covers a summary reversal or GVR. No identified intervening controlling decision makes a routine GVR the leading affirmative route, and the disputed temporal rule and foreign-sovereign context weigh against summary merits resolution.

Conditional on denial, I expect **no separate writing** with 78% probability. A dissent or statement has **22%** probability. If one appears, Thomas or Alito is the most plausible author, with Gorsuch another possibility; that identity forecast is lower confidence than the aggregate existence forecast. No public cert vote count or majority-opinion author is predicted.
