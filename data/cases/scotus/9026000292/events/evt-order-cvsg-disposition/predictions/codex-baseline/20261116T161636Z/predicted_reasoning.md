# Forecast of the Court's actions

The Court will most likely deny this petition after receiving the Solicitor General's invited views. The probability of any grant is 46%, making this a close call rather than a routine denial. The invitation signals that the government interests require attention, not that review has already attracted four votes.

The baseline contains **two distributions**, for the November 6 and November 13, 2026 conferences. The Court will distribute the petition at least once more after the SG files: probability **98%**. The central procedural path is one post-SG distribution and potentially one additional relist before disposition, rather than a long sequence of conferences while awaiting the government's brief. The petition will ordinarily wait during preparation of that brief. My central timing window for a petition decision is spring or early summer 2027, with later action if the invitation process runs long.

The Court **already called for the SG's views on November 16, 2026**. No new invitation is forecast. The numerical CVSG-increment entry is zero because the claim's no-prior-CVSG condition is no longer satisfied; it is vacuous for this cell.

If review is granted, the Court will most likely take the petition's single statutory timing question substantially as framed: whether TRIA requires terrorist-party or instrumentality status at the time the assets were blocked. It need not decide every issue concerning recognition of the Taliban, Afghanistan's continued statehood, or the competing plaintiffs' prejudgment remedies to address that question. If the Court ultimately rejects the blocking-date rule, the likely course would be a remand for the remaining execution questions, not an immediate Supreme Court award of the reserves to petitioners. This is conditional merits context, not a prediction that the petition has been granted.

Conditional on a grant, **plenary proceedings are more likely than a disposition in the grant order**: 84% versus **16%**. A coordinated grant in a companion followed by a GVR here is a plausible minority route. The present record identifies no intervening controlling Supreme Court ruling that makes an immediate GVR the natural default. Review of the statutory timing question would instead normally benefit from merits briefing about sanctions, instrumentality status, and the distinction between a foreign central bank and a terrorist organization.

If review is denied, the most likely order contains no explanation and no separate writing. The probability of some noted dissent or statement respecting denial, conditional on denial, is **22%**. Thomas or Alito is the likeliest author if one appears, with Gorsuch a secondary possibility; none is individually likely to write. Kagan will continue not to participate, consistent with the expressly recorded recusal. The numerical `order-writers` entries give each sitting Justice's unconditional writing probability, not a forecast of a majority-opinion author.
