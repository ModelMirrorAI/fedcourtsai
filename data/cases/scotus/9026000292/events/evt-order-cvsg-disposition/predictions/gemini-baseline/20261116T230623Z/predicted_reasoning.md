No further CVSG order will issue because the Court has already called for the Solicitor General's views. Once the Solicitor General files the invited brief—likely in late spring or late summer 2027—the petition will be distributed at least once more for a post-CVSG conference, with one to two additional distributions expected before final disposition unless the case is dismissed beforehand.

The Solicitor General will most likely recommend that certiorari be denied, urging that despite textual doubts about the Second Circuit panel's *sua sponte* "blocking-date" reading of Section 201(a) of the Terrorism Risk Insurance Act (TRIA), review is unwarranted because no square circuit split exists over sovereign central-bank reserves frozen prior to an unrecognized terrorist group's seizure of power, alternative sovereign-ownership and central-bank immunity defenses independently bar turnover of the $3.5 billion Da Afghanistan Bank account at the Federal Reserve Bank of New York, foreign-relations and central-bank custody equities counsel restraint, and Justice Kagan's recusal leaves an eight-Justice Court. Following that recommendation, the Court will most likely deny the petition without opinion, noting that Justice Kagan took no part.

If the Court instead grants review, plenary briefing and oral argument are substantially likelier than a summary disposition in the certiorari order itself. The Court would take the petition's single question presented as written—whether a plaintiff's right under TRIA § 201(a) to execute on blocked assets depends on whether the assets' owner was a terrorist party or its agency or instrumentality at the time the assets were initially blocked—likely consolidated with VIDED companion No. 26-293. Conditioned on a plenary grant, the Court would likely vacate and remand on textualist grounds, rejecting a categorical initial-blocking-date freeze under TRIA § 201(a) while leaving alternative sovereign-ownership and state-law execution defenses for remand. A grant, vacate, and remand (GVR) in the certiorari order is a secondary (~15% conditional) route—plausible mainly if the Solicitor General urges vacatur because the blocking-date rule was adopted *sua sponte* without briefing below, or if the Court grants only No. 26-294 (*Owens*, where Justice Kagan is not recused) on threshold Foreign Sovereign Immunities Act issues and later remands Nos. 26-292 and 26-293.

Conditional on a denial of certiorari, an unadorned denial order is the modal result, with a meaningful (~16%) chance that one or more conservative textualist Justices—most plausibly Justice Gorsuch, Justice Thomas, or Justice Alito—author a dissent from or statement respecting the denial of certiorari echoing the five-judge Second Circuit en banc dissent's textual critique of the blocking-date rule.
