The Supreme Court will most likely turn away this appeal, though there is about a two-in-five chance (41%) that it agrees to hear the case or sends it back to the lower court. Families of September 11 victims seek $3.5 billion in frozen Afghan central-bank reserves held in New York to satisfy terrorism judgments against the Taliban, and the Court has asked the Solicitor General—the federal government's top appellate lawyer—for the government's views. Although five federal appeals judges sharply criticized the lower court's statutory reasoning, the federal government will likely urge the eight participating Justices (Justice Kagan is recused) to decline review to protect foreign-policy interests and foreign central-bank reserves.
