The Supreme Court will most likely turn down this appeal—meaning it denies certiorari, a formal request to hear the case—though there is still nearly a two-in-five chance (39%) it agrees to review or send back the ruling. Families of September 11 victims seek $3.5 billion in frozen Afghan central-bank funds, and five appellate judges sharply criticized the lower court's reasoning, but the Court just asked the Solicitor General, the federal government's top courtroom lawyer, for the government's views, which will likely urge leaving the funds untouched for foreign-policy and central-bank protection reasons.
