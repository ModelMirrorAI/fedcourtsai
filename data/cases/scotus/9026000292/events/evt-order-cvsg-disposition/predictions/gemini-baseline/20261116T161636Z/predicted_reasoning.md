## Disposition Forecast

The most likely ultimate disposition after the Solicitor General files the United States' brief is **denial of certiorari** (`predicted_disposition: "denied"`), with a substantial minority probability of a grant (`probability: 0.39` across plenary grant and GVR).

Specifically, the Solicitor General is expected to recommend **denial of certiorari** across all three related petitions. Even if the United States declines to defend the full sweep of the Second Circuit panel majority's sua sponte "blocking-date" rationale in the abstract, the Executive Branch has strong institutional, diplomatic, and financial-system incentives—reflected in Executive Order 14064 and its annual continuations—to oppose private execution against the $3.5 billion in frozen sovereign reserves of Da Afghanistan Bank (DAB) held at the Federal Reserve Bank of New York. The Solicitor General is likely to argue that:
1. No square, outcome-determinative circuit conflict exists because neither *Bennett v. Islamic Republic of Iran* (9th Cir.) nor *Stansell v. FARC* (11th Cir.) confronted an unrecognized terrorist group's physical seizure of a foreign state's central bank *after* the United States had already frozen the bank's New York reserves;
2. Petitioners cannot ultimately prevail even without the panel's blocking-date rule because DAB's pre-August 15, 2021 sovereign central-bank reserves remain property of the recognized State of Afghanistan rather than assets "of" the unrecognized Taliban regime under TRIA § 201(a), 28 U.S.C. § 1611(b)(1), and the President's exclusive Article II recognition authority; and
3. An eight-Justice Court (with Justice Kagan recused in Nos. 26-292 and 26-293) and no appearing party respondent make this an unsuitable vehicle.

With four votes of eight participating Justices required for certiorari and the Executive Branch warning of foreign-relations and central-bank-custody consequences, the Court will most likely follow the Solicitor General's recommendation and deny review.

## Conditional Scope of Review and Summary-Disposition Route (If Granted)

- **Question presented if granted:** If the Court grants review in No. 26-292, it will take the petition's single statutory question presented as framed (potentially consolidated with `Vide` companion *Ashton*, No. 26-293, and either paired with or separated from *Owens*, No. 26-294): whether under TRIA § 201(a), a judgment creditor's right to execute on blocked assets depends on whether the asset owner was a terrorist party or its agency or instrumentality at the time the assets were initially blocked rather than when attachment or turnover is sought. Conditionally, if the Court reaches the merits, a textualist majority would likely reject the Second Circuit's categorical blocking-date rule as untethered from TRIA's present-tense text while vacating and remanding for the Second Circuit to address the bypassed ownership, central-bank immunity, and Article II recognition defenses in the first instance.
- **Summary disposition vs. plenary review (`summary-disposition-route` = 0.13):** Conditional on a grant, plenary briefing and oral argument is far likelier (87%) than a summary disposition in the cert order itself (13%). No pending Supreme Court merits case presents TRIA § 201(a)'s temporal question (*Exxon Mobil Corp. v. Corporación CIMEX, S.A.* was already decided in June 2026 and already briefed via supplemental filings), and the Court would not summarily reverse a $3.5 billion Second Circuit judgment per curiam without full merits briefing. A GVR is plausible primarily if the Solicitor General confesses error on the Second Circuit's unbriefed blocking-date rule while asking the Court to vacate and remand for consideration of the alternative Article II and sovereign-ownership grounds the panel bypassed (`152 F.4th at 360 n.124`).

## Separate Writings on Denial

- **Dissent or statement respecting denial (`dissent-from-denial` = 0.14):** Conditional on a denial of certiorari, expect no separate writing as the modal outcome (86%), because post-CVSG denials resting on alternative sovereign-ownership and vehicle obstacles generally issue without comment. There is, however, an elevated ~14% chance that one or more Justices—most plausibly Justice Thomas, Justice Alito, or Justice Gorsuch dissenting from denial, or Justice Kavanaugh or Justice Gorsuch filing a short statement respecting denial—writes separately to underscore that denial of certiorari rests on the unique foreign-central-bank posture of DAB's reserves and does not endorse the Second Circuit panel's extra-textual "blocking-date" limitation on TRIA § 201(a).
