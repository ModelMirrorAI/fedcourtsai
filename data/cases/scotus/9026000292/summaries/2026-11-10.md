## In brief

Families of terrorism victims and insurers ask the Court to let them seize blocked Taliban-linked central bank funds to satisfy multibillion-dollar judgments.

## What happened

Petitioners are family members of people killed in the September 11, 2001 attacks, other terrorism victims, and insurers who paid 9/11-related claims. They hold large court judgments against the Taliban. They sought to collect by reaching roughly $3.5 billion in blocked funds held at the Federal Reserve Bank of New York in the name of Afghanistan's central bank, which the Taliban now controls. The district court denied their turnover requests, and the Second Circuit affirmed, ruling that the bank's assets could be reached only if the bank already counted as a Taliban "agency or instrumentality" at the moment the funds were originally blocked by the government, not later when the Taliban took control. Rehearing en banc was denied over dissents.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked to decide when a bank or other entity must have been under a terrorist group's control for victims to collect a judgment against that group from the entity's frozen assets: at the moment the government first froze the funds, or at the later point when victims try to collect, even if control changed hands in between.

## Where it stands

The petition was filed and distributed for a November 6, 2026 conference; the docket does not record any action from that conference. Petitioners then filed a motion for leave to submit a supplemental brief, and the case was distributed again for a November 13, 2026 conference.

## What each outcome would mean

Havlish and the other petitioners are asking the Court to overturn the Second Circuit's decision, which left the frozen funds out of their reach. If the Court grants review, it would hear full arguments and decide the legal question itself, without yet deciding who wins. If it denies review, the Second Circuit's ruling stands, leaving the funds immune from execution under the lower court's timing rule, and petitioners could not collect from this money through this case.
