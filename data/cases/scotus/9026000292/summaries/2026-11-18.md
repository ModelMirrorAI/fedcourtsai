## In brief

Families of 9/11 victims and insurers ask the Court to let them seize blocked Afghan central bank funds to satisfy judgments against the Taliban.

## What happened

Petitioners, led by Fiona Havlish, hold multibillion-dollar court judgments against the Taliban for terrorist attacks including September 11, 2001. They sought to collect by seizing roughly $3.5 billion in blocked assets of Afghanistan's central bank, Da Afghanistan Bank, held at the Federal Reserve Bank of New York. The Second Circuit ruled that these assets can only be seized if the bank was already controlled by the Taliban at the moment the funds were first blocked by the U.S. government, back in 2021. Because the panel found the Taliban had not yet taken control of the bank on that exact date, it denied petitioners' request, over dissents from several judges. The full appeals court declined to rehear the case.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked to decide whether a law allowing terrorism victims to collect judgments by seizing a terrorist group's blocked assets requires that the group already controlled those assets at the moment the government first froze them, or whether it is enough that the group controls the assets now, when victims try to collect.

## Where it stands

The petition was distributed for conference twice, and the Court granted a motion to file a supplemental brief. The Court has now invited the Solicitor General to file a brief giving the federal government's views on whether the case should be heard, with a response due in mid-December 2026.

## What each outcome would mean

Havlish and the other petitioners are asking the Court to take up their case and reverse the Second Circuit's ruling. If the Court eventually denies review, the Second Circuit's decision stands, leaving the Taliban's blocked funds beyond the petitioners' reach under the lower court's rule. If the Court grants review and later rules for petitioners, they could proceed to seize the funds toward their judgments. If it rules for the Taliban and the Federal Reserve Bank of New York, the funds would remain protected. Right now, the case is still awaiting the government's views before the Court decides whether to hear it at all.
