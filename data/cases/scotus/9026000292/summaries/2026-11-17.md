## In brief

9/11 victims and insurers ask the Court to let them seize blocked Afghan central bank funds to satisfy judgments against the Taliban.

## What happened

Families of September 11 victims and insurance companies that paid 9/11-related claims hold multibillion-dollar court judgments against the Taliban. They sought to collect by reaching roughly $3.5 billion in blocked assets of Afghanistan's central bank, Da Afghanistan Bank, held at the Federal Reserve Bank of New York. A federal district court denied their request, and the Second Circuit Court of Appeals affirmed, ruling that the bank counted as a Taliban-controlled entity only if it already had that status at the moment the funds were first frozen, not later when the Taliban actually took control of the bank. Rehearing en banc was denied over several dissents.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked to decide when a bank or other entity must be shown to be controlled by a group like the Taliban for its frozen funds to be available to pay judgments against that group: at the moment the funds were originally frozen by the government, or at the later point when victims try to collect.

## Where it stands

The petition has been distributed for conference more than once, and the Court has granted a motion by the petitioners to file a supplemental brief and has invited the Solicitor General to file a brief expressing the federal government's views on whether the case should be heard.

## What each outcome would mean

The 9/11 victims and insurers, as petitioners, are asking the Court to overturn the Second Circuit's ruling. If the Court grants review and ultimately rules for them, they could proceed to seize the frozen bank funds to satisfy their judgments against the Taliban. If the Court denies the petition, or rules for the Taliban and the Federal Reserve Bank of New York, the Second Circuit's decision blocking access to those funds stands. Because the Solicitor General has now been invited to weigh in, any decision on whether to hear the case will likely wait until after that brief is filed.
