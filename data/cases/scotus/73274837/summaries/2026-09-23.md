## What happened

Washington State bans the manufacture, distribution, and sale of gun magazines that hold more than ten rounds. Gator's Custom Guns and its owner, Walter Wentz, kept selling these magazines anyway, and the state sued them for violating both the magazine law and its consumer protection law. Gator's argued the magazine ban violates the right to bear arms. A trial court agreed and struck down the law. Washington's Supreme Court reversed, ruling that Gator's had not shown the magazines counted as constitutionally protected "arms" or that people commonly use them in self-defense. Two justices dissented. Gator's now asks the Supreme Court to review that ruling; Washington answers that Gator's failed to present supporting evidence and that every appeals court to consider similar laws has upheld them.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked whether magazines that hold more than ten rounds count as "arms" that get automatic constitutional protection, or whether they are simply accessories that a state can restrict without triggering that protection in the first place. The case would decide how far the right to keep and bear arms extends to component parts and accessories, not just to firearms themselves.

## Where it stands

The petition has been distributed for conference many times since September 2025 without a decision, and the parties have filed supplemental briefs pointing to related cases as they developed. As of this snapshot, the petition remains under consideration and has not been granted or denied.
