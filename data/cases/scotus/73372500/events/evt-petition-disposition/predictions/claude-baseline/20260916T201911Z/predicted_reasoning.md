# Forecast: what the Court will do with No. 25-1299

**Disposition.** The petition will be denied at or shortly after the Conference of September 28, 2026, by an unsigned order on the October 5, 2026 order list. P(any grant) = 0.006.

**Relists.** The docket shows exactly one distribution (June 24, 2026, for the September 28 long conference); the petition has never been relisted. I expect no further distribution: no call for a response, no pending merits case that could make this a hold, and nothing in the September 11 supplemental brief that gives the Court a reason to carry it over. P(at least one more distribution) = 0.09, most of which is a routine reschedule off the long conference rather than a substantive relist.

**Call for a response.** Respondents waived on June 18 and the Court did not call for a response before distribution. I do not expect one to be requested after the conference (roughly 3%); a request would be the only realistic path to any grant and would show up as a further distribution.

**CVSG.** No federal party, statute, or program is implicated; this is a county zoning dispute. P(CVSG called for) = 0.003.

**Which question, if taken.** The Court would not take the question presented as written. If it did anything, it would be the petition's alternative request: a Philadelphia Newspapers v. Jerome-style vacatur and remand asking the South Carolina Supreme Court to say whether its unexplained refusal to entertain the original-jurisdiction petition rested on state grounds. Plenary review of "whether, and under what standard" unauthorized permitting violates substantive due process would require a vehicle in which some court actually adjudicated a federal claim by a property owner; this is not one. Conditional on any grant, P(disposed of in the cert order itself, i.e. a vacate-and-remand rather than plenary review) = 0.7.

**Summary disposition.** No intervening decision of this Court bears on the question, so a conventional GVR is off the table; the only summary route is the clarification vacatur above, and it is rare.

**Separate writings on denial.** None expected. P(a dissent from or statement respecting denial) = 0.02. The property-rights Justices who occasionally write on land-use denials (Thomas, Gorsuch) write when a clean vehicle exposes a doctrinal problem; a neighbor group's challenge to an unexplained state-court refusal of an extraordinary writ is not that vehicle.

**Conditional merits.** If the Court ever reached the question presented in a proper vehicle, the likely holding would follow County of Sacramento v. Lewis and require conscience-shocking executive conduct, rejecting the Second Circuit's ultra vires rule; that has no bearing on this petition, which will not be that vehicle.
