## In brief

A community group asks the Court to review South Carolina's refusal to block county officials from permitting a solar-manufacturing project a zoning board had ruled unlawful.

## What happened

Citizens Alliance for Government Integrity (CAGI), a nonprofit representing York County, South Carolina residents, says county officials kept issuing permits for Silfab Solar's manufacturing project even after the county's own Board of Zoning Appeals ruled unanimously that the use was prohibited on that property. CAGI asked the South Carolina Supreme Court, using its original jurisdiction, to order officials to stop issuing permits. The court declined to take up the matter in a brief, unexplained order, without saying whether it rejected CAGI's constitutional argument or relied on other grounds. CAGI then asked the U.S. Supreme Court to review that order.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked whether, and under what conditions, local officials who approve a land use after a zoning appeals board has already ruled that use prohibited can be considered to have acted arbitrarily in a way that violates constitutional protections against irrational government action. CAGI says federal appeals courts answer this differently: some allow such claims to proceed, others demand more extreme misconduct, and one forecloses this type of claim entirely. Alternatively, CAGI asks the Court to send the case back for an explanation of the state court's reasoning.

## Where it stands

The petition was distributed for the Court's September 28, 2026 conference, and the docket shows no action recorded since then. The respondents waived their right to file a response, and CAGI later filed a supplemental brief.

## What each outcome would mean

CAGI, the petitioner, is asking the Court to take up the case. If the Court grants the petition, it would hear full arguments and decide the constitutional question itself, without yet deciding who wins. If it denies the petition, the South Carolina Supreme Court's order declining to intervene stands, leaving that outcome in place, though this would not mean the Court agrees the lower court was right. The Court could also grant, vacate, and remand, sending the case back to the state court to clarify its reasoning without resolving the dispute either way.
