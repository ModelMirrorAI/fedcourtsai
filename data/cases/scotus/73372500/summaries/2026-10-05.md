## In brief
A nonprofit group's bid to challenge South Carolina county officials' permitting of a solar-manufacturing project, after a zoning board ruled the use prohibited, was denied.

## What happened
Citizens Alliance for Government Integrity (CAGI), a nonprofit representing York County residents, says county officials kept issuing permits for a solar-panel manufacturing project even after the county's own zoning appeals board ruled the proposed use was not allowed on the site. CAGI asked the South Carolina Supreme Court directly for mandamus, prohibition, and injunctive relief to stop the permitting. That court issued a brief, unexplained order declining to take up the case in its original jurisdiction. CAGI then asked the U.S. Supreme Court to review that order, while related disputes over the project continue in other South Carolina courts.

## What the Court is being asked
The Court is asked whether, and under what circumstances, local officials who issue land-use permits after a zoning appeals board has ruled the use prohibited can be found to have acted in a constitutionally arbitrary way, violating guarantees of fair treatment by the government. CAGI says federal appeals courts answer this differently depending on the circuit, and alternatively asks the Court to send the case back for an explanation of the state court's reasoning.

## Where it stands
The county respondents waived their right to respond, and the petition was distributed for conference before the Court denied it on October 5, 2026.

## What each outcome would mean
CAGI, the nonprofit, asked the Court to take up its case. Because the petition was denied, the South Carolina Supreme Court's order declining to hear CAGI's request for mandamus, prohibition, and injunctive relief stands. That means CAGI's attempt to use that original state-court proceeding to stop the county's permitting is at an end, though the record describes other related proceedings continuing in South Carolina's lower courts involving the same project.
