## What happened

Citizens Alliance for Government Integrity (CAGI), a nonprofit representing York County, South Carolina residents, says county officials let a company convert a warehouse into a solar-panel manufacturing facility even after the county's own Board of Zoning Appeals ruled the use prohibited, with no rezoning or variance granted. CAGI asked the South Carolina Supreme Court, using its original jurisdiction, for mandamus, prohibition, and injunctive relief to stop the permitting. The county disputed CAGI's account of its authority and argued the zoning board's ruling was not final. The South Carolina Supreme Court declined to hear the matter in its original jurisdiction, issuing a brief order without explaining its reasoning.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked whether, and under what conditions, local officials who issue land-use permits after a local zoning appeals board has already ruled the proposed use forbidden can be found to have acted arbitrarily in a way the Constitution forbids. CAGI's petition says federal appeals courts disagree sharply: some treat permitting without legal authority as potentially unconstitutional on its own, others demand additional shocking misconduct, and still others bar this type of claim entirely. CAGI also argues that, at minimum, the state court's unexplained order should be set aside and sent back for clarification of its reasoning.

## Where it stands

The county respondents waived their right to respond. The case was distributed for the Court's September 28, 2026 conference, where the justices would decide whether to hear it; the petitioner later filed a supplemental brief in September 2026. The docket does not show any decision yet on whether the Court will take the case.
