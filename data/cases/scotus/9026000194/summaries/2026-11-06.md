## In brief

Mei Guo and HK USA ask the Court whether a bankruptcy trustee can use a federal statute to pursue a state-law claim against their shell company.

## What happened

Ho Wan Kwok filed for bankruptcy after a New York court found he beneficially owned a yacht, the Lady May, despite claiming to own almost nothing. The bankruptcy trustee, Luc Despins, argued that HK USA, which formally owned the yacht, was actually Kwok's alter ego, making its assets part of the bankruptcy estate. The bankruptcy court and district court agreed, and the Second Circuit affirmed, holding that a federal bankruptcy statute let the trustee bring this claim standing in the shoes of a hypothetical creditor. Guo and HK USA appealed to the Supreme Court.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked whether the law giving bankruptcy trustees the same rights a creditor with a court-ordered claim on the debtor's property would have also lets a trustee bring a legal claim, available under state law to any creditor generally, seeking to hold a company liable for its owner's debts. Petitioners say the law does not stretch that far; the trustee says it plainly does.

## Where it stands

The petition has been distributed for conference twice, most recently for December 4, 2026, after the respondent opposed review, an amicus brief was filed supporting the petitioners, and the petitioners filed a reply. The docket shows no decision yet on whether the Court will hear the case.

## What each outcome would mean

Mei Guo and HK USA, the petitioners, are asking the Court to reverse the Second Circuit's decision, which the trustee, Luc Despins, won below. If the Court grants review and later rules for the petitioners, the trustee would lose this basis for claiming HK USA's assets, including the yacht and escrowed funds, for Kwok's creditors, though the trustee's brief says other legal grounds might still support the same result. If the Court instead rules for the trustee, or denies review, the Second Circuit's decision stands, and the assets remain part of the bankruptcy estate.
