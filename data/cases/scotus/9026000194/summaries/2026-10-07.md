## In brief

Mei Guo and HK USA ask the Court to decide whether a bankruptcy trustee may pursue an alter ego claim that belongs to creditors generally.

## What happened

Mei Guo's father, Ho Wan Kwok, filed for bankruptcy. The Chapter 11 Trustee, Luc Despins, argued that HK USA, a company holding a yacht called the Lady May and $37 million in escrow, was really Kwok's alter ego, making those assets part of the bankruptcy estate. The bankruptcy court and district court agreed. The Second Circuit affirmed, holding that a bankruptcy law provision called the "strong-arm clause" lets a trustee assert this kind of claim because it is a general claim available to any of the debtor's creditors, not a personal claim limited to one creditor. Guo and HK USA, the petitioners, asked for rehearing, which was denied, and then petitioned the Supreme Court.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked whether a bankruptcy law giving trustees certain hypothetical creditor powers also lets a trustee take over and pursue a state-law claim that belongs broadly to all of a bankruptcy estate's creditors, rather than only claims that belong to the debtor or to specific creditors personally. Lower courts disagree: some say this provision only lets trustees defeat liens and similar property interests, while others say it also lets trustees pursue shared creditor claims like alter ego claims against outside companies.

## Where it stands

The petition was distributed for the Court's September 28, 2026 conference. The respondent waived his right to respond but was later asked by the Court to respond, with that deadline extended to October 21, 2026. The docket shows no action yet on whether the Court will hear the case.

## What each outcome would mean

Guo and HK USA, as petitioners, are asking the Court to take up the case. If the Court grants review, it will decide the legal question itself, without yet deciding who wins. If the Court denies review, the Second Circuit's decision stands, meaning the Trustee may continue treating HK USA's assets, including the yacht and escrowed funds, as part of Kwok's bankruptcy estate. A denial would not mean the Supreme Court agrees the Second Circuit was right—only that its ruling remains in effect.
