# Forecast: what the Court will do with No. 26-194, Guo v. Despins

**Bottom line.** The petition will be denied, most likely in December 2026 or January 2027, after one further distribution following the brief in opposition the Court has called for. P(any grant) = 0.12.

## Procedural path from here

- **Distributions.** The docket shows **one** distribution (for the September 28, 2026 conference), entered the day before the Court called for a response. The call for a response pulled the petition off that conference, so the Court must distribute it again once the response is in. The brief in opposition is due October 21, 2026 (extended once); expect a reply from petitioners around early November and a redistribution for a late-November or December conference. So at least one further distribution is near-certain (P ≈ 0.95), the residual being a settlement-driven dismissal or withdrawal in a Kwok-estate fight that has shown no sign of settling. Beyond that first redistribution, I expect at most one relist: a genuine look at the vehicle, then a denial. I do not expect the three-plus relists that precede a grant or a dissent from denial.
- **CVSG.** No call for the views of the Solicitor General (P ≈ 0.06). Neither the United States nor the U.S. Trustee is a party, the respondent is a private Chapter 11 trustee, and the statutory question is one the Court can assess from the petition, the opposition, and the existing circuit opinions. A CVSG in a private bankruptcy case is not unheard of, which is why the number is not lower, but the call for a response is the attention signal here, not a CVSG.
- **Timing.** Denial order on the Monday after the first post-response conference, or after one relist; most likely window December 8, 2026 through January 20, 2027.

## If the Court grants

- **Question taken.** The question presented as written: whether 11 U.S.C. § 544(a) authorizes a trustee to prosecute a state-law alter ego claim because it belongs to creditors generally. It is a single, clean statutory question and would not need reformulation, though the Court might tighten it to "a creditor-owned state-law claim against a non-debtor third party" so the holding is not confined to alter ego.
- **Route.** Plenary review, not a summary disposition (P(cert-order disposition | grant) ≈ 0.05). There is no intervening decision of this Court to GVR in light of, and the question is not one the Court would resolve per curiam against a published Second Circuit opinion that joins the Seventh Circuit.
- **Likely ground, conditionally.** If granted, the Court would most likely side with the Eighth and Ninth Circuits: § 544(a) confers the rights and powers of a hypothetical lien creditor for avoidance and priority, not ownership of creditors' affirmative causes of action against third parties, with Caplin and the deleted § 544(c) doing the work. That is the textualist reading the current Court favored in Purdue Pharma. But the grant question turns on vehicle, not on the merits.

## If the Court denies

- **Separate writings.** No dissent from denial and no statement respecting denial (P(some writing | denial) ≈ 0.05). This is a technical Bankruptcy Code standing question with unsympathetic facts; the Justices who write at the cert stage on circuit splits (Thomas, Gorsuch, Kavanaugh) have shown no interest in this particular one in the four decades it has existed, and the alternative § 541(a) ground gives any Justice tempted to note the split a reason to wait for a cleaner case.
