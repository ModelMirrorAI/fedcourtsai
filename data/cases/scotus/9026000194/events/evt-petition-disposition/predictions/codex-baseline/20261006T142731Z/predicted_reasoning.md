# Forecast of the Court's action

The Court will most likely **deny certiorari**, after the requested response and the ensuing briefing cycle. The probability of any grant is **24%**. On the ordinary no-CVSG path, I expect disposition in late 2026 or early 2027, rather than treating the already-past September 28 conference date as an unresolved promise of immediate action.

The record shows **one distribution**, entered August 19 for the September 28 conference, followed by a request for a response and an extension of its deadline to October 21. I expect **at least one further distribution** (94%), with one additional distribution the modal path. That next entry will most likely return the petition to a conference after briefing; it need not represent a substantive relist from an earlier conference. If the Court moves toward a grant, another conference after that is plausible. I do not predict a long series of substantive relists as the ordinary path.

I expect **no call for the Solicitor General's views** (9% probability of a new CVSG). If a CVSG does issue, I expect it after the requested response and renewed conference consideration, with the ultimate cert decision pushed beyond the ordinary late-2026/early-2027 window.

**If review is granted**, the Court will most likely take the petition's single statutory question substantially as framed: whether section 544(a) permits a trustee to assert creditor-owned, generalized alter ego claims against a nondebtor. The likely focus is the boundary between the trustee's hypothetical-creditor powers and ownership of claims, not a freestanding reassessment of whether the yacht or other assets were controlled by the debtor. The prospect of resolving that federal boundary, rather than correcting case-specific facts, is the strongest route to review.

Conditional on a grant, I predict **plenary briefing and argument**, with only a **6% chance of disposition in the cert order itself**. No intervening authority identified in the materials makes a routine GVR the leading route, and the opposing statutory readings are better suited to plenary review than summary reversal.

Conditional on denial, I expect **no public dissent or statement respecting denial**; the probability of at least one such writing is **4%**. A denial would leave the judgment below in place without resolving the competing statutory readings. I predict neither an attributed cert vote nor a majority merits opinion on that denial path.
