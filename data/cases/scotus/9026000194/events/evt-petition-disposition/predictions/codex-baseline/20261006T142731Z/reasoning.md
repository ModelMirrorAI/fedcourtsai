## Why this petition is above the anchor

The question presented is concrete: whether section 544(a) authorizes a trustee to prosecute a state-law alter ego claim merely because it belongs to creditors generally. The petition describes a conflict between Ozark Restaurant in the Eighth Circuit and Koch Refining in the Seventh, with additional support claimed from other circuits (petition pp. 11–15). More importantly, the attached appellate opinion independently supplies the challenged holding: it permits generalized claims available to a hypothetical lien creditor, endorses Koch Refining, and distinguishes personal creditor claims (Appendix A, 10a–15a). This is a federal statutory-authority issue rather than merely disagreement about the factual veil-piercing result.

The Court requested a response on August 20 after the trustee's August 17 waiver. That is affirmative attention beyond an ordinary unopposed petition. A creditors' committee from a separate bankruptcy also filed an amicus brief. The existence of that filing supports relevance beyond these particular litigants; without its text, it does not establish the committee's precise position or the soundness of the split argument. Together, the legal issue, appellate holding, response request, and outside participation justify a substantial increase from the approximately 5% class anchor.

## Why denial remains more likely

The asserted conflict is longstanding: the principal contrasting decisions cited by petitioners are from 1987. Its age supports entrenchment, but also means the mere existence of opposing formulations is not sufficient reason to predict a grant. Whether different state-law ownership rules and general-versus-personal claim distinctions explain part of the apparent split remains important. The petition advocates a clean, direct conflict; the adverse briefing needed to test that presentation is not yet available here. An attempted independent CourtListener lookup of Ozark Restaurant was throttled, so I do not claim independent verification of every circuit's position.

The vehicle has some complexity. The district court relied on section 541, whereas the Second Circuit relied on section 544 and left the alternative theory undecided (petition pp. 9–11; Appendix A, 10a–11a n.4). The distinct yacht-ownership counterclaim and the underlying state-law analysis also complicate the litigation. Petitioners persuasively explain why those matters do not eliminate the federal question (petition p. 23), but an unresolved alternative basis for recovery can reduce the practical payoff of Supreme Court review. I treat that as a modest reservation, not an established jurisdictional defect. A response request is not a grant signal strong enough, by itself, to overcome these uncertainties. My 24% probability is a judgmental update, not an estimated frequency for this precise combination of features.

## Other probabilities and stakes

- **Further distribution: 94%.** The snapshot's single August 19 distribution was for September 28. A response was then requested and its deadline extended to October 21. A later distribution after completion of that briefing is therefore highly likely, even if the petition is ultimately denied. The claim counts an additional distribution, including administrative redistribution; it is not a 94% prediction of a substantive relist after an actual conference discussion. I do not infer that the September 28 conference considered the petition or that silence afterward means a hold.
- **New CVSG: 9%.** The systemic bankruptcy question creates some prospect of seeking federal views, but the present private dispute and direct statutory conflict make proceeding on the parties' briefing the more likely route. No CVSG is already recorded. This is a subjective forward hazard, not the terminal CVSG table read backward.
- **Summary route given a grant: 6%.** A contested question of trustee authority and an alleged longstanding split favor plenary briefing and argument. The materials reviewed identify no new controlling decision that would make a routine GVR the natural disposition. This is conditional on a grant, not a 6% unconditional chance of summary relief.
- **Separate statement or dissent given denial: 4%.** I expect an unexplained denial if review is refused. The issue is important within bankruptcy practice, but the record supplies no particular reason to anticipate a public denial-stage writing. This is an aggregated existence prediction, not a Justice-specific vote forecast.

The **0.58 stakes score** reflects nationally consequential control of creditor claims, litigation settlements, and bankruptcy recovery, tempered by the technical scope of the question. It is independent of the 24% grant probability.
