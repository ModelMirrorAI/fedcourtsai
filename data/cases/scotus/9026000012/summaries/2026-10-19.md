## In brief

The Supreme Court agreed to decide what proof of discriminatory impact a parent group must show before courts examine whether a school district's admissions policy was intentionally discriminatory.

## What happened

The Association for Education Fairness (AFEF) sued Montgomery County's school board, claiming its magnet middle school admissions policies discriminated against Asian American students. AFEF first challenged a "Field Test" admissions process, then amended its suit to challenge a pandemic-era lottery system, the "Pandemic Plan," after the district switched methods. The district court dismissed the case, finding AFEF had not plausibly shown the Pandemic Plan disproportionately hurt Asian American students or was adopted with discriminatory intent. The Fourth Circuit affirmed, relying on circuit precedent that requires showing the targeted group's admission rate fell below its share of applicants. AFEF appealed to the Supreme Court.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked whether someone challenging a school admissions policy that does not mention race, but is claimed to be discriminatory in purpose, must first prove the policy produced a disparate outcome for the targeted racial group as a whole, measured against that group's share of applicants, before a court will even look at other evidence suggesting the policy was adopted for discriminatory reasons.

## Where it stands

The Court granted the petition on October 19, 2026, limited to this question. The case has not yet been scheduled for briefing or argument on the merits.

## What each outcome would mean

AFEF, as petitioner, asked the Court to take up this question after losing in the lower courts. Because the Court granted review, the case will now receive full briefing and argument before a decision. If the Court ultimately rules that proof of group-wide disparate impact is not required first, AFEF's case could proceed to further review of its discrimination claims. If the Court rules that such proof is required, the dismissal of AFEF's lawsuit would likely stand. The Court could also send the case back for further proceedings without resolving which side is right.
