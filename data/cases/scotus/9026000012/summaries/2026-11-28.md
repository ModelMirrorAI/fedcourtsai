## In brief

The Court agreed to decide what proof of unequal effect a group must show before courts will examine a school admissions policy's intent.

## What happened

The Association for Education Fairness sued Montgomery County's school board, claiming the county's admissions process for competitive magnet middle schools was changed to reduce Asian American enrollment. After the county replaced its prior process with a lottery-based system during the pandemic, the group amended its complaint to challenge the new lottery. A federal district court dismissed the case, finding the group had not plausibly shown either that the lottery disproportionately hurt Asian American students or that officials intended discrimination. The Fourth Circuit affirmed, relying on circuit precedent requiring proof that the group's admitted share fell below its share of applicants.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked whether someone challenging a school admissions policy that does not mention race, but is claimed to be discriminatory in purpose, must first prove the policy produced a measurable disadvantage for the targeted group as a whole before a court will even look at evidence that officials intended to discriminate.

## Where it stands

The Court granted the petition on October 19, 2026, limited to this question. Briefing is underway: petitioner's merits brief is due December 10, 2026, and respondents' brief is due January 19, 2027. A motion by outside groups to intervene and to participate in oral argument was submitted November 25, 2026 and has not yet been decided.

## What each outcome would mean

The Association for Education Fairness, which lost in the lower courts, is asking the Court to reverse the Fourth Circuit's ruling. If the Court sides with the Association, it would hold that courts may consider evidence of discriminatory intent even without proof the policy reduced the group's enrollment below its applicant share, and the case would likely return to lower courts to examine that evidence. If the Court sides with the school board, the lower courts' dismissal would stand, leaving the lawsuit over. A ruling that vacates the lower decision would send the case back without resolving which side is correct.
