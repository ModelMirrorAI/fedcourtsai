## In brief

A parents' group asks the Court to revive its lawsuit claiming Montgomery County's lottery-based school admissions system intentionally discriminates against Asian American students.

## What happened

The Association for Education Fairness (AFEF) sued Montgomery County Public Schools, claiming that its admissions process for competitive magnet middle schools discriminated against Asian American students. AFEF first challenged an earlier admissions system, then amended its complaint to challenge a replacement lottery system adopted during the COVID-19 pandemic. A federal district court dismissed the case, finding AFEF had not plausibly alleged that the lottery system either disproportionately hurt Asian American students or was adopted with discriminatory intent. The Fourth Circuit Court of Appeals affirmed, relying on circuit precedent about how to measure disproportionate impact. AFEF now asks the Supreme Court to review that ruling.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked whether someone claiming a school district adopted neutral-looking admissions rules for discriminatory reasons must first show the rules actually hurt the targeted racial group's numbers, measured in a specific way, before a court will even look at evidence of discriminatory intent. AFEF argues that courts of appeals disagree on this question, with some circuits requiring this kind of numerical showing first and others allowing evidence of intent to be considered regardless.

## Where it stands

The petition has been distributed for the Court's conference twice, most recently September 28, 2026, and again for October 9, 2026, after full briefing including an opposition brief, a reply, and several amicus briefs. The docket does not yet show any order granting or denying the petition.

## What each outcome would mean

AFEF, as petitioner, is asking the Court to take up the case. If the Court grants the petition, it would hear the case and decide the legal question, with no guarantee of which side would win. If it denies the petition, the Fourth Circuit's decision stands, leaving in place the dismissal of AFEF's lawsuit and the Fourth Circuit's approach to measuring disparate impact in this kind of case. Montgomery County's Board of Education, as respondent, argues denial is appropriate because, in its view, this case would not change outcome regardless of how the legal question is resolved.
