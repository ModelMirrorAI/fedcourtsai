## In brief

The Supreme Court agreed to decide how parents challenging a school district's admissions lottery must prove the district discriminated against Asian American students.

## What happened

A parents' group, the Association for Education Fairness, sued Montgomery County's school board over how it picks students for competitive magnet middle school programs. The group first challenged an earlier admissions process, then amended its lawsuit to challenge a replacement lottery system adopted during the COVID-19 pandemic, arguing both were designed to reduce Asian American enrollment. A federal district court dismissed the case, finding the group had not plausibly alleged either that the lottery disproportionately hurt Asian American students or that officials intended to discriminate. The Fourth Circuit Court of Appeals affirmed, relying on a rule that Asian American students must show their admission rate fell below their share of applicants.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked to decide whether someone challenging a school's race-neutral admissions rules as intentionally discriminatory must first prove the rules caused an overall numerical disadvantage to the affected racial group, before a court will even look at other evidence suggesting the rules were adopted for discriminatory reasons.

## Where it stands

The Court granted the petition on October 19, 2026, limited to that question. Briefing is underway: the petitioner's opening brief is due December 10, 2026, and the school board's brief is due January 19, 2027. The case has not yet been argued.

## What each outcome would mean

The Association for Education Fairness, which asked the Court to take the case and is seeking to overturn the lower court's ruling, argues that courts should weigh evidence of discriminatory intent even without proof of an overall numerical disadvantage. If the Court rules for the Association, the case would likely return to the lower courts to reconsider the group's claims under a different legal standard, without deciding who ultimately wins. If the Court rules for the school board, the lower court's dismissal of the lawsuit would stand, leaving the admissions lottery in place and the group's challenge rejected.
