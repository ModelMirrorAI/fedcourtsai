## In brief

The Supreme Court agreed to decide what parents challenging a school district's race-neutral admissions lottery must show to prove it was intentionally discriminatory.

## What happened

Association for Education Fairness, a group of Asian-American parents, sued Montgomery County's school board over admissions criteria for competitive magnet middle schools. After the district switched to a lottery system during the COVID-19 pandemic, the group amended its lawsuit to challenge that lottery as intentionally discriminatory against Asian-American students. A federal district court dismissed the case, finding the complaint did not plausibly show either that the lottery harmed Asian-American students as a group or that officials adopted it for discriminatory reasons. The Fourth Circuit Court of Appeals affirmed, relying on circuit precedent requiring proof that the group's admission rate fell below its share of applicants.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked whether someone challenging a school admissions policy that does not explicitly mention race, but is claimed to be discriminatory in purpose, must first prove the policy actually reduced the targeted group's overall share of admitted students before a court will even look at evidence that officials intended to discriminate.

## Where it stands

The Court granted the petition on October 19, 2026, limited to this question. The case has not yet been briefed or argued on the merits, and the petitioner has since asked for more time to file its opening brief.

## What each outcome would mean

Association for Education Fairness, which lost in the lower courts, is asking the Court to reverse the Fourth Circuit's decision. If the Court affirms, the requirement to show a group-wide drop in admissions before examining discriminatory intent would stand, and this lawsuit would remain dismissed. If the Court reverses, lower courts could consider evidence of discriminatory intent without first requiring that aggregate showing, and the case would likely return to the lower courts for further proceedings. A vacatur would set aside the Fourth Circuit's ruling and send the case back without resolving the question for either side.
