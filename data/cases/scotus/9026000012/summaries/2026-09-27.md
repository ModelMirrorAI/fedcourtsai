## In brief

A parents' group asks the Court to review whether it must prove a school district's admissions lottery hurt Asian-American students in aggregate before courts can weigh evidence of discriminatory purpose.

## What happened

The Association for Education Fairness (AFEF) sued Montgomery County Public Schools, first challenging an admissions process called the Field Test, then amending its claim after the district replaced it with a lottery-based system, the Pandemic Plan, adopted during COVID-19. AFEF says the lottery still disadvantages Asian-American students because it uses local score comparisons. The district court dismissed the amended complaint, finding AFEF neither showed the lottery disproportionately hurt Asian-American students nor plausibly alleged the district intended discrimination. The Fourth Circuit affirmed, following its own prior ruling that requires proof of disproportionate impact measured against the applicant pool. AFEF appealed to the Supreme Court.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked whether someone claiming a school district adopted neutral-looking admission rules to discriminate by race must first show the rules actually produced a disproportionate effect on that racial group, before a court will look at evidence of the district's intent. AFEF says courts of appeals disagree on this sequencing; the school district says they do not.

## Where it stands

The petition has been distributed for conference twice, most recently for October 9, 2026, after the respondents filed their brief opposing review and AFEF filed a reply. The docket shows no action yet on whether the Court will hear the case.

## What each outcome would mean

AFEF, the parents' group, is asking the Court to take the case. If the Court grants review, it will decide the legal question and the case would continue, with no side yet declared right. If the Court denies review, the Fourth Circuit's decision affirming dismissal of AFEF's lawsuit stands, leaving Montgomery County's admissions lottery in place and AFEF's claims dismissed. A denial would not mean the Court agrees the lower court was correct; it would simply leave that ruling undisturbed.
