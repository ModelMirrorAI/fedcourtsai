## What happened

Montgomery County Public Schools changed how it admits students to competitive magnet middle school programs. It first used a process called the Field Test, then, during the COVID-19 pandemic, switched to a lottery-based system called the Pandemic Plan. The Association for Education Fairness, a parents' group, sued, arguing the admissions changes were designed to disadvantage Asian-American students. A federal district court dismissed the lawsuit, finding the group had not adequately alleged that the Pandemic Plan harmed Asian-American students or that officials adopted it for discriminatory reasons. The United States Court of Appeals for the Fourth Circuit affirmed, relying on circuit precedent about how to measure such harm. The school board disputes the group's account, saying the changes responded to pandemic conditions, not race.

## What the Court is being asked

The case concerns lawsuits claiming a school district adopted admission rules that look neutral on their face but were really meant to discriminate by race. The Court is asked to decide whether, before a court can even weigh evidence that officials acted with discriminatory purpose, a plaintiff must first show that the admissions rules actually produced worse outcomes for the targeted racial group. The petitioner says courts of appeals disagree on this sequencing question and on how to measure such an impact. The school board answers that no real disagreement exists and that this case would not change under any approach.
