## In brief

A parents' group asks the Court to revive its lawsuit claiming a Maryland school district's lottery-based magnet admissions policy intentionally discriminates against Asian American students.

## What happened

The Association for Education Fairness (AFEF) sued the Montgomery County Board of Education, claiming the district's admissions process for competitive magnet middle schools discriminated against Asian American students. After the district adopted a new lottery-based system during the COVID-19 pandemic (the "Pandemic Plan"), AFEF amended its complaint to challenge that system. The district court dismissed the amended complaint, finding AFEF had not plausibly alleged either that the plan disproportionately affected Asian American students or that it was adopted with discriminatory intent. The Fourth Circuit affirmed, relying on circuit precedent requiring plaintiffs to show the targeted group's admission rate fell below its share of applicants.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked whether someone challenging a school district's race-neutral admissions policy as intentionally discriminatory must first show the policy produced a disproportionate effect on the targeted racial group before a court can even consider other evidence that officials acted with discriminatory intent.

## Where it stands

The petition has been distributed for conference more than once, most recently for the October 16, 2026 conference, after full briefing including an opposition brief, a reply, and several amicus briefs. The docket shows no action yet on the petition.

## What each outcome would mean

AFEF, the parents' group, asks the Court to hear the case. If the Court grants review, it would hear the case in full and decide the legal question without yet deciding who wins. If the Court denies review, the Fourth Circuit's decision affirming dismissal of AFEF's lawsuit stands, leaving Montgomery County's admissions lottery in place and AFEF's suit over. A grant would not itself determine whether the lottery is lawful; that question would depend on further proceedings or a later decision on the merits.
