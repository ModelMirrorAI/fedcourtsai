## Counterweights and judgment class

The strongest counterweight is the district court's independent rejection of discriminatory intent. The opposition describes a pandemic-driven policy adopted through changed personnel, removal of individualized selection, and a lottery whose final selection does not use local norming (opposition pp. 6–13, 21–23). It also disputes that Coalition for TJ creates the categorical rule petitioner attacks (opposition pp. 26–28). I do not treat petitioner's allegations of proxy discrimination as established facts.

The reply responds that intent was preserved on appeal but never decided there (reply pp. 3–5). The appendix independently supports that appellate posture. It also records waiver of the separate Rule 60(b)(2) argument (appendix p. 4a); I therefore do not rely on later-submitted testing data as adjudicated proof of injury or intent. These features make remand more likely than a final plaintiff victory, but leave room for affirmance on an alternative ground or a DIG.
