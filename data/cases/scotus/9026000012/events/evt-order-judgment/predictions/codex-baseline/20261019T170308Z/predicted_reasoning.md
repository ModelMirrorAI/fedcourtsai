# Forecast

The Court will vacate the Fourth Circuit's judgment and remand. My modal lineup is 6–3: Roberts, Thomas, Alito, Gorsuch, Kavanaugh, and Barrett in the majority; Sotomayor, Kagan, and Jackson dissenting. The probability that the judgment is disturbed, encompassing reversal and a mixed judgment as well as vacatur, is 0.84.

The majority will answer the limited question in the October 19 grant order in the negative. A racial group's continued admissions success relative to its share of the applicant pool will not immunize an admissions policy from an individual applicant's intentional-discrimination challenge. The majority will apply Arlington Heights' inquiry into discriminatory purpose, informed by equal protection's protection of individuals, rather than treat aggregate group performance as a threshold bar to examining intent. This does not dispense with an actionable injury or turn a racial disparity alone into a constitutional violation.

The holding will reach other selective-admissions disputes using the same aggregate benchmark. It will not categorically prohibit local norming, poverty-based preferences, or lotteries. The Court will leave the sufficiency of the allegations concerning the operative lottery, including its connection to earlier policies, for further proceedings. It will not order admission of particular students or adjudicate the superseded field test's constitutionality.

That remedial restraint explains the forecast of vacatur rather than a final merits judgment declaring the lottery unlawful. The appellate court relied on its disparate-impact precedent without resolving the separate dispute about discriminatory intent. Reversal and remand remains a substantial alternative judgment label for the same threshold correction.

The dissent will emphasize the distinction between individual racial classifications and socioeconomic criteria, the limited allegations connecting the pandemic lottery to earlier officials' objectives, and the possibility of affirmance on the district court's independent intent rationale. A narrower opinion could attract additional votes for remand; the precise 6–3 split is less certain than the direction of the judgment. I expect disposition after briefing and argument, most likely by the end of the October 2026 Term in June 2027. A DIG remains possible if the independent-ground or preservation problems prove dispositive, but is not the modal route. No recusal is forecast.
