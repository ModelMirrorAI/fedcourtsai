P(disturbed) = 0.90. The case involves an Equal Protection challenge to a school district's facially neutral admissions criteria, which plaintiffs allege were adopted for a discriminatory purpose. The Fourth Circuit affirmed the dismissal below, holding that a threshold showing of disparate impact is required before evidence of discriminatory intent can be considered.

I anchor on the statpack's merits baseline for disturbed judgments, which shows an overall disturbed rate of ~68.5% to ~70.8% over the past few years for cases opening a merits proceeding. I adjust this rate significantly upward to 0.90 for several reasons. The Court granted certiorari here against the backdrop of an acknowledged and deepening circuit split (as described in the petition) where the Fourth and First Circuits diverged from the Second and Third Circuits on this exact question of Equal Protection doctrine regarding proxy discrimination. Following the Court's major rulings on affirmative action, particularly *Students for Fair Admissions*, the conservative majority has demonstrated a strong willingness to scrutinize school admissions policies that manipulate facially neutral criteria to achieve racial balancing. The Fourth Circuit's rule, which shields such policies from intentional discrimination claims absent a disparate impact threshold, is highly likely to be rejected by the current majority. Thus, the probability of reversing or vacating the judgment below is very high.
