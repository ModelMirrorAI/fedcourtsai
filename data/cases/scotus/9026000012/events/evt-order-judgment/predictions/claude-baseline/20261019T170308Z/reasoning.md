# Reasoning for P(disturbed) = 0.82

**What I worked from.** I used the provisioned snapshot `2026-10-19.json`, the petition, the QP section, the BIO, the cert reply, and the appendix, which contains the unpublished Fourth Circuit per curiam (Feb. 3, 2026) and the district court opinion. No merits briefs exist at this grant moment, and none were on disk, so there was no provisioning anomaly. This is a forecast from the cert-stage papers plus the docket skeleton. I did no MCP or corpus retrieval (see `retrieval.md`).

**Anchor.** I used the statpack's "merits docket (granted cases)" section. It publishes an `excluded` count, so it is quotable. The grant date is 2026-10-19, so the grant Term is OT2026 and the pool is Terms 2016–2025. The table renders 2017–2025 and has no 2016 row, so the pool is 377 disturbed / 540 parsed = **69.8%**. That is well above the 30-judgment floor. Coverage is complete for older Terms. OT2025 is thin (24 parsed of 51 granted), which is mostly pendency. This 69.8% is the bar my skill is scored against.

**Adjustments up from ~70%:**
- The Court **rewrote the question presented** to target the lower court's specific rule: an "aggregate disparate impact relative to its share of the applicant pool." A grant limited to a reformulated question attacking the precise rule below is a strong reversal signal.
- The Fourth Circuit's affirmance rested *only* on binding *Coalition for TJ* precedent. The panel said so, and the petitioner conceded the complaint fails under that standard. If the Court rejects the aggregate benchmark, the stated basis of the judgment falls.
- Alito, joined by Thomas, already dissented from the denials in *Coalition for TJ* and *Boston Parent Coalition* on this theme, and Gorsuch is a likely ally. The grant came after a response was requested, five amicus filings supporting the petitioner, and three distributions, which points to at least four Justices interested in correcting the rule.

**Adjustments down:**
- The BIO's vehicle point is real: the district court gave an independent no-intent ground, and the Fourth Circuit did not disturb it. This raises DIG risk (I put it at ~4%). It also leaves a small chance the Court affirms on the alternative ground. That is unlikely because the Fourth Circuit did not rely on it, and the usual course is to remand.
- Roberts or Barrett could read *Feeney* and *Arlington Heights* as requiring some group-level impact and affirm narrowly (~14%).

**Judgment label.** I chose `vacated` over `reversed` because the intent question remains open for remand. The Court often writes "reversed and remanded" even in that posture, so the label is roughly 55/45. Both labels are disturbed, so the binary is unaffected.

**Votes.** 6–3 on ideological lines is my modal lineup. Kagan's vote is the most uncertain. I left `writing` unset because I am not forecasting authorship.

**Discount me on:** I have not read any merits briefing or argument. I only skimmed the BIO's intent argument. The 0.82 leans on the reformulated-QP signal, which could also reflect the Court narrowing the question in order to *affirm* a cleaner rule.
