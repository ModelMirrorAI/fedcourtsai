# Forecast of the Court's action

The Court will deny certiorari in Association for Education Fairness v.
Montgomery County Board of Education. P(any grant), including a GVR or summary
reversal, is **0.22**. This forecasts the petition's disposition, not whether
the challenged admissions policy is constitutional.

## Further conference consideration

The snapshot records **two distributions**: July 22 for the September 28, 2026
conference, and September 16 for the October 9, 2026 conference. Both distribution
entries precede the first scheduled conference. This is a rescheduling-shaped
record, not evidence that the Justices have already considered and relisted the
petition. The starting count for the forecast remains two.

I predict **one additional distribution**, most likely after the October 9
conference, followed by denial in October or November 2026. P(at least one
distribution beyond the two already recorded) is **0.55**; zero is the principal
alternative. The split and the possibility of a separate writing will prolong
consideration, but this vehicle will not secure review. Timing is context-only.

## Solicitor General and route

There is no CVSG in the snapshot. I predict **no CVSG**, with **0.04** probability
of a new invitation before disposition. The Court can resolve the petition's
review-worthiness from the conflicting circuit approaches and the pleading
record without seeking the federal government's institutional views.

**Conditional on a grant**, plenary briefing and argument are the likelier
route. P(disposition in the cert order itself | grant) is **0.10**. The supplied
record identifies no intervening Supreme Court decision that straightforwardly
requires a GVR, and a contested evidentiary standard with an alternative
dismissal ground is not an obvious summary-reversal vehicle.

## Question and separate writing

If review is granted, the Court will focus the question on whether an
equal-protection plaintiff challenging a facially neutral admissions policy
must allege aggregate racial underrepresentation, rather than an individualized
discriminatory effect, to proceed. That is more precise than treating the case
as a dispute solely over the sequence in which courts may examine intent and
impact. Conditional on reaching that question, the likely ground is that
aggregate overrepresentation does not categorically defeat proof of an
individual injury from purposeful discrimination; the pleading of intent
would remain a separate issue on remand. Neither a grant nor a merits holding
is the primary forecast.

**Conditional on denial**, I narrowly predict a noted dissent or statement
respecting denial: **0.55** probability of any such writing. The likely theme
is the deepened disagreement over aggregate benchmarks, notwithstanding the
vehicle objections. Alito, potentially joined by Thomas, is the most plausible
source given the writings in the related cases discussed in the petition and
Sargent; that attribution is context-only, not a per-Justice cert-vote forecast.
No majority merits opinion accompanies the predicted denial.
