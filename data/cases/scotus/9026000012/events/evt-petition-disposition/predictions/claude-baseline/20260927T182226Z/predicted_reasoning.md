# Forecast: what the Court will do with No. 26-12

**Bottom line.** The petition will most likely be **denied**, probably with a
noted dissent, after at least one further relist. P(any grant) = 0.22.

## Relists

The docket shows **two distributions**: one for the 9/28/2026 long conference
(entered 7/22, before the Court called for a response on 7/28) and a
re-distribution for the 10/9/2026 conference (entered 9/16, after the BIO and
reply). The second entry is not a substantive relist — the response request
took the petition off the long-conference list, and 10/9 is the first
conference at which the Justices will actually consider it. So this petition
is functionally at its first conference with zero true relists.

I expect **at least one more distribution** (P = 0.72), and more likely
several. The two prior petitions on exactly this question were both relisted
heavily before denial — Coalition for TJ (No. 23-170) was distributed seven
times over December 2023–February 2024, and Boston Parent Coalition
(No. 23-1137) eight times over September–December 2024 — because a Justice
was writing a dissent from denial. The Court also called for a response here
after the respondent waived, which means at least one chambers is already
engaged. A relist-then-grant is the modern pattern for grants as well. The
scenario with no further distribution is a quiet denial on the 10/13 order
list, which I put at roughly a quarter.

## CVSG

**No CVSG** (P = 0.04). No federal party, no federal statute, and the Court
did not call for the Solicitor General's views in either TJ or Boston Parent.
The Solicitor General could file uninvited, but that is not a CVSG.

## Which question, if any, the Court would take

If the Court grants, it would take the petitioner's single question
**substantially as written**, though it might reformulate it to ask directly
whether Arlington Heights requires an aggregate "applicant-pool" showing of
disparate impact rather than the petition's "sequencing" framing, which the
BIO plausibly says no circuit actually applies. Given the posture (Rule
12(b)(6) dismissal, alternative intent holding not reached by the Fourth
Circuit), a grant would be for plenary review with a remand for the Fourth
Circuit to address intent under whatever standard the Court announces.

## Summary disposition

**Unlikely** (P = 0.06 conditional on a grant). There is no intervening
decision that undercuts the Fourth Circuit's rule — Mullin v. Doe (2026), the
Court's most recent Arlington Heights ruling, cuts if anything toward the
respondents — so a GVR has no anchor, and a per curiam reversal of an
unpublished, three-page pleading-stage affirmance with an unaddressed
alternative ground is not the Court's style. A grant here means argument.

## Dissent from denial

If the Court denies, I expect **some separate writing** (P = 0.6): Justices
Alito and Thomas dissented from denial in both TJ and Boston Parent and
called the Fourth Circuit's rule a "blueprint for evading SFFA"; Justice
Gorsuch wrote a statement in Boston Parent urging lower courts to consider the
dissent's reasoning. The split they predicted has now materialized (Second
and Third Circuits versus First and Fourth), which makes a written dissent
more likely than in the average denial. The counterweight is that this vehicle
is weaker on the facts than either prior case, and a dissenter may prefer to
wait for a cleaner one; the 0.4 no-writing branch is real.

## Conditional merits note

If granted, the likely ground would be that Arlington Heights makes disparate
impact one evidentiary factor among several and does not require a threshold
aggregate under-representation showing before intent evidence is considered,
with a reversal and remand; a merits affirmance on the alternative
no-plausible-intent ground is a live risk that would make the grant look
improvident.
