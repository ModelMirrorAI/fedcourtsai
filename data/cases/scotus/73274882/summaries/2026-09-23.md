## What happened

Virginia Duncan and other California residents, along with a gun-rights organization, sued the state over a law that bans possessing ammunition magazines holding more than 10 rounds. They argue the law violates the Second Amendment and forces people to give up property they lawfully bought without payment, which they say violates the Constitution's Takings Clause. After years of litigation, including an earlier trip to the Supreme Court that sent the case back for another look, a divided federal appeals court (the Ninth Circuit, sitting with all its judges) ruled for California, holding that such magazines are not constitutionally protected "arms" and that the law fits within a historical tradition of regulating especially dangerous weapons. Four judges dissented. California's attorney general opposes further review, arguing the Court recently turned away similar cases and there's no real disagreement among lower courts.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked whether a state's ban on possessing high-capacity ammunition magazines is allowed under the constitutional right to keep and bear arms, and separately, whether requiring people to give up, modify, or sell magazines they already legally owned—without paying them for it—crosses the line into an unconstitutional taking of property.

## Where it stands

The petition, opposition, and several rounds of supporting briefs have been filed, and the case has been distributed for conference many times over roughly a year without a decision on whether to take it up; the parties have also filed supplemental briefs pointing to related cases the Court has acted on in the meantime, and the petition remains pending.
