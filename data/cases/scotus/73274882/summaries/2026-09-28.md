## In brief

Gun owners and advocacy groups ask the Court to strike down California's ban on possessing ammunition magazines that hold more than 10 rounds.

## What happened

Virginia Duncan and other California residents, along with a gun-rights group, sued over a California law that bans possessing magazines holding more than 10 rounds of ammunition. A federal district court twice ruled for the challengers, but the full Ninth Circuit Court of Appeals, sitting en banc, twice upheld the law, most recently in March 2025. The appeals court held that such magazines are not "arms" protected by the Second Amendment, and separately rejected a claim that the law amounts to an uncompensated taking of property. The challengers now ask the Supreme Court to review that decision.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked whether a ban on possessing these ammunition magazines violates the constitutional right to keep and bear arms, and whether requiring owners to give up, alter, or sell magazines they already legally owned amounts to a taking of property that requires the government to pay compensation.

## Where it stands

The petition has been distributed for many conferences since November 2025, with the parties filing additional supplemental briefs into 2026, and the docket shows no action granting or denying review as of the snapshot date.

## What each outcome would mean

Duncan and the other challengers are asking the Court to take up the case. If the Court grants review, it would hear full arguments and decide the constitutional questions itself, deciding nothing yet about who is right. If it denies review, the Ninth Circuit's ruling upholding California's law stands, leaving the magazine ban and its enforcement in place. A denial would not mean the Court agrees the law is constitutional, only that the lower court's decision remains final.
