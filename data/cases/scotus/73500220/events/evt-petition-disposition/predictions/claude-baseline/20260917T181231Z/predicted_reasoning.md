# Predicted reasoning — Patel, et vir v. United States, No. 25-1319 (CA3)

**Disposition.** The Court will deny the petition on the order list following the
September 28, 2026 Long Conference (most likely the first October 2026 order
list), without calling for a response and without separate writing.

**Relists.** The docket shows **one** distribution (June 17, 2026, for the
Conference of 9/28/2026) and no relist. The petition is a paid private
petition in the baseline salience band, the United States has waived its
response, and the case comes to the Court in a § 2255 posture decided on the
briefs below. The likeliest path from here is a single conference and a
denial. The one realistic route to a second distribution is a request for a
response after the Long Conference, which would push the petition to a later
conference; I put that plus an ordinary relist at roughly one in seven. I do
not expect more than one further distribution if one occurs.

**CVSG.** None. The Solicitor General is counsel for the respondent United
States, so there is no third-party federal view to invite; the SG's position
arrives, if at all, as a requested response, not as an invited brief.

**Question presented, if taken.** If the Court took the case, it would not take
the question as written (a "sufficiently intertwined and sufficiently severe"
standard). It would reformulate to the narrower question whether *Padilla v.
Kentucky*'s rejection of a categorical direct/collateral distinction extends
beyond deportation to civil-liability consequences of a plea. A grant would
mean plenary review to clarify *Padilla* and *Chaidez*; the Court would not
adopt the petition's two-part test as the framing.

**Summary disposition.** Unlikely conditional on a grant. There is no
intervening decision of this Court bearing on *Padilla*'s scope, so a GVR has
nothing to be "in light of," and the Third Circuit's opinion is a reasoned
precedential holding rather than an obvious error inviting a per curiam
reversal. If granted, plenary review is the far likelier route.

**Dissent from denial.** None expected. The case lacks the features that draw a
statement respecting denial: no sympathetic pro se petitioner facing
deportation or lengthy incarceration on the collateral consequence, no
entrenched split acknowledged by the lower courts, and the government's
silence signals that even the respondent saw nothing needing an answer. A
short statement from Justice Sotomayor or Justice Gorsuch about the reach of
*Padilla* is conceivable but improbable.

**Merits, conditionally.** If the Court granted and reached the merits, the
likely ground would be that *Padilla* itself disclaimed the direct/collateral
distinction as the test and that the Sixth Amendment inquiry is *Strickland*'s
reasonableness standard applied to the consequence's severity and its
enmeshment with the conviction, likely with a remand for the prejudice
inquiry the courts below did not reach. That is conditional on a grant I put
at about two percent.
