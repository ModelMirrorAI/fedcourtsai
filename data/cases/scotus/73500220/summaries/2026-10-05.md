## In brief

The Patels asked the Court to decide whether lawyers must warn clients that pleading guilty could expose them to civil fraud liability; the Court declined to hear the case.

## What happened

Nita and Kirtish Patel pleaded guilty to health care fraud after operating medical testing companies that submitted false claims to Medicare. The same conduct later led the government to pursue them under the False Claims Act, resulting in a civil judgment exceeding $7.7 million, based partly on facts they could no longer dispute because of their guilty pleas. The Patels argued their criminal defense lawyer never warned them that pleading guilty could lock in that civil liability. Federal courts denied their request to undo their sentences, and the Third Circuit Court of Appeals ruled that the Constitution's right to counsel does not require lawyers to advise clients about this kind of civil consequence, only about direct criminal penalties. The Third Circuit later denied a request to reconsider that ruling.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked whether the right to a lawyer's effective help, when deciding to plead guilty, also covers warning a defendant about consequences outside the criminal case itself—here, civil fraud liability—when that consequence is closely tied to the conviction and is severe enough to matter.

## Where it stands

The petition was distributed for the Court's conference on September 28, 2026. On October 5, 2026, the Court denied the petition.

## What each outcome would mean

The Patels asked the Court to take up their case. By denying the petition, the Court leaves the Third Circuit's ruling in place: defense lawyers in that circuit are not constitutionally required to advise clients that a guilty plea could create civil liability under the False Claims Act, and the Patels' convictions and the related civil judgment against them stand. The denial does not mean the Court agreed with the Third Circuit's reasoning—it simply leaves that decision as the final word in this case.
