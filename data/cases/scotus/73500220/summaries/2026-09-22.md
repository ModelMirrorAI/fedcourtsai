## What happened

Nita and Kirtish Patel pleaded guilty to health care fraud after operating a diagnostic testing business that defrauded Medicare. Their lawyer, they say, never told them that a separate whistleblower lawsuit (a qui tam case, brought under the False Claims Act) was pending against them, or that admissions in their guilty pleas would legally prevent them from contesting liability in that civil case. That civil case ended in a judgment against them for over $7.7 million. The Patels argued in federal court that this failure amounted to constitutionally inadequate legal advice. The district court disagreed, and the Third Circuit Court of Appeals affirmed, holding that the Sixth Amendment only requires lawyers to advise clients about direct consequences of a guilty plea, not collateral ones like civil liability. The full appeals court declined to rehear the case.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked whether the right to a lawyer's effective help extends to advice about a consequence of a guilty plea that is not part of the criminal punishment itself but is instead a related civil or legal outcome, at least when that consequence is severe and closely connected to the underlying conviction. The Patels argue lower courts are split on how broadly an earlier Supreme Court ruling about immigration consequences applies to other kinds of collateral fallout from guilty pleas, and they ask the Court to clarify the rule and rule in their favor.
