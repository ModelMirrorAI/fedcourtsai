## In brief

The government asked the Court to review whether a detained immigrant who entered the country years ago must be held without bond, a question the Court has agreed to decide.

## What happened

Ricardo Aparecido Barbosa da Cunha entered the United States without inspection around 2004 or 2005 and has lived here since, applying for asylum in 2016. In 2025 immigration officers arrested him, and the government argued a statute requiring detention without bond for people "seeking admission" to the country applied to him. A district court and the Second Circuit disagreed, ruling that a different statute, which allows release on bond, governed his case, and ordering a bond hearing; he was released. The government, represented by Philip L. Rhoney of ICE, appealed to the Supreme Court.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked to decide which of two immigration detention laws applies to someone who entered the country without authorization years earlier and has been living here since: a rule that requires detention without bond for people trying to gain entry, or a different rule that allows release on bond while removal proceedings continue.

## Where it stands

The Court granted the petition on October 1, 2026. The case has not yet been argued; the deadline for the government's opening merits brief was extended to November 30, 2026, and the respondent's brief is due January 13, 2027.

## What each outcome would mean

The government is asking the Court to overturn the Second Circuit's ruling, which favored Barbosa da Cunha. If the Court affirms, Barbosa da Cunha's position prevails: people in his situation remain eligible for bond hearings. If the Court reverses, the government's reading would prevail, meaning such individuals could be held without bond during removal proceedings. If the Court vacates, it would send the case back without deciding the question for either side.
