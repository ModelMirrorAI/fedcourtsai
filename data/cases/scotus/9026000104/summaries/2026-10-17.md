## In brief

The government asked the Court to review whether immigration law requires detaining, without bond, people who entered the country unlawfully years ago.

## What happened

Ricardo Aparecido Barbosa da Cunha entered the United States without inspection around 2004 or 2005 and has lived here since, applying for asylum in 2016. In 2025 the government arrested him and, under a new policy, argued a statute covering people "seeking admission" at the border required his detention without any bond hearing. A district court and the Second Circuit both ruled for Barbosa da Cunha, holding that a different statute, which allows bond, governs people already living in the country rather than those seeking to enter it. Several other circuits have agreed with that reading; two have sided with the government.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked whether the law mandating detention without bond for people "seeking admission" to the country applies to someone who entered the country without permission years earlier and has been living here since, or whether a different law that allows release on bond applies instead.

## Where it stands

The Court granted the petition on October 1, 2026. The case has not yet been argued; the government has since asked for an extension of time to file its merits brief.

## What each outcome would mean

The federal government, through officer Philip Rhoney, asked the Court to take the case after losing in the lower courts. If the Court affirms, Barbosa da Cunha and others in similar circumstances would remain eligible for bond hearings while their removal cases proceed. If the Court reverses, such individuals could be detained without any bond hearing while their cases proceed. If the Court vacates, the Second Circuit's decision would be set aside and the case sent back for further proceedings without a final ruling for either side.
