# Forecast — Rhoney v. Barbosa da Cunha, No. 26-104 (petition disposition, forecast at arrival)

The docket shows **zero distributions** — this is the arrival moment, forecast
before any conference signal exists. Everything below forecasts forward from
that state.

## What the Court will do

**The Court will grant this petition** — most likely consolidated with, or as a
companion to, the Solicitor General's earlier petition in *Raycraft v.
Lopez-Campos*, No. 25-1415 (CA6) — and will resolve whether 8 U.S.C.
§ 1225(b)(2)(A)'s mandatory-detention provision reaches noncitizens who entered
without inspection and are apprehended in the interior, or whether § 1226(a)'s
discretionary bond regime governs. The Second Circuit's decision below
(*Barbosa da Cunha v. Freden*, 175 F.4th 61 (2d Cir. 2026)) is the lead
published opinion on the anti-government side of an acknowledged split that now
stands roughly 7–2 (CA1, CA2, CA6, CA7, CA9, CA10, CA11 against the
government's reading; CA5 and CA8 for it, each over a dissent). A split that
deep, invalidating a flagship federal enforcement policy, with the United
States as petitioner in multiple circuits, is close to the paradigm of a
mandatory grant.

## Relists and timing

The brief in opposition is due August 24, 2026 (extensions likely). Expect
first **distribution for the late-September 2026 long conference or one shortly
after**, then **one to three further distributions** — not doubt about
certworthiness, but calendar coordination among the parallel government
petitions (25-1415, this one, and likely later-filed petitions from the CA7,
CA9, CA10, CA11, and CA1 losses). Grant order likely **October–December 2026**,
argument in the winter or spring sitting, decision by end of June 2027.

## CVSG

**None.** The Solicitor General is the petitioner; the Court does not call for
the views of a party already before it.

## Which question presented

The Court will take the SG's question essentially **as written** — whether an
alien present without admission and apprehended in the interior is subject to
detention under § 1225(b)(2)(A) rather than § 1226(a). No reformulation
expected; the question is already framed identically across the circuit
opinions. Constitutional avoidance (due-process limits on prolonged detention)
will ride along in briefing but will not be a granted question.

## Summary disposition

**Unlikely as the primary route.** A summary reversal on a deeply contested,
percolated split is implausible. The realistic summary route is conditional: if
the Court grants only the companion vehicle and the government prevails on the
merits, this held petition would be **GVR'd** in light of that decision — that
is the substance of my 0.20 conditional summary-route probability.

## If denied

A denial most plausibly arrives as housekeeping after the Court resolves the
split **against** the government in another vehicle; in that posture expect
**no dissent from denial**. An outright denial with no grant anywhere is very
unlikely, and only that scenario would plausibly draw noted dissents.

## If granted (conditional merits view)

If granted and argued, the government's odds are genuinely contested: the
textualist case against its reading persuaded seven circuits, including
Republican-appointed panel members, but two circuits and respected dissenters
(e.g., Judge Sykes in the Seventh Circuit) accepted it, and this Court has been
receptive to the government's immigration-enforcement positions on the
emergency docket. I would put the government slightly below even odds on the
merits — which is what makes the deny-after-held-vehicle branch non-trivial.
