The petition has been distributed twice (for the June 25 and September 28 conferences). Expect one or two further relists as the Court coordinates this petition with the companion petitions in *Conti* and *Cantero III*, which present the same circuit split. The Court may also call for the views of the Solicitor General (a CVSG) to address the impact of the OCC's new rule on interest-on-escrow preemption before deciding whether to grant, though the Court is likely familiar with the SG's general views from the previous *Cantero* proceedings. 

If review is granted, the Court will take the single question presented regarding whether the National Bank Act preempts state interest-on-escrow laws under the standard articulated in the Court's recent *Cantero* decision. The Court is highly unlikely to dispose of this case via a summary disposition (GVR or summary reversal); having already vacated and remanded *Cantero* for the lower courts to apply the proper standard, the ensuing circuit split indicates plenary review is required to resolve the application of that standard. 

Should the Court deny review, it will likely be because it has chosen to grant one of the companion petitions (*Cantero III* or *Conti*) instead. In that scenario, expect this petition to be held and eventually denied without separate writings or dissents. If the Court denies all three petitions, a dissent from denial by justices favoring broad NBA preemption is plausible but unlikely, as they may prefer to wait for the new OCC rule to percolate through the lower courts.
