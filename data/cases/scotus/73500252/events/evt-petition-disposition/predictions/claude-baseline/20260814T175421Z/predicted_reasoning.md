# Forecast — Flagstar Bank, N.A. v. Kivett, No. 25-1350 (evt-petition-disposition)

State forecast from: the docket shows **one distribution** (June 9, 2026, for the
Conference of June 25, 2026), which the June 15 call for a response effectively
superseded before the conference met; **no CVSG**; respondent waived, the Court
requested a response, the BIO arrived July 30, 2026. No reply brief is on the
docket yet as of the August 14 snapshot.

## Relists

The petition **will be distributed again** — it is fully briefed and must return
to conference to be disposed of. Expect distribution for the long conference
(late September 2026), and then **one to three further relists**: three vehicles
present the same question (this petition, the *Cantero III* petition from the
Second Circuit, and the pending rehearing petition in *Conti*), and the Court
will align their consideration before acting, which historically produces
serial redistribution. This petition's last trip (No. 22-349) was held for
months behind *Cantero*; some echo of that pattern is likely.

## CVSG

**No CVSG this time** (~10%). The Solicitor General's views on this very case
were delivered in the 2023 round, and the OCC has since formalized the federal
position in a May 19, 2026 preemption rule and determination — the Court knows
what the government thinks, and an invitation would buy six months for a
predictable answer. The residual chance is a CVSG asking specifically about the
new OCC rule's effect on the retrospective question.

## Disposition

Near a coin flip, with denial the single most likely label: **P(grant family —
plenary grant, consolidated grant, or eventual GVR) ≈ 0.45; P(denied) ≈ 0.53**.
If the Court engages the question at all — which the entrenched post-remand
split (2d Cir. preempted; 1st and 9th not), the OCC's plea for uniformity, and
the call for response all make more likely than not — this petition is the
strongest vehicle in the trio: a final money judgment plus injunction, a full
summary-judgment record, the circuit that adhered to its pre-*Cantero*
precedent over a dissent, and the United States' earlier statement that this
case was the better vehicle. Expect either a grant of this petition (quite
possibly **consolidated with *Cantero III***, covering both directions of the
split) or a hold behind a *Cantero III* grant — in which case a bank-favorable
merits ruling flows back here as a **GVR**. The path to denial runs through the
Court's demonstrated minimalism: it declined to decide this exact question in
2024 when squarely presented, and the new OCC rule gives it a reason to let
prospective uniformity arrive by regulation while the retrospective split
percolates.

## Question presented, if taken

**As written.** The petition presents a single clean QP (whether the NBA
preempts state interest-on-escrow laws like Cal. Civ. Code § 2954.8(a)); there
is no second question to shed. If consolidated with *Cantero III*, the New York
statute joins but the QP's substance is unchanged.

## Summary disposition

Unlikely as a first move. No intervening decision exists to GVR against today;
a GVR becomes likely only downstream, if the Court decides the question in a
companion case in the banks' favor. A summary reversal without argument is
improbable — the First Circuit reached the Ninth's result while purporting to
apply *Cantero*'s method, so the Court cannot treat the ruling below as mere
defiance.

## If denied

Expect the denial to be more likely clean than not, but there is a real chance
(~20%) of a **statement or dissent respecting denial** — most plausibly from
Justice Kavanaugh (the *Cantero* author) or Justice Gorsuch — given the OCC's
formal position and Judge R. Nelson's dissent below.

## Timing

Disposition of the petition by **January 2027** (grant) or on an order list
between October 2026 and early 2027 (denial). If granted, argument in spring
2027 and a decision by June 2027.
