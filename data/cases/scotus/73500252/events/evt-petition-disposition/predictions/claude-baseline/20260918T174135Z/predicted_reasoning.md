# Forecast: Flagstar Bank, N.A. v. Kivett, No. 25-1350 (evt-petition-disposition)

**Bottom line.** The Court grants certiorari (P = 0.57). The likelier form of grant is plenary review on the question presented as written, with the petition either serving as the vehicle for the post-*Cantero* interest-on-escrow question or being granted alongside a companion petition from the Second Circuit's *Cantero* remand decision.

## Relists

The docket shows two distributions: for the June 25, 2026 conference (before the Court called for a response) and for the September 28, 2026 conference (distributed August 19, rescheduled August 20, redistributed August 26 for the same conference). The petition has therefore not yet actually been considered at a conference; September 28 is its first real look.

I expect at least one further distribution after September 28 (P = 0.72). The Court's standing practice is to relist once before granting to check the vehicle, and here it also has to coordinate with the rehearing petition in *Citizens Bank v. Conti*, No. 25-1004 (whose cert denial preceded the Second Circuit's May 2026 decision that created the current three-way conflict), and with the *Cantero* plaintiffs' pending petition from the Second Circuit. Expect one to two relists, then an order in October or November 2026. A denial straight off the long conference is the main way the count stays at two.

## Call for the views of the Solicitor General

Unlikely but live (P = 0.22). The Court already called for the government's views on this exact question in this very case in 2023, and the Solicitor General then urged the Court to grant Flagstar rather than *Cantero*. Since then the OCC has issued a final rule (effective June 19, 2026) purporting to preempt state interest-on-escrow laws prospectively, so the government's position is a matter of public record. The one reason for a fresh CVSG is the respondents' argument that the new rule makes the question non-recurring; the Court could want the government's word on whether the case is still worth deciding. I think the Court more likely acts without asking.

## Which question the Court would take

The question presented as written: whether the National Bank Act preempts state interest-on-escrow laws like California Civil Code section 2954.8(a). The respondents' reformulation (whether the split is important enough) is a cert argument, not a merits question, and would not be adopted. If the Court grants the *Cantero* plaintiffs' petition as the lead case, it would grant and consolidate here or hold this petition; a hold would end in a GVR if the Court finds New York's law preempted and a denial if it does not.

## Summary disposition versus plenary review

Conditional on a grant, plenary review is the strong favorite; a summary route (a GVR after a decision in a companion vehicle) is the minority path (P = 0.20 given a grant). There is no intervening decision to GVR in light of today, and a summary reversal of a published Ninth Circuit opinion applying *Cantero* on a full record is implausible.

## If the Court denies

A denial would most likely be quiet (P of any dissent or statement respecting denial = 0.15 given denial). The Court denied *Conti* without a noted dissent, and the natural rationale for a denial here, that the OCC rule has resolved the question prospectively and the remaining conflict affects only a handful of legacy cases, is not one a Justice would need to write about. If anyone writes, it would be a short statement noting that the question remains open for a case arising under the new rule.

## If granted (conditional merits context)

The Court would likely hold that *Cantero* requires the comparative analysis against the Barnett Bank line of precedents as the operative method, and would resolve whether a state statute dictating the price term of a federally authorized bank product significantly interferes with a national bank's powers. Judge Nelson's dissent below and the Second Circuit's remand opinion give the pro-preemption side a ready-made analysis, so the bank would enter argument as the favorite, but the merits are not what this cell resolves.
