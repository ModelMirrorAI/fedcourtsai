# Forecast — Flagstar Bank, N.A. v. Kivett, No. 25-1350 (evt-petition-disposition)

The Court grants review, more likely than not. My probability that the petition
ends in the grant family (plenary grant, GVR, or summary disposition) is
**0.55**, with a plenary grant the single most likely route.

**Relists.** The docket shows two distributions: June 9, 2026 (for the June 25
conference, superseded when the Court called for a response on June 15) and
August 19, 2026 (for the September 28 long conference), with Flagstar's reply
already in. I expect the petition to be considered at the long conference and
**relisted at least once** past the two distributions shown — probability
0.78 — and most plausibly one to three further distributions before
disposition. Two mechanisms point the same way: the modern practice of
relisting at least once before any grant, and coordination with the two
companion petitions the BIO identifies as pending on the same question
(the *Cantero III* plaintiffs' petition from the Second Circuit and the
*Conti* rehearing petition), which may not all be ripe for the same
conference. If the Court instead holds this case for a merits decision in a
companion, the eventual disposition still produces a further distribution.

**No CVSG.** Probability 0.05. The Solicitor General's views on this exact
question, in this exact case, are already on the record: the Court CVSG'd
Flagstar's prior petition (Nos. 22-349/22-529) and the SG responded in August
2023, recommending Flagstar's case as the better vehicle. Since then the OCC
has issued a final preemption rule (91 Fed. Reg. 29350, effective June 19,
2026). A second invitation in the same litigation, with the agency's position
formalized by rule, would be close to unprecedented.

**Which question.** If the Court takes the case it takes the QP essentially as
written — whether the NBA preempts state interest-on-escrow laws like Cal.
Civil Code § 2954.8(a). The petition presents a single clean question; there is
no second QP to strip and no plausible reformulation beyond generalizing
across the three pending cases. (Registered context-only; not scored.)

**Summary route.** Conditional on a grant, probability **0.20** that the case
is disposed of in the cert order itself rather than set for argument. The main
path is a hold for the Second Circuit *Cantero* petition followed by a GVR if
the Court decides that case and disturbs the Ninth Circuit's premise. A
summary reversal is conceivable — Judge R. Nelson's dissent frames the panel
as refusing to perform the comparative analysis *Cantero* directed on remand
of this very case — but the Court took full briefing last time and the
question is fact-inflected under its own standard, so I weight it low. The
likeliest single outcome remains a plenary grant of this petition: both
parties concede it is a sound vehicle (final judgment, summary-judgment
record), and the SG said in 2023 that Flagstar's case was the better vehicle
among the same three.

**Timing, if granted.** Grant by late 2026 (October–December order lists, after
one or more relists), argument in spring 2027, decision by June 2027.

**If denied.** Probability 0.15, conditional on denial, that some Justice notes
a dissent from or statement respecting the denial. A denial here would most
likely rest on the respondents' transience theory — the OCC's June 2026 rule
preempts prospectively, leaving only three retrospective cases — and Justice
Kavanaugh, *Cantero*'s author, is the most plausible writer of a statement or
dissent objecting that the circuits have split over his opinion's application.
Aggregated existence only; no per-Justice claim is scored.
