# Forecast

The petition has been distributed for two conferences so far, with no CVSG on the docket. I expect one further distribution after the September 28 conference before the Court acts. The direct disagreement among the Second, First, and Ninth Circuits after *Cantero*, together with this Court's earlier GVR in this case, makes a grant more likely than a denial. I do not expect another CVSG: the OCC has already taken a clear position on the issue, the United States considered this vehicle during the earlier certiorari round, and the new OCC rule supplies additional federal views.

If review is granted, the Court is most likely to take the single question presented substantially as written: whether the National Bank Act preempts state laws setting the interest national banks must pay on mortgage escrow funds. Plenary review is more likely than a cert-order disposition because resolving the competing comparative applications of *Cantero* calls for more than identifying an intervening precedent. A hold followed by a GVR in light of a companion case, or a summary correction of the Ninth Circuit's approach, remains a meaningful secondary route.

Conditioned on plenary review, the Court is more likely to hold California's interest-on-escrow requirement preempted, reasoning that a state-mandated price term materially interferes with a federally authorized banking power and resembles the restrictions invalidated in the precedents surveyed by *Barnett Bank* and *Cantero*. The factual record concerning Flagstar's compliance and the prospective OCC rule could narrow the holding, but they are unlikely to eliminate the retrospective controversy represented by the final class judgment.

If certiorari is denied, I expect no noted dissent or statement respecting denial.
