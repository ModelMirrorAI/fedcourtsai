# Rationale

I assign a 0.57 probability to any grant. The frozen context is a forward-mode, paid OT2025 petition in the sal-v3 `elevated` band, with two recorded distributions and no CVSG. Pooling the bracketed `reached` rates in the committed statpack over the eight strictly prior Terms (OT2017–OT2024) gives the relevant elevated-band anchor: 688 estimated grant-family outcomes over 3,419 weighted resolved petitions, or 20.12%. The paid scored-segment relist cuts are terminal rather than forward hazards, but they confirm that petitions ending with two or more relists have much higher grant-family rates than those ending after one.

I adjust sharply upward from the anchor because the petition presents a concrete post-*Cantero* outcome conflict: the Second Circuit found a materially similar two-percent interest-on-escrow law preempted, while the First and Ninth Circuits found no preemption. The disagreement also concerns method because the Ninth Circuit continued to rely on *Lusnak* rather than independently performing the comparison the petitioner says *Cantero* requires. This Court previously granted review in *Cantero* and GVR'd this case, the Ninth Circuit has now reaffirmed a final judgment and prospective injunction, and both sides describe this case as a sound vehicle if the Court decides to intervene. The petition and BIO identify no jurisdictional, interlocutory, or preservation obstacle. The OCC's position and the issue's implications for uniform national-bank product terms add institutional importance.

The principal downward adjustment is the OCC rule effective June 19, 2026, which prospectively preempts state interest-on-escrow requirements if valid. Respondents plausibly argue that this makes the three-case split transient and leaves principally retrospective liability. They also dispute practical interference using Flagstar's compliance on much of its servicing portfolio. Companion petitions create vehicle-selection uncertainty, although a hold and later GVR would still resolve as a grant for this prediction. The petition text was marked truncated in `documents.json`, and the August 19 reply was shown on the docket but was not provisioned as extracted text; I therefore relied on the complete QP extraction, the available petition text, and the complete 32-page BIO.

For the procedural claims, I put a 0.55 probability on another distribution because the strength of the split and the possibility of coordinating companion cases make at least one post-conference relist likely. I put only 0.10 on a later CVSG because the federal banking regulator and the United States have already supplied views during this controversy. Conditional on a grant, I put 0.28 on disposition in the cert order: plenary review is the likeliest grant route, but a companion-case hold/GVR or summary correction is material. Conditional on denial, I put 0.09 on any noted dissent or statement because the case is important but separate writings from cert denials remain uncommon.

My confidence is 0.68. The legal and procedural signals are unusually strong, but the new regulation and the Court's choice among related cases make the ultimate disposition materially uncertain.
