## In brief

Flagstar Bank asks the Court to hear its case over whether federal banking law overrides state laws requiring interest payments on mortgage escrow accounts.

## What happened

Flagstar Bank serviced mortgages for William Kivett and other California borrowers and did not pay interest on their escrow accounts, as California law requires. The borrowers sued, and a district court ruled for them, ordering payment of interest and about $9 million in damages. Relying on its earlier decision in a case called Lusnak, the Ninth Circuit Court of Appeals sided with the borrowers, holding that federal banking law does not override California's interest-on-escrow requirement. Flagstar's rehearing request was denied. Other circuits have split on similar state laws: the First Circuit also found no override, while the Second Circuit found one existed.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked whether a federal law governing national banks overrides state laws that require banks to pay interest on mortgage escrow accounts, and whether the Ninth Circuit used the right method for deciding that question after the Supreme Court's 2024 ruling in a related case.

## Where it stands

The petition has been distributed for conference twice, most recently for the conference of September 28, 2026, after full briefing including a reply from Flagstar and an amicus brief supporting it. The docket shows no decision yet on whether the Court will take the case.

## What each outcome would mean

Flagstar, which lost in the lower courts, is asking the Court to take up the case. If the Court grants review, it would hear full arguments and decide whether California's law is overridden by federal banking law, ruling for either Flagstar or Kivett and the other borrowers. If the Court denies review, the Ninth Circuit's decision favoring the borrowers stands, and Flagstar remains required to pay the interest and damages ordered below. A denial would not mean the Court agrees with the Ninth Circuit's reasoning, only that the decision remains in place.
