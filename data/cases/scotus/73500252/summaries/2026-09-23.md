## What happened

Flagstar Bank services home mortgages and holds borrowers' money in escrow accounts to pay taxes and insurance. California law requires lenders to pay at least 2% interest on that escrowed money. William Kivett and other borrowers sued, and courts found Flagstar liable for not paying this interest, awarding the class about $9 million and ordering Flagstar to comply going forward. Flagstar argued federal banking law overrides the state requirement. After the Supreme Court's 2024 decision in a similar case (Cantero) sent this case back for another look, the Ninth Circuit again ruled against Flagstar, relying on its own earlier precedent. A dissenting judge disagreed. Flagstar again asked the Supreme Court to step in, noting that other appeals courts have split on the same question.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked whether federal banking law overrides state laws, like California's, that require national banks to pay interest on mortgage escrow accounts. Flagstar argues these state rules improperly interfere with powers Congress gave national banks. The borrowers respond that the dispute is narrow, affects only a handful of pending cases, and has been overtaken by a new federal banking regulation addressing the same question going forward, so no further review is needed now.

## Where it stands

The petition has been distributed for conference twice, most recently for September 28, 2026, after full briefing including a reply from Flagstar and an amicus brief supporting review; the Court has not yet said whether it will hear the case.
