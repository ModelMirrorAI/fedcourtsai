Flagstar Bank asks the Court to decide whether federal banking law overrides state rules requiring interest payments on mortgage escrow accounts.

## What happened

Flagstar serviced mortgages for California borrowers, including William Kivett and others, and did not pay interest on their escrow accounts as a California law requires. Borrowers sued, and the district court ruled for them, awarding damages and ordering Flagstar to comply going forward. The Ninth Circuit affirmed, holding itself bound by its own earlier precedent finding no federal preemption of the state law, even after the Supreme Court's 2024 decision in a related case gave lower courts new guidance on how to analyze such preemption questions. A dissenting judge argued that guidance should have changed the result.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked whether a federal law governing national banks blocks enforcement of state laws, like California's, that require banks to pay interest on mortgage escrow accounts. The dispute centers on how lower courts should apply the Supreme Court's existing framework for deciding when state banking rules interfere too much with federally chartered banks' powers, and whether a circuit court can keep following its own older precedent when that framework does not clearly resolve the question.

## Where it stands

## What each outcome would mean
