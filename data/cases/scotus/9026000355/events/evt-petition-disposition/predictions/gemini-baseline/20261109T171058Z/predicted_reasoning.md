## Disposition and Route

- **Disposition (`disposition` = 0.06, `predicted_disposition` = `denied`):** The Court will deny the petition for a writ of certiorari after receiving Respondents' Brief in Opposition. While the high-profile backdrop of the July 19, 2024 global CrowdStrike Falcon outage and the superficial novelty of applying ADA preemption to a third-party cybersecurity vendor prompted a Call for a Response, the Brief in Opposition will expose decisive vehicle and split defects:
  1. The Fifth Circuit affirmed without opinion under Fifth Circuit Rule 47.6, leaving no appellate reasoning to review and establishing no binding circuit precedent.
  2. The longstanding circuit split between *Hodges v. Delta Airlines, Inc.*, 44 F.3d 334 (5th Cir. 1995) (en banc), and *Charas v. Trans World Airlines, Inc.*, 160 F.3d 1259 (9th Cir. 1998) (en banc), over peripheral onboard or terminal amenities (such as beverage carts or baggage handling) is not outcome-determinative here. Petitioners' alleged injuries stem directly from flight delays and cancellations—the core "schedules, origins and destinations of the point-to-point transportation of passengers" that qualify as a "service of an air carrier" even under the narrowest circuit definitions.
  3. No court of appeals has held that state-law tort claims by airline passengers seeking damages for flight delays and cancellations against an air carrier's technology or operational vendor escape preemption under 49 U.S.C. § 41713(b)(1).
- **Summary disposition vs. plenary review (`summary-disposition-route` = 0.08):** Conditional on the small (6.0%) probability that the Court grants review, plenary briefing and oral argument would be far more likely (92%) than a summary disposition in the certiorari order itself (8%). There is no pending Supreme Court merits case on ADA preemption for which this petition would be held for a post-decision GVR, making a summary vacate-and-remand (for instance, to require the Fifth Circuit to issue a reasoned opinion) only a remote possibility conditional on a grant.
- **Questions Presented if Granted (context-only):** If the Court were to grant certiorari, it would likely reformulate or narrow the Questions Presented to focus on the second question—whether 49 U.S.C. § 41713(b)(1) preempts state-law tort claims against a non-carrier third-party software vendor whose defective update disrupted air carrier flight operations—rather than taking up the broad, thirty-year-old *Hodges*/*Charas* definitional split in Question 1, which is not squarely implicated on facts involving cancelled and delayed point-to-point flights. If granted on the merits, the Court would likely evaluate whether state tort liability against an upstream enterprise vendor has a "forbidden significant effect" on air carrier prices, routes, or services under *Morales v. Trans World Airlines, Inc.*, 504 U.S. 374 (1992), and *Northwest, Inc. v. Ginsberg*, 572 U.S. 273 (2014).
