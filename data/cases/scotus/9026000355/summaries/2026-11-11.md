## In brief

Airline passengers ask the Court to decide whether a federal airline law shields a cybersecurity vendor from lawsuits over a software failure that disrupted flights.

## What happened

In July 2024, a defective software update from CrowdStrike caused a massive computer outage that disrupted airlines, leading to widespread flight delays and cancellations. Julio del Rio and several other passengers sued CrowdStrike for negligence and related claims over costs and harm they suffered from disrupted flights. A federal district court dismissed the suit, ruling that the Airline Deregulation Act, which bars state-law claims "related to" an airline's price, route, or service, preempted the claims. The Fifth Circuit affirmed without issuing a written opinion, and later denied rehearing.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked whether a federal law barring state lawsuits that interfere with airline pricing, routes, or services reaches claims against an outside software company that makes a product airlines use, where that company does not transport passengers, deal with them directly, or have any contract with them.

## Where it stands

The petition was distributed for the Court's November 6, 2026 conference. After that conference, the Court asked CrowdStrike to respond by November 18, 2026, and CrowdStrike has since asked for more time to file that response. The docket does not yet show the Court's decision on whether to hear the case.

## What each outcome would mean

Del Rio and the other passengers, who lost below, are asking the Court to take up the case and reverse the Fifth Circuit's ruling. If the Court grants the petition, it would hear full arguments on whether the preemption law covers CrowdStrike's conduct, with no outcome yet decided. If the Court denies the petition, the Fifth Circuit's ruling that the law bars the passengers' claims would stand, leaving CrowdStrike free of liability in this suit.
