## In brief

Air travelers whose flights were disrupted by the 2024 CrowdStrike software outage ask the Court to revive their lawsuit against CrowdStrike over its software defects.

## What happened

Julio del Rio and several other travelers had flights delayed or canceled after a faulty CrowdStrike software update caused a widespread computer outage in July 2024, which disrupted airline computer systems. They sued CrowdStrike in federal court for negligence and related claims, seeking damages for expenses like hotels, meals, and rebooking costs. The district court dismissed the case, ruling that a federal aviation law preempts their claims because the claims relate to airline services. The Fifth Circuit affirmed that decision without issuing a written opinion, and later denied rehearing.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked whether a federal law that blocks state-law lawsuits touching on airline prices, routes, or services also blocks claims against an outside software company whose product airlines used, where that company does not transport passengers, does not deal directly with passengers, and has no business relationship with them. The petition argues that federal appeals courts disagree about how broadly to define an airline "service" for these purposes, and that this case lets the Court resolve that disagreement.

## Where it stands

The petition was distributed for the Court's November 6, 2026 conference. The Court then asked CrowdStrike to respond, and CrowdStrike's deadline to do so has since been extended to December 18, 2026. No action on the petition itself is yet recorded.

## What each outcome would mean

Del Rio and the other travelers are asking the Court to overturn the Fifth Circuit's ruling in favor of CrowdStrike. If the Court grants the petition, it would take up the case and decide whether the law covers claims like theirs, with no outcome yet determined. If it denies the petition, the Fifth Circuit's ruling stands, leaving their claims against CrowdStrike barred. A grant would not itself decide who wins; it would simply mean the issue gets full briefing and argument.
