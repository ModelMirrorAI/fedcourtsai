## In brief

Air travelers whose flights were disrupted in the 2024 CrowdStrike software outage ask the Court to revive their lawsuit against the cybersecurity company.

## What happened

In July 2024, a defective software update from CrowdStrike caused widespread computer system failures, leading airlines to delay or cancel many flights. A group of affected passengers sued CrowdStrike in federal court, raising state-law claims including negligence and public nuisance over costs and injuries they say resulted from the disruptions. The district court dismissed the suit, ruling that a federal aviation law preempts, or overrides, such state-law claims because they relate to airline services. The Fifth Circuit Court of Appeals affirmed that dismissal without issuing a written opinion, and later denied rehearing.

## What the Court is being asked

The federal aviation law bars state-law claims connected to an airline's prices, routes, or services. The Court is asked whether that bar also covers claims against an outside software company that sold a product to airlines, where the company does not transport passengers, does not deal directly with passengers, and has no business relationship with them.

## Where it stands

The petition was distributed for the Court's November 6, 2026 conference. After that conference, the Court asked CrowdStrike to respond to the petition, with a response due November 18, 2026; the docket shows no further action since.

## What each outcome would mean

The travelers are asking the Court to overturn the Fifth Circuit's ruling, which favored CrowdStrike. If the Court grants the petition, it would hear full arguments on whether the aviation law shields a third-party software vendor from these claims, without yet deciding who wins. If it denies the petition, the Fifth Circuit's decision stands, leaving the travelers' lawsuit against CrowdStrike dismissed.
