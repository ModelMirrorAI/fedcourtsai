## In brief

Kyle Wagner asked the Supreme Court to pause a federal appeals court's order detaining him before trial while he pursued further review; the Court denied that request.

## What happened

Kyle Wagner was charged with cyberstalking and making interstate threats after online exchanges connected to protests against immigration enforcement in Minneapolis. A magistrate judge ordered him detained, but a district court later released him on conditions, including limits on internet use and movement. The government appealed, and a divided panel of the Sixth Circuit reversed, ruling that Wagner should be detained pending trial. Wagner asked the Sixth Circuit to pause that ruling while he sought Supreme Court review; it refused. He then asked the Supreme Court to pause the ruling and, separately, filed a petition asking the Court to take up his case.

## What the Court is being asked

Wagner asked the Court to pause his detention while his broader appeal moves forward. The underlying dispute is about how closely appeals courts should examine a trial judge's decision to release or detain someone before trial: whether appeals courts must largely accept the trial judge's judgment, or may instead reexamine the evidence themselves and reach their own conclusion.

## Where it stands

A single justice asked the government to respond to Wagner's request, and after receiving a response and reply, referred the application to the full Court. The Court denied the request for a pause.

## What each outcome would mean

Wagner, the applicant, asked the Court to pause the Sixth Circuit's order detaining him while his case continued. Had the Court granted the pause, Wagner would have been released under the district court's conditions while his petition for review proceeded. By denying it, the Court leaves the Sixth Circuit's ruling in effect, meaning Wagner remains detained pending trial. This order does not decide the broader legal question Wagner raised or resolve his separate petition asking the Court to hear his case.
