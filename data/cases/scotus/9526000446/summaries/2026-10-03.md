## In brief

Kyle Wagner asks the Court to pause a federal appeals court order detaining him before trial while he seeks review of that order.

## What happened

Kyle Wagner was charged with cyberstalking and making interstate threats after an online dispute with another social media user. A federal district court held a two-day hearing and ordered him released before trial under conditions limiting his travel and internet use, finding he was not a danger given his lack of violent history. The government appealed, and a divided Sixth Circuit panel reviewed the record itself and reversed, ordering Wagner detained. Wagner asked the Sixth Circuit to pause its own order while he sought further review; a single judge there refused. He then filed both a petition for certiorari and this application asking the Supreme Court to pause the detention order.

## What the Court is being asked

Wagner's application asks the Court to pause his detention while his broader request for review goes forward. That broader request concerns how closely appeals courts should examine a trial judge's decision to detain or release someone before trial: whether appeals courts must largely defer to the trial judge's judgment, or may instead re-examine the evidence themselves and reach their own conclusion.

## Where it stands

The application for a stay was submitted to Justice Kavanaugh, the justice who handles emergency matters from the Sixth Circuit. He has asked the government to respond by October 8, 2026; the Court has not yet ruled on the request.

## What each outcome would mean

Wagner, the applicant, asks the Court to pause the Sixth Circuit's order detaining him. If the Court grants the stay, Wagner would be released under the conditions the district court had set while his case continues, and the detention order would not take effect for now. If the Court denies the stay, Wagner would remain detained while his petition for review proceeds separately. Either way, this ruling would be temporary and would not resolve his underlying petition asking the Court to decide the legal question itself.
