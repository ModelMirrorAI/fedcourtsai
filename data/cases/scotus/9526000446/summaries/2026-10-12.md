## In brief

A pretrial detainee asks the Court to pause an appeals court order detaining him while he pursues review of which standard appellate courts should use for release decisions.

## What happened

Kyle Wagner was charged in Michigan federal court with cyberstalking and making interstate threats after an online dispute with another social-media user. A magistrate judge ordered him detained; a district court later released him on conditions like GPS monitoring and internet restrictions. The government appealed, and a divided Sixth Circuit panel reversed, reviewing the district court's decision without deference and ordering Wagner detained pending trial. Wagner asked the Sixth Circuit to pause that ruling; a single judge refused. Wagner then asked the Supreme Court to stay the Sixth Circuit's order while it considers his separately filed petition asking the Court to take up his case.

## What the Court is being asked

Wagner's underlying petition asks the Court to decide how closely appeals courts should examine a trial judge's decision to detain or release a defendant before trial — whether appeals courts should reexamine that decision fresh or instead defer to the trial judge's judgment. The immediate request before the Court is narrower: whether to temporarily block Wagner's detention while that larger question is considered, given his claim that circuit courts disagree on the issue and that the Sixth Circuit applied the wrong approach.

## Where it stands

The application for a stay has been fully briefed: Wagner filed it, the government (as respondent) filed an opposition, and Wagner filed a reply. The docket does not show that the Court has yet ruled on the stay application.

## What each outcome would mean

Wagner, the applicant, is asking the Court to pause his detention. If granted, the stay would release Wagner from custody while his certiorari petition is considered, though it would not decide the underlying legal question. If denied, Wagner would remain detained under the Sixth Circuit's order while his petition proceeds through the normal process, and his case would continue in the lower courts toward trial. Either way, the stay decision itself would not resolve whether the Sixth Circuit used the correct standard of review.
