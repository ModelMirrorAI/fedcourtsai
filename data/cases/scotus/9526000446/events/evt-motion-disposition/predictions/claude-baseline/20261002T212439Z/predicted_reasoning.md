# Forecast: what the Court will do with Application 26A446 (Wagner v. United States)

**Disposition.** The application for a stay of the Sixth Circuit's judgment will be
**denied**. P(the disposing entry reads as an unqualified grant) = 0.12. The most
likely form is "Application (26A446) denied by the Court" after a referral, entered
without opinion; a short statement respecting the denial from one or two Justices
(noting that the standard-of-review question is better addressed on the petition in
No. 26-391) is possible but less likely than a bare denial. The grant arm, if it comes,
is a stay of the Sixth Circuit's judgment pending disposition of the petition, which
would reinstate the district court's conditional release order; a Nken-style
construction of the application as a petition for certiorari, with the stay granted
and the case set for expedited argument, is the least likely route of all.

**Response requested.** Yes, and already done: Justice Kavanaugh called for a
response on October 2, 2026, due October 8. The record I was provisioned (frozen at
the September 29 submission entry) shows no request, so the increment from that
state resolves true; I put it at 0.96 rather than 1.0 only because the entry was
observed on the linked petition docket (No. 26-391) and had not yet propagated to the
application docket page when I read it.

**Referral to the full Court.** Likely. An application on which a Circuit Justice has
called for a response, filed by experienced Supreme Court counsel on an acknowledged
circuit split, is ordinarily referred to the Conference rather than decided in
chambers; the alternative is a single-Justice denial after the response arrives.
P(referred after prediction time) = 0.72.

**Amicus filings.** A coin flip. The record shows zero amicus entries. The case sits
at the intersection of pretrial detention, speech-based dangerousness findings, and
immigration-enforcement politics, so criminal-defense and civil-liberties
organizations have reason to file, and the six-day response window plus a reply period
leaves room to do so. Against that, amici on a stay application often hold their
briefs for the petition docket, where they would not count toward this application's
entries. P(the count rises above 0) = 0.50.

**Timing.** The response is due October 8; expect a reply by about October 10 and a
disposition between roughly October 10 and October 24, most likely in the week of
October 13. The Court may act on the applicant's motion to expedite the petition in
No. 26-391 at the same time, and a denial of the stay paired with an expedited
schedule for the petition is the single most probable overall shape.

**Grounds, conditionally.** The stay standard asks for a reasonable probability of
certiorari, a fair prospect of reversal, and irreparable harm. The first factor is
the applicant's strongest: the 5-4-3 split is real, acknowledged by the Sixth
Circuit's own stay-denial order, and the Court has taken standard-of-review questions
regularly. Denial is nonetheless the forecast because the Court almost never uses the
stay power to release a federal pretrial detainee over the government's dangerousness
case, because the government's response will stress the panel's finding that no
conditions mitigate the risk, because the equities in a speech-and-threats prosecution
are contested rather than one-sided, and because the Court can reach the split through
the petition without releasing the applicant in the interim. If the stay is granted,
the likely ground is that the standard-of-review question is cert-worthy and the
district court's detailed findings on conditions make a fair prospect of reversal, with
the liberty interest supplying irreparable harm.
