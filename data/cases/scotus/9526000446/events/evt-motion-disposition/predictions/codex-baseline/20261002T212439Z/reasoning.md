## Anchor and adjustments

The committed `metrics/statpack.md` interim section supplies the relevant anchor. For application Term 2026 the eligible window is 2016–2025. Only 2024 and 2025 contribute resolved substantive applications: **(14 + 17) / (70 + 226) = 31/296 = 10.47%**. That exceeds the 50-case floor. I exclude the 2026 row and do not substitute the pack-wide 37/359 rate or a cert band rate. This is the committed pack consulted in this run, not a freshly queried corpus estimate. Coverage is uneven: 2024 has 972 unparsed applications out of 1,297, versus zero unparsed out of 1,467 in 2025. The denominator includes only machine-matched substantive resolutions. Selection into prediction also differs from that raw cohort. Its escalation counts include pending cases and are not arrival-conditioned transition rates.

I move above 10.47% because the application presents more than an unsupported request for release. It identifies an acknowledged disagreement over appellate review of Bail Reform Act detention decisions, a divided panel, and a district court that conducted hearings and imposed detailed release conditions. The applicant's argument that a fact-intensive predictive judgment warrants appellate deference is substantial. Ongoing confinement makes the asserted irreparable injury concrete. See application printed pages 1–5, 11–14, 23–30, and 31–34.

Nevertheless, denial remains substantially more likely. A review-worthy disagreement does not itself establish a sufficiently strong prospect of reversal and entitlement to immediate release. As the application describes it, the panel already distinguishes factual findings reviewed for clear error from the ultimate detention determination reviewed independently. Whether the different formulations actually change this result remains disputed: the application reports that the lower-court stay order questioned outcome-determinacy. The Court could regard this as an unsuitable vehicle for resolving that distinction on an emergency schedule.

Public safety is a separate obstacle. Even the applicant's presentation acknowledges inflammatory posts and the majority's concern that release restrictions could be evaded. The district court's contrary assessment and reported compliance on bond matter, but do not eliminate that conflict. I do not adopt the application's assertion that release can cause no public harm. Its account of an upcoming November 3 trial and a stipulated additional continuance also creates a short-lived-order and scheduling problem, not a guaranteed path to expedited Supreme Court merits review. Those considerations limit the upward adjustment to 20%, rather than a near-even chance. These are judgmental adjustments, not fitted likelihood ratios.
