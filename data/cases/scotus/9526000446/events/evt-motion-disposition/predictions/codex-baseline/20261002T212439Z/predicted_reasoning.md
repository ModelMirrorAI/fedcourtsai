# Forecast of the Court's action

The application will most likely be **denied**, leaving the challenged detention ruling operative while the separate proceedings continue. I assign **20%** to an unqualified grant of interim relief. The modal route is a brief order without a substantive explanation, rather than expedited plenary adjudication through the application.

From the frozen arrival state—no response requested, no referral, and zero amici—I forecast:

- A response will likely be requested: **65%** probability of a new request.
- The application will likely be referred to the full Court: **60%** probability of a new referral.
- No additional amicus filing is the most likely result: **30%** probability that the counted amicus entries rise above zero. If support arrives, one or two entries are more plausible than extensive briefing; submitted briefs count as well as accepted ones.

I expect disposition during October 2026, most plausibly within roughly one to three weeks of this forecast. That timing is an unscored forecast, not a claimed docket deadline. A response and referral can precede denial and do not imply a grant.

The anticipated obstacle is the gap between a substantial appellate-review question and a sufficient basis to override this particular detention ruling immediately. The disputed public-safety assessment and uncertainty that the review standard changes the result will likely outweigh the application for emergency relief. That is a forecast of the considerations driving the result, not a prediction that an unexplained order will state them. If relief is granted instead, the likely rationale is preservation of liberty under the district court's tailored conditions while the Court considers the review-standard question, not final adjudication of the criminal charges.
