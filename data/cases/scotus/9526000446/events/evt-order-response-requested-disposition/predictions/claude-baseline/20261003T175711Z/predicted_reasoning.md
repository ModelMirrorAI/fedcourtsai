# Forecast: what the Court will do with application 26A446 (Wagner v. United States)

**Where the record stands at this moment.** The application for a stay of the
Sixth Circuit's pretrial-detention order was submitted to Justice Kavanaugh on
September 29, 2026. On October 2 he called for a response from the Solicitor
General, due 4 p.m. on October 8. No referral to the full Court is on the docket
yet, and no amicus brief has been filed. A separate cert petition (No. 26-391,
docketed September 22) is linked to the application.

**Disposition: denied, by the full Court, without a stay of any kind.** The
disposing entry will read as a denial of the application presented to Justice
Kavanaugh and by him referred to the Court. P(unqualified grant) = 0.06. A
partial grant is not a realistic shape here: the relief sought is binary
(release on the district court's conditions, or continued detention), so the
realistic alternatives to a flat denial are a deferral of the application
pending the Court's consideration of No. 26-391 (which would delay rather than
change the disposition) or, at the margin, a grant of certiorari on the
petition with the stay still denied.

**Referral.** The Circuit Justice will refer the application to the full Court
rather than act alone: P(referral after prediction time) = 0.82. A requested
response is the step that precedes referral in nearly every counseled
substantive application of the current era, and a single-Justice denial of a
represented liberty-interest application after calling for the government's
views would be out of pattern for this Justice.

**Response requested.** Already on the record (October 2), so this claim is
vacuous for this cell. Stated at 0.97 as the counterfactual: a counseled
application from a detained defendant with a docketed cert petition and an
acknowledged circuit split draws a response call almost without exception.

**Amicus briefs.** The record shows zero. P(the count rises above zero) = 0.30.
The case sits at the intersection of pretrial detention and political speech
about immigration enforcement, which is the profile that draws civil-liberties
and criminal-defense amici (NACDL, ACLU, FIRE-type filers). Against that, the
window is short: the response is due October 8 and the Court will likely act
within one to two weeks after, and amicus participation at the application stage
remains the exception rather than the rule.

**Timing.** Disposition most likely between October 9 and October 24, 2026, by
an order of the full Court posted to the docket (not an order-list item). If the
government's response asks the Court to hold the application for the petition,
the order could slip to the petition's first conference, which would fall in
November.

**Separate writings on denial.** Modest chance of a statement or noted dissent
from one or more of Justices Sotomayor, Kagan, or Jackson, on the strength of
the liberty interest and the dissent below; more likely the denial is unadorned.
Not a scored claim at this stage.

**The ask to construe the application as a petition and set it for argument.**
The Court will not take that route. A Nken-style conversion of a stay
application into a merits vehicle is reserved for cases where the government
agrees or the question cannot otherwise be reached; here a conventional petition
is already on file.

**Conditional merits note.** If the Court ever reaches the question (through
No. 26-391, not this application), the deferential camp's framework (Pierce,
Highmark, U.S. Bank v. Village at Lakeridge) gives the applicant the better of
the doctrinal argument, and the panel dissent below supplies a ready-made
opinion. That bears on the "fair prospect of reversal" prong but does not move
the stay forecast much, because the stay turns on the Court's unwillingness to
order a detainee released on the emergency docket over a court of appeals'
dangerousness finding.
