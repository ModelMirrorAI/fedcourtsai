# Forecast: Wagner v. United States, No. 26A446 (stay application, response filed)

**Disposition.** The application will be **denied**. Wagner asks the Court to stay the Sixth Circuit's judgment reversing his pretrial release order, pending his cert petition (No. 26-391) on the appellate standard of review for Bail Reform Act detention decisions. The Court will not find a reasonable probability of certiorari: the asserted three-way circuit split is decades old, the Court denied review of the same question in Gray v. United States (No. 22-5113) in 2022, and the Solicitor General's opposition presses two vehicle problems the Court takes seriously on the emergency docket, that the panel's result would likely be the same under any standard, and that the detention challenge becomes moot when the January 12, 2027 trial ends. On the equities, the record below (calls for armed resistance to ICE, the Signal exchange about a former official, the fund-raising to "evade" law enforcement, prior failures to appear, and a second indictment in the District of Minnesota) gives the Court every reason to leave a pretrial detention order in place. An unqualified grant is a remote outcome; a partial or conditional order (for example, expediting the petition without staying the judgment) would resolve as ungranted under the interim vocabulary and is itself unlikely.

**Referral to the full Court.** Justice Kavanaugh called for a response, which is the usual prelude to referring an application to the Conference rather than acting alone. I expect the application to be **referred to the Court** and denied by the Court, most likely in a short order with no noted dissent. The alternative, a single-Justice denial by the Circuit Justice after reading the response, is a real possibility given how weak the cert-likelihood showing is, so the referral is likely but not certain.

**Further response.** The record already shows the response requested (October 2) and filed (October 8). No further call for a response is expected; at most a reply from the applicant, which is not a response call.

**Amicus briefs.** The docket shows no amicus entry. A stay application on a standard-of-review question, disposed of within days to a few weeks of the response, is unlikely to draw amici on the application docket itself, even though the underlying prosecution is politically salient. Any criminal-defense amicus support is more likely to appear on the cert docket (26-391), which does not count here. Expect the count to stay at zero.

**Timing.** The response was filed October 8 and the Court sits in October; I expect disposition within roughly one to three weeks, by a referred-and-denied order, with some chance the Court simply holds the application and disposes of it alongside the petition after the November 6 conference. The alternative motion to expedite consideration of the petition on 26-391 is also likely to be denied or left unacted on, since the petition is already distributed for the November 6 conference.

**Conditional merits content.** If, against expectation, the Court granted the stay, it would signal that at least five Justices see the standard-of-review question as cert-worthy and the Sixth Circuit's de novo approach as likely wrong under the Pierce v. Underwood / Highmark framework the application invokes. The more plausible route to any relief is not a stay but a grant of certiorari on the petition at a later conference, after which a stay or expedited argument would be revisited; nothing on this record makes that likely before trial moots the question.
