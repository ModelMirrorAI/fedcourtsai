## In brief

A policyholder asks the Court to revive her hurricane-damage insurance suit after lower courts excluded her expert testimony and ruled against her.

## What happened

Diana Stevens-Reynolds sued Wilshire Insurance Company and IAT Insurance Group over disputed payments for hurricane damage to her rental properties and a motel in Lake Charles, Louisiana. A federal district court granted Wilshire summary judgment on two issues, excluded her expert testimony as a discovery sanction, and after a bench trial found Wilshire had not acted in bad faith. The Fifth Circuit affirmed every ruling, and that court denied rehearing. Stevens-Reynolds, representing herself, now asks the Supreme Court to take the case.

## What the Court is being asked

The petition raises three questions: whether courts can exclude a party's expert testimony as a discovery sanction without first finding the violation was willful or made in bad faith, or considering lighter penalties; whether a court can grant summary judgment on technical issues that needed expert testimony after excluding that testimony; and whether courts must resolve conflicting evidence about whether discovery materials were actually provided before imposing a case-ending sanction. The petition also argues that federal courts applied these rules in a way that conflicts with how other appeals courts handle similar sanctions.

## Where it stands

The petition and Wilshire's brief opposing it have both been filed, and the case was distributed for the Court's conference on November 6, 2026. No decision on whether the Court will hear the case has yet been recorded.

## What each outcome would mean

Stevens-Reynolds is asking the Court to take up her case. If the Court grants the petition, it will hear full arguments on these discovery and due-process questions and decide them, without yet deciding who wins. If the Court denies the petition, the Fifth Circuit's ruling stays in place, meaning the summary judgments against Stevens-Reynolds, the exclusion of her expert testimony, and the trial finding of no bad faith by Wilshire all remain final.
