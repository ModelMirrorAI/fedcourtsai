## In brief

A policyholder asks the Court to review a lower court's decision upholding the exclusion of her expert testimony in her hurricane-damage insurance dispute.

## What happened

Diana Stevens-Reynolds sued her insurer, Wilshire Insurance Company, and IAT Insurance Group, claiming they underpaid and delayed insurance proceeds for hurricane damage to her rental properties and motel in Lake Charles, Louisiana. The district court granted summary judgment to Wilshire on how losses should be valued and on a roof exclusion barring motel claims, excluded Stevens-Reynolds's expert testimony for failing to meet disclosure deadlines, and after a trial found no bad faith by Wilshire. The United States Court of Appeals for the Fifth Circuit affirmed on all issues, and later denied rehearing.

## What the Court is being asked

Stevens-Reynolds asks the Court to decide whether a court may exclude a party's expert testimony as a penalty for discovery problems without first deciding whether the party acted willfully or in bad faith, whether less severe penalties could work instead, and whether courts must investigate conflicting evidence about discovery compliance before imposing such a penalty. She contends that courts of appeals disagree on what rules apply when expert exclusion effectively decides the outcome of a claim.

## Where it stands

The case has not yet been scheduled for the Court's consideration. After the respondents filed a brief opposing review and Stevens-Reynolds replied, the petition was distributed for the Court's conference on November 6, 2026, and the docket shows no action on it since.

## What each outcome would mean

Stevens-Reynolds, as petitioner, asked the Court to take up her case. If the Court grants her petition, it will hear the case fully and decide the question about expert-testimony exclusion; a grant would not itself decide who wins. If the Court denies the petition, the Fifth Circuit's decision affirming judgment for Wilshire and IAT stands, and Stevens-Reynolds's lawsuit remains dismissed.
