## In brief

The Court declined to hear a Louisiana policyholder's bid to revive her hurricane-damage insurance lawsuit after lower courts ruled against her.

## What happened

Diana Stevens-Reynolds sued Wilshire Insurance Company and IAT Insurance Group over damage to her rental properties and a motel caused by Hurricanes Laura and Delta. A federal district court ruled against her on several issues before trial, excluded her expert witness for failing to make required disclosures, and after a trial found Wilshire had not acted in bad faith in handling her claims. The United States Court of Appeals for the Fifth Circuit affirmed. Stevens-Reynolds then asked the Supreme Court to take up her case.

## What the Court is being asked

Stevens-Reynolds asks the Court to decide whether courts can exclude a party's expert evidence over a discovery dispute without first deciding whether the violation was intentional or in bad faith, and without considering lighter penalties; whether a court can later grant judgment against a party on technical issues that the excluded expert evidence was needed to address; and whether courts must resolve conflicting evidence about whether required materials were actually turned over before imposing such a sanction. Wilshire responds that these arguments were not properly raised in the lower courts and that the rulings were correct.

## Where it stands

The petition was distributed for the Court's conference on November 6, 2026, and on November 9, 2026, the Court denied the petition.

## What each outcome would mean

Stevens-Reynolds asked the Court to take up her case and reverse the Fifth Circuit's decision, which had upheld rulings in Wilshire's favor. With the petition denied, the Fifth Circuit's decision stands, leaving in place the exclusion of her expert evidence, the dismissal of her motel-related claims, and the trial court's finding that Wilshire did not act in bad faith. The denial does not mean the Supreme Court agreed with the lower courts' reasoning, only that it will not review the case further.
