## In brief

Descendants of a law school's 1878 donor ask the Court to revive their claim that California's renaming of the school broke a contract and punished them.

## What happened

In 1878 California passed a law letting Serranus Hastings found a public law school, naming it after him and reserving a board seat for his heirs, in exchange for his payment of $100,000. In 2022 the legislature passed a law finding that Hastings had financed violence against Native Americans, renaming the school and ending the reserved board seat. Hastings's descendants and an alumni group sued the state and the school's leaders, arguing this broke a contract and amounted to unconstitutional punishment without a trial. California's trial court and court of appeal dismissed the suit, and the state's supreme court declined further review.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked whether an old state law establishing a public institution's name and leadership structure, in return for a payment, created binding contractual promises that a later legislature cannot undo, and separately, whether a law that brands someone's past conduct as wrongdoing and strips benefits from him and his descendants singles them out for punishment without a trial in a way the Constitution forbids.

## Where it stands

The petition has been distributed for conference twice, most recently for October 16, 2026, after the state and the school's leaders filed briefs opposing review and the petitioners replied; the docket shows no decision yet on whether the Court will hear the case.

## What each outcome would mean

The Hastings descendants and alumni group, as petitioners, are asking the Court to take up the case. If the Court grants review, it would hear full arguments and decide these contract and punishment questions itself, without yet determining who wins. If the Court denies review, the California Court of Appeal's decision rejecting the petitioners' claims and upholding the renaming law stands, leaving the state and school officials's win in place.
