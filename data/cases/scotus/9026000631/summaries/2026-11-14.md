## In brief

A veteran's widow asks the Court to let survivors challenge erroneous VA benefit decisions made during the veteran's lifetime.

## What happened

Gerald Robison received VA pension benefits instead of service-connected disability benefits in 1999, despite being found 90% disabled. He died in 2007. In 2020, his widow, Linda Kay Robison, asked the VA to revise that decision, claiming it contained a clear and unmistakable error. The Board of Veterans' Appeals ruled she lacked standing to raise such a claim because she, not her husband, was not the original claimant. The Veterans Court affirmed, and the Federal Circuit dismissed her further appeal, concluding it lacked jurisdiction because her argument only challenged how existing precedent applied to her case rather than raising a new legal question. The Federal Circuit also denied rehearing.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked whether two veterans-benefits statutes, read together, allow a surviving spouse to seek correction of an erroneous VA decision made during the veteran's lifetime, in order to recover benefits that were due but unpaid when the veteran died, or whether existing circuit precedent correctly bars survivors from raising such claims at all.

## Where it stands

The petition for certiorari was filed on November 11, 2026, and a response is due December 14, 2026. The Court has not yet acted on it.

## What each outcome would mean

Linda Kay Robison, the widow, is asking the Court to take up her case and reject the lower court's rule. If the Court grants the petition, it will hear the case in full and decide whether survivors may raise this kind of claim, without yet deciding who wins. If it denies the petition, the Federal Circuit's dismissal stands, leaving in place the rule that bars Robison and similarly situated survivors from seeking this kind of correction, and leaving the Secretary of Veterans Affairs's position undisturbed.
