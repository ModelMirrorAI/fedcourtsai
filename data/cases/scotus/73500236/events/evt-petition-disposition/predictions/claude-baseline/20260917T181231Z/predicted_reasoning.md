# Forecast: what the Court will do with No. 25-1334

**Disposition.** The petition will be denied on the order list following the September 28, 2026 Long Conference, without comment. P(any grant) = 0.012.

**Relists.** The docket shows exactly one distribution (July 1, 2026, for the conference of 9/28/2026); the petition has never been relisted. I expect no further distribution: no call for a response, no reschedule, no relist. P(at least one more distribution) = 0.12. The one realistic path to a second distribution is a call for a response, because both respondents waived; if that happens the petition would be redistributed after the brief in opposition arrives, most likely for a November or December 2026 conference, and would still very probably be denied then.

**CVSG.** None. No federal party, no federal statute, and no federal program is implicated; the questions concern a Colorado metropolitan district's mill levy and a state-law unjust-enrichment claim. P(CVSG after prediction time) = 0.01.

**Which question, if any.** If the Court took anything, it would be a reformulated version of question 1 only: whether the constitutional bar on special assessments that exceed the benefit conferred rests on the Takings Clause (and so turns on the owner's investment-backed expectations at acquisition) or on due process. Question 2, the "money is gone" limit on unjust-enrichment recovery against a municipality, is a state-law question dressed in nineteenth-century federal common-law citations, and the Court would not take it as written.

**Summary disposition.** Conditional on a grant, plenary review is the likelier route: there is no intervening decision of this Court to GVR in light of, and the petition asks for a legal clarification rather than a correction of a plain error. P(cert-order disposition | grant) = 0.25.

**Dissent from denial.** None expected. The petition alleges no split, the decision below is unpublished, and the Justices who write on takings questions have no natural hook in a case where the state court says its holding rested on state due-process doctrine. P(any dissent or statement respecting denial | denial) = 0.02.

**Conditional merits.** If granted on question 1, the likely ground would be that the Colorado court applied a federal constitutional rule (Norwood v. Baker) without asking whether the assessment actually confiscated anything from a purchaser who priced it in; a reversal or vacatur would be the likelier judgment. This is entirely conditional on a grant I do not expect.
