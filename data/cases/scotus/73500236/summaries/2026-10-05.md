## In brief

The Court declined to hear bondholders' challenge to a Colorado ruling that freed a special taxing district from repaying bonds secured by a disputed land assessment.

## What happened

Colorado Bondshares and UMB Bank held bonds issued by Marin Metropolitan District, secured by a special property assessment on land now owned by Century at Landmark, LLC. Century bought the land knowing about the assessment and about litigation suggesting the land received no benefit from the bond-funded improvements. The district and Century sued, and a Colorado trial court ruled the assessment could not be imposed and that the bondholders could not recover the money through an unjust-enrichment claim because the district had already spent it. The Colorado Court of Appeals affirmed, and the Colorado Supreme Court denied further review.

## What the Court is being asked

The bondholders ask the Court to decide two things: first, whether a landowner who bought property already knowing about an assessment and the lack of benefit to the land can still use constitutional due-process protections to avoid paying it; and second, whether a government body that owes money for an invalid arrangement can avoid repaying it simply by having already spent the funds.

## Where it stands

After the respondents waived their right to respond, the petition was distributed for the Court's September 28, 2026 conference. The Court denied the petition on October 5, 2026.

## What each outcome would mean

Colorado Bondshares and UMB Bank, as petitioners, asked the Court to take up the case. By denying the petition, the Court leaves the Colorado Court of Appeals' decision in place: the special assessment cannot be imposed on Century's land, and the bondholders cannot recover the unpaid bond money from the district through an unjust-enrichment claim. The denial does not mean the Court agreed with that outcome, only that the lower court's ruling stands and the case is not reviewed further.
