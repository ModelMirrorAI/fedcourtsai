## In brief

A bondholder's bid to revive its claim that a Colorado special district must repay its bonds was denied by the Supreme Court.

## What happened

Colorado Bondshares and UMB Bank bought bonds from Marin Metropolitan District, secured by a special tax assessment on district land. Century at Landmark later bought the taxed parcel knowing about the assessment and a related lawsuit, then joined the district in suing to invalidate the bonds. A Colorado trial court and the Colorado Court of Appeals agreed, ruling that charging Century the assessment would violate due process because the land had not been benefited by the bond-funded improvements, and that the district need not repay the bondholders under an unjust-enrichment theory because it had already spent the money. The Colorado Supreme Court declined to review that decision.

## What the Court is being asked

Colorado Bondshares asks the Court to decide two questions. First, whether the lower court wrongly read constitutional case law as barring the special assessment here, even though Century bought the land knowing about the assessment and the lack of benefit. Second, whether the lower court wrongly read precedent as excusing the district from repaying the money it borrowed, simply because the district says it already spent the money.

## Where it stands

After an extension of time to file, Colorado Bondshares filed its petition on May 27, 2026. Both respondents waived their right to respond. The petition was distributed for the Court's September 28, 2026 conference, and on October 5, 2026, the Court denied it.

## What each outcome would mean

Colorado Bondshares and UMB Bank, the bondholders, asked the Court to take the case and overturn the Colorado Court of Appeals' ruling. By denying the petition, the Court leaves that ruling in place: the district is not required to impose the assessment on Century or to repay the bondholders under an unjust-enrichment theory. The denial does not mean the Court agreed with the lower court's reasoning, only that the case will not be reviewed, leaving Century and the district's win intact.
