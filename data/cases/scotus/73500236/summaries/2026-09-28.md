## In brief

Bondholders ask the Court to revive their claim that a Colorado special district must still repay bonds despite a state court ruling shielding a landowner from the assessment funding repayment.

## What happened

Colorado Bondshares and UMB Bank, trustee for bonds issued by Marin Metropolitan District, sued to enforce repayment through a special assessment on land now owned by Century at Landmark, LLC, which controls the District. Colorado courts found that imposing the assessment on Century would violate due process, since Century's land was never benefited by the improvements the bonds financed, and that Bondshares could not recover under an unjust-enrichment theory because the District had already spent the money. The Colorado Court of Appeals affirmed a trial court ruling against the bondholders, and the state supreme court declined further review.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked whether the lower court wrongly read Supreme Court precedent in ruling that landowners who buy property already knowing about an assessment and knowing it won't benefit them can still avoid paying it on due-process grounds. The Court is also asked whether the lower court wrongly read precedent in ruling that a lender cannot recover money it lent to a government body, on unjust-enrichment grounds, once that body has spent the funds.

## Where it stands

The petition was filed after an extension of time, both respondents waived their right to formally respond, and the case was distributed for the Court's September 28, 2026 conference, with no action on it recorded since.

## What each outcome would mean

Colorado Bondshares and UMB Bank, as petitioners, are asking the Court to take up the case. If the Court grants review, it will hear full arguments and decide the constitutional and equitable questions itself, deciding nothing yet about who wins. If the Court denies review, the Colorado Court of Appeals ruling stands, leaving Century free of the assessment and the District under no obligation to repay the bonds through that means, without the Supreme Court ruling on whether that result was correct.
