## What happened

Colorado Bondshares, a mutual fund, and UMB Bank bought bonds issued by Marin Metropolitan District to fund public infrastructure. Repayment was to come from a special tax levy on landowners in the district. Century at Landmark, LLC later bought the only remaining taxed parcel, reportedly knowing about the levy and about a lack of benefit to the land, and then gained control of the district's board. Century and the district sued to avoid repaying the bonds. Colorado's trial court and the Colorado Court of Appeals agreed, holding that charging Century would violate constitutional due process protections and that the district could keep the money already spent. The Colorado Supreme Court declined further review.

## What the Court is being asked

The bondholders ask the Court to review two rulings. First, whether a government body can be excused from repaying investors, on constitutional fairness grounds, when the person now objecting to paying the special charge bought the land already knowing about the charge and knowing the land wouldn't benefit from the improvements it funded. Second, whether a lender who provided money to a government under an agreement later found invalid can be denied repayment simply because the government says it already spent the money.

## Where it stands

The petition was filed in May 2026 after a court-approved extension. Both respondents waived their right to file a response, and the case was distributed for the Court's conference on September 28, 2026, where the Justices will decide whether to take up the case.
