# Forecast — Reguli v. Hetzel, No. 26-501 (arrival moment)

**Disposition: denied.** The Court will deny the petition without noted
dissent, most likely at the first conference it is distributed for.

- **Distributions.** The docket shows **zero** distributions (filed Oct 9,
  docketed Oct 15, 2026; response due Nov 16, 2026). I expect the petition to
  be distributed for conference once — most likely after the respondents waive
  a response, for a late-November or December 2026 conference — and denied
  there with no relist. A call for a response is possible but unlikely; if one
  issues, the first distribution slips into early 2027, still ending in denial.
- **CVSG.** None. Every respondent is a state or local official or a
  municipality, and no federal program or federal-government interest is at
  stake, so the Solicitor General's views will not be invited.
- **Which question, if granted.** If the Court took anything it would be a
  reformulated version of QP 1: whether an officer's or prosecutor's
  "reasonable mistake of law" under *Heien v. North Carolina* can supply
  probable cause (or qualified immunity) for a malicious-prosecution claim
  under *Chiaverini*'s charge-specific framework. QP 2 (Buckley's functional
  line for absolute prosecutorial immunity when drafting an indictment) is
  well-trodden and would be left behind; QP 3 (the panel's sua sponte
  rationale at Rule 12) is a case-management complaint the Court would not take.
- **Summary route.** Conditional on any grant, plenary review is far likelier
  than a summary disposition; there is no pending decision this Term that
  would naturally ground a GVR.
- **Dissent from denial.** None expected. No Justice has made the
  Heien/malicious-prosecution intersection a recurring theme, and the posture
  (a published but fact-specific Sixth Circuit immunity ruling) is not the
  kind that draws a separate writing.
