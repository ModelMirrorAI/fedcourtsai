## In brief

Two women who were criminally prosecuted and later cleared ask the Court to revive their civil rights lawsuit against the prosecutors, police, and officials involved.

## What happened

Connie Reguli, an attorney, and her client Wendy Hancock were prosecuted in Tennessee for custodial interference after Hancock kept her daughter from state custody during a dependency dispute. Both were convicted, but Tennessee appeals courts later ruled that an essential element was missing from the charge and vacated the convictions. Reguli and Hancock then sued the prosecutors, police officers, a state child-services attorney, and the City of Brentwood under federal and state law, alleging the officials knowingly left a required element out of the charge to manufacture a crime. The district court dismissed all claims on immunity and pleading grounds, and the Sixth Circuit affirmed, reasoning in part that officials had made a reasonable mistake of law.

## What the Court is being asked

The petition asks the Court to decide how far Fourth Amendment protections against prosecution without probable cause reach when officials are accused of deliberately omitting a legal requirement from a charge, rather than fabricating evidence. It also asks whether prosecutors can claim immunity for preparing a charge they allegedly knew did not describe a crime, and whether an appeals court may rely on a legal theory, like mistake of law, that no party actually argued, to dismiss a case without letting the plaintiffs respond to it.

## Where it stands

The petition for certiorari was filed October 9, 2026, and the respondents' response is due November 16, 2026. The docket shows no further action yet.

## What each outcome would mean

Reguli and Hancock, as petitioners, are asking the Court to take up the case and overturn the Sixth Circuit's ruling, which favored Hetzel, Russ, O'Neil, the City of Brentwood, and the prosecutors' estate and colleague. If the Court grants the petition, it will hear full briefing and decide the legal questions; this does not by itself mean either side wins. If the Court denies the petition, the Sixth Circuit's decision affirming dismissal of the lawsuit stays in place, and the respondents keep the benefit of that ruling.
