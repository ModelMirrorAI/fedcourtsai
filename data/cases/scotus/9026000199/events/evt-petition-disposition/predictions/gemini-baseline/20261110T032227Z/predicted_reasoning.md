## 2. Scope of Grant and Question Presented

- **Grant and Consolidation of `No. 26-199` and `No. 26-201`:** The Court will grant certiorari in `No. 26-199` and consolidate it with the FTC's companion petition in `No. 26-201`, while holding the Sixth Circuit petition in *Oklahoma v. United States* (`No. 25-1325`) and either denying or holding the respondents' cross-petitions (`No. 26-332` and `No. 26-335`).
- **Question Presented Taken:** If granted, the Court will take the single Question Presented in `No. 26-199` (and `No. 26-201`) as written: *"Whether the enforcement provisions of the Horseracing Integrity and Safety Act are facially unconstitutional under the private-nondelegation doctrine."* The Court is unlikely to add the cross-petitioners' questions regarding HISA's amended rulemaking provisions or Article II Appointments Clause challenges, on which the Fifth, Sixth, and Eighth Circuits are unanimous.

## 3. Plenary Review vs. Summary Disposition (`summary-disposition-route: 0.04`)

- **Plenary Briefing and Oral Argument (`96%` conditional on grant):** Conditional on a grant, the Court will almost certainly set the case for full merits briefing and oral argument rather than disposing of it summarily in the cert order (`summary-disposition-route: 0.04`). The Court already granted, vacated, and remanded the prior Fifth, Sixth, and Eighth Circuit decisions on June 30, 2025 for further consideration in light of *FCC v. Consumers' Research*, 606 U.S. 656 (2025). With the Fifth Circuit (178 F.4th 224) and Sixth Circuit (163 F.4th 294) having issued thorough, dueling post-remand opinions reaching opposite conclusions on how *Consumers' Research* and the private-nondelegation doctrine apply to HISA's enforcement structure, no intervening decision warrants a second GVR, and the constitutional question is too substantial for a summary per curiam reversal.

Unconditionally across all dispositions, each Justice's probability of writing on the cert-stage order (`order-writers`) ranges between `0.01` and `0.03`.

## 5. Conditional Merits Outlook

- If granted, the Court's merits analysis will center on two issues highlighted by the split between Chief Judge Sutton's opinion in *Oklahoma II* and Judge Duncan's opinion in *Horsemen's III*: (1) whether, under the facial-challenge standard of *United States v. Rahimi*, 602 U.S. 680 (2024), and *Moody v. NetChoice, LLC*, 603 U.S. 707 (2024), HISA's enforcement provisions have valid constitutional applications (such as racetrack safety steward adjudications subject to de novo FTC ALJ and Commission review and stay authority under 15 U.S.C. § 3058); and (2) whether the FTC's plenary rulemaking power under 15 U.S.C. § 3053(e) to "abrogate, add to, and modify" Authority rules "as the Commission finds necessary or appropriate . . . in furtherance of the purposes of this chapter," combined with de novo sanction review under § 3058, satisfies the constitutional requirement that a private entity function subordinately to a supervising federal agency.
