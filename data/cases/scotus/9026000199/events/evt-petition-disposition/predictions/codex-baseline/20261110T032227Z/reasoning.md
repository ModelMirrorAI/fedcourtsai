## Anchor and adjustment

The starting point is the harness-frozen `cert_anchor.rate` of **0.051587928582714486**, approximately 5.16%, with ten-term lookback and statpack digest `sha256:9da7dbfa464075bfc7fab6e6b641ec9060684cd53259a76380a32823adf6f653`. The `sal-v8` table in `metrics/statpack.md` supplies the matching historical risk-set context; the 2026 row is not a prior-term anchor.

The modern-cert disposition table shows how low the unconditional rate is. The paid-segment signal cuts are cross-checks, not substitute anchors or independent likelihood multipliers: the no-CVSG bucket has 13,550 resolved weighted cases, with 4.1% plenary grants and 2.3% GVRs; terminal relist buckets have 10,204 resolved cases at zero, 2,532 at one, 495 at two, and 483 at three or more. These terminal buckets do not supply the hazard of another distribution from this snapshot. I count **two amicus briefs** in the snapshot, the Volokh-group brief and the Repole Stable brief, both filed September 16. The corresponding 2+ bucket has 1,446 resolved weighted cases, with 18.2% grants and 4.5% GVRs. That is a marginal, disposition-time stakes proxy, not a causal boost to stack on other signals.

The move to 92% is a strong case-specific judgment, not a fitted estimate from those cuts. The principal evidence is unusually convergent:

1. **The disputed issue is concrete and dispositive.** The exact question in `questions-presented.txt` is whether HISA's enforcement provisions are facially unconstitutional under the private-nondelegation doctrine. The lower-court opinion reproduced in `appendix.txt`, App. 2a–5a, confirms that the Fifth Circuit reissued its enforcement invalidation after considering the Supreme Court's remand. Its conclusion is not merely an advocate's characterization.
2. **The split survived the intervening Supreme Court decision.** The petition, printed pages 11–17, describes the Sixth Circuit's renewed rejection of the same facial enforcement challenge in Oklahoma, 163 F.4th 294, after Consumers' Research. The Fifth Circuit expressly maintained its contrary view. The Horsemen's response, printed pages 1 and 12–16, and Gulf Coast's response, pages 1–3, acknowledge the conflict. This is stronger than a one-sided claim of tension between different statutes.
3. **The respondents favor review rather than identify a reason to deny it.** The Horsemen ask the Court to grant; Gulf Coast acquiesces while seeking its additional Appointments Clause question; Texas says it does not oppose review of the enforcement ruling. Those positions appear in the three responses combined in `brief-in-opposition.txt`. Their disagreement concerns the constitutional answer and whether to broaden the case, not whether the central conflict merits resolution.
4. **The government is seeking the same review.** The staged `related/26-201.json` shows the FTC's petition challenging the same June 11, 2026 Fifth Circuit judgment, a September 30 reply, and distribution for the same December 4 conference. The Horsemen's response, printed page 38, quotes the Solicitor General's preference for these Fifth Circuit vehicles over the Sixth Circuit petition. I have not read the FTC petition itself and do not infer its wording beyond that quotation and the record.
5. **An earlier reconsideration route has already failed to settle the conflict.** The petition and appendix recount the prior 2025 GVR in light of Consumers' Research. The 2026 opinion says that decision does not change its analysis. A further unexplained remand on precisely the same ground would be less useful than deciding the dispute after briefing and argument.

The related manifest classifies all three staged dockets as **`vided-amicus`**, each with one shared brief, not as the Court's own `Vide` link. A shared brief alone would be weak evidence. Here the matching parties, identical judgment date, same conference, and the responses' express cross-petition discussions independently establish the litigation relationship. Dockets 26-332 and 26-335 concern the additional questions, and their snapshots show federal memoranda and Authority briefs in opposition. That cautions against assuming that every related petition will be granted, but is not opposition to this petition's enforcement question.

## Why not certainty; ancillary claims

There remains a real **docket-specific** risk: the Court might grant only the FTC petition, use the Sixth Circuit vehicle, or hold this petition while choosing a different lead. The Authority's reply, printed pages 7–8, notes the alternative Sixth Circuit petition and addresses a potential finality issue concerning abandoned claims. I rely on its explanation only as advocacy, not an independent jurisdictional determination. Coordinating several requests and deciding whether to add rulemaking or Appointments Clause issues also creates procedural uncertainty. These risks keep the probability below certainty despite the exceptional review case.

I assign **0.72 to at least one additional distribution**, with one further distribution modal and one or two the central procedural forecast. This reflects anticipated pre-grant screening and coordination of the related petitions, not an empirical hazard extracted from terminal relist counts. **A new CVSG is 0.005**: the Solicitor General is already seeking review for the FTC, so an invitation to obtain the government's position adds almost nothing.

**Conditional on a grant, summary disposition is 0.06.** The more likely route is a grant for briefing and argument on the enforcement question, possibly consolidated with 26-201. Both courts have already applied Consumers' Research, and the dispute concerns meaningful differences between supervisory control of rules and control of investigation and enforcement. **Conditional on denial, a dissent or statement respecting denial is 0.30**; no writing remains more likely even on that branch. Thomas, Gorsuch, and Alito are the most plausible potential writers if denial prompts an objection. The structured order-writer probabilities are unconditional across possible dispositions, hence much smaller than the denial-conditional existence probability. They are not forecasts of cert votes or of a majority-opinion author.

The **0.78 significance score** reflects the facial validity of a national statute, national horseracing regulation, and possible implications for other private-regulator arrangements. It does not assume that a decision will invalidate other regulatory systems or confuse stakes with grant probability.

No separate `decision-below.txt` or `lower-court-docket.txt` was staged, but the petition's appendix supplies the operative appellate opinion. Consequently the doctrinal and split assessment rests on the provisioned opinion and adversarial briefs, not an independently retrieved Sixth Circuit opinion.
