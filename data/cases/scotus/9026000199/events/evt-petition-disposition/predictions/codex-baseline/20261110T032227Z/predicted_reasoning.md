# Forecast of the Court's action

The Court will grant this petition for plenary review of whether the enforcement provisions of the Horseracing Integrity and Safety Act are facially unconstitutional under the private-nondelegation doctrine. It will most likely take the question presented as written and consolidate this petition with the FTC's petition, No. 26-201. It will not need to add the related Appointments Clause and rulemaking questions to resolve the acknowledged enforcement conflict.

There has been **one distribution and no relist**. The Court will most likely distribute the petition once more after its December 4, 2026 conference before granting review; one or two additional distributions are the central forecast. The probability of any additional distribution is 72%. A decision on review is most likely in December 2026 or January 2027 rather than after a prolonged wait for a new government brief.

The Court will not call for the Solicitor General's views. The FTC's own petition already supplies the federal government's request for review; the probability of a new CVSG is 0.5%.

Conditional on a grant, plenary briefing and argument are substantially more likely than a summary order resolving the case: the summary-route probability is 6%. The Fifth Circuit has already reconsidered the case after the earlier Consumers' Research remand and retained its contrary enforcement holding. The Court will use a plenary case to settle the continuing dispute over federal supervision of private enforcement rather than simply send the same question back again.

The predicted grant order will carry no separate writing. Even if the Court instead denies review, no dissent or statement remains the more likely result; the probability of such a writing conditional on denial is 30%. If a denial does draw an objection, Thomas, Gorsuch, or Alito is the likeliest author. No vote division or majority-opinion author is forecast at this cert stage.
