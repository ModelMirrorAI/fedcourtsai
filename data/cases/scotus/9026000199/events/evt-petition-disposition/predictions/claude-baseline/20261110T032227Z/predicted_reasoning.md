# Forecast — No. 26-199, HISA v. National Horsemen's Benevolent & Protective Ass'n

- **Disposition:** certiorari granted, very likely together with the Solicitor General's parallel
  petition in No. 26-201 and consolidated. The Sixth Circuit's *Oklahoma* petition (No. 25-1325) is
  probably held.
- **Relists:** distributed once so far (conference of December 4, 2026). Expect one relist and then
  a grant at the following conference, in December 2026 or early January 2027, in time for
  argument this Term. A grant straight out of the December 4 conference is the main alternative.
- **CVSG:** none. The United States is already a petitioner.
- **Question taken:** the petition's question as written, whether HISA's enforcement provisions
  are facially unconstitutional under the private-nondelegation doctrine. I expect the Court to
  deny the cross-petitions or hold them rather than add the Appointments Clause and rulemaking
  questions. A limited grant of the Horsemen's rulemaking question is a minority possibility.
- **Summary disposition:** unlikely. There is no intervening decision to GVR on, and a facial
  invalidation of a federal statute calls for plenary review.
- **If (unexpectedly) denied:** a written dissent from denial is plausible, most likely from
  Justice Gorsuch, Thomas or Alito.
- **If granted, likely merits ground (conditional):** whether the Authority "functions
  subordinately" to the FTC when it enforces the Act, given the FTC's de novo review and its
  amended rulemaking power over the Authority. *Consumers' Research*'s instruction against reading
  statutes "extravagantly, the better to create a constitutional problem," and the high bar for
  facial challenges, point toward reversal.
