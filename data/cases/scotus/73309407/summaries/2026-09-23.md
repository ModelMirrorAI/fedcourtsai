## What happened

Ricky Patterson was in a car accident in Jackson, Michigan, in September 2024, when a motorcyclist, Paul Conaway, struck his vehicle. Patterson was charged under a Michigan law covering driving under the influence after testing showed THC (an active ingredient in marijuana) in his blood, even though the trooper who administered field sobriety tests testified Patterson showed no signs of impairment. The trial court found probable cause that Patterson was under the influence. Michigan's appeals court and Michigan's Supreme Court both declined to review that ruling further. Patterson, representing himself through a public defender's office, now asks the U.S. Supreme Court to step in.

## What the Court is being asked

The petition asks the Court to decide whether drivers have fair warning of what blood-THC level counts as illegal impairment, given that no national or Michigan standard defines a specific numeric limit, and whether being charged without such a defined standard violates constitutional guarantees of fair notice before someone can be punished. The petition also raises whether this kind of dispute is one that can keep happening to others while escaping the chance for full court review, since individual cases may resolve before the underlying legal question gets settled.

## Where it stands
