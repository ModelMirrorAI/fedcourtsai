## In brief

The Supreme Court declined to review a Michigan driver's challenge to his impaired-driving charge based on his blood THC level.

## What happened

Ricky Patterson was charged under Michigan law after a motorcycle collided with his car; the responding trooper testified Patterson passed field sobriety tests, but a blood test showed THC in his system. A district court found probable cause that Patterson was impaired, and the circuit court held that his THC level supported a finding that he drove under the influence of marijuana. Michigan's Court of Appeals and Supreme Court both declined to hear further appeals, leaving the circuit court's ruling in place. Patterson then asked the U.S. Supreme Court to take the case.

## What the Court is being asked

Patterson argues that without any national or state standard defining how much THC in a driver's blood counts as impairment, he had no fair warning that his THC level could lead to a conviction. He also argues this kind of dispute will keep recurring in future cases without ever getting full court review, so the Court should decide it now even though his own case has not reached a final judgment.

## Where it stands
