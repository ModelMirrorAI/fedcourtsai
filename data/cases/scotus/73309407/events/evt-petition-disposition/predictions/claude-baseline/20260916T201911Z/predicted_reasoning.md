# Forecast: Patterson v. Michigan, No. 25-1263 (evt-petition-disposition)

**Disposition.** The Court will deny certiorari at or shortly after the September 28, 2026 conference, in the orders list that follows it.

**Relists.** The docket shows exactly one distribution (June 24, 2026, for the September 28 conference), and the petition has never been relisted. I expect no further distribution: the petition will be disposed of at its first conference. A single reschedule or relist is possible only for housekeeping reasons, not because any Justice is considering a grant or a separate writing.

**CVSG.** No call for the views of the Solicitor General. The case is a state prosecution under Michigan's operating-while-intoxicated statute; no federal statute, program, or party is implicated, and nothing in the petition invites federal input.

**Question presented, if taken.** The Court would not take either question as written. Question I is framed as a fact question about one defendant on one night ("did petitioner have actual notice of what is an acceptable level of THC"), and Question II ("is the case capable of repetition yet evading review") is a mootness doctrine invoked where nothing is moot. A grant would require the Court itself to reformulate a due-process vagueness question about Michigan's "under the influence" standard for marijuana. There is no lower-court opinion addressing that question (the Court of Appeals and Michigan Supreme Court denied leave without reasoning), no asserted split, and the prosecution is still pending at the trial level, so the Court will not do that work.

**Summary disposition.** No. There is no intervening decision of this Court to GVR against, and nothing to reverse summarily. In the unlikely event of any grant, a GVR is still the likelier grant route than plenary review simply because plenary review of this vehicle is close to unthinkable, but both are remote.

**Dissent or statement respecting denial.** None. No Justice will write; the denial will be a bare line in the orders list.

**If granted (conditional only).** The only plausible ground would be a Fourteenth Amendment fair-notice holding about prosecuting "under the influence" of marijuana without a quantitative standard. That would be a narrow, case-specific ruling rather than the national per se threshold the petition asks for.
