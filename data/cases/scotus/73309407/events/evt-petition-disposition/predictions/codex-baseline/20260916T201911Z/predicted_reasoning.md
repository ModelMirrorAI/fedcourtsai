# Forecast of the Court's action

The Court will **deny certiorari**, most likely in an orders release following the September 28, 2026 conference shown in the provisioned docket, in late September or early October. This timing is a forecast, not a separately verified publication schedule.

The record shows **one distribution and no CVSG**. I expect **zero additional distributions** and no request for the Solicitor General's views. The probabilities of at least one further distribution and a new CVSG are **4%** and **0.1%**, respectively. If the petition is distributed again, a single additional distribution is the likeliest alternative to immediate denial; a long hold is not the expected path.

The expected denial will be **unexplained**, without a separate statement or dissent. Conditional on denial, the probability of any noted dissent or statement respecting denial is **0.5%**. I do not predict an opinion author or an individual Justice's cert vote. A denial would leave the challenged state-court order undisturbed, not adjudicate the truth of the impairment allegations or endorse the state's legal reasoning.

The likely screening considerations are the petition's interlocutory criminal posture, its failure to develop a conflict on a common federal question, and the individualized factual and state-law character of the requested correction. These explain the forecast; they are not a prediction that the Court will announce its reasons in the denial order.

**If review is granted**, the most plausible question is a narrowed version of question one: whether due process supplies adequate notice for an impairment-based marijuana-driving prosecution in the absence of a numerical THC threshold. The Court would be more likely to address that legal question than decide the petition's factual formulation about this driver's actual notice or adopt the petition's proposed blood-alcohol conversion. I expect it to leave the second, capable-of-repetition question aside unless needed to resolve a genuine justiciability problem.

**Conditional on any grant**, I assign **15%** to a disposition in the cert order itself and **85%** to plenary treatment. No identified intervening decision supplies an evident GVR path, and an important new constitutional notice rule is more plausibly addressed through briefing and argument than summary reversal. The overall probability of any grant remains **0.5%**; the conditional discussion does not predict that an opinion on impairment standards will actually issue.
