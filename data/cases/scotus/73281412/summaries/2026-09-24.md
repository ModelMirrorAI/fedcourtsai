## What happened

Ronnie Alexander was jailed in Henderson County, Texas, in March 2021. After conflicts with cellmates, he told officers he was suicidal, which he later admitted was false, and was moved to a bare "suicide prevention" cell for about five days. He sued the county, several jail officers, and the jail's mental health contractor, Philip Taft and his practice, arguing the cell conditions amounted to unconstitutional punishment and that he received inadequate mental health care. Lower courts dismissed his claims. A divided panel of the Fifth Circuit Court of Appeals affirmed the dismissal, with one judge dissenting. The full appeals court declined to rehear the case.

## What the Court is being asked

Alexander's petition raises three issues. First, whether jails' duty to attend to detainees' mental health needs covers only preventing violence and suicide, or extends further. Second, whether restrictions imposed in the name of suicide prevention can still count as unlawful punishment if they are harsher or longer than necessary for that purpose. Third, whether a court reviewing a request to dismiss a case can refuse to draw any inference about jail staff's intentions from the facts alleged, by labeling that inference speculative. The respondents answer that the appeals court did not create new rules on any of these points and simply applied existing legal standards to this case's facts.

## Where it stands

The petition was distributed for conference twice, and after the second distribution the Court asked for a response, which the respondents filed along with the petitioner's reply. The docket shows no ruling yet on whether the Court will hear the case.
