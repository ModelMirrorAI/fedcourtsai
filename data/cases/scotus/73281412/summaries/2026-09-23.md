## What happened

Ronnie Alexander was held for about a week in the Henderson County, Texas jail in 2021. After a scare with a cellmate, he told guards he was suicidal, which he later admitted was untrue, and was moved to a bare "suicide prevention" cell with no toilet, shower, bedding, or clothing. He sued the county, several officers, and the jail's mental health contractor, arguing the cell's conditions amounted to punishment and that mental health care at the jail was constitutionally inadequate. The district court dismissed his claims, and a divided Fifth Circuit panel affirmed, with one judge dissenting. Alexander asked the full appeals court to rehear the case; that request was denied, though the dissent was revised. He then asked the Supreme Court to review the decision.

## What the Court is being asked

The case raises three questions about the rights of people held before trial. One is how far a jail's duty to provide mental health treatment extends, and whether it can be satisfied simply by guarding against violence and suicide. Another is how courts should decide whether jail conditions imposed in the name of suicide prevention have become excessive punishment rather than a legitimate safety measure. The third is whether, at an early stage of a lawsuit, a court can reject as too speculative any inference that jail staff acted with an intent to punish, even when a detainee has pointed to specific statements and conduct suggesting that motive. The respondents argue the appeals court did not create any new rule and simply applied existing legal standards to the specific facts of this case.

## Where it stands

The case has been distributed for conference more than once, and the respondents were asked to respond after initially waiving that right. Both sets of respondents have now filed briefs opposing review, and Alexander has filed a reply. The petition remains pending; the Court has not yet acted on whether to take the case.
