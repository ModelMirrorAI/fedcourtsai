## In brief

The Supreme Court declined to hear a former pretrial detainee's challenge to how jails must handle mental health care and suicide-prevention conditions.

## What happened

Ronnie Alexander was held in the Henderson County, Texas jail in 2021. After threats from cellmates, he told officers he was suicidal, which he later said was false, and was moved to a bare "suicide prevention cell" for five days. He sued the county, several officers, and the jail's mental health contractor, claiming the cell's conditions amounted to unconstitutional punishment and that the jail's mental health care was inadequate. The district court dismissed his claims, and a divided Fifth Circuit panel affirmed, over a dissent. Alexander then asked the Supreme Court to review that ruling.

## What the Court is being asked

Alexander asked the Court to decide three things: whether jails' duty to care for detainees' mental health needs can be satisfied simply by protecting against violence and suicide; whether conditions imposed in the name of suicide prevention can still count as unlawful punishment if they are excessive and deprive someone of basic necessities; and whether courts reviewing a dismissed complaint may refuse to draw any inference about an official's state of mind, even when a detainee's allegations might support one.

## Where it stands

The respondents opposed review, Alexander filed a reply, and the Supreme Court denied the petition for certiorari.

## What each outcome would mean

Alexander, as the petitioner, asked the Court to take up his case after losing in the lower courts. By denying the petition, the Court leaves the Fifth Circuit's ruling in place, meaning the dismissal of Alexander's claims against the county, the officers, and the mental health providers stands. The denial does not mean the Supreme Court agreed with the Fifth Circuit's reasoning; it simply means the case will not be reviewed, and the lower court's judgment is final.
