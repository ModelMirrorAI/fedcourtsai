# Forecast: what the Court will do with No. 25-1128, Alexander v. Philip R. Taft Psy D and Associates

**Disposition.** The Court will deny the petition, most likely on the order list following the September 28, 2026 long conference or, if relisted, within one or two further conferences. P(any grant) = 0.10.

**Relists.** The docket shows two distributions: one for the May 21, 2026 conference (from which the petition was pulled when the Court called for a response after respondents had waived) and one for the September 28, 2026 conference, where it will receive its first real consideration. I forecast a 25% chance it is distributed at least once more after September 28. The long conference produces relists at a higher rate than an ordinary conference, and a petition that has already drawn a call for response, with a published dissent below and a request for summary reversal, is the kind a chambers may hold for a conference or two while deciding whether to write. If it is relisted, I expect one or two further distributions, not a long string; it is not a hold for a pending merits case.

**CVSG.** None. No federal party, no federal statute, and no federal program is implicated; this is a § 1983 suit against a Texas county, its jailers, and a private psychology contractor. P(CVSG called for) = 0.01.

**Which question the Court would take, if it took one.** Not QP 1 as written: the "limited to protection from violence and suicide" framing rests on one sentence of the Fifth Circuit's opinion that reads, in context, as "medical care *includes* protection from violence or suicide," and the Court would not grant on a question the opinion does not squarely hold. If the Court acted at all, it would be on the combination of QP 2 and QP 3: whether a five-day confinement in a bare cell without toilet, water, bedding, or hygiene, ordered after guards had refused a transfer and were alleged to have taunted the detainee, plausibly states a Bell v. Wolfish punishment claim at the pleading stage. That is a fact-bound application, and the Court would frame it as one, not as a rewrite of the Bell test.

**Summary disposition versus plenary review.** Conditional on a grant, I put the summary route at 50%. There is no clean circuit split for argument, and the petition itself concedes the case "as a whole is likely not simple enough for summary reversal" while asking for summary reversal on individual holdings. If the Court is moved, the natural vehicle is a short per curiam in the mold of Taylor v. Riojas vacating the pleading-stage dismissal of the conditions claim against the county defendants, not a plenary case. There is no intervening decision that would support a GVR; Hikma Pharmaceuticals v. Amarin (2026), which respondents cite on plausibility pleading, cuts against the petitioner and would not be a remand basis.

**Dissent from or statement respecting denial.** Conditional on denial, I put a separate writing at 10%. The conditions alleged are vivid and the Fifth Circuit's opinion drew a strenuous dissent, which is the profile that has drawn statements from Justice Sotomayor or Justice Jackson in prisoner cases. Against that, the petitioner's own admission that he falsely reported suicidal ideation, the unchallenged alternative holdings, and the absence of a qualified-immunity ruling to criticize make it a poor occasion for a writing. If one appears, it will come from Justice Sotomayor or Justice Jackson, and it is more likely a short dissent than a statement.

**If granted, the likely ground.** A holding that the complaint plausibly alleged an expressed intent to punish, or that a five-day stay in the violent cell without reassessment was excessive in relation to suicide prevention under Bell, with the mental-health-care claims left for remand. The Court would not reach the Taft respondents, whose dismissal on deliberate indifference the petition does not contest.
