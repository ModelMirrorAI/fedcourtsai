# Forecast: what the Court will do with No. 26-18, Westcott v. Voice of the Experienced

**Disposition.** The petition will be denied. P(any grant) = 0.20.

**Relists.** The docket shows one distribution (for the September 28, 2026 long
conference), which was overtaken when the Court called for a response on
August 3. The BIO arrived October 2. Expect a reply brief by mid-October and a
redistribution for a late-October or early-November conference, so at least
one further distribution is close to certain (0.97). Past that first
redistribution I expect the petition to be decided at its first real
consideration or after a single relist: the questions are purely procedural,
the record is short, and the vehicle problems the BIO raises are the kind the
pool memo resolves rather than the kind that draws a hold. One relist is
plausible if a Justice is drafting a statement respecting denial; two or more
would signal a summary disposition in the works.

**CVSG.** None, with high confidence (0.04). No federal party, no federal
program, and the question is about the mootness exception's application to a
PLRA durational limit. The United States litigates under the PLRA as both
plaintiff and defendant, so an invitation is not impossible, but the Court has
already taken the cheaper step of calling for the respondents' views and will
decide on those.

**Which question, if it took one.** The Court would not take QP 1 as written
(whether 90 days is categorically too short to litigate an issue). It would
take a reformulated version of QP 2: whether the "evading review" element asks
if the dispute can reach plenary review in this Court, as Bellotti and Turner
phrase it, rather than review in a court of appeals. That is the one question
on which the Fifth Circuit's Smith reasoning sits clearly apart from the
other circuits' formulations. Even then, the Court would be more likely to
act by summary vacatur than by setting the case for argument.

**Summary route versus plenary review.** Conditional on a grant, I expect the
summary route (0.55): the petition asks for summary reversal first, the
legal point is short, and plenary argument on an expired-injunction mootness
question in a case the petitioners have already won at trial is an unattractive
use of an argument slot. The form would be a per curiam vacatur and remand for
the Fifth Circuit to apply the correct evading-review inquiry, not a GVR in
light of an intervening decision (there is none: Consumers' Research predates
the Fifth Circuit's VOTE II and VOTE III decisions).

**If the Court denies.** A statement respecting the denial, or a short dissent
from it, is a live possibility (0.20). The hook is the one Judge Jones's
dissent below supplied: a district court entering a new 90-day injunction the
day after the previous one expired, without the PLRA's need-narrowness-
intrusiveness findings, and the court of appeals calling the result moot each
time. The Justices most likely to write are those who have written on PLRA
limits and on district-court compliance with Congress's remedial constraints;
a statement would say the denial does not endorse Smith and that the question
can return in a case not already mooted by final judgment.

**Why denial is still the likelier outcome.** The petitioners have obtained
final judgment in their favor, so the preliminary-injunction orders have merged
into a judgment that resolves the Eighth Amendment claim. Any vacatur would
send the appeals back to a court of appeals that would dismiss them again on
that independent ground. The capable-of-repetition prong was never found in
petitioners' favor below, and the BIO's account of three materially different
injunctions and repeated policy changes makes it hard to satisfy. The Court
will treat this as the wrong vehicle and wait for a case where the mootness
question arises before final judgment.
