3. **Disposition (`disposition`: 0.28)**:
   The Court will most likely **deny** the petition for a writ of certiorari after the Brief in Opposition is filed. While the Fifth Circuit's rule in *Smith v. Edwards*, 88 F.4th 1119 (5th Cir. 2023)—treating a 90-day PLRA preliminary injunction as long enough to obtain "meaningful" expedited circuit review and therefore as not "evading review"—is in direct tension with *Turner v. Rogers*, 564 U.S. 431, 440 (2011), and *FCC v. Consumers' Research*, 606 U.S. 656, 671 n.1 (2025), the underlying litigation suffers from a serious vehicle problem on the "capable of repetition" prong. Specifically, the district court has already completed the full bench trial on permanent relief and entered a comprehensive post-trial remedial order on May 26, 2026 (*Voice of the Experienced v. LeBlanc*, 2026 WL 1472248 (M.D. La. May 26, 2026)). Because the expired summer 2025 pre-trial preliminary injunctions at issue in *VOTE II* and *VOTE III* have been superseded by post-trial permanent remedial proceedings—the very posture Judge Wilson cited in concurring in the denial of rehearing en banc—the Court will likely conclude that this case is an unsuitable vehicle to resolve the *Smith v. Edwards* split.

4. **Summary Disposition vs. Plenary Review Conditional on Grant (`summary-disposition-route`: 0.45)**:
   Conditional on the Court granting review, there is a high probability (0.45) that it disposes of the petition in the cert order itself—via a per curiam summary reversal or a grant, vacate, and remand (GVR)—rather than setting the case for full merits briefing and oral argument. Petitioners explicitly lead with a request for summary reversal, and the doctrinal error on the "evading review" prong is straightforward under *Turner* and *Consumers' Research*. If the Court grants plenary review instead, it will take both Questions Presented together, as they jointly address whether a 90-day statutory expiration window is too short to complete judicial review including plenary review by this Court.

5. **Separate Writings on the Disposition Order (`dissent-from-denial`: 0.18)**:
   Conditional on a denial of certiorari, there is a meaningful likelihood (0.18) that one or more Justices—most likely Justice Alito (the Circuit Justice for the Fifth Circuit), Justice Thomas, or Justice Gorsuch—will author a dissent from the denial of certiorari or a statement respecting the denial of certiorari. Such a writing would parallel Judge Jones's six-judge en banc dissent and Judge Wilson's separate statement by emphasizing that *Smith v. Edwards* misapplies this Court's "capable of repetition, yet evading review" precedents, while noting or disputing whether the intervening post-trial order in the district court renders this petition an imperfect vehicle.

## Conditional Merits Outlook

If the Court grants review (either summarily or for plenary consideration), it would likely reverse or vacate the Fifth Circuit's mootness dismissals on the ground that under *Turner v. Rogers*, 564 U.S. 431, 440 (2011), and *FCC v. Consumers' Research*, 606 U.S. 656, 671 n.1 (2025), a 90-day statutory duration under 18 U.S.C. § 3626(a)(2) is categorically too short for complete judicial review (which includes plenary review in this Court, not merely expedited review in the court of appeals), and remand for the Fifth Circuit to determine whether the post-trial posture leaves any live "capable of repetition" showing or warrants vacatur under *United States v. Munsingwear, Inc.*, 340 U.S. 36 (1950).
