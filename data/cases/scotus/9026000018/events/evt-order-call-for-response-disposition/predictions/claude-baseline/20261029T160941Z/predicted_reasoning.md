# Forecast: Westcott v. Voice of the Experienced (No. 26-18)

**Posture at my vantage.** One distribution so far (for the 9/28/2026 conference). The Court called for a response on August 3, after the respondents waived. No CVSG.

**Disposition.** I expect a denial more often than not, but a grant is a real possibility (P = 0.37). The petition asks whether a 90-day period is too short for the "capable of repetition yet evading review" mootness exception. The Fifth Circuit says no, under *Smith v. Edwards* (5th Cir. 2023), and that holds every PLRA preliminary injunction, which expires by law after 90 days, out of appellate review in that circuit. The petition claims a split with this Court's cases (*Turner v. Rogers*: one to two years "too short") and with nearly every other circuit. Rehearing en banc was denied 11–6, with dissents. If the Court grants, it takes **QP 1 as written**, possibly folding in QP 2 (whether the "review" that counts is this Court's plenary review). The likely ground would be that 90 days is too short under *Turner*'s line of cases.

**Further distribution.** Near certain. The requested response puts the petition back in front of a conference. I expect it at the next conference after the reply. If the Court is weighing a summary reversal or a dissent from denial, I expect one or two relists.

**CVSG.** Unlikely. No federal party is involved, and the Court has already asked the respondents directly.

**Summary route.** Conditional on a grant, I put about one in four on a disposition in the cert order itself. That would most likely be a per curiam summary reversal saying a 90-day window evades review under *Turner*, not a GVR, since no intervening decision bears on the question. Plenary review is the likelier grant route.

**Separate writing if denied.** About one in four. The most likely writers are Justice Alito (Circuit Justice for the Fifth Circuit, who handled the extension) and Justices Thomas or Gorsuch, who would argue that the Fifth Circuit's rule shields PLRA injunctions from review. A summary reversal could draw a dissent from Justice Sotomayor or Justice Jackson.
