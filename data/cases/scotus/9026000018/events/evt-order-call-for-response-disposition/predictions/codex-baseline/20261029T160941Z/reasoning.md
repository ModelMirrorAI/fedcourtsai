I move modestly above 24.4% to 30%, balancing three case-specific considerations rather than mechanically adding independent likelihood ratios:

- **Attention and a serious review problem:** the Court affirmatively requested an answer after a waiver. The petition describes three successive expired injunctions and unsuccessful efforts to obtain review despite expedition. The petition's discussion of the Fifth Circuit proceedings and rehearing materials supplies an unusually concrete illustration of the alleged review gap (petition pp. 11–18; staged text around lines 952–1245). The 11–6 rehearing vote in VOTE III and the five-signatory Jones dissent strengthen the case for attention, but neither is a Supreme Court vote count.
- **An arguable conflict with established doctrine:** the questions ask about a specific 90-day limit and the relevant reviewing court. The petition invokes Turner v. Rogers, 564 U.S. 431, 439–40 (2011), and First National Bank of Boston v. Bellotti, 435 U.S. 765, 774 (1978), and collects circuit authorities in its reasons section (petition pp. 19–24). These are petitioners' characterizations, not an independently confirmed census of a split. The retrieved majority discussion in Honig v. Doe, 484 U.S. 305, 318–23 (1988), reproduced by Cornell's Legal Information Institute, confirms that evasion of review and reasonably expected recurrence are separate requirements; it does not establish that Louisiana satisfies both here. The cited circuit examples also span different contexts rather than establishing a uniform published PLRA-specific split.
- **A substantial vehicle discount:** the petition itself says Louisiana won final judgment on May 26, 2026, with plaintiffs' claims dismissed with prejudice (petition p. 18; staged lines 1232–1238). That is a pre-petition lower-court development, not this cell's predicted Supreme Court disposition. Judge Wilson's statement identifies preservation and vehicle objections and anticipates the merits proceeding ending this cycle (App.16a–17a; staged lines 2140–2178). Petitioners answer that final judgment does not resolve the separate PLRA preliminary-relief issues (petition pp. 28–29; staged lines 1648–1677). Their answer is substantial but does not automatically demonstrate a reasonable expectation of future recurrence involving the same complaining party. The vacatur of the expired orders, visible in the appendix's panel dispositions, further limits the immediate practical relief. These problems can justify denial even if the abstract 90-day timing argument is persuasive.

The strongest counterargument to my denial call is the combination of repeated thwarted review, a state petitioner, a clear summary-reversal request, and the Court's response request. The strongest counterargument to raising grant odds much further is that this is now a dispute about expired preliminary orders after the state prevailed at trial, with preservation complications and no opposition text to test either side's framing. Thirty percent represents that balance, not a measured response-requested base rate.

## Other forecast fields

The 98% distribution increment reflects the ordinary procedural next step following a requested response, not evidence that an actual relist has already occurred. The CVSG probability is 4% because the record presents a general procedural question and no particular executive recommendation to await. The summary-route probability is 65% **conditional on any grant**: petitioners expressly seek summary reversal first (petition conclusion, staged lines 1678–1680), and their core argument is correction under existing precedent. The unconditional probability of that route is therefore 19.5%; it is not the JSON conditional claim. Vehicle disputes keep plenary treatment material if the Court does grant.

The 16% denial-writing probability is conditional on denial. Its unconditional probability is 0.16 times P(denial), approximately 11.2% if denial accounts for nearly all the 70% non-grant mass, and slightly lower when other non-grant terminations are allowed for. Per-Justice order-writer numbers are unconditional marginals, not a categorical distribution and not a concealed vote forecast. My slightly higher numbers for Thomas, Alito, and Gorsuch reflect a speculative match between the petition's constraints-on-injunctions argument and possible interest in writing, not information about their views in this case. Liberal Justices retain small writing probabilities because a summary intervention could also provoke disagreement. The modal order is unsigned and without separate writing.

The stakes score of 0.48 reflects the potential reach of an appellate-jurisdiction rule across short-lived injunctions, together with the practical prison-litigation setting. It is not the 30% grant probability and does not assume the Court will adjudicate the underlying prison conditions.
