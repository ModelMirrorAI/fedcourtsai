## In brief

Louisiana asks the Court to revive its appeals of expired prison heat-safety injunctions, arguing the Fifth Circuit wrongly found those appeals moot.

## What happened

Prisoners at Louisiana's Angola prison sued over heat conditions for men working the "Farm Line," winning three successive preliminary injunctions requiring heat-safety measures. Louisiana appealed each one, but each injunction automatically expired after 90 days under a federal law limiting prison-conditions injunctions, before the Fifth Circuit ruled. The Fifth Circuit dismissed each appeal as moot, relying on its own precedent, and denied rehearing. Several judges dissented, saying this let the district court evade appellate review. After trial, the district court ultimately ruled for Louisiana.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked whether a 90-day period is too short to count as a full opportunity for appellate review, so that an appeal of an expired injunction should still go forward under an exception to the usual mootness rule for disputes capable of repeating but evading review. It is also asked whether that exception requires that review be available from the Supreme Court itself, rather than just from a federal appeals court.

## Where it stands

The petition has been fully briefed, including a brief opposing review and Louisiana's reply, and the case has been distributed for the Court's conference on November 6, 2026. No decision on whether to hear the case has yet been recorded.

## What each outcome would mean

Louisiana, which lost its appeals below, is asking the Court to step in. If the Court grants the petition, it would hear the case and decide the mootness questions, deciding nothing yet about who is right. If it summarily reverses, Louisiana's position would prevail without full briefing or argument, reviving its appeals challenging the injunctions. If it denies the petition, the Fifth Circuit's dismissals stand, leaving the injunctions unreviewed and the case as resolved by the district court's final judgment for Louisiana after trial.
