## In brief

Louisiana prison officials ask the Court to revive appeals of expired preliminary injunctions over heat conditions for incarcerated workers, after lower courts ruled the appeals moot.

## What happened

Incarcerated men at the Louisiana State Penitentiary who work outdoors on the "Farm Line," along with an advocacy organization, sued prison officials claiming inadequate heat protections violated the Eighth Amendment. The district court issued three successive preliminary injunctions over two years, each imposing different heat-safety requirements as officials revised their policies. Each injunction expired after 90 days under a federal law limiting preliminary relief in prison-conditions cases, and the Fifth Circuit dismissed Louisiana's appeals of two of them as moot each time, relying on its own prior precedent. After a trial, the district court ultimately entered final judgment for the officials.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked whether a 90-day period is too brief to let a legal dispute be fully argued through the courts, for purposes of an exception that lets courts hear otherwise-moot appeals when an issue is likely to recur but typically expires before review finishes. The Court is also asked whether that exception requires that full review by the Supreme Court itself be possible, rather than only review by a federal appeals court.

## Where it stands

The petition has been distributed for conference, and the Court asked for a response, which the respondents filed opposing review. No action on the petition has been recorded since.

## What each outcome would mean

Louisiana officials are asking the Court to step in. If the Court grants review, it would hear the case and decide whether the 90-day expiration period and the appeals court's approach to mootness were correct, without yet deciding who wins. If it denies review, the Fifth Circuit's dismissal of the appeals as moot stands, leaving the expired injunctions unreviewed and the final judgment for the officials in place. The respondents argue the dispute is doubly moot since final judgment has already been entered.
