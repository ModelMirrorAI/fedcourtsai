## In brief

Louisiana asks the Court to revive its appeals of expired prison heat-safety injunctions, arguing lower courts wrongly found the appeals moot before reaching the merits.

## What happened

Prisoners at Louisiana's Angola prison, working outdoors on the "Farm Line," sued claiming extreme heat exposure violated their constitutional rights. The district court issued three successive preliminary injunctions over two years, each requiring heat-safety measures. Louisiana appealed each one, but under a federal prison-litigation law, preliminary injunctions automatically expire after 90 days unless made final. Each injunction expired before the appeals court ruled, and the Fifth Circuit dismissed all three appeals as moot, relying on its own prior decision. The full Fifth Circuit declined to rehear the case, over several judges' dissents. The district court later ruled for Louisiana after a trial.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked whether a 90-day period is too short for a legal dispute to be fully resolved on appeal, such that the case should still be heard despite becoming technically moot, and whether the relevant measure of "enough time to be fully litigated" is the time needed for the Supreme Court itself to review the case, rather than just a federal appeals court.

## Where it stands

The petition was distributed for conference, after which the Court asked the respondents to respond; the respondents filed a brief opposing review, and Louisiana filed a reply. The case was distributed again for a later conference, and the docket shows no decision yet on whether the Court will hear it.

## What each outcome would mean

Louisiana, as petitioner, is asking the Court to summarily reverse the lower court or hear the case. If the Court denies review, the Fifth Circuit's dismissals of Louisiana's appeals as moot stand, leaving its precedent on the 90-day mootness question in place. If the Court grants review, it would decide whether Louisiana's appeals should have been heard despite the injunctions' expiration; summary reversal would revive Louisiana's challenge without full briefing, while vacating and returning the case would leave the underlying question undecided for now.
