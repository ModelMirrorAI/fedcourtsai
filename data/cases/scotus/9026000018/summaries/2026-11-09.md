## In brief

Louisiana asked the Court to review a Fifth Circuit rule that repeatedly let prison-conditions injunctions expire before Louisiana could appeal them; the Court denied review.

## What happened

Prisoners at Louisiana's state penitentiary sued over outdoor labor conditions in extreme heat, winning three successive preliminary injunctions against the prison system. Louisiana appealed each one, but under a federal prison-litigation statute, preliminary injunctions automatically expire after 90 days unless a court makes certain findings and finalizes the order first. Each injunction expired before the Fifth Circuit ruled, and the appeals court, following its own precedent, dismissed all three appeals as moot. The prisoners later won a bench trial, and the district court entered final judgment for Louisiana. The Fifth Circuit denied rehearing, over dissents from several judges.

## What the Court is being asked

The case asks whether a 90-day period is too brief to count as a full opportunity to litigate an appeal, which matters for an exception that keeps a case alive even after the original order expires, when the same dispute is likely to recur. It also asks whether that exception requires a chance for full review by the Supreme Court itself, rather than just by a federal appeals court.

## Where it stands

The petition was distributed for conference twice, and after full briefing including a response and reply, the Supreme Court denied the petition on November 9, 2026.

## What each outcome would mean

Louisiana, as petitioner, asked the Court to reverse the Fifth Circuit's mootness rulings. With the petition denied, the Fifth Circuit's decisions dismissing Louisiana's three appeals as moot remain in place, and the question of whether the district court properly complied with the injunction statute in those earlier orders will not be reviewed by the Supreme Court. The prisoners' organization, which opposed review, keeps the benefit of that outcome, though the underlying case had already ended with a final judgment for Louisiana after trial.
