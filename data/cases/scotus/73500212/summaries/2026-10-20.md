## In brief

Apple asked the Court to overturn a ruling that Apple violated a court injunction's "spirit" by charging a commission on purchases made outside its App Store.

## What happened

Epic Games sued Apple years ago over App Store rules, winning a ruling that Apple's ban on telling users about cheaper purchase options elsewhere violated California's unfair competition law. A court ordered Apple to stop blocking such notices. After Apple began allowing outside links but charged a new commission on resulting purchases, Epic argued Apple was defying the order. A district court held Apple in civil contempt, and the Ninth Circuit largely upheld that finding, while sending the case back to reconsider the penalty. Apple asked the Supreme Court to review the contempt ruling and the scope of the underlying order.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked whether a company can be held in contempt of a court order for conduct the order's own wording never mentions, based on what a judge sees as the order's underlying purpose, or whether contempt requires that the order's text clearly and specifically forbid the exact conduct at issue.

## Where it stands

The Court granted review limited to this contempt question. Briefing is complete and the case awaits oral argument and a decision. Separately, Apple's request to pause related district court proceedings while the case is pending was denied by Justice Kagan.

## What each outcome would mean

Apple, which lost on this issue in the lower courts, is asking the Court to reverse the contempt finding. If the Court sides with Apple, the contempt finding would be thrown out, removing the basis for forcing Apple to defend its commission rate as a proven contemnor, and lower court proceedings over Apple's commission could be affected or curtailed. If the Court sides with Epic, the contempt finding would stand, and the case would continue in the lower courts, which could proceed with the ordered reassessment of what commission Apple may charge on outside purchases.
