## In brief

Apple asks the Court to decide whether it was properly held in civil contempt for charging a commission on purchases made outside its app ecosystem.

## What happened

Epic sued Apple years ago, claiming Apple's app store rules violated antitrust and competition laws. A court found Apple's rule against letting developers direct customers to outside payment options violated California's unfair competition law and ordered Apple to stop blocking such links. After that ruling became final, Apple allowed the links but began charging a new commission on purchases made through them. Epic argued this violated the court's order, and the district court held Apple in civil contempt. The appeals court mostly upheld that finding, ruling that Apple had violated the "spirit" of the order, though it sent part of the case back for further proceedings on the commission's size.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked whether a party can be held in contempt of a court order for conduct the order's own wording never addressed, if a judge believes the conduct defeats the order's purpose, or whether contempt requires that the order's text clearly and specifically cover the conduct in question.

## Where it stands

The Court granted review limited to this contempt question and the case was argued; briefing, including a brief from the federal government, is complete, and the case is awaiting decision. A related emergency request by Apple to pause lower-court proceedings while the case was pending was denied.

## What each outcome would mean

Apple, which asked the Court to hear the case, is seeking to overturn the lower court's contempt finding. If the Court rules for Apple, the contempt finding would be reversed, likely undoing the commission restrictions imposed as a sanction and limiting when courts can use contempt based on an order's unstated purpose rather than its text. If the Court rules for Epic, the contempt finding and its consequences for Apple's commission practices would stand. The Court could also send the case back for further proceedings without deciding the issue for either side.
