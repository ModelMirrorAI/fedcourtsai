## In brief

Apple asked the Court to review a contempt finding over its commission on steered purchases in its long-running dispute with Epic Games; the Court agreed to hear the contempt question.

## What happened

Epic sued Apple years ago, and a court found Apple's rules against letting app developers steer customers to outside payment options violated California's unfair competition law. A court ordered Apple to stop prohibiting developers from including buttons, links, or other calls to action directing customers elsewhere. After Apple began allowing such links but charged a commission on purchases made through them and imposed design restrictions, a court held Apple in civil contempt for violating that order. The United States Court of Appeals for the Ninth Circuit largely upheld the contempt finding, though it reversed a complete ban on commissions and sent the commission question back for further proceedings. Apple asked the Supreme Court to review that ruling.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked whether a judge may hold someone in civil contempt for violating what a court order was generally meant to achieve, even when the order's own wording does not clearly cover the conduct in question, or whether contempt requires that the order's text clearly and specifically forbid the precise conduct at issue.

## Where it stands

The Court granted Apple's petition limited to this contempt question, and the case was argued; briefing is complete and the case is scheduled for oral argument on January 11, 2027. A separate emergency request by Apple to pause related district court proceedings while the appeal continued was denied by Justice Kagan.

## What each outcome would mean

Apple, which lost on this issue below, is asking the Court to reverse the contempt finding. If the Court rules for Apple, the contempt finding tied to its commission would be set aside, and any remand instructions built on that finding would likely fall with it. If the Court rules for Epic, which defended the contempt finding, the Ninth Circuit's decision upholding contempt would stand, leaving in place further proceedings over what commission, if any, Apple may charge. The Court could also vacate and send the case back for the lower courts to apply a clarified standard without deciding the outcome for either side.
