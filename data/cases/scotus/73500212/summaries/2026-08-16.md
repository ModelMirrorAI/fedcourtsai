## In brief

Apple asked the Supreme Court to review contempt and injunction rulings from its dispute with Epic Games over App Store payment rules; the Court granted partial review.

## What happened

Epic sued Apple years ago over App Store rules, winning an injunction barring Apple from stopping developers from linking users to outside payment options. After Apple later imposed a steep commission and design restrictions on such links, a district court held Apple in contempt, finding it had evaded the injunction's requirements, and ordered a broader remedy. The Ninth Circuit largely upheld the contempt finding and the injunction's scope, while sending part of the remedy back for reconsideration. Apple asked the Supreme Court to review both the contempt standard used and whether the injunction improperly extended relief beyond Epic to other developers.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court agreed to decide one issue: whether a court may find a party in contempt for violating what it sees as the "spirit" of an injunction, even when the injunction's text does not clearly address the specific conduct at issue, or whether contempt requires a clear, unambiguous violation of the order's actual wording.

## Where it stands

The Court granted review limited to that contempt question; the case is now in the briefing stage before argument. Separately, an emergency request by Apple to pause district court proceedings was denied by Justice Kagan after a brief administrative pause.

## What each outcome would mean

Apple, which sought review, asked the Court to reject the "spirit of the injunction" contempt standard the lower courts applied. If the Court ultimately rules for Apple, it would reject that standard and could undo the contempt finding against Apple, affecting the related remedies. If it rules for Epic, the contempt finding and the Ninth Circuit's approach would stand. The Court could also send the case back without resolving the question either way. The separate denial of Apple's stay request left district court proceedings free to continue in the meantime.
