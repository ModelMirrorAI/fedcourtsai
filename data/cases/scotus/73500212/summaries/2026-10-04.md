## In brief

Apple asked the Court to review a contempt ruling and an injunction's scope in its long-running dispute with Epic Games over App Store payment rules.

## What happened

Epic sued Apple years ago over rules restricting how app developers could direct users to payment options outside Apple's App Store. A district court found Apple's rules partly unlawful and issued an injunction barring Apple from prohibiting developers from linking users to outside purchasing options. The Ninth Circuit affirmed. Apple later adopted a new commission and design restrictions on such links, which the district court found violated the injunction, holding Apple in contempt. The Ninth Circuit largely affirmed that contempt finding and the injunction's scope, while sending part of the remedy back for reconsideration. Apple then asked the Supreme Court to review that decision; Epic opposed.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked to decide, first, whether a court may hold a party in contempt for violating an order's general purpose or "spirit" even when the order's specific words did not address the conduct involved, or whether contempt requires a clear, specific violation of the order's actual text. Second, the Court is asked whether the lower court wrongly allowed an injunction extending beyond the original parties in an antitrust-related case, departing from recent Supreme Court limits on such broad injunctions.

## Where it stands

The Court granted review limited to the first question. Merits briefing is complete and numerous outside groups have filed supporting briefs; a separate emergency request by Apple to pause district court proceedings was denied by Justice Kagan. The case now awaits further proceedings, including oral argument, as shown by the docket.

## What each outcome would mean

Apple, as the party that petitioned for review, is asking the Court to overturn the contempt finding against it. If the Court affirms the lower court's decision, the contempt finding and resulting restrictions on Apple's commission and link practices would stand. If the Court reverses, the contempt finding would be set aside, which could remove the basis for related remedies imposed against Apple, benefiting Apple's position in the case. If the Court vacates, it would send the case back without deciding the question for either side, leaving further proceedings to the lower courts.
