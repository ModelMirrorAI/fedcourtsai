## In brief

Apple asks the Supreme Court to overturn a contempt finding stemming from its long-running fight with Epic Games over App Store commission rules.

## What happened

Epic sued Apple years ago over restrictions on how app developers could direct users to payment options outside Apple's in-app purchase system. A district court found a narrow violation of California's unfair competition law and issued an injunction. After that injunction took effect, Apple began charging a commission on purchases made through outside links, and the district court later held Apple in civil contempt for this and related practices, concluding Apple had violated the "spirit" of the injunction even though its text did not mention commissions. The United States Court of Appeals for the Ninth Circuit upheld the contempt finding but sent the case back for the district court to reconsider the remedy regarding Apple's commission.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked whether a party can be held in civil contempt for violating what a court believes an order's general purpose required, even when the order's actual wording never addressed the specific conduct at issue, or whether contempt requires that the order's own terms clearly and unambiguously cover that conduct.

## Where it stands

The Court granted Apple's petition limited to this contempt question. Briefing is complete, the case was argued, and it has been set for oral argument on January 11, 2027. Separately, Apple's request to pause related district court proceedings while the appeal continues was denied by Justice Kagan after a brief administrative stay.

## What each outcome would mean

Apple, which lost on this issue below, is asking the Court to reverse the Ninth Circuit's decision upholding the contempt finding. If the Court sides with Apple, the contempt finding would be reversed or the case sent back, undercutting the basis for further proceedings over Apple's commission on outside purchases. If the Court affirms, the contempt finding and the Ninth Circuit's instructions for setting a new commission rate would stand, and the case would return to the district court to address Apple's commission under that framework. The Court could also vacate and return the case without deciding the issue for either side.
