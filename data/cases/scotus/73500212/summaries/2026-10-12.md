## In brief

Apple asks the Supreme Court to decide whether a court may hold a company in civil contempt for violating an injunction's "spirit" rather than its explicit text.

## What happened

Epic Games sued Apple over App Store policies, winning a ruling that Apple's rules blocking developers from steering customers to outside payment options violated California's unfair competition law. A federal district court ordered Apple to stop blocking such links. After Apple complied by allowing links but charging a commission on purchases made through them, Epic asked the court to hold Apple in contempt. The district court agreed, and the Ninth Circuit Court of Appeals upheld that contempt finding, reasoning that Apple's commission violated the "spirit" of the injunction even though the injunction's text never mentioned commissions. The Ninth Circuit sent the case back for further proceedings on an appropriate commission rate.

## What the Court is being asked

The Court is asked whether a company can be found in contempt of a court order for conduct the order's text never actually addressed, so long as a judge believes the conduct defeats the order's underlying purpose, or whether contempt requires that the order's own words clearly and specifically forbid the conduct in question.

## Where it stands

The Court granted Apple's petition limited to this contempt question in June 2026. The case has been fully briefed on the merits, with the petitioner's and other merits briefs filed through September 2026, and it awaits further proceedings before the Court. Separately, Apple's request to pause related district court proceedings while this appeal continues was denied by Justice Kagan in August 2026.

## What each outcome would mean

Apple, as the petitioner, asked the Court to take up this contempt question. If the Court rules for Apple, it would reverse or vacate the contempt finding, meaning Apple could not be penalized for its commission or related practices under that ruling, and related instructions for setting Apple's commission on remand would likely fall as well. If the Court rules for Epic Games, the contempt finding and the Ninth Circuit's instructions for determining Apple's permissible commission on outside purchases would stand, and proceedings to set that commission would continue in the lower courts.
