The Court will vacate the Ninth Circuit’s judgment and remand, 7–2. Justice Barrett will write for Chief Justice Roberts and Justices Thomas, Alito, Kagan, Gorsuch and Kavanaugh. Justice Sotomayor will dissent, joined by Justice Jackson.

The majority will apply *Taggart*’s objective fair-notice standard to civil contempt for violating federal injunctions generally. Rule 65(d) requires a court to specify what its order commands. Context can show that a new method of obstructing purchases falls within an existing prohibition. The Ninth Circuit nevertheless allowed the order’s competitive purpose and Apple’s intention to frustrate it to supply an obligation the words did not clearly impose.

The decree required Apple to allow developers to include buttons and links to outside payment options. That language left fair ground to doubt that charging 27% on a resulting sale itself exposed Apple to contempt. The decree’s express protection of “buttons” gave Apple specific notice that developers could offer a button that users could recognize. The Court will return the design restrictions and the sanctions that depend on the contempt findings for reconsideration under the clarified standard. It will leave the district court free to consider a properly supported prospective modification. The limited grant gives the Court no occasion to set a permissible commission or reach the separate *Trump v. CASA* question about relief for nonparties.

Barrett’s opinion will reconcile the specificity required by Rule 65 with the traditional equitable power to enforce an order against evasions. Sotomayor will credit the trial judge’s finding that Apple’s fee and interface restrictions together made directing customers elsewhere economically futile. Her dissent will conclude that this amounted to prohibiting links under the order’s own terms. The parties’ concrete dispute over the contempt finding gives the Court a question it can decide on this record.
