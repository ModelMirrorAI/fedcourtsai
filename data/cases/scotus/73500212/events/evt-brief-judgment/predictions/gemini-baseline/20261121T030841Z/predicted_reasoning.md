# Predicted Reasoning — *Apple Inc. v. Epic Games, Inc.* (No. 25-1311)

## Judgment and Vote Lineup

The Court will **vacate the judgment of the Ninth Circuit and remand for further proceedings** (`vacated`), deciding the case **9–0** with all nine sitting Justices—Roberts, Thomas, Alito, Sotomayor, Kagan, Gorsuch, Kavanaugh, Barrett, and Jackson—participating and voting with the judgment (`majority`). Neither recusal nor dismissal as improvidently granted will prevent a merits decision: no Justice recused at the certiorari or stay stages, and the district court's compensatory attorneys'-fee sanction and permanent link-design injunctions keep the retrospective contempt controversy live regardless of ongoing district-court proceedings on prospective commission structure.

## Scope of Review

Because the Court granted certiorari limited to Question 1, the opinion will address only the substantive standard governing civil contempt of a federal-court injunction and will not revisit Question 2's challenge to developer-wide relief under *Trump v. CASA, Inc.*, 606 U.S. 831 (2025).

## Doctrinal Ground and Breadth

**Doctrinal basis (`majority-ground`).** Extending *Taggart v. Lorenzen*, 587 U.S. 554 (2019), and Federal Rule of Civil Procedure 65(d), the majority will hold that a party may be held in civil contempt only when there is **no fair ground of doubt** that its conduct violated the **operative text** of the injunction. Specifically, the Court will:

1. **Disapprove the Ninth Circuit's "spirit of the injunction" doctrine** (*Institute of Cetacean Research v. Sea Shepherd Conservation Society*, 774 F.3d 935 (9th Cir. 2014)), holding that a court cannot assume the strict letter of an order was obeyed yet impose civil contempt for violating an unstated implicit command;
2. **Reject the Ninth Circuit's subjective-bad-faith override**, holding that although bad faith can bear on remedies or close textual calls where conduct is already objectively prohibited, subjective intent to minimize compliance costs cannot negate a defense where the party's reading of the order's text is objectively reasonable; and
3. **Confine *McComb v. Jacksonville Paper Co.*, 336 U.S. 187 (1949), and *United States v. Terminal Railroad Ass'n*, 226 U.S. 420 (1912)**, to two propositions consistent with *Taggart*: express terms in a decree are read reasonably in context rather than with evasive hyper-literalism, and when an enjoined party exploits a genuine gap in an existing decree—such as charging a commission where an anti-steering order addresses only communication—the proper judicial response is **prospective modification or clarification** under Rule 60(b) and equity, not retroactive civil contempt.

Applying those principles to provide guidance for remand, the Court will hold (or direct) that the 2021 injunction—which barred prohibiting buttons, external links, or other calls to action, said nothing about pricing, and accompanied a liability opinion contemplating commissions—left at least a fair ground of doubt as to whether Apple could charge a 27% commission on linked-out purchases, foreclosing civil contempt on the commission while leaving the Ninth Circuit to reassess the five link-design restrictions (including the express "buttons" and "calls to action" terms) and recalculate any surviving contempt remedies and fees under *Taggart*.

**Breadth (`ground-breadth`).** The majority will state a **categorical, trans-substantive rule** governing civil contempt across federal-court injunctions generally—refusing to confine *Taggart* to bankruptcy discharge orders or create a looser standard for antitrust and unfair-competition decrees—while preserving contextual reading of express decree language and district courts' authority to modify decrees prospectively rather than demanding magic-words specificity for every variation of textually barred conduct. Because the lower courts' remedial provisions and attorneys'-fee award rested on an undifferentiated "spirit" and bad-faith analysis, the Court will vacate and remand the judgment below rather than entering an outright reversal of every link-design specification or crafting a three-part partial affirmance/reversal decree in the first instance.

## Authorship and Separate Writings

Justice **Barrett** is the single likeliest author of the opinion of the Court, followed closely by Justice **Kagan**, Chief Justice **Roberts**, and Justice **Kavanaugh**—all natural assignments for a unanimous or near-unanimous civil-procedure and equitable-remedies opinion tracking the *Taggart* framework. Separate writings are most likely from Justice **Jackson** (and/or Justice **Sotomayor**), either concurring to emphasize that contextual reading of "prohibiting" on remand still reaches sham link-design restrictions and leaves Rule 60(b) modification intact, or dissenting in part if the majority definitively forecloses contempt on the 27% commission; and from Justice **Thomas** (or Justice **Gorsuch**), concurring to underscore historical equity limits and Rule 65(d)'s strict specificity requirement, or Justice **Kavanaugh** concurring briefly to frame the scope of remand.
