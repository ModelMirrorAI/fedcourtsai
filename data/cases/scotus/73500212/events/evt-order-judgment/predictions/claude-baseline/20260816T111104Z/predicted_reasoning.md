# Forecast: Apple Inc. v. Epic Games, Inc., No. 25-1311 (evt-order-judgment)

**Judgment class: `vacated`.** The Court will hold that civil contempt cannot
rest on violation of an injunction's "spirit" where the order's text does not
clearly and unambiguously proscribe the conduct at issue, repudiating the Ninth
Circuit's Sea Shepherd line as inconsistent with Taggart v. Lorenzen's
objective no-fair-ground-of-doubt standard, Rule 65(d)'s specificity command,
and International Longshoremen's Ass'n v. Philadelphia Marine Trade Ass'n. It
will confine McComb v. Jacksonville Paper to evasions of what a decree's text
actually commands. It will then vacate rather than reverse outright: Epic's
alternative ground — that the "prohibitive effect" of Apple's 27% link-out
commission violated the injunction's actual text — was embraced by the panel
alongside the spirit rationale, so the Court will remand for the Ninth Circuit
to decide whether the contempt finding survives under the corrected standard.
P(judgment below disturbed) = **0.82**; the residual mass sits mostly on an
affirmance on the text-violation ground (~0.14), with a small DIG tail (~0.03,
if merits briefing convinces the Court the decision rests independently on
text and the QP is not presented) and negligible equally-divided risk (no
recusal is expected).

**Vote lineup: 7–2.** Majority: Roberts, Thomas, Alito, Kagan, Gorsuch,
Kavanaugh, Barrett. Dissent: Sotomayor and Jackson, on the ground that McComb
forbids rewarding contumacious evasion and that the district court's
bad-faith findings — a commission set to nullify the injunction, justified by
a manufactured study — sustain contempt under any standard. A unanimous
judgment on Taggart's model is the next most likely lineup; the ugly
compliance record is what makes at least one dissent more likely than not.
Predicted majority author: Gorsuch (unscored today; the writing forecast is
banked, not graded).

**Question taken as granted:** the Court limited the grant to Question 1 as
written (the contempt standard) and declined the CASA universal-injunction
question; expect the opinion to reach only the contempt standard and to leave
the injunction's scope untouched.

**Timing:** with petitioner's merits brief due September 14, 2026 and
respondent's November 13, 2026, expect argument in the January or February
2027 sitting and a decision by late June 2027 — more likely the back half of
the Term given a probable separate writing.
