The Court will reverse the judgment of the Ninth Circuit.

The majority opinion will likely hold that a finding of civil contempt requires the violation of an order that clearly and unambiguously proscribes the specific conduct at issue. The Court will reject the Ninth Circuit's willingness to base contempt on the "spirit" of an injunction, emphasizing that Rule 65(d) of the Federal Rules of Civil Procedure demands specificity to ensure that parties have fair notice of exactly what conduct is prohibited. The reasoning will center on the traditional limits of federal equitable authority, establishing a categorical rule that prevents courts from expansively enforcing silent or ambiguous injunctions through the contempt power.

A 6-3 lineup is anticipated. The conservative majority (Roberts, Thomas, Alito, Gorsuch, Kavanaugh, Barrett) will drive the strict interpretation of equitable boundaries and Rule 65(d). The liberal wing (Sotomayor, Kagan, Jackson) is expected to dissent, likely arguing for a more functional approach that affords district courts the flexibility to enforce the practical meaning of their injunctions against sophisticated evasion, especially in complex commercial litigation contexts like this one. No procedural exits (like a DIG) are expected, as the circuit split is squarely presented.
