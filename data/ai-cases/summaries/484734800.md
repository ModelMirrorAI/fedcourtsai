## In brief

The court flagged concerns about publishers' claims that Anthropic's chatbot outputs infringed copyrights and ordered further discussions before ruling.

## What the court decided

This order followed a hearing on motions to dismiss brought by Anthropic and its CEO. The court had already granted the CEO's motion, letting the publishers try to fix the problems identified, but it held off deciding Anthropic's motion. The court explained its concern about one claim: that Claude's outputs reproduced copyrighted lyrics. The publishers' briefing had suggested all 20,517 works at issue were reproduced in outputs, but at the hearing it appeared they were instead asking the court to simply infer infringement from the works' use in training, without pointing to any actual infringing output. The court said this shift did not match what the complaint or briefing had represented.

The court ordered the parties to keep discussing this issue, extended the deadline for a status report to November 13, 2026, and said publishers need not amend their complaint until Anthropic's motion is resolved.
